## Background

Monotone co-design treats each component of a robot as a feasibility relation between a partially ordered set of
delivered functionality and a partially ordered set of required resources. Carrying a payload for a shift needs
motor torque, electrical supply and heat rejection; adding battery capacity adds mass that flows back into the
torque requirement. Where every relation is monotone, that feedback has a least fixed point, found by Kleene
iteration (start at the bottom element, apply the map until it stops moving). The co-design lane of the crate
`frob-synth` would return the minimal resources (least mass, least power) that deliver the functionality, or a
proof that the iteration leaves the admitted resource bound.

Its catalog-closure part is Solid. Its Pareto-antichain part (several incomparable minimal resource vectors, used
to prune and rank whole design variants) is Frontier, and a Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier before it is funded. The danger the pilot looks for is specific: a monotone budget model is easy to
force onto a plant that is not monotone. A changed gearing can trade a larger static moment for a blocked swing;
a shared electrical bus is a joint, time-dependent resource (two fingers on one unit-current bus have zero
feasible one-tick schedules and two feasible two-tick schedules with identical local motor limits, so a rule that
adds peak demands prunes a feasible design). A prune is wrong if any discarded design is independently feasible
under the same mission, even when the eventual winner survives.

## Scope

In: the sweep; the two arms; the independent feasibility check of every pruned variant; the rank comparison
against the referee; the falsifier; the record. Out: the lane's product code (BD-360); the gate script (N25d-10);
the kill decision (N25d-11).

## Technical approach

**The sweep (frozen before the run).** A wheeled carrier on the cart-and-sill mission family (a differential or
single-axle drive carrying a payload over a threshold and docking). Twenty variants from three levers: wheel
radius (4 values), body mass with payload (the co-design feedback variable; 2 payload levels) and gearbox ratio
(catalog steps), chosen as a fractional design so that the count is 20 and each lever takes every value at least
four times. DESIGN, a regular fraction that meets both conditions: five catalog ratios, and the half fraction
{(r, p, g) : r + p + g even} of the 4 × 2 × 5 full factorial (indices from 0), which gives 20 variants with each
radius 5 times, each payload level 10 times and each ratio 4 times (with six or more ratios some ratio appears
fewer than four times; with four the full factorial has 32 runs and no regular fraction of size 20). The
variant list, the catalog rows and the mission are hashed before either arm runs.

**The feedback path that both arms must trace end to end**: electrical limits (bus voltage and current ceiling,
winding resistance) → drive torque available at the wheel through the gearbox → normal load on the driven wheels
(mass and its distribution) → traction (friction coefficient times normal load) → what the controller can see
(wheel speed, current). A variant is feasible when the torque needed to climb the threshold at the required
speed is inside the voltage- and current-limited envelope, traction suffices, and the bus schedule closes.

**Arm A, the lane on paper.** The monotone relations written down as tables over the catalog (torque needed is
monotone in mass and wheel radius; torque available is monotone in gear ratio at fixed speed until the voltage
limit; traction is monotone in normal load), the Kleene iteration run by script in exact rationals, the
antichain of minimal (mass, peak power) vectors returned, variants outside it pruned or ranked below.

**Arm B, the comparator.** A conventional dependency graph over the same variants given the same checked
envelopes and warm solves: each variant evaluated by the existing closure analysis and actuator-envelope
analysis, memoized on its inputs. A conventional monotone co-design solver is an admissible implementation of
arm A; if it gives the same answers it may be the implementation.

**Independent check.** Every variant that arm A prunes is evaluated in full under the same mission: static
analyses, then a rollout on the CPU referee with the wheeled baseline controller. Every variant in the top five
of arm A's ranking is ranked again by the referee result (time to complete with hard limits respected).

**Falsifier (fixed before the run).** Any pruned design is independently feasible under the same mission; or the
lane mis-ranks more than 20% of the top designs against the referee (more than one of the top five out of place
relative to the referee order).

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any timing.** Bounded by the share of the design decision that is
component sizing. No ratio is claimed; the phase ledger of the incumbent design loop gives that share.

**Record (pilot register, N38a-09; the row is N25d-40's row 18).** The record `[[pilot]]` `PILOT-CODESIGN-01`
(DESIGN identifier in the register's `PILOT-<set>-<nn>` form; N25d-40 enters row 18 and is asked to confirm it)
in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`: `mechanism` Pareto
antichains over catalogs used to prune and rank; `tier = "F"`; `owner = "§25.21.1"`; `bead = "N25d-09"`;
`work_package = "WP-67"`; `protocol = { path = "docs/pilots/PILOT-CODESIGN-01.md", blake3, frozen, ledger_seq }`;
`falsifier` as above, with its counts (one falsely pruned design; more than one of the top five out of place);
`ceiling = { statement = "the component-sizing share of the design decision", value = "none on speed", basis =
"the phase ledger of the incumbent design loop" }`; `fallback = { ships = "the closure analysis A-10 for
actuator, battery and compute selection", kill_gate = "KILL-SYNTH-CODESIGN" }`; `cost` (TARGET: two
engineer-days, CPU minutes plus 20 short referee rollouts; `envelope = "default"`); `tool` (the comparator solver
and its pin, `site = "host"`); `status = "registered"`; `integrated_gate = "GATE-AUGUR-17"`. Before either arm
runs this bead writes the protocol (the 20 variants, the catalog rows, the mission, both arms, the independent
check, the falsifier and the ceiling) to that path and registers its hash. The outcome is appended as one
`[[pilot.result]]` under the registered hash (verdict `falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds`), citing the
report `docs/pilots/codesign_sweep.md`. A pilot result never sets any part of GATE-AUGUR-17.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Variant list, catalog rows, mission and protocol are hashed and committed before either arm runs.
- [ ] For every variant: arm A's verdict and rank, arm B's verdict, and for pruned or top-five variants the
      independent referee result, in one table.
- [ ] The count of falsely pruned designs (must be 0 to pass) and the count of mis-ranked top designs are
      reported with the falsifier's verdict in one sentence.
- [ ] The bus control is exercised: a variant whose two drive motors share one current-limited bus and need
      staggered current peaks is not pruned by arm A; an arm using the additive peak rule prunes it, and that arm
      is recorded as falsified.
- [ ] The result, including a negative one, is recorded in the pilot register and the review log.
- [ ] The protocol is hashed and `PILOT-CODESIGN-01` is `registered` with that hash in `pilots.toml` before
      either arm runs; the register's load checks accept it; the outcome is an appended `[[pilot.result]]`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot script's own checks: `kleene_matches_closed_form` (actuator mass m_a = τ_req/ρ_τ, τ_req = c·m·g·L,
m = m_f + n·m_a; with n = 12, c = 1/4, L = 3/10 m, ρ_τ = 15 N·m/kg and g = 981/100 m/s² the loop gain is
κ = 2943/5000 and a 6 kg structure closes at 30000/2057 kg ≈ 14.58 kg); `divergent_loop_excluded` (L = 13/25 m
gives κ = 38259/37500 > 1); `bus_two_tick_feasible` (zero one-tick schedules, two two-tick schedules);
`fraction_balanced` (the 20 variants of the half fraction: radius 5 times each, payload 10, ratio 4; no variant
twice); `falsifier_counts` (one false prune holds the falsifier; two of five out of place holds it; one of five
does not); `additive_peak_arm_prunes_bus_variant` (the planted peak-sum arm prunes the staggered-bus variant).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_codesign_sweep.sh` first asserts that `PILOT-CODESIGN-01` is registered and that the protocol
file hashes to its registered digest, then runs both arms and the independent checks: `frob design check
<variant> --json` (a read verb) and `frob sim run <variant> --scene <mission scene> --tier referee
--key cd-ref-<variant>-<run> --json` per pruned or top-five variant (one key per variant), and appends the
`[[pilot.result]]`. Through the STD-E2E helper it writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_codesign_sweep/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25d-09`), `step`, `event`, the exact command,
key, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block. Per variant: levers, arm verdicts, rank, referee
outcome, wall time; `metric` lines for false prunes (threshold 0) and mis-ranked share (threshold 0.20); the bus
control's outcome per arm; the protocol hash; on failure the first falsely pruned variant with its replay
address; a `summary` with the verdict and the kill gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-643 and CX-278 (the two-finger bus and the additive peak rule); KA-077 (the fixed point matches the closed
form of the mass spiral). Planted control: one variant constructed to be feasible only through a non-monotone
effect (a higher gear ratio that passes the torque check and fails the speed requirement); a lane that prunes by
torque monotonicity alone mis-ranks it, and the pilot must show the lane keeps it in a separate cell.

## Considerations

- The referee rollouts need the wheeled baseline controller and the bus model; if either is late the pilot uses
  the static analyses plus the planar referee fixture and says so.
- A tie with the dependency graph is the expected outcome on correctness; the pilot is about false prunes.
- Locomotion quality is not monotone in geometry; any performance surrogate used for ranking makes that ranking
  `Estimated`.
