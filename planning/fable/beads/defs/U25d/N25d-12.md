## Background

Trajectory optimization with design variables refines one design for one motion: a direct transcription whose
decision variables are states, controls and contact forces at knot points together with a vector θ of continuous
design parameters (link lengths, attachment points, trunk shape, motor mass, gear ratio), under dynamics
defects, friction cones, actuator envelopes, joint limits and the mission's hard limits, with the contact
schedule, the topology and the task fixed in advance. In this program it would be the strategy `refine` of
`mission.synthesize` in the crate `frob-synth`. It is Frontier, so it needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier
before its code is funded.

What is known and what is not. Its published scale (FACT, source register; to be re-read from the primary
sources before a gate cites it): on a 12-degree-of-freedom quadruped with a design vector of 4 to 17 parameters
the worst observed solve was 252 s against 600 s for a covariance-matrix evolution strategy on 8 threads, and
cost improvements held in simulation within 10% of those found in optimization (Dinev et al.); a two-joint
monoped took about 9 hours for 10³ trajectory problems at 33.72 s per solver call (Fadini et al.). The claim
that survives is: about 12 degrees of freedom, up to 17 continuous parameters, a fixed contact schedule, 4 to 10
minutes on 8 CPU threads. What no source reports is the fraction of optimized designs that pass an independent
closed-loop evaluation. That fraction is the deciding number, it is unmeasured, and this pilot measures it
before any transcription is written here.

Ceiling, stated before any measurement: the lane refines; it does not decide more designs per turn. An existing
optimizer over an existing dynamics library does this today, so the lane is parity, not an advantage.

## Scope

In: generating candidates with an existing external optimizer outside the product; evaluating each on this
program's referee; the falsifier; the record. Out: writing a transcription (N25d-13) or the strategy (BD-362);
the gate on the product lane (N25d-14); the kill decision (N25d-15).

## Technical approach

**Robot and missions (frozen before the run).** The reference 12-degree-of-freedom quadruped. Three missions:
a trot at 1 m/s on flat ground; a 0.3 m step up; carrying a 5 kg payload. Design vector: between 4 and 17
continuous parameters drawn from link lengths, hip attachment points, trunk length and width, motor mass and
gear ratio, with their bounds. Twenty candidates per mission, from twenty starting designs sampled inside the
bounds by address (seeded), so 60 solves. (The pilot's sentence reads "20 candidate designs for three missions";
the gate that follows it counts 20 per mission, so the pilot uses 20 per mission and records the reading.)

**Arm: the external optimizer.** An existing trajectory optimizer with design variables over an existing rigid
body dynamics library, at pinned versions recorded in the pilot register, run outside the workspace on 8 CPU
threads with a wall-clock limit of 10 minutes per solve. For each solve record: wall time, iterations, final
constraint violation, objective before and after, and the optimized θ.

**Evaluation, independent of the optimizer.** Each optimized design and the nominal design are written as URDF
documents (the nominal document with the optimized θ substituted, by the pilot's own conversion step, since
`design.import` reads URDF or MJCF), imported as designs, given the synthesized baseline controller for their class (no per-design tuning, the same
synthesis rule for both), and run on the CPU referee under the mission's requirement on held-out terrain: terrain
instances generated with seeds the optimizer never saw, at the mission's difficulty setting. Outcome per
candidate: passes the requirement (hard limits respected, goal reached in time), and beats the nominal design on
the mission's metric in a paired comparison over the same held-out worlds (paired by world address and seed;
a lower confidence bound on the paired difference above zero).

**Falsifier (fixed before the run).** For any of the three missions: more than 10 of 20 candidates fail or do
not beat nominal; or solves exceed the budget (any solve over 10 minutes on 8 threads counts as a failed
candidate, and a median above 10 minutes fails the pilot outright).

**Record (pilot register, N38a-09; the row is N25d-40's row 19).** The record `[[pilot]]` `PILOT-TRAJOPT-01`
(DESIGN identifier in the register's `PILOT-<set>-<nn>` form; N25d-40 enters row 19 and is asked to confirm the
identifier) in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`: `mechanism`
trajectory optimization with design variables; `tier = "F"`; `owner = "§25.21.2"`; `bead = "N25d-12"`;
`work_package = "WP-67"`; `protocol = { path = "docs/pilots/PILOT-TRAJOPT-01.md", blake3, frozen, ledger_seq }`;
`falsifier` as above, with its counts (more than 10 of 20 per mission; a median above 600 s); `ceiling =
{ statement = "parity with existing optimizers", value = "none on speed", basis = "the lane refines; it does
not decide more designs per turn" }`; `fallback = { ships = "continuous optimization with the baseline
controller in the inner loop (opt.start)", kill_gate = "KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT" }`; `cost` (TARGET: three
engineer-days and 10 GPU-hours, `envelope` as approved under N44-01); `tool` (the external optimizer and the
dynamics library with their pins, `site = "host"`); `status = "registered"`; `integrated_gate =
"GATE-AUGUR-18"`. Before the first solve this bead writes the protocol (missions, design bounds, starting
designs, held-out battery and its seeds, the falsifier, the ceiling, the study rule) to that path and registers
its hash. The outcome is appended as one `[[pilot.result]]` under the registered hash (verdict
`falsifier-rejected` or `falsifier-holds`): per mission the pass count, the beat-nominal count, solve-time
quantiles, and the share of the optimizer's predicted improvement that survived on the referee; the report
`docs/pilots/trajopt_design.md` is cited from it. A pilot result never sets any part of GATE-AUGUR-18.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Missions, design bounds, starting designs, held-out terrain seeds and the protocol are hashed and
      committed before the first solve.
- [ ] 60 solves are recorded, each with wall time, iterations, violation and objective; a solve that hits the
      limit is recorded as such, not dropped.
- [ ] Every candidate and the nominal design have referee results on the same held-out worlds, with the paired
      comparison and its lower bound.
- [ ] The falsifier's verdict per mission is stated in one sentence and recorded in the pilot register and the
      review log.
- [ ] The protocol is hashed and `PILOT-TRAJOPT-01` is `registered` with that hash in `pilots.toml` before the
      first solve; the register's load checks accept it; the outcome is an appended `[[pilot.result]]`.
- [ ] The published figures are re-read from their primary sources and the reading is recorded (confirmed or
      corrected).
- [ ] Potency control: a deliberately worsened candidate (a link length pushed to its bound against the
      optimizer's direction) must fail to beat nominal on the referee.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot's evaluation script: `paired_bound_constant_ratio` (ten pairs with a constant difference return that
difference as the bound); `held_out_seeds_disjoint` (no terrain seed appears in the optimizer's inputs);
`import_round_trip` (an optimized θ written as URDF and imported as a design reproduces the optimizer's link
lengths to the importer's stated loss); `falsifier_counts` (synthetic results (11, 10, 12) failing-or-not-beating per mission
hold the falsifier; (10, 10, 10) reject it; a median of 610 s holds it); `record_fields_complete` (the
`PILOT-TRAJOPT-01` record passes the register's load check and fails it with the falsifier's number removed).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_trajopt_design.sh` first asserts that `PILOT-TRAJOPT-01` is registered and that the protocol
file hashes to its registered digest, then drives the evaluation side per candidate and for the nominal design:
`frob design import <m>-<i>.urdf --name <m>-<i> --key imp-<m>-<i>-<run> --json` (the URDF document the
conversion step wrote from the optimized θ), `frob control synth <design>
--key ctl-<design>-<run> --json`, `frob eval run <design> --battery <m>-held-out --tier referee
--key ev-<m>-<design>-<run> --json` (the held-out battery holds the reserved terrain seeds, frozen with the
protocol), and `frob eval compare <nominal> <design> --task <m> --battery <m>-held-out --key cmp-<m>-<i>-<run> --json`
against nominal (with `--battery` the paired worlds are drawn from the held-out battery's items, not from the
task's own worlds); no two compared calls share a key. It appends the `[[pilot.result]]`. Through the STD-E2E helper it
writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_trajopt_design/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N25d-12`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, key, exit code, wall time and the envelope's
`evidence` block. Per candidate: the mission, θ, solve time, the referee verdict, the paired difference with its
bound and threshold 0; `metric` lines per mission for pass count and beat-nominal count with threshold 10 of 20
and the median solve against 600 s; the tool pins; the A/A and potency controls' outcomes; on failure the first
failing candidate with its replay addresses; a `summary` with the verdict and the kill gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
No exact known answer exists for this pilot; its controls are the potency control above and an A/A comparison
(the nominal design against itself must not "beat nominal"). Determinism: referee results twice on one machine
are identical.

## Considerations

- The optimized plan assumes rigid contact and ideal actuators unless the optimizer models the cards; a design
  that looks better in the optimizer and worse on the referee is the expected failure and is the point.
- GPU-hours are for batched evaluation only if the batched tier is used to pre-screen; the verdicts come from
  the referee.
- A result at the falsifier's edge (exactly 10 of 20) passes; the rule is "more than 10".
