## Background

The refinement lane of Augur's constructive crate `frob-synth` improves one design for one motion by solving a
nonlinear program: a direct transcription whose decision variables are states, controls and contact forces at
knot points and a vector θ of continuous design parameters (link lengths, attachment points, trunk shape, motor
mass, gear ratio). Constraints are the dynamics defects, friction cones, actuator envelopes from the cards,
joint limits and the mission's hard limits. The contact schedule, the topology and the task are fixed in
advance. Gradients with respect to θ come from the derived kernels.

The solver exists in a sibling; the transcription does not. FACT (FrankenSim, to be re-checked at the pinned
commit): `fs-ascent` has sequential quadratic programming and an augmented Lagrangian, both with certificates of
KKT residuals; `fs-adjoint` has an implicit-function-theorem module. No collocation, multiple-shooting or
differential-dynamic-programming trajectory optimizer was found in a search of `fs-opt`, `fs-ascent`,
`fs-control` and `fs-adjoint`. So the transcription is new work here, on the CPU, in double precision. This bead
builds the nonlinear program; BD-362 solves it and packages the result.

Design decisions. Direct transcription (all knot variables explicit) over shooting: the constraint Jacobian is
banded in time with a dense border for θ, which the solver's sparse linear algebra exploits, and path
constraints (cones, envelopes) are stated at knots without integration. A fixed contact schedule: optimizing
through contact switches needs complementarity constraints or mode enumeration, which the lane does not attempt;
a motion whose best schedule differs is a different query. Rejected: writing a solver (the sibling has two);
unrolling the simulator and differentiating through contact (derivatives through contact switches are unreliable
and carry validity labels elsewhere in the program).

Target scale (TARGET, from the published record): about 12 actuated degrees of freedom, up to 17 parameters,
a solve in 4 to 10 minutes on 8 CPU threads.

## Scope

In: the recheck of the sibling fact; the problem builder; variable layout, bounds and scaling; constraint and
objective evaluation with exact sparsity; first derivatives; the initial guess; unit fixtures with closed-form
answers; the example program `transcription_report` and its own end-to-end script. Out: calling the solver, sensitivities, candidates and the verb strategy (BD-362); second-order
information beyond what the solver approximates.

## Technical approach

**Step 0, the recheck.** Search the pinned FrankenSim commit for a trajectory optimizer (collocation, multiple
shooting, differential dynamic programming). If one exists and passes an admission test on fixture 1 below, it
is admitted through the airlock and this bead reduces to an adapter; the outcome is recorded either way.

**Variables** for N knot intervals (DESIGN default N = 50), step h_k (fixed by the schedule):
- states x_k = (q_k, v_k), k = 0 … N, with q on its configuration group (rotations updated by a retraction, never
  by adding angles to a quaternion);
- controls u_k (joint torques), k = 0 … N − 1;
- contact forces λ_{c,k} ∈ ℝ³ for each contact c that the schedule marks active on interval k;
- design parameters θ ∈ ℝᵖ, 1 ≤ p ≤ 17, each with unit, chart and box from the query's design domain.
Layout: per-knot blocks in time order, θ last. For a quadruped with a floating base (18 velocities), 12 controls
and 4 feet: about 60 variables per knot, about 3,000 in all.

**Constraints.**
1. Dynamics defects, per interval (implicit midpoint by default):
   M(q̄, θ)·(v_{k+1} − v_k) − h·[Sᵀ·u_k − c(q̄, v̄, θ) + Σ_c J_c(q̄, θ)ᵀ·λ_{c,k}] = 0 and
   q_{k+1} = q_k ⊞ h·v̄, with q̄, v̄ the midpoint state, M the mass matrix, c the bias force, S the actuation
   selector, J_c the contact Jacobian.
2. Contact kinematics of the fixed schedule: for an active contact, zero height φ_c(q_k, θ) = 0 and zero
   tangential velocity at the contact point; for an inactive one, λ = 0 and φ_c ≥ clearance.
3. Friction cones on every active force: λ_n ≥ 0 and ‖λ_t‖ ≤ μ·λ_n, μ from the interface card (stated as a
   second-order cone; a linearized pyramid is an option recorded in the problem identity).
4. Actuator envelopes from the cards at each knot: peak torque |τ| ≤ η·N_g·τ_peak; voltage
   R·|τ|/(η·N_g·k_t) + k_e·N_g·|q̇| ≤ V_bus; a root-mean-square torque limit over the motion for the thermal
   rating. Where gear ratio N_g or motor mass is in θ these are nonlinear in θ and are differentiated as such.
5. Joint limits, and the mission's hard limits as path constraints (clearance, payload support, speed caps).
6. Boundary conditions from the task (initial and terminal sets; periodicity for a gait stride).

**Objective.** The mission's declared cost over the motion (for example electrical energy
Σ_k [R·(τ/(η·N_g·k_t))² + τ·q̇]·h per joint, or time to goal), plus no hidden regularizer: any regularization
term is named and its weight is part of the problem identity.

**Derivatives.** First derivatives of every constraint and of the objective with respect to x, u, λ and θ are
assembled from the derived derivative programs (N21a-20), with an exact sparsity pattern: each defect touches
knots k and k + 1 and all of θ. A finite-difference check on random points runs in tests and on demand.

**Scaling and initial guess.** Variables are scaled by declared magnitudes (lengths by a body length, torques by
card peak). The initial guess is the nominal design's motion: a rollout under the baseline controller or a gait
orbit, resampled to the knots, with θ at the nominal design.

**Interface (DESIGN).**
```rust
pub struct TrajProblem { layout: Layout, bounds: Bounds, schedule: ContactSchedule, theta: Vec<DesignParam>,
                         identity: ProblemId }           // hash of model, task, schedule, N, options
impl TrajProblem { fn eval(&self, z: &[f64]) -> Residuals;  fn jacobian(&self, z: &[f64]) -> SparseJacobian;
                   fn objective(&self, z: &[f64]) -> (f64, Vec<f64>);  fn initial_guess(&self) -> Vec<f64>; }
```
Determinism: assembly is deterministic for a fixed thread count; reductions use fixed slots. Class declared D1
on one machine (same bits run to run); across machines D3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The recheck of the sibling fact is recorded with the commit searched and its outcome.
- [ ] Fixture 1 (point mass) and fixture 2 (gear ratio) below are assembled; evaluating the known optimal
      trajectories gives defects below 10⁻¹⁰ and the closed-form costs within the stated discretization error.
- [ ] The Jacobian agrees with central differences to 10⁻⁶ relative at 100 random points on the reference
      quadruped problem, and its sparsity pattern contains every structurally non-zero entry.
- [ ] A contact outside the declared schedule cannot carry force (its λ block does not exist).
- [ ] The problem identity changes when the schedule, N, a cone option or a regularization weight changes.
- [ ] Assembly of the reference problem (12 actuated joints, p = 17, N = 50) is timed and recorded in the log of
      `scripts/e2e/synth_transcription.sh`, which passes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-synth/tests/transcription.rs` (synthetic fixtures with closed forms, real kernels for the robot):
- `point_mass_rest_to_rest`: mass m moved from rest at 0 to rest at 1 m in 1 s minimizing ∫u² dt. Continuous
  optimum: acceleration a(t) = 6 − 12·t, u = m·a, cost 12·m². At m = 1 the discrete cost converges to 12 with
  second-order error in h (checked at N = 25, 50, 100).
- `gear_ratio_inertia_match`: a load of mass 4 kg driven through ratio N_g by a rotor of reflected inertia
  1 kg·N_g² (unit radius); motor effort τ = (4/N_g + N_g)·a; cost ∫τ² dt = 12·(4/N_g + N_g)² for the same
  move. Minimum at N_g = √(4/1) = 2 with cost 192; cost 300 at N_g = 1 and at N_g = 4. The builder's objective
  and its derivative with respect to N_g reproduce these (derivative zero at 2, negative at 1, positive at 4).
- `friction_cone_block`: a force (λ_n, λ_t) = (10, 6) N with μ = 0.5 violates the cone by 1 N; with μ = 0.8 it
  is inside.
- `voltage_envelope_row`: R = 1 Ω, η = 1, N_g = 10, k_t = k_e = 0.1, τ = 20 N·m at the joint, q̇ = 20 rad/s:
  R·τ/(N_g·k_t) + k_e·N_g·q̇ = 20 + 20 = 40 V, feasible at 48 V and infeasible at 36 V.
- `jacobian_matches_differences`; `sparsity_is_superset`; `schedule_excludes_inactive_forces`.
- Property test: for random feasible linear-dynamics problems, a trajectory integrated by the same midpoint rule
  has zero defect.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/synth_transcription.sh` (owned here, built on this bead's dependencies: there is no verb surface, so
it drives the example program `crates/frob-synth/examples/transcription_report.rs`, which builds a problem and
prints its identity, sizes, assembly time, the defects and cost at a supplied trajectory and the objective's
derivative with respect to θ as JSON; BD-362's lane script runs this script as a stage). Steps: `cargo run -p
frob-synth --example transcription_report -- --fixture point-mass --knots <N> --at optimal --json` at N = 25, 50
and 100 (defects below 10⁻¹⁰; cost converging to 12 with error ratios near 4); `--fixture gear-ratio --theta
N_g=<r>` at r = 1, 2 and 4 (costs 300, 192 and 300 within the discretization error; derivative negative, zero
and positive); `--fixture reference-quadruped --params 17 --knots 50` (variables near 3,000, constraints,
non-zeros and assembly time logged; the inactive contacts have no force block in the printed layout); the same
with `--knots 40` and with `--cone pyramid` (a different problem identity each); the reference problem twice at a
fixed thread count (identical identity and residual digests). Through the STD-E2E helper it writes
`target/e2e-logs/synth_transcription/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript: every line `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N25d-13`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code and wall time; `assert` lines with expected and
observed values; `metric` lines per problem for the identity, knots, variables, constraints, non-zeros and
assembly time; on failure the first differing value and the command to reproduce; a `summary`. The derivative
check against central differences stays `jacobian_matches_differences` above, not a script step.

### Known answers and negative controls
The two closed forms above (synthetic, derived on file in `docs/derivations/augur-transcription.md`). Planted
defects the tests must catch: a defect that omits the contact force term (the point-mass-on-ground variant no
longer balances gravity); a θ column dropped from the Jacobian (the gear-ratio derivative reads zero at
N_g = 1); angles added to a quaternion in place of the retraction (unit norm lost; the group-membership check
fails).

## Considerations

- KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT stands over this work: if solves exceed the budget at 12 degrees of freedom the lane falls
  back to continuous optimization with the baseline controller in the inner loop.
- The optimized plan assumes rigid contact and ideal actuators except where the cards add limits; the builder
  records which card terms were active so the candidate's assumptions are complete.
- Expression swell in derivative programs for 17 parameters is a known risk of the derivation toolchain; the
  fallback is derivative programs per limb with finite differences for the remaining θ columns, labelled.
