## Background

A kill gate states in advance when a mechanism is abandoned and what ships instead. KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT governs
the refinement lane of the crate `frob-synth`: trajectory optimization with continuous design variables (the
strategy `refine` of `mission.synthesize`), which improves one design for one motion with a fixed contact
schedule and returns it as a candidate.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | GATE-AUGUR-18 fails, or solves exceed the budget at 12 degrees of freedom |
| Fallback, named in advance | Continuous optimization of the optimization chapter with the baseline controller in the inner loop |
| Decided | M4, recorded as an ADR |
| Decides on | The pilot with an external optimizer (N25d-12); the gate on the product lane (N25d-14) |

The lane's stated ceiling is parity with existing optimizers, so the gate is not about speed against a rival.
It asks whether optimized designs are worth evaluating at all: do at least half of them survive an independent
closed-loop evaluation and beat the design they started from, at a solve cost that fits a design loop.

## Scope

In: the rule on recorded values; the ADR; the registry update; re-reading dependents; the fallback as work.
Out: the measurements.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** From the pilot: per mission the count of external-optimizer candidates that pass and beat nominal
on the referee, and solve-time quantiles. From the gate: the same counts for the product lane, per mission, and
the slowest and median solve at 12 degrees of freedom on 8 threads.

**Rule (a pure function of the record).**
1. Before any product code: if the pilot's falsifier held (for any mission more than 10 of 20 fail or do not
   beat nominal, or solves exceed the budget), fire. The transcription and the strategy are then not built.
   This is the funding rule of the pilot register (N38a-09: a work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not
   funded and its fallback ships) applied to the trigger's own two clauses measured on the external-optimizer
   arm; the trigger text is not restated or changed, and the ADR records the reason `pilot falsifier held`
   beside it. Readiness in this branch is rule 7 of the lifecycle rules (N41-17): the beads that exist only for
   the funded mechanism can never become ready, so the session that records the funding decision on the pilot's
   result (`fundable(WP-67)` = `Blocked{falsifier-holds}`) closes N25d-13, BD-362 and N25d-14 `withdrawn`, citing
   the pilot record PILOT-TRAJOPT-01 and its `[[pilot.result]]`; that makes this bead ready, and its ADR lists
   the three as "withdrawn before this decision".
2. After the product lane exists: fire if GATE-AUGUR-18 fails for any of the three missions after one repair
   cycle, or if the median solve at 12 degrees of freedom exceeds 600 s on 8 threads. Reading of "solves exceed
   the budget" (DESIGN, the same as the pilot's falsifier): a single solve above 600 s is not a trigger by
   itself; it counts as one failed candidate in the gate's per-mission count, which is how it reaches clause 1
   of the trigger. A median above 600 s is a budget failure of the lane and fires directly.
3. Otherwise keep (outcome `not-fired`).

**Outcomes and downstream.**

| Outcome | Record | Downstream |
|---|---|---|
| Kept | ADR with counts and solve times; outcome `not-fired` | `--strategy refine` ships as a proposer of candidates; consumers treat its sensitivities as `fixed-mode` |
| Fired on the pilot | ADR; outcome `fired`, status `killed`, fallback adopted | N25d-13, BD-362 and N25d-14 are already closed `withdrawn` by the funding session (N41-17 rule 7) and the ADR lists them "withdrawn before this decision"; `--strategy refine` routes to the fallback adapter below and says so in the answer, so an agent's refine-shaped query keeps working (DESIGN: one behaviour for both branches; `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, only if `opt.start` itself is unavailable in the build) |
| Fired on the gate | ADR; outcome `fired`, status `killed`, fallback adopted | The lane's code is removed or left behind a disabled feature; `--strategy refine` routes to the fallback adapter and says so in the answer |

**The fallback as work.** Continuous optimization with the baseline controller in the inner loop already
belongs to the optimization verbs (`opt.start`): derivative-free search over the same boxed θ, each evaluation a
closed-loop rollout of the design under its synthesized baseline controller, with racing between candidates.
What this bead adds if the gate fires: (1) a thin adapter so that a refine-shaped query (one base design, one
task, free continuous θ) is translated into an `opt.start` request with the mission metric as objective and the
hard limits as constraints; (2) the answer's `explanations[]` entry "refinement withdrawn by
KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT; candidates below come from derivative-free search with closed-loop evaluation"; (3) a test
on the gear-ratio fixture that the adapter finds ratio 2 within its tolerance. Candidates from the fallback are
`Tested` on the tier they were rolled out on and still have no verdict.

**Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`) with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the
trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the
trigger's number (per mission the pass counts from pilot and gate against 10 of 20; the median and slowest
solve at 12 degrees of freedom against 600 s); the outcome and, in the pilot branch, the reason; the fallback
now in force and the capability's name from now on ("derivative-free refinement with the baseline
controller"); the beads re-read (N25d-13, BD-362, N25d-14, and the consumers of `--strategy refine`), each
"stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR" (in the pilot branch the first three are listed "withdrawn
before this decision"); what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and
seeds; the recording session, which did not implement the lane or run the measurements. The kill row of
KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT in `registries/gates.toml` (`kind = "kill"`) receives `decision = { outcome, adr, date,
session }` with `outcome = "fired"` or `"not-fired"`.

**Registry and closing.** Status, measured values and the ADR identifier go into the kill-gate row; this bead
closes with the typed reason `pass` (kept, citing this bead's decision test and its output) or `fallback` (with
the ADR and the fallback's name; N41-17). It also writes the gate's owner declaration, the block N40-30 compares with
the ledger: `[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and
`reads` copied, not restated, and the `decision` block (outcome, ADR, measured values each citing its receipt);
N25d-41's map names this bead as the deciding bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The rule is applied to recorded values, each cited by ledger identifier.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT-M4.md` exists with every field N40-03 requires and gives per-mission
      counts from pilot and gate, solve-time quantiles, and the outcome; the kill row carries `decision` with the
      outcome, the ADR path, the date and the session, and the register's load checks accept it.
- [ ] In the pilot branch, N25d-13, BD-362 and N25d-14 are closed `withdrawn` citing the pilot record before this
      bead starts, and the ADR lists them as "withdrawn before this decision".
- [ ] If fired: the fallback adapter is in force with its test, and the capability is named
      "derivative-free refinement with the baseline controller" in the status table.
- [ ] Dependents are re-read and closed or kept as the table says; the M4 exit review finds the ADR.
- [ ] Negative control of the rule: a synthetic record with counts (12, 9, 15) returns "fire".

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `rule_pilot_falsifier_fires`: pilot counts (11, 10, 12) keep; (9, 14, 14) fire.
- `rule_gate_any_mission_fails`: (10, 10, 10) keep; (12, 9, 15) fire.
- `rule_solve_budget`: one solve at 640 s with median 310 s keeps (and that candidate is counted failed);
  median 620 s fires.
- `rule_is_pure`.
- `fallback_adapter_gear_ratio`: the translated request converges to ratio 2 ± 0.05 on the closed-form fixture.
- `fallback_answer_explains`: a refine query under the fired state returns candidates from `opt.start` with the
  `explanations[]` entry naming KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT, each labelled `candidate` with a null verdict.
- `rule_matches_ledger_trigger`: the rule's clauses and thresholds (10 of 20; 600 s at 12 degrees of freedom)
  equal the ledger row's (N40-26), and the owner declaration's trigger text is byte-equal to it.
- `pilot_branch_needs_withdrawn_gate_bead`: a pilot record `falsifier-holds` with N25d-14 (or N25d-13, or BD-362)
  still open makes the decision tool refuse with the reason "gate bead not withdrawn"; with it closed `withdrawn` citing the pilot
  record the tool writes outcome `fired` with reason `pilot falsifier held`.
- `adr_fields_complete`: an ADR missing any N40-03 field, or a kill row whose `decision.adr` names a missing
  file, is rejected by the register check.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_synth_trajopt.sh` reads the pilot and gate records through `frob evidence trace`, applies the
rule, and logs each input with its ledger identifier and threshold (10 of 20; 600 s), the outcome and the
proposed registry row; it re-runs the rule on the synthetic (12, 9, 15) record and requires `fire`; it asserts
that the kill row's `decision` and the ADR state the same outcome; if the outcome is `fired`, it runs
`frob mission synthesize <gear-ratio fixture> --strategy refine --key kill-trajopt-fb-<run> --json` and asserts
the fallback's explanation, the ratio 2 ± 0.05 and the null verdict. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/kill_synth_trajopt/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the evidence block of the record read;
one `metric` line per clause with the measured value, threshold and verdict and the record digests; a
`summary` names the kill gate; non-zero exit on missing inputs or dangling evidence.

### Known answers and negative controls
The synthetic records above; the gear-ratio closed form for the fallback.

## Considerations

- The pilot can kill the lane before a line of it is written; that is the cheap branch and the reason the
  pilot runs first.
- The fallback costs more rollouts per refined design and needs no derivative programs with respect to θ.
