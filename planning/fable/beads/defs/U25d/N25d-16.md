## Background

The linkage lane of Augur's crate `frob-synth` would design parallel legs, ankles, steering linkages and small
closed chains constructively from precision poses (poses the mechanism must reach exactly), in exact arithmetic,
inside the real limits of the symbolic sibling FrankenSymPy. FACT (its source, to be re-checked at the pin):
the real solver is capped at 8 variables, 64 equations, 4,096 terms, total degree 256, 4,096-bit coefficients,
2 × 10⁸ work units and 512 MiB, for zero-dimensional ideals only; Gröbner bases are capped at 1,024 basis
polynomials and 10⁶ pending pairs; there is no homotopy solver. Unbounded whole-robot synthesis is not built.

The algebra is textbook; the certificate is the value. A root of the synthesis equations is not a mechanism: a
linkage can satisfy every pose equation and still be unable to move from one pose to the next without
disassembly, because the poses lie on different assembly circuits (connected components of its configuration
set). The lane's claim is that a linkage it certifies moves through its poses in order on one circuit, without
crossing a branch point, without self-collision, with the required reach, for every as-built unit inside a
tolerance box.

The lane is Frontier and needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before funding. Two things can sink it: a
certificate that is wrong when a certified linkage is actually driven, or a problem class that mostly does not
fit inside the symbolic caps.

Ceiling, stated first: three-position synthesis has closed-form circumcentres, so no speed claim is made against
a competent baseline; the claim is the certificate.

## Scope

In: generating 240 instances; the exact arm; the numerical arm; the referee check of every certified linkage;
the cap census; the falsifier; the record. Out: product code (N25d-17, N25d-18, BD-361); the gate script
(N25d-19); the kill decision (N25d-20).

## Technical approach

**Instances (frozen and hashed before the run).** 240 template instances of the four-bar-from-two-dyads
problem: a moving body with three prescribed planar poses (rational rotations and translations); a catalog of
moving-lug positions on the body; convex keep-in polygons with rational vertices for fixed pivots on the frame
and for moving pivots on the body; a reach target; a clearance margin; a dimensional tolerance (±0.2 mm on each
length, TARGET). Strata: 80 legs (a foot path through three poses), 80 ankles, 80 steering linkages; within
each, a quarter are constructed so that the obvious solution has a circuit or branch defect (planted), the rest
are drawn by address-seeded sampling.

**Arm A, exact (outside the product).** Per instance: (1) for each catalogued moving lug with positions
P₁, P₂, P₃ in the three poses, the fixed pivot O from the rational 2×2 system
2·(Pᵢ − P₁)·O = ‖Pᵢ‖² − ‖P₁‖², i = 2, 3, degenerate cases split by determinant rank; keep dyads whose O lies in
the keep-in polygon; (2) pair dyads into four-bars; (3) for each four-bar attempt the circuit certificate by
script: interval enclosure of the loop-closure Jacobian determinant along the driving interval and over the
tolerance box, sign constant and non-zero, poses in order, clearance and reach enclosed. The symbolic sibling's
command-line solver is used only for the instances whose template is beyond dyads (none in this pilot), and the
cap census below is computed for all.

**Arm B, numerical comparator.** Multi-start numerical optimization of the same dimensions (20 starts per
instance), minimizing pose error, with the same circuit and clearance checks applied to its results.

**Referee check, independent of both arms.** Every linkage that arm A certifies is built as a closed chain and
driven through its three poses in order on the CPU referee. Outcomes: `moves` (all poses reached in order, same
assembly mode throughout), `binds` (the drive stalls or constraint forces exceed a declared bound), `changes
circuit` (it ends in the other assembly mode or needs disassembly).

**Cap census.** For each instance and for its five-pose and four-bar path extensions, record variables,
equations, estimated terms and degree against the caps, and whether the system is zero-dimensional.

**Falsifier (fixed before the run).** A certified linkage binds or changes circuit on the referee (one is
enough); or more than a quarter of the template instances fall outside the caps (more than 60 of 240).

**Record (pilot register, N38a-09; the row is N25d-40's row 20).** The one record `[[pilot]]` `PILOT-C-34`, the
identifier that row C-34 of the construction register names in its `pilot` field (SPEC-C-34), in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`: `mechanism` bounded exact linkage
synthesis (lane 1 of C-34); `tier = "F"`; `owner = "§25.21.3"`; `bead = "N25d-16"`; `work_package = "WP-67"`;
`protocol = { path = "docs/pilots/PILOT-C-34.md", blake3, frozen, ledger_seq }`; `falsifier` carrying both
statements, the chapter's (a certified linkage binds or changes circuit on the referee, or more than 60 of 240
instances outside the caps) and the construction's (one planted defect certified as valid, which the
planted-defect quarter tests), with the difference between them noted (24 templates in the construction against
240 instances here; the 240 are run, and the 24-template statement is met because every planted defect must be
refused); `ceiling = { statement = "the certificate, not speed", value = "none on speed", basis = "three-position
synthesis has closed-form circumcentres" }`; `fallback = { ships = "multi-start numerical optimization with the
same circuit and clearance check, classed Tested", kill_gate = "KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE" }`; `cost` (TARGET: two
engineer-days, CPU minutes plus 240 short referee runs; `envelope = "default"`); `tool` (the scripts and the
symbolic sibling's command-line solver with their pins, `site = "host"`); `status = "registered"`;
`integrated_gate = "GATE-AUGUR-17"`. Before the run this bead writes the protocol (the instance generator and
seed, the strata, the planted quarter, both arms, the referee rig with its stall criterion and force bound, the

appended as one `[[pilot.result]]` under the registered hash (verdict `falsifier-rejected` or

hash is what N25d-19 re-uses.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Instances, catalog, tolerances and protocol are hashed and committed before the run.
- [ ] Every planted circuit or branch defect is rejected by arm A's certificate (a planted defect certified as
      valid is itself a falsifying event and is recorded).
- [ ] Every certified linkage has a referee outcome; the count of `binds` and `changes circuit` is reported
      (must be 0 to pass).
- [ ] The cap census reports the share of instances outside the caps (must be at most 25% to pass).
- [ ] The falsifier's verdict is stated in one sentence and recorded in the pilot register and the review log.
- [ ] The protocol is hashed and `PILOT-C-34` is `registered` with that hash in `pilots.toml` before the run,
      with both falsifier statements; the register's load checks accept it; the outcome is an appended
      `[[pilot.result]]`, and row C-34's `pilot` check (SPEC-C-34) then reads it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot script's checks: `circumcentre_known` (P = (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 2) give O = (1, 1), radius² = 2;
P = (1, 0), (0, 1), (−1, 0) give O = (0, 0), radius 1); `collinear_degenerate` ((0, 0), (1, 0), (2, 0) has
determinant 0); `crank_rocker_two_circuits` (ground 4, crank 1, coupler 4, rocker 3: B = (11/3, ±√80/3) at
crank angle 0 and (11/5, ±12/5) at π; the determinant (B − A) × (O₄ − A) is −√80 and −12 on the upper circuit,
+√80 and +12 on the lower); `falsifier_counts` (60 of 240 outside the caps does not hold the falsifier, 61
does; one `changes circuit` among the certified holds it); `instances_reproducible` (the generator with the
logged seed rebuilds the 240 instances and their hash; the planted quarter is 20 of each stratum's 80, 60 in
all).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_linkage_240.sh` first asserts that `PILOT-C-34` is registered and that the protocol file
hashes to its registered digest, then runs both arms and the referee checks: `frob design check <rig> --json`
for mobility (a read verb) and `frob sim run <rig> --scene <pose drive> --tier referee
--key pl-ref-<i>-<c>-<run> --json` per certified linkage (one key per instance and candidate), and appends the
`[[pilot.result]]`. Through the STD-E2E helper it writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_linkage_240/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25d-16`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, key, exit
code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block. Per instance: stratum, planted flag, arm A's result and
certificate summary, arm B's best result, the referee outcome with its replay address, cap figures; `metric`
lines for wrong certificates (threshold 0), planted defects certified (threshold 0) and the outside-caps share
(threshold 0.25); on failure the first offending instance with the artifacts to reproduce it; a `summary` with
the verdict and the kill gate touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-653 (dyad: pivot (1, 1), radius² = 2), KA-654 (crank-rocker circuits); CX-280 (a root of the synthesis
equations is not a mechanism). Control of the pilot: the planted-defect quarter; a certificate script that
checks only the pose equations must certify a planted defect and thereby show the control bites.

## Considerations

- The referee's closed-chain support (the closure constructor and mobility analysis) must exist; a planar
  four-bar has mobility 1 and is the smallest case.
- "Outside the caps" is counted on the systems the lane would actually submit (five-pose and template
  extensions), since three-pose dyads themselves need no polynomial solver.
- A negative result ships the numerical lane, classed `Tested`.
