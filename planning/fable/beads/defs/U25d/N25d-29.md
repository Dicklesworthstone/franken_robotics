## Background

The second slice of Augur's delivery order is the one the plan singles out as the place a ten-fold opportunity
could first appear. Change one measured surface law in a warehouse with multiple routes, body variants and
sensor mounts. Compile the exact dependency support of each accepted result. Prove that an edit disjoint from a
result's support preserves it, or recheck the precise obligation where the changed interval still lies within
slack. A conventional dependency graph, an incremental optimizer and a hot-started simulator are eligible
comparators, and their fastest correct implementation sets the bar.

What is already known constrains the claim. Exact reuse of rollouts after a mass or geometry edit was refuted
as a lever at plan time: at most about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on our own memo graph). On a
toy four-state fixture a correctly keyed dependency graph tied a semantic receipt: both did 3 pair evaluations
and 2 reductions and matched fresh replay, and the variant that omitted one dependency inherited a stale
promotion. So no reuse advantage may be assumed. What remains open is whether a physical non-dependence or
sensitivity certificate ("this verdict used only these parameters, with this much slack") can be obtained
cheaply enough to matter when an edit touches something measured, like a floor's friction law. That is the
question of this experiment, and it is asked against the strongest conventional answers.

Dependency support: the physical parameters, metric intervals, stages, scene cells, clocks, cards and model
versions a certificate actually used, including implicit ones (a common noise model, the gravity frame, a
torque derating). An edit disjoint from the support preserves the certificate; an edit inside it triggers
either the precise obligation whose slack the changed interval must still fit, or a rerun.

## Scope

In: the fixture; the edit set; four arms; correctness accounting; cost accounting with the ceiling first; the
record for the slice register and for the kill gate of reuse by implication. Out: the revalidation engine
(BD-292); the frozen 30-amendment campaign that measures GATE-AUGUR-12 (the revalidation bead's own gate).

## Technical approach

**Fixture (frozen before any arm runs).** The warehouse archetype with three routes between a rack and a
counter that share one doorway, floor patches with measured friction intervals from interface cards, four body
variants of one wheeled carrier (two wheel radii by two payload placements) and two sensor mounts. Baseline
results to protect: per (variant, route) a traction-margin verdict for each segment, a route-feasibility
verdict from the clearance analysis, a deadline verdict, and a ranking of variants by a paired comparison on
the batched tier. Each accepted result is stored with its support.

**Edits (hashed before the run; none seen by an arm in advance).** Twelve single edits of one surface law
each: the friction interval of one floor patch widened downward (for example [0.55, 0.70] to [0.45, 0.70]),
narrowed, or shifted; four of them touch a patch on no route in use (disjoint from every support); four change
an interval inside a support while staying inside its slack; four exceed the slack and must flip or reopen a
verdict. Two decoys change a visual property of a patch with no mechanical effect.

**Arms.**
- A: `mission.revalidate` (N25a-16) with dependency support and slack obligations.
- B: a conventional dependency graph keyed on full inputs (the memo graph with shadow recompute).
- C: an incremental optimizer or solver with sensitivity information, written by a different engineer: the
  incremental incumbent of N25ap2-01 (field-keyed graph, demand-driven recomputation, LP ranging), extended to
  the warehouse's lowered computations by a session that implements neither arm A nor this fixture, and run
  through the incumbent harness of BD-039.
- D: a hot-started simulator that re-runs affected rollouts from cached states.
All arms get the same eligibility guard, the same caches warmed on the baseline, and the same hardware.

**Correctness (decides first).** Every decision of every arm after every edit is compared with an independent
full recomputation from a clean store. Recorded per arm: incorrectly inherited verdicts (unsafe reuse counts as
a false accept even if a later change would have restored feasibility), unnecessary invalidations, and
verdicts left unresolved. An arm with one incorrectly inherited verdict is out of the cost comparison.

**Cost (only among correct arms).** Before timing: the ceiling. On the fastest correct conventional arm,
measure the fraction f of critical-path time that reuse could remove (the dirty fraction of the campaign), the
factor r by which arm A could at best shrink it, and the overhead h of compiling and checking supports; the
bound is 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h). The comparison is registered with that ceiling; if the ceiling is below the
ratio the experiment would claim, the timing is not run and the result is recorded as "no reuse advantage
available on this fixture". Otherwise: paired times per edit from edit to revalidated answer, checker time
included, reported as a lower confidence bound on the paired ratio (best conventional arm over arm A).

**Outcome for the slice.** `evidence-met` requires arm A correct on all edits and a paired lower bound above 1
against the best correct comparator; anything else is recorded under its own name (tie, slower, incorrect).
"Tie" is the expected outcome and a complete result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fixture, baseline results with supports, edits and the experiment's six parts are committed before any
      arm sees an edit.
- [ ] Correctness tables per arm: incorrectly inherited verdicts, unnecessary invalidations, unresolved.
      Arm A's incorrectly inherited count is reported to the kill gate of reuse by implication (one such
      verdict from an implication rule removes that rule).
- [ ] The ceiling f, r, h is measured and recorded before any paired timing; a refused timing is recorded as
      such.
- [ ] Among correct arms, the paired lower bounds are reported with the disclosure block.
- [ ] The four disjoint edits preserve every result in arm A with zero recomputation; the four over-slack edits
      flip or reopen exactly the results the full recomputation flips.
- [ ] Controls: a variant of arm A with one dependency omitted from a support (the patch's friction dropped
      from a traction verdict's support) inherits a stale verdict on an over-slack edit and is caught; the two
      decoys invalidate nothing in arm A; an A/A timing does not clear 1.02.
- [ ] The slice record is written; the experiment's six parts are in the method-experiment register (N38a-07)
      before any arm sees an edit; its outcome is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` to the record of revalidation
      by implication in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/augur_surface_law.rs`: `edit_classes_balanced` (4 disjoint, 4 in-slack, 4
over-slack, 2 decoys); `support_lists_friction_patch` (every traction verdict's support names its patches);
`slack_obligation_arithmetic` (a margin proved for μ ≥ 0.50 survives a lower end of 0.52 and not of 0.45);
`ceiling_formula` (f = 0.10, r = ∞, h = 0 gives 1.11: the plan-time expectation for exact reuse).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_surface_law_reuse.sh` runs each arm on each edit (`frob mission revalidate`,
`frob result revalidate`, the comparator commands; every run-verb call with its own key
`sl-<run>-<arm>-<edit>`, so that no arm or repeat is answered from another call's receipt) and the full
recomputation. It sources the helper of STD-E2E and logs to `target/e2e-logs/augur_surface_law_reuse/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript. Log per (arm, edit): exact command lines, exit codes, the envelope's evidence block,
wall time by stage, verdicts kept, withdrawn, rechecked, recomputed; agreement with the full recomputation and
the first differing verdict if any; `metric` lines for false inheritances (threshold 0), unnecessary
invalidations, f, r, h, the ceiling and the paired bounds; a `summary` with the slice status and the kill gate
touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-287 (a memo keyed by a semantic receipt beats a dependency graph, or may omit an input: both tie; omitting
one dependency inherits a stale promotion). The omitted-dependency control, the decoys and the A/A timing.
Determinism: every arm's decisions repeat exactly twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The comparators must be the fastest correct implementations available; a weak comparator measures the
  comparator. The comparator engineer does not see arm A's supports.
- If uncertainty in the measured law dominates (most over-slack edits end unresolved in every arm), the slice's
  pre-declared decision applies: spend the next increment on measurement, or settle for a narrower envelope.
- This is a development experiment; any confirmatory claim would run in the qualification lane.
