## Background

Slice 4 of Augur's delivery order is a measurement, taken before any specialized GPU solver for design
families is written. The slice: time source-to-verdict amendments on heterogeneous bodies with sensing,
controller updates, contact and independent checking. The evidence that buys the next investment: the matched
rival spends enough time on repeatable work for ten-fold to remain possible; a breakdown by stage locates the
saving; both native backends agree on accepted outcomes for the tested profile. The decision if it fails: stop
specialized GPU work in cells where transfers, authoring, training, checker work or baseline reuse rule out the
target. The M4 milestone repeats that sentence as its own stop decision.

The arithmetic behind it. If a fraction f of matched wall time is improvable by a factor r and the new method
adds relative overhead h, the speed-up is at most 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h) under a serial cost model. Infinite
acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 6.7; with f = 0.97, r = 40 and h = 0.02 the ceiling is about
13.5 (illustrative values). Two rules follow: cheap GPU kernels on already-pruned cells are unlikely to decide
anything, and a pruning ratio is never multiplied by a GPU speed-up measured on work the pruning already
removed. The kill rule: if the cost that cannot be removed already exceeds one tenth of the strongest
baseline's time, a ten-fold improvement requires changing another stage or changing the claim.

A device fact fixes where family screens run (FACT, plan-time pilot on a 65,536-row screen, re-run at M0):
dispatch and transfer dominated a small host-resident job, and the host integer path was fastest at that size.
A family screen is placed where its data lives; that is measured here, not assumed.

## Scope

In: the amendment workload; the stage ledger for our pipeline and for the matched rival; agreement of accepted
outcomes across CUDA and Metal; the per-cell ceiling and stop decision; the record. Out: the ceiling check
itself as a mechanism on every registered comparison (BD-354, GATE-AUGUR-14, to which this bead supplies
measured f, r and h); family execution on devices (BD-355); the tournament.

## Technical approach

**Workload (frozen before timing).** Twelve amendments drawn from the families the chapter has delivered by
M4, on heterogeneous bodies (two wheeled, two legged, one hybrid variant per family batch): a changed actuator
rating; a changed read error or packet delivery tick; a changed floor friction interval; a changed payload
envelope; a changed controller gain set (a controller update); a body edit that changes one module. Each
amendment starts from a qualified answer and ends at an independently checked amended answer.

**Cells.** A cell is (mission family, body class, backend). Backends: `batch-cpu`, CUDA on the two RTX 4090,
Metal on the M5 Ultra. Each native device completes the closed loop on its own before any hybrid profile may
claim acceleration.

**Stages timed, summing to wall-clock (the phase ledger).** Authoring of the amendment (agent or script time,
recorded separately); admission and lowering; dependency revalidation; proposing on the CPU; proposing on a
device (launch, kernel, transfers in and out, each separate); controller synthesis or training triggered by
the amendment; simulation campaigns the query chose; certificate writing; checker time in the offline auditor;
serialization and ledger writes; queueing. The same stages are recorded for the matched rival, driven through
its own tools on the same hardware, with its caches (graphs, factors, compiled kernels) warm: a fair
comparator also caches.

**Per cell, computed before any speed claim.**
1. From the rival's ledger: the share of its time that is repeatable work our pipeline could remove (f), and
   the residual it shares with us (authoring, checking, serialization, review).
2. The ceiling 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h) with r from our measured stage times and h from our added overhead.
3. The one-tenth rule: if our unremovable cost (transfers, authoring, training, checker work) exceeds one tenth
   of the rival's time, mark the cell `ten-fold-excluded`.
4. Where queues overlap, the serial model does not apply: report the actual critical path and the throughput
   of accepted outcomes instead.

**Agreement.** For every amendment, the accepted outcomes (inner, excluded and unresolved labels of the
amended answer) are identical on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal under the exact-integer or guarded kernel class;
a difference is a defect, recorded and excluded from timing.

**Stop decision (written per cell, read by the M4 exit review).** `continue` (the ceiling is at or above the
target for that class and the saving is located in a named stage), `stop specialized GPU work` (the one-tenth
rule fired, or the ceiling is below the claimed ratio, or the rival's reuse already removes the saving), or
`claim changed` (the cell is kept with a claim other than speed, for example auditability). The decision is an
ADR per cell group.

**Record.** `frob.phase-ledger/1`-style tables for both pipelines per amendment and cell; f, r, h, ceiling and
decision per cell; fed to the ceiling-before-speed registry.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The workload and the stage list are frozen before timing; each ledger sums to wall-clock within 1%.
- [ ] The rival's ledger exists for every amendment, warm, on the same hardware, with its versions pinned.
- [ ] Accepted outcomes agree across the three backends for every amendment, or the defects are listed.
- [ ] Every cell has f, r, h, a ceiling and a written decision; no speed ratio is quoted for a cell whose
      ceiling is below it (the comparison is refused, which is GATE-AUGUR-14's behaviour).
- [ ] The M4 exit review has the ADRs; specialized GPU work is stopped in every cell marked so.
- [ ] Controls: an A/A campaign (our pipeline against itself) does not clear 1.02; a planted stage that sleeps
      for 100 ms appears in the ledger under its own name with 100 ms; a cell with f = 0.85 is refused a claim
      of 10 (ceiling 6.7).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/augur_slice4.rs`: `ceiling_values` (f = 0.85, r = ∞, h = 0 → 6.67; f = 0.97,
r = 40, h = 0.02 → 13.47); `one_tenth_rule` (unremovable 12 s against a rival's 100 s marks the cell);
`ledger_sums_to_wall`; `decision_is_function_of_record`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_slice4_timing.sh` runs each amendment on each backend through `frob mission revalidate` and
`frob mission synthesize --backend …`, and the rival's script through the incumbent harness. Log: per stage
the wall time with its unit, device receipts (requested and actual backend per stage), transfer bytes,
checker seconds, outcome digests per backend; `metric` lines for f, r, h, the ceiling and the one-tenth test
with their thresholds; the decision per cell; the disclosure block (hardware fingerprint, load, versions, cold
and warm separated); a `summary` naming GATE-AUGUR-14.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-285 (run the speed test, then interpret it: the pusher residual of 575.7 µs bounded a zero-cost solver at
about 1.46) and CX-284 (strict predicates in unguarded single precision) as the reasons for the two rules.
Controls as above. Determinism lanes: outcome digests twice on one machine and across backends under their
class.

## Considerations

- Compute: the workload runs on both machines; it names its budget and needs the owner's approval if it
  exceeds the default envelope.
- Authoring time by an agent is recorded and not optimized here; it is often the largest residual.
- A stop in a cell does not stop the CPU path there: the family answer still ships, without a specialized
  device solver.
