## Background

Augur answers a question about a set of designs with a pair of sets: an inner set I of designs with a checked
reason why each works, and an outer set O outside which each design has a checked reason why it cannot work,
with I ⊆ F ⊆ O for the true feasible set F, and unresolved cells in O ∖ I. Eleven invariants say what must
always hold of such answers, of mission identities, of policies, of certificate bundles and of the constructive
lanes. They are the properties an optimizer, a cache, a learned proposer or a GPU kernel must not be able to
change while answering.

Each invariant needs a property test and at least one mutant that the test catches (a gate that nothing can
fail validates nothing). The mechanisms that keep the invariants live in several beads; this suite states them
in one place and tests them from the outside, on fixtures small enough that F is known exhaustively.

## Scope

In: one property test per invariant, its fixture family and its mutant; the script that runs the suite through
the public verbs. Out: the mechanisms (the mission core, admission, bundles, verbs); the proof obligations
(N25d-34 to N25d-36); the checker's mutation corpus.

## Technical approach

Tests live in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/augur_invariants.rs`. Exhaustively solvable fixtures give ground
truth: the pusher (y = max(0, g·u − d) on a rational grid of gains, commands and gaps: per-build command set
[1/2, 2], shared set empty), the docking grid (561 cells: 344 admitted, 217 excluded, 9 on the boundary), and
the stance grid (u, v ∈ {2, 3, 4}, ratings in {0, 80, 160, 240}⁴, four vertices and centroid: 2,254 of 11,520
states feasible, 114 of 2,304 bodies cover the mission).

| Invariant | Statement | Property test | Mutant that must be caught |
|---|---|---|---|
| INV-AUGUR-01 | I ⊆ F ⊆ O under the declared model: every inner cell has an inclusion proof for the query's exact prefix, and every cell outside O has an exclusion proof for it | On the three fixtures compute F by enumeration; every grid point in an inner cell is in F; every point of F is in no excluded cell; each proof's recorded prefix equals the query's | An answer formed by intersecting per-world answers (the clairvoyant order): its inner set exceeds F on the pusher |
| INV-AUGUR-02 | The inner, excluded and unresolved cells of an answer partition the declared domain, with disjoint interiors and stated boundary ownership | Exact box arithmetic over every answer produced in the suite: the union equals the domain; pairwise interiors are disjoint; every boundary point is owned by exactly one cell | A cell list with the boundary v = 14/3 of the stance family closed on both sides |
| INV-AUGUR-03 | `MissionId` changes whenever a quantifier, comparator, world support, persistence declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon changes | For each of the seven fields, a random edit changes the identity; re-serializing with reordered keys or whitespace does not | A canonical form that omits `world.persistence` from the hashed bytes |
| INV-AUGUR-04 | Answers are monotone in work: a later answer to the same `MissionId` moves cells only out of the unresolved set. A cell leaves I or re-enters O only through a logged withdrawal that names its counterexample | Run each fixture at budgets b₁ < b₂ < b₃: point sets satisfy inner(b₁) ⊆ inner(b₂) and excluded(b₁) ⊆ excluded(b₂); any shrink must cite a withdrawal record | A search that re-opens a decided cell silently when it re-splits |
| INV-AUGUR-05 | The class of an answer is the weakest class among its cells, and no operation raises a class | Random multisets of cell classes: the answer's class is their meet; an API scan finds no public function from a lower to a higher class | An answer of one `Tested` and three `Certified` cells labelled `Certified` |
| INV-AUGUR-06 | No numerical failure, timeout or failed search is recorded as an exclusion | Inject non-convergence, a NaN, a timeout and a zero-budget search into each lowering: the affected cells are unresolved | A lowering that maps "solver reported infeasible without a dual" to `Excluded` |
| INV-AUGUR-07 | A policy is never indexed by a quantity absent from the observation contract at the decision instant | Random policy tables against random observation contracts with arrival instants: a table that reads a field before its arrival is rejected by admission and by the checker | The docking witness that uses the velocity packet in the first command |
| INV-AUGUR-08 | A `.frobcert` family payload is self-contained: checking needs no network, no GPU, no simulator and no producer | Run the standalone checker on every bundle of the suite in an empty environment under a system-call trace: no socket, no child process, no file outside the bundle | A bundle whose primitive equations are a reference to the producer's store |
| INV-AUGUR-09 | Every exported answer, witness and bundle carries the `authorization` object with its three flags, all `false` | Every export path of the suite's answers and bundles is scanned for the object; a missing object or a `true` flag is rejected by the checker | A bundle with `execution_authorized: true`; a witness exported without the object |
| INV-AUGUR-10 | Scheduling heuristics (opportunity loss, learned proposers, agent guesses) can change the order and cost of work and cannot change any verdict | Run each fixture under 50 random cell orders, proposer seeds and scheduling scores, to completion: the final cell verdicts are identical as point sets; at any common budget no point has conflicting verdicts across schedules | A scheduler that marks low-priority cells excluded when the budget ends |
| INV-AUGUR-11 | A candidate from `frob-synth` has no verdict until `frob-mission` assigns one through a proof rule | Every candidate in every synthesis answer has a null verdict or names a checked cell. Here the row stays `pending`: the test and this mutant belong to `frob-synth`'s own bead N25d-06 (M4), which carries INV-AUGUR-11 and asserts it on the co-design and linkage fixtures (with N25d-10's script); this M1 suite does not wait on that M4 bead and reads no result from it | A dispatch that copies a candidate into `inner` (registered by N25d-06) |

**Method notes.**
- Comparing answers as point sets (on the fixture's rational grid) makes the tests independent of how cells
  are split.
- For INV-AUGUR-04 the withdrawal path is exercised with the passive-spring amendment of the stance family
  through `result.revalidate` and the re-solve (N25a-13, a dependency, with N25c-11's passive lowering behind
  it): the cell at half-width 1/4 m leaves I with a record naming (175, 115, 85, 25) N against the 160 N
  rating.
- For INV-AUGUR-03 the seven edits are: reorder two binders; `<` to `≤`; add a world vertex; flip a persistence
  declaration; move an arrival instant; change what indexes the policy; change the horizon from static to one
  tick.
- Mutants are built as test-only variants (a feature flag in the test harness or a doctored artifact), never as
  code paths reachable in a release build.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Ten property tests pass at M1; the eleventh row is present, reports `pending` and names N25d-06 (M4),
      where INV-AUGUR-11 is asserted (convention: no bead waits on what waits on it).
- [ ] Each of the ten invariants' mutants is caught by its test; those mutants are registered as negative
      controls.
- [ ] The identifiers INV-AUGUR-01 to -11 appear in the test names and in `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-mission` with
      their statements.
- [ ] The suite runs through the public verbs (not private functions) wherever the invariant is about answers
      or bundles.
- [ ] The system-call trace of INV-AUGUR-08 is recorded as an artifact.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The eleven tests named `inv_augur_01_inner_subset_f_subset_outer` … `inv_augur_11_candidate_has_no_verdict`,
as specified in the table (the eleventh a `pending` row naming N25d-06), each with its fixture values: pusher sets [1/2, 2] and ∅; docking counts 344, 217,
9; stance counts 2,254 and 114; stance boundary 14/3 with `closed: [true, true]` on the inner cell and
`[false, true]` on the excluded one.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_invariants.sh`: produces the answers and bundles with `frob mission synthesize`,
`frob mission refute`, `frob prove bundle`, every call with its own key `--key inv-<nn>-<run>` (nn the
invariant, run the budget, schedule or repetition), so that runs the tests compare are never served each
other's stored receipt (INV-AUGUR-04's budgets b₁ < b₂ < b₃ and INV-AUGUR-10's 50 schedules are distinct runs),
runs the standalone checker under the trace, then runs the test binary against the produced artifacts. It sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_invariants/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence`
block. Per invariant: its fixtures, the counts compared with their expected values, the mutant's outcome;
identities and digests of every answer; on failure the first violating point or field and the artifact paths
to reproduce; a `summary` with eleven rows (passed, pending or failed).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-624, KA-625, KA-628, KA-637; CX-272, CX-275. The eleven mutants. Metamorphic relations used: relabelling
ports consistently in query and answer leaves verdicts unchanged; splitting a cell in two leaves the point
sets unchanged. Determinism lanes: the suite's answers twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug and
release, with identical digests (exact fragments).

## Considerations

- These are tests of properties, not proofs; the corresponding general statements are the proof obligations
  and candidate theorem cards of the chapter.
- INV-AUGUR-10's test needs runs to completion; on fixtures with unresolved remainders it compares only that
  no point is decided two ways.
- The suite is re-run at every exit review that touches Augur.
