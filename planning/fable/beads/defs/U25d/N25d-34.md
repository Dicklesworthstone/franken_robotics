## Background

A mission answer is checked by an independent program, `frob-verify`, which recomputes every witness from the
primitive equations that travel in the certificate bundle. What the checker cannot check is whether those
equations are the right translation of the robot and the mission. A proof about the wrong translation of a
contact law is still a physical error. That gap is the proof obligation PO-AUGUR-01:

> Each lowering from a design family and a mission to primitive equations is correct. Verification: a second,
> independently written lowering for every supported template, compared exactly; one deliberately invalid
> fixture per lowering that the checker must reject.

A lowering is the code that turns a query (a design family, a world family, an observation channel, a policy
class, predicates, quantifiers) into small subproblems: a static linear program, a finite observation game, an
interval constraint. The obligation is mitigated, not solved: two independent lowerings that agree exactly on
an exhaustive fixture make a shared mistranslation unlikely, and the equations stay in the bundle for a human
to read. Every plan-time pilot already has the needed pair, because its conventional comparator is a second
lowering.

## Scope

In: the register of supported templates with their two lowerings; the independence rule; the exact comparison
harness; one invalid fixture per lowering; the rule for templates that lack a pair. Out: writing the lowerings
themselves (they belong to the template beads: the stance pair to BD-130 and N25c-10, the docking pair to
BD-129 and N25c-20, the pusher pair to BD-131; each of those beads runs its own pair, and this bead keeps the
register and the one harness that runs them all); checker arithmetic (N25d-35); soundness of the proof rules
(N25d-36).

## Technical approach

**Register** (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/lowerings.toml`, DESIGN): one record per template: `template`,
`primary` (module path), `second` (module path), `authors` (sessions; must differ), `comparison` (the fixture
and what is compared), `invalid_fixtures`, `status`.

**Templates supported at M1 and their pairs.**

| Template | Primary lowering | Second lowering | Exhaustive comparison | Exact agreement required on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Four-point stance (static payload envelope) | Lifted robust linear program: one force vector per mission vertex with shared geometry; for the fixture 22 variables, 12 equalities, 16 inequalities | Projected form: the wrench zonotope Σ [0, cᵢ]·gᵢ with generators gᵢ = (1, qᵢ); for each pair of generators with non-zero cross product n = g_j × g_k and each load vertex w the support inequalities Σᵢ min(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ) ≤ n·w ≤ Σᵢ max(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ); for the fixture 6 variables and 32 distinct inequalities | The validation grid: u, v ∈ {2, 3, 4} per metre, ratings in {0, 80, 160, 240}⁴ N, four mission vertices and their centroid: 11,520 body–load states | Feasibility of each state (2,254 feasible); bodies covering the mission (114); the boundary 3/14 m of the continuous family |
| Docking (three ticks, one velocity packet, hidden gain) | Finite observation game over causal histories with a policy table | Scenario-tree linear program from the raw state transition: two linear-program extrema for the first command and one common-command feasibility program per actuator bound (51 solves) | The 561-cell grid (17 actuator bounds at spacing 1/32, 33 error levels at spacing 1/64), on-time and late packet | Admitted (344), excluded (217), boundary (9) cells; all 561 late-packet cells excluded |
| Pusher (y = max(0, g·u − d)) | Specialized interval solver for per-build and shared command sets | Composed solver on a general elimination route | Ten frozen cases, twenty intervals | Every interval end and its open or closed status (for example `shared-tight`: {1}; `both-ends`: [2/3, 5/4]; `branch-crossing`: shared [0, 3/8]) |

**Independence rule.** The two lowerings of a template share no source file beyond the query parser and the
exact rational type, are written in different sessions, and the second is written from the mission's statement
and not from the first's code. A build-time check lists the files each uses and fails on overlap outside the
allowed set.

**Comparison harness.** For each template: run both lowerings on the exhaustive fixture; compare verdicts
point by point and boundaries as exact rationals; report the first disagreement with both lowered systems
printed. Any disagreement blocks `Certified` answers for that template until resolved, and the disagreeing
case becomes a permanent fixture.

**Invalid fixtures (one per lowering, six at M1).** Each is a deliberately wrong lowering output, with
consistent hashes, that the checker must reject, plus the reason it must:
1. Stance, lifted: feet exchanged in the port order with forces unchanged (Σ x·f becomes (270 − 130)/3 = 140/3,
   not 60).
2. Stance, projected: the first moment (60, 30) passed where the torque (30, −60, 0) N·m is expected.
3. Docking, game: a policy table whose first command depends on the velocity packet (information before
   arrival).
4. Docking, scenario tree: the position coefficient of the first command written as 2 where exact zero-order
   hold gives 5/2 (x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2)); the exported witness then misses the target under exact
   replay.
5. Pusher, specialized: the saturation branch dropped (y = g·u − d), which admits commands that give y < 0
   where the plant gives 0.
6. Pusher, composed: the per-build witness offered for the shared-command query (the wrong quantifier order).

**Templates without a pair.** A template is listed as supported only when its second lowering, its exhaustive
comparison and its invalid fixtures are registered. Otherwise its answers carry the assumption
`lowering: single` and are not cited under the Proof claim; later templates (region certificates, product
systems, labels, composition) each bring their pair when they land, and this bead's harness is what they
register with.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The register lists the three M1 templates with two lowerings each and different authors.
- [ ] Exact agreement: 11,520 of 11,520 stance states; 561 of 561 docking cells for both packet timings; 20 of
      20 pusher intervals with identical ends and closure.
- [ ] Each of the six invalid fixtures is rejected by `frob-verify`, with the first failing rule named.
- [ ] The file-set independence check passes and fails on a planted shared helper.
- [ ] A template with no second lowering cannot produce an answer cited as `Certified` under the Proof claim
      (test with a stub template entry).
- [ ] A planted disagreement (the second stance lowering with one generator sign flipped) is found by the
      harness, which prints the first disagreeing state.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/augur_lowerings.rs`: `stance_two_lowerings_agree` (2,254 feasible; 114 bodies;
the first weak-screen counterexample u = v = 2 with ratings (80, 80, 80, 160) N at vertex (60, 30), where t
would need to be at least 180 N and at most 80 N, is infeasible in both); `stance_continuous_boundary` (3/14
from both; 165 N needed at b = 1/5 m); `docking_two_lowerings_agree`; `pusher_two_lowerings_agree`;
`invalid_fixture_<n>_rejected` for n = 1 … 6; `independence_file_sets`; `single_lowering_not_proof`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_lowerings.sh` runs the comparisons through the verbs where a public switch exists
(`frob mission synthesize --lowering primary|second`, a DESIGN test option) and the checker on the invalid
fixtures. Log at `target/e2e-logs/augur_lowerings/<utc>.ndjson` through the `STD-E2E` helper (`ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step`, `event` on every line): every command with exit code and wall time; per template the register
record (both module paths and both authors), the counts compared against the required ones (11,520; 561 twice;
20), the first disagreement if any with both lowered systems' paths; per invalid fixture the checker's verdict,
its first failing rule and the bundle digest; the file sets and their intersection; the planted disagreement and
the state the harness printed; a `summary` with agreements, rejections and the independence result.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-624, KA-625, KA-629, KA-637, KA-657 (51 linear programs agree with the exact docking answer), KA-658
(11,520 of 11,520 states agree between two exact classifiers; 102 of 102 spot checks). CX-274 (the
support-polygon-plus-capacity screen accepts 1,596 bodies the exact model rejects): a third "lowering" that
must disagree. The six invalid fixtures and the planted disagreement. Determinism: exact arithmetic; identical
results twice, across worker counts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- Agreement of two lowerings is evidence, not proof; who reviews the primitive equations in a bundle, and
  whether that review is recorded, is an open question with a fail-closed default (unreviewed bundles say so).
- The second lowering is often the conventional formulation and may be the faster one; then it becomes the
  primary and the specialized one the cross-check.
