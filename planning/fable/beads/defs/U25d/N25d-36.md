## Background

Four proof rules are the only ways a cell of a mission answer changes status (a cell is a box of designs; its
status is inner, excluded or unresolved). Each rule is implemented as a proposer in `frob-mission` and as a
checking rule in the independent auditor. The auditor checks instances: this dual vector separates, this
policy table wins. Whether each rule is sound in general, that is, whether an accepted instance really implies
the claimed verdict about the feasible set F = { d : ∃ policy ∀ world ∀ execution, the mission holds }, is the
proof obligation PO-AUGUR-06:

> Each proof rule is sound on its finite and polyhedral fragments. Verification: stated as candidate theorem
> cards for the proof subsystem; until proved, tested against the mutation corpus and classed by the weakest
> step.

A theorem card is a precisely stated claim with a proof route (a polynomial identity, an exact enumeration, a
Lean proof) and a tier. This bead writes the candidate cards, pairs each with the deliberately invalid fixture
that the checker must reject, and fixes how answers are classed while the cards are unproved.

## Scope

In: four candidate statements on the fragments where they can be proved or exhaustively tested; their
hypotheses as checkable side conditions; one invalid fixture per rule; property tests on finite models; the
classing rule. Out: proving the cards in Lean (the proof subsystem at M5); the checkers; the monotone-transport
harness; the mutation corpus itself (this bead adds four fixtures to it).

## Technical approach

Document `docs/math/augur-proof-rules.md` plus tests in `crates/frob-mission/tests/proof_rules.rs`. Notation:
D the design domain, W the world family, Π_Y the causal policies on the observation history, T(d, π, w) the
set of admitted executions, A the acceptance predicate.

**Candidate card R1: necessary-condition exclusion.** Let E ⊆ D be a cell. Suppose a relaxed system
S_rel(d) is given together with recorded set inclusions showing that for every d ∈ E, every policy and every
world, each admitted execution's forces, torques, energies and observations lie in the relaxed sets (the true
force set inside the outer cone, the true torque set under the envelope, every admissible energy source
included). If S_rel(d) is infeasible for every d ∈ E, witnessed by a dual vector valid over E (constant, or
rational in the cell parameters with a sign proof), then E ∩ F = ∅. Fragment: linear relaxations with box
bounds; instance form is Farkas' lemma (if Mᵀy ≥ 0 and rᵀy < 0 then no x ≥ 0 has M·x = r).
*Invalid fixture*: a quasi-static work cut that omits approach kinetic energy: lifting 8 kg over 12 mm needs
8 × 9.81 × 0.012 = 0.94176 J against a 0.90 J budget and "excludes" the crossing; 0.05 J of approach energy,
absent from the budget, removes the exclusion. The checker rejects it because a declared energy source has no
term in the relaxation. Second: infeasible sample points offered as an exclusion of a cell that straddles the
boundary.

**Candidate card R2: constructive inclusion.** (a) Finite games: if a policy table is a function of the
observation history available at each decision instant, and for every world of a finite scenario tree and
every vertex of the error set the replayed execution satisfies A with bounds respected, then the design is in
F for that finite world family. (b) Static polyhedral families: if for each vertex of the load polytope a
command satisfies the linear constraints, the constraints are convex in the command, the declared law class
can realize the interpolated commands from quantities observed before the decision, then the design is in F
for the whole polytope. Fragment: finite scenario trees; linear static families.
*Invalid fixture*: a clairvoyant witness: per-world optimal commands on the pusher (u = 1/g for each gain)
offered for the shared-command query, whose true answer is the empty set.

**Candidate card R3: monotone inheritance and symmetry.** For an order d ≼ d′ on a chart: exclusion transports
from d′ down to d when the relaxed feasible set at d is contained in that at d′. Inclusion transports from d
up to d′ when (i) every disturbance admitted at d′ is admitted at d, (ii) the witness controller is realizable
at d′ with the same or a finer observation history, (iii) the mission outputs at d′ satisfy A whenever those
at d do, and (iv) every admitted branch at d′ is simulated by an admitted branch at d. For a group element g:
a verdict transports along g when g fixes the mission and maps W onto itself and Π_Y onto itself. Fragment:
finite-branch models and polyhedral relaxations.
*Invalid fixtures*: a widened sole that passes the static inclusion and adds a swing-collision branch at d′
that no branch at d simulates (condition iv missing); and two symmetric actuators where success needs exactly
one to act: the winning actions (0, 1) and (1, 0) are exchanged by the symmetry, and the only invariant
deterministic actions (0, 0) and (1, 1) both fail, so restricting to equivariant policies changed Π_Y.

**Candidate card R4: exact partitions and composition.** The union of proved inner cells is inner. A cell E
leaves the outer set only by a proof that E ∩ F = ∅; disjointness of E from the known inner cells proves
nothing. Two stage contracts compose when the first stage's guaranteed end set lies within the second's
admissible entry set, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory, and no resource is
spent twice. Fragment: boxes with rational ends; finite stage contracts.
*Invalid fixtures*: a cell excluded because it is disjoint from every inner cell; two individually successful
stage executions offered as a composed mission where the first exit set x ∈ [0, 2] is not inside the second
entry set x ∈ [0, 1].

**Tests while unproved.** For each card, a property test on finite models where F can be enumerated: random
small instances (up to 4 designs, 4 worlds, 3 observation symbols, 2 decision instants) with random
certificates satisfying the card's hypotheses; assert the conclusion against enumeration. For each card, its
invalid fixtures go into the checker's mutation corpus and must be rejected with the expected rule.

**Classing rule ("classed by the weakest step").** A bundle names, for each proof, the rule it relies on and
that rule's status: `proved` (a sealed card exists) or `tested` (candidate card, property-tested). The
instance certificate is `Certified` because the auditor recomputed it. The general soundness of an unproved
rule is not claimed as `Proved` anywhere, and a statement that needs the general rule (for example a
documentation claim that "every accepted exclusion is sound") is classed `Tested` until the card is sealed. The
answer's class remains the weakest among its cells.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/math/augur-proof-rules.md` states the four candidate cards with hypotheses, conclusion, fragment,
      proposed proof route and the existing cards each builds on (Farkas soundness; the stance specimen; the
      docking family; the indistinguishability witness; dominance on product models).
- [ ] The candidate statements are submitted to the proof subsystem's card table; the bead records the
      disposition of each (admitted with a number, merged into an existing card, or deferred).
- [ ] Four property tests pass on enumerable finite models.
- [ ] Every invalid fixture above is rejected by `frob-verify` with the expected first failing rule and is
      registered in the mutation corpus.
- [ ] Bundles carry the rule status field; no output prints an unproved rule as `Proved`.
- [ ] Negative control of the property tests: with hypothesis (iv) removed from R3's generator, the test finds
      a counterexample within 10⁴ instances.

## Considerations

- One certificate accepted by the checker and later shown false disables its proof kind and checker version,
  withdraws the bundles that used them, and adds the counterexample to the corpus; if the cause is a rule and
  not an implementation defect, the proof kind is removed and its cells become `Tested`. A sound card is the
  defence against the second case.
- The Lean proofs depend on which order-theory and linear-arithmetic modules the proof tools accept; the
  fragments here were chosen to stay inside linear arithmetic over ordered fields and finite enumeration.
