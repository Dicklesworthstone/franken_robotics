## Background

A known answer is a case whose truth is exact under its stated model and hand-checkable. The Augur chapter
(mission queries over families of robot designs) lists 33 such fixtures; they are the ground truth for its
verbs, its certificate kinds and its checker. The program's rules for a known-answer row: a derivation on file
(`docs/derivations/<fixture>.md`: model, derivation, value with its digits, error budget); a tolerance with a
reason (`exact` for integers, rationals, Booleans, verdicts and digests); a negative control (the mutation or
planted failure that must make the row fail; a row printed with `none stated` cannot support a gate until one
is registered); and a tier and a site. Values are not improved in transit: the registry carries the chapter's
printed value.

This bead delivers the rows that the first mission slice makes runnable at M1: fifteen rows. The mechanisms
have their own tests and gates; what this battery adds is one place where every row exists as a fixture file
with its derivation and its control, runs through the public verbs, and feeds the registry. Later rows are in
the sibling batteries (N25d-38, N25d-39).

## Scope

In: fixture files, derivation notes, registry entries, registered controls and the script for the fifteen rows
below. Out: the mechanisms; the chapter's known-answer registry bead (the table of record); the gates'
own measurement beads, which this battery supports with fixtures and controls.

## Technical approach

Fixtures under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/augur/ka/`; one `.fmission` (or command file) and one
`expect.json` per row; derivations under `docs/derivations/augur-*.md`. A fixture whose query asks for inclusion
over a load set declares `policy_class.law` (N25e-28 refuses one that does not): the stance rows KA-624 and
KA-626 declare `adjustable`, the class under which their printed values hold (the passive row changes the
authority, not the class).

**Derivations the notes must contain** (each hand-checkable): the stance one-parameter family
f = (t, r − t, s − t, W − r − s + t) with r = (W + u·P_x)/2, s = (W + v·P_y)/2 and the feasibility condition
max{0, r − c₊₋, s − c₋₊, r + s − W} ≤ min{c₊₊, r, s, c₋₋ + r + s − W}, giving t ≥ 90 + 15·v against t ≤ 160 at
the vertex (400, 60, 30) with u = 3; the pusher argument (g·u = 1 gives y ∈ [3/4, 1]; a shared u would need
u ≥ 2 and u ≤ 5/8); the docking integration v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂), x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2), the bounds
1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + 1/2)/2 and p > 2ε; the three-gain case (final positions c and 33c/32, least worst error at
c = 64/65); the midpoint arithmetic 1.45 × 1.45/2 = 841/800.

**Registry.** Each row is entered in `known_answers.toml` under its KA number with value, tolerance `exact`
(KA-632 to the printed digits), tier and site (G1 host; auditor where a certificate is involved; G2 for
KA-657 and KA-658), its control's identifier, and the gate it supports. The three controls registered here
close the `none stated` cells of KA-636, KA-657 and KA-658.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fifteen fixtures, fifteen `expect.json` files and their derivation notes exist; every value in the table
      is asserted exactly through the public verbs.
- [ ] Every row has a registered control and the control is caught in the same run.
- [ ] Certified rows (624, 628, 629, 635, 636, 637, and 638's `alias-pair` exclusion for g = 1, g = 33/32) are also accepted by the standalone `frob-verify`.
- [ ] No row's expected value was generated from the implementation: each derivation note predates the first
      passing run and is cited by the registry row.
- [ ] The registry entries validate against the schema and none carries `none stated`.
- [ ] The battery is cited by the measurement beads of GATE-AUGUR-02, -03 and -04 as their fixture source.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/augur_ka_m1.rs`: one test per row re-deriving the truth from first principles
in exact rationals (independent of the lowerings): `ka_624_boundary` (t ≥ 90 + 15v ≤ 160 ⇒ v ≤ 14/3;
at v = 5 the need is 165), `ka_625_grid_counts`, `ka_626_passive` (2t − 150 = 550 − 2t ⇒ t = 175),
`ka_627_midpoint`, `ka_628_pusher_sets`, `ka_629_intervals`, `ka_630_net_displacement`, `ka_631_joint_law`,
`ka_632_work` (0.94176), `ka_635_sequences`, `ka_636_no_common_vector`, `ka_637_grid` (561 = 17 × 33),
`ka_638_three_gains` (1/65), `ka_657_lp_count` (17 actuator bounds × (2 first-command extrema + 1
common-command feasibility program) = 51 programs), `ka_658_agreement`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_known_answers_m1.sh`: for each row runs its command (`frob mission synthesize`,
`frob mission refute`, `frob mission check`, `frob prove verify`), every run verb with its own key (`--key
ka-6nn-<run>`, so that a row's re-run and its control never return each other's stored receipt), compares with
`expect.json`, then runs the row's control (and, for KA-636 and KA-657, the additional control) and requires the
row to fail under it. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/augur_known_answers_m1/<utc>.ndjson` through the STD-E2E helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event` on every line, a transcript beside it): per row the KA number, command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, expected and observed values as exact
strings, the control's identifier and outcome, bundle digests and the checker's verdict; on failure the first
differing value; a `summary` with rows passed, failed and the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fifteen rows are the known answers. Counterexamples behind the controls: CX-270 (midpoint), CX-272
(command per design), CX-273 (allocation exists), CX-274 (support polygon), CX-275 (sample points), CX-276
(stage rates), CX-282 (a certificate holds when a world is added). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine and
debug against release through the verbs (the second run with its own keys); 1, 2 and 8 workers in process, in
the test `ka_m1_workers_lane` (STD-TEST's `testkit::det::lane` over the stance, pusher and docking lowerings),
since no M1 mission verb carries a worker option; identical answer and bundle digests (exact class).

## Considerations

- KA-627 equals an earlier row of the suite stated for the cone constructions, KA-632 an earlier work row;
  the registry records the equivalences and the row runs once.
- The pusher battery's ten cases and twenty intervals come from the locked pilot fixture; this bead takes
  their values from the re-run record of the pilots, not from the archive directly.
- A recomputation that disagrees with a printed value does not overwrite it: the row keeps the printed value,
  is listed as disputed, and the dispute is resolved in the derivation file.
