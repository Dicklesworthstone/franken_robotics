## Background

The program funds no Frontier [F] or Moonshot [M] mechanism before a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has
run: small enough for a CPU in a day, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance, and
with the ceiling of the benefit stated before any speed test. The pilot register
(`frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`) holds one record per mechanism: the mechanism and tier, the protocol
frozen before execution, the falsifier, the computed ceiling, the fallback and kill gate, the cost, the tool
and its pin, and then the result. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback
ships. A bead for an [F] or [M] mechanism is not ready until its pilot has a recorded result.

Augur (mission queries and inverse design) is almost entirely Frontier and Moonshot, so its rows are many. Some
pilots were run at plan time and exist as small programs in the planning reproduction archive; "run" in the
table below means that paper-scale pilot exists, and its integrated counterpart is still required by the gate
named. The others are still to run. Every plan-time pilot's strong baseline tied or won, which is why most
ceilings read "none claimed".

This bead enters the rows. It runs nothing; the carriers do.

## Scope

In: 21 rows from the chapter's table and one row this drafting adds (the two small exact constructions, for
which the chapter names no pilot), each with its record identifier; the linkage test that ties mechanism beads to
pilot rows. Out: running any pilot; the register's schema and the funding gate (N38a-09); the kill decisions; the
Augur pilot records that their own pilot beads enter for mechanisms outside the chapter's table (listed under
the linkage test).

## Technical approach

**Rows.** Every row is a record of the pilot register in its schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1` (N38a-09): `tier`
is the letter `F` or `M`; the ceiling is the text below; the falsifier is the table's text where it states one,
and otherwise the trigger of the row's kill gate as the chapter's kill-gate table words it; `fallback.ships` is
that kill gate's fallback (row 22: the text in its row). These are the texts the fallback ledger (N40-26) enters
with each kill record, so a record and its kill gate cannot drift apart; the carrier's frozen protocol states the
falsifier with its tolerances and counts, and the record's texts stay as entered. Two kinds of row,
told apart by where their result comes from (DESIGN: one stored result per check, so a mechanism row and the
plan-time ledger cannot disagree):
- **Run (paper-scale)** (rows 1, 2, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 for their plan-time part). The row carries
  `plan_time_records`, the identifiers of the plan-time ledger records (N38a-10) that hold the pilot: row 1
  `PILOT-PT-01`, `-02`, `-06`, `-07`, `-08`, `-09`; row 2 `PILOT-PT-08`; row 6 `PILOT-PT-11`; row 8 `PILOT-PT-05`;
  row 9 `PILOT-PT-03`, `-04`; row 10 `PILOT-PT-06`, `-07`; row 12 `PILOT-PT-10`. No value is copied into the row:
  its plan-time result is the ledger's derived status (`pilots::rerun_status`), `reproduced` only when every
  linked record is `reproduced`. The field `plan_time_records` is an extension of the register's schema
  requested from N38a-09, in the same way the ledger added its own fields.
- **To run** (rows 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13 to 22, and the second part of rows 6 and 9; row 5's plan-time enumeration
  has no record in the reproduction archive, so its carrier BD-357 repeats it under a frozen protocol). The row
  is entered without a protocol; it stays `unregistered` (a funding block) until its carrier pilot bead commits the frozen protocol and
  its hash, at which point the status becomes `registered` and the carrier appends results.

`fallback.kill_gate` holds the kill-gate identifier. The Augur and synthesis kill records are entered in the gate
register by the fallback ledger at M1 (N40-26), after this bead; until then each row carries `to-register` with
the identifier as its proposed wording, which the register accepts and which blocks funding (no Augur work package
is funded before M1, so nothing waits on it). `integrated` names the gate that must still measure the mechanism
in the product.

**Record fields (as the register's owner N38a-09 names them; values this bead supplies).** `id`, `mechanism`,
`tier` (one letter), `owner` (the owning section, §25.27 or the section the row cites), `bead` (the carrier, one
key), `work_package` (the carrier bead's parent), `protocol` (path, hash and freeze date; for the plan-time
pilots, the path inside the reproduction archive), `falsifier`, `ceiling`, `fallback` (its two fields `ships`
and `kill_gate`), `cost`, `tool` (name and pin), `status`, `integrated_gate`, `plan_time_records`. Results are
appended `[[pilot.result]]` tables, never overwritten; this bead appends none.

DESIGN, two mappings from the chapter's table onto that schema, so that no carrier and no tier is lost:
- *One tier per record.* Rows 16 and 17 each name a Frontier and a Moonshot mechanism, and `tier` holds one
  letter, so each is entered as two records: 16a holonomy query [F] and 16b shape-space atlas [M] (both carried
  by the holonomy pilot N25c-42, one pilot, `work_package` the epic BD-425); 17a symmetry–event identity [F]
  (N25d-01) and 17b amortized use [M] (N25d-04). Row 14 likewise: the chapter's table tags skill transport
  [M], while §25.11 makes its static wrench-coverage core [F] inside the [M] section and the Moonshot queue
  (N25d-49) names that core as the [F] part that must pass first; so 14a the static core [F] and 14b the full
  relation [M] are two records, both carried by the tray-interface pilot N25c-01 (one pilot, as for row 16). The
  22 rows are therefore 25 records.
- *One carrier per record.* `bead` holds one key, and it is the pilot bead that commits the protocol and appends
  the result, never the mechanism or epic it funds (rows 4, 7, 9, 11 and 13 to 16 name their pilot beads, as
  those beads and the mechanism beads that exclude them from their scope state, and as N25d-41's kill-gate
  carrier map does for rows 14 to 16). Where the table names several carriers, `bead` names the
  carrier of the part still to run (row 6: N25a-17; row 9: N25c-15) or, for row 1, whose pilots are all plan-time,
  the carrier of its first pilot (BD-042); the other carriers are reached through `plan_time_records`, since
  each linked ledger record names its re-run bead in `rerun_owner` (N38a-10). The linkage test checks that the
  union of `bead` and those `rerun_owner` values equals the carrier column of the table. A carrier registers the
  record entered here (commits the protocol and its hash, which makes it `registered`) and never adds a second
  record for the same mechanism, which would leave this one `unregistered` and block funding.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 22 rows are in `pilots.toml` as 25 records with the identifiers and texts above, using the register's
      field names (`id`, `owner`, `bead`, `fallback.kill_gate`, `plan_time_records`, `integrated_gate`); each
      `bead` is the pilot bead of the carrier column; the registry check passes.
- [ ] No row's result is a product measurement; the run rows read their plan-time status from the linked ledger
      records (`not-run` until BD-041, BD-042 and BD-043 append results), and nothing is copied or overwritten.
- [ ] `pilots fundable --all --json` reports every Augur work package with an [F] or [M] mechanism `Blocked`
      while its row has no protocol, no result or a `to-register` kill gate (GATE-GAUNT-08's rule, exercised on
      these rows).
- [ ] Negative controls: a row with an empty falsifier fails the registry check; an upper-tier Augur capability
      record with no pilot row fails it; a pilot result copied into a gate's `measured` field is rejected; a
      `plan_time_records` entry naming a missing ledger identifier fails it; a second record for a mechanism
      already entered here (a carrier writing its own record beside its row) fails it.
- [ ] Row 22 is marked as added in drafting, so that the chapter's owner can confirm or remove it.

## Considerations

- The probe ceiling: with times in seconds, a direct measurement dwelling 1 s (the mission dwell), a generated
  probe dwelling 1/8 s (125 ms), setup h, common validation cost v and engineering cost e per decision, the
  ratio of direct to probe cost is (h + 1 + v + e_direct)/(h + 1/8 + v + e_probe): at most 8 at zero overhead,
  1.25/0.375 ≈ 3.33 at h = 0.25 s with v = e = 0, and 1 against a programmed expert who chooses the same
  schedule.
- Row 22's kill gate is not in the plan; the proposed `KILL-SYNTH-PLACEMENT` is asked of the fallback ledger's
  owner. Until it is registered or the owner rules the two constructions Solid, N25d-08 stays unfundable, which
  is the fail-closed default.
- The owner decides which Moonshot rows are funded and in what order, under the cap of four active Moonshot
  items (decision bead N25d-49).
- A pilot result changes a mechanism's tier and scope in its bead; a negative result is recorded, not hidden.
- Row 14's split follows §25.11 and the Moonshot queue rather than the one tag of the chapter's table; if the
  chapter's owner rules the static core part of the [M] mechanism, 14a's record is set `withdrawn` (N38a-09's
  status) with the ruling cited; records are never deleted.
