## Background

A gate is a measurable pass condition tied to exactly one of the seven claims (Proof, Known answer, Parity,
Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality, Performance). A kill gate names in advance the condition under which a
mechanism is abandoned and the fallback that ships. Gate status lives in `registries/gates.toml`, one record
per gate, and nowhere else: the README's status table, the release rubric and the exit reviews are generated
from it. A gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold, with at least one registered
negative control caught in the same run, recorded by a session that did not implement the work. Where a
chapter and the program-wide register differ, the register governs.

Augur (mission queries and inverse design) defines 24 convergence gates and 18 kill gates in its gates
section, and a further 11 gates and 4 kill gates in later subsections. This bead enters the 42 rows of the
gates section with their carriers, records where the chapter and the register differ, and tests the linkage.
It measures nothing.

## Scope

In: 24 gate rows and 18 kill-gate rows; the reconciliation notes; carriers; the linkage test; the note on the
ten-fold strata. Out: measuring or deciding any gate (carriers do); rows of the later subsections
(GATE-AUGUR-25 to -35 and their four kill gates: entered with the program register by N40-10 and N40-29,
measured and decided by the beads N25e-01 to N25e-44); the controls' definitions (the counterexample registry
and each carrier).

**Where the chapter and the register differ (the register governs).** GATE-AUGUR-07 is "Performance" in the
chapter's table and "Design comparison" in the register (a row about a method, not the engine, is a Design
comparison). GATE-AUGUR-09 is M4 in the chapter and "M3 (pilot); M4" in the register. The chapter's kill table
gives no decision milestone; the register's are used above.

**Ten-fold strata.** The partial ten-fold gate at M3 and the tournament at M7 draw their body-envelope and
sensing strata from this chapter's fixtures (the stance family for body envelopes; the docking and alias
fixtures for sensing); their thresholds are defined with those gates, not here. The rows above carry a
`strata_fixture` note so that the ten-fold protocol can cite the fixture identities.

**Linkage test.** `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g0 -- registry::augur_gates`: (1) all 42 identifiers
present once; (2) each convergence gate names exactly one claim from the seven; (3) each gate's milestone
equals the register's; (4) every convergence gate has at least one control registered before it can record
`pass`; (5) each carrier bead exists in the bead graph; (6) a kill-gate row has a trigger, a fallback and a
decision milestone; (7) the status at registration is `not-run`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 42 rows are in `registries/gates.toml` with the texts above; the linkage test passes.
- [ ] The two reconciliations are recorded in the rows' notes; no row carries the chapter's superseded value.
- [ ] The honest-status table regenerates and prints all 42 as open (never as passed) at registration.
- [ ] Negative controls: a row with status `pass` and empty `controls` is refused; a row whose recording
      session equals its implementing session is refused; a duplicate identifier fails the load; a kill-gate
      row with no fallback fails the test.
- [ ] The later-subsection gates and kill gates are listed in a pointer table with their owning beads once
      those exist, so the chapter's full set of 35 gates and 22 kill gates is traceable from one place.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`registry::augur_gates` as specified, plus `claim_reconciliation` (GATE-AUGUR-07 is `design-comparison`),
`milestone_reconciliation` (GATE-AUGUR-09 lists M3 and M4), `kill_rows_complete` (18), `gate_rows_complete` (24).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/augur_gate_register.sh`: runs the registry test and regenerates the status table from the
registry; logs per row its claim, milestone, status, control count and carriers, each planted defect's
refusal, and a `summary`. Re-run at each milestone exit that has an Augur gate due.

### Known answers and negative controls
The four planted registry defects above.

## Considerations

- Carriers named here follow the seed beads and this unit's beads; if another unit carries a gate under a
  different key the row is updated, never duplicated.
- A kill gate's trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement
  it governs; the registry test compares the texts with a frozen copy.
