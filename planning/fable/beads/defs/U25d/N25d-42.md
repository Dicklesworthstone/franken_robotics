## Background

A mission query (`.fmission`, schema `frob.mission/1`) is a question about a whole family of robot designs: a
design domain, a family of worlds, an observation channel, a policy class, a horizon, acceptance and hard-loss
predicates, and the quantifiers that relate them. Its `predicates` field holds `accept` (the Boolean acceptance
predicate A), `hard_loss` and optional `margins`; "atoms carry their comparator (<, ≤, =) and units, and may
reference requirement terms of a `.ftask`".

A task file (`.ftask`, schema `frob.task/1`) already has a requirement language: atoms (a comparison of two
expressions of one dimension, with a scale or the mark `:exact`), past-time operators H, O, S (historically,
once, since), future-time operators G, F, U (always, eventually, until), windows with exact rational bounds, an
online formula, a verdict formula, and labelled example traces (at least two satisfying and two violating)
without which a requirement is not admitted.

OPEN-AUGUR-1 asks which of two things a mission predicate is. **(A) By reference:** a mission predicate that
needs temporal structure names a requirement or atom defined in a `.ftask` by its identifier; the only
predicates written inside a `.fmission` are single comparisons. **(B) Embedded:** a `.fmission` may contain
formulas of the requirement language itself. Owner: the Arena lead (the requirement language is Arena's).
Deadline: GATE-AUGUR-01 at M1 (admission and checker reject 100% of a corpus of at least 60 mutated queries and
certificates with the expected refusal code), because the corpus must know what a predicate mutation is. The
fail-closed default, which holds until decided: **(A), so that there is one grammar.** It is fail-closed
because it removes a capability (temporal formulas written inline) and adds no claim.

Why the default is the better engineering position (DESIGN): a second place where temporal formulas are parsed
is a second place where a comparator, a window bound or a clock can be read differently, and a mission
certificate proves a statement about what was parsed. The requirement language is admitted only with labelled
examples and a mutation check (reversing a comparator or deleting an atom must change an example's label); an
embedded formula would either carry those examples inside the mission or skip the check. Rejected alternative
(B) is not wrong, only more expensive, and its cost is written below so that the owner can choose it knowingly.

## Scope

In: the two options stated exactly; the predicate inventory of the M1 fixtures; the decision rule and its
re-open trigger; the ADR and the register row; six probe documents with their expected outcome under each
option, handed to the admission bead and the mutation corpus. Out: implementing admission (the schema bead and
the semantic-admission bead do, under the default); running the probes through the public verbs (N25d-48,
the defaults-in-force battery); the requirement reader itself.

## Technical approach

**1. The default, stated so that it can be tested (DESIGN; the schema bead fixes spellings).**
- An *inline atom* is `{ "id", "expr" }` holding exactly one comparison with comparator `<`, `≤` or `=` (and
  their mirrors) between algebraic expressions over symbols the query declares (design parameters, world
  parameters, observation fields, the witness variables and port-indexed symbols of the family template), with
  units checked. It has no temporal operator, no window, no clock, no `delay`, no reduction and no scale.
- A *reference* names a requirement or atom of a task by identifier (in the carrier example: `"fits_loaded_door"`,
  `"payload_supported"`, `"driver_limits"`, `"goal_before_deadline"`). The canonical bytes of the query carry the
  identifier together with the `TaskId` (the BLAKE3 hash of the task's canonical JSON) it resolves in, so
  editing the referenced requirement changes `MissionId` (INV-AUGUR-03: `MissionId` changes whenever a
  quantifier, comparator, world support, persistence declaration, observation timing, policy class or horizon
  changes).
- Any token of the requirement language inside a `.fmission` (G, F, U, H, O, S, a window, `online`, `verdict`,
  `example`, `:scale`) is refused with `FROB-REF-868` (the `.fmission` file is not valid; exit 3), pointer at
  the predicate, first repair "define the requirement in a task and reference it by identifier".

**2. The predicate inventory (`docs/decisions/open-augur-1/inventory.json`, DESIGN path).** One record per
predicate of the M1 fixtures and of the carrier example, classed `static-atom`, `terminal-atom` or `reference`.

| Fixture | Predicates | Class | Temporal operator needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stance family (400 N on four feet) | Σ fᵢ = W; Σ qᵢ·fᵢ = P; fᵢ ≥ 0; fᵢ ≤ capᵢ | 4 static atoms | none |
| Pusher, y = max(0, g·u − d) | y ≥ 3/4; y ≤ 5/4 | 2 static atoms | none |
| Docking, three ticks | x₃ = 1; v₃ = 0; abs(u_k) ≤ U for k = 0, 1, 2 | 3 terminal or per-tick atoms | none (the horizon is a tick count) |
| Carrier (delivery and placement) | four identifiers | 4 references | yes, inside the task |

Known answer of the inventory: 9 inline atoms, of which 0 need a temporal operator; 4 temporal predicates, of
which 4 are references. Under (A) every M1 fixture is expressible.

**3. Decision rule (DESIGN proposal; the Arena lead may amend it before reading the inventory, not after).**
`decide(inventory) -> {confirm-A, reopen}`: `confirm-A` when no inline predicate of a fixture due at M1 needs a
temporal operator; otherwise `reopen` with the offending predicates listed. Re-open trigger written into the
ADR: at the M3 exit, if more than 20% of the mission queries of the M3 briefs needed a task created solely to
hold one requirement used by one mission (an "orphan task"), the question is re-asked with option (B′) below.

**4. What (B) would cost, for the record.** (B′, the cheapest form: the `.fmission` reader calls the same
requirement reader, so there is still one parser.) The mutation corpus gains at least six predicate mutations
(comparator reversed, window bound changed, operator swapped, atom deleted, scale changed, clock changed), each
of which must change `MissionId` and be caught; labelled examples must travel with the mission or the
example check is lost for embedded formulas; the bundle's primitive equations must render the formula; every
lowering must state which fragment of the language it supports and refuse the rest.

**5. The six probe documents** (`fixtures/open-augur-1/p1…p6.fmission`), with expected outcomes.

| Probe | Content | Under (A) | Under (B′) |
|---|---|---|---|
| p1 | The stance query as printed in the schema | admitted | admitted |
| p2 | The carrier fragment with its four references and a bound task | admitted; `MissionId` covers the `TaskId` | same |
| p3 | p2 with one identifier absent from the task | 868, pointer `predicates.accept[3]`, nearest identifiers offered | same |
| p4 | p1 plus an inline `(G (0 s 8 s) cleared)` | 868 with the "define and reference" repair | admitted if `cleared` resolves |
| p5 | p2 after reversing one comparator inside the referenced requirement | admitted with a different `MissionId` | same |
| p6 | An inline atom with comparator `≠`, or with no unit on a dimensional side | 868 | 868 |

**6. Records.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-1.md` states the option in force, the inventory digest, the
rule's output, the re-open trigger and the owner's name and date; the register row of OPEN-AUGUR-1 moves from
`open (default in force)` to `decided` with the ADR identifier. If the deadline arrives with no decision, the
default is recorded as the ADR and the capability removed (inline temporal predicates) is named in the M1 exit
review.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The inventory file exists and its counts equal the known answer (9 inline atoms, 0 temporal; 4 references).
- [ ] `decide` is a pure function of the inventory; it returns `confirm-A` on the recorded inventory.
- [ ] The six probe documents exist, each with its expected outcome under both options, and are cited by the
      schema bead's tests and by at least two rows of the mutation corpus (a mutated identifier; an embedded
      formula).
- [ ] An ADR exists before GATE-AUGUR-01 is measured, signed by the Arena lead or recorded as "default at
      deadline"; the register row cites it.
- [ ] Negative control of the rule: an inventory in which one M1 inline predicate is `F_[0,8] docked` returns
      `reopen` and names that predicate.

## Considerations

- The stance example prints atoms as Unicode text (`"Σ fᵢ = W"`). That text is a rendering; what is proved is
  the primitive equation in the bundle. Whether inline atoms are parsed from text or bound to the template's
  named constraints is the schema bead's choice and does not change this decision.
- The requirement language is also a defect surface for mission certificates under (A): a defect in its
  reader is a defect in every mission that references it, which is why its evaluators are tested at scale.
- Shaping rewards are not predicates under either option.
- What would make us revisit: the re-open trigger; or a mission family whose acceptance cannot be written as a
  task requirement at all (for example one that quantifies over designs inside the predicate).
