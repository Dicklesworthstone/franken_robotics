## Background

Mission queries are quantified statements: "there is a policy such that for every world and every execution
the acceptance predicate holds". On small exactly solvable plants a general engine answers such statements
directly. Two kinds of engine are meant here: a quantifier-elimination engine (it rewrites a quantified
formula over the reals into an equivalent quantifier-free one) and an SMT solver (satisfiability modulo
theories: it decides whether a formula over linear or nonlinear real arithmetic has a model, and returns a
model or `unsat`).

FACT from the plan-time pilots (small programs on small models, to be re-run): on the one-axis pusher an
off-the-shelf engine produced the family answer directly from the raw plant for 8 of 12 queries; on the
label-minimization fixtures an independent SMT encoding returned unsatisfiable for one shared label and
satisfiable for two, and symbolic elimination agreed on all five cases; a lifted linear program tied the
stance projection and a scenario-tree program tied the docking family. Every pilot's strong baseline tied or
won, which is why two decisions stand: conventional formulations first (ADR-AUGUR-03) and a rival's answer
goes through the same checker (ADR-AUGUR-09).

OPEN-AUGUR-4 asks whether such an engine may be a **production dependency** or only a **comparator outside
the product**. It is the owner's question, because it trades the program's dependency posture (a core whose
runtime dependencies are the sibling libraries only) against coverage. **Default if undecided: comparator
only; the product uses in-tree elimination for small linear systems.** What the default costs: some family
cells stay unresolved that a general solver would close. Deadline: before the partial ten-fold gate at M3,
because the kill gate KILL-AUGUR-TIE depends on it: if a short robust program over a conventional solver
reaches the same checked scope at comparable turnaround, that program ships behind the same verbs, schema and
checker, and if that program needs such an engine, shipping it needs this decision.

One technical fact frames the choice. Under "propose anywhere, check on the CPU" (ADR-AUGUR-04) an engine's
verdict changes no cell by itself. A `sat` answer is useful when its model is a witness the independent
checker accepts (a rational point: `lp-primal`; a policy table: `finite-game`). An `unsat` or an eliminated
formula is useful only when a certificate in the closed registry can be reconstructed from it (a Farkas dual,
an alias pair, a partition of exact cells). So the value of an engine in production is the fraction of its
answers that become checkable certificates, not the fraction it answers.

## Scope

In: the evidence brief for the owner (census, certifiable fraction, dependency facts); the options with their
costs; the default at deadline; the ADR and register row; the definition of "in-tree elimination" that the
default relies on. Out: the comparator's use in known-answer tests (the oracle tooling); the kill-gate
decision itself (the unit that drafts the tie gate reads this decision's state); admitting any engine (a
separate dependency-admission bead if option B or C is chosen).

## Technical approach

**1. What the default ships (DESIGN reading of "in-tree elimination for small linear systems").** Exact
elimination of variables from small systems of linear inequalities over ℚ (Fourier–Motzkin with redundant-row
removal), in the proposer, bounded by declared limits on variables and rows; beyond the limits the cell is
unresolved with explanation `SolverFailure` or `BudgetExhausted`, never excluded. It produces exact cell
boundaries (closed ends, singletons, strict ends) for one-parameter families such as the pusher battery.

**2. The brief (`docs/decisions/open-augur-4/brief.json`, DESIGN path). Inputs, each with a ledger identifier:**
- *Census.* Run the comparator engine, outside the product (`tools/oracle`), on every unresolved cell of the M1
  fixtures (stance, docking, pusher) and of the M3 rehearsal families. Per family: unresolved by the product
  (u), closed by the comparator (c), and of those the number for which a certificate of a registered kind was
  reconstructed and accepted by the offline auditor (k). Certifiable fraction = k / u.
- *Pusher re-run.* The twelve plan-time queries re-run: how many the engine answers directly (plan-time: 8),
  and for how many the answer becomes an accepted certificate.
- *Turnaround.* Median wall time per query for the product's route and for the engine's route including
  certificate reconstruction and checking (checker time is part of decision time).
- *Dependency facts for each candidate engine.* Licence; size of the build closure; whether it runs in process
  or as a separate worker process; determinism of its answers across hosts and versions; whether it emits
  proof objects for `unsat`.

**3. Options put to the owner.**

| Option | Meaning | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A (default) | Comparator only, under `tools/oracle` and in the rival arm; never in the dependency closure of any `frob` binary | Cells the in-tree elimination cannot close stay unresolved; if KILL-AUGUR-TIE fires on a stratum whose tying program needs an engine, the report says the product ships in-tree elimination and names the cells left open |
| B | Admitted as an out-of-process proposer behind the airlock, with a dependency-admission record | Its answers are proposals; only reconstructed certificates change cells; a missing engine is "backend unavailable" and the cell is unresolved; the core's dependency statement is amended to name it |
| C | Not shipped, but accepted when present: any producer may submit a bundle and `frob-verify` checks it | No dependency; the capability exists only on machines that have the engine; answers name their producer |

**4. Completeness rule.** `brief_complete(brief)` is true only when every input above is present for every
family in the census and each number cites a ledger identifier. The script never recommends an option: the
decision is the owner's judgement. It computes the certifiable fraction and prints the three options with
their measured consequences.

**5. Default at the deadline.** If the owner has not answered when the suite hash of the M3 partial gate is
fixed, option A is recorded as `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-4.md` with the capability removed stated in numbers
(u − k cells per family). The ADR is cited by the tie-gate decision and by the M3 exit review.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The brief is complete (rule above) at least one milestone-review cycle before the M3 suite hash is fixed.
- [ ] The certifiable fraction is reported per family with u, c and k; every k is a bundle accepted by the
      offline auditor built from an empty environment.
- [ ] An ADR exists before the partial gate runs: the owner's choice, or "default at deadline".
- [ ] Under option A, `xtask check-deps` shows no quantifier-elimination or SMT crate, and no invocation of such
      a binary, in the closure of any core crate; the comparator lives only under `tools/oracle`.
- [ ] Negative controls: a brief with a missing census family is reported incomplete; an engine answer with no
      reconstructed certificate is counted in c and not in k.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_questions.rs`:
- `oa4_certifiable_fraction`: u = 12, c = 8, k = 5 → 5/12; c > u is rejected as malformed.
- `oa4_brief_incomplete`: the docking family missing → `false`, with the missing key named.
- `oa4_default_at_deadline`: no owner record at the deadline → ADR text "comparator only", u − k = 7 cells.
- `oa4_uncertified_not_counted`: an `unsat` with no Farkas dual adds 1 to c and 0 to k.
- `fm_elimination_pusher` (runs the real in-tree elimination): for y = max(0, g·u − d), g ∈ [1/2, 2],
  d ∈ [0, 1/4], y ∈ [3/4, 5/4], eliminating d and u per build gives the gain set [1/2, 2]; eliminating d and g
  for a shared command gives ∅ (the constraints u ≥ 2 and u ≤ 5/8 conflict).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_augur_4.sh`: runs the product on the census families through `frob mission synthesize`,
runs the comparator through the oracle tool on the unresolved cells, submits reconstructed bundles to
`frob-verify`, assembles the brief and logs per family u, c, k, both turnaround medians in milliseconds, each
dependency fact, the completeness verdict and a `summary`. It runs `xtask check-deps` and logs the result as
the default-in-force assertion.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-628 (per-build command set all of [1/2, 2]; shared set empty), KA-629 (twenty intervals of the ten frozen
pusher cases), KA-644 (three variants: every pair intersects, the triple does not). Planted failures: a scratch
copy of `frob-mission`'s manifest with an SMT crate added must fail `check-deps`; a reconstructed certificate
with one corrupted multiplier must be rejected by the auditor and so not counted in k.

## Considerations

- RISK-26 (a short robust program over an incumbent engine reaches the same accepted answers in comparable
  time) is the scenario this decision prepares for; the ship-the-tying-program rule is the mitigation.
- A sibling symbolic library exists with a real solver capped at 8 variables, 64 equations and zero-dimensional
  ideals; it performs no quantifier elimination and is not what this question is about.
- Option C is already implied by the rule that the auditor accepts a bundle from any producer; choosing A does
  not forbid a user from bringing certificates produced elsewhere.
- Licence terms of a candidate engine are checked before option B is put to the owner at all.
