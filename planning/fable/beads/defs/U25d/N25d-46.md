## Background

Every verb returns one envelope and one exit code. The table, fixed for all verbs:

| Exit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Completed, nothing to flag |
| 2 | Completed with findings or flags |
| 3 | Refused input or question |
| 4 | Budget: refused at admission or stopped; nothing partial is published as a result (refusal family 70x) |
| 5 | Capability not implemented, not admitted, or outside the session's scopes |
| 6 | Backend or device unavailable |
| 7 | Conflict |
| 8 | Accepted and still running: the envelope carries a job handle |
| 9 | Indeterminate |

Exit 1 is never emitted on purpose. On the MCP surface exits 3 to 7 are tool results with `isError: true`
carrying the same envelope.

A mission answer is different from most results: it is a list of cells, and **each decided cell is certified on
its own** (answers are anytime, ADR-AUGUR-08: more work can only move cells out of the unresolved set). When a
budget line runs out in the middle of a query, the cells already decided are complete results, not fragments.
The chapter's DESIGN therefore reads the standing rule of exit 4 as applied to "a result whose parts are
separately complete":

- exit 0 when the unresolved set satisfies the query's `resolution` (the largest unresolved set the caller
  accepts, in a stated measure, with mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations);
- **exit 2 with the flag `resolution-unmet` when it does not**: the certified cells and the unresolved
  remainder are returned, each cell complete in itself;
- exit 4 only when the budget ended before admission and the first checked cell, in which case nothing is
  published.

Whether this reading stands is OPEN-AUGUR-7, owned by the Tether lead (the owner of the envelope), due with the
registry entry of `mission.synthesize` at M1. The fail-closed default is the reading above. It is fail-closed
in the sense that matters here: no unresolved cell is ever printed as a verdict (ADR-AUGUR-07: unknown is a
verdict), and nothing uncertified is published under any option.

## Scope

In: the options and their consequences on both surfaces; the decomposability criterion that makes the default
a rule and not an exception; three probe queries with expected envelopes under each option; the ADR and the
register row. Out: implementing budget stops (the mission-verbs bead does, under the default); the estimate
and budget machinery; job cancellation.

## Technical approach

**1. Options.**

| Option | Budget stop with certified cells | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A (default) | `ok: true`; exit 0 if `resolution` is met, else exit 2 with `warnings`/flag `resolution-unmet`; exit 4 only before the first checked cell | Exit 4's sentence needs the qualifier "unless every published part is separately complete" |
| B | `ok: false`, exit 4 with a 70x refusal; the certified cells are stored as an answer `a:…` named in the refusal's `repairs`, and fetched by a second call (`mission.show`) | An agent pays a turn to read what it already paid to compute; scripts that treat 4 as failure discard certified work; the anytime property is hidden from the caller |
| C | A new exit code for "stopped on budget with a complete partial answer" | Every client and both surfaces learn a tenth code; the parity gate between the command line and MCP grows a row |

**2. The decomposability criterion (DESIGN proposal to the Tether lead).** A verb may publish a result after a
budget stop if and only if (i) every published part carries its own completeness evidence (for a mission cell:
its proofs, or its typed explanation as unresolved), (ii) the envelope states what is missing in a
machine-readable flag, and (iii) a later run with more budget can only refine the published result
(INV-AUGUR-04: a later answer to the same `MissionId` moves cells only out of the unresolved set). A truncated
rollout or a half-trained policy fails (i) and stays exit 4. This makes option A a rule that other verbs can be
tested against, instead of a special case for one cluster.

**3. Probes (`fixtures/open-augur-7/`), on the stance family** (400 N on four feet at (±1/3, ±b) m, 160 N
ratings; free parameter v = 1/b ∈ [2, 6]; exact answer: inner [2, 14/3], excluded (14/3, 6], minimum
half-width 3/14 m).

| Probe | Budget and resolution | Expected under A | Under B |
|---|---|---|---|
| q1 | Budget ends after the inner cell is certified and before the exclusion; `max_unresolved` interval length 0 in v | exit 2, flag `resolution-unmet`; `inner` = [2, 14/3] with four `lp-primal` proofs; `unresolved` = (14/3, 6] with explanation `BudgetExhausted`; the cost claim is `feasible-candidate` at 3/14 m with no matching lower bound | exit 4, refusal 70x, answer address in `repairs`; `mission.show` returns the same cells |
| q2 | Same stop; `max_unresolved` interval length 4/3 in v (6 − 14/3 = 4/3) | exit 0 | exit 4 |
| q3 | Budget ends during admission | exit 4, `ok: false`, nothing published, estimate attached | exit 4 |

**4. Invariants under every option (tested by the defaults-in-force battery, N25d-48, and the budget-stop
script of the mission verbs).** No unresolved cell appears as a verdict in any output path; a re-run of q1 with
ample budget returns exit 0 with (14/3, 6] excluded and never moves [2, 14/3] out of `inner`; an idempotent
replay returns the same envelope and exit; on MCP `isError` is false for exits 0 and 2 and true for exit 4;
`frob job wait` on the same query as a job returns the same exit as the synchronous call.

**5. Records.** `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-7.md`: the option, the criterion as adopted or amended, the three
probes' expected envelopes, the sentence added to the exit table. The register row moves to `decided`. If the
registry entry of `mission.synthesize` is frozen with no decision, option A is recorded as the ADR.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists before the verb registry entry of `mission.synthesize` is frozen at M1, signed by the
      Tether lead or recorded as "default at deadline".
- [ ] The probe table gives, for each option, the exit code, `ok`, the flag and the published sets for q1 to
      q3; the mission-verbs script reads its expected codes from this table (one place to change).
- [ ] The criterion classifies correctly: mission answer → may publish; truncated rollout → may not.
- [ ] The exit-code documentation generated for agents states the adopted reading in one sentence.
- [ ] Negative control: an envelope for q1 that reports exit 0 while `resolution` is unmet is rejected by the
      table check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_questions.rs`:
- `oa7_expected_exit`: `expected(option, probe)` returns (A, q1) = 2, (A, q2) = 0, (A, q3) = 4,
  (B, q1) = 4, (B, q2) = 4, (B, q3) = 4.
- `oa7_resolution_arithmetic`: unresolved (14/3, 6] has interval length 4/3 in v exactly; it meets a limit of
  4/3 and fails a limit of 0 and a limit of 5/4.
- `oa7_decomposable`: parts = [cell with proofs, cell with explanation] → true; parts = [rollout truncated at
  tick 412 of 1,000] → false.
- `oa7_flag_required`: under A an envelope with exit 2 and no `resolution-unmet` flag is malformed.
- `oa7_mcp_mapping`: exit 0 and 2 → `isError: false`; exit 4 → `isError: true`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_augur_7.sh`: reads the option in force from the register, prints the expected envelope for
each probe, checks the ADR against the table, and logs option, probe, expected exit, flag and published sets
per line, then a `summary`. It runs no query itself: the mission verbs depend on this decision, so their own
budget-stop script executes the probes against the table.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-624 (b ≥ 3/14 m; forces (160, 130, 110, 0) N at the (60, 30) N·m vertex) fixes what q1's inner cell must
contain. Planted failures: the malformed envelopes of `oa7_flag_required` and of the acceptance list; a table
row in which q3 publishes a cell (rejected: nothing is published before the first checked cell).
Metamorphic relation: raising the budget never lowers the exit from 0 to 2 for the same query.

## Considerations

- The choice is about one integer; the published content (certified cells, typed unresolved cells) is the same
  under A and B and must be tested independently of the code.
- Exit 2 already means "completed with findings or flags" for analyses that found a problem with a design; a
  caller must read the flag to tell "unmet resolution" from other findings. The flag name is part of the
  registry entry.
- A query whose estimate exceeds the session budget is refused before it runs, under the budget family with
  the estimate attached; that is not a budget stop and is unaffected.
- What would make us revisit: evidence from the fresh-agent gate that agents misread exit 2 as a design
  finding and act on unresolved cells.
