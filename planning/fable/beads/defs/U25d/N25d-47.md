## Background

A mission query fixes who decides what, knowing what, when. Its `policy_class` field names the decision makers
(`"decision_makers": ["allocator"]` in the stance example). The semantic rule is: **each actuator has a named
decision maker with its own history of received packets, observable actions and local measurements at its
commitment time; a centralized controller is admitted only when the information actually reaches it, and its
command actually arrives, by the same deadline.** With one robot and one controller this is one history. With
two robots handing a payload over it is two, and the difference decides feasibility.

The smallest case is the handoff bit. Robot A sees a payload bit β ∈ {0, 1}. Robot B must choose its matching
latch ℓ ∈ {0, 1} at its commitment instant, before observing A; the handoff succeeds iff ℓ = β. A planner with
the pooled history (it sees β, then chooses ℓ) succeeds on both bits. Each of B's two deterministic local
choices (ℓ ≡ 0, ℓ ≡ 1) succeeds on exactly one bit, so no local policy succeeds in every world, and private
randomization does not rescue a guaranteed mission. Delivering the bit to B before its commitment restores
success; the certificate then records the channel capacity (one bit) and the worst admitted delivery time.

OPEN-AUGUR-9 asks whether `.fmission` version 1 expresses several decision makers with separate histories.
Owner: the Augur lead. Deadline: M8, before slice 5 of the delivery order (the two-carrier brownfield handoff,
then the multi-room shift), which is a Moonshot. **Fail-closed default: the field is present and accepts one
decision maker; more than one is refused** (capability not implemented in this build, exit 5) until that
slice. The default removes a capability; it cannot certify a pooled-history policy for a system whose
histories are separate, which is the error it exists to prevent.

## Scope

In: the three options; the per-maker record that option A or B would add; the byte-stability test that
protects existing mission identities; the handoff-bit variants as the acceptance fixture of the decision; the
decision rule; the ADR and register row. Out: composing stages across robots, the timed schedule and resource
ledgers (the facility epic's beads); the admission refusal under the default (the semantic-admission bead).

## Technical approach

**1. Options.**

| Option | Meaning | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| A | Extend `frob.mission/1` in place: `decision_makers` accepts n ≥ 1 records | Safe only if every stored single-maker query keeps its canonical bytes, hence its `MissionId` (the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical JSON); needs the byte-stability proof below |
| B | Open `frob.mission/2` for queries with more than one decision maker; version 1 stays single-maker for ever | Two readers and two admission paths; a migration verb; answers of the two versions are never mixed in one revalidation |
| C | Never in the query: express a multi-robot mission as a chain of single-maker missions joined by `composition` proofs | Rejected in advance unless A and B both fail: the handoff bit shows that the answer depends on what each maker knows at its own commitment time, which a chain of independent single-maker games does not carry unless every delivered message is modelled as an observation with an arrival instant, and then it is option A under another name |

**2. The per-maker record (DESIGN, for A or B).** `{ "name", "actuators": [...], "observes": [field…],
"timing": {field: arrival instant}, "commits_at": instant }`. The policy class becomes a tuple of causal
policies, one per maker, each a function of that maker's own history at each of its decision instants. Rules:
every actuator is commanded by exactly one maker; a field a maker `observes` must have an arrival instant in
that maker's `timing` (otherwise `FROB-REF-847`: an observation has no arrival instant, or a policy uses
information before it arrives; first repair: the instant at which it becomes usable); a message between makers
is an observation field with a declared capacity and worst delivery time; INV-AUGUR-07 holds per maker (a
policy is never indexed by a quantity absent from that maker's observation contract at the decision instant).
In the canonical form a single-maker query written as `["allocator"]` keeps exactly that spelling.

**3. Byte-stability test.** Corpus S = every `.fmission` fixture stored for M1 to M7 gates (all
single-maker). For each: canonical bytes and `MissionId` under the candidate reader equal the stored ones.
Required: |S| of |S|. One mismatch rules out option A.

**4. The acceptance fixture of the decision: four variants of the handoff bit** (worlds β ∈ {0, 1}; B commits
at instant t_c; success iff ℓ = β).

| Variant | Decision makers and information | Expected answer | Proof kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| pooled | One maker, observes β before t_c | inner | `finite-game` (policy ℓ = β) |
| local | A observes β; B observes nothing that depends on β before t_c | excluded for every admitted policy | `alias-pair` (B's history is common to both worlds; success sets {ℓ = 0} and {ℓ = 1} are disjoint) |
| timely bit | As local, plus a one-bit message from A arriving at B before t_c | inner; capacity and worst delivery time recorded | `finite-game` |
| late bit | The same message arriving after t_c | excluded | `alias-pair` |

**5. Decision rule (DESIGN proposal, fixed before the tests are run).** `A` iff byte-stability holds on all of S
and the four variants are expressible in the extended version 1 and receive the expected answers through the
public verb with bundles the offline auditor accepts. Otherwise `B` iff the variants pass under version 2.
Otherwise `C-or-park`: the multi-robot slice reduces to single-robot missions with priced world edits, which is
also the fallback of the facility kill gate.

**6. Records.** `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-9.md` (option, |S|, mismatches, the four verdicts, the schema change
as a diff); the register row moves to `decided`. Until then the default refusal stays in force and is tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three options and the per-maker record are written as a schema diff against `frob.mission/1`.
- [ ] Byte-stability is measured on the whole stored corpus and reported as matches of |S|.
- [ ] The four variants are run through `frob mission synthesize` under the candidate schema; each answer
      matches the table and its bundle is accepted by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] The ADR exists before any bead of the multi-robot slice starts, signed by the Augur lead or recorded as
      "default at deadline" (in which case the slice does not start).
- [ ] Negative controls: the late-bit variant is excluded; a two-maker document in which B's policy is indexed
      by β is refused; a reader that reorders the keys of a single-maker `policy_class` fails byte-stability.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-spec/tests/decision_makers.rs` and `crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_questions.rs`:
- `oa9_default_refuses_two_makers`: before the ADR, a query with two decision makers is refused with exit 5 and
  a pointer at `policy_class.decision_makers`.
- `oa9_byte_stability`: for the stance query printed in the schema, canonical bytes are identical under the
  current and the candidate reader; a planted key reorder changes the hash and fails the test.
- `oa9_local_choices_enumerated`: the two deterministic local choices of B each win exactly 1 of 2 worlds.
- `oa9_pooled_wins_both`, `oa9_timely_bit_wins_both`, `oa9_late_bit_excluded`.
- `oa9_actuator_has_one_maker`: an actuator named by two makers, or by none, is refused.
- `oa9_rule`: (stable, 4 of 4) → `A`; (1 mismatch, 4 of 4 under version 2) → `B`; (stable, 3 of 4) → not `A`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_augur_9.sh`: runs `frob mission check` on a two-maker query (expects the default refusal
while the question is open), then, with the candidate schema enabled by its feature flag, `frob mission
synthesize` on the four variants and `frob-verify` on each bundle; recomputes `MissionId` for every query in
S. Logs per variant the verdict, proof kinds, capacity and delivery time; per corpus query the two hashes; the
rule's inputs and outcome; a `summary`. Seeds: none (finite enumeration).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-642 (handoff bit: pooled history wins both cases; every local deterministic choice loses one; a timely bit
restores both; negative control: a late bit). KA-643 (shared bus: zero one-tick schedules, two two-tick
schedules) is run as a second two-maker fixture where the makers share a resource. Determinism: verdicts and
hashes identical twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- A schema that accepts several makers but lowers them to one pooled game would be the worst outcome: it
  would certify the pooled answer for the local problem. The local and late-bit variants exist to catch that.
- Everything multi-robot is a Moonshot and off the critical path; if the owner does not fund slice 5
  (OPEN-AUGUR-10), this decision is recorded as "not needed; default stands".
- `MissionId` changes whenever the policy class changes (INV-AUGUR-03); adding a maker to an existing query is
  a new mission, never an amendment of the old answer.
- What would make us revisit after deciding: a fixture in which makers' clocks disagree (a skewed handoff
  clock is one of the facility fixture's four seeded failures).
