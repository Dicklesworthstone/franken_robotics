## Background

Every mechanism in the program carries a tier. A Moonshot [M] mechanism is one that would make the product
unlike anything else and may not work; it sits behind a feature flag and off the critical path. Two program
rules govern Moonshots. **The cap:** at most four [M] items are active at once; an item is active from the
funding of its pilot until its expiry gate, and the others wait in a queue. **Pilot before funding:** no
Frontier or Moonshot work is funded before a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run and the ceiling of the
benefit has been stated; a pilot whose falsifier holds frees the slot and the named fallback ships.

Augur (mission queries over families of designs) has six Moonshot items. OPEN-AUGUR-10 asks for their compute
and calendar envelope; the chapter phrases it for the slices on facility co-design, labels from contact, the
shape-space atlas and symmetry at contact, and the M8 milestone asks the same funding question "per epic", so
this bead records the answer for all six in one place (DESIGN). Owner: the project owner; this is a decision no
measurement inside the program can take. **Fail-closed default: none is funded before its pilot passes.** What
the default costs: nothing on the critical path (no North-Star scenario is tagged M6 or M8); the six items stay
parked with their fallbacks in force.

## Scope

In: the decision brief for the owner (one row per item: pilot, falsifier, ceiling, kill gate, requested
envelope, calendar, queue order); the default at each deadline; where each answer is recorded; the checks, run
on the six Augur items, that pilots and Moonshot experiments pass before they spend anything. Out: running any
pilot or slice; the program-wide queue and its records (NAB-21) and the transitions that write them (N41-09);
the cap and pilot-first predicates (N10a-02, and the pilot register's `fundable`, N38a-09); the envelope
classes, approvals and refusal (N36-20, under the owner decision N21c-35); the kill decisions.

**2. What the owner is asked, per item.** (a) Whether its pilot is funded, which makes the item active and
takes one of the four slots; (b) the compute class its pilot and its slice may use: E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one
hour, one device; the default), E1 (a named gate measurement: both machines, 2 hours per run, 12 device-hours
per gate), E2 (a design campaign: 48 device-hours), E3 (pretraining: 500 device-hours, approved per run);
(c) a calendar limit in weeks, after which an item that has not passed its pilot returns to the queue and
frees its slot; (d) its place in the order. Defaults where the owner says nothing: (a) no; (b) E0; (c) the
planned weeks in the item's short plan; (d) the queue order above, by three rules: an item whose consumers
land at an earlier milestone goes first; within a milestone the cheaper pilot goes first, because a cheap
pilot that fails frees its slot soonest; an item that depends on another waits for it.

**3. Where the answers are recorded: no ledger of its own** (DESIGN, for the reason NAB-21 and N41-09 give:
one file holds the queue, and two would drift; an earlier draft of this bead specified a separate
`registries/moonshot_funding.toml`, which does not exist). The six items are the queue records MQ-09, MQ-10,
MQ-11, MQ-14, MQ-16 and MQ-20 of `registries/ev_matrix.toml` (NAB-21).
- (a) Pilot funding: the transition of N41-09 sets the item's record to `state = active` with `activated_at` and
  the `adr` of this decision (`ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-10-<milestone>`); funding a pilot takes a slot.
- (b) Compute class: an approval of the class in N36-20's approval registry, scoped to the item's epic or gate;
  no approval leaves the item at E0.
- (c) Calendar limit: the weeks, stated in the ADR and the item's short plan; an item that has not passed its
  pilot within them becomes `expired` and frees its slot (NAB-21's rule).
- (d) Order: N41-09's rewrite of `order` (OPEN-PROG-5); this decision's ADR is cited there.
- Pilot results are those of the pilot register (N38a-09), never copied here.

Three checks, applied by this bead's script to the six Augur records and by each Augur Moonshot bead's script
before it spends compute; each calls the predicate that owns it and adds none:
1. *Pilot first.* The slice's work package is funded only when `pilots fundable` (N38a-09) returns `Fundable`
   for it, which needs a passed pilot; for MQ-09, MQ-10 and MQ-11 (`frontier_part_first = true`) the [F] gate of
   the same section must also have a recorded pass (N10a-02 reads that field).
2. *Cap.* The doctrine check D-6 (N10a-02, `moonshot_cap`) counts records with `state = "active"`, program-wide,
   at most four; the pilot register refuses a fifth running Moonshot (`moonshot-cap`); both must agree.
3. *Envelope.* A job whose estimate exceeds the item's approved class is refused before it starts
   (`FROB-REF-761`, N36-20).
An Augur Moonshot bead whose item is not `active`, or whose work package is not `Fundable`, stops with a typed
reason `not funded` and records that as its outcome; an unfunded experiment is never recorded as passed and
never as killed.

**4. Deadlines and defaults.** The question is put at the M5 exit for the M6 items (orders 9, 10; 11 as soon
as a slot is free after M4) and at the M7 exit for the M8 items (14, 16, 20). At each deadline an unanswered
row takes its default, recorded as `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUGUR-10-<milestone>.md` with the capability left parked.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/moonshot_funding.rs`:
- `brief_rows_complete`: six rows; orders {9, 10, 11, 14, 16, 20}; no empty ceiling or fallback.
- `pilot_first` (scratch registries): MQ-14's work package with its pilot still `registered` (not run) is not
  `Fundable`.
- `f_part_first`: MQ-10's slice with GATE-AUGUR-21 `not-run` is refused by N10a-02's predicate.
- `cap_of_four`: four `active` records pass; a fifth activation of an Augur item is refused and names the four
  holders; the pilot register returns `moonshot-cap` for the same case.
- `slot_freed`: an item whose pilot is `falsifier-holds` (N38a-09's status) no longer counts toward the cap.
- `default_at_deadline`: with no owner record at the M5 exit, MQ-09 and MQ-10 stay `queued` and an ADR text is
  produced that names both items and their fallbacks.
- `no_second_ledger`: a file `registries/moonshot_funding.toml` makes the check fail.
- `order_rule`: among two free candidates at one milestone, the one with the cheaper pilot is proposed first.

## Considerations

- "Active from the funding of its pilot" and "none is active before its pilot passes" are both written in the
  plan. This bead reads them as: funding a pilot takes a slot; the slice's own work is funded only after the
  pilot passes. Paper-scale pilots within E0 still take a slot, so that four at once remains a real limit.
- The whole of M6 pauses whenever M4 or M7 is slipping; a paused item keeps its slot only if its calendar
  limit has not run out.
- Breadth starving the spine is a named program risk (more than four Moonshots active is one of its
  signals); the cap check is its mitigation here.
- If the owner funds none of the six, nothing in M0 to M7 changes; every fallback is already the design of
  record.
