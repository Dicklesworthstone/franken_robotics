## Background

Augur is the subsystem that answers questions about sets of robot designs. One of its verbs, `mission.transport`,
moves a certified claim about a skill from a source robot A to a target robot B through a relation R between their
states and a causal map from abstract commands to target commands. The relation used for that is an alternating
simulation: for every admitted target disturbance and every resulting target successor there must be an allowed
source successor related to it. That relation preserves universal claims ("every execution succeeds"). It preserves
only the support of a probability law, so by itself it carries no chance claim and no expected value.

The smallest case that shows this (a counterexample every implementation must reproduce): source and target each
have one action and two outcomes, success and failure. The source succeeds with probability 0.99 and the target
with probability 0.01. Every target successor has a matching source successor, so the relation holds, and the claim
"succeeds with probability at least 0.95" is true of the source and false of the target. Every coupling of the two
laws disagrees on at least 0.98 of its mass, which is their total-variation distance.

The proposed remedy is a coupling budget: per step t of the horizon, a bound εₜ on how far apart the two transition
laws are at related states, with the transported bounds P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ and, for a
quantity in [0, B], |E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ. The mechanism is Frontier [F]. The program rule for Frontier
work is that a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before the mechanism is funded. This bead is that pilot for
the finite part; the second stage on a physical fixture (a tray carried by a wheeled base and by a quadruped) is
N25e-04. Rejected alternative: funding the proposer and checker first and testing afterwards; a single violated
bound would then cost the whole mechanism instead of two days.

## Scope

In: a standalone CPU program with exact rational arithmetic, a seed-explicit generator of 200 finite fixtures, the
exact budget and exact target probability for each, the recorded falsifier outcome, and the slack statistics that
bound the benefit. Out: the product proposer (N25e-02), the bundle and checker (N25e-03), the tray stage (N25e-04),
the kill decision (N25e-06).

## Technical approach

Location (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/pilots/coupling_budget/`, a small binary with no dependency on
`frob-mission`. Arithmetic: exact rationals (reduced integer pairs), so every comparison is exact.

1. Fixture model. A fixture is (S_A, S_B, labels, K_A, K_B, R, T, accept, reward): finite state sets, a labelling
   ℓ_A: S_A → Λ and ℓ_B: S_B → Λ into a shared label set Λ, one transition kernel per step for each system (the
   interface has already selected the command, so there is one action), the relation R = {(x_A, x_B) :
   ℓ_A(x_A) = ℓ_B(x_B)}, a horizon T, an acceptance predicate on label histories, and a reward in [0, B] on
   label histories. Kernel entries are rationals with denominators at most 1000.
2. Generator (seed-explicit, DESIGN): |S_A|, |S_B| ∈ [3, 8], |Λ| ∈ [2, min sizes], T ∈ [1, 12]; the target's
   label-level kernel is the source's label-level kernel perturbed by a per-step size drawn from
   {0, 1/1000, 3/1000, 1/100, 1/20, 3/10}, then renormalized exactly. Fixture 0 is the two-outcome reversal
   (0.99 against 0.01). Fixture 1 is a chain of 10 steps with ε = 3/1000 per step and a source acceptance
   probability of 99/100. The other 198 are generated from seeds 2 to 199.
3. Exact budget. For a related pair (x_A, x_B) at step t, let p and q be the laws of the successor's label
   under K_A and K_B. Because R is equality of labels, the least mass any coupling of the two successor laws
   places on unrelated pairs equals the total-variation distance TV(p, q) = ½·Σ_λ |p(λ) − q(λ)|. Set
   εₜ = max over all related pairs at step t of TV(p, q). The maximum is over every related pair, reachable or
   not, which is sound and slightly conservative.
4. Soundness argument being piloted. Build the step-wise maximal coupling. The probability that the coupled
   pair leaves R at some step is at most Σₜ εₜ (a union bound). While the pair stays in R the label histories
   are equal, so acceptance and reward agree. Hence |P_B(accept) − P_A(accept)| ≤ Σₜ εₜ and
   |E_B − E_A| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ. The argument needs acceptance and reward to be functions of the label history; the
   generator enforces that, and a fixture that violates it is a negative control (step 7).
5. Exact truth. P_A(accept), P_B(accept), E_A, E_B by forward propagation of the exact state distributions
   over the horizon (enumeration; state counts are tiny).
6. Record per fixture: Σₜ εₜ, the transported lower bound LB = P_A(accept) − Σₜ εₜ, the exact P_B(accept), the
   slack P_B(accept) − LB, and the same for the expectation bound. A violation is LB > P_B(accept) or
   |E_B − E_A| > B·Σₜ εₜ.
7. Negative controls run in the same program: (a) a mutant that takes the mean of TV over related pairs
   instead of the maximum; (b) a mutant that marks the claim universal and transports 0.99 unchanged on
   fixture 0; (c) a fixture whose acceptance predicate reads a state feature that R does not preserve. Each
   must produce at least one violated bound, which shows the falsifier can fire.
8. Output: `pilot_coupling_budget.json` with one row per fixture and a summary; a short report
   `docs/pilots/coupling_budget.md` stating the falsifier outcome, the distribution of slack, and the share of
   fixtures whose budget is smaller than a claim slack of 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05 (the ceiling evidence).

Falsifier (fixed before the run): one transported bound that the exact target value violates. Ceiling, stated
before any benefit is claimed: the budget helps only when Σ ε is small against the slack of the claim; it saves one
qualification study per transported skill and no simulation time.

Determinism class D0 (exact arithmetic, seeds explicit). Evidence class of the pilot's statements: Tested for "no
violation on 200 fixtures"; the pilot proves nothing general.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The program runs from a clean checkout with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin pilot-coupling-budget -- --seeds 0..200` in under 60 s on one core (TARGET) and writes the JSON and the report.
- [ ] Fixture 0 reports Σ ε = 49/50 (0.98), a transported bound of 1/100 (0.01), and the claim "at least 0.95" not transported.
- [ ] Fixture 1 reports Σ ε = 3/100, a transported bound of 24/25 (0.96) from a source probability of 99/100, and an exact target value at or above 0.96.
- [ ] Zero violated bounds over the 200 fixtures, for the chance bound and for the expectation bound; the count is in the summary line.
- [ ] Each of the three negative controls produces at least one violation and is reported as caught.
- [ ] The report states the falsifier, its outcome, the ceiling, and the first reading of KILL-COUPLING (N25e-06): "no violated bound" or the violating fixture with its seed.
- [ ] Two runs on one machine and a run on the second host give byte-identical JSON.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/pilots/coupling_budget/tests.rs`: `tv_two_outcome` (TV of (99/100, 1/100) and
(1/100, 99/100) is 49/50); `tv_identical_is_zero`; `chain_ten_steps_budget` (ten steps at 3/1000 give 3/100);
`bound_is_never_above_truth_on_seed_grid` (property test over seeds 0..200); `mean_instead_of_max_is_caught`
(planted fixture: two related target states, one rarely visited with TV 1/2 and one with TV 0; the mean mutant
reports 1/4 per step and its bound exceeds the exact target value); `universal_label_is_caught` (fixture 0);
`unpreserved_feature_is_caught`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_pilot_coupling_budget.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E, runs the binary with the seed
range, and asserts the criteria above. It writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_pilot_coupling_budget/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript beside it. Each line carries
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-01`), `step`, `event`, the command line, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines carry
the fixture seed, Σ ε, the bound, the exact value and the slack as exact strings; on a violation the line carries
the seed and the path of the fixture dump. The final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and names
GATE-AUGUR-25 (pilot reading).

### Known answers and negative controls

Two-outcome reversal: Σ ε = 0.98, bound 0.01, refused. Ten-step chain: 0.99 − 10 × 0.003 = 0.96, exact target
above it. Negative controls: the three mutants of step 7. Metamorphic relations: relabelling states leaves every
number unchanged; appending a step with identical kernels adds zero to the budget; scaling the reward by c scales
the expectation bound by c. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine, debug and release, both hosts (class D0).

## Considerations

- A pass here licenses the mechanism bead, not a claim about robots. The tray stage (N25e-04) is where the
  budget is measured on physics, and where it may well be too large to help.
- The generator's label-equality relation is the easy case. Relations that are not functions of a label need a
  transport problem per pair; N25e-02 handles them with an explicit coupling witness, and its own tests cover it.
- If the pilot finds a violation, the first action is one repair cycle on the argument (step 4), not on the
  fixtures; a violation that survives is the first trigger of KILL-COUPLING.
