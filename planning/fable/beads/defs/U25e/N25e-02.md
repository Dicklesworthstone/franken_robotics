## Background

`mission.transport` (crate `frob-mission`, subsystem Augur) moves a certified claim from a source robot to a target
robot through a relation R(x_A, x_B) and a causal map from abstract commands to target commands. The relation is an
alternating simulation: every possible successor of the target is matched to an allowed successor of the source. It
preserves universal claims. It preserves only the support of a probability law, so it carries no chance claim and no
expected value: with one action and two outcomes, a source that succeeds with probability 0.99 and a target that
succeeds with probability 0.01 satisfy the relation, and "succeeds with probability at least 0.95" is true of the
source and false of the target. Their total-variation distance is 0.98.

This bead restricts the verb and supplies the proof object that lifts the restriction. DESIGN: a transport
certificate declares the kind of each transported claim (`universal`, `chance`, `expectation`); a chance or
expectation claim crosses only with a coupling budget, and the transported value is the source value degraded by
that budget. Tier Frontier [F]; the pilot N25e-01 must have run without a violated bound before this work starts.
Rejected alternatives: (1) transporting chance claims through the simulation relation alone (unsound, by the case
above); (2) forbidding chance claims outright (that is the kill-gate fallback, kept in reserve, because a small
budget can save a whole qualification study per transported skill); (3) a per-claim correction factor fitted from
target rollouts (it is an estimate presented as a transported proof).

## Scope

In: the claim-kind field, the coupling-budget proof object for finite kernels, the transported bounds, the verb's
output fields, the refusal of an unbudgeted chance claim on the proposer side, ledger records. Out: the bundle
payload and the checking rules (N25e-03); budgets measured from sealed paired runs and the tray fixture (N25e-04);
the relation and refinement map themselves (the transport epic, BD-436).

## Technical approach

Types (DESIGN, in `crates/frob-mission/src/transport/claims.rs`; plain proof objects with types from `frob-core`):

```rust
pub enum TransportedClaimKind { Universal, Chance, Expectation }
pub struct TransportedClaim { id: ClaimRef, kind: TransportedClaimKind, statement: ClaimStatement,
    source_value: Ratio,              // P_source(accept) lower bound, or E_source
    range: Option<(Ratio, Ratio)>,    // [0, B] for an expectation claim
    threshold: Option<Ratio>,         // the requirement the claim must still meet on the target
    budget: Option<CouplingBudget>, transported: Option<TransportedBound> }
pub struct CouplingBudget { per_step: Vec<(u32, Ratio)>,   // (t, εₜ)
    total: Ratio, source: BudgetSource, class: EvidenceClass, conditions_on: Vec<InstantRef> }
pub enum BudgetSource { ExactFiniteKernel { couplings: Vec<CouplingWitness> },
                        CommonNoisePairedRuns { study: ReceiptId, n: u64, k: u64, level: Ratio },  // N25e-04
                        None }
pub struct CouplingWitness { t: u32, pair: (StateRef, StateRef), command: CommandRef,
    gamma: Vec<(OutcomeRef, OutcomeRef, Ratio)> }          // joint mass on (source outcome, target outcome)
pub struct TransportedBound { lower: Ratio, upper: Option<Ratio>, slack_to_threshold: Option<Ratio> }
```

Definition of the budget. At step t, for every related pair (x_A, x_B) ∈ Rₜ and every abstract command a admitted
at x_A, let u be the target command the interface selects for a (using only information available before
disturbances act). Let p be the source law of (successor, observation) under a and q the target law under u. A
coupling γ of p and q is a joint law with marginals p and q. Its mismatch is the mass γ places on pairs whose
successors are not related by Rₜ₊₁ or whose observations differ. εₜ is an upper bound on the supremum, over related
pairs and admitted commands, of the least mismatch over couplings. When R is equality of a label, the least mismatch
is the total-variation distance ½·Σ |p − q| on labels.

Algorithm for finite kernels (class `Certified`):
1. Enumerate related pairs and admitted commands per step. A fixture beyond the declared caps (DESIGN: 4,096
   pairs per step, 64 steps) is not budgeted exactly and falls to step 6.
2. For each, compute a maximal coupling exactly. With a label relation: γ(λ, λ) = min(p(λ), q(λ)), remaining mass
   paired arbitrarily. In general: scale p and q by their common denominator to integers and solve a bipartite
   maximum flow from source outcomes to target outcomes along related pairs (integer capacities, exact); the
   flow is the related mass and the rest is mismatch.
3. εₜ = the maximum mismatch at step t; `total` = Σₜ εₜ, exact.
4. Transported bounds. Chance: P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ. Expectation of a quantity in [0, B]:
   |E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ. Reason: under the step-wise coupling the pair leaves R with probability at
   most Σₜ εₜ, and while it stays in R observations and task outputs agree. A lower bound below 0 is reported
   as 0 with the flag `vacuous`.
5. Decision per claim: if `threshold` is given and the transported lower bound is below it, the claim is not
   transported; the answer says so with the slack, and `next_actions` proposes measuring the claim on the
   target with `eval.qualify`. Nothing converts this into an exclusion of the target design.
6. No budget available (kernels not finite, no paired study): the budget source is `None`, the claim is not
   transported, and the verb says the claim must be measured on the target.
7. Causality. The coupling at step t is keyed by (t, related pair, command) only. `conditions_on` lists the
   instants whose information the interface map uses; any dependence on a disturbance realized at or after the
   command instant is a refusal in the proposer and a rejection in the checker.
8. Universal claims cross unchanged under the relation, with no budget.

Verb surface. `frob mission transport <skill-or-answer> --to <design> --json` gains `result.claims[]`: `id`, `kind`,
`source_value`, `budget {total, per_step, source, class}`, `transported {lower, upper}`, `threshold`, `verdict`
(`transported`, `not-transported`, `universal`), and `evidence.class` per claim. The answer's class is the weakest
among its claims (no operation raises a class). A request that supplies a certificate marking a chance claim
`universal`, or that asks to transport a chance claim with the budget omitted, is refused: "chance claim transported
without a coupling budget". The refusal has no number in the registry yet; until N25e-45 allocates one it is
emitted as `FROB-REF-852` (a transport that lacks a side condition) with `detail: coupling-budget-missing`; exit
code 3; ranked repairs: (1) supply or compute the budget, (2) re-declare the claim kind, (3) measure on the target.

Ledger: one row per transported claim with the claim identity, kind, Σ ε, source of the budget, class, and the
bundle hash. Determinism class D0 for exact budgets.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `mission.transport` on the two-outcome fixture returns Σ ε = 49/50, a transported bound of 1/100, and `not-transported` for the claim "at least 0.95", with `eval.qualify` proposed.
- [ ] On the ten-step chain (ε = 3/1000 per step, source 99/100) the bound is 24/25 and the claim "at least 0.95" is `transported` with class `Certified`.
- [ ] A universal claim on the same fixtures crosses with no budget and is labelled `universal`.
- [ ] A chance claim submitted as `universal`, and one submitted with no budget, are refused with exit 3 and the repairs above; the refusal names the claim.
- [ ] A coupling that conditions on a disturbance after the command instant is refused by the proposer.
- [ ] Expectation claims return a two-sided interval of half-width B·Σ ε; the vacuous flag appears when the lower bound would be negative.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json`, accepts `--estimate`, drains on cancellation (computed budgets are kept, in-flight pairs discarded), and writes the ledger row.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-mission` documents the claim kinds, the budget and the refusal.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/transport_claims.rs`: `label_coupling_is_tv` (maximal coupling mismatch equals ½·Σ|p − q|
on random rational laws); `general_relation_max_flow` (a relation that is not a function of a label: three source
and three target outcomes with related pairs {(1,1), (1,2), (2,2), (3,3)}, p = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4), q = (1/4, 1/2, 1/4):
mismatch 0); `two_outcome_reversal_refused`; `chain_bound_24_25`; `expectation_interval` (B = 10, Σ ε = 3/100 gives
half-width 3/10); `vacuous_flag`; `noncausal_coupling_refused`; `class_is_weakest` (a `Tested` source value yields a
`Tested` transported claim); property test `bound_never_above_exact_target` over the generator of N25e-01 (INV:
transported lower bound ≤ exact target probability).

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/augur_coupling_transport.sh` (N25e-05), which drives `frob mission transport` and
`frob-verify` on the 200 fixtures with the structured log of STD-E2E.

### Known answers and negative controls

Two-outcome case: Σ ε = 0.98, bound 0.01, refused. Ten-step chain: 0.96 with the exact target above it. Negative
controls: the chance-as-universal request; the missing budget; the mean-instead-of-maximum mutant (a planted
proposer defect that the property test must catch). No Appendix M number exists for these rows yet. Determinism
lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; across worker counts (pairs are enumerated in sorted order and
reduced by exact maximum, so the result is order-independent).

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-COUPLING (N25e-06): one violated transported bound that survives a repair cycle, or no skill on
  the tray fixture with a budget below its slack. Fallback: `mission.transport` carries universal claims only, and
  chance and expectation claims are re-measured on the target in the blinded qualification lane.
- Ceiling: the budget helps only when Σ ε is small against the claim's slack. It saves one qualification study per
  transported skill and no simulation time.
- A relation over continuous states needs a finite abstraction before step 1; without one the exact route does
  not apply and the paired-run route of N25e-04 is the only source.
- Multi-stage missions: budgets add across stages, and the stage rule of chance constraints still applies
  (marginal stage rates are never multiplied without a dependence assumption, `FROB-REF-846`).
- The plan names no registry row for the budget; N25e-03 carries it as a typed block with its own rules.
