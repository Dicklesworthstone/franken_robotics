## Background

A mission answer leaves the proposer (`frob-mission`) as plain proof objects. `frob-lemma` serializes them into a
`.frobcert` bundle, and `frob-cert` (binary `frob-verify`) re-checks the bundle with the standard library alone, on
a machine with no GPU, no simulator and no access to the proposer. The two share a format and no code (ADR-AUGUR-05:
a proposer and a checker that share a mistranslated model agree on the wrong answer).

A claim transported from a source robot to a target robot through a simulation relation is sound for universal
claims and unsound for chance claims: a source that succeeds with probability 0.99 and a target that succeeds with
probability 0.01 satisfy the relation, and "at least 0.95" is true of one and false of the other. The rule that
repairs this (DESIGN): a transport certificate declares the kind of each transported claim, `universal`, `chance` or
`expectation`; a chance or expectation claim carries a coupling budget, a per-step bound εₜ on the mismatch between
the source and target laws at related states, and the transported bounds are P_target ≥ P_source − Σₜ εₜ and
|E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ for a quantity in [0, B]. A certificate that marks a chance claim `universal`, or
omits the budget, is rejected by `frob-verify`.

This bead is the checker's side and the writer's side of that rule. The plan adds no row to the closed registry of
proof kinds for the budget. DESIGN, with its reason: the budget is a typed block of the bundle that accompanies a
transported claim, checked by rules of its own area in the rule catalogue, so no row is added to the registry of
kinds (shape 2A of N12x-07, the default). The registry revision still increments: each kind whose row admits the
block (`monotone-transport`, `symmetry-transport`, `composition`) gets a `kind-version` change, recorded in the
revision ledger of N12x-08 (`registries/cert-kinds-revisions.toml`) under the slug `transport-block`. Rejected
alternative: a new proof kind `coupling-budget` (shape 2B: an `add-kind` revision for what is a side condition of
an existing claim, with two bundles under one root).

## Scope

In: the `transport` block of the bundle schema, its writer in `frob-lemma`, the rules of area `transport` in
`frob-cert`, the `kind-version` entry `transport-block` in the revision ledger, accepted specimens and rejected
mutations. Out: computing budgets (N25e-02, N25e-04); the relation and refinement map of the transport
certificate itself (N25c-03, M8) and whether refinement proofs get a kind (N12x-07, question 1); the ruling on the
block's shape (N12x-07, question 2, which depends on this bead); the revision ledger's mechanics (N12x-08); the
generic container, schema and dispatch rules (N12-02, N12-03).

## Technical approach

Bundle block (DESIGN; canonical JSON, exact rationals as reduced "p/q" strings):

```json
"transport": { "source_bundle": "b3:…", "relation": { "kind": "label-equality", "labels_source": [...],
               "labels_target": [...] },
  "claims": [ { "id": "k0", "kind": "chance", "source_value": "99/100", "threshold": "19/20",
                "budget": { "source": "exact-finite-kernel", "per_step": [["0", "3/1000"], …], "total": "3/100",
                            "kernels": { "source": [...], "target": [...] },
                            "couplings": [ { "t": 0, "pair": ["a2", "b5"], "command": "c0",
                                             "gamma": [["s1", "t1", "1/4"], …] } ],
                            "conditions_on": ["decision"] },
                "transported": { "lower": "24/25" }, "expects": "transported" } ] }
```

Writer (`crates/frob-lemma/src/bundle/transport.rs`): maps the proof objects of N25e-02 to this block field by
field; no arithmetic, no defaults; a claim with no kind is a writer error, never a silent `universal`.

Checker rules (`crates/frob-cert/src/rules/transport.rs`; each returns the first failing rule):
1. `transport.claim-kind`: every claim has a kind from the closed set.
2. `transport.chance-as-universal`: the checker derives the kind from the embedded statement, not from the label.
   A statement whose quantifier prefix contains a `prob` binder is a chance claim; one whose objective or
   predicate is an expected value is an expectation claim. A derived kind that disagrees with the declared kind
   is rejected.
3. `transport.budget-missing`: a `chance` or `expectation` claim without a `budget`, or with `source: none` and
   `expects: transported`, is rejected.
4. `transport.coupling-marginals`: for an exact budget, each coupling's row sums equal the source law and its
   column sums equal the target law, both recomputed from the kernels in the bundle at the named pair and
   command; every related pair and admitted command at every step has a coupling (coverage is recomputed from
   the relation).
5. `transport.coupling-mismatch`: the mass each coupling places on unrelated or observation-unequal pairs is
   recomputed and must be at most the declared εₜ of its step. The checker verifies a witness; it never solves
   for an optimal coupling.
6. `transport.sum`: `total` equals Σₜ εₜ exactly; the transported lower bound equals source value minus total
   (chance), or the interval has half-width B·total (expectation); a declared bound tighter than the recomputed
   one is rejected.
7. `transport.causal`: every coupling is keyed by (t, pair, command) only, and every instant in `conditions_on`
   precedes the command instant in the bundle's clock definitions.
8. `transport.class`: an exact budget supports `Certified`; a budget whose source is `paired-runs` is a
   `sample-coverage` sub-certificate and caps the claim at `Tested`. For it the checker recomputes validity of
   the stated one-sided upper bound p̄ from the counts (n pairs, k relation breaks, level α): it verifies
   Σ_{i=0..k} C(n, i)·p̄ⁱ·(1 − p̄)ⁿ⁻ⁱ ≤ α in exact rationals. It does not attest that the pairs ran.
9. `transport.threshold`: `expects: transported` with a transported lower bound below the threshold is rejected.
10. Size limits: integers, entries per coupling and pairs per step are capped; a block beyond the caps is refused
    (`FROB-REF-849`), never approximated.

Refusal mapping, applied by `mission.transport` when a caller-built certificate (`--certificate`) fails a rule
before use (BD-133's rule-to-refusal mapping; `frob-verify` and `prove.verify` report the failing rule under the
Lemma codes only, as VERBS-prove states): rules 2 and 3 are the refusal "chance claim transported without a
coupling budget", row 1 of N25e-45 (slug `coupling-budget-missing`, `held` in the allocation ledger), raised
under its interim code `FROB-REF-852` with `detail: coupling-budget-missing`; rules 4 to 6 and 9 map to
`FROB-REF-849`/`-850` as arithmetic or field mismatches; rule 7 to `FROB-REF-847` (information used before it
arrives, the code the proposer raises for the same coupling, N25e-02); rule 8 to `FROB-REF-834` (a result
presented above the class of its weakest input). Exit 3 in every case. Determinism D0: the same bundle gives the
same verdict and the same first failing rule on every machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-verify` accepts the ten-step chain specimen (total 3/100, transported 24/25, threshold 19/20).
- [ ] `frob-verify` rejects the two-outcome reversal offered as transported with threshold 19/20 (rule 9), the same claim relabelled `universal` (rule 2), and the same claim with the budget removed (rule 3).
- [ ] Each of rules 4 to 8 has at least one rejected mutation with the rule named: a coupling entry changed by 1/1000; a missing coupling for one related pair; εₜ understated; total understated; a coupling conditioned on a later instant; a paired-runs budget claiming `Certified`; an upper bound p̄ too small for its counts.
- [ ] `frob-cert` still has an empty dependency table; the writer and the reader share no source file.
- [ ] The block round-trips: writer output re-serialized by the reader is byte-identical (`transport_block_roundtrip` in `frob-conformance`).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/cert_transport_block.sh` passes: every manifest entry gives its expected verdict and rule under the standalone `frob-verify`, and the chain fixture's bundle is accepted in and out of tree.
- [ ] The specimens and mutations are placed in `crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/transport/` with the manifest the auditor script reads, at least 200 seeded mutations for the checker-independence campaign (the script of BD-294 takes them, under the admitting kinds, when it is re-run after this bead lands at M4).
- [ ] The revision ledger holds the `kind-version` entry `transport-block` for each admitting kind, and a bundle with a `transport` block under a kind that does not admit it is rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-cert/tests/transport_rules.rs`: `chain_specimen_accepts`; `reversal_rejected_threshold`;
`chance_marked_universal_rejected`; `budget_missing_rejected`; `marginal_off_by_one_thousandth_rejected`;
`uncovered_pair_rejected`; `epsilon_understated_rejected`; `sum_understated_rejected`; `noncausal_rejected`;
`paired_runs_cap_tested`; `clopper_pearson_validity` (n = 74, k = 0, α = 1/20: p̄ = 1/25 accepted because
(24/25)⁷⁴ ≤ 1/20; p̄ = 3/100 rejected because (97/100)⁷⁴ > 1/20); `size_cap_refused`;
`block_under_nonadmitting_kind_rejected` (the chain specimen's block attached to an `lp-primal` proof, whose row
does not list it). Property test: for random
valid blocks from the generator of N25e-01, mutate one field at random and assert rejection or a still-valid block
as judged by the independent reference in `frob-conformance` (an equivalent mutant is logged, not counted).
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/transport_block_roundtrip.rs` (in `frob-conformance`, which may depend on both
crates, so that `frob-cert`'s dependency table stays empty): `transport_block_roundtrip` (the writer's block for
the chain specimen and for every accepted specimen of the manifest, parsed by `frob-cert`'s reader and
re-serialized, is byte-identical to the writer's output; a block with one field reordered by hand is not, and the
canonical-JSON rule names it).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/cert_transport_block.sh` (this bead's own, built on its dependencies only; header `bead=N25e-03
milestone=M4 gates=GATE-AUGUR-25`). Steps: (1) `cargo test -p frob-cert --test transport_rules` and `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test transport_block_roundtrip`, both exit 0; (2) `cargo tree -p frob-cert --edges
normal,build` prints one line; (3) for every entry of the manifest in `crates/frob-cert/tests/specimens/transport/`,
the standalone `frob-verify <file> --json` under `env -i`: the verdict and the first failing rule equal the
manifest's (accept for the chain specimen; rules 2, 3 and 9 for the reversal cases; rules 4 to 8 and the size cap
for the mutations); (4) the chain fixture of N25e-02 (`fixtures/augur/transport-claims/chain10/`) end to end:
`frob mission transport chain10/claims.fmission --from chain10/source.fkernel.json --to
chain10/target.fkernel.json --relation chain10/relation.json --claims all --key ctb-<run>-chain --json`, then
`frob prove bundle a:<answer> --out <dir> --key ctb-<run>-bundle --json`, then `frob-verify
<dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json` under `env -i` and `frob prove verify <dir>/<bundle>.frobcert --json`: both
`accept`, the block's `total` "3/100" and `transported.lower` "24/25". It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/cert_transport_block/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-03`),
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block; per file the bundle
digest, the expected and observed verdict and rule; the closure line count against 1; a `summary` with assertions
passed and failed. The gate script `scripts/e2e/augur_coupling_transport.sh` (N25e-05) runs `frob-verify` on the
200 generated fixtures and may run this script as a stage; this script records no gate value.

## Considerations

- OPEN, ruled by N12x-07 (owner: Lemma lead), which depends on this bead and decides after it lands shape 2A:
  whether the budget stays a block or becomes its own kind. Fail-closed default until the ruling: a bundle with a
  `transport` block under a kind whose registry row does not admit one is rejected. If the ruling is 2B, the kind
  and its checker are a new bead and this block is retired in the same ledger revision.
- A false certificate here has the standing consequences: the rule set is disabled until the cause is found,
  stored bundles that used it are withdrawn, and the counterexample joins the mutation corpus
  (KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT).
- Exact binomial tails with n in the thousands produce large integers; the size limits apply and p̄ should be
  written with a short denominator.
