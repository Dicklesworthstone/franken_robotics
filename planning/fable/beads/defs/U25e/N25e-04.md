## Background

A chance claim ("the tray skill succeeds with probability at least 0.95") crosses from a source robot to a target
robot only with a coupling budget: a bound on how often the two closed loops, driven as similarly as physics allows,
stop being related. With the budget Σₜ εₜ the transported bound is P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ.
N25e-02 computes the budget exactly for finite kernels. Real robots have no finite kernel. This bead supplies the
second source of a budget, in order of strength after the exact one (DESIGN): a common-noise construction in which
both systems are driven by one disturbance stream compiled at physical coordinates, the relation is shown to
persist, and its failure rate is bounded from sealed paired runs. Its class is `Tested`: a one-sided upper
confidence bound, not a proof. Where neither source exists there is no budget, and the claim is measured on the
target with the blinded qualification verb `eval.qualify`.

"Compiled at physical coordinates" is the rule of the product-system construction (C-40): shared inputs are one
space-indexed material field read at each robot's actual contact point, and one latent state (lighting, payload
shift) seen by each robot from where it is. Pairing by seed and tick is not that: two bodies on different paths
over one floor do not meet the same friction at the same tick.

The fixture is the tray of the transport chapter: a tray carried by a wheeled base (source) and by a quadruped
(target). Only the tray stabilization objective and its feedback state are ported, and only when the target's
reachable wrench set at the tray interface covers the requested one. At a ramp crest the target may lose lateral
authority; that loss is planted and must show up as relation breaks. Tier [F] as a mechanism; the fixture sits in
the Moonshot transport epic, so this bead is at M8 and off the critical path. Rejected alternative: estimating εₜ
per step and summing (a per-episode break count bounds the sum directly and needs no independence between steps).

## Scope

In: the paired-run protocol, the relation monitor, the upper bound and its sample-size arithmetic, the

pre-registered three-treatment tray experiment of GATE-AUGUR-23 (N25c-04); the kill decision (N25e-06); the
checker rule for the bound (N25e-03, rule 8).

## Technical approach

1. Sealed pairs. A paired run is one world address executed by the source closed loop and by the target closed
   loop under the interface map, with every declared shared disturbance drawn from one stream keyed by physical
   coordinates or absolute time (floor friction by tile, tray payload offset by trial, pushes by wall-clock
   instant). The addresses come from the blinded qualification lane: clusters the development of either
   controller never read. A pair whose address appears in either lineage is refused (`FROB-REF-843`).
2. Relation monitor. A streaming monitor evaluates R at every control tick on the pair: tray pose and velocity
   differences inside the relation's tolerance, interface wrench inside the covered set, equal task outputs,
   equal observation packets at the interface. The first tick at which R fails is a relation break; the pair
   is then labelled `broken` and its remaining ticks are not used. A numerical failure of either run is
   `unresolved`, counted as a break for the bound (conservative) and reported separately.
3. Bound. With n sealed pairs and k breaks, the budget for the whole horizon is the one-sided upper confidence
   bound p̄ at level α on the per-episode break probability (exact binomial, Clopper–Pearson): the largest p
   with Σ_{i ≤ k} C(n, i)·pⁱ·(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ⁱ ≥ α, rounded up to a short rational. With k = 0, p̄ = 1 − α^(1/n). At
   α = 0.05: n = 59 gives 0.0495; n = 74 gives 0.0397; n = 299 gives 0.00997; n = 2,995 gives 0.0010. The
   budget is Σₜ εₜ ≤ p̄ because a break at any step is counted once per episode.
4. Fixed-n only. n is declared before the first pair runs, with the reachability pre-analysis: the smallest
   budget a clean study can return is 1 − α^(1/n), and a study that cannot reach below the smallest claim slack
   is refused before it starts. No peeking; an anytime-valid variant uses a confidence sequence and is declared
   as such, never mixed.
5. Claim slack. For a transported skill with source claim P_source(accept) ≥ p_s (with its own class) and
   requirement p_req on the target, slack = p_s − p_req. The claim is transported when p̄ < slack, with
   transported bound p_s − p̄ and class `Tested` (the weakest of the source's class and `Tested`).
6. Output. `mission.transport --coupling-source paired-runs --pairs n --level 0.05` (options added by this bead
   and registered on VERBS-mission's `mission.transport` row from M8; DESIGN spelling: `budget` is the generated
   compute budget of every run verb, `--budget cpu_core_s=60`, so the coupling budget's source has its own option,
   which selects the variant `CommonNoisePairedRuns` of N25e-02's `BudgetSource`) returns per skill: n, k, p̄, slack,
   the verdict, the study receipt, and the list of broken pairs as replay addresses with the tick and the
   contact mode at the break. The bundle's `transport` block carries `source: paired-runs` with the counts.
7. Tray measurement. Run the protocol for each ported tray skill (level carry, ramp ascent, ramp crest), with
   the ramp-crest loss of authority planted. Record p̄ and slack per skill.

Ceiling, stated before the run: the budget helps only when p̄ is small against the slack; it saves one
qualification study per transported skill and no simulation time (the n pairs cost about what a small
qualification study costs, so the saving exists only when one paired study serves several claims of the skill).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol refuses a study whose smallest reachable budget exceeds every declared claim slack, with the reachable values attached.
- [ ] Shared disturbances are verified shared: for each declared shared field, the values read by the two runs at equal physical coordinates are bit-identical (asserted per pair).
- [ ] The planted ramp-crest loss of authority produces relation breaks, reported with their contact mode; a mutant monitor that ignores lateral wrench reports zero breaks there and fails the test.
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-25, tray part (TARGET, M8): on the tray fixture the 95% upper bound of the measured budget is below the claim's slack for at least one skill, or KILL-COUPLING fires. The measured p̄ and slack per skill are recorded in `registries/gates.toml`.
- [ ] Every transported bound is confirmed or contradicted by a sealed target-only qualification run of the same claim; one contradiction is the first trigger of KILL-COUPLING.
- [ ] Budgets from paired runs are never labelled above `Tested`; `frob-verify` recomputes the bound from the counts.
- [ ] Cancellation drains: completed pairs are kept, in-flight pairs discarded, and a study short of n returns no budget.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/transport_paired.rs`: `cp_upper_zero_breaks` (n = 59, α = 1/20 gives p̄ in
[0.0494, 0.0496]); `cp_upper_with_breaks` (n = 100, k = 2: p̄ = 0.0616 to three digits); `reachability_refusal`
(n = 20 against a slack of 0.04 is refused: 1 − 0.05^(1/20) = 0.139); `break_is_first_tick`;
`numerical_failure_counts_as_break`; `lineage_overlap_refused`; `shared_field_bit_identical` on a synthetic
two-body fixture where one body leads the other by half a tile; `tick_keyed_stream_detected` (a planted stream
keyed by tick makes the shared-field assertion fail).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_coupling_tray.sh`: runs `frob mission transport tray-carry.fskill --from d:wheeled-base --to
d:quad-a --interface tray.json --coupling-source paired-runs --pairs 299 --level 0.05 --key act-<skill>-<seed> --json` per
skill (the base spelling of N25e-02 and N25c-02, one key per skill and seed), then `frob eval qualify <target> --task
<task> --claim '<claim>' --key act-qual-<skill>-<seed> --json` for the same claim on the target (a key distinct
from the transport call's), then `frob-verify` on the bundle; the planted controls (the tick-keyed stream, the
lateral-blind monitor, the per-pair re-read) run as builds with their mutant features and must fail. Asserts the criteria above. Logs to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_coupling_tray/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N25e-04`), `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, n, k, p̄, slack
with units and thresholds, the study receipt, and on failure the first broken pair's replay address. Logs carry no
private-scene data. The `summary` line names GATE-AUGUR-25 (tray part).

### Known answers and negative controls

Arithmetic known answers: 1 − 0.05^(1/n) for n = 59, 74, 299. A synthetic paired fixture with a planted break
probability of 1/10 (class `synthetic`): over 200 repetitions of n = 100 the bound covers 1/10 in at least 95% of
repetitions. Negative controls: the tick-keyed stream; the lateral-blind monitor; a study re-read after each pair
(must be refused as mixing study types). Determinism lanes: the pair set and the counts are identical twice on one
machine and across worker counts; across backends the counts agree within the decision-level equivalence class.

## Considerations

- KILL-COUPLING (N25e-06): no skill on the tray fixture with a budget below its slack, or one violated bound that
  survives a repair cycle. Fallback: universal claims only; chance and expectation claims are re-measured on the
  target in the qualification lane.
- This bead exists only on the branch where the first clause did not fire at M4 (N25ex-01, a dependency). If
  N25ex-01 records `fired at M4`, this bead is closed `not funded` with the ADR cited, and N25e-06 closes on the
  same ADR.
- A relation with loose tolerances rarely breaks and proves little: the tolerances must be tight enough that R
  implies equal acceptance, and that implication is part of the transport certificate.
- Contact chaos can make breaks frequent even for similar bodies; then p̄ is large and the mechanism correctly
  declines to transport.
- Compute: 299 pairs of 20 s episodes on the referee per skill; the envelope is requested with the M8 epic and
  nothing in M0 to M7 depends on it.
