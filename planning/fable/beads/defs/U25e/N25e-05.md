## Background

GATE-AUGUR-25 is the gate of the rule "chance claims transport only through a coupling". A claim that holds with
some probability on a source robot crosses to a target robot through `mission.transport` only with a coupling
budget Σₜ εₜ, and the transported bound is the source probability minus that budget. The gate (claim: Known answer;
thresholds are TARGET) reads: zero violated bounds over 200 finite Markov fixtures with known kernels; the
two-outcome reversal (source success 0.99, target 0.01) is refused; on the tray fixture the 95% upper bound of the
measured budget is below the claim's slack for at least one skill, or the kill gate fires. The finite part is due at
M4 and the tray part at M8. This bead is the end-to-end script for the finite part: it drives the real `frob`
binary and the real offline auditor on the fixtures and records the gate. The pilot N25e-01 ran the same fixtures
through a standalone program; this script runs them through the product, so a defect in lowering, serialization or
checking shows up here and not in the pilot.

A gate that nothing can fail validates nothing, so the script also runs planted failures: the two certificates the
rule names as rejected (a chance claim marked `universal`; a chance claim with the budget omitted) and a proposer
mutant.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/augur_coupling_transport.sh`, the fixture export from the generator, the assertions, the gate
record for the finite part. Out: the tray part (N25e-04); the kill decision (N25e-06).

## Technical approach

Fixtures: the generator of N25e-01 exports each of the 200 fixtures as a pair of finite models, a relation, a
horizon and a claim, in the input form `mission.transport` accepts (`fixtures/augur/coupling/<seed>/`). The exact
target probability computed by enumeration is stored beside each fixture as the pinned truth and is never
regenerated from the candidate implementation.

Steps (plain shell, seed-explicit, idempotent):
1. `start`: commit, host, seed range 0..199, tool versions.
2. For each seed: `frob mission transport fixtures/augur/coupling/<seed>/source.fmission --to
   fixtures/augur/coupling/<seed>/target.fbot --claims all --json`. Assert exit 0 or 2; read each claim's `kind`,
   `budget.total`, `transported.lower`, `verdict`, and the envelope's `evidence` block.
3. Assert, per chance claim: `transported.lower` ≤ pinned exact target probability (exact rational comparison done
   by a small checker script on the "p/q" strings). Per expectation claim: the pinned target expectation lies in
   the transported interval. Count violations; the threshold is 0.
4. Seed 0 (the two-outcome reversal): assert Σ ε = 49/50, bound 1/100, and `not-transported` for the claim "at
   least 19/20", with `eval.qualify` among `next_actions`.
5. Seed 1 (ten-step chain): assert Σ ε = 3/100 and bound 24/25, class `Certified`.
6. For each transported claim: `frob prove bundle` and then `frob-verify` on the bundle under `env -i`; assert
   `accept`.
7. Planted failures: (a) rewrite seed 0's certificate with the claim marked `universal`; assert `frob-verify`
   rejects with rule `transport.chance-as-universal`; (b) remove the budget; assert rejection with
   `transport.budget-missing`; (c) submit both through `frob mission transport --certificate`; assert exit 3 and
   the refusal "chance claim transported without a coupling budget" with its repairs; (d) run the script's
   own self-test build in which the proposer takes the mean instead of the maximum over related pairs
   (feature flag `mutant-coupling-mean` in the test profile); assert that step 3 then counts at least one
   violation.
8. `summary`: fixtures run, violations (0 required), refusals observed, bundles accepted, mutants caught; write
   the gate row for GATE-AUGUR-25 (finite part) with the measured counts.

Logging (STD-E2E): newline-delimited JSON to `target/e2e-logs/augur_coupling_transport/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and
a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-05`), `step`, `event` (`start`,
`cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed). `metric` lines: seed, Σ ε, transported bound,
pinned exact value, slack, each as an exact string with its threshold. `refusal` lines: code, detail, pointer,
repairs. On failure: the first violating seed, the first differing value, and the paths of the fixture and bundle.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 from a clean checkout on a host with no GPU and non-zero on any failed assertion.
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-25, finite part (TARGET, M4): zero violated bounds over the 200 finite fixtures; the two-outcome reversal is refused. Recorded with the counts and the commit.
- [ ] Every transported claim's bundle is accepted by `frob-verify` in an empty environment.
- [ ] The two named bad certificates are rejected with the expected rule and refusal, and the proposer mutant is caught by the violation count.
- [ ] Two runs give identical verdicts, identical bundle digests and identical log content apart from timestamps and wall times.
- [ ] The log contains no fixture payload beyond seeds, digests and the numbers listed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `coupling_transport_selftest_violation.sh` (a pinned truth lowered by
1/100 for one seed makes step 3 fail and names the seed); `coupling_transport_selftest_permissive_verify.sh` (an
auditor build with the transport rules disabled accepts the relabelled certificate, and the script fails);
`coupling_transport_selftest_rational_compare.sh` (the "p/q" comparison helper orders 24/25 and 96/100 as equal and
3/100 below 1/25).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; steps and logging as above.

### Known answers and negative controls

Two-outcome case: Σ ε = 0.98, bound 0.01, refused. Ten-step chain: 0.99 to at least 0.96, exact target above.
Negative controls: steps 7(a) to 7(d) and the self-tests. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and
release; the Mac and the Linux host (class D0: exact arithmetic; digests must match).

## Considerations

- The finite part can pass while the mechanism is useless on physics; the M8 tray reading decides that.
- If step 3 finds a violation, the fixture and seed are filed as a counterexample and the first trigger of
  KILL-COUPLING is live after one repair cycle.
- Run time is dominated by 200 verb invocations; a warm daemon is allowed, and the cold path is logged once.
