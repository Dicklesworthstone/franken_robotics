## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is recorded
as an architecture decision record at the milestone it is due, and a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-COUPLING belongs to the rule "chance claims transport only through a coupling". `mission.transport` moves a
certified claim from a source robot to a target robot through a simulation relation, which preserves universal
claims and nothing probabilistic. The mechanism under decision lets a chance claim or an expected value cross with
a coupling budget Σₜ εₜ: P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ, and |E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ for a
quantity in [0, B]. Its ceiling was stated before any test: the budget helps only when Σ ε is small against the
slack of the claim, and it saves one qualification study per transported skill and no simulation time.

- Trigger: one violated transported bound that survives a repair cycle, or no skill on the tray fixture with a
  budget below its slack.
- Fallback: `mission.transport` carries universal claims only, and every chance or expected-value claim is
  re-measured on the target in the blinded qualification lane (sealed world clusters, pre-committed requests,
  receipts, level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] per request).

The register gives this kill gate no milestone of its own, so it is decided at the milestones of the gate beside
it, GATE-AUGUR-25: a first reading at M4 on the finite fixtures and the decision at M8 on the tray fixture.
Identifier note: one index table of the plan lists this identifier beside the exposure-domain coupling test of the
task chapter; that subsection's own kill gate is KILL-EXPODOMAIN, and KILL-COUPLING is only the gate described here.

## Scope

In: the two readings, the decision record, and the switch to the fallback if the gate fires. Out: producing the
measurements (N25e-01, N25e-05 for the finite part; N25e-04 for the tray).

## Technical approach

1. M4 reading (from N25e-05). Inputs: the violation count over the 200 finite fixtures and any violating seed. A
   violation opens one repair cycle: the defect is located (argument, proposer, or checker), fixed, and the whole
   fixture set re-run. A violation that reappears after the cycle fires the gate at M4 without waiting for the
   tray. A clean reading is recorded as `not fired at M4; decision due M8`.
2. M8 decision (from N25e-04). Inputs per ported tray skill: n, k, the 95% upper bound p̄ on the budget, the
   claim's slack, and the sealed target-only qualification result for the same claim. The gate fires if no skill
   has p̄ below its slack, or if any transported bound is contradicted by the sealed target result and the
   contradiction survives one repair cycle.
3. Record. An ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING.md` with: the trigger as written, the measured values, the commit,
   the receipt hashes, the decision (`pass` or `killed: fallback adopted`), and the date. The row of
   `registries/gates.toml` for KILL-COUPLING cites the ADR and resolves in the artifact graph.
4. If fired, downstream changes, all mechanical:
   - `mission.transport` refuses a claim of kind `chance` or `expectation` with the refusal "chance claim
     transported without a coupling budget" and the repair "measure on the target with `eval.qualify`"; the
     `budget` field is removed from the verb's request schema and kept readable in stored bundles.
   - The checker's `transport` rules stay (they reject, so they stay sound); bundles that carried a transported
     chance claim are marked withdrawn and dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded.
   - N25e-04's paired-run protocol is parked; its relation monitor is kept as a diagnostic.
   - The skill-transport experiment counts one qualification study per transported chance claim in its cost.
   - The honest-status table shows chance-claim transport as `killed: fallback adopted`.
5. If not fired: the status table shows the measured p̄ and slack per skill; the mechanism stays [F] and is
   described no more broadly than the fixture that qualified it.

A trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment to the register made before the measurement it
governs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The M4 reading is on file with the violation count from N25e-05 and its log path.
- [ ] The M8 decision is recorded as an ADR with measured p̄ and slack for every ported tray skill, or the gate is recorded as fired at M4.
- [ ] The gate row in `registries/gates.toml` has status `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted`, cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and resolves through `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] If fired: the refusal is live for chance and expectation claims, affected bundles are withdrawn, and the M8 exit review lists the fallback as adopted.
- [ ] The decision was made by a session that did not implement N25e-02 to N25e-04.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own; the decision consumes recorded measurements. A registry check
(`xtask check-contracts`) verifies that the gate row cites an existing ADR and existing claim identifiers.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_kill_coupling_fallback.sh` (run only on the fallback branch, and once in a dry-run profile to
prove the branch exists): with the fallback flag set, `frob mission transport` on the ten-step chain fixture with a
chance claim must exit 3 with the refusal and the `eval.qualify` repair, and a universal claim on the same fixture
must still cross. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_kill_coupling_fallback/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with command, exit code, refusal block and a
`summary` naming KILL-COUPLING.

### Known answers and negative controls

The trigger values are the known answers of the neighbouring beads: zero violations on 200 fixtures; the reversal
refused; p̄ against slack on the tray. Negative control of the decision procedure: a dry run with a planted
violating fixture must produce the record `fired at M4` and switch the dry-run profile to the fallback.

## Considerations

- The likeliest outcome is honest uselessness on physics: contact-rich bodies decorrelate, breaks are frequent,
  p̄ exceeds every slack, and the gate fires on its second clause. That costs one refusal and no architecture.
- The fallback is strictly safer and only dearer: one sealed qualification study per chance claim on the target.
- Owner of the decision: the Augur lead, confirmed by the project owner at the M8 exit review.
