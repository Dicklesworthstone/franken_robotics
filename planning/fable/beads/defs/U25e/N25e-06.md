## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is recorded
as an architecture decision record at the milestone it is due, and a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan.

KILL-COUPLING belongs to the rule "chance claims transport only through a coupling". `mission.transport` moves a
certified claim from a source robot to a target robot through a simulation relation, which preserves universal
claims and nothing probabilistic. The mechanism under decision lets a chance claim or an expected value cross with
a coupling budget Σₜ εₜ: P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ, and |E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ for a
quantity in [0, B]. Its ceiling was stated before any test: the budget helps only when Σ ε is small against the
slack of the claim, and it saves one qualification study per transported skill and no simulation time.

- Trigger: one violated transported bound that survives a repair cycle, or no skill on the tray fixture with a
  budget below its slack.
- Fallback: `mission.transport` carries universal claims only, and every chance or expected-value claim is
  re-measured on the target in the blinded qualification lane (sealed world clusters, pre-committed requests,
  receipts, level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] per request).

The register gives this kill gate no milestone of its own, so it is decided at the milestones of the gate beside
it, GATE-AUGUR-25: a first reading at M4 on the finite fixtures and the decision at M8 on the tray fixture.
Identifier note: one index table of the plan lists this identifier beside the exposure-domain coupling test of the
task chapter; that subsection's own kill gate is KILL-EXPODOMAIN, and KILL-COUPLING is only the gate described here.

## Scope

In: the M8 decision on the second clause, the completion of the decision record, and the switch to the fallback
if the gate fires at M8. Out: the M4 reading of the first clause and its repair cycle (N25ex-01, a dependency,
whose record this bead reads and never re-decides); producing the measurements (N25e-01, N25e-05 for the finite
part; N25e-04 for the tray).

## Technical approach

1. M4 reading, read from `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M4.md` and the KILL-COUPLING row's decision entry
   `{ scope = "clause:finite", exit = "M4", … }` (both written by N25ex-01; this bead never edits them). If the
   entry's outcome is `fired`, this bead records the gate as decided at M4: it writes a short
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md` whose outcome is `not-reached` (the tray work was never funded; the fallback
   has been in force since M4 by `ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M4.md`, which it cites; no tray input is read), appends
   `{ scope = "clause:tray", exit = "M8", outcome = "not-reached", adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md", date,
   session, receipts = [] }` (an M8 entry names the M8 path, or the load fails `decision-adr-path`, N40-03; the M8
   due list then shows a decision and not a gap), closes citing both ADRs, and N25e-04 is closed `not funded`. If the outcome is `not-fired` (the reading `not
   fired at M4; decision due M8`), steps 2 to 5 follow. If there is no `clause:finite` entry, the bead stops: the
   M4 decision is missing and is not re-decided here.
2. M8 decision (from N25e-04). Inputs per ported tray skill: n, k, the 95% upper bound p̄ on the budget, the
   claim's slack, and the sealed target-only qualification result for the same claim. The gate fires if no skill
   has p̄ below its slack, or if any transported bound is contradicted by the sealed target result and the
   contradiction survives one repair cycle.
3. Record. The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md` with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate;
   the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value (n, k, p̄ and slack
   per ported tray skill; the sealed target-only result and, if one was used, the repair cycle) with its receipt
   hash beside the trigger's number; the outcome (`not-fired` or `fired`); the fallback now in force and the name
   the capability carries from now on ("`mission.transport` carries universal claims only"); the beads re-read,
   each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream (step 4 or 5); the commit and
   the date; the session that recorded it, which implemented none of N25e-02 to N25e-04. It cites
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M4.md` for the finite clause. The KILL-COUPLING row of `registries/gates.toml`
   carries `decision` as an array (N40-03; decided M4, `reread = ["M8"]`); this bead appends `{ scope =
   "clause:tray", exit = "M8", outcome, adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md", date, session, receipts }` (the
   hashes of the tray receipts the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`) beside the
   `clause:finite` entry, which stays in force for its scope. On `fired`, N40-03's propagation gives the tray part
   of GATE-AUGUR-25 the status `killed` with the fallback's name.
4. If fired, downstream changes, all mechanical:
   - `mission.transport` refuses a claim of kind `chance` or `expectation` with the refusal "chance claim
     transported without a coupling budget" (row 1 of N25e-45: the interim `FROB-REF-852` with `detail:
     coupling-budget-missing` while the ledger holds the row) and the repair "measure on the target with
     `eval.qualify`".
   - The coupling-source options of N25e-04 (`--coupling-source paired-runs`, `--pairs`, `--level`) stay in the
     verb's request schema, because a registered option is never removed: they answer `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5,
     status `killed`, the pointer at the option, with the fallback ("`mission.transport` carries universal
     claims only"; measure on the target with `eval.qualify`) named in the first repair (VERBS-mission's rule for
     killed options). The run verb's compute `budget` (`--budget cpu_core_s=…`) is unchanged. Stored bundles whose
     `transport` block carries `source: paired-runs` stay readable.
   - The checker's `transport` rules stay (they reject, so they stay sound); bundles that carried a transported
     chance claim are marked withdrawn and dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded.
   - N25e-04's paired-run protocol is parked; its relation monitor is kept as a diagnostic.
   - The skill-transport experiment counts one qualification study per transported chance claim in its cost.
   - The honest-status table prints chance-claim transport under the fallback's name (N40-03's printout of a
     fired decision).
5. If not fired: the status table shows the measured p̄ and slack per skill; the mechanism stays [F] and is
   described no more broadly than the fixture that qualified it.

A trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment to the register made before the measurement it
governs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The decision consumes recorded measurements; its own logic is the M8 reading rule
(`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_coupling_m8`): `fired_at_m4_is_inherited` (N25ex-01's entry says
`fired`: this bead's reading is `decided at M4`, no tray input is read, and the appended `clause:tray` entry is
`not-reached` with `adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md"`, which cites `ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M4.md`; an entry
naming the M4 path at exit M8 fails `decision-adr-path`); `missing_finite_entry_stops` (no `clause:finite` entry: no
reading, nothing appended); `tray_entry_shape` (the appended entry has `scope = "clause:tray"`, `exit = "M8"` and
an outcome from N40-03's four words; `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted` fails the load check);
`finite_entry_untouched` (the `clause:finite` entry's bytes are equal before and after); `no_skill_below_slack_fires` (three skills
with p̄ = 0.04, 0.06, 0.10 against slacks 0.03, 0.05, 0.08: fired); `one_skill_below_slack_passes` (p̄ = 0.00997
against slack 0.02 for one skill, the others above: not fired); `contradiction_opens_one_cycle` (a sealed
target-only result below a transported bound opens the repair cycle; the same contradiction after it fires);
`fallback_kills_coupling_source` (in the fallback profile, a request with `--coupling-source paired-runs` is
answered 724, exit 5, status `killed`, with the fallback named, and a request with only the compute `budget`
parses as before).
A registry check (`xtask check-contracts`) verifies that the gate row cites an existing ADR and existing claim
identifiers.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_kill_coupling_fallback.sh` (its `record` stage runs on every branch; its `fallback` stage runs on
the fallback branch, and once in a dry-run profile to prove the branch exists). Stage `record`: the unit tests
above, then the register's load checks on the KILL-COUPLING row (both entries present, the `clause:finite` entry's
digest unchanged, every cited ADR path resolving). Stage `fallback`: with the fallback flag set, `frob mission
transport chain10.fmission --from <source> --to <target> --claims <chance-claim> --key akc-chance-<seed> --json`
on the ten-step chain fixture must exit 3 with the refusal and the `eval.qualify` repair, and the same call with a
universal claim (`--key akc-universal-<seed>`) must still cross; then the same chance-claim call with
`--coupling-source paired-runs --pairs 299 --level 0.05 --key akc-killed-<seed>` must exit 5 with
`FROB-REF-724`, status `killed`, the fallback named in the first repair, and the same call with
`--budget cpu_core_s=60` alone (`--key akc-budget-<seed>`) must not be answered 724 (the compute budget is
untouched). It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_kill_coupling_fallback/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-06`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the
entries read and appended with their scope, exit and outcome; the ADR paths and hashes; refusal blocks with code
and repair; on failure the first field that disagrees, with the paths to reproduce; and a `summary` with
assertions passed and failed, naming KILL-COUPLING.

### Known answers and negative controls

The trigger values are the known answers of the neighbouring beads: zero violations on 200 fixtures; the reversal
refused; p̄ against slack on the tray. Negative control of the decision procedure: a dry run with a planted tray
reading in which every skill's p̄ exceeds its slack must produce `fired at M8` and switch the dry-run profile to
the fallback; the M4 control (a planted violating fixture) is N25ex-01's.

## Considerations

- The likeliest outcome is honest uselessness on physics: contact-rich bodies decorrelate, breaks are frequent,
  p̄ exceeds every slack, and the gate fires on its second clause. That costs one refusal and no architecture.
- The fallback is strictly safer and only dearer: one sealed qualification study per chance claim on the target.
- Owner of the decision: the Augur lead, confirmed by the project owner at the M8 exit review.
