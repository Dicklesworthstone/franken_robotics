## Background

A mission answer is a pair of design sets with a checked reason per cell; a cell is inner, excluded or unresolved,
and nothing converts unresolved into either of the others (ADR-AUGUR-07). Proof rule 3 of the mission semantics
lets a verdict move across a cell by monotone inheritance, under conditions that are checked. The temptation this
bead closes is the informal version of that rule in a parameter sweep: "it succeeded at μ = 0.50, so it succeeds at
every higher friction". An observed pattern is not a monotonicity argument.

The counterexample is on record. FACT (planning archive, the 300-trajectory planar friction sweep of the sill
fixture; to be re-run before a gate cites it): for the 75 mm wheel, a sharp lip and the payload shifted 40 mm
backward, the planar scorer returns success, timeout, success at μ = 0.500, 0.525 and 0.550. Filling every friction
level above the first success adds one success that did not occur. With the scoring period held at 1 ms, halving the
physics step from 50 µs to 25 µs changes the triplet to timeout, timeout, success; at 25 µs with a stiffer contact
and adjusted damping all three succeed. The physical reading of the middle timeout is unresolved, and the fill is
refuted without resolving it.

The rule (tier Solid [S]): a verdict at an unevaluated parameter value is never inferred from its neighbours unless
a checked monotonicity argument for that parameter, model and verdict is attached. It restricts proof rule 3 and
every sweep that feeds a mission answer. Rejected alternatives: filling with a warning label (labels are dropped by
consumers; an unsound verdict in a table is read as a verdict); nearest-neighbour or isotonic smoothing of verdicts
(both invent outcomes).

## Scope

In: the three-status point model in `mission.synthesize`, `nemesis.search` and `phylum.sweep`; the licence that
turns a fill into a proof step; the refusal of an unlicensed fill; the licence for the creep-limit rows. Out: the
detector for non-monotone outcomes and its routing (N25e-08); fixtures and the gate script (N25e-09); the
`monotone-transport` checker (U12).

## Technical approach

1. Point status (DESIGN). Every swept parameter value in a result carries one of three statuses:
   `evaluated` (a verdict from an execution or an analysis at that value, with its evidence), `proved` (covered
   by a proof object, with its `ProofRef`), `unresolved` (neither). Shared type in `frob-core`:

   ```rust
   pub enum PointStatus { Evaluated { verdict: Verdict3, evidence: EvidenceRef },
                          Proved { verdict: Verdict3, proof: ProofRef },
                          Unresolved { reason: Explanation } }
   ```
   No verb interpolates, extrapolates or smooths verdicts. A result schema has no `filled` field. The three verbs
   return `unresolved` for every parameter value that was neither evaluated nor covered by a proof, and the
   count of unresolved points is in the summary.
2. A fill is a proof step of the monotone-inheritance kind. `frob-mission` may mark an unevaluated value `proved`
   only by emitting a `monotone-transport` proof object that cites a theorem making the verdict monotone in the
   parameter under the declared model. The object carries the direction, the sign proof over the whole interval
   (an outward-rounded enclosure of the partial derivative that excludes zero, or the parameter entering only
   as the bound of a set that provably grows), the side conditions of the monotonicity test, and the model
   stratum it holds in (fixed contact mode sequence, fixed topology, fixed observation timing). Crossing a
   stratum boundary ends the transport.
3. Licences. A registry `registries/monotone_licences.toml` (DESIGN) lists the (model, parameter, verdict)
   triples for which such a theorem is on file, each with its statement, its derivation path and its stratum.
   The first rows: the creep-limit rigs of the cart-sill fixture. In the quasi-static front lift-off rig the
   lip force is vertical, so the friction demand is μ ≥ tan θ, with tan θ = x/(r − h) and x = √(2rh − h²) for
   wheel radius r and lip height h (0.7500 for the 60 mm wheel and 0.6459 for the 75 mm wheel on a sharp 12 mm
   lip; 0.7332 and 0.6351 with 2 mm fillets). Success is "μ at or above the demand", which is monotone in μ by
   construction. In the rear pull-over rig the demand is ¾·(32/55 − δ)/(23/55 + δ) for payload offset δ in
   metres, again a threshold in μ.
4. No licence by default for rollouts. A verdict produced by a dynamic rollout has no monotonicity theorem by
   default. A request to fill such a sweep is refused with `FROB-REF-852` (a monotone transport that lacks a
   side condition), pointer at the swept parameter, repairs ranked: (1) evaluate the missing values, (2) supply
   the monotonicity proof and register a licence, (3) accept `unresolved` for those values. Exit 3.
5. Verb changes. `mission.synthesize` cells built from a sweep carry point statuses and never widen an evaluated
   success into an interval without step 2. `nemesis.search` reports the searched grid with statuses; a failing
   world found at one parameter value says nothing about its neighbours. `phylum.sweep` scorecards mark
   unevaluated designs `unresolved`. Each verb accepts `--fill licensed` (default off): with it, licensed fills
   are attempted and each fill appears as a `proved` point with its proof reference.
6. Ledger: every fill writes the licence identifier, the proof reference and the interval filled.

Determinism: statuses are a pure function of the evaluated set and the licence registry (class D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A sweep with evaluated values {0.500, 0.550} of a dynamic rollout returns 0.525 as `unresolved` in all three verbs; no result contains a verdict at an unevaluated, unproved value.
- [ ] `--fill licensed` on a dynamic rollout sweep is refused with `FROB-REF-852` and the three ranked repairs.
- [ ] On the creep rig, evaluating only the two grid points that bracket the threshold and filling the rest under the licence reproduces the exhaustive grid at every point, and every filled point carries a `monotone-transport` proof that `frob-verify` accepts.
- [ ] A mutant build that fills every value above the first success is caught by the unit tests and by the gate script (N25e-09) on both fixtures.
- [ ] A fill that would cross a declared stratum boundary (a change of contact mode sequence) stops at the boundary.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json`; result schemas document `PointStatus`; `CONTRACT.md` of each crate states the rule.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/no_fill.rs`: `unevaluated_is_unresolved`; `no_schema_field_named_filled` (schema
introspection); `licensed_fill_matches_grid` (creep rig for the 60 mm wheel: threshold 3/4; grid 0.50 to 1.00 in
steps of 0.01 excluding points within 0.005 of the threshold; evaluated at 0.74 and 0.76; filled points equal the
exhaustive verdicts); `unlicensed_fill_refused`; `fill_stops_at_stratum`; `mutant_fill_above_first_success_caught`
(the archive triplet success, timeout, success at 0.500, 0.525, 0.550 as a `synthetic` fixture: the mutant reports
success at 0.525). The same three status tests in `crates/frob-nemesis/tests/` and `crates/frob-phylum/tests/`.
Property test for the invariant "the set of points with a verdict equals evaluated ∪ proved" on random sweeps.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_no_monotone_fill.sh` (N25e-09) runs the three verbs on the banded fixture and on the creep rig
and logs point statuses, refusals and proof references with the STD-E2E helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Creep thresholds 0.7500 and 0.6459 (sharp lip), 0.7332 and 0.6351 (2 mm fillets): the licensed fill equals the
exhaustive grid. The archive triplet as a synthetic negative control for the mutant. Related counterexamples of
the chapter: CX-275 (sample points in a cell do not decide the cell) and CX-283 (a static rejection is a candidate
for a dynamic test). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine, across worker counts (statuses must not depend on
completion order), debug and release.

## Considerations

- The rule costs evaluations: every value a caller wants a verdict for must be run or proved. That is the price
  of not inventing outcomes; the detector of N25e-08 says where the price is worth paying.
- A licence is a theorem about a model. The creep licence holds for the quasi-static rig and not for the full
  cart, where momentum, impact and the drive transient decide the outcome.
- Interaction with reports: a result that depends on a non-monotone interval is `model-conditional` until the
  variants agree, and a report must print the condition (BD-302).
- The archive FACT is exploratory and keeps its own scorer; it is re-run before any gate cites its numbers.
