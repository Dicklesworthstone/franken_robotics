## Background

Sweeps over one scalar (friction, mount stiffness, a gear ratio) feed mission answers. A sweep whose verdict flips
more than once along the sorted grid is a warning about the model, not a feature of the robot to be averaged away.
The recorded case (FACT, planning archive, the 300-trajectory planar friction sweep of the sill fixture; to be
re-run before a gate cites it): for the 75 mm wheel, a sharp lip and the payload shifted 40 mm backward, the planar
scorer returns success, timeout, success at μ = 0.500, 0.525 and 0.550. With the scoring period held at 1 ms,
halving the physics step from 50 µs to 25 µs changes the triplet to timeout, timeout, success; at 25 µs with a
stiffer contact and adjusted damping all three succeed. So the pattern depends on the step and on contact
parameters, and its physical reading is unresolved.

The rule this bead implements (tier Solid [S], owner `frob-analyze`): a detector runs on every swept scalar; a
non-monotone outcome is recorded with what is needed to reproduce it, routed to the fidelity ledger for step-halving
and stiffness variants, and every result that depends on the affected interval is typed `model-conditional` until
the variants agree. The fidelity ledger is the table, keyed by model class, scene class, quantity, tier pair,
profile pair, contact-law pair and kernel versions, of the disagreement between a faster and a slower
configuration; task decisions are among its quantities, and a state error inside its envelope with a different
decision is a disagreement. `model-conditional` is the label of a result that reverses between contact laws or
other model forms; it cannot be promoted to `established`, cannot authorize a build packet, and must be reported
with its condition.

One more rule keeps the comparison meaningful: when the physics step is refined the requirement clock stays fixed,
so two refinements are compared on one definition of the verdict. A requirement names the clock it is evaluated on
(a rational period and a phase, advanced by an exact accumulator), and a signal on a faster clock enters it through
a declared reduction per requirement tick. Rejected alternative: letting the scoring period follow the physics
step (then a changed verdict could be a changed question).

## Scope

In: the detector, its record, the ledger request, the labelling of dependent results, the requirement-clock check
on variants. Out: the no-interpolation rule and licences (N25e-07); running the variants (the model-form check of
the fidelity chapter); the fixtures and gate script (N25e-09).

## Technical approach

Module (DESIGN): `crates/frob-analyze/src/sweep_monotone.rs`; pure function of a sweep result, class D0.

1. Input: for one swept scalar, the points with status `evaluated` or `proved`, each with value v and a verdict
   in {pass, fail} (every non-success terminal class, timeout included, is fail; `unresolved` points carry no
   verdict), plus the run configuration: physics step, requirement clock, contact law and its stiffness and
   damping, tier, fixture identity, seed.
2. Sort by v (exact rational order; ties are a schema error). Walk the sorted list and count sign changes of the
   verdict between consecutive verdict-bearing points. Unresolved points are skipped and reported as gaps: a
   gap between two equal verdicts is not counted as a change and is not evidence of constancy.
3. Classification: 0 changes `constant`; 1 change `single-threshold` (the bracket of the change is reported; no
   verdict is inferred between the bracketing points); 2 or more changes `non-monotone`.
4. Record for `non-monotone` (schema `frob.sweep.nonmonotone/1`): parameter name and unit, the grid, the verdict
   sequence, the indices of the changes, the affected interval, the physics step, the requirement clock, the
   contact parameters, tier, fixture, seed, replay addresses of the points adjacent to each change. The
   affected interval is the closed interval from the last point before the first change to the first point
   after the last change.
5. Routing. The record is written to the fidelity ledger as a model-form request for the affected interval
   with the variant set: physics step h and h/2; contact stiffness × 2 with damping adjusted to keep the
   damping ratio; (where the tier offers them) the alternative contact law. Each variant re-runs the points of
   the affected interval at the same world addresses.
6. Requirement clock. Every variant must evaluate the requirement on the same clock (same rational period and
   phase) as the original run. The detector checks this from the run configurations and marks a variant
   `not-comparable: requirement clock differs` if it does not; such a variant never clears a label.
7. Labelling. From the moment the record exists, every result whose inputs include a point in the affected
   interval carries the condition `model-conditional { parameter, interval, record }` in its claim product
   (conditions join by union and cannot be dropped by a consumer). The label is cleared only when every
   comparable variant returns the same verdict sequence on the affected interval; then the interval is
   reclassified with that sequence. If variants disagree the label stays, with `flipped_by` naming the variant.
8. Surfaces. `frob analyze sweep-monotone <result-id> --json` (the row of NJLx-51, kind `read`, a dependency)
   returns the classification and the record; exit 0 for `constant` and `single-threshold`, 2 for
   `non-monotone`, as that row states; no chapter refusal of its own. The detector also runs automatically inside `mission.synthesize`, `nemesis.search` and `phylum.sweep` on every
   swept scalar, and the classification appears in each verb's result.

Cost: linear in the number of points; no physics.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The sequence pass, fail, pass on a three-point grid is classified `non-monotone` with two changes and the affected interval equal to the whole grid.
- [ ] Sequences with one change are `single-threshold` and produce no ledger request; constant sequences produce none.
- [ ] A `non-monotone` record contains the grid, the step and the contact parameters, and appears in the fidelity ledger with its three variants requested.
- [ ] A mission answer that depends on the affected interval is typed `model-conditional` and leaves the interval's cells `unresolved`; the label survives every downstream combination (claim product union).
- [ ] The label clears only when all comparable variants agree; a variant with a different requirement clock is marked not comparable and does not clear it.
- [ ] On 100 seeded synthetic sweeps, each with a planted pass, fail, pass band at a seeded position, the detector flags the band in 100 of 100 (the GATE-AUGUR-26 clause on the banded fixture is measured through the verbs by N25e-09, which depends on this bead).
- [ ] The detector's output is identical under any completion order of the sweep and any worker count.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/sweep_monotone.rs`: `pass_fail_pass_is_nonmonotone`; `single_change_is_threshold`
(fail, fail, pass, pass: one change, bracket between the second and third points); `gap_is_not_a_change` (pass,
unresolved, pass: constant with one gap, and no claim about the gap); `archive_triplet` (the triplet success,
timeout, success at 0.500, 0.525, 0.550 as a `synthetic` row: non-monotone; its step-halved variant timeout,
timeout, success: single-threshold; the stiffer variant all success: constant; variants disagree, so the label
stays with `flipped_by: step-halving`); `clock_mismatch_not_comparable` (a variant scored at 0.5 ms against an
original at 1 ms); `label_union_survives_combine`; `order_independent` (property test: any permutation of the
input points gives the same record); `planted_band_100_seeds` (the acceptance item above);
`exit_code_follows_classification` (0, 0, 2).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/analyze_sweep_monotone.sh` (this bead's own; the gate script `scripts/e2e/augur_no_monotone_fill.sh`
of N25e-09 later repeats the check on the banded fixture). Steps: (1) produce three stored sweeps on N25e-07's
synthetic triplet fixture, `frob mission synthesize triplet.fmission --key sm-<run>-trip --json` and its
step-halved and stiffer variants (`--key sm-<run>-trip-h2`, `--key sm-<run>-trip-k2`; distinct keys, so that a
variant is never answered from the original's receipt), plus a one-change and a constant sweep; (2) `frob analyze
sweep-monotone a:<answer> --json` on each, asserting the classification, the exit code (2 for the triplet, 0
otherwise), the affected interval and the record's fields; (3) read the fidelity ledger entry for the triplet and
assert the three variants requested; (4) assert the `model-conditional` condition on the triplet's answer and
that it stays after the variants (they disagree, `flipped_by: step-halving`); (5) a variant scored on a 0.5 ms
clock, asserted `not-comparable`. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/analyze_sweep_monotone/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-08`),
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block; per sweep the verdict sequence,
classification and change indices against the expected ones; the record digest; the ledger request; the labels;
a `summary` with assertions passed and failed naming GATE-AUGUR-26.

### Known answers and negative controls

The planted band of N25e-09 (pass, fail, pass in mount stiffness). The archive triplet and its two variants as
synthetic rows. Negative controls: a detector mutant that counts only the first change (classifies the band as a
threshold) must fail the unit tests and the gate; a mutant that treats `unresolved` as fail must fail
`gap_is_not_a_change`. Metamorphic relations: reversing the parameter axis leaves the classification unchanged;
refining the grid can only keep or raise the change count on the same underlying verdict function. Determinism
lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- A non-monotone outcome can be real physics (a resonance band) or a numerical artifact (step, stiffness). The
  detector does not decide which; the variants do, and until they agree the result is conditional.
- Coarse grids hide bands: zero or one change on a coarse grid is not a proof of monotonicity, which is why no
  verdict is inferred between grid points (N25e-07).
- The variant runs cost up to three times the affected interval; the ledger budget applies and an exhausted
  budget leaves the label in place.
- A `model-conditional` result offered for promotion, a build or a design comparison without its condition is
  refused elsewhere (`FROB-REF-820`, `FROB-REF-830`, `FROB-REF-557`).
