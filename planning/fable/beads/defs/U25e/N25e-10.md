## Background

Augur answers questions about families of designs with two sets, inner and outer, and a checked reason per cell.
The cheapest reasons are static exclusions. Many static sizing relations of a robot are monomial: holding torque
m·g·ℓ ≤ N·τ, no-load speed v·N ≤ ω·ℓ, bus current, ground pressure against sinkage. A conjunction of such relations
can be contradictory for every positive value of the design parameters, and when it is, the whole family is
excluded by one line of integer arithmetic. This bead builds that screen. It extends the "monotone bounds first"
step of region evidence and the static-exclusion step of the family worklist. Tier Solid [S]; owner `frob-mission`
for the screen, `frob-cert` for the proof kind `monomial-product` (class `Certified`).

Why it earns a place beside per-constraint paving: a paving does not see such a contradiction cheaply. In the known
answer below each constraint alone is satisfiable over the box, and the two feasible sets are separated by a
diagonal band that boxes approximate only by refinement. Rejected alternatives: a logarithmic change of variables
handed to a linear decision procedure for general posynomials (the logarithm of a sum of monomials is a convex
log-sum-exp, neither linear nor polynomial; sums are handled only through sound monomial relaxations, N25e-11);
trusting the floating-point linear program (only the certificate is trusted).

No speed claim is made: for a single proposed design a pointwise check gives the same answer. The value is a family
exclusion with a one-line certificate and a named binding pair of constraints.

## Scope

In: the monomial constraint form, the search for multipliers, exact integer reconstruction, caps, the proof
object. Out: sums of monomials, worklist integration and the `mission.refute` report (N25e-11); the checker
(N12-14); the generated corpus and gate script (N25e-12).

## Technical approach

Object. Design parameters θ₁, …, θₙ are positive (lengths, masses, ratios, ratings). A monomial constraint is
c·θ₁^a₁ ⋯ θₙ^aₙ ≤ 1 with c a positive rational and integer exponents. Rational exponents are cleared by raising the
inequality to a common power (c becomes c^D, still rational). Parameter bounds are monomial constraints: lo/θᵢ ≤ 1
and θᵢ/hi ≤ 1. A cell's box bounds therefore enter as constraints, and one rule excludes one cell or the whole
positive orthant.

Certificate. For constraints k = 1, …, K with coefficients c_k and exponent rows a_k, nonnegative integers λ_k, not
all zero, with

  Σ_k λ_k·a_k = 0   and   Π_k c_k^λ_k > 1

prove that no positive θ satisfies all K constraints: multiplying the constraints raised to the powers λ_k cancels
every parameter and leaves Π_k c_k^λ_k ≤ 1, a contradiction. In the coordinates y = ln θ the constraints are the
linear system a_k·y ≤ −ln c_k; by the theorem of the alternative such λ exists whenever the system is infeasible,
and because the exponent matrix is rational, λ can be taken integer.

Algorithm (module `crates/frob-mission/src/screen/monomial.rs`, DESIGN):
1. Lower: collect the cell's monomial constraints as rows (id, c as reduced "p/q", exponent vector, source
   constraint, physical meaning). Append the box bounds.
2. Search, in floating point, untrusted: maximize Σ_k λ_k·ln c_k subject to Aᵀλ = 0, Σ_k λ_k = 1, λ ≥ 0, where A is
   the K × n exponent matrix. Solver: the admitted linear-program route (the cone solver of `frob-algebra`, or
   the second admitted solver as a cross-check). A nonpositive optimum means no certificate: the screen says
   nothing and the cell goes on to the next step of the worklist.
3. Reconstruct exactly. Take the support S of the float solution (entries above 10⁻⁹). A vertex of the program
   has at most n + 1 nonzeros. Solve A_Sᵀ·λ_S = 0 over the integers by fraction-free elimination (Bareiss);
   take the null vector, clear denominators, divide by the gcd, fix the sign so that entries are nonnegative.
   If the null space restricted to S has dimension above one, or a sign is mixed, fall back to continued-
   fraction rounding of the float λ with denominators up to the multiplier cap and re-verify; if that fails the
   screen returns no certificate.
4. Verify in exact arithmetic inside the proposer: n integer sums are zero; the product Π c_k^λ_k, computed as a
   pair of big integers, exceeds 1. A product equal to 1 is not a certificate.
5. Caps (declared, DESIGN defaults): each λ_k ≤ 64; numerator and denominator of the product at most 4,096 bits;
   K ≤ 256, n ≤ 64. A certificate beyond the caps is refused, not approximated: the screen returns no
   certificate and records `beyond-caps`; a bundle that exceeds them is rejected by the checker as
   `FROB-REF-849`.
6. Emit a proof object of kind `monomial-product`, `expects: Excluded`, class `Certified`:

   ```json
   { "kind": "monomial-product", "cell": "c7", "parameters": ["ell", "N"],
     "constraints": [ { "id": "hold-torque", "c": "2943/50", "exp": [1, -1] },
                      { "id": "no-load-speed", "c": "3/160", "exp": [-1, 1] } ],
     "multipliers": [1, 1], "product": "8829/8000",
     "scope": "static rigid model named in the bundle", "expects": "excluded" }
   ```
   with the primitive equations in Unicode text and as an expression DAG. The checker verifies n integer sums,
   one per parameter, and compares one product of integer powers of rationals with 1. It never evaluates a
   logarithm.

Scope of an exclusion: the static rigid model named in the bundle; it says nothing about dynamic performance.
Determinism class D0 for the certificate (the search may differ between solvers; the reconstructed λ is reduced to
lowest terms, and when several certificates exist the one with the lexicographically smallest (Σλ, λ) among those
found is emitted).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The leg fixture (below) returns λ = (1, 1) and product 8829/8000, and the bundle is accepted by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] The control (τ = 2.5 N·m) returns no certificate, and the screen makes no claim about the cell.
- [ ] No feasible system is ever excluded (property test over generated systems with a planted feasible point).
- [ ] Every generated infeasible system whose contradiction has a product of at least 1.01 and fits the caps is excluded; systems beyond the caps return `beyond-caps` and no certificate.
- [ ] A float solution whose exact reconstruction fails yields no certificate; nothing is emitted on the strength of the float solve.
- [ ] The proof object lists the binding constraints (those with λ_k > 0) by identifier.
- [ ] The screen is reachable through `mission.refute` and `mission.synthesize` (N25e-11) and its result carries evidence class `Certified`, tier `static`, determinism `exact`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/monomial_screen.rs`:
- `leg_fixture_certificate`: a leg must hold m·g = 12 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 117.72 N at lever arm ℓ and move the foot
  at v = 1.5 m/s through a reduction N, with a motor of torque τ = 2 N·m and speed ω = 80 rad/s. Constraints
  (117.72/2)·ℓ·N⁻¹ ≤ 1 and (1.5/80)·ℓ⁻¹·N ≤ 1: coefficients 2943/50 and 3/160, exponent rows (1, −1) and (−1, 1).
  Expect λ = (1, 1) and product 8829/8000 = 1.103625.
- `leg_control_no_certificate`: τ = 5/2 gives product 8829/10000 = 0.8829; expect none; the point ℓ = 1/5 m,
  N = 10 satisfies both constraints with values 2943/3125 = 0.94176 and 15/16 = 0.9375.
- `box_bounds_as_constraints`: θ ≤ 2 and θ ≥ 3 as (1/2)·θ ≤ 1 and 3·θ⁻¹ ≤ 1: λ = (1, 1), product 3/2.
- `rational_exponent_cleared`: c·θ^(1/2) ≤ 1 with c = 3/2 becomes (9/4)·θ ≤ 1.
- `product_exactly_one_is_not_certificate`; `multiplier_cap_refused` (a system whose only certificate needs
  λ = 65); `bit_cap_refused`.
- `integer_reconstruction_three_constraints`: exponent rows (2, 0), (0, 3), (−1, −1) with coefficients 2, 2, 1:
  the null vector is (3, 2, 6), product 2³·2²·1⁶ = 32 > 1.
- Property tests: `never_excludes_feasible` (plant θ*, draw constraints with value at most 1 at θ*);
  `excludes_planted_infeasible` (plant λ*, scale one coefficient so the product is ρ ≥ 1.01);
  `certificate_reverifies` (every emitted object passes the exact check).

### Known answers and negative controls

Leg fixture: λ = (1, 1), 8829/8000; the certificate reads as physics, the mission needs m·g·v = 176.58 W and the
motor's corner power τ·ω is 160 W. Control: 8829/10000 with the witness (0.2 m, 10). A per-constraint paving over
ℓ ∈ [0.1, 0.4] m, N ∈ [5, 50] finds each constraint satisfiable alone (0.118 at ℓ = 0.1, N = 50 for the first; 0.234
at ℓ = 0.4, N = 5 for the second), with feasible sets ℓ/N ≤ 0.01699 and ℓ/N ≥ 0.01875. Negative controls: a
proposer mutant that compares the product in floating point (must be caught by a differential test on products
within 2⁻⁶⁰ of 1 on both sides, where exact arithmetic decides and a double cannot); a mutant that skips the
exponent check (must be caught by λ = (1, 2) on the leg fixture, whose
exponents do not cancel). No Appendix M number exists for these rows yet. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine;
two solvers; debug and release; identical certificate bytes.

## Considerations

- The screen only excludes. Inclusion, and cells the screen does not decide, go to the per-constraint lane.
- Coefficients carry measured constants (masses, ratings). The certificate is exact about the stated rationals;
  whether 117.72 N is the right load is a fidelity question outside the certificate.
- Large exponents or many constraints inflate the product; the caps turn that into `beyond-caps`, never into an
  approximate verdict.
- KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT applies: one accepted false certificate disables the kind until the cause is found.
