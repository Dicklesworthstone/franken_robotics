## Background

A design studio spends most of its compute acquiring controllers for candidate bodies. A utility bracket is a way to
stop spending controller effort on a body that cannot win. For a mission whose task declares a utility u ∈ [0, 1] (a
success probability, a normalized payload, a normalized energy margin), a design d and a world law, a bracket is a
pair L_d ≤ U*_d ≤ Ū_d, where U*_d is the best value any admitted causal controller achieves. The upper end Ū_d comes
from relaxations that cover every controller (any contact force inside an outer wrench cone, any torque under the
card envelope, the energy budget with every admitted energy source, reach and deadline limits, any observation a
controller could wish for). The lower end L_d is achieved performance of one frozen controller. If Ū_d < L_c for a
competitor c, design d is dominated and receives no further training, fine-tuning or planner budget in that
comparison: the dominance stop.

The stop is sound only if the upper end really is an upper bound. The standing hazard is a relaxation that leaves
out a resource. Worked case (every number an exact consequence of the stated model): a carrier of body mass 10 kg
lifts a payload p through h = 2 m on a ramp with 300 J of usable stored energy at the foot, and the mission admits
arrival at the foot at up to 1.5 m/s. Utility is p divided by 10 kg. Every controller obeys
(10 + p)·(g·h − v²/2) ≤ 300 J, so 10 + p ≤ 300/(19.62 − 1.125) = 16.22 kg and Ū = 0.622. A relaxation that leaves out
the approach energy gives 10 + p ≤ 300/19.62 = 15.29 kg and Ū = 0.529. Against a competitor with L_c = 0.60 the
incomplete relaxation issues a dominance stop, and it is false: p = 6 kg is feasible with a run-up, because
16 × 9.81 × 2 = 313.92 J ≤ 300 + ½·16·1.5² = 318 J. The sound bound 0.622 does not stop the design.

The mechanism is Frontier [F] (hypothesis H-25: brackets on what a body can achieve), so a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier runs before the product work. This bead is that pilot. Rejected alternative: piloting on a contact-rich
full-order task (there the bound is often vacuous, Ū = 1, and a vacuous bound cannot produce a false stop, so the
pilot would have no power).

## Scope

In: a standalone exact program that defines the family, solves each pair by dynamic programming, computes the
bracket ends two ways (complete and deliberately incomplete relaxation), applies the dominance rule and counts false
stops. Out: the product bracket (N25e-14), the labels and the stop in the ladder (N25e-15), the held-out brief
(N25e-16), the kill decision (N25e-17).

## Technical approach

Location (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/pilots/utility_bracket/`; exact rationals; g = 981/100 m/s².

1. Family (class `synthetic`). Design: body mass m_b ∈ {8, 10, 12} kg, usable stored energy E ∈ {200, 250, 300,
   350} J, drive force cap F_max ∈ {60, 120} N: 24 designs. Scenario: ramp height h ∈ {1, 3/2, 2, 5/2} m at a
   slope of 30° (length 2h), admitted arrival speed at the foot v_max ∈ {0, 3/4, 3/2} m/s, rolling-loss
   coefficient c ∈ {0, 1/50}: 24 scenarios. 576 design and scenario pairs. Payload p on a grid of 1/100 kg in
   [0, 10]; utility u = p/10.
2. Discretized plant. The ramp is cut into 64 segments of length Δs = h/32. On each segment the controller
   chooses a drive force in {0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1}·F_max. With M = m_b + p, kinetic energy changes by
   F·Δs − M·g·(Δs/2) − c·M·g·Δs·(√3/2 rounded outward to a rational), stored energy falls by F·Δs, no
   regeneration. The carrier starts at the foot with any speed in [0, v_max] and must reach the top with
   nonnegative kinetic energy at every segment boundary.
3. Truth by dynamic programming. State (segment index, number of force quanta spent). Kinetic energy is an
   exact function of the state and the start speed (taken at v_max, which dominates). A payload is feasible if
   a path reaches the top with stored energy and kinetic energy never negative. U*_d(w) is the largest feasible
   grid payload over 10. Exact, 65 × 257 states per payload.
4. Upper end, complete relaxation: energy balance with every admitted source,
   (m_b + p)·(g·h·(1 + c·√3) − v_max²/2) ≤ E, and the force-cap necessary condition
   (m_b + p)·(g·h·(1 + c·√3) − v_max²/2) ≤ F_max·2h (the drive does at most F_max times the ramp length of work).
   √3 enters as a rational enclosure, rounded outward. Ū is the largest p allowed by both, over 10, rounded
   outward. Upper end, incomplete relaxation: the same without the term v_max²/2 (the seeded defect).
5. Lower end: a frozen controller ("full force until the remaining stored energy just covers the climb, then
   coast"), evaluated on the discretized plant; L is its achieved utility, exactly.
6. Checks per pair: L ≤ U* ≤ Ū (complete). For every ordered pair of designs within one scenario: a dominance
   stop is issued when Ū_d < L_c; it is false when U*_d ≥ L_c and a controller for d achieving at least L_c
   exists (read from the DP). Count false stops under each relaxation.
7. The worked case is pair (m_b = 10, E = 300, F_max = 120; h = 2, v_max = 3/2, c = 0) with a competitor fixed at
   L_c = 3/5: complete Ū = 0.622, incomplete Ū = 0.529, DP optimum 0.622 (p = 6.22 kg is the largest grid payload
   under the energy balance: 16.22 × 18.495 = 299.99 J, which is 160 force quanta of 1.875 J; the force cap does
   not bind).
8. Output: `pilot_utility_bracket.json` (per pair: U*, L, both Ū, labels) and `docs/pilots/utility_bracket.md`
   with the falsifier outcome and the share of design pairs in which a sound stop was available (ceiling
   evidence).

Falsifier (fixed before the run): one Ū below the optimum, one L above it, or one false dominance stop, under the
complete relaxation. The incomplete relaxation must produce at least one false stop, which shows the pilot can
fail. Ceiling, stated first: the stop removes controller-stage work only. If a fraction x of candidates is dominated
before training and computing the bounds costs a fraction y of baseline controller work, the stage shrinks by
1/((1 − x) + y): 10 at x = 0.93 and y = 0.03, and 1.4 at x = 0.3 (illustrative values).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The program solves all 576 pairs by dynamic programming in exact arithmetic and writes the JSON and the report, in under 5 minutes on one core (TARGET).
- [ ] Under the complete relaxation: every Ū is at or above the optimum, every L is at or below it, and the count of false dominance stops is zero.
- [ ] The worked case reports Ū = 0.622 (16.22 kg), the incomplete bound 0.529 (15.29 kg), and feasibility of p = 6 kg with a run-up (313.92 J against 318 J).
- [ ] Under the incomplete relaxation the worked case is a false stop against L_c = 0.60, and the report lists every false stop it causes across the family.
- [ ] The report states the falsifier, its outcome, the ceiling formula and the measured share of pairs with a sound stop.
- [ ] Output is byte-identical across two runs and across the two hosts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/pilots/utility_bracket/tests.rs`: `energy_balance_worked_case` (300/(19.62 − 1.125) =
16.2206 kg; 300/19.62 = 15.2905 kg); `run_up_feasible` (16 kg: 313.92 ≤ 318); `dp_matches_closed_form_lossless`
(with c = 0 and a force cap that does not bind, the DP optimum equals the grid floor of the energy-balance payload
computed with the stored energy the drive can actually spend, ⌊E/q⌋·q for the force quantum q = (F_max/4)·Δs =
F_max·h/128; on the worked case E = 300 J is exactly 160 quanta of 1.875 J and the two coincide, while for E = 200 J
at F_max = 120 N and h = 1 m, 213 quanta of 0.9375 J give 199.6875 J and the DP optimum may sit below the floor of
the bound computed with E; the complete Ū, computed with E, stays an upper bound in both cases);
`force_cap_binds` (m_b = 12 kg, E = 300 J, F_max = 60 N; h = 2 m, v_max = 3/2 m/s, c = 0: the energy balance alone
allows p ≤ 300/18.495 − 12 = 4.22 kg, the force cap allows only (m_b + p) ≤ 240/18.495 = 12.976 kg, p ≤ 0.97 on the
grid; the DP optimum is 0.97 kg or below, strictly below 4.22, and the complete Ū = 0.0976 bounds it);
`no_false_stop_complete`; `false_stop_incomplete` (worked case); `frozen_controller_below_optimum`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Worked case: 16.22 kg and 0.622; 15.29 kg and 0.529; 313.92 J against 318 J (N25-KA's late row `augur-late-06`,
cited by the KA number N38a-40 issues to that slug from N25-KA's block; a tag in a test name or derivation file
fails `provisional-tag-past-due`). Negative control: the incomplete
relaxation (must yield a false stop). Second control: a lower end computed on development addresses with a
controller tuned after seeing the scenario (must be flagged as not a frozen controller). Metamorphic relations:
scaling E, F_max, both masses and the payload grid by one factor k scales every payload (optimum, both Ū, L) by k
and leaves every label unchanged (kinetic, potential, loss and drive energies all scale by k, so feasibility is
unchanged; scaling E and the masses without F_max would move the force-cap constraint and is not a relation);
adding a scenario with v_max = 0 makes the two relaxations coincide. Determinism: class D0.

## Considerations

- This is the utility form of the sill case of proof rule 1 (a quasi-static work cut excludes a crossing that
  0.05 J of approach energy permits). The same lesson: a bound covers only what its relaxation encloses.
- A pass says the bracket logic is sound on a family where truth is computable. It says nothing about how often
  the bound is informative on real briefs; that is N25e-16's measurement.
- If the pilot finds a false stop under the complete relaxation, the relaxation is wrong and is fixed before the
  gate's measurement (N25e-16 Part A); a false stop in that recorded measurement meets the first trigger of
  KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET, which grants no repair cycle (N25e-17).
