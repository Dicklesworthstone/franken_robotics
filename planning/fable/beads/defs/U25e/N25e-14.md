## Background

Proof rule 1 of the mission semantics excludes a design by relaxing the controller and the dynamics (any contact
force inside an outer wrench cone, any torque under an upper envelope, any observation the controller might wish
for) and showing the relaxation infeasible. Proof rule 2 includes a design by exhibiting one causal controller that
works. A utility bracket extends both from feasibility to a bounded task utility u ∈ [0, 1]. For a design d and a
world law, the bracket is L_d ≤ U*_d ≤ Ū_d, where U*_d is the best value any admitted causal controller achieves. It
gives the evaluation ladder a sound reason to stop spending controller effort on a design (N25e-15). Tier Frontier
[F], hypothesis H-25. The pilot N25e-13 must have shown zero false stops on the solvable family first.

Honest limits, which shape the design: a bound over all controllers is only as tight as its relaxation. For
contact-rich full-order tasks it is often vacuous (Ū = 1); the bracket then says nothing and has cost one small
program. Whole-body certifying relaxations are not built here. The value concentrates in briefs limited by energy,
torque, reach or a deadline. Rejected alternatives: an upper end fitted from the best controller seen so far (that
is a lower end; it bounds nothing from above); an upper end from a learned predictor (an estimate cannot justify
removing work on soundness grounds).

## Scope

In: the upper end per scenario and over a world law, the lower end, their certificates and confidence levels, the
proof objects. Out: the labels, the multiple-comparison correction and the stop (N25e-15); the comparison
experiment (N25e-16).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-mission/src/bracket/` (DESIGN); relaxations are requested from `frob-analyze`.

Upper end Ū_d(w) for one scenario w:
1. Assemble the relaxation of rule 1 for (d, w): every contact force inside an outer wrench cone; every torque
   under the card envelope; the energy budget of the energy-feasibility analysis (A-20) with every admitted
   energy source (stored, injected, and approach kinetic energy); reach limits; deadline limits; and full
   observation (the controller is given any observation it could wish for). Each relaxation states the set
   inclusions that make it a relaxation and the list `encloses` of states and energy sources it covers.
2. Enclosure check before any solve: every state and energy source the mission declares must appear in
   `encloses`. If one is missing the upper end is not issued (`FROB-REF-551`: an exclusion from an analysis
   whose model does not enclose a declared state or energy source; repairs: bind the missing source, or accept
   `unresolved`).
3. Optimize the relaxation for u instead of testing it for feasibility: maximize u subject to the relaxed
   constraints, a linear or second-order-cone program solved by the one cone solver, on the CPU.
4. Certify by the dual. For a program max cᵀx subject to A·x ≤ b, x in a cone, any dual-feasible y gives the
   bound bᵀy. Round the solver's dual to rationals, verify dual feasibility exactly, and set Ū_d(w) to the
   exact bᵀy rounded outward. The proof object is a Farkas certificate (`lp-farkas` or `conic-farkas`) that the
   relaxation together with u > Ū_d(w) is infeasible. If reconstruction fails, Ū_d(w) = 1 (vacuous) with the
   reason recorded; a solver failure never produces a bound below 1.
5. The binding constraint is read from the dual (largest multiplier contribution) and reported.

Upper end over a world law: on n scenarios sampled from the law (one sample shared by every design in the
comparison), the mean of Ū_d(w) carries a one-sided upper confidence bound at the level the comparison assigns
(N25e-15): for values in [0, 1], mean + √(ln(1/α′)/(2n)) (DESIGN: a distribution-free bound; an anytime-valid
confidence sequence is used instead when the study is declared sequential, never mixed). Per-scenario bounds are
`Certified`; the bound over the law is `Estimated`, and the claim product takes the weakest.

Lower end L_d: achieved performance. Rollouts of one frozen controller (identified by its content hash) on
confirmation addresses that its development never read; the address set is checked against the controller's
lineage and an overlap is refused (`FROB-REF-843`). L_d is a one-sided lower confidence bound at the comparison's
level: for a binary utility an exact binomial bound, for a bounded utility mean − √(ln(1/α′)/(2n)). Class `Tested`.
A stronger controller raises L_d and leaves Ū_d valid.

Types (DESIGN):

```rust
pub struct BracketEnd { value: Ratio, level: Ratio, class: EvidenceClass, proofs: Vec<ProofRef>,
                        binding: Option<ConstraintRef>, encloses: Vec<ResourceRef>, n: u64 }
pub struct Bracket { design: DesignId, world_sample: SampleId, lower: BracketEnd, upper: BracketEnd,
                     controller: ControllerId, vacuous: bool }
```

Ledger: every bracket writes both ends, the world sample, the controller identity, the level and the proof
references. Determinism: upper ends D0 given the scenario set; lower ends in the determinism class of their
rollouts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the solvable ramp-and-carrier family (576 pairs, N25e-13) every Ū is at or above the dynamic-programming optimum and every L is at or below it.
- [ ] The worked case returns Ū = 0.622 with the energy balance as binding constraint, and its Farkas certificate is accepted by `frob-verify`.
- [ ] The relaxation without approach energy is refused with `FROB-REF-551` before any solve; it never yields a bound.
- [ ] A dual that cannot be reconstructed exactly yields the vacuous bound 1 with its reason.
- [ ] A lower end whose confirmation addresses overlap the controller's development lineage is refused.
- [ ] Both ends carry their level, class, sample identity and proof references; the bracket's class is the weakest of its parts.
- [ ] A CPU reference path exists for every step; cancellation drains (finished scenarios are kept).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/bracket_ends.rs`: `worked_case_upper` (m_b = 10 kg, E = 300 J, h = 2 m, v_max = 3/2 m/s:
Ū = 0.622, dual multiplier on the energy row); `incomplete_relaxation_refused` (the same without approach energy in
`encloses`); `dual_bound_is_exact` (a two-variable linear program with known optimum 7/3: the reconstructed dual
gives exactly 7/3); `reconstruction_failure_is_vacuous`; `hoeffding_upper` (n = 200, mean 0.5, α′ = 1/100: bound
0.5 + √(ln 100/400) = 0.607); `binomial_lower` (n = 100 successes 80, level 1/100: lower bound 0.69 to two
digits); `lineage_overlap_refused`; `stronger_controller_raises_lower_only`; property test
`upper_never_below_dp_optimum` over the 576 pairs.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_utility_bracket.sh` (N25e-16) drives `frob mission refute` and `frob eval compare --bracket`
and logs both ends per design with the STD-E2E helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Worked case: (10 + p)·(19.62 − 1.125) ≤ 300 gives 16.22 kg and Ū = 0.622; the incomplete relaxation gives 15.29 kg
and 0.529 and must be refused. Negative controls: the incomplete relaxation; a lower end from a controller tuned on
the confirmation addresses; an upper end taken from the primal solver value without the dual (a planted solver
that stops early returns a primal value below the optimum, and the test must reject it). Determinism lanes: twice
on one machine; two solvers; debug and release.

## Considerations

- Vacuous bounds are a result, not a failure: the report says the bracket is uninformative for that brief.
- The confidence levels are set by the comparison, not here; an end computed at one level must not be reused at
  a stricter one without recomputation.
- KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET (N25e-17): on a false stop, or a gain under 3 times, brackets become advisory labels.
- The upper end over a law is `Estimated`; a dominance stop that relies on it is recorded with that class.
