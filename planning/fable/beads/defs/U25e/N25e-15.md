## Background

The evaluation ladder scores a design by cheap methods first and expensive ones later (static checks, a shared
policy, a fine-tuned policy, a specialist, the referee, a rare-failure bound). Most of its cost is controller
effort: training, fine-tuning and planner samples. A utility bracket (N25e-14) gives for each design d a certified
upper end Ū_d on what any admitted causal controller can achieve and an achieved lower end L_d from one frozen
controller, with L_d ≤ U*_d ≤ Ū_d. This bead turns brackets into four labels and one action, the dominance stop,
and exposes them through two verbs. Tier Frontier [F]; owners: `frob-mission` produces both ends, `frob-opt`
applies the labels and the stop in the ladder.

Only the dominance stop removes work, and it is sound only when both bounds hold at once. That is why the
confidence levels are split across all designs of the comparison, and why a bound whose relaxation omits a declared
resource is refused. The stop ends effort inside one comparison. It is not an exclusion from the outer set: a
dominated design may still satisfy the mission. Rejected alternatives: stopping on a point estimate of the upper
end (unsound); letting a `controller-limited` label demote a design (the label says the opposite: the body may be
good and the controller is what is missing).

## Scope

In: the label rule, the level split, the stop and its ledger record, `eval.compare --bracket`, the upper end in
`mission.refute`, the refusal for a non-enclosing relaxation, the audit hook. Out: computing the ends (N25e-14);
the budget curves a controller-limited design escalates along (the Atelier chapter's mechanism); the comparison
experiment and gate (N25e-16).

## Technical approach

Labels. With m the comparison's margin, u_req the requirement threshold and k the number of designs the comparison
keeps:

| Condition | Label | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Ū_d < u_req | `body-limited` | An exclusion under proof rule 1, with its certificate |
| Ū_d − L_d ≤ m | `saturated` | More controller effort cannot change a decision at this margin |
| Ū_d − L_d > m, and Ū_d at or above the k-th best lower end | `controller-limited` | Effort belongs on the controller; the design escalates along its controller-budget curve |
| Ū_d < L_c for a competitor c | `dominated` | The dominance stop: d receives no further training, fine-tuning or planner budget in this comparison |

Evaluation order (DESIGN): `body-limited`, then `dominated`, then `saturated`, then `controller-limited`. The rule
is total: if Ū_d is below the k-th best lower end there are k competitors with a lower end above Ū_d, so d is
dominated. A vacuous bracket (Ū_d = 1 with no informative relaxation) is labelled `controller-limited` or
`saturated` by the same arithmetic and flagged `bracket: vacuous`.

Levels. With M designs compared at study level α, each one-sided bound is taken at level α/(2M) (DESIGN: a union
bound over 2M bounds). A design's bounds across scenarios share one world sample. The level is recorded on every
end, and the stop refuses ends computed at a looser level.

The stop, in `crates/frob-opt/src/ladder/bracket.rs`:
1. Before each rung transition, request brackets for the designs still in the comparison (cost is one small
   program per design and scenario for the upper end; the lower end reuses rollouts the rung already paid for
   when their addresses are confirmation addresses, otherwise it requests them).
2. Apply the labels. For each `dominated` design, record the competitor c, both certificates (the upper end of
   d and the lower end of c), the level, the world sample, and remove d from the allocation of every later
   rung of this comparison. The design keeps its verdict status; nothing is written to the mission answer's
   outer set.
3. `body-limited` designs are handed to `frob-mission` as rule-1 exclusions with their certificates.
4. `controller-limited` designs are passed to the escalation rule of the controller-budget curves; `saturated`
   designs proceed without extra controller budget.
5. Audit hook: dominated designs are a stratum of the screening audit. The audit samples them at the rate in
   the campaign manifest and trains them like survivors (blinded: the evaluating job cannot tell an audit
   design from a survivor). A sampled dominated design that reaches at least its competitor's lower end is a
   false stop.
6. A stop is reversible only by a new comparison; within the comparison it is final and logged.

Verbs.
- `frob mission refute <mission> --design <d> --utility --json` returns Ū_d with its binding constraint, the
  relaxation's `encloses` list, the certificate reference and the label `body-limited` when Ū_d < u_req.
- `frob eval compare <designs…> --bracket --json` returns per design both ends (value, level, class, n), the
  label, and for a dominated design the competitor and both certificates; plus the comparison's α, M and the
  shared world sample. `--bracket advisory` prints labels and takes no action (the kill-gate fallback).
- Refusal: a request to stop on a bound whose relaxation does not enclose a declared state or energy source is
  refused under the existing rule for exclusions, `FROB-REF-551`; pointer at the missing resource; repairs: bind
  the missing source, or accept `unresolved`. Exit 3.

Reporting: campaign reports print the label beside the rank; a `controller-limited` design is never described as
inferior; the count of stops, the controller effort avoided and the audit outcome are in the phase ledger.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the solvable family, with brackets at level α/(2M), the stop issues zero false dominance stops; the run-up candidate (Ū = 0.622 against L_c = 0.60) is not stopped.
- [ ] A stop requested on the relaxation without approach energy is refused with `FROB-REF-551`.
- [ ] `eval.compare --bracket` returns both ends, the label and, for a dominated design, the competitor and both certificates, each of which resolves through `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] `mission.refute --utility` returns Ū_d and its binding constraint.
- [ ] A dominated design receives no allocation at any later rung of the comparison (asserted from the allocation ledger), and its mission verdict is unchanged.
- [ ] Labels are a pure function of the ends, m, u_req and k (property test); the label table is total.
- [ ] `--bracket advisory` changes no allocation.
- [ ] The audit stratum for dominated designs exists and its results reach the rank-agreement ledger.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/bracket_labels.rs`: `label_table_rows` (u_req = 0.5, m = 0.05, k = 2: (L, Ū) = (0.30, 0.45)
body-limited; (0.58, 0.62) saturated; (0.40, 0.90) with second-best lower end 0.60 controller-limited; (0.40, 0.55)
with a competitor at L_c = 0.60 dominated); `label_total` (random ends always receive exactly one label);
`level_split` (α = 0.05, M = 25 gives 0.001 per bound); `looser_level_refused`; `stop_removes_allocation`;
`stop_does_not_touch_outer_set`; `advisory_changes_nothing`; `non_enclosing_refused`; `vacuous_bracket_flagged`;
`audit_false_stop_detected` (a planted bracket with an understated upper end: the audited design beats its
competitor and the audit reports a false stop).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_utility_bracket.sh` (N25e-16) runs both verbs on the solvable family and on the held-out brief
and logs labels, levels, stops and the allocation ledger with the STD-E2E helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Worked case: sound bound 0.622 against L_c = 0.60 gives no stop; the incomplete bound 0.529 would stop and is
refused. Ceiling arithmetic that reports must reproduce: stage shrink 1/((1 − x) + y) = 10 at x = 0.93, y = 0.03,
and 1.37 at x = 0.3, y = 0.03. Negative controls: the understated upper end (audit must catch it); a stop taken at
level α instead of α/(2M) (must be refused); a label computed from point estimates (planted mutant; the property
test with the solvable family must show a false stop). Determinism lanes: labels and stops identical twice on one
machine and across worker counts, given the same ends.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET (N25e-17): one false stop on the solvable family or in an audited campaign; or less than 3
  times lower controller effort at matched decisions; or the label changes the agent's next action in under a
  tenth of comparisons. Fallback: advisory labels, no automatic stop.
- The end-to-end gain is capped by the controller stage's share of the phase ledger; reports state that share.
- With many designs the level α/(2M) is strict and brackets widen; the stop then fires less often, which is the
  correct price of soundness.
- Interaction with the controller-budget curves: both may flag `controller-limited`; the bracket's flag is the
  certified one and takes precedence in reports.
