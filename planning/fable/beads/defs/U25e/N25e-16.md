## Background

A utility bracket gives each candidate design a certified upper end Ū_d on the task utility any admitted causal
controller can reach and an achieved lower end L_d. A design whose upper end is below a competitor's lower end is
dominated and gets no further controller effort in that comparison (the dominance stop). The mechanism is Frontier:
it is funded only if it is sound where truth is known and worth its cost where it is not.

GATE-AUGUR-28 (claims: Known answer and Design comparison; M4; thresholds TARGET) has two halves. Known answer: on
an exhaustively solvable planar ramp-and-carrier family (states and controls discretized so the optimum of each of
at least 500 design and scenario pairs is known by dynamic programming), every Ū is at or above the optimum, every L
is at or below it, and there are zero false dominance stops; the run-up candidate is not stopped and the relaxation
without approach energy is rejected. Design comparison: on a held-out brief limited by energy or torque, controller
effort per accepted decision is at least 3 times lower than static filters followed by the ladder, at matched
decisions.

"Matched decisions" means both arms reach the same accepted answers, as adjudicated; a saving bought by a different
answer does not count. The comparator arm is the strongest ordinary pipeline: the static filters, then the ladder
with its own rules, no brackets. This bead owns the script for both halves and the experiment protocol for the
second. Rejected alternative: measuring the saving on the solvable family (its controller stage is trivial, so a
ratio there would say nothing about real briefs).

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/augur_utility_bracket.sh`; the protocol, pre-registration and analysis of the held-out comparison;
the gate record. Out: the mechanism (N25e-14, N25e-15); the kill decision (N25e-17).

## Technical approach

Part A, known answer, through the public verbs. Fixture: the 576 pairs of N25e-13 exported as designs and
scenarios (`fixtures/augur/ramp-carrier/`), with the dynamic-programming optimum pinned in `truth.json`.
1. For each scenario: `frob eval compare <24 designs> --task ramp-carrier --bracket --scenario <w> --level 0.05
   --key ub-<run>-<w> --json` (options of BD-262 and N25e-15; a run verb: one key per scenario, so that no
   scenario is answered from another's receipt); read both ends, labels and stops.
2. Assert for every pair: Ū ≥ pinned optimum; L ≤ pinned optimum. Count violations (threshold 0).
3. For every issued stop (d dominated by c): assert the pinned optimum of d is below L_c (otherwise it is a false
   stop). Count false stops (threshold 0).
4. Named case: design (10 kg, 300 J, 120 N) on scenario (h = 2 m, v_max = 1.5 m/s, lossless) with a competitor
   pinned at L_c = 0.60: assert Ū = 0.622 and no stop. Then `frob mission refute
   fixtures/augur/ramp-carrier/carrier.fmission --design <that design> --utility --key ub-<run>-refute --json`:
   assert Ū = 0.622, the binding constraint is the energy balance, and the `encloses` list names approach kinetic
   energy.
5. Seeded defect: `frob eval compare <24 designs> --task ramp-carrier --bracket --scenario <the named scenario>
   --relaxation energy-no-approach --key ub-<run>-551 --json` through `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-551 3`; assert
   the pointer names approach kinetic energy and no bound is written.
6. `frob-verify` on every upper-end certificate under `env -i`; assert `accept`.
7. Negative control of the gate: the build whose complete relaxation omits the approach kinetic energy while
   still listing it in `encloses` (so the enclosure check of step 5 does not catch it), a registered mutation
   control in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch of
   `frob-mission`, built with `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-mission/nc-<nnnn>`, numbered at registration
   by N38b-04), rerun on steps 1 to 4 with keys `ub-<run>-mut-…`; Part A's checks must fail (the run-up candidate
   is stopped at Ū = 0.529 against L_c = 0.60, a false stop by the pinned optimum 0.622).
8. `summary` with the receipt hash of Part A and, once the campaign is read back, of Part B. The script never
   records a value (STD-E2E's `self-recording` lint): both halves are recorded from those receipts, with the
   counts, x, y, the ceiling, r with its lower bound and the audit outcome, by a session that did not implement
   N25e-14 or N25e-15, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUGUR-28 --receipt
   <hash>` (N38b-19; the gate has one part, so no `--part`), which refuses the record unless the control of
   step 7 is listed as caught in the same run (`pass-without-control`, `control-not-caught`).

Part B, design comparison. Pre-registered in `docs/prereg/augur_bracket_brief.md` before either arm runs: the
brief, the two arms, the metric, the matching rule, the threshold, the ceiling.
1. Brief: one held-out design brief whose binding limits are energy or torque (for example payload transport up a
   ramp under a battery budget), with at least 30 candidate designs from the morphology generator and a sealed
   world sample.
2. Arms, at equal compute caps and the same candidates, worlds and ladder table: arm S, static filters followed
   by the ladder; arm B, the same with brackets and the dominance stop enabled.
3. Metric: controller effort per accepted decision, in the phase ledger's unit (GPU-seconds of training,
   fine-tuning and planner sampling, plus the cost of computing the brackets, charged to arm B). Ratio
   r = effort_S / effort_B with a 95% paired lower bound over world-sample replicates.
4. Matching: the accepted decisions of the two arms are compared by the adjudication rule of the brief; the
   ratio is reported only if they match.
5. Ceiling before speed: with x the fraction of candidates dominated before training and y the bracket cost as
   a fraction of baseline controller work, the stage ratio is at most 1/((1 − x) + y); the measured x and y are
   recorded first, and a claimed ratio above that ceiling is an accounting error. The end-to-end effect is
   capped by the controller stage's share of the phase ledger.
6. Audit: dominated designs are sampled and trained by the screening audit; a sampled design that reaches its
   competitor's lower end is a false stop and fails the gate.
7. Behaviour measure for the kill gate: the share of comparisons in which the label changed the agent's next
   action (a stop taken, an escalation chosen), from the session log.
8. Pass: r ≥ 3 at the 95% paired lower bound, at matched decisions, with zero audited false stops.

Script: `scripts/e2e/augur_utility_bracket.sh` runs Part A fully and Part B's smoke form (a 6-design slice of the
brief, to prove the pipeline end to end); the full Part B campaign is launched by the same script with
`--campaign` under a job handle and its result is read back for the gate record.

Logging (STD-E2E): `target/e2e-logs/augur_utility_bracket/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line:
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-16`), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
block. `metric` lines: pair identifier, optimum, L, Ū, label, stop competitor; for Part B, effort per accepted
decision per arm with units, x, y, the ceiling, r and its lower bound against the threshold 3. `refusal` lines for
the seeded defect. On failure: the first violating pair or the first false stop with its certificates' paths.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-28, known-answer half (TARGET, M4): on at least 500 solvable pairs every Ū at or above the optimum, every L at or below it, zero false dominance stops; the run-up candidate not stopped; the relaxation without approach energy rejected. Recorded with counts through `record_gate` (N38b-19) from the script's receipt by a session that did not implement N25e-14 or N25e-15.
- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-28, comparison half (TARGET, M4): controller effort per accepted decision at least 3 times lower than static filters followed by the ladder, at matched decisions, at a 95% paired lower bound; recorded with x, y, the ceiling and the measured ratio, whatever its value.
- [ ] The pre-registration is hashed before the first arm runs, and the comparison is refused if its claimed ratio exceeds the recorded ceiling.
- [ ] Zero audited false stops; the audit sample size and outcome are in the record.
- [ ] The share of comparisons in which a label changed the next action is recorded (input to KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET).
- [ ] The script exits 0 on success and non-zero on any failed assertion; Part A runs on a host with no GPU; the registered control of step 7 makes Part A fail and is listed as caught in the receipt.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `bracket_selftest_false_stop.sh` (a pinned optimum raised above L_c for
one stopped design makes Part A fail and names the pair); `bracket_selftest_unmatched.sh` (arms with different
accepted decisions produce no ratio and a clear message); `bracket_selftest_ceiling.sh` (x = 0.3, y = 0.03 gives a
ceiling of 1.37; a claimed ratio of 3 is refused as an accounting error). Analysis code tests in
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/bracket_analysis.rs`: `paired_lower_bound` on a synthetic set with known ratio;
`effort_includes_bracket_cost`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; steps and logging as above.

### Known answers and negative controls

Part A: the worked case (0.622 against 0.60: no stop; 0.529 would stop and is refused). Negative controls: the
incomplete relaxation; a planted understated upper end that the audit must catch; an A/A run of Part B (both arms
without brackets) whose ratio's interval must contain 1. Determinism lanes: Part A twice on one machine, debug and
release, both hosts, identical labels and certificate digests; Part B under the determinism class of its rollouts,
with paired addresses.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET fires on one false stop, on a ratio under 3 at matched decisions, or if the label changes
  the agent's next action in under a tenth of comparisons; then brackets become advisory (N25e-17).
- The 3 times threshold is reachable only when most candidates are dominated before training: at x = 0.7 and
  y = 0.03 the ceiling is 3.03. A brief where bounds are vacuous will fail the comparison half, and that is an
  honest result for that brief class.
- Compute for Part B is a design campaign and is requested under the campaign class of the work package.
