## Background

For a fixed topology with fixed joint axes and link lengths, the inverse dynamics of a prescribed motion is linear
in the inertial parameters. At knot k of a motion (q_k, q̇_k, q̈_k) with active contacts C_k,

  Y(q_k, q̇_k, q̈_k)·π = Sᵀ·τ_k + Σ_{c ∈ C_k} J_c(q_k)ᵀ·λ_{c,k},

where π stacks the ten inertial parameters of every body, S selects the actuated coordinates, Y is the regressor of
the inverse dynamics, J_c the contact Jacobian and λ_{c,k} the contact force. So "where should the battery sit, and
how large must each motor be, for this motion" is one cone program over masses at admissible sites, contact forces
and joint torques. The proposed constructive lane (`mission.synthesize --strategy placement`, crate `frob-synth`)
solves one such program per motion and per catalog choice. It is Frontier [F] (hypothesis H-28). Its ceiling was
stated before any test: the lane decides only the share of a design decision that is placement and sizing for
known motions, and it starts a search; it does not replace one. A conventional optimizer over a dynamics library
can pose the same program, so this is a convenience of the typed core and not a differentiator.

The program rule for Frontier work is a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before funding. The plan names the
pilot: the cart and one quadruped trot with a movable battery, two engineer-days on the CPU. This bead is that
pilot. Rejected alternative: piloting only on the cart (it has no joint torques, no thermal or voltage rows and no
friction cone in three dimensions, so it cannot show whether the full program is well posed).

## Scope

In: a standalone CPU prototype of the program for the two cases, with an existing cone solver; the cart's exact
answers; the trot's solve, sensitivity and timing; the falsifier outcome. Out: the regressor emitted from the
derived kernel (N25e-19; the pilot may build Y numerically by evaluating inverse dynamics on unit parameter
vectors); the product lane (N25e-20); the design-comparison experiment (N25e-21); the kill decision (N25e-22).

## Technical approach

Location (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/pilots/placement_program/`.

Case 1, the cart (exact rationals; g = 981/100 m/s², μ = 1/2). A rear-drive cart with wheelbase 1 m and centre of
mass at floor height: 15 kg fixed at 0.5 m from the rear axle and a 5 kg battery at x_b on a rail, measured from the
rear axle. The rear normal force is N_r = g·[7.5 + 5·(1 − x_b)] and traction needs μ·N_r ≥ 20·a for acceleration a.
1. Sites: the rail is a set of sites at 0, 1/100, …, 1 m; the battery mass is splittable among sites (Σ m_s =
   5 kg, m_s ≥ 0), so the program is exact in the first moment Σ m_s·x_s.
2. Solve at a = 3 m/s²: feasible; maximize the first moment to find the boundary: x_b ≤ 35/654 m (53.5 mm).
3. Largest acceleration over all placements: 0.5 × 9.81 × 12.5/20 = 3.066 m/s² (exactly 981/320), at x_b = 0.
4. Solve at a = 4 m/s²: infeasible for every placement; the dual certificate is the traction inequality (the
   multiplier on μ·N_r ≥ 80 N against the largest attainable N_r = 122.625 N).
5. Controls: the same 4 m/s² motion with all wheels driven is feasible (μ·g = 4.905 m/s²); the original cart
   reaches the same goal by a slower motion. The certificate excludes a motion, not the cart.

Case 2, the trot. The reference quadruped at one nominal trot (knots at the control rate over one stride; diagonal
contact pairs), with a battery of fixed mass movable among 9 sites on a 3 × 3 grid in the trunk.
1. Variables: site masses m_s ≥ 0 with Σ m_s = battery mass; contact forces in the friction cone of the scene's
   interface card at each active foot (second-order cone ‖λ_t‖ ≤ μ·λ_n); joint torques τ_{j,k}.
2. Constraints: the knot equation above; peak |τ_{j,k}| ≤ η·N·τ_peak; thermal ‖τ_j‖₂ ≤ √K·η·N·τ_cont over the K
   knots; voltage R·|τ_{j,k}|/(η·N·k_t) + k_e·N·|q̇_{j,k}| ≤ V_bus (linear because q̇ is data), with the motor's
   resistance R, torque constant k_t, back-EMF constant k_e, gear ratio N and efficiency η from the card.
3. Objective: electrical energy Σ_j Σ_k [R·(τ_{j,k}/(η·N·k_t))² + τ_{j,k}·q̇_{j,k}]·Δt, a convex quadratic.
4. Record: solve status, time, the optimal placement, the energy at the optimum against the energy with the
   battery at the template site, the dual energy lower bound, and the sensitivity of energy to each site.
5. Unsplittable module: round the fractional optimum to its heaviest site, re-solve with the mass fixed there,
   and record the energy gap between relaxation and rounded solution.
6. Non-convexity note reproduced numerically: g(τ, N) = τ²/N² gives g(0, 1) = 0, g(2, 3) = 4/9 and g(1, 2) = 1/4 >
   2/9, so gear ratio is enumerated, one program each; run the trot at two catalog ratios.

Falsifier (DESIGN, fixed before the run; the plan names the pilot and leaves its falsifier to the kill gate): the
pilot fails if the cart's three answers are not reproduced exactly; or the trot program reports infeasible for a
motion the referee executes with the template placement (a false exclusion of a motion); or a single trot solve
exceeds 60 s on one core; or the rounded single-site solution is infeasible while a site enumeration finds a
feasible one. Budget: two engineer-days, CPU only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Cart: boundary x_b = 35/654 m at a = 3 m/s², largest acceleration 981/320 m/s², infeasibility at a = 4 m/s² with the traction row as the dual, all in exact rationals; the all-wheel-drive control is feasible.
- [ ] Trot: the program solves at two catalog gear ratios; status, time, optimal placement, energy against the template placement and the dual bound are recorded.
- [ ] The three limits are demonstrated: a single unsplittable module handled by relaxation, rounding and re-solve; the midpoint counterexample for τ²/N²; link lengths held fixed.
- [ ] `docs/pilots/placement_program.md` states the falsifier, its outcome, the ceiling, and the measured solve times.
- [ ] No product crate depends on the pilot code.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/pilots/placement_program/tests.rs`: `cart_boundary_35_over_654`; `cart_max_accel_981_320`;
`cart_infeasible_at_4` (dual verified: 80 N required against 0.5 × 122.625 = 61.3125 N available);
`cart_awd_feasible` (4 ≤ 4.905); `copper_loss_midpoint_nonconvex` (1/4 > 2/9); `voltage_row_convex_in_ratio`
(c₁·|τ| + k_e·|q̇|·N² ≤ V_bus·N defines a convex set in (τ, N): midpoint test on random feasible pairs);
`regressor_numeric_identity` (Y built from unit parameter vectors reproduces inverse dynamics to 10⁻¹² relative on
100 random states of the pilot's quadruped model).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_pilot_placement.sh` runs the pilot binary for both cases and asserts the criteria. It logs
through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/augur_pilot_placement/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript:
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-18`), `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time; `metric` lines with the cart's
exact values as strings, trot solve status, time (threshold 60 s), energies in joules and the rounding gap; on
failure the first failing check and the dump of the program. The `summary` names GATE-AUGUR-29 (pilot reading).

### Known answers and negative controls

Cart: 35/654 m; 3.066 m/s²; infeasible at 4 m/s²; feasible with all wheels driven. Negative controls: a program
that drops the friction cone reports the 4 m/s² motion feasible (must be caught by the dual check); a program that
treats a single module's position as a linear variable in the second moments returns a placement the re-solve
rejects on a fixture where the centre of mass is raised off the floor. Determinism: exact arithmetic for the cart
(class D0); the trot within solver tolerance, with the certificate re-checked in rationals.

## Considerations

- The certificate's statement is the pair (design cell, motion). A design that completes the mission by a
  different motion is untouched; the pilot's control cases make that visible.
- A favourable trot result is not evidence of a better robot: closed-loop evaluation is N25e-21's job.
- If the pilot's falsifier holds, the lane is not funded and candidates start from class templates with the
  existing mass-placement construction and task-wrench analysis (the fallback of KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR).
