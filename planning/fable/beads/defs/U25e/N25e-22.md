## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance; its decision is an architecture decision record at its
milestone, and a fired gate means the fallback is the plan.

KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR governs the fifth constructive lane of `frob-synth`, placement and sizing along a prescribed
motion: one cone program per motion and catalog choice over masses at sites, contact forces and joint torques,
which returns a least-energy placement, an energy lower bound, or a dual certificate that no placement realizes
the motion. The lane also emits a screening signal: the number of library motions a design can realize.

- Trigger: the design-comparison part of GATE-AUGUR-29 fails (on 30 designs and two missions, lane-initialized
  candidates neither beat their class templates by at least 15% in electrical cost of transport in closed-loop
  evaluation at a 95% paired lower bound, nor start within 10% of the optimum that forward co-design reaches); or
  more than 15% of designs infeasible on every library motion pass the mission in closed loop, in which case the
  motion-count screening signal is withdrawn first.
- Fallback: the mass-placement program of the constructive-lanes section (nonnegative masses on a finite basis of
  admissible sites realizing a target pseudo-inertia, with a primal witness or a Farkas exclusion) and the
  task-wrench attainability analysis A-24 as they stand; candidates start from class templates.

The gate has no milestone of its own in the register; it is decided at M4 with GATE-AUGUR-29. It is a two-step
gate: the second clause first removes one output of the lane, and only the first clause removes the lane.

## Scope

In: reading the record of N25e-21, the two-step decision, the record, the switch. Out: the measurements.

## Technical approach

1. Inputs from N25e-21's gate record: the paired improvement of lane-initialized candidates over templates with
   its 95% lower bound; the ratio to the forward co-design optimum; the count and share of designs infeasible on
   every library motion that pass the mission; the known-answer results (cart, regressor identity, routing).
2. Step one, the screening signal. If the share of infeasible-everywhere designs that pass exceeds 15%:
   `realizable_motions` is withdrawn as a screening signal. Mechanically: the field stays in the lane's result
   as a diagnostic with `screening: withdrawn`; the ladder's static rung no longer accepts it as a reason to
   discard; any discard already made on it in an open campaign is re-opened. The lane itself continues if the
   comparison part passed.
3. Step two, the lane. If the comparison part failed after one repair cycle (a repair may enlarge the motion
   library or fix a card mapping, and requires a full re-run on the frozen design set):
   - `--strategy placement` is removed from the default strategies of `mission.synthesize` and remains
     available only behind an explicit flag labelled `parked`;
   - candidates start from class templates; placement questions are answered by A-24 (BD-348) with the
     mass-support predicate (`FROB-REF-106`), and by the static mass-placement program of §25.21 (N25d-08) only
     where its own pilot funded it: PILOT-REGRESSOR-01 carries that program as its `fallback.also` entry with
     `pilot = "PILOT-PLACEMENT-01"` (N25e-18, N38a-09 rule 5), which ships only while `fundable` answers
     `Fundable` for that pilot's mechanism. This bead does not wait on N25d-08 (convention 10);
   - the regressor kernel (N25e-19) is kept, because identification in the calibration lane uses it;
   - the dual "no placement realizes this prescribed motion" is kept as an analysis when a mission prescribes
     a motion, since its certificate is sound regardless of the comparison's outcome.
4. Known-answer failures (the cart or the regressor identity) are not kill triggers; they are defects that block
   the gate until repaired.
5. Record: the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
   `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`) with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the
   trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the
   trigger's number (the paired improvement's lower bound against 15%, the ratio to F against 1.10, the share of
   infeasible-everywhere designs that pass against 15% with its count, denominator and exact binomial
   interval); the decision per step, shown as `pass`, `signal withdrawn` or `killed: fallback adopted`; the
   fallback now in force and the capability's name from now on ("placement and sizing for library motions",
   or "A-24 and the mass-support predicate, with static mass placement (§25.21) where its pilot funded it" if
   killed); the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or
   "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds; the recording
   session, which did not implement the lane or run the measurements. The kill row of KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR in
   `registries/gates.toml` (`kind = "kill"`) receives its decision as two entries in N40-03's per-scope form,
   because the gate is two-step: `{ scope = "clause:screening", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` and
   `{ scope = "clause:comparison", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, each `fired` or `not-fired`, with
   the receipt hashes of N25e-21's record (the form N40-03 gives KILL-COUPLING's two clauses: N40-29 gives this
   row `scope_kind = "clause"` with the values `screening` and `comparison`, both decided at M4, and N40-03's
   closed table of scope kinds lists them; a single `fired` would put the fallback in force when only the
   signal is withdrawn). The display words map onto them: both `not-fired` is `pass`; screening `fired` is
   `signal withdrawn`; comparison `fired` is `killed: fallback adopted`. The row cites the ADR, which resolves in
   the artifact graph. This bead also writes the
   gate's owner declaration, the block N40-30 compares with the ledger (its check 1 fails
   `owner-declaration-missing` from the M4 exit without it): `[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`, the ledger's
   current `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied from N40-29's row, not restated, and the
   two decision entries with their measured values, each citing its receipt.
6. If not fired: the status table records the measured improvement and the share; the lane stays Frontier and is
   described as "placement and sizing for library motions", no more broadly.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR-M4.md` exists at the M4 exit with every field N40-03 requires and every measured value traceable through `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] The kill row carries the two per-clause decision entries (outcome, ADR path, date, session, receipts), and the register's load checks accept them; its display status is `pass` or `killed: fallback adopted` (with `signal withdrawn` noted where it applies), citing commit, command, output and receipt hash.
- [ ] If the signal is withdrawn: the ladder refuses `realizable_motions` as a discard reason (test on the allocation ledger), and affected discards are re-opened.
- [ ] If the lane is killed: the default strategy list no longer contains `placement`, and `mission.synthesize` on a placement question returns the fallback's answer with the note that the lane is parked: the template candidates with A-24 and the mass-support predicate, and the §25.21 placement only where `fundable` answers `Fundable` for PILOT-PLACEMENT-01's mechanism.
- [ ] The decision session did not implement N25e-19 or N25e-20.
- [ ] The owner declaration exists and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` (N40-30) reports no `trigger-differs`, `fallback-differs` or `decision-copies-differ` for KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-synth/tests/placement_fallback.rs`: `signal_withdrawn_not_a_discard_reason` (with the withdrawal
configuration, the static rung's discard request citing `realizable_motions` is refused);
`parked_lane_absent_from_defaults`; `fallback_without_placement_funding` (with PILOT-PLACEMENT-01 not fundable,
the fallback answer is the A-24 and mass-support form with the `parked` note, and names no §25.21 placement);
`prescribed_motion_dual_survives` (the cart's 4 m/s² dual is still returned
when the mission prescribes the motion). A registry check verifies the gate row cites an existing ADR.
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_regressor_rule.rs`: `rule_matches_ledger_trigger` (the two clauses and the
15% thresholds equal the ledger row's, N40-29, and the owner declaration's trigger text is byte-equal to it);
`two_step_entries` (a planted share of 25% with a passing comparison writes screening `fired` and comparison
`not-fired`, and the fallback is not put in force); `known_answer_failure_blocks` (a failed cart or regressor
identity yields no decision entry and the defect, never `fired`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_kill_regressor_fallback.sh`: reads N25e-21's gate record through `frob evidence trace`,
applies the two-step rule and asserts that the kill row's two entries and the ADR state the same outcomes; in
the fallback profile, runs `frob mission synthesize <cart placement mission> --key kr-fb-<run> --json` and
asserts the fallback's answer with the `parked` note (the A-24 and mass-support form; the §25.21 program's own
answer, where its pilot funded it, is asserted by N25d-08's script, not here); in the signal-withdrawn profile, runs a
two-design ladder (one key per run-verb call) and asserts no discard cites the motion count. It runs once in a
dry-run profile at M4 whatever the decision. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_kill_regressor_fallback/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N25e-22`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, key, exit code, wall time and the evidence
block of the record read; one `metric` line per clause with the measured value, threshold and verdict; the
strategy list and ledger rows; non-zero exit on missing inputs or dangling evidence; a `summary` naming
KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR.

### Known answers and negative controls

Arithmetic of the triggers: 5 passing of 20 infeasible-everywhere designs is 25%, above 15%; 2 of 20 is 10%,
below. A dry run fed a planted comparison failure must produce `killed: fallback adopted`; one fed only a planted
share of 25% must produce `signal withdrawn` and keep the lane. Negative control: an A/A comparison (template
against template) must fail the comparison clause, proving the clause can fail.

## Considerations

- The share clause protects against a quiet harm: discarding designs because the library lacks the motion they
  need. Its denominator can be small; the record states the count and an exact binomial interval beside the
  point share. The trigger is evaluated on the point share as written; a change to that rule needs a dated
  amendment to the register before the measurement.
- The fallback loses a convenience, not a capability no other tool has; a conventional optimizer can pose the
  same program.
- Owner of the decision: the Augur lead; confirmed at the M4 exit review.
- The fallback's static mass-placement program (N25d-08) is itself Frontier and blocked from funding until
  KILL-SYNTH-PLACEMENT is registered or the owner rules it Solid (N25d-07). This decision does not wait on it
  (convention 10: a decision bead closes whichever way): N25d-08 is `related`, not a dependency; the ADR's
  fallback names A-24 and the mass-support predicate FROB-REF-106 unconditionally and the §25.21 program as the
  conditional part, in force only while `fundable` answers `Fundable` for PILOT-PLACEMENT-01's mechanism.
