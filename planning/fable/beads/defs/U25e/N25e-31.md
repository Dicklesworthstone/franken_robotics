## Background

The docking family is the first mission slice: a robot docks in three one-second ticks from x₀ = v₀ = 0, with
acceleration g·u_k held constant over tick k, a hidden fixed gain g, commands bounded by abs(u_k) ≤ U, and target
(x₃, v₃) = (1, 0). Exact zero-order-hold integration gives v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂) and
x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2). The only informative packet is the first velocity reading, which arrives before the
second command. With two possible gains {1, 2} the family has an exact answer. With a manufacturing tolerance on
the gain it does not: for bounded probes and read error ε > 0, any nondegenerate interval of hidden positive gains
contains an indistinguishable pair that requires different second commands, so exact docking fails. The base
chapter leaves the amended game, docking to a finite position tolerance over a continuous gain interval, open.

This bead solves it in closed form and makes Augur return it. It is a second known answer of the docking family: a
least guaranteed tolerance over a continuum of hidden gains, with an irrational algebraic optimum, a rational
witness above it and a rational exclusion below it. Tier Solid [S]; owner `frob-mission`, with `frob-conformance`
for the fixture. What the fixture is for: it checks that Augur returns a minimum that is not rational, that a
closed tolerance is reported as attained, and that a continuum of hidden parameters is settled by endpoint
reasoning with a certificate and not by sampling gains. Rejected alternatives: sampling gains on a grid (a grid
cannot certify a continuum; it is the audit, not the proof); rounding the optimum to a decimal (the answer must
say what the number is).

## Scope

In: the game as a fixture, the lowering by endpoint reasoning, the objective "least guaranteed tolerance", the
algebraic answer with its enclosure and `attained`, the general parametric formula. Out: the certificates, the
witness policy's exhaustive check and the eligibility refusal (N25e-32); the gate script (N25e-33).

## Technical approach

The game. The plant, the three ticks and the packet timing are those of the docking family. The gain g is any
constant in [1, 2]. The command bound is U = 3/4. The first velocity reading r = g·p + e arrives before the second
command, with e in [−ε, +ε] and ε = 1/64, where p = u₀ is the first command (the probe). The robot must be exactly at
rest after the third tick, and the guarantee sought is abs(x₃ − 1) ≤ δ for every admitted g and e. Fixture:
`fixture:docking-gain-interval` with `docking-tolerance.fmission` (objective: minimize δ; claim
`minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`).

The answer. The least guaranteed tolerance is δ* = (√1745 − 41)/32 m, about 24.1624 mm, the positive root of
16·δ² + 41·δ − 1 = 0. It is attained with the first command p* = (39 + √1745)/128, about 0.631041. A 20 mm
requirement is infeasible. A 25 mm requirement is met with the rational probe p = 5/8.

Derivation, which is also the lowering (module `crates/frob-mission/src/lower/docking_interval.rs`):
1. Write c = 2p + u₁. Rest forces u₂ = −p − u₁, and then x₃ = g·c. For p > 0 the limits on u₁ and u₂ confine c to
   [2p − U, p + U].
2. Endpoint reduction over the hidden gain. A reading r admits the gains [a, b] with a = max(1, (r − ε)/p) and
   b = min(2, (r + ε)/p). Because x₃ = g·c is monotone in g for c > 0, the tolerance holds for every admitted gain
   exactly when it holds at a and b: (1 − δ)/a ≤ c ≤ (1 + δ)/b. This sign fact is recorded as the licence for
   replacing the continuum by its endpoints.
3. A common c exists at every reading exactly when three conditions hold.
   - Ambiguity. The ratio b/a is largest at the reading p + ε, where a = 1 and b = 1 + 2ε/p. The interval for c is
     nonempty there exactly when ε/(p + ε) ≤ δ, that is p ≥ ε·(1 − δ)/δ.
   - Reach at the high gain. Where the reading admits g = 2, c is at most (1 + δ)/2, so 2p − U ≤ (1 + δ)/2, that
     is p ≤ (2U + 1 + δ)/4.
   - Reach at the low gain. Where the reading admits g = 1, c is at least 1 − δ, so p + U ≥ 1 − δ.
4. A larger probe eases the first condition and tightens the second. Equating them gives
   4ε·(1 − δ) = δ·(2U + 1 + δ), which is 16·δ² + 41·δ − 1 = 0 at U = 3/4 and ε = 1/64, with p* = (2U + 1 + δ*)/4.
   The third condition is slack. A probe p ≤ 0 gives c ≤ U = 3/4, which is short of 1 − δ in the world g = 1 for
   every δ < 1/4.
5. The error interval and the tolerance are closed, so the optimum is attained and the answer carries
   `attained: true`, unlike the strict two-gain family, whose infimum is not attained.
6. DESIGN arithmetic for other parameter values with U ≥ (1 + δ)/2: the same argument gives the positive root of
   δ² + (2U + 1 + 4ε)·δ − 4ε = 0. The lowering implements this parametric form and checks its side conditions
   (the slack third condition; U ≥ (1 + δ)/2) before using it; outside them it falls back to cells.

Returning an irrational optimum (DESIGN). `CostBounds` gains an optional algebraic value:

```rust
pub struct AlgebraicRoot { polynomial: Vec<Ratio>,       // coefficients, lowest degree first: [-1, 41, 16]
                           isolating: (Ratio, Ratio),    // rational enclosure of the root, width ≤ requested
                           which: RootSelector }         // the unique root in the isolating interval
pub struct CostBounds { incumbent: Ratio, lower_bound: Ratio, unit: Unit, attained: bool, gap: Ratio,
                        optimum: Option<AlgebraicRoot> }
```
`incumbent` is the upper end of the enclosure (certified feasible, N25e-32), `lower_bound` the lower end
(certified infeasible below it), `gap` their difference, at most 10⁻⁹ m by default (`--enclosure 1e-9`). The
enclosure is found by exact bisection on the sign of the polynomial; no floating-point square root enters the
answer. The answer also reports p* as an algebraic number with its own enclosure.

Result fields: `cost_bounds.optimum`, `attained: true`, the witness probe for the incumbent, the binding
conditions (ambiguity and reach at the high gain), and `next_actions` naming the hardware trade (a smaller read
error or a larger command bound, each with the formula's sensitivity). Evidence: `Certified`, tier `static`
(the exact discrete-time plant), determinism `exact`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `mission.synthesize` with the objective "least guaranteed tolerance" returns the minimum as the root of 16·δ² + 41·δ − 1 with a rational enclosure no wider than 10⁻⁹ m containing 0.0241624…, and `attained: true`.
- [ ] The probe is returned as p* = (39 + √1745)/128, the positive root of 512·p² − 312·p − 7 = 0 (equivalently p* = (2U + 1 + δ*)/4), with an enclosure containing 0.631041.
- [ ] `mission.synthesize` at δ = 1/40 returns an inner answer with the probe p = 5/8; at δ = 1/50 it returns an exclusion (certificates in N25e-32).
- [ ] The parametric form reproduces the fixture and one more instance by an independent computation: U = 1, ε = 1/32 gives δ² + (25/8)·δ − 1/8 = 0, δ* = (√657 − 25)/16 ≈ 0.03950.
- [ ] No answer is derived from sampled gains; the endpoint licence (monotonicity of x₃ in g for c > 0) is in the proof objects.
- [ ] The lowering has a second, independently written implementation in `frob-conformance` that agrees exactly on the polynomial and on the enclosure's ends, and one deliberately invalid fixture (PO-AUGUR-01).
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json`; `CONTRACT.md` documents the algebraic optimum field.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-mission/tests/docking_interval.rs`: `polynomial_from_parameters` (U = 3/4, ε = 1/64: coefficients
(16, 41, −1) after clearing denominators); `root_enclosure_width` (ends bracket the root: polynomial negative at
the lower end, positive at the upper, width ≤ 10⁻⁹); `p_star_from_delta_star` ((2U + 1 + δ*)/4, enclosure contains
0.6310406); `conditions_at_1_40` (p = 5/8: 1/41 ≤ 1/40; 2p − U = 1/2 ≤ 41/80; p + U = 11/8 ≥ 39/40);
`conditions_at_1_50` (ambiguity needs p ≥ 49/64, reach needs p ≤ 63/100: empty); `third_condition_slack`
(p* + U ≥ 1 − δ*); `nonpositive_probe_fails` (c ≤ 3/4 < 1 − δ for δ < 1/4); `x3_equals_g_times_c` (symbolic identity
from the raw tick equations); `attained_true_closed_sets`; `parametric_second_instance` (U = 1, ε = 1/32);
`side_condition_violation_falls_back` (U = 1/2 with a large ε, where U ≥ (1 + δ)/2 fails).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_docking_tolerance.sh` (N25e-33) drives `frob mission synthesize` and `frob mission refute` on
the fixture and logs the polynomial, the enclosure, `attained` and the certificates with the STD-E2E helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

δ* = (√1745 − 41)/32 ≈ 24.1624 mm; p* = (39 + √1745)/128 ≈ 0.631041; 20 mm infeasible; 25 mm met with p = 5/8.
Related registry rows of the family: KA-635 to KA-638 (two gains on time, late packet, the family ε < (U + ½)/4, the
third gain 33/32 with best worst-case error 1/65 m). Negative controls: a solver that samples gains {1, 3/2, 2} and
reports a smaller tolerance (the exhaustive check of N25e-32 must refute it); a solver that reports the optimum as
`attained: false`; a solver that returns a rounded decimal with no polynomial. No Appendix M number exists yet for
this subsection's rows. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; both hosts; identical bytes.

## Considerations

- The closed form is specific to this plant; the general method for continua of hidden parameters is endpoint
  reasoning under a recorded monotonicity licence, and where no licence exists the cell stays unresolved.
- FACT (a recorded plan-time pilot; to be re-run before a gate cites it): a solver on the raw three-tick dynamics
  excluded 20 mm, and independent interval checks confirmed inclusion at the threshold.
- The enclosure's ends need certificates at rationals with large denominators; their size is bounded by the
  requested width, and the checker's size limits must admit a 10⁻⁹ enclosure.
