## Background

A guarantee about information must carry the law that makes it true. A sensor budget states the joint law of its
read errors, not their pairwise tables. The rule exists because the value of repeated reads is set by error
dependence, not by per-read accuracy, and because dependence has more shapes than "independent" and "one shared
fault". Tier Solid [S]; owner `frob-sense` for the error law, `frob-analyze` for the observability and aliasing
analysis A-17; verbs `sense.alias` and, through it, `mission.synthesize`.

The smallest case in which omitting the joint law gives a wrong answer. A hidden binary class is read seven times,
and each read is right with probability 3/4.

| Declared law of the errors | Best success, equal priors |
|---|---|
| Jointly independent | 3807/4096, about 0.9294 |
| One fault bit shared by all reads | 3/4 |
| The pairwise-independent law below | 55/64, about 0.8594 |

The pairwise-independent law: in each class draw the number K of wrong reads as 1, 2, 3 or 4 with probabilities
(43, 3, 9, 9)/64, and place them uniformly among the seven slots. Each read is wrong with probability
E[K]/7 = (112/64)/7 = 1/4. Each pair is wrong together with probability E[K·(K − 1)]/42 = (168/64)/42 = 1/16, so every
pair has the table of two independent reads. A history with one or two errors relative to one class has six or
five relative to the other, which has probability zero there, and is decoded with certainty. A history with three
errors relative to one class has four relative to the other, and its likelihood is (9/64)/35 under both. The best
decoder succeeds with probability 46/64 + ½·(18/64) = 55/64. A requirement of 0.9 is met by the independent law and
missed by this one, with the same accuracy and the same pair tables.

Rejected alternatives: inferring joint independence from measured pairwise tables (false, by the case above);
a correlation coefficient as the dependence summary (pairwise by construction, so it cannot separate rows one and
three).

## Scope

In: the card field, the credit rule in A-17 and `sense.alias`, the exact decoder by enumeration, the refusal.
Out: the label channel (N25e-35); the alias-witness search itself (the Sensorium beads); the gate script (N25e-36).

## Technical approach

1. Card field (DESIGN). A sensor card states `error_dependence`, one of:
   `joint-independent`; `shared-fault` (one fault bit shared by all reads of the declared group);
   `declared-law` (a finite joint law by reference: a content-addressed table of error patterns with exact
   rational probabilities per hidden class); `pairwise` (only pairwise tables are known); `unknown`. The field
   has per-field provenance like every card field, and no default: a card used in a redundancy analysis with
   the field absent is treated as `unknown`.
2. Declared-law table (schema `frob.sense.errorlaw/1`): number of reads n, hidden classes, and for each class a
   list of (error pattern as a bit string, probability "p/q") summing to one, or a symmetric form (the law of
   the number of errors K with uniform placement). Caps (DESIGN): n ≤ 16.
3. Exact decoder by enumeration (`crates/frob-analyze/src/a17/redundancy.rs`). For n reads of a hidden class
   with priors π_c and law P(h | c) over the 2ⁿ histories h, the best decoder picks argmax_c π_c·P(h | c) and
   succeeds with probability Σ_h max_c π_c·P(h | c), computed in exact rationals (128 histories for n = 7). For
   `joint-independent` with accuracy q the law is the product law; for `shared-fault` the success is the
   single-read accuracy.
4. Credit rule. A-17 and `sense.alias` credit redundancy, meaning a success probability above the single-read
   accuracy, only under `joint-independent`, under a `declared-law` whose best decoder is found by enumeration,
   or under a proved lower bound on the histories that decide (a certificate listing a set of histories, each
   with a class it identifies with probability one under the law, whose total prior-weighted probability is
   the bound). Under `pairwise` or `unknown` the guaranteed value is the single-read accuracy, and a request for
   more is refused: "redundancy credited without a joint error law". The refusal has no registry number yet;
   until N25e-45 allocates one it is emitted as `FROB-REF-846` (marginal rates combined without a dependence
   assumption) with `detail: joint-error-law-missing`; pointer at the card field; ranked repairs: declare the
   joint law with its evidence; add a distinct modality; accept the single-read value. Exit 3.
5. Surfaces. `frob sense alias … --reads n --json` returns `redundancy { law, single_read, guaranteed,
   decoder: enumeration | closed-form | lower-bound, histories, law_marginals { per_read_error[], pair_error[] } }`
   (the marginals are computed from the declared table, so a reader can see what the pair tables alone would
   have shown); A-17's finding states which law it used.
   `mission.synthesize` and `mission.refute` on a sensing mission read the guaranteed value and name the law in
   the answer's assumptions.
6. Evidence: the enumeration result is `Certified` for the stated law (exact arithmetic over a finite table);
   whether the law describes the sensor is a fidelity and reality question and stays with the card's
   provenance.

Deadlines still apply: the guaranteed value is for the fixed number of reads that fit before the commitment
instant; sequential stopping rules are priced separately, as in the base alias fixture.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Enumeration of the 128 histories returns exactly 55/64 for the pairwise law, 3807/4096 for the independent law and 3/4 for the shared fault.
- [ ] A redundancy claim on a card marked `pairwise` (or `unknown`, or with the field absent) is refused with the repairs above, and the guaranteed value reported is 3/4.
- [ ] A requirement of 0.9 on seven reads is met under `joint-independent` and missed under the declared pairwise law and under `shared-fault`, through `mission.refute`.
- [ ] The declared pairwise law's marginals are verified by the tool: each read wrong with probability 1/4, each pair wrong together with probability 1/16.
- [ ] A lower-bound certificate for the pairwise law listing the one- and two-error histories credits 46/64 and no more.
- [ ] A-17 and `sense.alias` never output a success above the single-read accuracy without naming a law that licenses it (property test over random cards).
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json`; card schema, `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-sense` and of `frob-analyze` updated.

## Considerations

- The declared law is a claim about hardware. A card that declares `joint-independent` without evidence carries
  provenance `assumed`, and under strict-data requests that is itself refused elsewhere.
- Enumeration is exponential in n; the cap of 16 reads keeps it at 65,536 histories; beyond it only closed forms
  and lower-bound certificates are accepted.
- The pairwise law is a constructed adversary, not a model of any sensor; its role is to make the omission of
  the joint law fail in the smallest way.
