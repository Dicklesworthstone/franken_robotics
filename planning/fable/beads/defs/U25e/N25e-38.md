## Background

Probe and instrument design in Augur compares what different physical mechanisms predict and asks for the cheapest
experiment whose readings separate the mechanisms that imply different design decisions. Its bounds are computed
over nuisance parameters known only as intervals. This bead adds a rule to that machinery: nuisance parameters
that two quantities share in the world stay shared in every bound that compares them. Tier Frontier [F] (it sits
in the probe-design mechanism); owner `frob-mission`; verbs `mission.distinguish` and `experiment.suggest`.

The fixture shows why. A gripper holds its closure displacement. A compact pad A has contact radius a₀ and initial
normal load 120 N; a broad pad B has radius 2a₀ and initial load 100 N; the Coulomb coefficient is 0.5; the
supported tangential load is evaluated at the handover, T = 1 s. The normalized load relaxes as
R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t/(τ·f(r))) with radius ratio r = a/a₀, residual fraction q ∈ [0.18, 0.22] and time constant
τ ∈ [0.9, 1.1] s. Four synthetic laws differ only in f: V (f = 1), P (f = r²), C (f = min(r, 1)²), and D (for r ≥ 1 the
exponent is −[k·min(s, 1/4) + (1 + (1 − k)/3)·max(s − 1/4, 0)] with s = t/τ and k = 1/r²; for r < 1, as P). In the
fixture both pads of one trial have the same q and the same τ. The margin of the compact pad over the broad pad at
the handover is m(q, τ) = 60·R(1, T; q, τ) − 50·R(2, T; q, τ) newtons. Minimizing the two terms separately lets the
compact pad take (q, τ) = (0.18, 0.9 s) while the broad pad takes (0.22, 1.1 s). No such trial exists.

| Law | Least m with q and τ shared | Least m with independent extremes |
|---|---|---|
| V and C | 4.50 N, at (0.18, 0.9 s) | 0.28 N |
| D | 4.72 N, at (0.18, 1.1 s) | −0.078 N |
| P | −13.06 N; the broad pad wins everywhere, by at least 10.02 N | −15.08 N |

Under D the independent bound changes sign: it reports that the broad pad might win where the compact pad wins by
more than 4.7 N over the whole box. Rejected alternative: always evaluating independently "to be safe" (it is safe
only for inclusion; for refutation it manufactures counterexamples that are not worlds).

## Scope

In: the `scope` declaration, shared-symbol evaluation, the `scope: undeclared` marking, the witness rule and its
refusal, the margin table as a fixture. Out: the four-law probe (N25e-39); the base probe algorithm (the probe
design bead); the gate script (N25e-41).

## Technical approach

1. Declaration (DESIGN). Every nuisance parameter of a mission or a measurement contract has a `scope`: the set
   of quantities that read one realization (a trial, a pad pair, a batch of material, a unit). In the schema: a
   parameter entry gains `scope: { instance: "trial" | "pad-pair" | "batch" | "unit" | <named>, read_by: [quantity
   ids] }`. The scope is part of the canonical query.
2. Evaluation (module `crates/frob-mission/src/eval/scoped.rs`). Interval and affine evaluation treats a scoped
   parameter as one variable across every expression in its scope, by a shared noise symbol (affine forms:
   the same εᵢ appears in both terms, so their difference cancels what is common) or by subdividing the shared
   box (interval evaluation: bisect the box of the scoped parameters, evaluate the whole comparison on each
   sub-box, take the hull of the results; refine until the bound's width is below the requested tolerance or
   the box budget ends), never as an independent interval per expression.
3. Undeclared scope. A parameter with no declared scope is evaluated independently and the result is marked
   `scope: undeclared`. That direction is safe for inclusion, since it only widens the unresolved set. It is not
   safe for refutation: an extreme point of an independent box is not a world, and it cannot serve as a
   counterexample witness.
4. Witness rule. A counterexample or a refutation built from independent extremes of a parameter that is shared
   (declared, or undeclared and therefore not known to be unshared) is refused: "witness assembled from unshared
   values of a shared parameter". The refusal has no registry number yet; until N25e-45 allocates one it is
   emitted as `FROB-REF-850` (a field in a certificate does not match its definitions) with `detail:
   unshared-witness`; pointer at the parameter; ranked repairs: evaluate with the shared scope; declare the
   scope; or return the two witness mechanisms with a margin interval instead of a counterexample. Exit 3.
5. Certificates. Every witness in a bundle lists, per scoped parameter and scope instance, the single realized
   value used by all quantities in the scope; the checker rejects a witness in which two quantities of one
   scope instance read different values. Enclosure certificates carry the scope table so that the checker's
   own evaluation shares the symbol as well.
6. Fixture `fixture:pad-relaxation-shared` (class `synthetic`): the four laws, the box, the loads, and the
   margin table above, derived on a 201 × 201 grid of the box (the least shared margins are attained at box
   corners: V and C at (0.18, 0.9 s), D at (0.18, 1.1 s), P at (0.18, 0.9 s)).

Result fields: every bound reports `scope: shared | undeclared` per parameter; refutations report the realized
values per scope instance. Evidence class of enclosures: `Certified` for outward-rounded evaluation; the grid
values are `Tested` audits.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The margin table reproduces to 10⁻³ N: shared 4.50 (V, C), 4.72 (D), −13.06 (P, with the largest margin −10.02); independent 0.28, −0.078, −15.08.
- [ ] A refutation built from the independent extremes under law D (compact pad at (0.18, 0.9 s), broad pad at (0.22, 1.1 s)) is refused with the repairs above.
- [ ] With `scope` declared, `mission.distinguish` reports that under D the compact pad wins over the whole box by at least 4.7 N; with the scope removed, the same query reports an inconclusive interval marked `scope: undeclared` and issues no counterexample.
- [ ] A certificate or counterexample whose witness assigns different values of a shared parameter to two quantities of one scope instance is rejected by `frob-verify` (this is the added trigger of KILL-AUGUR-PROBE: if such a bundle passes the checker, the kill gate fires).
- [ ] Shared-symbol enclosures are sound: on 10⁴ random points of the box the true margin lies inside the reported enclosure for each law.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json`; schema and `CONTRACT.md` document `scope`.

## Considerations

- KILL-AUGUR-PROBE gains one trigger from this rule: a certificate or a counterexample that depends on unshared
  values of a parameter declared shared passes the checker. Its fallback is unchanged (`experiment.suggest`
  ships the expert grid and the direct measurement; the experiment grammar is parked). The decision bead for
  that kill gate belongs to the probe-design unit; this bead and N25e-41 supply the new trigger's measurement.
- Sharing must match physics: sharing a parameter that is in fact drawn per pad is as wrong as splitting one
  that is shared. The scope is a declaration with provenance, and an undeclared scope never licenses a
  refutation.
- Subdivision cost grows with the number of shared parameters; two here, a handful in practice.
