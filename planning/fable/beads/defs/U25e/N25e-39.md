## Background

`mission.distinguish` returns the smallest admissible intervention or instrument whose readings separate
alternatives that imply different mission decisions. Its standing fixture is a gripper with two pad choices and a
family of synthetic relaxation laws. The normalized load relaxes as R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t/(τ·f(r))) with radius
ratio r, residual fraction q ∈ [0.18, 0.22] and time constant τ ∈ [0.9, 1.1] s. Laws: V (f = 1), P (f = r²),
C (f = min(r, 1)²) and D (for r ≥ 1 the exponent is −[k·min(s, 1/4) + (1 + (1 − k)/3)·max(s − 1/4, 0)] with s = t/τ and
k = 1/r²; for r < 1, as P). The design decision is which pad carries more at the handover: the compact pad under
V, C and D, the broad pad under P. Probe candidates are r ∈ {1/4, 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 3} and
t ∈ {1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2} s, with normalized read error bounded by ±0.02 and cost 0.25 s of setup plus the
exposure t. Each law is scored on 225 deterministic cases (25 pairs of q and τ, three probe errors, three errors on
the archived reading).

The base result is a warning: a probe generated for the three laws {V, P, C} (r = 2 at 125 ms) is right in 675 of
675 admitted cases and wrong in 225 of 225 cases of the withheld law D, whose early trace is identical to P's. This
bead adds the complementary known answer: once the delayed law D is admitted to the mechanism set, a probe exists
that separates all four laws, at a longer dwell than the three-law probe. Tier Frontier [F]; owner `frob-mission`;
verbs `mission.distinguish` and `experiment.suggest`.

What this does not change, and must be printed with the answer: the 225 reversals of the three-law probe on the
withheld law stand, and so does the normative lesson (a probe verdict is a conditional witness whose condition is
the admitted mechanism set). This is a result on a known mechanism set. A fifth law outside it can reverse it
again, and the direct measurement at mission conditions stays in the candidate set. Ceiling, stated before any
speed test: 5/3 in modeled exposure time on this fixture, and 1 against a programmed expert who is given the same
four laws. Rejected alternative: breaking cost ties by the smallest radius or the first candidate found (it
returns a gap eight times smaller at the same cost).

## Scope

In: the four-law mechanism set in the fixture; the separation test with both read errors; the answer "no probe" at
short dwell; the equal-cost tie-break by largest gap; the conditional-witness fields. Out: the base algorithm
(`mission.distinguish`, BD-291) and the experiment grammar (N25c-17); `experiment.suggest` itself (BD-351);
shared nuisance scope and its scoped evaluation (N25e-38, a dependency: the separation test of step 2 evaluates
through it); the gate script (N25e-41).

## Technical approach

1. Decision partition. With D admitted the decisions are compact for V, C and D and broad for P. The probe must
   separate P from each of V, C, D; it need not separate V, C and D from one another (same decision).
2. Separation test for a candidate (r, t). Over the admitted nuisance box (q, τ shared within a probe reading,
   by the scope rule), compute the least reading under P and the greatest reading under V, C, D (or the reverse
   order if P reads lower). The gap after both read errors is the difference minus 0.04 (the probe error and
   the error on the reading it is compared with, ±0.02 each). The candidate separates when the gap is positive.
   Bounds are outward-rounded enclosures; the grid values below are their audit.
3. Short dwell. On the candidate grid no probe at a dwell of 1/4 s or less separates P from D, because D follows
   P until t/τ = 1/4 (and for r ≤ 1, D coincides with P or all laws coincide). The verb then returns no probe
   for that dwell bound, with the two witness mechanisms (P and D) and their opposing margins, as the base
   algorithm requires where observation sets overlap.
4. At 1/2 s three radii separate them after both read errors of ±0.02 are included:

   | Probe | Least reading under P | Greatest reading under V, C, D | Gap after both errors | Cost |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | r = 3/2, t = 1/2 s | 0.8206 | 0.7677 | 0.0129 | 0.75 s |
   | r = 2, t = 1/2 s | 0.8937 | 0.7874 | 0.0662 | 0.75 s |
   | r = 3, t = 1/2 s | 0.9509 | 0.8019 | 0.1090 | 0.75 s |

   Each of the three decides all 900 enumerated cases correctly (four laws, 225 cases each).
5. Choice rule (DESIGN). `mission.distinguish` chooses the least costly separating intervention; among probes of
   equal cost it chooses the largest gap, here r = 3. A tie-break by smallest radius returns a gap eight times
   smaller at the same cost (0.0129 against 0.1090).
6. Comparison with the direct measurement. Against the direct measurement at mission dwell, 1.25 s with the
   archived compact reading, the modeled time ratio is 1.25/0.75 = 5/3. The direct measurement is always offered
   in the proposal list and stays the stronger comparator.
7. Answer fields: the chosen probe (r, t), its threshold reading (the midpoint of the gap), the gap, the cost,
   the decision each reading implies, the admitted mechanism set as the condition of the witness (in the
   certificate and in the answer's assumptions), the alternatives at equal cost with their gaps, the direct
   measurement with its cost, and the measurement contract (what is read, when, with what error bound, which
   reading implies which decision).
8. `experiment.suggest` ranks the same proposals by decision relevance and cost and never licenses a verdict.

Evidence: the separation is `Certified` for the admitted laws and box (enclosures checked by `interval-box`
pieces); the 900-case tally is a `Tested` audit; the answer is conditional on the mechanism set.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With four laws admitted, `mission.distinguish` returns a probe at t = 1/2 s with r = 3 and a gap of at least 0.10 (0.1090 to four digits).
- [ ] The returned probe decides 900 of 900 enumerated cases (four laws, 225 cases each).
- [ ] With the dwell bounded at 1/4 s or less the verb returns no probe, with P and D as witness mechanisms and their opposing margins.
- [ ] The alternatives at equal cost are listed with gaps 0.0129 (r = 3/2) and 0.0662 (r = 2); a mutant tie-break by smallest radius returns r = 3/2 and fails the gap threshold.
- [ ] The answer names the admitted mechanism set {V, P, C, D} as its condition, offers the direct measurement (1.25 s) in the proposal list, and reports the modeled time ratio 5/3 and the ratio 1 against the programmed expert.
- [ ] With only {V, P, C} admitted the verb still returns a three-law probe at 125 ms. Two candidates separate at that cost: r = 2 (gap 0.0158, the probe the plan-time pilot recorded) and r = 3 (gap 0.0312). Under this bead's largest-gap tie-break the verb returns r = 3 and lists r = 2 as the equal-cost alternative (BD-291 asserts both tie-breaks). On law D either probe is wrong in 225 of 225 cases: the earlier result stands.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json`; `CONTRACT.md` documents the tie-break.

## Considerations

- The existing kill gate KILL-AUGUR-PROBE (the generator never beats the programmed expert plus direct
  measurement at matched correctness on the coupled-layout experiment, or issues one false lower bound) is
  unaffected by this known answer; against the expert the ratio here is 1, and no ten-fold effect is supported.
- A stopping rule derived from a probe is not used on an uncharacterized material; an early reading taken
  during the same stroke may be continued to the mission dwell, which preserves the audit trail and forfeits
  the exposure saving.
- The laws are artificial and the loads stipulated; nothing here is a claim about a material.
