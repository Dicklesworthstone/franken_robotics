## Background

A mission query may ask for a Pareto set: the builds that no other feasible build beats on every declared
objective (body cost, facility-change cost, energy per item). Scalarizing several objectives is the caller's
decision; Augur never combines them into one number on its own. The common way to produce a "Pareto set" is to
sweep weights of a weighted sum and collect the minimizers. On a discrete catalog that method misses builds, and a
set that silently omits a nondominated build is a false completeness claim.

The smallest case. Three feasible builds have (body cost, facility cost) equal to (0, 6), (4, 4) and (6, 0). None
dominates another. For weights (w₁, w₂) ≥ 0, not both zero, the build (4, 4) is best only if 4w₁ ≤ 2w₂ and
4w₂ ≤ 2w₁, which together force both weights to zero: the two extremes average to (3, 3), which is not a build and
lies below (4, 4). So no weighted sum ever returns (4, 4). Yet a facility budget of four makes (4, 4) the unique
build of least body cost: it is the answer to a perfectly ordinary engineering question.

The rule (tier Solid [S]; owner `frob-synth`, with `frob-algebra` for the order checks; verb
`mission.synthesize`): a complete Pareto claim over discrete builds is accepted on a disposition for every omitted
option, never on a sweep of weights. Rejected alternatives: a denser weight sweep (no density finds a point inside
the convex hull's upper-left-to-lower-right chord); reporting the weighted-sum set as `pareto` with a caveat (the
claim word is what downstream consumers read).

## Scope

In: the disposition list, the downgrade to `pareto-candidates`, candidate generation by dominance filtering and by
constrained queries, the role left to weighted sums, the certificate. Out: the continuous sizing witness (N25e-42);
the co-design iteration itself; the gate script (N25e-44).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-synth/src/codesign/pareto.rs`; order primitives (product order on objective vectors,
dominance, antichains) from `frob-algebra`.

1. Definitions. Objectives are minimized. Build a dominates build b when a is at most b in every objective and
   strictly below in at least one. An option is a catalog entry (a build), feasible or not for the mission.
2. Claim rule. An answer with the claim `pareto` over a discrete catalog lists, for every option it omits, one
   disposition: `dominated-by` with the dominating option (which must itself be feasible and in the catalog),
   `infeasible` with its exclusion (a proof reference), or `unresolved`. If any option is unresolved the claim
   is downgraded to `pareto-candidates`, and the answer carries the remaining coverage obligation (the list of
   unresolved options and what would resolve each).
3. Candidate generation.
   - On a finite catalog whose options all have verdicts and objective values: dominance filtering. Sort by the
     first objective and sweep (two objectives: one pass keeping the running minimum of the second; more
     objectives: pairwise comparison against the current antichain). The result is the set of nondominated
     feasible options, each returned with its objective vector.
   - Otherwise (a catalog too large to enumerate with verdicts, or options generated on demand): constrained
     queries. For each objective in turn, minimize it subject to a bound on each other objective at its
     attained values (the ε-constraint method on the discrete attained values); every solve is a
     `mission.synthesize` query with `minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`, so each returned point comes with its
     lower bound.
   - Weighted sums may propose points and never close the set: a point found by a weighted sum is a candidate
     like any other, and the claim still needs a disposition for every omitted option.
4. Budget queries. "Least body cost subject to facility cost at most B" is the constrained query directly; on
   the fixture B = 4 returns (4, 4).
5. Result fields (`result.pareto`): `claim` (`pareto` or `pareto-candidates`), `set[]` (option, objective
   vector, feasibility proof), `dispositions[]` (option, kind, by or proof), `coverage_obligation[]`,
   `generation` (`dominance-filter`, `constrained-queries`, `weighted-sum-proposals`).
6. Certificate. The checker verifies, in exact rationals: every listed dominance (componentwise comparison);
   that every catalog option is either in the set or has exactly one disposition (coverage of the catalog,
   whose option list is in the bundle); that the set is an antichain; that every `infeasible` disposition
   references an accepted exclusion. Kind: `partition` over the catalog's option list with per-option
   sub-certificates (DESIGN: the catalog is a finite set, and the claim is that the dispositions cover it).
7. After an edit (a new catalog part, a changed price), the Pareto claim is an optimality obligation and is
   revalidated separately from feasibility witnesses: the set's witnesses survive, the completeness claim is
   withdrawn until dispositions are recomputed for the new options.

Evidence: `Certified` when every disposition is certified; the claim's class is the weakest among its
dispositions (an `Estimated` objective value from a surrogate makes the claim `Estimated`). Determinism D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the three-build catalog all three builds (0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0) are returned with claim `pareto` and an empty omitted list.
- [ ] A weights-only mutant omits (4, 4) and fails: its answer has an omitted option with no disposition and is rejected by the checker (and by the gate script).
- [ ] The budget query (least body cost subject to facility cost at most 4) returns (4, 4).
- [ ] On 20 generated catalogs of at most 200 options the returned set equals brute-force dominance filtering.
- [ ] With one option's feasibility unresolved the claim is `pareto-candidates` and the coverage obligation names that option.
- [ ] A `dominated-by` disposition whose dominating option is infeasible or absent from the catalog is rejected.
- [ ] After adding a catalog option, a stored `pareto` claim is withdrawn by revalidation while its members' feasibility witnesses survive.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob mission synthesize … --json` with objective claim `pareto`; `CONTRACT.md` documents the dispositions.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-synth/tests/pareto_coverage.rs`: `three_builds_all_nondominated`; `weighted_sum_never_returns_4_4`
(for w₁, w₂ on a grid of 10⁴ nonnegative pairs not both zero, the minimizer of w₁·c₁ + w₂·c₂ is (0, 6) or (6, 0);
ties at w₁ = w₂ between the two extremes at value 6·w, with (4, 4) at 8·w); `budget_four_returns_4_4`;
`dominance_filter_matches_bruteforce` (random catalogs up to 200 options, two to four objectives, integer costs
with ties); `ties_are_not_dominance` (two options with equal vectors are both kept and reported as equivalent);
`unresolved_downgrades_claim`; `disposition_must_cite_feasible_dominator`; `set_is_antichain`;
`epsilon_constraint_enumerates_all` (the constrained-query route recovers the same set as filtering on catalogs
of at most 50 options). `crates/frob-algebra/tests/dominance.rs`: `product_order_laws` (reflexive,
antisymmetric, transitive), `dominance_is_strict_partial_order`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_sizing_pareto.sh` (N25e-44) drives the verb on the three-build catalog and the generated
catalogs and logs sets, dispositions and claims with the STD-E2E helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Three builds (0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0): none dominated; (4, 4) is never a weighted-sum minimizer (the extremes average
to (3, 3)); a facility budget of four selects (4, 4). Negative controls: the weights-only mutant; a filter that
treats "at most in every objective" as dominance (it would drop one of two equal options); a claim that lists a
dominating option outside the catalog. No Appendix M number exists yet for these rows. Metamorphic relations:
permuting the catalog leaves the set unchanged; scaling one objective by a positive constant leaves the set
unchanged; adding a dominated option leaves the set unchanged and adds one disposition. Determinism lanes: twice
on one machine; debug and release; identical output order (canonical sort by objective vector, then option id).

## Considerations

- The rule is about the claim word. A caller who wants a quick shortlist can ask for `feasible-candidate`
  points; only `pareto` carries the coverage duty.
- Large catalogs make full dispositions expensive; `pareto-candidates` with an explicit obligation is the
  honest intermediate, and the obligation is work a later query can finish (answers are anytime).
- The flagship multi-objective query of the program (body cost, facility-change cost, energy per item,
  worst delivery time, route availability) is a consumer of this rule at M8; its catalog is larger and partly
  generated, so the constrained-query route matters there.
