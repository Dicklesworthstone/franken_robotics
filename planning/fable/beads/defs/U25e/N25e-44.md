## Background

Two acceptance rules were added to the co-design lane of `frob-synth`. A sizing answer on a continuous resource is
accepted on a witness, never on a small increment: the lane returns a bracket whose upper end U satisfies
F(U) ≤ U for the monotone loop map F, and an answer without such a witness is labelled `lower-bound-only` and
cannot be returned as a design. A complete Pareto claim over discrete builds is accepted on a disposition for
every omitted option (`dominated-by`, `infeasible`, or `unresolved`, the last downgrading the claim to
`pareto-candidates`), never on a sweep of weights.

GATE-AUGUR-35 (claim: Known answer; M4; thresholds TARGET): on the scalar pack the lane returns a bracket whose
upper end passes F(U) ≤ U and lies within 1 g of 100 kg; the increment-stopping mutant returns 99.5 kg and is
refused. On the three-build catalog all three builds are returned with dispositions; a weights-only mutant omits
(4, 4) and fails; the budget query returns (4, 4). On 20 generated catalogs of at most 200 options the returned set
equals brute-force dominance filtering.

The two fixtures: the pack that needs F(m) = 1 + 0.99·m kilograms of pack to carry itself and the payload (least
feasible mass 100 kg; Kleene iterate after 527 steps about 99.5 kg with a next increment of 5 g); and three
feasible builds with (body cost, facility cost) = (0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0). This bead is the script that records the
gate. Rejected alternative: accepting "within 1 g of 100 kg" for the lower iterate (the gate's point is the
upper witness; an iterate close to the fixed point from below is still infeasible).

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/augur_sizing_pareto.sh`; the two fixtures; the 20 generated catalogs with brute-force truth; the
two mutants; the gate record. Out: the mechanisms (N25e-42, N25e-43).

## Technical approach

Fixtures (`fixtures/augur/sizing-pareto/`): `pack.fmission` (one continuous resource, pack mass; loop map
F(m) = 1 + (99/100)·m with the payload term 1 kg; objective: least pack mass; contraction constant 99/100 declared
with its one-line proof); `three-builds.fmission` (a catalog of three feasible builds with objective vectors
(0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0), claim `pareto`); `three-builds-budget4.fmission` (minimize body cost subject to facility
cost ≤ 4); `gen/<i>.fmission`, i = 0..19 (catalogs of 20 to 200 options, two or three integer objectives with
ties, a random subset infeasible with a stored exclusion; seed pinned), with `truth.json` from an independent
brute-force filter in `frob-conformance`.

Steps (plain shell, idempotent):
1. `start`: commit, host, generator seed.
2. Pack: `frob mission synthesize …/pack.fmission --strategy codesign --tolerance 1e-3 --json` (tolerance in
   kilograms). Assert `result.sizing.label` is `bracket`; `upper` within 0.001 kg of 100 kg; the recorded
   `witness_check` shows F(U) ≤ U; the script's exact helper recomputes 1 + (99/100)·U and confirms the
   inequality; `lower` ≤ 100 ≤ `upper`.
3. `frob prove bundle`, then `frob-verify` under `env -i` on the witness certificate; assert `accept`.
4. Increment-stopping mutant (test-profile build flag `mutant-increment-stop`, threshold 10 g): assert the
   mutant's value is about 99.5 kg (between 99.49 and 99.51); submit it as a design through
   `frob mission synthesize … --candidate m=<value>`; assert exit 3 with the refusal "sizing returned without a
   feasibility witness" and that the helper confirms F(value) > value (99.505 against 99.5 at the nominal
   point).
5. Three builds: `frob mission synthesize …/three-builds.fmission --json`. Assert claim `pareto`, the set
   {(0, 6), (4, 4), (6, 0)}, and an empty omitted list (so no disposition is missing).
6. Weights-only mutant (build flag `mutant-pareto-weights`): assert its answer omits (4, 4); assert the product's
   own checker rejects the claim (an omitted option with no disposition); the wrapper records "mutant fails".
7. Budget query: `…/three-builds-budget4.fmission`; assert the returned build is (4, 4) with a matching lower
   bound.
8. Generated catalogs: for each, assert the returned set equals `truth.json` and that every omitted option has a
   disposition whose kind matches the truth (dominated with a valid dominator, or infeasible with its
   exclusion). Tally 20 of 20. `frob-verify` each claim's bundle.
9. Unresolved option: one generated catalog is re-run with one exclusion withheld; assert the claim is
   `pareto-candidates` and the coverage obligation names the option.
10. `summary` and the gate row for GATE-AUGUR-35.

Logging (STD-E2E): `target/e2e-logs/augur_sizing_pareto/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line:
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N25e-44`), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
block. `metric` lines: the bracket's ends in kilograms, the distance of the upper end from 100 kg against the
0.001 kg threshold, the witness check as exact strings, the mutant's value; for catalogs the set sizes, the
disposition counts by kind and the tally against 20. `refusal` lines with code, detail, pointer and repairs. On
failure: the first catalog and option whose disposition differs from the truth.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-AUGUR-35 (TARGET, M4) recorded: a bracket whose upper end passes F(U) ≤ U and lies within 1 g of 100 kg; the increment-stopping mutant returns 99.5 kg and is refused; all three builds returned with dispositions; the weights-only mutant omits (4, 4) and fails; the budget query returns (4, 4); 20 of 20 generated catalogs equal brute-force dominance filtering.
- [ ] The witness and the Pareto claims are accepted by `frob-verify` in an empty environment.
- [ ] A withheld exclusion downgrades the claim to `pareto-candidates` with its coverage obligation.
- [ ] The script exits 0 from a clean checkout on a host with no GPU and non-zero on any failed assertion.
- [ ] Results are identical across two runs and two hosts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Script self-tests in `scripts/e2e/tests/`: `sizing_selftest_helper.sh` (the helper evaluates F(100) = 100,
F(99.5) = 99.505 and F(99.999) = 99.99901 exactly); `sizing_selftest_lower_only.sh` (an answer whose `upper` is
absent fails step 2); `pareto_selftest_truth.sh` (a truth set with one option removed fails and names the option).
Brute-force tests in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pareto_bruteforce.rs`: `three_builds`;
`ties_kept`; `generated_catalog_reproducible`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script; steps and logging as above.

### Known answers and negative controls

Pack: x_k = 100·(1 − 0.99ᵏ); after 527 iterations about 99.5 kg, next step 5 g; least feasible mass 100 kg;
contraction bound 0.99 × 0.005/0.01 = 0.495 kg above 99.505 kg gives 100 kg, and F(100) = 100. Catalog: (0, 6),
(4, 4), (6, 0), none dominated; (4, 4) is best for no weights (the conditions 4w₁ ≤ 2w₂ and 4w₂ ≤ 2w₁ force both to
zero); a facility budget of four selects it. Negative controls: the two mutants; the withheld exclusion; a
witness check in floating point. No Appendix M number exists yet for these rows. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; debug and release; both hosts; class D0.

## Considerations

- The mutants are build flags of the test profile only; the script asserts that the release build does not
  contain them (a `frob capabilities` check) before recording the gate.
- The pack fixture's loop gain of 0.99 is deliberately close to one; the closure analysis refuses designs at or
  above one, and the fixture sits just inside.
