## Background

Every refusal of the system has a stable code, a message and ranked repairs. The machine-readable registry is
`registries/refusals.toml`; an unknown code is a load-time error and codes are never reused. Exit codes: 3 for a
refused input or question (codes 1xx to 5xx, and 8xx other than conflicts), 4 for budget refusals, 7 for
conflicts.

The mission chapter's refusal table defines twenty codes (N25-REF). Five rules added to the chapter afterwards
state a refusal in words and give it no code:

| # | Refusal as stated | Rule it enforces | Raised by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Chance claim transported without a coupling budget | Chance claims transport only through a coupling | `mission.transport`; `frob-verify` |
| 2 | Inclusion over a disturbance set without a declared law class | The controller class is part of a family answer | `mission.synthesize`, `mission.refute` at admission |
| 3 | Redundancy credited without a joint error law | A sensor budget states the joint law of its read errors | `sense.alias`; analysis A-17 |
| 4 | Witness assembled from unshared values of a shared parameter | Shared nuisance parameters stay shared | `mission.distinguish`, `mission.refute`; `frob-verify` |
| 5 | Sizing returned without a feasibility witness | A sizing answer is accepted on a witness, never on a small increment | `mission.synthesize --strategy codesign` |

Without codes these refusals cannot be loaded, tested ("every refusal code maps to exactly one exit code") or
documented, and five gates assert them (GATE-AUGUR-25, -31, -33, -34, -35). Two of the gates are due at M1 and
M3, so the decision is urgent: the beads that emit these refusals (N25e-02, N25e-28, N25e-34, N25e-38, N25e-42,
N25c-43 for the sixth below and BD-362 for the seventh) take the rows' content and the interim mapping from this
bead. This is a
decision for the owner of the refusal registry, not for the implementers of the rules. Rejected alternatives:
inventing numbers in each mechanism bead (five drafters would collide); folding each refusal permanently into an
existing code (the registry's rule is one meaning per code, and agents repair by code).

Where the numbers come from (FACT, from the suite's refusal beads): numbers are never typed by a chapter. The
allocation ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (rule of N35-33) holds one request per uncoded refusal;
N35x-02 filed these seven under the slugs `coupling-budget-missing`, `law-class-missing`,
`joint-error-law-missing`, `unshared-witness`, `sizing-witness-missing`, `synthesis-out-of-scope` (family `8xx`)
and `connection-outside-stratum` (family `1xx`, a model-admission refusal). The `8xx` family has four free
numbers (836 to 839) against about twenty requests, so the six `8xx` rows are `held` under the hold of N35x-01
until the project owner rules on the overflow (recommended there: reclassify, then a four-digit continuation
`FROB-REF-8100` onward); the `1xx` row is assigned by the rule. This bead decides everything about the rows
except the number.

A sixth refusal without a code comes from an earlier subsection of the chapter, the geometric-phase query: a
local connection requested outside the stratum on which it was qualified. A connection is the map from a
change of body shape to the body's displacement under no-slip contact; it is derived and admitted for one
contact mode and one rank of the constraint matrix, and it means nothing at a shape where the rank drops,
across a slip or lift-off boundary, or for a motion component the constraints remove (the yaw of a skid-steer
base). The chapter's refusal table has no row for it. It is decided here with the other five so that one
decision covers every uncoded refusal of the chapter. Its number is not held: N35x-02's run at M0 assigns it
(above), and every bead that raises it lands after that run and raises the assigned code
from the start (N35x-02's interim column lapses at the run for assigned rows). It is due at M3, not M6: BD-243
step 1 already refuses, at M3, a stratum whose constraint rows do not give rank W_g = dim G or over-determine the
group motion, and VERBS-analyze lists that refusal under this row on `analyze.curvature`; N25c-43 adds the other
kinds at M6.

A seventh comes from the synthesis lanes (§25.21): a synthesis query outside the admitted scope of the lane
that would answer it. The trajectory-refinement lane (`mission.synthesize --strategy refine`, BD-362, M4)
admits one bound base design, a declared motion and contact schedule, and at most 17 free continuous
parameters; a free contact schedule, a topology choice or an eighteenth parameter is refused with the nearest
admissible query. The chapter gives the case no code. BD-362 already emits it under the interim `FROB-REF-857`
(a synthesis system outside a lane's caps) with `detail: synthesis-out-of-scope`, and N35x-02 filed the slug as
shared with the other lanes' scope refusals, so the row is decided here with the others and is due at M4.

## Scope

In: the decision record; seven new registry rows with message, pointer, `retry`, ranked repairs and exit code
(five for the late rules, due from M1; one for the geometric-phase query, written at M1 with its assigned
number, first raised at M3 and complete at M6; one for a synthesis query outside a lane's scope, due at M4); the
interim mapping that holds for the held rows until they are assigned; the switch-over; the expected content of
each row as a fixture that the raising beads' scripts assert against. Out: the mechanisms that raise the
refusals and the end-to-end triggers of each (rows 1 to 5: N25e-02, N25e-28, N25e-34, N25e-38, N25e-42, whose
triggers are asserted by N25e-05, N25e-30, N25e-36, N25e-41 and N25e-44; row 6: BD-243 at M3, N25c-43 and
N25c-44 at M6; row 7: BD-362 and any later lane that reuses the row); the twenty coded rows (N25-REF).

## Technical approach

1. Decision requested (owner: the Tether lead with the Augur lead). Confirm the families (six in `8xx`,
   evidence and protocol: rows 1 to 5 and 7; the sixth in `1xx`) and the content of the seven rows below, and
   record the numbers as the allocation ledger assigns them: the `1xx` row when the rule runs, the six `8xx`
   rows when the hold of N35x-01 is lifted by the owner's ruling on the family's overflow (the `1xx` row is
   assigned at N35x-02's run at M0, before this bead lands, so it is written here with its number). The numbers
   840 to 859 of the family are
   used, the six out-of-block mission codes (868, 874, 877, 881, 884, 887) show that the family is nearly full,
   and retired numbers (for example 882) are not reusable; nobody chooses a number by hand.
2. Row content, fixed here so that only the number is open:

| # | Message | Pointer | Retry | Ranked repairs | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A chance or expectation claim was offered for transport with no coupling budget, or marked `universal` | The claim identifier | `after-change` | Supply or compute the budget; re-declare the claim kind; measure the claim on the target with `eval.qualify` | 3 |
| 2 | Inclusion over a load or disturbance set was requested and `policy_class.law` is not declared | `policy_class.law` | `after-change` | Declare `adjustable` if the load is observed before the command and any function of it is realizable; `affine` for a linear or passive allocator; `constant` if the load is not observed | 3 |
| 3 | A success probability above the single-read accuracy was requested from a card whose `error_dependence` is `pairwise` or `unknown` | The card field | `after-change` | Declare the joint error law with its evidence; add a distinct modality; accept the single-read value | 3 |
| 4 | A counterexample or refutation uses different values of a shared parameter for quantities of one scope instance | The parameter and scope instance | `after-change` | Evaluate with the shared scope; declare the scope; return two witness mechanisms with a margin interval | 3 |
| 5 | A sizing on a continuous resource has no upper witness U with F(U) ≤ U and was requested as a design | The resource | `after-change` | Supply or search for a witness; prove a contraction constant; choose from a finite catalog; accept `lower-bound-only` as a bound | 3 |

3. Fail-closed interim (in force until the rows are allocated; DESIGN). Each refusal is emitted under the nearest
   existing code, with a `detail` discriminator in the refusal block so tests and agents can tell them apart:
   1 → `FROB-REF-852` (a transport lacks a side condition), `detail: coupling-budget-missing`;
   2 → `FROB-REF-868` (the `.fmission` file is not valid: missing field), `detail: law-class-missing`;
   3 → `FROB-REF-846` (marginal rates combined without a dependence assumption), `detail:
   joint-error-law-missing`; 4 → `FROB-REF-850` (a field in a certificate does not match its definitions),
   `detail: unshared-witness`; 5 → `FROB-REF-853` (a minimum claimed without a matching bound), `detail:
   sizing-witness-missing`; 7 → `FROB-REF-857` (a synthesis system outside a lane's caps), `detail:
   synthesis-out-of-scope` (the interim BD-362 already emits). The interim never weakens a refusal: the request
   is refused either way.
4. Switch-over. When the ledger assigns the numbers: add the rows; change the emit sites of rows 1 to 5 and 7 (one
   constant each, defined in one module `frob_core::refusal::augur_late`); keep the `detail` strings as aliases for one release; update
   the refusal lists of the affected verbs, which the registry beads own (VERBS-mission for `mission.transport`,
   `mission.synthesize`, `mission.refute` and `mission.distinguish`; VERBS-sense for `sense.alias`; VERBS-analyze
   for `analyze.curvature`) and which carry the interim codes with their `detail` until then, are changed in the
   same change that assigns the number, by request to those beads (convention 12); regenerate the agent
   documentation.
5. Two related reconciliations recorded with the same decision, because they touch the same table:
   - the mission chapter says its codes use the block 840 to 859, while 840 to 845 belong to the qualification
     lane and six mission codes lie outside the block; the registry (Appendix O) governs, and the sentence is
     corrected in the generated documentation;
   - the registry has two codes for a quantifier-prefix mismatch: `FROB-REF-826` (the auditor: the prefix,
     domain or assumption set of a certificate does not match the query) and `FROB-REF-881` (the mission layer:
     evidence or a certificate establishes a different prefix, including clairvoyant evidence offered for a
     robust claim). DESIGN default: the auditor's rule reports 826 and the mission verbs map it to 881 when
     the mismatch is of the prefix, citing 826 as the cause; the known-answer and counterexample registries
     that cite 826 for the pusher prefix case stay valid.
6. The sixth row (the geometric-phase query; raised by the connection's admission in `frob-analyze` through
   `analyze.curvature`, and by the inverse holonomy query through `mission.synthesize` and `mission.refute`).
   Content: message "a local connection was used outside its qualified stratum or scope", with the kind of
   violation as `detail`: `rank-drop` (rank W_g < dim G at the shape) and `over-determined` (the constraint rows
   over-determine the group motion for generic shapes; the redundant rows named), both raised from M3 by BD-243
   step 1; `stratum-crossing` (a loop across a slip or lift-off boundary), `component-removed` (a motion
   component the constraints remove, such as the yaw of a skid-steer base) and `rank-only-displacement` (a
   finite displacement requested on the strength of accessibility rank alone, CX-027), raised from M6 by
   N25c-43. DESIGN: the last kind is in this row and not a row of its own because VERBS-analyze lists it among
   this refusal's kinds and a connection asked for more than it qualifies is the same repair class; the owner
   may split it in the ADR, which then needs a new ledger request. Pointer: the shape or loop segment and the
   stratum predicate that fails there (the redundant rows for `over-determined`); `retry`: `after-change`;
   ranked repairs, the first chosen by kind: restrict the loop to the admitted stratum (or choose shape
   variables and wheels whose rows give full rank); split the loop at the mode boundary and supply reset maps
   with a fresh rank check on each side; supply a separately qualified slip model for the removed component;
   request the integrated holonomy of a finite loop as the witness instead of the rank; exit 3. Family `1xx`
   (what is refused is a model's admission, N35x-02); the number is the one N35x-02's run assigns at M0, carried
   by the constant `frob_core::refusal::augur_late::CONNECTION_OUTSIDE_STRATUM`. No interim applies: no raising
   bead lands before the run, so none ever emits a stand-in code (BD-243, N25c-43, N25c-44 and the
   `analyze.curvature` row are asked to use the constant). Its emit sites and end-to-end triggers belong to the
   beads that raise it.
7. The seventh row (a synthesis query outside a lane's admitted scope; raised first by the refinement lane's
   admission, BD-362, through `mission.synthesize --strategy refine`; reusable by any lane's scope refusal).
   Content, fixed here so that only the number is open: message "a synthesis query lies outside the admitted
   scope of the lane asked to answer it", naming the lane; pointer: the offending field (the free contact
   schedule, the topology choice, or the parameter list); `observed` and `required` (for example 18 free
   parameters against at most 17); `retry`: `after-change`; ranked repairs: bind the contact schedule; reduce the
   free parameters θ to the lane's limit; use the search of the optimization chapter for a topology choice;
   exit 3. Family `8xx`, number from the ledger; held with the other `8xx` rows. The linkage and
   symbolic-cap cases keep `FROB-REF-857` itself: this row is for a query the lane does not admit, not for a
   system it admits and cannot finish within its caps. Its emit site and end-to-end trigger are BD-362's (M4).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR records the seven rows' content, their families (six `8xx`, one `1xx`), the ledger status of each (`assigned` with its number, or `held` with its interim), the date and the reason.
- [ ] Once assigned, `registries/refusals.toml` contains the rows with the content of the table; the loader accepts them, every row has its ledger row (N35-33's check), and the exit-code map gives each exactly one exit code (3).
- [ ] Until allocation, the emit sites of rows 1 to 5 and 7 use the interim codes with their `detail` strings, and a test asserts both code and detail for each.
- [ ] After allocation, each emit site uses its own code; the interim aliases are accepted by tests for one release and then removed.
- [ ] The ADR names, for each row, the verbs whose registry rows list it (`mission.transport`, `mission.synthesize` including `--strategy refine` for row 7, `mission.refute`, `mission.distinguish`, `sense.alias`, `analyze.curvature`) and the registry bead that owns each list; the generated documentation shows message and repairs for every loaded row. (The lists themselves are tested by those registry beads, which this bead does not depend on.)
- [ ] The two reconciliations are recorded: the block sentence corrected; the 826 and 881 mapping documented and tested.
- [ ] The sixth row (a connection outside its qualified stratum or scope) is in the ADR and, with the number N35x-02's run assigned, in `registries/refusals.toml` at M1 with the content of step 6; it maps to exit 3; the test `sixth_row_loads` asserts its five `detail` kinds; the ADR states that no interim applies to it and names its raisers (BD-243 from M3; N25c-43 and N25c-44 at M6).
- [ ] The seventh row (a synthesis query outside a lane's scope) is in the ADR with the content of step 7 and its interim `FROB-REF-857` with `detail: synthesis-out-of-scope`, matching what BD-362 emits; the test `seventh_row_content` asserts exit 3, `retry` `after-change`, a pointer, `observed` and `required`, and the three repairs in order.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-core/tests/refusal_augur_late.rs`: `seven_rows_loaded_or_held` (each of the seven slugs is either a row
of `registries/refusals.toml` with the table's message, pointer, retry and repairs, or a `held` ledger row whose
interim code and `detail` are in `frob_core::refusal::augur_late`; never neither, never both; the sixth is always
a row); `held_rows_load` (rows 1 to 5 and 7, after assignment); `each_maps_to_exit_3`;
`interim_codes_carry_detail` (six cases: rows 1 to 5 and 7); `unknown_detail_rejected`;
`prefix_mismatch_maps_826_to_881`; `sixth_row_loads` (the connection-outside-stratum row: present with its
assigned number, never an interim; exit 3; `detail` one of `rank-drop`, `over-determined`, `stratum-crossing`,
`component-removed`, `rank-only-displacement`; any other kind rejected; the first repair follows the kind);
`expected_fixture_matches_registry` (every row of `fixtures/augur/late-refusals/expected.toml` agrees with the
registry or the held ledger row); `seventh_row_content` (exit 3;
`after-change`; repairs in the order bind the schedule, reduce θ, use the optimization search; under the interim
the code is 857 and `detail` is `synthesis-out-of-scope`, which distinguishes it from 857's own cap refusals);
`adr_names_list_owners` (every row of the ADR names its verbs and the registry bead that lists it).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_late_refusals.sh` checks what this bead delivers and nothing a later bead delivers
(convention 7: every raising bead depends on this one). It runs `frob schema --refusals --json` and asserts, for
each of the seven slugs, either a row with the table's message, pointer, retry, ranked repairs and exit 3, or a
`held` ledger row whose interim code and `detail` match step 3 (the sixth always a row with its assigned
number); it asserts that each interim code is a loaded row and that no number is duplicated.
It asserts that the committed fixture `fixtures/augur/late-refusals/expected.toml` (slug, code or interim code,
`detail`, message, pointer, retry, repairs; this bead's) agrees with the registry: that fixture is the stage the
raising beads' scripts read to assert their trigger against this bead's content rather than against a copy: row 1 in `augur_coupling_transport.sh` (N25e-05), row 2 in
`augur_law_class.sh` (N25e-30), row 3 in `augur_error_law.sh` (N25e-36), row 4 in `augur_shared_nuisance.sh`
(N25e-41), row 5 in `augur_sizing_pareto.sh` (N25e-44), row 6 in the scripts of BD-243 (M3) and N25c-43 (M6),
row 7 in BD-362's (18 free parameters: observed 18, required at most 17). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_late_refusals/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N25e-45`), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, and a `refusal` line per row with slug, status
(`assigned` or `held`), code, detail, message, pointer, retry and repairs; the `summary` gives rows assigned and
rows held against seven, with the raising bead and script named for each row.

### Known answers and negative controls

The seven rows' content above, and the trigger fixtures named for each raising bead. Negative controls: a registry file with a duplicate
number fails to load; a row with exit code 4 for an 8xx protocol refusal fails the exit-map test; an emit site
that omits `detail` under an interim code fails `interim_codes_carry_detail`. Determinism: the registry and its
generated documentation are byte-identical across hosts. A sixth row emitted under a stand-in code with `detail:
connection-outside-stratum` fails `sixth_row_loads` (no interim applies to it).

## Considerations

- Fail-closed default until decided: the interim mapping of step 3. It is safe (every case is still refused) and
  slightly less precise for an agent that repairs by code alone. It is also the state while the ledger holds the
  six `8xx` rows, however long N35x-01's ruling takes. It never applies to the sixth row, which is assigned.
- If the owner's ruling on the `8xx` overflow is the four-digit continuation, the six held codes are four-digit
  (`FROB-REF-81nn`); the rows' content does not change.
- Further behaviours of the late rules need no new code: the dominance stop on a relaxation that does not
  enclose a declared state or energy source is refused under the existing `FROB-REF-551`; an unlicensed monotone
  fill under `FROB-REF-852`; certificates beyond size caps under `FROB-REF-849`; a region certificate over too
  many parameters under `FROB-REF-856`; a two-gain screen reused on a gain interval under `FROB-REF-854`.
