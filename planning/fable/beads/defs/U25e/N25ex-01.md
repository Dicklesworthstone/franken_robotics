## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is decided at a stated milestone; a kill gate
that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan. This bead is one such
decision, taken at the fourth milestone (M4).

The mechanism under decision belongs to the mission subsystem's verb `mission.transport`, which moves a
certified claim from a source robot to a target robot through a refinement relation between their task
interfaces. The relation matches every possible successor of the target to an allowed successor of the
source. That preserves universal claims. It preserves only the *support* of a probability law, so by itself
it carries no chance claim and no expected value. Counterexample: source and target each have one action
and two outcomes; the source succeeds with probability 0.99 and the target with 0.01; the relation holds,
and "succeeds with probability at least 0.95" is true of the source and false of the target. The Frontier
mechanism that lifts the restriction is a *coupling budget*: for each step t of the horizon a bound εₜ on
the total-variation distance between the source and target transition-and-observation kernels at related
states under the command the interface selects, giving

  P_target(accept) ≥ P_source(accept) − Σₜ εₜ,   |E_target − E_source| ≤ B·Σₜ εₜ for a quantity in [0, B].

The kill gate, as written: **KILL-COUPLING. Trigger: one violated transported bound that survives a repair
cycle, or no skill on the tray fixture with a budget below its slack. Fallback: `mission.transport` carries
universal claims only, and every chance or expected-value claim is re-measured on the target in the blinded
qualification lane.**

The trigger has two clauses with different decision points, because the gate measured beside it
(GATE-AUGUR-25) has two parts: a finite part at M4 (zero violated bounds over 200 random finite Markov
fixtures; the two-outcome reversal refused) and a tray part at M8 (measured budgets on a physical skill).
The program's fallback ledger and the M4 exit review both list the first clause as an M4 decision. The only
decision bead for this kill gate sits at M8, where the second clause is decided; it describes the M4
reading but cannot be closed, or even started, at M4. Without an M4 bead the exit review has a kill gate
due and nothing that decides it. This bead is that decision; the M8 bead keeps the second clause and reads
this bead's record.

Why the first clause can be decided alone: it is a soundness question about exact arithmetic on finite
kernels. A violated bound there means the argument, the proposer or the checker is wrong, and no amount of
tray data could rescue it. Tier of the mechanism: Frontier; the decision procedure is Solid.

## Scope

In: the M4 reading of the first clause; the repair-cycle rule; the decision record; the switch to the
fallback at M4 if the gate fires; what the M8 decision inherits.

Out: producing the measurements (the pilot on the 200 fixtures and the gate script of the finite part); the
second clause and the tray measurements (the M8 decision bead and the paired-run bead); the mechanism and
its checker rules.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs** (from the finite-part gate script's record): the number of fixtures run (200, with their
   seeds), the number of transported bounds checked, the number violated (a bound is violated when the exact
   target value computed by enumeration lies below the transported lower bound for a chance claim, or
   outside the transported interval for an expectation), each violating seed with its claim kind, Σ ε, the
   bound and the exact value; whether the two-outcome reversal was refused; the checker's verdict on every
   bundle.
2. **Reading rule.**
   1. Zero violations and the reversal refused: record `not fired at M4; decision due M8`.
   2. One or more violations: open **one** repair cycle. Locate the defect (the argument, the proposer, or
      the checker), fix it, and re-run the whole fixture set. The cycle is one: a second fix after a second
      failure is not a repair cycle, it is a fired gate.
   3. A violation that reappears in the re-run: the gate **fires at M4** without waiting for the tray.
   4. A reversal that is not refused is treated as a violation.
   5. A reading on fewer than 200 fixtures, or on fixtures generated after the mechanism's code was frozen
      differently from the registered generator, is `not decided` and the exit is blocked; it is never
      recorded as a pass.
3. **Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M4.md` (every kill-gate decision is
   `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`; the M8 decision of the second clause writes its own
   `ADR-KILL-COUPLING-M8.md` and cites this one) with the fields the kill-gate record requires (N40-03): the kill
   gate and the clause decided; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each
   measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (fixtures run against 200, violations against
   0, the reversal's refusal); the outcome; the fallback now in force, if any, and the name the capability carries
   from now on; the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes
   downstream (step 4 or 5); the commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds; the log path; whether a repair cycle was
   used and what was repaired; the date; the deciding session. KILL-COUPLING is decided per clause (N40-29:
   `decided = "M4"`, `reread = ["M8"]`), so its kill row in `registries/gates.toml` carries `decision` as an array
   and this bead appends the entry `{ scope = "clause:finite", exit = "M4", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`
   (`receipts`: the hashes of the finite-part receipts the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`) with
   `outcome = "not-fired"` (the reading `not fired at M4; decision due M8`) or `"fired"` (the reading `fired at
   M4`); the M8 bead appends the `clause:tray` entry. The reading `not decided` writes no entry: the gate stays
   due and the M4 exit is blocked. The entry resolves through `evidence.trace`.
4. **If fired at M4**, the downstream changes are those the gate names, applied now:
   - `mission.transport` refuses a claim of kind `chance` or `expectation` (refusal: chance claim transported
     without a coupling budget, row 1 of N25e-45, the interim `FROB-REF-852` with `detail:
     coupling-budget-missing` while the ledger holds the row; repair: measure on the target with `eval.qualify`);
     the exact coupling budget of finite kernels is no longer offered, and the coupling-source options registered
     for M8 (`--coupling-source paired-runs`, `--pairs`, `--level`) stay in the request schema and answer
     `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `killed`, with the fallback named in the first repair (VERBS-mission's rule
     for a killed option); the run verb's compute `budget` is unchanged, and stored bundles that carried a budget
     stay readable;
   - the checker's transport rules stay (they reject, so they stay sound); bundles that carried a
     transported chance or expectation claim are withdrawn and dependent answers are re-derived or
     downgraded through revalidation;
   - the tray paired-run work and the M8 reading of the second clause are not funded; the M8 decision bead
     records the gate as fired at M4 and closes citing this ADR;
   - the honest-status table and the register printout show chance-claim transport under the fallback's name,
     and the failed part of GATE-AUGUR-25 takes status `killed` with the detail `KILL-COUPLING: <fallback>`
     (N40-03's propagation);
   - the finite part of GATE-AUGUR-25 is recorded FAIL with the violating seed.
5. **If not fired**, nothing changes downstream: the mechanism stays Frontier, the M4 static core of
   `mission.transport` ships with exact budgets for finite kernels (class Certified), and the tray work
   remains gated by its own pilot and by the M8 decision.
6. **Independence.** The reading is made by a session that did not implement the claim kinds, the budget
   computation or the checker rules.

A trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment to the gate register made before the
measurement it governs.

- [ ] If a repair cycle was used, the ADR names the defect and the re-run of the whole fixture set.

      accept it and it resolves through `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] If fired: the refusal is live for chance and expectation claims, `--coupling-source paired-runs` answers
      `FROB-REF-724` with status `killed` while the compute `budget` is unchanged, affected bundles are withdrawn,
      and the M4 exit review lists the fallback as adopted (the M8 decision bead, which depends on this one,
      records the tray clause `not-reached` from this entry; that is its criterion, not this bead's).
- [ ] The dry run with a planted violating fixture produces `fired at M4` and switches the dry-run profile
      to the fallback; the dry run with clean inputs produces `not fired at M4; decision due M8`.
- [ ] The deciding session did not implement the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The decision consumes recorded measurements; its own logic is the reading rule
(`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_coupling_m4`):

- `clean_reading_not_fired`: 200 fixtures, 0 violations, reversal refused → `not fired at M4; decision due
  M8`.
- `violation_opens_one_cycle`: 1 violation → state `repair-cycle-open`; a clean re-run of the whole set →
  `not fired at M4; decision due M8` with `repair_cycle: used`.
- `violation_after_cycle_fires`: a violation in the re-run → `fired at M4`.
- `second_cycle_not_allowed`: a request to open a second cycle is refused by the state machine.
- `reversal_accepted_is_violation`.
- `short_reading_not_decided`: 150 fixtures → `not decided`; never a pass.
- `gate_row_cites_adr`: the registry check fails when the row's `clause:finite` entry cites no ADR.
- `reading_maps_to_register`: `not fired at M4; decision due M8` writes outcome `not-fired`, `fired at M4`
  writes `fired`, both with `scope = "clause:finite"` and `exit = "M4"`; `not decided` writes no entry; a later
  `clause:tray` entry leaves this entry in force for its scope.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/augur_kill_coupling_m4.sh`: (1) read the finite-part gate record, the receipt of N25e-05's
`scripts/e2e/augur_coupling_transport.sh` and the metric lines of its log; (2) apply the reading rule and print the
reading; (3) dry-run profile A: a planted violating record (a ten-step chain whose per-step budget is understated:
ε declared 0.001 where the kernels differ by 0.003) must give `fired at M4`; then, with
`C=fixtures/augur/transport-claims/chain10` (N25e-02's ten-step chain), `frob mission transport $C/claims.fmission
--from $C/source.fkernel.json --to $C/target.fkernel.json --relation $C/relation.json --claims <chance-claim> --key
kc-m4-chance-<run> --json` must exit 3 through `e2e_expect_refusal` with the code, `detail` and repairs read from
row 1 of N25e-45's `fixtures/augur/late-refusals/expected.toml` (the interim `FROB-REF-852` with `detail:
coupling-budget-missing` while the ledger holds the row; never typed into the script) and the `eval.qualify`
repair, while the same call with the chain's universal claim (`--key kc-m4-universal-<run>`) still crosses, and
the chance-claim call with `--coupling-source paired-runs --pairs 299 --level 0.05` (`--key kc-m4-killed-<run>`)
exits 5 with `FROB-REF-724`, status `killed`, the fallback named in the first repair; (4) dry-run profile B: clean
inputs give `not fired at M4; decision due M8`. NDJSON per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/augur_kill_coupling_m4/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, fixture and violation counts
against the threshold of 0, each violating seed with bound and exact value, the repair-cycle state, the
refusal block of the fallback run, and the reading. The `summary` line lists
`gates: ["KILL-COUPLING", "GATE-AUGUR-25"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The two-outcome case: Σ ε = 0.98, the transported bound is 0.01, and the claim "at least 0.95" is refused.
- A finite chain of 10 steps with ε = 0.003 per step: a source probability of 0.99 transports to at least
  0.99 − 0.03 = 0.96, and the exact target value computed by enumeration lies above it.
- The planted fixture of profile A: the ten-step chain built so that the source never fails before its last
  step (success 99/100 there) and the target, in addition, falls with probability 3/1000 per step into an
  absorbing failure; its exact success is (997/1000)¹⁰ × 99/100 ≈ 0.96070, which lies above the true bound 0.96.
  With the budget understated (ε declared 1/1000 per step) the transported bound is 0.99 − 0.01 = 0.98 > 0.96070:
  a violation the reading must catch.
- Negative controls of the procedure: a reading rule that passes on 150 fixtures; a rule that allows a
  second repair cycle; a rule that ignores an accepted reversal. Each is caught by a unit test above.

## Considerations

- The expected outcome of the first clause is `not fired`: exact budgets on finite kernels are arithmetic.
  The likelier place for this kill gate to fire is its second clause at M8, on physics, where contact-rich
  bodies decorrelate and measured budgets may exceed every slack.
- The fallback is strictly safer and only dearer: one sealed qualification study per chance claim on the
  target.
- Identifier note: one index table of the plan lists KILL-COUPLING beside the exposure-domain coupling test
  of the task chapter; that subsection's kill gate is KILL-EXPODOMAIN. KILL-COUPLING is only the gate
  decided here and at M8.
- Owner of the decision: the Augur lead, confirmed at the M4 exit review.
