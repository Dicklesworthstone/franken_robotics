## Background

A search that minimizes a margin on a fast simulator finds the simulator's weaknesses as readily as the
design's. ADR-NEMESIS-03 therefore says a failure counts after promotion: a failure found on a fast tier is
replayed one tier up and under numerical variants before it enters any count. FACT (source register, to be
re-read): in one study 62.5% of unsafe simulated test cases led to unsafe behaviour on a test track; simulated
failures do not all transfer, and the first transfer to check is between our own tiers. The same applies to a
solvability witness, which is itself a simulated run: a witness that succeeds through solver-created energy is
not a witness.

The guard is three replays (INV-NEMESIS-05: a failure and its witness count only after the artifact guard). It
is Solid: it composes mechanisms that exist elsewhere (cross-tier replay and the promotion machinery, the
decision-level equivalence test, the model-form variants, the energy ledger) and adds bookkeeping. The tiers are
`gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal` (fast, single precision), `batch-cpu` (the CPU reference path, here in double
precision) and `referee` (the trusted transactional tier).

A failure that the guard removes is not thrown away. It is labelled, excluded from every counterexample count,
and written to the fidelity ledger as a tier-sensitive case, because a failure of the simulator is also a
finding.

## Scope

In: `guard::check(run, role)` for a failing run or a witness run; the three checks; the label
`artifact-candidate` with the variant that removed the failure; exclusion rules; the fidelity-ledger record;
GATE-NEMESIS-06's record.
Out: the planted artifacts and genuine failures (N26a-15); the end-to-end script (N26a-16); promotion statistics
for populations of results (BD-263); the hard-world set's admission check that consumes the verdict (BD-275);
shrinking's refusal of artifact candidates (BD-274).

## Technical approach

1. **Input.** A finished episode with its world, candidate, tier, numerical profile and verdict: for a failure,
   `violated` with failure class, binding atom, margin and failure time; for a witness, `satisfied` with margin.
   `role` is `Failure` or `Witness`. `target` is `Count` (default) or `HardSet` (adds the referee).
2. **Check 1: one tier up.** From the fast tier to `batch-cpu` in double precision; for anything that will enter
   the hard-world set, also on the referee. The same world address is re-run (same scene draw, disturbances,
   randomization and policy weights). The decision-level equivalence test decides whether the higher tier
   reproduces the run: for a failure, the same verdict, the same failure class and the same binding atom, with
   the failure time inside the recorded envelope for "time of first failure" of the fidelity-ledger stratum (the
   model class, scene class, tier pair and kernel versions of the run); for a witness, the verdict `satisfied`.
   A higher-tier run that ends in a numerical outcome is not a reproduction and not a refutation: the check is
   `unresolved`, the failure is not counted, and it is reported as such.
3. **Check 2: numerical variants.** Four replays on the tier that reproduced the run, each changing one numerical
   thing with physical parameters held, each a distinct step-plan identity:
   | Tag | Variant |
   |---|---|
   | `half-step` | Half the physics step; the requirement's clock is unchanged |
   | `iterations-x2` | Twice the solver iterations |
   | `law-2` | The alternative contact law (the second qualified compliant law in place of the default) |
   | `tol-div-10` | A solver tolerance ten times tighter |
   These are the perturbations that should not matter to a physical failure. The run survives a variant when the
   same criterion as in check 1 holds under it.
4. **Check 3: energy audit.** The energy ledger of the original run and of the tier-up run is read. A failure or
   a witness whose integrator-and-solver residual exceeds its envelope is flagged: created energy above 1% of
   the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier, or above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee
   (TARGET, the one rule of the created-energy audit); the companion monitors `contact-active` (a contact
   returned more work than it absorbed) and `cone-excess` (friction utilization above 1.05 for more than 1% of
   stance time; a pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal) are read with it.
5. **Verdict.**
   ```rust
   pub enum GuardStatus { Passed, ArtifactCandidate { removed_by: Vec<Removal> }, Unresolved { reason } }
   pub enum Removal { TierUp(Tier), Variant(VariantTag), EnergyAudit(EnergyFlag) }
   pub struct GuardVerdict { status, promotion: { tier, reproduced }, variants: Vec<(VariantTag, bool)>,
                             energy: EnergyReadout, steps: u64 }
   ```
   A failure that vanishes under any of the three checks is labelled `artifact-candidate` with every check that
   removed it. `Passed` is recorded on the wire as `"promotion": {"tier": "referee", "reproduced": true,
   "variants": ["half-step", "iterations-x2", "law-2", "tol-div-10"]}`.
6. **Consequences.** `Passed`: the failure is promoted and may be counted, catalogued, shrunk and (with the
   referee check) offered to the hard-world set; a passed witness may support an oracle class.
   `ArtifactCandidate`: excluded from the hard-world set and from counterexample counts; shrinking refuses it
   (FROB-REF-564: shrinking requested for a failure that does not replay, or that the guard labelled an artifact
   candidate; repairs: rerun the guard after the simulator defect is fixed, or shrink a different failure; exit
   3); one record is written to the fidelity ledger as a tier-sensitive case with the world, the candidate, the
   quantity "task decision", the tier pair or variant, and both outcomes. `Unresolved`: not counted, reported.
7. **Cost.** One tier-up replay, four variant replays and, for the hard-world set, one referee replay: six
   episodes per guarded run, charged to the campaign arm that produced the run. The guard runs only on candidate
   failures and witnesses, never on every evaluated world.
8. **Surface.** No verb of its own. Its verdict appears in `nemesis.search` results (`artifact_guard`, the
   `not_counted.artifact_candidate` bucket), in `nemesis.oracle` results (for witnesses) and in the `promotion`
   block of every counterexample file. Determinism: each replay has the determinism class of its tier; the
   comparison is a decision-level comparison, not a bitwise one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, GATE-NEMESIS-06 (TARGET): 100 of 100 planted artifact failures are labelled
      `artifact-candidate` and none is in the hard-world set; at least 95 of 100 genuine planted failures
      survive promotion. The first and third counts are measured here by the crate-level gate test
      `guard_gate` on the planted registry (N26a-15); the hard-world-set clause is closed by the set's
      admission check (BD-275) and asserted from the user's side by the script of N26a-16, which writes the
      complete gate record with measured counts.
- [ ] The label names what removed the failure: the box-cone traction plant is removed by the production cone on
      the tier-up replay or by `law-2`; the energy-pumping plant is flagged by the energy audit; the expected
      removal per plant is fixed in the planted registry at its first measurement and asserted thereafter.
- [ ] A genuine planted failure survives every variant and returns `Passed` with all four tags.
- [ ] A witness that succeeds only through a planted artifact is rejected (role `Witness`).
- [ ] Every `artifact-candidate` has a fidelity-ledger record; `nemesis.shrink` on it is refused with
      FROB-REF-564; `nemesis.hardset add` on it is refused.
- [ ] A higher-tier numerical fault yields `Unresolved`, never `Passed` and never `artifact-candidate`.
- [ ] No failure appears in a count, a catalogue or a counterexample file without a `Passed` verdict (property
      over the campaign records: INV-NEMESIS-05).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the three checks, the label and its consequences; a CPU reference path exists by
      construction (the guard's own tier is the CPU).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/guard.rs` (real replays on small fixtures):
- `reproduction_criterion`: tier-up outcomes (same verdict, class, atom, time inside envelope) → reproduced;
  each of the four ways of differing (verdict; class; atom; time outside the envelope) → not reproduced.
- `box_cone_failure_removed`: a 10 kg block on friction 0.5 pushed along the diagonal with 60 N. The true cone
  limit is 0.5 × 10 × 9.81 = 49.05 N, so the block slides and is delivered; the box-linearized cone holds up to
  √2 × 49.05 = 69.37 N, so under it the block sticks and the requirement "delivered" is violated. The guard
  returns `ArtifactCandidate` naming the removal.
- `energy_pump_failure_flagged`: a 1 kg body whose 1 mm penetration is removed in one 2 ms step gains 0.5 m/s
  and 0.125 J per event; the hopping fixture gains height and violates "apex below limit"; the energy audit
  flags `energy-created`. The compliant law at stiffness 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J and returns less: under `law-2`
  the failure vanishes.
- `genuine_failure_survives`: a slip on a grease patch under a push fails under all four variants and one tier
  up.
- `witness_role`: the false witness of the box cone (holding on a slope with tan θ = 0.6 at friction 0.5) is
  rejected.
- `numerical_fault_is_unresolved`.
- `ledger_record_written`; `hardset_target_adds_referee`.
- `inv_nemesis_05` (property over 500 synthetic campaign records): counted implies guarded and passed.
- `guard_gate` (the planted registry, real replays): counts of artifact failures labelled and of genuine
  failures surviving, printed against 100 of 100 and at least 95 of 100.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_guard.sh` (N26a-16) offers the planted artifact failures and the genuine failures to
`frob nemesis search --method given`, then tries to shrink and to add artifact candidates to a hard-world set.
Logged per world: tier and variant of each replay, verdict, class, atom and failure time of each, the energy
readout with its thresholds, the status, the removals, the ledger record identity.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-662: planted artifacts (box-cone traction; energy-pumping penetration recovery) are labelled
`artifact-candidate` with the variant that removed each; control: a genuine planted failure survives every
variant. CX-295: "a failure found under one contact law is a failure of the design" (it is not). The
hand-checkable values: √2 times the friction coefficient along the diagonals of a box cone; 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J
per event for the penetration pump; 0.05 J stored by the compliant law. Negative controls: a guard that runs no
variant (the planted artifacts pass: the gate must catch it); a guard that labels everything (the genuine
failures then fail the 95-of-100 row). Determinism lanes: the guard's verdict on one world is identical twice on
one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The guard cannot tell a real failure that happens to be sensitive (a knife-edge slip) from an artifact; it
  errs toward excluding. That costs yield (the 95-of-100 row measures it) and never admits an artifact.
- All four variants share one contact formulation family; an artifact common to both contact laws passes. The
  referee check for hard-set entry and the fidelity ledger are the second line.
- Interaction with promotion statistics: the guard is a per-world decision check; population-level promotion of
  scores uses the sequential promotion rule and is not replaced by it.
- KILL-NEMESIS, KILL-RARE and KILL-MCW all keep the guard.
