## Background

*Is this design's failure probability below p⋆?* is a claim, and only estimators with a valid finite-sample bound
may make it. The certification lane issues a statistical bound; its proof class is `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ,
never `Certified`, which is reserved for certificates a checker accepted. Three estimators belong to the lane:

| Estimator | Returns | Valid bound at a fixed budget | Foundation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crude nominal sampling, zero-failure rule | p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) after n episodes without a failure | Yes, exact | Ours; integer counts and outward rounding (N26a-21) |
| Crude nominal sampling, sequential | A confidence sequence for p that ends certified, refuted or exhausted | Yes, at any stopping time | The sibling's Bernoulli mixture sequence, cap 10⁶ (N26a-22) |
| Bounded-weight importance sampling | An estimate of p, a two-sided confidence sequence, and a lower bound for refutation | Yes, for an explicit Gaussian law and a proposal fixed before the run | The sibling's defensive importance execution (this bead) |

The third is this bead's mechanism. The proposal is a **defensive mixture** q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p_shifted, so the
likelihood ratio w = p/q is at most 1/ε, and ε·w·1[failure] is an observation in [0, 1] whose mean is ε·p. A
confidence sequence on that mean, divided by ε, bounds p at every stopping time.

FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`): the sibling provides exactly this construction for
parameters that are independent or jointly Gaussian in normal germs: `ImportanceConfig { germ_shift,
defensive_fraction }`, `ImportanceExecution::new(plan, config)`, `advance` and `advance_interruptible`,
`report()` with the weighted mean, a descriptive standard error, an effective-sample count and the mean
likelihood weight (whose population value is one), `assess_failure_probability`, checkpoints, and a cap of 10⁶
evaluations; the proposal must be chosen before the run, or fitted by `proposal::fit_proposal` on a separate
completed pilot whose data are never reused.

What we add (DESIGN): declared monotone transforms from normal germs to non-Gaussian card distributions and the
same mixture for discrete scene edits (N26a-24), and a variance-adaptive betting sequence on the scaled
observation (N26a-25). The scope is narrow and is stated in every result.

**The honest limit.** With weights bounded by 1/ε, the upper bound after n runs without a failure cannot fall
below about ln(1/δ)/(ε·n), so importance sampling is never the cheaper way to certify absence. It earns its place
in two cases: refuting a target, by a lower confidence bound that exceeds p⋆, and tightening an estimate once
failures have been observed. Bet BET-32 is worded accordingly. Rejected: unbounded weights from a bare tilted
proposal (one published cross-entropy sampler returned 7.6 × 10⁻²² for a true 2.0 × 10⁻³); extreme-value
extrapolation (checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ takes the same nominal episodes that certify directly).

## Technical approach

1. **Request.** `frob nemesis bound <candidate> --task <task> --tier <tier> --target <p⋆> --delta <δ> --mode
   certify|refute|estimate --budget … [--proposal <id> | --shift … --epsilon …] [--episodes <run set>]
   [--finalist] --key … --json`. Defaults: δ = 1/20; ε = 1/10 (TARGET); tier as stated by the caller (no
   default: the scope is part of the claim). `--finalist` is the decision policy's recorded attestation that a
   candidate not drawn from a recorded campaign's finalists is one (N26a-26's finalists-only rule; requested of
   VERBS-nemesis, whose row does not carry it yet).
2. **Commit.** The request is hashed into the ledger (candidate, task, requirement, predicate identity, p⋆, δ,
   tier, randomization, stream and address range, proposal, design) before the first draw (N26a-23).
3. **Mode `certify`.** Crude nominal sampling only, under the design that N26a-22's `choose_design` rule picks
   at admission and records in the commitment: `sequential` when the sequence's clean count
   n_clean = `plan_sequential(p⋆, δ)` fits both the request's budget and the sibling's 10⁶ cap, otherwise
   `fixed-n` at n = `required(p⋆, δ)` (N26a-21), whose clean run certifies (at δ = 0.05: `sequential` at 10⁻⁴
   when the budget reaches 92,872 episodes; `fixed-n` at 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁶ whatever the budget, since 1,050,021
   and 11,705,893 exceed the cap). Ends `certified` (upper bound below p⋆), `refuted` (lower bound above p⋆) or
   `exhausted` with the bound reached; the result names the design. Importance sampling is never used to
   certify absence.
4. **Mode `refute`.** Bounded-weight sampling under the committed mixture; the one-sided lower betting test on
   the scaled observation against m₀ = ε·p⋆. Ends `refuted` the first time the wealth reaches 1/δ, or
   `exhausted` with the lower bound reached. Only failures that passed the artifact guard contribute (a failure
   labelled an artifact candidate contributes zero, which is conservative for a lower bound).
5. **Mode `estimate`.** Bounded-weight sampling; returns the point estimate p̂ = (mean of w·1[failure]), the
   two-sided betting confidence sequence for p, and the sibling's descriptive figures (standard error, failure
   effective samples, mean likelihood weight). The label is `Tested` for the interval and descriptive for p̂.
6. **Execution (numbered, bounded-weight modes).**
   1. Build the sampler from the committed proposal: shift μ in germ space, discrete tilts, ε.
   2. Draw worlds by address, one mixture indicator per episode; legality is checked on the final world.
   3. Run episodes on the stated tier in batches; evaluate the frozen predicate; compute w and y = ε·w·1[failure]
      exactly in log space.
   4. Feed the airlock's importance execution (for its report and checkpoint) and our betting sequence (for the
      bound). The sibling's effective-sample floor is honoured: if the weights miss the failure region and the
      floor trips, the estimate is refused upstream and the mode ends `exhausted` with the reason.
   5. After each batch evaluate the stopping rule of the mode; checkpoint; on cancellation drain and finalize.
7. **Result (output schema `frob.nemesis.bound.out/1`, input `frob.nemesis.bound.in/1`: the names the verb
   registry VERBS-nemesis gives by rule).**
   ```json
   { "mode": "refute", "outcome": "refuted", "target": "1/100000", "delta": "1/20",
     "tier": "batch-cpu", "randomization": "rand:…", "predicate": "pred:…",
     "n": 18432, "failures_observed": 7311, "failures_counted": 7309,
     "estimate": 3.2e-5, "lower": 1.9e-5, "upper": 5.1e-5,
     "weights": { "epsilon": "1/10", "bound": 10, "max_observed": 9.97, "mean": 1.003, "failure_ess": 2210 },
     "proposal": { "id": "prop:…", "law": "defensive-mixture", "fitted_on": "campaign:… (disjoint)" },
     "design": "bounded-weight", "floor_if_clean": "ln(1/δ)/(ε·n) ≈ 1.6e-3",
     "scope": "probability under the task's nominal randomization rand:…, on tier batch-cpu, for the frozen requirement; a statement about the model at that tier; provisional until promoted; never a claim about reality" }
   ```
   Exit 0 for `certified`, 2 for `refuted`, `exhausted` and `estimate` results (completed with findings), 8 while
   running; refusals per the table below. Evidence block: class `Tested` at the stated confidence, the tier
   and determinism class of the run, `claims: []` (a bound is a statement about the model at one tier and is
   cited only together with its scope line), `reality: no-claim`.
8. **Refusals raised on this verb.**
   | Code | Meaning | Exit |
   |---|---|---|
   | FROB-REF-560 | A bound was offered episodes that were not drawn nominally or with recorded bounded weights | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-563 | Importance sampling outside its admitted law, or with a proposal fitted on the run it is applied to | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-568 | The failure predicate changed after sampling began | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-760 | The episodes a claim needs at its level exceed the budget; the bound reachable within budget is attached | 4 |
   Repairs are those of N26a-23 and N26a-21. The verb's row (VERBS-nemesis) also carries three codes raised at
   its boundary by other owners, and the verb's declared refusal list is exactly the row's seven:
   | Code | Where it arises on this verb | Exit |
   |---|---|---|
   | FROB-REF-549 | A bound requested by the co-design cascade over battery-selected or search-selected episodes: the cascade's code for the rule 560 enforces here; surfaced unchanged when the call comes through the cascade | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-569 | The policy's exploration state asked the splitting stage (BD-277) for splitting on a task with no per-step randomness after the fork. Under the decision policy this is fallback trigger (b): the bound completes by nominal sampling and the refusal is recorded in the result's `fallback` block, not returned as the verb's exit | 3 (as a code) |
   | FROB-REF-840 | A world address in the confirmation namespace without the qualification administration scope (owner: the qualification lane; raised by the address admission every Nemesis verb shares) | 5 |
   Two more refused inputs have no code in the plan; both are uncoded refusals of the chapter's refusal registry
   (N26-REF), on file in the second allocation round of the refusal registry (N35x-02), and tests assert exit
   code, slug and pointer, never a number, until that ledger assigns one:
   - `certify-by-importance-sampling`: `--mode certify` with `--proposal`, `--shift` or `--epsilon` (importance
     sampling never certifies absence). Refused at admission, exit 3, with the repairs "use `--mode refute` or
     `--mode estimate`; drop the proposal to certify by nominal sampling".
   - `importance-over-sibling-cap`: a bounded-weight run (`refute` or `estimate`) planned above the cap of the
     pinned sibling's importance execution, 10⁶ evaluations (each bounded-weight episode is one evaluation).
     Refused at admission, exit 4 (DESIGN: like FROB-REF-760, a count refused before anything runs, with the
     reachable alternative attached): `observed` the planned evaluations, `required` at most the cap in force,
     the bound reachable at the cap attached (for a clean run the floor ln(1/δ)/(ε·10⁶)), and the repairs
     "split the estimate into independent stages under separate commitments; use crude nominal sampling".
     Nothing runs and no run is truncated at the cap; the verb never splits a plan by itself, because stages
     that share one commitment are not independent estimates. The cap is the constant the airlock's `stats`
     group exposes for the pinned sibling (N33b-13, a dependency). N26b-22, which depends on this bead, pins
     that constant with an admission test and repeats this refusal from the user's side; the cap becomes the
     configured one when the upstream change FD-FSIM-18 is adopted (N26b-24).
9. **Surface.** Verb `nemesis.bound`, kind run, first milestone M3; MCP tool `nemesis_bound` in the cluster
   `attack`; scope `nemesis:run`; idempotent by key; long-running with progress in episodes.
10. **Gate.** GATE-NEMESIS-04 (known answer; TARGET): over 10⁴ synthetic studies with known p the 95% upper bound
    covers p in at least 95%; the defensive mixture covers under a mis-centred proposal; non-nominal episodes are
    refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_bound.sh` (N26a-30) drives all three modes and the four refusals. No verb row has an
option that runs many studies of a chosen size, so the 10⁴-study fixed-n coverage campaign is this bead's
`gate_rows` campaign run through the Gauntlet runner by the gate's name (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-NEMESIS-04`), with 20 of its studies repeated through the
binary for parity. Logged: per study n, failures, bounds and whether the truth is covered; per bounded-weight
batch the maximum and mean weight; the gate rows with thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-665 (Gaussian tail by defensive mixture; control: a mixture without its nominal share on a mis-centred
proposal). KA-664 (zero-failure counts), KA-139 (the mixture's floor), KA-141 (bounds bracket a closed-form
probability in at least 1 − δ of 10⁴ repetitions). CX-290, CX-291, CX-065. INV-NEMESIS-03 through the admission
layer. Determinism lanes: a bound run twice on one machine gives identical draws, weights and bounds; across
worker counts identical; across backends the bound is re-measured on each tier, since a bound is scoped to its
tier.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NEMESIS-4 (the sample caps of 10⁶ in the sibling's Bernoulli sequence and importance execution, and the
  absence of splitting upstream; owner: airlock maintainer). Default: our own exact binomial bound for larger
  counts; upstream work items FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 are open and no milestone waits on them.
- OPEN-NEMESIS-3 (compute for a 10⁻⁶ claim). Default: certify to 10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.
- BET-32's falsifiers, all read from this bead's gate and N26a-30: coverage below nominal on fixtures with a
  planted rate; effective sample size below 30% on the reference family; on a fixture whose planted rate of 10⁻⁴
  is above the target, the episodes needed for the lower bound to exceed the target not at least 3 times fewer
  than crude sampling needs; a planted second failure mode outside the proposal reported as absent.
- A bound says how rare failures are under the nominal randomization of one tier. It says nothing about worlds
  outside that randomization, and the legality profile is wider than the randomization on purpose.
- The verb depends on the search's seed bead because proposals come from completed search campaigns and because
  every episode's proposal law is recorded by the same machinery.
