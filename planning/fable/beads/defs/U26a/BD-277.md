## Background

*Where does this design fail?* is a search, and any method that finds failures faster is welcome. Two exploration
methods serve rare failures. Cross-entropy tilting moves static parameters toward low margin; it needs whole
episodes and reuses no snapshot (N26a-08). **Splitting** restores a snapshot of a world that has reached a
distress level and continues it many times under fresh per-step disturbances. This bead is splitting.

DESIGN: a child world has the address of its parent extended by a fork path; its per-step disturbance streams
after the fork tick are re-keyed by the child address (the replicate mode of a fork); and its prefix is shared
bit for bit. The distress score is an online past-time margin with fixed levels chosen before the run
(N26a-29). A fork replays its parent identically unless noise enters after the fork, so splitting applies only
to failures driven by per-step disturbances; a request to split on static parameters alone is refused
(FROB-REF-569).

Why it is exploration and never a bound. The splitting estimator (the product of the fractions of trials that
reach each next level) is unbiased whatever the score and the number of replicas, in theory. But continuations
of one parent are dependent, its accuracy rests on the score, and no finite-sample guarantee on its error exists
at a fixed budget (in one published study adaptive splitting estimated 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ for a true 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ with a
spread five times the mean). That is why exploration issues failures, and rates labelled `Estimated`, and never
a bound.

TARGET, in its surviving form: with an informative score, exploration reaches failures of nominal probability
10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁵ with 10 to 100 times fewer episodes than crude sampling. FACT: measured gains in the sources read
are 2 to 20 times in one driving study and about 5 times in mean-square error on one policy-in-the-loop task.
Worked numbers, illustrative: splitting to 10⁻⁶ with six levels of conditional probability 0.1 and 10⁴
continuations per level uses 6 × 10⁴ fragments, about fifty times fewer than the 3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes of the
certification table, and under independence its coefficient of variation is at least
√(6 × 0.9/(0.1 × 10⁴)) = 7%; correlation between continuations of one parent makes it larger.

Frontier; bet BET-32's exploration half; no splitting code exists in the sibling libraries (FACT, to be re-read;
upstream work item FD-FSIM-19 would add one, and a differential test on the random-walk fixture would then
choose between them).

## Technical approach

1. **Preconditions.**
   - Levels are frozen (a `LevelRecord` for this task, requirement and tier) and the score is not declared
     `uninformative` or `flat`.
   - Fresh randomness after the fork: the task has at least one registered per-step random stream that is active
     after the earliest possible fork tick and is not held fixed by the request. Otherwise refuse with
     FROB-REF-569 ("splitting requested where no randomness enters after the fork"; `pointer`: the stream
     registry; ranked repairs: (1) use tilting or the defensive mixture, which act on static parameters;
     (2) enable a per-step disturbance the task declares; (3) use crude sampling; exit 3).
2. **Algorithm (fixed levels ℓ₁ > … > ℓ_L = 0, fixed effort N per stage).**
   1. Stage 0: run N₀ nominal episodes from their start (addresses by keyed draw). An episode *hits* level 1 if
      its running-minimum margin reaches ℓ₁; the world is snapshotted at the first crossing tick. R₀ = number
      of hits.
   2. Stage k (1 ≤ k < L): draw N parents by a keyed draw, uniformly with replacement, from the R_{k−1}
      snapshots of the previous stage. For each, create a child: address = parent address extended by the fork
      path `(tick, k, child index)`; state restored from the snapshot bit for bit; every per-step random stream
      re-keyed by the child address from the fork tick on; nothing else changed. Continue the child to the end
      of the episode or until it crosses ℓ_{k+1} (snapshot at the crossing). R_k = number of children that
      cross.
   3. If some R_k = 0 the run stops: the estimate is 0 with the note "extinct at level k + 1", and the stage's
      trials are reported.
   4. Estimate p̂ = (R₀/N₀)·Π over k = 1 … L − 1 of (R_k/N). Children that cross ℓ_L = 0 after the boundary check
      are failures.
3. **Child identity and the shared prefix.** The fork classifies as an exogenous intervention (a future
   disturbance changed): restart at the fork tick, the whole checkpoint restored with nothing regenerated. A
   child never mutates its parent. Each failing child is a replayable world: parent address, fork path, and the
   keyed streams reproduce it.
4. **Duplicate detection (the fork control).** After each stage, state digests of the children of one parent are
   compared a fixed number of ticks after the fork (64, TARGET). If two children of one parent are identical,
   the streams were not re-keyed or carry no randomness: the run aborts with FROB-REF-569 and nothing is
   reported. A fork without fresh randomness is an identical copy, and counting copies as evidence is exactly
   the error this guards against.
5. **Spread.** The run is repeated in G independent groups (default 8, TARGET) with disjoint streams; the
   reported spread is the coefficient of variation across groups, printed beside the independence lower bound
   √(L·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·N)). A spread far above that bound is a warning that continuations are strongly correlated.
6. **Downstream.** Failing children go to the solvability oracle and the artifact guard like any candidate
   failure, then to the failure-mode catalogue. The per-level fork fractions feed the informativeness trigger
   (under 1% at a level: fallback to crude sampling).
7. **Lane.** Every splitting episode carries `law = Fork { parent, path, level }`, which is an exploration law:
   the certification lane refuses it (FROB-REF-560; INV-NEMESIS-03). The estimate is published with class
   `Estimated`, the words "exploration estimate; not a bound", the levels' hash, the per-level table and the
   spread.
8. **Surface.** `frob nemesis search … --method splitting` for discovery, `--method exploration` for the lane
   as one arm (splitting with cross-entropy tilting on one archive, BD-273; the arm GATE-NEMESIS-05 measures),
   and the exploration step of `frob nemesis bound` (the decision policy). No verb of its own. This bead registers `Splitting` as a
   `SearchMethod` in the search driver's portfolio (BD-273, which reserved the slot: until this registration
   `--method splitting` returns FROB-REF-724 and `auto` gives the 15% share to cross-entropy). It also owns the
   switch `nemesis.rare.exploration = splitting | off` (default `splitting`): `off` withdraws the registration
   (FROB-REF-724 again, now naming KILL-RARE and its ADR), and the decision policy (N26a-26) reads it to skip its
   exploration state. The kill decision (N26a-32) only sets the switch; the frontier-splitting row of the crate's
   `contract.toml` (N26a-01) is what the build drops if KILL-RARE fires.
9. **Gate.** GATE-NEMESIS-05 (Design comparison; TARGET): on planted failures of probability 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one
   with two separate modes, at equal episode budget and 30 repetitions, exploration reaches failures with at
   least 10 times fewer episodes than crude sampling at 10⁻⁵, and both modes are found in at least 80% of
   repetitions.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, GATE-NEMESIS-05, in the part this bead delivers: every quantity the gate and
      KILL-RARE read is in each splitting run's result (episodes and fragments used, the failures with their
      fork paths, the modes they cover, the per-level fork fractions, the estimate with its group spread),
      checked by `fork_law_recorded`, `walk_unbiased` and `cv_matches_independence_bound`. The measurement
      belongs to N26a-31, which depends on this bead: the paired lower 95% bound of the episode ratio at 10⁻⁵ and
      the share of repetitions in which both modes were found, entered from its script's receipt with
      `record_gate` by a session that did not implement the lane (no script writes a gate record); N26a-32
      decides KILL-RARE from that record. This bead waits on neither (convention 7).
- [ ] KA-666: on the biased random walk (up-probability 0.4, start at level 1) the estimate of reaching level 20
      before level 0 has mean within three standard errors of 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴ over 500
      replications, for two different level sets (unbiased whatever the levels).
- [ ] A walk with no noise after the fork returns identical children and the run is refused with FROB-REF-569;
      a request on a task with only static randomization is refused before any episode runs.
- [ ] With L = 6, p₀ = 0.1 and N = 10⁴ on an independent synthetic fixture the across-group coefficient of
      variation is near 7% (between 5% and 10%); on a correlated fixture it is larger and the warning prints.
- [ ] A child's prefix digests equal its parent's up to the fork tick; a child with the empty re-key equals the
      parent's continuation bit for bit; two children with different keys differ.
- [ ] A splitting episode offered to `nemesis.bound` is refused (FROB-REF-560); the published rate is labelled
      `Estimated` and never appears in a field named bound.
- [ ] `Splitting` is registered in the search portfolio and `--method splitting` runs; the switch
      `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` withdraws it (FROB-REF-724 naming KILL-RARE) without touching any other
      method.
- [ ] Failing children are replayable from (parent address, fork path) alone, and pass through oracle, guard and
      catalogue before being counted.
- [ ] Cancellation mid-stage drains and leaves no orphan snapshots; `CONTRACT.md` updated; a CPU reference path
      exists.

  probabilities 0.4, 0.3077, 0.1649, 0.03755, 0.1973 for the first set).

- `child_address_extends_parent`; `prefix_shared_bitwise`; `null_rekey_equals_parent`; `children_differ`.
- `parents_drawn_uniformly`: chi-square over 10⁵ parent draws at one stage.
- `extinction_reported`: a stage with zero hits ends with estimate 0 and the note.
- `fork_law_recorded`; `estimate_label_is_estimated`.
- `failing_child_replays`: replay from address and fork path gives the same digests.
- `uninformative_trigger`: a fixture where 0.5% of forks reach the next level raises the trigger.
- `cancel_no_orphan_snapshots`.
- `registers_with_search_portfolio`: with this crate feature built, `nemesis.search --method splitting` runs
  and `auto` on a task with per-step streams gives splitting 15%; with `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` the
  same request returns FROB-REF-724 (exit 5) naming KILL-RARE, and `auto` falls back to 55, 30, 15, 0.
  `--method exploration` (BD-273's exploration arm) charges half the search arm to splitting and half to
  cross-entropy on one archive, with no grammar or descent proposal; on a static-only task it is refused with
  FROB-REF-569 before any episode; with the switch `off` it returns FROB-REF-724 naming KILL-RARE.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_explore.sh` (N26a-31): per stage N, hits, fraction, snapshots taken; per run the estimate,
the group spread against the independence bound, duplicates found, failures with their fork paths, modes found;
the gate metrics with thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (the random-walk level probability; control: no noise after the fork gives identical children and is
refused). KA-140 (coefficient of variation near 7% for L = 6, p₀ = 0.1, N = 10⁴). CX-292 and CX-066 (forks of a
deterministic simulator do not give independent children; a fork without fresh randomness is an identical copy).
Metamorphic relation of the chapter: a fork with no fresh randomness is refused; a child with its own address
has fresh streams. Negative controls: children keyed by the parent address (duplicates; must be refused);
levels adapted during the run (the frozen-level assertion fails); fork episodes counted as nominal (the lane
test fails). Determinism lanes: a run replays bit for bit on one backend; across worker counts identical; across
backends the estimate is re-measured.

## Considerations

- KILL-RARE. Trigger: in the exploration pilot the gain is below 10 times at 10⁻⁵, or the coverage of any
  interval offered as a bound is below 85%, or a planted mode is missed in more than 20% of repetitions.
  Fallback: crude nominal sampling with the binomial bound alone; splitting is removed and tilting is kept only
  inside `nemesis.search`. Decided at M3 (N26a-32).
- OPEN-NEMESIS-2 (a requirement whose margin is flat until shortly before failure) blocks GATE-NEMESIS-05 for
  such requirements; default: no splitting there, crude sampling.
- OPEN-NEMESIS-4 (no splitting upstream; owner: airlock maintainer): ours is the implementation, with its
  unbiasedness fixture; if the sibling later ships one, the two are compared on the random-walk fixture and one
  is chosen.
- Memory: snapshots of worlds that hit a level are tens of kilobytes each; N hits per stage on the fast tier is
  hundreds of megabytes at N = 10⁴, which fits, and snapshots of a finished stage are released.
- The ceiling of the benefit: a clean bound at 10⁻⁵ still costs about 3 × 10⁵ nominal episodes; splitting saves
  at most about a factor of three on a finalist (when it leads to an early refutation) and nothing on other
  candidates.
