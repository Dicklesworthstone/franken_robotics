## Background

Nemesis is the subsystem that looks for failures instead of waiting for them. Randomization from card uncertainty
(drawing each world's parameters from the uncertainty recorded on actuator, interface, material and sensor cards)
reports the tails it happens to sample; it never looks for a failure and never reduces one to its cause. Nemesis
does both. It searches the space of scenes, disturbances and parameters for worlds in which a candidate (a design
with its controller or policy) violates its requirement, keeps only worlds the candidate's own body could have
solved, checks that a failure is not an artifact of the simulator, shrinks it to the smallest world that still
fails, and stores that world as a regression. It also answers the other question about failures, how rare they
are, and keeps the search for failures apart from the bound on their rate.

It lives in one crate, `frob-nemesis`, at layer 5 of the workspace (above the evaluation crates of layer 4; scope
cap ten thousand lines of Rust including tests, a cap that triggers a scope conversation at 115%, not an
estimate). It is a crate of its own, and not a module of the evaluation crate, for a stated reason: the discovery
lane may not read confirmation worlds, and a crate boundary lets the dependency checker enforce that the
qualification code does not link the searcher.

This bead creates the crate and the few types every later Nemesis bead shares, and records the seven architecture
decisions so that the reasons travel with the code.

| Decision | Statement | Reason; alternative rejected |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-NEMESIS-01 | Search inside a legality set, and keep priors apart from admissibility | An unconstrained adversary wins by building worlds no robot is meant to face. Legality is a set; the nominal randomization is a distribution on part of it. A rare but admissible world can be a useful counterexample with negligible operational probability, so the search does not discard it and the report states both facts. Rejected: an unconstrained zero-sum adversary; using the prior as a filter |
| ADR-NEMESIS-02 | Only solvable worlds count | A world is a counterexample to a design only with a witness that the candidate's own body, actuators and mission constraints could have satisfied the requirement in it. This separates a useful trap from an impossible one. Rejected: counting any world that defeats the design |
| ADR-NEMESIS-03 | A failure counts after promotion | A failure found on a fast tier is replayed one tier up and under numerical variants before it enters any count. FACT (source register, to be re-read): in one study 62.5% of unsafe simulated test cases led to unsafe behaviour on a test track; simulated failures do not all transfer, and the first transfer to check is between our own tiers |
| ADR-NEMESIS-04 | Smallest, not worst | The smallest change that flips a verdict is more informative than the world with the lowest score. Every failure is shrunk before it is reported. Rejected: reporting the minimum-margin world |
| ADR-NEMESIS-05 | Exploration and certification never share data | Search-selected, tilted, forked and battery-selected episodes find failures. Only nominal draws, or draws with recorded bounded weights, enter a bound. Rejected: one sample serving both |
| ADR-NEMESIS-06 | Everything Nemesis finds is discovery data | Hard worlds and counterexamples train and test designs; they are never a confirmation sample of the blinded qualification lane |
| ADR-NEMESIS-07 | Screens are audited, not trusted | Whatever a cheap stage discards is sampled, and a known winner is planted |

Tiers of the mechanisms, with the reason for each (the tier decides whether a pilot and a kill gate apply):

| Mechanism | Tier | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Legality set, solvability bookkeeping, artifact guard, hard-world set | Solid | Bookkeeping over mechanisms that exist elsewhere |
| Adversarial search by margin | Frontier | Established in falsification practice; its gain over randomization on robots in interiors is unmeasured |
| Minimal counterexample worlds | Frontier | Delta debugging is established for programs; its yield on physical worlds is unmeasured |
| Certification by crude sampling; bounded-weight importance sampling | Solid | Exact binomial bounds and bounded likelihood ratios |
| Splitting and cross-entropy tilting as exploration | Frontier | Published gains are real and uneven |
| Screening audit; coverage selection | Solid | Stratified sampling; a greedy algorithm with a classical guarantee |

## Scope

In: the crate, its module map, `CONTRACT.md`, the machine-readable `contract.toml` and `tests/conformance.rs`

discovery record (BD-275), the refusal registry rows (registry bead N26-REF).

## Technical approach

1. **Crate and modules (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-nemesis/` with:
   `world.rs` (worlds and typed edits, N26a-02), `legality.rs` (BD-270), `prior.rs` (N26a-03),
   `provenance.rs` (this bead), `score.rs` and `search/` (BD-273, N26a-07 to N26a-10), `oracle/` (BD-271,
   N26a-13, N26a-14), `guard/` (BD-272), `shrink/` (BD-274, N26a-17, N26a-18), `mcw.rs` (N26a-19), `hardset.rs`
   (BD-275), `rare/` (N26a-21 to N26a-27, N26a-29, BD-276, BD-277), `audit.rs` and `cover.rs` (the screening audit
   and coverage selection, owned by the other half of the chapter).
2. **What the crate consumes.** Requirement margins and verdicts from Arena; scene grammars and typed scene edits
   from Habitat; snapshots and forks from Chronicle and Autopsy; promotion between tiers from Kinesis; and, only
   through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, FrankenSim's `fs-uq` (importance sampling and proposal fit), `fs-eproc`
   (confidence sequences), `fs-dfo` (derivative-free optimizers) and `fs-archive` (quality-diversity archives).
   FACT (read in the sibling tree): `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`,
   `fs-eproc/src/bernoulli.rs` and `fs-eproc/src/lib.rs`, `fs-dfo/src/lib.rs`, `fs-archive/src/lib.rs` exist.
   `frob-nemesis` names no FrankenSim type directly; it uses the airlock's feature groups `stats` and `optimize`.
3. **Shared types (DESIGN sketch; fixes intent, not signatures).**
   ```rust
   /// How an episode's world was chosen. Recorded on every episode Nemesis runs, in the ledger and in `.frun`.
   pub enum ProposalLaw {
       Nominal { randomization: RandomizationId, stream: StreamId },          // the task's own randomization
       DefensiveMixture { proposal: ProposalId, epsilon: Ratio, weight: f64 }, // weight = p/q ≤ 1/ε, recorded
       Tilted { proposal: ProposalId },                                        // cross-entropy or other tilt
       Search { method: SearchMethodId, campaign: CampaignId },                // any search method
       Fork { parent: WorldAddr, path: ForkPath, level: u8 },                  // a splitting child
       Given { source: ArtifactId },                                           // an imported or hand-written world
       BatterySelected { battery: BatteryId }, HardSet { set: HardSetId },
   }
   pub enum Lane { Certification, Exploration }
   impl ProposalLaw { pub fn lane(&self) -> Lane }   // Nominal and DefensiveMixture: Certification; all others: Exploration
   pub struct EpisodeProvenance { law: ProposalLaw, predicate: PredicateId, tier: Tier, addr: WorldAddr }
   ```
   `PredicateId` is the hash of the task identity, the requirement name and the failure predicate; a bound is tied
   to one such identity.
4. **The discovery record.** Every world Nemesis evaluates under an exploration law, every world it writes to a
   counterexample file and every member of a hard-world set is recorded in the ledger with origin `nemesis`
   (table `nemesis_worlds`: world identity, address, proposal law, campaign, ledger sequence). The record is the
   single source for INV-NEMESIS-06 (hard-world and search-selected worlds are discovery data): the qualification
   lane and the hard-world set read it to refuse such a world as a confirmation sample. Writers live here;
   readers elsewhere.
5. **Dependency rule.** `frob-nemesis` may depend on layers 0 to 4 and on `frob-fsim`. The qualification executor
   and its crate may not depend on `frob-nemesis`, directly or transitively; `xtask check-deps` gains this rule
   as a named forbidden edge. Nemesis samplers also reject any world address in the confirmation namespace, and
   every Nemesis verb refuses a confirmation run (the sealed-read refusal FROB-REF-840 of the qualification
   lane).
6. **`CONTRACT.md`** with the ten sections the project requires of every crate: purpose, inputs, outputs,
   invariants (INV-NEMESIS-01 to -06, each with the bead that tests it), refusals (the twelve codes of the
   chapter), determinism class (inherits the tier of the run; identities and counts are exact), evidence class of
   outputs (`Tested` for promoted failures and for bounds at confidence 1 − δ; `Estimated` for exploration rates;
   never `Certified`, which is reserved for certificates a checker accepted), validity domain, oracles, known
   gaps.
   Beside it, `crates/frob-nemesis/contract.toml` in the record schema `check-contracts` validates (N34-08),
   DESIGN values:
   ```toml
   [crate]
   name = "frob-nemesis"
   layer = 5                     # rank as in the crate map; cap 10 thousand lines (scope conversation at 115%)
   plane = "decision"            # reads results and writes requests (worlds to evaluate); never changes what a
                                 # world computes
   determinism = "D2"            # the strongest class claimed: identities, counts and bounds are exact (D0); runs
                                 # inherit the class of their tier, at best D2 on one backend
   evidence = "Tested"           # promoted failures and bounds at 1 − δ; exploration rates are `Estimated`
   gates = ["GATE-NEMESIS-01", "GATE-NEMESIS-02", "GATE-NEMESIS-03", "GATE-NEMESIS-04", "GATE-NEMESIS-05",
            "GATE-NEMESIS-06", "GATE-NEMESIS-07", "GATE-NEMESIS-08"]
   refusals = ["FROB-REF-360", "FROB-REF-361", "FROB-REF-362", "FROB-REF-560", "FROB-REF-563", "FROB-REF-564",
               "FROB-REF-565", "FROB-REF-566", "FROB-REF-567", "FROB-REF-568", "FROB-REF-569", "FROB-REF-760"]
   [safe_mode]

                                       # a search that exhausts its budget returns the worlds evaluated so far as a

   [features]
   "frontier-search"    = { tier = "F", kill = "KILL-NEMESIS", pilot = "PILOT-C-28" }   # N26a-06
   ```

   `PILOT-C-27` (N10b-18) is the record whose verdict `funded` lets the exploration lane start. It is the later
   of two records of one family in the pilot register: it depends on the subsystem-scale exploration pilot
   (N26a-28, M2, record `PILOT-RARE-01`), and the lane is funded only if neither falsifier holds, so naming it
   covers both.
   The three feature flags gate exactly the modules each kill gate removes (`search/` methods other than the
   baseline and `given` arms; the shrinker's minimality claim and pair check; `rare/splitting.rs`), so a fired
   kill gate is a manifest change that `check-contracts` sees (the gate records themselves exist from M0, N40-02).
   `tests/conformance.rs` starts
   with the contract's own checks (the six invariants listed, each naming its testing bead) and is extended by
   every mechanism bead.
7. **The known-answer rule of the chapter.** A value that follows from a stated formula is a derived value and is

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/provenance.rs`:
- `lane_of_each_law`: eight constructed laws; expected lanes C, C, E, E, E, E, E, E in the order of the sketch.
- `mixture_weight_bound_recorded`: constructing `DefensiveMixture` with weight 10.5 at ε = 1/10 is rejected
  (weight above 1/ε = 10); weight 10.0 is accepted.
- `predicate_id_changes_with_atom`: changing one comparator of the requirement changes `PredicateId`.
- `discovery_record_roundtrip`: write three worlds (search, fork, given), read back by origin.
- `confirmation_namespace_rejected`: a sampler handed an address in the confirmation namespace returns the
  sealed-read refusal and evaluates nothing.
`xtask` test `nemesis_forbidden_edge`: the planted dependency edge is reported with both crate names.
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/conformance.rs`: `contract_lists_invariants` (six invariants, each naming a testing
bead); `features_match_manifest` (the three `frontier-*` features of the manifest are the three rows of
`contract.toml`, each with a registered kill gate).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_crate_boundary.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-free and idempotent; log
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_crate_boundary/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript):
1. `cargo xtask check-layers`, `cargo xtask check-deps`, `cargo xtask check-contracts`: assert exit 0 each.
2. In a scratch copy of the manifests, plant the edge qualification crate → `frob-nemesis`: assert
   `check-deps` exits non-zero and its report names both crates and the rule `nemesis-forbidden-edge`.
3. In a scratch copy, delete the `kill` field of `frontier-search` in `contract.toml`: assert `check-contracts`
   exits non-zero with `contract.features-mismatch`.
4. `frob --json doctor`: assert exit 0 and that the crate's contract is reported present.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-01`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and
wall time; `assert` lines carry the expected and observed exit code and, for the planted defects, the rule
identifier reported; on failure the scratch-tree path is logged so the defect can be reproduced. A final
`summary` gives assertions passed and failed. No scene data is touched.
The scripts of the chapter (`scripts/e2e/nemesis_*.sh`) also log, for every episode they cause, the
`proposal_law` and `lane` fields this bead defines, inside the envelope's evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative controls: a `ProposalLaw` match with a wildcard arm mapping to `Certification` (the exhaustive-match test
must fail to compile or fail the lint); a discovery writer that skips fork children (the round-trip test catches
it). CX-289 (any world that defeats the design is a counterexample) and CX-290 (a good sampler discounts the price
of proving absence) are the two shortcuts these types exist to block; they are exercised in BD-271 and N26a-23.

## Considerations

- The lane is a property of how a world was chosen, not of the verb that ran it; the type makes a reused episode
  carry its origin wherever it goes.
- The crate is a proof boundary: a change that adds a dependency from qualification code to this crate is a
  design change needing a decision record, not a refactor.
- Kill gates KILL-NEMESIS, KILL-RARE and KILL-MCW each remove modules (`search/` methods; `rare/splitting.rs`;
  the shrinker's claim), never the types here.
- RISK: the discovery record grows with every search; retention follows the ledger's retention rules, and
  hard-set members are pinned.
