## Background

ADR-NEMESIS-01 keeps two facts about a world apart. **Admissibility**: is the world inside the legality profile?
That decides whether the world may be used at all. **Prior**: how often would this world arise under the task's
nominal randomization (the distribution a task draws its worlds from: card uncertainties for parameters, the
scene generator's draws for places)? That says how often and decides nothing. A world that is legal and has a
density one ten-thousandth of a typical draw is a valid counterexample and a rare one; a reader needs both
statements to judge it, and a search that filtered on the prior would throw away the worlds it exists to find.

The same subsection fixes what "small" means for a world, because "smallest, not worst" (ADR-NEMESIS-04) needs an
order: the size of a world relative to its base is lexicographic, first the number of atomic edits, then the
normalized physical distance.

Both are small computations, kept in one bead because every world record carries both and the shrinker and the
search read them together.

## Scope

In: the nominal density of a world relative to its base draw; the "where one is defined" rule; the size order;
the fields on world records and counterexample files.
Out: the legality verdict (BD-270); proposal densities and importance weights of samplers (N26a-08, N26a-24,
BD-276); the search and the shrinker that use the order (BD-273, N26a-18).

## Technical approach

1. **The nominal law, factor by factor (DESIGN).** The task's nominal randomization is a product of factors, each
   attached to one quantity: a card parameter with a density on its uncertainty support; a discrete scene feature
   with a probability mass from the generator; a declared disturbance with the task's law for it, if the task
   randomizes it at all. `prior::NominalLaw::for_task(task, scene_family)` collects the factors with their
   identities.
2. **The density ratio.** For a world w derived from a base world b (itself a nominal draw), over the set T of
   quantities any edit touches:
   log₁₀ r(w) = Σ over q in T of log₁₀[ f_q(value of q in w) / f_q(value of q in b) ],
   where f_q is the nominal density of q (continuous) or its probability mass (discrete). r says how much less
   likely the edited values are than the values the base drew: `log10_density_ratio: -3.9` means about 8,000
   times less likely. Quantities the edits do not touch cancel and are not evaluated. The ratio is computed in
   log space in double precision and stored to three significant figures; it is descriptive and never enters a
   decision.
3. **Where one is defined.** If some touched quantity has no nominal factor (a push the task never randomizes; a
   geometry edit to a hand-authored scene with no generator), the prior is undefined for that quantity. The
   record then says so and gives the partial ratio over the quantities that have a factor:
   `"prior": {"defined": false, "undefined_for": ["/disturbances/push[0]"], "log10_density_ratio_partial": -1.2}`.
   A value outside the support of its factor but inside the legality profile has density zero: the record says
   `"outside_nominal_support": ["/floor/patch[galley-2]/mu"]` and no ratio is printed for it. This is the case
   ADR-NEMESIS-01 names: admissible, and of negligible operational probability.
4. **Size relative to the base.** `size(w) = (n, D)` compared lexicographically:
   - n = the number of atomic edits (after canonical merging, N26a-02);
   - D = Σ over edits of the normalized magnitude: for an interval-bounded quantity |to − from| divided by the
     width of its legal range (max − min of its bound in the profile); for a discrete quantity, an added or a
     removed object, 1.
   n is an integer; D is computed in exact rational arithmetic when the authored values are rational and compared
   exactly, so ties are real ties. The order is used by the shrinker to choose among candidate reductions, by the
   pair check to choose the smaller of two counterexamples, and by the search to break ties among failures in one
   archive cell.
5. **What the order does not mean.** It is not a distance to a failure boundary. Failure sets are not monotone
   (an edit can mask another), so no world is called the closest failure; the size order only ranks descriptions.
6. **Record fields.** Every world record: `legality` (from BD-270), `prior` (this bead), `size: {"edits": n,
   "normalized_distance": "D"}`. The counterexample file carries `prior` in the same form.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For a world whose single edit moves a normal card parameter from its mean to 3 standard deviations,
      `log10_density_ratio` = −(9/2)·log₁₀ e = −1.954 (to three decimals).
- [ ] For a world whose single edit turns on a film that the generator draws with probability 1/100 on a patch
      that was dry (probability 99/100), the ratio is log₁₀(1/99) = −1.996.
- [ ] A world with a touched quantity that has no nominal factor reports `defined: false`, names the quantity and
      gives the partial ratio; nothing is refused.
- [ ] A legal value outside the nominal support is reported as `outside_nominal_support`, and the world is still
      admitted for search.
- [ ] `size` orders (1 edit, D = 0.9) below (2 edits, D = 0.1); among equal n it orders by D; equal (n, D) is a
      tie (property test: the order is total on (n, D) and consistent with equality).
- [ ] No code path reads `prior` to accept, reject, rank for promotion or discard a world (lint: the field has
      no reader outside reporting and the proposal-weight modules).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/prior.rs`:
- `gaussian_ratio`: z from 0 to 3: −1.954; z from 0 to 4: −(16/2)·log₁₀ e = −3.474.
- `uniform_ratio_is_zero`: a uniform card parameter moved inside its support: 0.
- `discrete_mass_ratio`: 1/100 against 99/100: −1.996.
- `product_of_factors`: the two edits above together: −3.950 (the sum).
- `undefined_factor_partial`; `outside_support_reported`.
- `size_lexicographic`: the four cases of the acceptance list; `normalized_magnitude`: closer torque 14 → 19 N·m
  on a legal range [5, 20] N·m has magnitude 5/15 = 1/3 exactly; a film `dry → grease` has magnitude 1.
- `size_exact_ties`: 1/3 + 2/3 equals 1 exactly (rational arithmetic), so two worlds tie.

### End-to-end script and logging
Logged by `scripts/e2e/nemesis_search.sh` (N26a-11): for each reported failure the `legality.inside` flag, the
`prior` block and `size`, so a reader of the log sees admissibility and probability as separate values.

### Known answers and negative controls
Derived values above are hand-checkable (−(z²/2)·log₁₀ e with log₁₀ e = 0.43429). Negative control: a search
arm with a prior filter switched on (worlds below a density threshold dropped) must fail the test that a planted
admissible trap of ratio −4 is still reported. Metamorphic: rescaling a quantity's units changes neither the
ratio nor D.

## Considerations

- DESIGN choice, stated because the plan gives only the field: the ratio is taken against the base world's own
  draw, since the base is the nominal sample the counterexample was derived from. If the owner prefers the ratio
  against the mode of the nominal law, only step 2 changes; the field name stays.
- The ratio compares densities, which depend on parametrization for continuous quantities; it is computed in the
  authored quantity (the one the card states its uncertainty in) and reports say so.
- Interaction: the importance weights of the certification lane are exact likelihood ratios of whole proposals
  and are computed elsewhere; the descriptive ratio here must never be used as a weight.
