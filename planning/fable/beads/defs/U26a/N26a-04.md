## Background

Every Nemesis gate is a planted-truth gate: 100 of 100 planted illegal edits refused; 0 of 100 planted impossible
traps counted; at least 95 of 100 planted solvable traps given a witness; a planted trap of nominal probability
at most 3 × 10⁻⁵ found faster than by randomization; planted failures of probability 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one with two
separate modes, found by exploration. A gate that nothing can fail validates nothing, and a gate whose plants do
not exist cannot run. This bead builds the plants, with their truths fixed and frozen before any mechanism is
tuned against them, and registers them in the planted-defect registry of `frob-conformance`
(`crates/frob-conformance/registry/planted/nemesis/`).

Terms. A **trap** is a set of worlds inside the legality profile in which a named candidate (a reference robot
with its baseline controller at nominal gain) violates its requirement by a named mechanism. A trap is
**solvable** when some controller on the same body satisfies the requirement in it, **impossible** when a
necessary condition shows that no controller on that body can. A trap's **nominal probability** is the
probability that the task's nominal randomization draws a world in it.

## Scope

In: three registries of 100 plants each, their generators, their truth records, the calibration protocol that
fixes each solvable trap's probability, the two-mode fixture, the four potency traps of the method experiments,
the freeze.
Out: the mechanisms that must detect them (BD-270, BD-271, BD-273, BD-277); planted simulator artifacts and
planted genuine failures for the artifact guard (N26a-15); planted causes for the shrinker (N26a-34, built on the
solvable traps of this registry); the registry wiring into the M3 conformance run (BD-318).

## Technical approach

1. **Registry record (DESIGN).** One TOML record per plant: `id`, `kind` (`illegal-edit` | `impossible-trap` |
   `solvable-trap`), `archetype`, `base` (scene family, generator seed), `candidate`, `task`, `profile`, the
   plant itself (an edit list, or a trigger set), `truth` (the expected refusal and bound; or the expected oracle
   class and bottleneck; or the trap's probability with its interval, mechanism, failure class, binding atom and
   witness controller), `frozen_at` (commit and date). The registry directory has a digest; any change is a new
   version and is visible in the gate receipt.
2. **Illegal edits (100).** Generated across all seven edit classes and four violation kinds, 25 each: an
   interval bound exceeded above; an interval bound exceeded below or a value outside an allowed set; a composite
   bound violated by two or three edits that are each legal alone (two obstacles narrowing a passage; a rug on a
   threshold exceeding the combined height bound); an edit to a path the profile does not list. Each record names
   the bound that must appear in the refusal. Each has a **legal twin** (the same edit pulled just inside the
   bound) that must be admitted, so a checker that refuses everything fails.
3. **Impossible traps (100).** Each defeats every controller on the candidate body by a checked necessary
   condition, and each is legal (inside the profile), since an illegal world is refused before the oracle sees
   it. Three families:
   - *Clearance (50).* A doorway, aisle or checkout lane narrower than the body at every heading: body width
     0.75 m against a door of 0.70 m clear (the legal range for an interior door is 0.70 to 0.90 m). Expected
     class `infeasible-for-candidate`, bottleneck: the named opening. The same world with a point robot has a
     route; that is the negative control for the oracle.
   - *Energy (25).* A mission whose mandatory potential rise and unavoidable rolling loss exceed the battery
     card's energy at full charge. Expected: the energy-budget condition fails with its bound.
   - *Static load (25).* A payload that must be held at rest on a slope where the required support wrench lies
     outside the candidate's contact-wrench cone for every admissible stance. Expected: a separating dual vector.
   No impossible trap relies on a quasi-static screen where dynamics could succeed (a wheel meeting a lip is
   excluded: an approach speed can carry it over).
4. **Solvable traps (100), with known nominal probability.** DESIGN. Each trap has a **trigger box** R in the
   space of normal germs of two independent randomized quantities (germs are standard-normal coordinates mapped
   monotonically to the card or generator distribution, N26a-24), or in two independent discrete generator
   draws. The box's nominal probability is analytic. Three probability classes:

   | Class | Box | Probability p_T | Read by, besides GATE-NEMESIS-01's witness row (N26a-33, all 100) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | T3 | z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 2.7478 | 0.01 × 0.003 = 3 × 10⁻⁵ | GATE-NEMESIS-02 (N26a-11: its ten traps) |
   | T4 | z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 2.3263 | 0.01 × 0.01 = 10⁻⁴ | GATE-NEMESIS-05 (N26a-31) |
   | T5 | z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 3.0902 | 0.01 × 0.001 = 10⁻⁵ | none: the witness row only |
   | T5-two-mode | mode A: z₁ > 4.4172; mode B: z₂ > 4.4172 | 5 × 10⁻⁶ each; union 9.999975 × 10⁻⁶ | GATE-NEMESIS-05 (N26a-31; both modes must be found) |

   T5 is the witness row's single-mode case at 10⁻⁵, the exploration gate's probability: GATE-NEMESIS-05
   measures 10⁻⁵ on T5-two-mode (N26a-31 reads T4 and T5-two-mode), and no exploration gate reads T5. The two
   M2 pilots (N26a-06 for the search, N26a-28 for exploration) precede this registry and build their own
   fixtures; neither reads it.

   **A box is recorded by its tail probabilities (DESIGN).** The record holds the exact rationals (q₁, q₂):
   (1/100, 3/1000) for T3, (1/100, 1/100) for T4, (1/100, 1/1000) for T5, 1/200000 for each mode of the
   two-mode fixture and (1/10, 1/10) for the potency class T2 below. Each threshold is z = Φ̄⁻¹(q), computed
   from q in double precision (Φ̄ is the upper tail of the standard normal): 2.3263479, 2.7477814, 3.0902323,
   4.4171734, 1.2815516. The four-decimal thresholds printed in this bead and in the beads that cite it are
   those values rounded for reading. Reason: the box probability is then q₁·q₂ exactly, "at most 3 × 10⁻⁵"
   holds literally, and the union 2q − q² = 9.999975 × 10⁻⁶ is exact. With the four-decimal values taken as the
   thresholds the probabilities would be 3.0002 × 10⁻⁵, 1.0003 × 10⁻⁴, 1.0002 × 10⁻⁵ and 4.9994 × 10⁻⁶ per
   mode: the first is above the gate's 3 × 10⁻⁵, and the last is not 5.000 × 10⁻⁶ to four figures.

   **Class split and placement (DESIGN; reason: each gate needs its own fixture inside the 100).** 60 T3, 20 T4,
   10 T5 and 10 T5-two-mode, spread over all seven archetypes so that every edit class appears. Of the T3 traps,
   ten (five in each of two generated interiors, the commercial kitchen and the warehouse of the M3 grammar) are
   the fixture of GATE-NEMESIS-02 and of its pilot's product repeat. The T4, T5 and T5-two-mode traps (T4 and
   T5-two-mode are GATE-NEMESIS-05's fixture) sit on the reference quadruped's task with per-step push
   disturbances, and in each of them one
   germ of the box is the germ of a per-step quantity: the standardized sum of the push impulses drawn on the
   per-step disturbance stream over a stated window that starts after the earliest fork tick (standard normal
   under the nominal law, so the box probability stays analytic). A fork taken before the window therefore
   carries fresh randomness that decides the trap, which is what splitting needs; the static germ is what
   tilting and the defensive mixture act on. In the two-mode fixture mode A's germ is static and mode B's is the
   per-step sum, so a method that only tilts static parameters and a method that only splits are each able to
   miss one mode.

   The physical content: z₁ and z₂ drive two quantities whose joint extreme produces a real mechanism with a
   wide margin, for example floor-patch friction (low) with a door-closer torque (high) so that the robot slips
   while pushing; a grease film with a payload fill level so that the tray tips; sensor latency with a push so
   that recovery is late. In the two-mode fixture mode A is a slip (binding atom `upright`) and mode B a torque
   saturation (binding atom `delivered`), so the modes land in different cells of the failure catalogue.

   **Potency traps (DESIGN; outside the counted 100).** Every method experiment that reads this registry needs a
   potency control: a planted failure common enough that every arm must find it, so that an arm that finds
   nothing shows a broken arm and voids the run rather than passing as "no gain". Class T2: box z₁ > 1.2816 and
   z₂ > 1.2816, probability 0.1 × 0.1 = 10⁻² (tails 1/10 each). Four records, kind `solvable-trap` with
   `role = "potency"`: one in each of the two GATE-NEMESIS-02 interiors (commercial kitchen and warehouse; read by
   N26a-11 and N26a-12's `--first` check) and two on the GATE-NEMESIS-05 quadruped task, one with its
   second germ per-step and one with both static (read by N26a-31). They are calibrated like every solvable trap,
   are frozen with the registry, and are excluded from every count of the 100 and from every gate's measured
   rows.
5. **Calibration, which is what makes the probability known.** For each solvable trap, on the CPU referee:
   1. Draw 400 worlds from the nominal law conditioned on R (exact conditional sampling in germ space; for a
      per-step germ the window's sum is drawn from its truncated normal and the individual impulses from the
      Gaussian conditional on that sum, then replayed as recorded per-step values). The trap
      is accepted only if the candidate fails with the trap's failure class and binding atom in at least 380.
      The trap's probability is then recorded as the interval [L·p_T, p_T], where L is the one-sided exact
      binomial 95% lower bound on the share of in-box worlds that fail (computed by the registry tool from the
      count; it is above 0.9 whenever the count is at least 380).
   2. Draw 400 worlds in the shell just outside R (each germ within 0.5 of its threshold on the passing side).
      The trap's mechanism must appear in none; a trap that leaks is re-centred or dropped.
   3. Run the witness controller (the baseline controller at speed scale 0.5, or another member of the same
      family named in the record) on 64 worlds inside R: it must satisfy the requirement in at least 61.
   4. Record all three counts in `truth`. "Found" is then defined for every gate: a promoted failure whose world
      lies in R and whose failure class and binding atom are the trap's.
   Failures of the candidate outside R are not the planted trap; they are reported and not counted for or against
   a gate.
6. **Freeze.** The registry is frozen and hashed before BD-273 and BD-277 run any measured arm; a later change
   is a new version and voids recorded gate values that used the old one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Three registries of exactly 100 records each validate against the record schema; the directory digest is
      in `registries/` and in the gate receipts.
- [ ] Every illegal edit names its bound and has a legal twin; every violation kind and every edit class is
      represented.
- [ ] Every impossible trap is inside its legality profile, and its record carries the necessary condition that
      fails with the bottleneck or dual vector; the point-robot variant of each clearance trap has a route.
- [ ] Every solvable trap passed calibration: at least 380 of 400 inside, 0 of 400 in the shell, witness at
      least 61 of 64; the counts are in the record.
- [ ] Every solvable record holds its tails as exact rationals and thresholds equal to Φ̄⁻¹ of them; a test
      reproduces the box probabilities from the registry's thresholds to 10⁻⁹ relative: 3 × 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵,
      5 × 10⁻⁶ per mode with union 9.999975 × 10⁻⁶, and 10⁻² for the T2 potency box; no T3 box is above
      3 × 10⁻⁵.
- [ ] Four calibrated potency traps (`role = "potency"`) exist, two per gate fixture as listed, and no gate
      count or measured row includes them.
- [ ] The freeze commit predates the first measured run of any gate that reads the registry (checked from the
      ledger).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/nemesis_planted.rs`:
- `registry_counts_and_schema`: 100, 100, 100; kinds and classes covered; the solvable split is 60, 20, 10, 10;
  the ten GATE-NEMESIS-02 traps are five per interior; every GATE-NEMESIS-05 fixture has one per-step germ;
  four potency records, excluded from the solvable count.
- `per_step_germ_standard_normal` (`synthetic`): the standardized window sum of 10⁵ nominal push sequences passes
  a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test against N(0, 1) at level 0.001; its conditional sampler stays in the box.
- `box_probabilities`: at the registry's thresholds (2.3263479, 2.7477814, 3.0902323, 4.4171734, 1.2815516)
  Φ̄ returns the recorded tails 1/100, 3/1000, 1/1000, 1/200000 and 1/10 to 10⁻⁹ relative, so the boxes have
  probability 3 × 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵, 5 × 10⁻⁶ per mode (union 2q − q² = 9.999975 × 10⁻⁶) and 10⁻².
  `rounded_thresholds_are_not_the_box`: with the four-decimal values 2.3263, 2.7478 and 4.4172 taken as
  thresholds the same products are 3.0002 × 10⁻⁵ and 4.9994 × 10⁻⁶, which the test reports as different from
  the recorded tails (a registry that stored the rounded thresholds would fail `box_probabilities`).
- `conditional_sampler_in_box`: 10,000 conditional draws all lie in R; the marginal of each germ matches the
  truncated normal (Kolmogorov–Smirnov at level 0.001, a `synthetic` fixture).
- `legal_twin_admitted`, `illegal_refused` (through the real legality checker, all 100 pairs).
- `impossible_traps_are_legal`: `check_world` returns `inside: true` for all 100.
- `calibration_counts_recorded`: every solvable record has the three counts and they meet the thresholds.

### End-to-end script and logging
The registry is consumed by `scripts/e2e/nemesis_legality.sh` (N26a-05), `nemesis_search.sh` (N26a-11),
`nemesis_explore.sh` (N26a-31) and `nemesis_oracle.sh` (N26a-33). This bead's own script,
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_planted_calibrate.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; seed-explicit; idempotent), reruns the
calibration of ten traps sampled by a keyed draw (at least one of each class, including one per-step fixture)
through `frob sim run … --tier referee --key <trap>-<stage> --json` on the CPU referee (a run verb: each of the
three calibration stages of each trap carries its own idempotency key, so a rerun returns the stored receipts
and two stages are never the same request), and checks the registry digest against `registries/`. Log
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_planted_calibrate/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N26a-04`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence`
block; per trap one `metric` line each for the in-box failure count (threshold ≥ 380 of 400), the shell count
(threshold 0 of 400) and the witness count (threshold ≥ 61 of 64), with the trap identifier, class, registry
digest, seeds and tier; on failure the first trap whose count misses, with its record path and the seeds that
reproduce it. A final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed. Planted control: the script run against a
copy of the registry in which one trap's box is moved into the nominal bulk (a dud trap) must fail at step 1 of
the calibration.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-659 (planted illegal edit refused, bound named), KA-660 (a doorway narrower than the body is
`infeasible-for-candidate`; a point-robot oracle calls it solvable), KA-661 (a planted solvable trap of known
nominal probability is found with a causal witness). Negative controls built into the registry: legal twins; the
point-robot variants; the shell outside each box. A registry whose traps all pass the nominal controller (a dud
registry) is caught by calibration step 1.

## Considerations

- The probability of a calibrated trap is an interval with p_T as its upper end; gates that say "at most
  3 × 10⁻⁵" read the upper end, and gates about rates read the interval.
- Calibration costs about 100 × 864 referee episodes; it runs once per registry version and is recorded.
- RISK: traps written by the people who write the search can be shaped to it. The registry is generated from
  seeds by a script, frozen first, and reviewed by a session that did not implement the search.
- The corpus is discovery data: it is never a confirmation sample and never the nominal draw of a bound.
