## Background

Two claims of the adversarial-worlds subsystem are Frontier: that a search guided by the requirement margin finds
failures faster than randomization on robots in interiors, and that a failing world shrinks to a few edits.
Falsification by margin and delta debugging are both established elsewhere; their yield here is unmeasured. The
program's rule is that every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot: a small experiment with existing tools,
run before the mechanism's work package is funded, with a falsifier written down first and a ceiling computed
first. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships.

This is the pilot for search and shrinking (bet BET-22: adversarial search inside legality bounds finds failures
faster than randomization, and each failure shrinks to a world of at most three edits that reproduces bit for bit
and that the design's own body could have coped with). The seed beads for the search (BD-273) depend on it.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** HYPOTHESIS: for failures of nominal probability near 3 × 10⁻⁵ the
search reaches a first failure with at least 3 times fewer simulated steps than card-uniform randomization. A
planning recomputation that charges the solvability oracle and the shrinking puts the ratio near 6. Published
measurements of adaptive stress testing range from no gain over naive sampling on a policy-in-the-loop task to 2
to 20 times in a driving study (FACT, source register, to be re-read). The ceiling of this lever on the cost of
one found failure is therefore under ten, and its effect on the time to a design decision is bounded by the share
of that time spent finding failures, which the phase ledger measures. For shrinking the ceiling is not a speed at
all: a better artifact per failure, measured in agent repair iterations.

## Scope

In: the protocol, frozen before execution; ten planted traps in two interiors; two arms; a hand-rolled search and
a hand-rolled shrinker of pilot quality; the record in the pilot register; the recommendation to fund or to ship
the fallback.
Out: the product search, the legality profile and the shrinker (BD-273, BD-270, BD-274); the planted registry of
the gates (N26a-04); the kill decisions themselves (N26a-12 for the search; N26a-35 for the shrinker); the
gate rows of the chapter and the test that this pilot's row is complete (N26b-18).

## Technical approach

1. **Setting.** Two interiors written by a throwaway generator script (not the M3 grammar): a corridor with a
   door, a threshold and a low-friction patch; a galley with a swing door, a mat and a film patch. One reference
   robot with its baseline controller, on the CPU referee in double precision (or the pinned development oracle).
   Parameters randomized from stated distributions that stand in for cards.
2. **Ten planted traps**, five per interior, each a trigger box in two independent standard-normal germs with
   analytic probability at most 3 × 10⁻⁵ (for example z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 2.7478, probability 0.01 × 0.003),
   each with a physical mechanism (slip while pushing a heavy door; tip at a threshold under a late push) and a
   known witness (the same controller at half speed). One additional **unsolvable** world (a doorway narrower
   than the body) is planted as the control that must be rejected. Traps are checked by conditional sampling
   inside the box before the arms run.
3. **Arms, at equal budget in simulated steps.**
   - *Randomization:* worlds drawn from the nominal law; the count of steps to the first failure inside a trap
     box. Its expectation is known in advance (about 1/p draws: 33,333 at 3 × 10⁻⁵), so runs are capped at
     10⁵ draws and a capped run is recorded as censored.
   - *Search:* cross-entropy on the germs (elite fraction 0.1, mean update at unit covariance, truncated at the
     bounds), scored by the minimum requirement margin over the episode, followed by a privileged-state witness
     check and one replay at half step for each candidate failure. Steps spent on the witness check and the
     replay are charged to this arm.
   Ten repetitions per trap with paired seeds (100 pairs).
4. **Shrinking arm.** Each failure the search returns is padded with 6 to 10 inert legal edits and shrunk by
   delta debugging on 16 paired seeds (a subset fails when at least 12 of 16 fail by the same mechanism);
   recorded: final size, whether a single edit can still be removed (it must not), whether the result replays
   with identical state digests.
5. **Falsifiers (TARGET; any one fires).**
   - Search: the paired lower 95% confidence bound on the ratio (randomization steps ÷ search steps to the first
     promoted failure) is below 3. This is the falsifier of GATE-NEMESIS-02 at pilot scale.
   - Witness: fewer than 70% of returned worlds carry a solvability witness, or the unsolvable control is
     reported as a counterexample.
   - Shrinking: one returned world from which a single edit can still be removed; or fewer than half of shrunk
     worlds have at most three edits; or fewer than 90% replay.
6. **Record.** One entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism and tier (adversarial search and
   minimal counterexample worlds, Frontier); protocol hash, frozen before execution; the falsifiers; the ceiling
   above; fallbacks and kill gates (KILL-NEMESIS: the search methods are removed and Nemesis keeps the legality
   profile, the oracle, the guard and shrinking, applied to failures that card randomization finds; KILL-MCW:
   full failing worlds with replay and single-edit contrasts ranked by margin, the format stays); cost (a few
   CPU core-hours); tool and pin; then the result with all measured values.
7. **Statistics.** The ratio is reported as a lower confidence bound on the paired ratio, never as a ratio of
   means; ten pairs of constant ratio r must return the bound r (the harness self-test). Censored randomization
   runs enter at their cap, which makes the bound conservative.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol file's hash is recorded in the pilot register before the first measured run (ledger order).
- [ ] The ceiling (under ten on the cost of one found failure) is in the record before any timing exists.
- [ ] All ten traps passed their conditional-sampling check; the unsolvable control exists.
- [ ] The record holds, for each arm and trap, steps to first failure per repetition, the censoring flags, the
      paired lower bound, the share of returned worlds with a witness, and for shrinking the sizes, the
      single-removal check and the replay check.
- [ ] The record states which falsifiers fired and the recommendation: fund the search work package and the
      shrinker, or ship the named fallback for each.
- [ ] A reviewer who did not run the pilot can rerun it from the record (seeds, tool pin, commands).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot's own harness has three self-tests, run before the measured arms:
- `constant_ratio_bound`: ten synthetic pairs with ratio exactly 4 return a lower bound of 4.
- `aa_arm`: randomization against itself returns a bound below 1.2 (an A/A arm that clears 3 means a broken
  harness).
- `planted_effect_detected`: a synthetic arm that is exactly 5 times faster is reported above 3.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/nemesis_search_shrink.sh` runs the arms and writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/pilot-logs/nemesis_search_shrink/<utc>.ndjson`: per run the arm, trap, repetition, seed, steps, censoring
flag, failure class and binding atom, witness outcome; per shrink the edit counts before and after, tests run,
seeds failing, digest equality; a final summary with each falsifier's measured value against its threshold.

### Known answers and negative controls
The trap probabilities are analytic (0.01 × 0.003 = 3 × 10⁻⁵). The unsolvable doorway is the control for the
witness rule (CX-289: a doorway narrower than the body defeats every controller). The order-dependence example of
delta debugging (CX-288: failure when {a, b} or {c, d, e} is present; listed (a, c, d, e, b), delta debugging
ends at {c, d, e}) is run as a check that the pilot's shrinker claims 1-minimality only.

## Considerations

- A pilot on stand-in interiors and stand-in cards can pass where the product fails, and the reverse; the gate at
  M3 (GATE-NEMESIS-02) decides the product. The pilot only decides funding.
- The construction card for rare failures states a pilot falsifier of its own for a planted trap ("not found at
  least three times sooner than by uniform sampling"); it coincides with the search falsifier here and is
  recorded as the same measurement.
- If the search falsifier fires and the shrinking falsifiers do not, the shrinker is funded alone and runs on
  failures that randomization finds: exactly the KILL-NEMESIS fallback.
- Nothing in this pilot is product code; it lives under `tools/pilots/` and is not linked by the workspace.
