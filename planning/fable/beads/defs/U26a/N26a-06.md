## Background

Two claims of the adversarial-worlds subsystem are Frontier: that a search guided by the requirement margin finds
failures faster than randomization on robots in interiors, and that a failing world shrinks to a few edits.
Falsification by margin and delta debugging are both established elsewhere; their yield here is unmeasured. The
program's rule is that every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot: a small experiment with existing tools,
run before the mechanism's work package is funded, with a falsifier written down first and a ceiling computed
first. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships.

This is the pilot for search and shrinking (bet BET-22: adversarial search inside legality bounds finds failures
faster than randomization, and each failure shrinks to a world of at most three edits that reproduces bit for bit
and that the design's own body could have coped with). The seed beads for the search (BD-273) depend on it.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** HYPOTHESIS: for failures of nominal probability near 3 × 10⁻⁵ the
search reaches a first failure with at least 3 times fewer simulated steps than card-uniform randomization. A
planning recomputation that charges the solvability oracle and the shrinking puts the ratio near 6. Published
measurements of adaptive stress testing range from no gain over naive sampling on a policy-in-the-loop task to 2
to 20 times in a driving study (FACT, source register, to be re-read). The ceiling of this lever on the cost of
one found failure is therefore under ten, and its effect on the time to a design decision is bounded by the share
of that time spent finding failures, which the phase ledger measures. For shrinking the ceiling is not a speed at
all: a better artifact per failure, measured in agent repair iterations.

One pilot, two statements. The plan states this pilot twice: with the chapter (ten planted traps in two
generated interiors on the CPU referee, the search against card randomization) and with construction C-28
(planted traps in three scenes; falsifier: fewer than 70% of returned worlds carry a solvability witness, or one

in agent repair iterations and not in steps; fallback: the failing world unshrunk, with its edit list). It is
run once, here, from one frozen protocol, and recorded as two records of the pilot register, one per mechanism:
`PILOT-NEMESIS-01` for the adversarial search and `PILOT-C-28` for minimal counterexample worlds (the record
that row C-28's `pilot` field names). DESIGN, the two records; reason: a record of N38a-09 carries one
`fallback.kill_gate`, and the search (KILL-NEMESIS) and the shrinker (KILL-MCW) have different kill gates, and
the register's funding rule answers per mechanism, so that a held search falsifier does not block the shrinker
(the case named in the Considerations). DESIGN, the scene count: the three scenes of the
construction's statement are the two interiors and the narrow-doorway scene that carries the planted unsolvable
trap; the ten solvable traps stay in the two interiors, as the chapter's statement has them. Both of the
construction's falsifiers are among the falsifiers of step 5.

## Technical approach

1. **Setting.** Two interiors written by a throwaway generator script (not the M3 grammar): a corridor with a
   door, a threshold and a low-friction patch; a galley with a swing door, a mat and a film patch. One reference
   robot with its baseline controller, on the CPU referee in double precision (or the pinned development oracle).
   Parameters randomized from stated distributions that stand in for cards.
2. **Ten planted traps**, five per interior, each a trigger box in two independent standard-normal germs with
   analytic probability at most 3 × 10⁻⁵ (for example z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 2.7478, probability 0.01 × 0.003),
   each with a physical mechanism (slip while pushing a heavy door; tip at a threshold under a late push) and a
   known witness (the same controller at half speed). One additional **unsolvable** world (a doorway narrower
   than the body) is planted as the control that must be rejected. Traps are checked by conditional sampling
   inside the box before the arms run.
3. **Arms, at equal budget in simulated steps.**
   - *Randomization:* worlds drawn from the nominal law; the count of steps to the first failure inside a trap
     box. Its expectation is known in advance (about 1/p draws: 33,333 at 3 × 10⁻⁵), so runs are capped at
     10⁵ draws and a capped run is recorded as censored.
   - *Search:* cross-entropy on the germs (elite fraction 0.1, mean update at unit covariance, truncated at the
     bounds), scored by the minimum requirement margin over the episode, followed by a privileged-state witness
     check and one replay at half step for each candidate failure. Steps spent on the witness check and the
     replay are charged to this arm.
   Ten repetitions per trap with paired seeds (100 pairs).
4. **Shrinking arm.** Each failure the search returns is padded with 6 to 10 inert legal edits and shrunk by
   delta debugging on 16 paired seeds (a subset fails when at least 12 of 16 fail by the same mechanism);
   recorded: final size, whether a single edit can still be removed (it must not), whether the result replays
   with identical state digests.
5. **Falsifiers (TARGET; any one fires).**
   - Search: the paired lower 95% confidence bound on the ratio (randomization steps ÷ search steps to the first
     promoted failure) is below 3. This is the falsifier of GATE-NEMESIS-02 at pilot scale.
   - Witness: fewer than 70% of returned worlds carry a solvability witness, or the unsolvable control is
     reported as a counterexample.
   - Shrinking: one returned world from which a single edit can still be removed; or fewer than half of shrunk
     worlds have at most three edits; or fewer than 90% replay.
6. **Records.** Two entries in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`,
   N38a-09), both with `tier = "F"`, `bead = "N26a-06"`, `work_package = "WP-55"`, the same `protocol` (one file,
   one hash, frozen before execution, with its ledger sequence), cost (a few CPU core-hours, shared) and tool and
   pin; each then gets its own `[[pilot.result]]` from the one run, with all measured values:
   - `PILOT-NEMESIS-01`, mechanism adversarial search by margin. `gated_beads = ["N26a-08", "N26a-09",
     "N26a-11"]` (the method beads and the gate's measuring bead, which N26a-12's unfunded branch withdraws; the
     driver BD-273 and the baseline arms of N26a-07 are not gated). Falsifier: the paired lower 95% bound on the
     step ratio is below 3, or fewer than 70% of returned worlds carry a solvability witness (GATE-NEMESIS-02's
     two clauses at pilot scale, the register's row in N26b-18). Ceiling: under ten on the cost of one found
     failure, with the basis above. Fallback: `ships` card randomization with the legality profile, the oracle,
     the guard and replayable failing worlds with ranked single-edit contrasts; `kill_gate = "KILL-NEMESIS"`;
     `also = [{ ships = "minimal counterexample worlds of the failures card randomization finds", pilot =
     "PILOT-C-28" }]` (rule 5: the shrinker ships beside the fallback only where its own record funds it).
     `integrated_gate = "GATE-NEMESIS-02"`.
   - `PILOT-C-28`, mechanism minimal counterexample worlds. `gated_beads = ["N26a-34"]` (the gate's measuring
     bead, which N26a-35's unfunded branch withdraws; BD-274, N26a-17, N26a-18 and N26a-19 stand on the
     contrasts path and are not gated). Falsifier: fewer than 70% of returned worlds carry a solvability witness
     or the unsolvable control is reported as a counterexample; or one returned world from which a single edit
     can still be removed; or fewer than half of shrunk worlds have at most three edits; or fewer than 90%
     replay (the construction's two falsifiers and the chapter's). Ceiling: `none on speed`, with the reason: a
     better artifact per failure, measured in agent repair iterations. Fallback: `ships` full failing worlds with
     replay and the single-edit contrasts ranked by margin, the format kept (the construction's "failing world
     unshrunk, with its edit list" with the contrasts beside it); `kill_gate = "KILL-MCW"`.
     `integrated_gate = "GATE-NEMESIS-03"`.
   Each result's `receipt = "blake3:<hash>"` is committed byte-identical at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the
   commit that appends it (N38a-09 rule 2a; otherwise the register does not load, `pilot-receipt-uncommitted`).
7. **Statistics.** The ratio is reported as a lower confidence bound on the paired ratio, never as a ratio of
   means; ten pairs of constant ratio r must return the bound r (the harness self-test). Censored randomization
   runs enter at their cap, which makes the bound conservative. The paired lower bound, the potency arm and the
   A/A arm are the experiment-statistics routines of `frob-conformance` (N38a-08), called by the pilot script,
   not re-implemented in it, so the pilot and the gate (N26a-11) compute their bound with one routine.

      are in the records before any timing exists.

      mandatory parts, one kill gate each: KILL-NEMESIS and KILL-MCW), with the one protocol hash; `PILOT-C-28`

- [ ] The results hold, for each arm and trap, steps to first failure per repetition, the censoring flags, the
      paired lower bound, the share of returned worlds with a witness, and for shrinking the sizes, the
      single-removal check and the replay check; each result's receipt is committed at
      `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`.
- [ ] Each record's result states which of its falsifiers fired and the recommendation for its mechanism: fund
      the search methods or ship KILL-NEMESIS's fallback; fund the minimal-world shrinker or ship KILL-MCW's;
      `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --package WP-55 --json` then lists the two
      mechanisms among the package's, each with its own answer.
- [ ] A reviewer who did not run the pilot can rerun it from the records (seeds, tool pin, commands).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot's own harness has three self-tests, run before the measured arms:
- `constant_ratio_bound`: ten synthetic pairs with ratio exactly 4 return a lower bound of 4.
- `aa_arm`: randomization against itself with different seeds does not clear 1.02 at the paired lower bound,
  the pass rule of the A/A routine of N38a-08 that the script calls (the program's rule for every method
  experiment: an A/A campaign that clears 1.02 means a broken harness, and the pilot's results are void until
  it is fixed).
- `planted_effect_detected`: a synthetic arm that is exactly 5 times faster is reported above 3.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/nemesis_search_shrink.sh` sources the STD-E2E helper, is seed-explicit and resumable by run key,
and writes newline-delimited JSON to `target/pilot-logs/nemesis_search_shrink/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-06`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time, the tool pin and the protocol hash. Per run: arm, trap, repetition, seed, steps charged (evaluation, witness
check, replay separately), censoring flag, failure class and binding atom, witness outcome. Per shrink: the edit
counts before and after, tests run, seeds failing, the single-removal check and digest equality. `metric` lines
give each falsifier's measured value with its threshold (paired lower bound ≥ 3; witness share ≥ 70%; shrunk
worlds with at most three edits ≥ 50%; replay ≥ 90%; zero removable edits). On a failed self-test or a falsifier
that fires, the first offending run with its seed and trap is logged. A final `summary` lists assertions passed
and failed, the falsifiers fired and the recommendation. The harness self-tests run first; a self-test failure
aborts the pilot before any measured arm.
The bead has no `scripts/e2e/` script (convention 11): it delivers nothing a `frob` command can reach (the pilot
code lives under `tools/pilots/` and is not linked by the workspace), and its two records are read by the
register's own command, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- complete --json`, which the pilot
script runs last and logs.

### Known answers and negative controls
The trap probabilities are analytic (0.01 × 0.003 = 3 × 10⁻⁵). The unsolvable doorway is the control for the
witness rule (CX-289: a doorway narrower than the body defeats every controller). The order-dependence example of
delta debugging (CX-288: failure when {a, b} or {c, d, e} is present; listed (a, c, d, e, b), delta debugging
ends at {c, d, e}) is run as a check that the pilot's shrinker claims 1-minimality only.

## Considerations

- A pilot on stand-in interiors and stand-in cards can pass where the product fails, and the reverse; the gate at
  M3 (GATE-NEMESIS-02) decides the product. The pilot only decides funding.
- The construction card for rare failures states a pilot falsifier of its own for a planted trap ("not found at
  least three times sooner than by uniform sampling"); it coincides with the search falsifier here and is
  recorded as the same measurement.
- If the search falsifier fires and the shrinking falsifiers do not, the shrinker is funded alone and runs on
  failures that randomization finds: exactly the KILL-NEMESIS fallback.
- Nothing in this pilot is product code; it lives under `tools/pilots/` and is not linked by the workspace.
