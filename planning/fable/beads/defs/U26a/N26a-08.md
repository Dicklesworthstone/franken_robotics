## Background

The search minimizes the requirement margin: a dimensionless number per episode, negative when the requirement is
violated. Over the continuous part of a world (values inside card uncertainties: actuator gain, friction
coefficient, mass, sensor bias; continuous scene quantities; disturbance magnitudes) two established tools apply.

**Cross-entropy search** tilts a sampler toward low margin: sample worlds, keep the worst-margin fraction, refit
the sampler to them, repeat. Its foundation exists in the sibling library: FACT,
`frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance/proposal.rs` fits the mean of a unit-covariance Gaussian in
standard-normal germ space from a completed, independent pilot, as μ̂ = Σᵢ wᵢ·1[i elite]·zᵢ / Σᵢ wᵢ·1[i elite],
with wᵢ the target-to-proposal likelihood ratio of sample i. Its ranking threshold is a pilot heuristic, not a
probability estimate, and production sampling must use a fresh key and must not include pilot data.

**Derivative-free descent** follows the margin downhill from a promising world. FACT: `fs-dfo` provides CMA-ES
with restarts and a Nelder–Mead polish, with sampling from keyed streams, bitwise reproducible from the seed.

Both are Frontier here: their gain over randomization on robots in interiors is unmeasured (the pilot N26a-06
decides funding; the gate GATE-NEMESIS-02 decides the product). In the rare-failure vocabulary both are
*exploration*: they produce failures, and cross-entropy also produces a proposal that a later, separate stage
may use; neither produces a bound. Published evidence cuts both ways (FACT, source register): in one study a
cross-entropy sampler returned 7.6 × 10⁻²² for a true probability of 2.0 × 10⁻³. That is why nothing here is
ever labelled more than `Estimated`, and why the proposal it exports is admitted to the certification lane only
inside a defensive mixture.

## Scope

In: `search::cem::CrossEntropy` and `search::dfo::Descent` as `SearchMethod`s; truncation at legality bounds with
exact proposal densities; common random numbers; exporting a fitted proposal for a later stage.
Out: germ transforms (N26a-24); the defensive mixture and bounds (BD-276); the lane firewall that refuses a
proposal fitted on the run it is applied to (N26a-23); structural edits (N26a-09); splitting (BD-277).

## Technical approach

1. **Coordinates.** Continuous quantities are searched as normal germs z ∈ ℝᵈ: independent standard-normal
   coordinates mapped monotonically to each quantity's nominal distribution (N26a-24). The nominal law is
   z ~ N(0, I). A legality bound [a, b] on a quantity is an interval [z_lo, z_hi] on its germ.
2. **Cross-entropy, per round r (numbered).**
   1. Proposal q_r = N(μ_r, I) truncated to the legality box B = Π [z_lo, z_hi]; μ₀ = 0.
   2. Draw N worlds (default 1,024, TARGET) by address from q_r, by rejection of draws outside B. The proposal
      density is exact: log q_r(z) = Σⱼ [log φ(zⱼ − μ_r,ⱼ) − log(Φ(z_hi,ⱼ − μ_r,ⱼ) − Φ(z_lo,ⱼ − μ_r,ⱼ))].
      Each draw is recorded with `law = Tilted { proposal }` and `log_q`.
   3. Evaluate the margin of each world on the fast tier, at paired addresses for the non-searched randomness.
   4. Elite set: the worlds with margin at or below the ρ_e-quantile (ρ_e = 0.1, TARGET), or all worlds with
      negative margin if more.
   5. Update μ_{r+1} = (1 − s)·μ_r + s·μ̂ with μ̂ the weighted elite mean above (wᵢ = p(zᵢ)/q_r(zᵢ)) and
      smoothing s = 0.7 (TARGET); clip μ_{r+1} so that the box keeps at least 10% of the proposal's mass in every
      coordinate.
   6. Stop on budget, or when the round's failure share exceeds one half (the sampler is in the failure region;
      further tilting finds nothing new).
   Worlds with negative margin are candidate failures and go to the driver.
3. **Exporting a proposal.** At the end, `CrossEntropy::export() -> ProposalRecord { id, mu, box, fitted_on:
   campaign, episodes: [addresses] }`, hashed into the ledger. A later certification stage may use it only as the
   shifted component of a defensive mixture, only with fresh addresses, and never on the episodes it was fitted
   on (the firewall refuses that with FROB-REF-563).
4. **Derivative-free descent.** For each of the k lowest-margin worlds of the archive (default k = 8):
   - objective f(z) = margin of the episode at z **with every other random stream held at the start world's
     address** (common random numbers), so f is a deterministic function and differences are due to z alone;
   - optimizer: CMA-ES from `fs-dfo` through the airlock, population λ = 4 + ⌊3·ln d⌋, initial step 0.3 in germ
     units, box constraints B handled by resampling; then a Nelder–Mead polish from the best point;
   - each generation is one batch of worlds; stop at negative margin after the boundary check, on budget, or
     after 30 generations without improvement of more than 10⁻³ in margin.
   Proposals carry `law = Search { method: dfo }` and no `log_q` (a local optimizer has no sampling density).
5. **Truncation is not clamping.** A draw outside the box is rejected and redrawn, never moved to the boundary:
   clamping would pile mass on the bounds and make the recorded density wrong.
6. **Determinism.** Draws are functions of (campaign seed, round, index) on registered streams; the fit is a
   deterministic reduction in index order. A campaign replays bit for bit on one backend.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the `synthetic` margin f(z) = 4 − z₁ (failure when z₁ > 4, nominal probability 3.167 × 10⁻⁵), the
      cross-entropy method reaches a first failure within 6 rounds of 1,024 in at least 95 of 100 seeded runs;
      card-uniform sampling needs about 31,600 draws in expectation.
- [ ] Every draw lies inside the legality box; the recorded `log_q` integrates to one over the box (numerical
      quadrature in one and two dimensions, to 10⁻⁹).
- [ ] With the box at z₁ ≤ 3 (the failure region made illegal), the method reports no failure and proposes no
      world outside the box.
- [ ] Descent under common random numbers is deterministic: two runs give identical trajectories of f; with the
      common-random-number rule switched off (test feature) the same objective evaluated twice at one z differs.
- [ ] The exported proposal's record is in the ledger; offering its fitting episodes to a bound is refused with
      FROB-REF-563 (asserted through N26a-23's accumulator).
- [ ] Both methods implement `SearchMethod`, record the proposing law of every world, and pass the driver's
      legality check with zero refused proposals on the fixtures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/cem.rs`, `dfo.rs` (all fixtures `synthetic`: closed-form margins on germs):
- `cem_finds_gaussian_tail`: as in the acceptance list; logs rounds to first failure per seed.
- `cem_mean_update_formula`: three hand-made samples z = (1, 2, 3) with weights (1, 1/2, 1/4), all elite:
  μ̂ = (1 + 1 + 3/4)/(7/4) = 11/7.
- `truncated_density_normalized`; `no_clamping`: no draw equals a bound exactly.
- `cem_two_modes`: failure when z₁ > 4 or z₂ > 4; a single-mean sampler concentrates on one mode. The test
  asserts the method reports which modes its failures cover, and that the archive (N26a-09) restarts the sampler
  from the other cell: both modes found in at least 80 of 100 runs with the archive, fewer without.
- `dfo_descends_quadratic`: f(z) = ‖z − (2, 2)‖² − 1 reaches f < 0 within 30 generations.
- `dfo_crn_deterministic`; `dfo_respects_box`.
- `export_record_hash_stable`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Run inside `scripts/e2e/nemesis_search.sh` (N26a-11) with `--method cross-entropy` and `--method descent`; logged
per round: proposal mean norm, share of mass in the box, elite threshold, failures, steps charged.

### Known answers and negative controls
The Gaussian tail P(z > 4) = 3.167 × 10⁻⁵ fixes the baseline's expected cost (1/p ≈ 31,575 draws). Negative
controls: a clamping sampler (the density test fails); a fit that reuses the round's own samples as nominal
(weights omitted: the update formula test fails). CX-291 (importance weights may be unbounded if the proposal is
good) is why the exported proposal is never used bare. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker
counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- Cross-entropy collapses onto one failure mode; the quality-diversity archive and the Good–Turing monitor exist
  to notice and counter that. A campaign reports the modes found, never "the failure region".
- KILL-NEMESIS removes these methods from `nemesis.search`. KILL-RARE keeps tilting only inside
  `nemesis.search` and removes splitting. Both leave the exported-proposal path to the defensive mixture intact.
- The upstream proposal fit covers Gaussian germs with unit covariance; anything richer (a full covariance, a
  mixture proposal) is ours to add later and is not needed for the gate.
- Budget: each cross-entropy round costs N episodes; the default 1,024 fits one fast-tier batch.
