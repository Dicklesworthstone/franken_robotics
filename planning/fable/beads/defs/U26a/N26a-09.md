## Background

Half of what makes an interior hard is discrete: a film is present or not, a cart stands in the aisle or not, a
mat lies over a drain, a door has a closer. A sampler over continuous parameters never proposes such changes.
Two methods of the search cover them.

**Grammar mutation** makes structural changes to a world using Habitat's scene grammar and the edit vocabulary
drawn from the hazard registries (N26a-02), and is guided by experience: which requirement atoms an edit has
moved before. A requirement is a formula over atoms (comparisons with a scale, such as "upright", "cleared",
"delivered"); a verdict reports the minimum margin of each atom over the episode and names the binding atom, the
one that decides the verdict. If past edits of kind "film on a floor patch" lowered the margin of "upright", and
"upright" is what currently binds, that kind is worth trying again.

**A quality-diversity archive** keeps the best failure found in each bin of a behaviour space instead of one
global best, so that one failure mode does not absorb the whole budget. FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-archive/src/lib.rs` provides `MapElites` (a grid over a descriptor space keeping one elite
per cell, with `add`, `coverage`, `qd_score`, `elites`) and `CvtArchive`; it is deterministic and has no
dependencies. Here the bins are failure class by binding atom: the same key under which the failure-mode
catalogue files failures. The failure taxonomy supplies the classes (`fall`, `stuck`, `collision`, `slip`,
`torque-saturation`, `timeout`, `film-slip`, `perception-dropout` and the rest).

Both are Frontier (their gain over randomization is unmeasured; pilot N26a-06, gate GATE-NEMESIS-02).

## Scope

In: `search::grammar::GrammarMutation` and `search::archive::FailureArchive`; the atom-movement table; parent
selection across bins; the mixed discrete-continuous handoff to the continuous methods.
Out: the scene grammar and typed scene edits themselves (Habitat); the continuous methods (N26a-08); the
failure-mode catalogue and its unseen-mass monitor (N26a-27); the campaign driver (BD-273).

## Technical approach

1. **The archive.** A grid with two discrete axes: failure class index (plus one cell row for "no failure yet,
   lowest margin") and binding-atom index of the task's requirement. Fitness of a world = −margin (higher is
   worse for the candidate). `FailureArchive::add(world, outcome)` keeps a world in its cell if it has lower
   margin than the incumbent; ties are broken by smaller world size (fewer edits, then smaller normalized
   distance, N26a-03). Implemented on `fs_archive::MapElites` through the airlock, with class and atom indices
   mapped to bin centres. The archive is shared by all methods of a campaign.
2. **Parent selection.** Each round, parents are drawn by a keyed stream **uniformly over occupied cells**, then
   the cell's elite is taken. A cell with many visits has the same chance as a cell with one. This is the whole
   mechanism by which one mode does not absorb the budget. A floor share of rounds (10%, TARGET) starts from a
   fresh nominal world instead of a parent.
3. **The atom-movement table.** `M[edit_kind][atom]` holds a running mean and count of
   Δ = (atom's minimum margin after the edit) − (before), measured on paired addresses (the same world with and
   without the edit, same random streams). It is updated from every mutation the method evaluates.
4. **Mutation, per offspring (numbered).**
   1. Take a parent world and its binding atom a (or the lowest-margin atom if the parent passes).
   2. Choose an edit kind: with probability 0.7 (TARGET) from the kinds in the lowest quartile of M[·][a]
      (most negative mean effect on a), weighted equally; otherwise uniformly from the whole vocabulary of the
      archetype. Kinds with fewer than 5 observations are treated as unknown and sampled uniformly. The
      exploration floor guarantees every kind a positive probability, and the choice probability is recorded as
      part of the proposing law.
   3. Instantiate the edit: a site from the scene (a patch, a door, an aisle station) by keyed draw; a value
      inside the legality bound, uniformly for the first trial of a site and by bisection toward the bound that
      lowered the margin before.
   4. Operators: add one edit; remove one edit (toward smaller worlds); change one edit's value; swap a site.
      Operator probabilities 0.5, 0.2, 0.2, 0.1 (TARGET).
   5. The offspring must be legal on the final world; an illegal offspring is counted and not run.
5. **Handoff to continuous methods.** When an offspring's margin falls within 0.2 of zero (TARGET) without
   failing, its discrete structure is frozen and its continuous quantities are handed to descent (N26a-08) for
   one local run charged to the same budget.
6. **Records.** Each proposal: `law = Search { method: grammar }` with the operator, the edit kind and its
   choice probability. The table M and the archive are stored with the campaign so that a later campaign on the
   same task can start from them (they are discovery data, marked as such).
7. **Determinism.** Selection, instantiation and the table update are functions of (campaign seed, round, index)
   and of the outcomes in index order.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a `synthetic` task with three atoms and two planted failure modes of different class and atom (mode A
      needs edit kinds {film, push}; mode B needs {narrow aisle}), the method with the archive finds both modes
      in at least 80 of 100 seeded runs within 5,000 evaluations; with parent selection by global best it finds
      both in clearly fewer (the difference is recorded and must be positive).
- [ ] After 2,000 evaluations on that task, M[film][upright] is negative with at least 5 observations and
      M[light-level][upright] is within noise of zero; the guided choice picks `film` for a parent bound by
      `upright` at least three times as often as an unguided choice would.
- [ ] Every offspring passes the declaration check and the final-world legality check before it runs; refused
      offspring are counted per kind.
- [ ] The archive never holds two worlds in one cell and never replaces an elite by a world of higher margin
      (property test over 10⁴ random insertions).
- [ ] Parent draws are uniform over occupied cells (chi-square at level 0.001 over 10⁵ draws on a fixed archive).
- [ ] The method implements `SearchMethod`; a campaign replays to identical archives.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/grammar.rs`, `archive.rs`:
- `archive_keeps_lower_margin`; `archive_tie_breaks_by_size`; `archive_one_elite_per_cell`.
- `parent_uniform_over_cells`: an archive with visit counts (1,000, 10, 1) yields parents in ratio 1:1:1.
- `movement_table_update`: paired outcomes (before 0.40, after 0.10) and (0.30, 0.20) for one kind and atom give
  mean Δ = −0.20, count 2.
- `guided_choice_distribution`: in a vocabulary of eight kinds the lowest quartile for atom a holds two kinds,
  and each is chosen with probability 0.7/2 + 0.3/8 = 0.3875; in a vocabulary of four kinds the quartile holds
  one, chosen with probability 0.7 + 0.3/4 = 0.775 (asserted to ±0.01 over 10⁵ draws).
- `unknown_kinds_sampled`: kinds with fewer than 5 observations are never excluded.
- `operators_preserve_legality_or_refuse`; `handoff_threshold`.
- `two_mode_fixture` (the acceptance case).

### End-to-end script and logging
Inside `scripts/e2e/nemesis_search.sh` (N26a-11) with `--method grammar`: logged per round the occupied cells,
the cell each parent came from, the operator and edit kind, the count of illegal offspring, failures by cell.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative controls: global-best parent selection (the two-mode test must show the loss); a table updated from
unpaired outcomes (the paired-address assertion fails); an archive keyed by world identity instead of failure
mode (every world is its own cell and nothing is compared). Metamorphic: renaming atoms or permuting the class
enumeration permutes cells and changes no count. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The guidance table learns from the candidate under test; a table carried to another candidate is a prior, not
  evidence, and is marked with the candidate it came from.
- The archive's bins depend on the failure taxonomy; a taxonomy change invalidates stored archives (their
  identity includes the taxonomy version).
- KILL-NEMESIS removes this method. The archive type stays: the failure-mode catalogue uses the same key.
- Structural edits change the compiled scene; variants share untouched bricks and are memoized, and the world
  bank bounds how many variants are resident at once.
