## Background

The adversarial search has a second customer. The usual question is "in which legal world does this design
fail?". A design studio more often asks: "I prefer design A to design B; is there a legal world in which that
preference reverses?". Redesigning the body and designing the world are two sides of one decision problem, and
the smallest world change that flips a preference is often more informative than the largest simulated reward:
for a threshold, one changed edge radius; for a warehouse, a short compliant mat under load.

The same search serves it: for rivals A and B, find a legal world in which the predicted preference reverses by
a practically important margin. The mission subsystem owns the question (a pair of designs and a decision, the
admissibility contract, the verb `mission.separate`, seed bead BD-293); this bead supplies the search objective
and the ordering inside `frob-nemesis`.

When the question is which of two designs is better, the order in which candidate worlds are evaluated may follow
a value-of-information ranking. FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-voi/src/lib.rs` provides
`ranking_flip_probability(chosen, other)`, which for two designs with estimated means and standard deviations
returns Φ(−Δ/√(s₁² + s₂²)), Δ being the gap of the means. The ranking orders work and never decides a verdict.

Frontier: it inherits the search's status. Its own kill gate belongs to the mission subsystem
(KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE: if minimization changes the failure mechanism in more than 20% of planted cases, or a
conventional scenario tool with the same generator matches the result at equal cost, the plain search and
minimization ship without the design-pair query and counterexamples are kept as regressions).

## Scope

In: the pair objective; pairing by world address; the reversal predicate; solvability and guard requirements for
a pair; the value-of-information ordering; the library entry point `search::pair::search_reversal`. This bead
is the one definition of the objective and of the pairing for the design-pair question.

Out, and which bead has it:

- The loop around the entry point: confirmation of a candidate on 64 paired seeds, reproduction on the referee
  with the promotion variants, classification of the failure and its first cause, minimization with the
  first-cause check, the counterfactual neighbour, the `.fmcw` artifacts and the update of each design's
  known-handled region: N25b-23, the mission subsystem's engine, which calls this entry point and depends on
  this bead. It defines no objective or pairing of its own.
- The verb and its certificate (`mission.separate`): BD-293. The paired comparison statistics: BD-262. The
  single-design search: BD-273. Shrinking the separating world: BD-274.

## Technical approach

1. **Objective.** Let S_A(w) and S_B(w) be the two designs' scores in world w: the requirement margin by
   default, or the task's declared scalar metric. The predicted preference is A over B (from a comparison on
   nominal worlds). Define the preference gap g(w) = S_A(w) − S_B(w), evaluated with **both designs on the same
   world address** (same scene, same disturbances, same random streams, each design in its own settled start
   pose), so that g differs from zero because of the designs and not the draw. Shared inputs that depend on
   where a robot is (a friction field, lighting) are taken from the world at each robot's own contact points
   and poses, not from a seed indexed by tick: two designs that take different paths must still read one
   field.
   The search minimizes g over legal worlds. A candidate reversal is a world with g(w) ≤ −Δ_min, where Δ_min is
   the practically important margin the question declares (a margin difference such as 0.1, or a metric
   difference in its unit); after the boundary check on both verdicts. The mission answer prints the reversal
   score, which is −g = S_B − S_A (the engine N25b-23 and the verb use that name); it is this gap with the
   opposite sign, not a second objective, and every threshold is stated on g.
2. **Methods.** The pair objective plugs into the same `SearchMethod`s as the single-design score: the methods
   receive g in place of the margin; the archive is binned by (which design fails or loses, binding atom of the
   losing design).
3. **Two verdict-level kinds of reversal**, reported separately: *verdict reversal* (A violates the requirement
   where B satisfies it) and *margin reversal* (both satisfy or both violate, and the gap is at most −Δ_min).
   Only the first is a statement about meeting the requirement.
4. **Solvability and guard for a pair.** A separating world counts only if (a) it is inside the legality
   profile; (b) the oracle classifies it `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible` for the design that loses there
   (a world neither design could solve separates nothing); (c) the reversal survives the artifact guard for
   both designs: replayed one tier up and under the numerical variants, the sign of g and the verdict pair are
   unchanged. A reversal that vanishes is an `artifact-candidate`, written to the fidelity ledger.
5. **Value-of-information ordering.** When a batch of candidate worlds waits and the budget cannot evaluate all,
   the driver ranks them by the flip probability Φ(−ĝ/√(ŝ_A² + ŝ_B²)), where ĝ and the standard deviations are
   predicted from the outcomes of the k nearest evaluated worlds in normalized edit space (k = 8, TARGET; a
   world with fewer than 3 evaluated neighbours gets probability ½ and is evaluated early). Candidates with the
   highest flip probability go first. The ranking is recorded with the proposal; it is never an input to a
   verdict, a count or a stopping rule other than budget.
6. **Output.** `PairResult { reversals: [{ world, kind, gap, verdict_a, verdict_b, oracle, guard }],
   evaluated: [{ address, flip_probability, rank, gap }], worlds_searched, baseline_arm: {
   reversals_at_equal_budget }, not_counted, certificate: false }`. `guard` holds the guard's outcome for each of
   the two designs. `evaluated` has one row per world the search evaluated, in evaluation order, with the flip
   probability and the rank the world had when it was proposed (null for a world evaluated with the ordering
   off) and its gap; the mission engine N25b-23 carries these rows and the reversal record unchanged into its
   result (`search` and `reversal`), from which the verb `mission.separate` prints the `search` and `pair`
   blocks of its answer (BD-293). The
   baseline arm is card-uniform randomization evaluated on the same pair objective at equal budget. Every world
   is discovery data.
7. **Claim.** A reported reversal is a Design comparison of class `Tested` on the stated tier: "B beats A in
   world w by g". It says nothing about how often such worlds arise (the `prior` field says that) and one world
   is a trace witness, not a universal verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a `synthetic` pair with S_A(z) = 1 − z₁ and S_B(z) = 0.6 (A preferred nominally, since E[S_A] = 1), the
      search returns a world with z₁ ≥ 0.5 for Δ_min = 0.1 (gap ≤ −0.1), and the baseline arm's count is printed.
- [ ] Both designs of every evaluated pair ran on one world address (asserted from the episode records); a
      planted unpaired run (different seeds) is rejected by the driver; on the shared-friction-field control,
      where the two designs take diverging paths, a build that draws the field from a tick-indexed seed shows a
      spurious reversal and the space-indexed build shows none.
- [ ] A separating world that is `infeasible-for-candidate` for the losing design is not reported as a reversal.
- [ ] A reversal that changes sign at half step is labelled `artifact-candidate` and excluded.
- [ ] With the ordering on, the same reversals are found as with it off given enough budget (the ordering
      changes order only); with a tight budget on the planted fixture the ordered run finds the reversal in
      fewer evaluations in at least 70 of 100 seeds.
- [ ] No code path lets the flip probability alter a verdict or a count (test: scrambling the ranking leaves the
      set of reported reversals unchanged at full budget).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/pair.rs`:
- `gap_is_paired`: episode records of A and B share the address; the gap of two identical designs is exactly 0
  in every world on one backend.
- `space_indexed_shared_inputs`: two designs on diverging paths over one friction field read the field at their
  own contact points; the tick-indexed mutant gives a non-zero gap for a pair that differs only in path.
- `reversal_predicate`: gaps −0.05, −0.10, −0.20 with Δ_min = 0.1 → no, yes, yes.
- `kinds_of_reversal`: (violated, satisfied) is a verdict reversal; (satisfied, satisfied) with gap −0.2 is a
  margin reversal.
- `flip_probability_value`: means 1.0 and 0.6, standard deviations 0.3 and 0.4 →
  Φ(−0.4/0.5) = Φ(−0.8) = 0.2119 (to four decimals), through the airlock.
- `ordering_is_only_ordering` (the scramble test); `few_neighbours_gets_half`.
- `evaluated_rows_recorded`: `PairResult.evaluated` has exactly one row per evaluated world, with its address,
  its gap and the flip probability and rank it had when proposed (the ranks of one waiting batch are 1 to its
  size without gaps; both are null with the ordering off), and its length equals `worlds_searched`.
- `unsolvable_separator_not_counted`; `artifact_reversal_excluded`.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead has no verb of its own: its entry point is reached from the user's side only through
`frob mission separate`, so it is exercised by that verb's script, `scripts/e2e/mission_separate.sh` (BD-293,
which depends on this bead through the separation engine N25b-23), on the ten planted reversals. That script
logs, per case, the reversal gap g, both verdicts and the reversal kind, the oracle class of the losing design,
the guard outcome for both designs and the flip-probability rank of each evaluated world (read from the `pair`
and `search` blocks of the verb's answer, which BD-293 defines from this bead's `PairResult`). The search
gate's script (N26a-11) does not run it, so the KILL-NEMESIS decision never waits on the mission subsystem.

### Known answers and negative controls
Φ(−0.8) = 0.2119 is the closed-form check of the ordering's input (the sibling's admission gate checks the same
formula to 10⁻¹²). Negative controls: unpaired evaluation (identical designs then show non-zero gaps: the
`gap_is_paired` test catches it); an ordering wired into the stopping rule (the scramble test catches it).
Metamorphic: swapping the names A and B negates every gap and swaps the reversal lists.

## Considerations

- A margin reversal between two designs that both pass is weak evidence of anything; it is reported as such and
  cannot be cited as "B is better".
- If KILL-NEMESIS fires, this entry point runs on card-uniform proposals only; if KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE fires, the
  entry point is withdrawn and single-design counterexamples remain as regressions.
- The dual question (the smallest priced edit to the world that restores the mission) belongs to the retrofit
  search and is not built here.
- Pairs cost twice the episodes of a single-design search; the budget ledger charges both designs' steps.
