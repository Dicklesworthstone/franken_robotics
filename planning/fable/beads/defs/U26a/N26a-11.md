## Background

GATE-NEMESIS-02 is the gate on which the adversarial search lives or dies (claim: Design comparison, since it
compares a method with a competing arm; earliest milestone M3; TARGET): on planted traps of nominal probability
at most 3 × 10⁻⁵, in 30 paired repetitions, the search reaches a first promoted failure with at least 3 times
fewer simulated steps than card-uniform randomization at a 95% lower bound, and at least 70% of reported
counterexamples are `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible`. If it fails after one repair cycle, kill gate
KILL-NEMESIS removes the search methods.

A gate of this kind is a method experiment, and the program admits a method experiment only if six things are
written down before its first run: the ceiling (the largest effect it could show), the competing arms, a potency
control (a planted effect it must detect and an A/A arm it must not), a paired lower bound as the reported
quantity, the pre-declared fallback, and a reachability pre-analysis (that the threshold can be crossed at the
planned sample size under the declared alternative). A test whose ceiling is below its success threshold cannot
confirm anything. This bead writes those six down, runs the experiment from the user's side on the real binary,
and records the gate.

## Scope

In: the registration record; the script; the measurement and its statistics; the gate record; the logs.
Out: the search and its methods (BD-273, N26a-07 to N26a-09); the planted traps (N26a-04); the decision on the
kill gate (N26a-12).

## Technical approach

1. **Registration (before any run; hashed into the ledger).**
   - *Ceiling:* under ten on the cost of one found failure (published gains 2 to 20 times, and none on one
     policy-in-the-loop task; a planning recomputation charging the oracle and shrinking gives about 6). The
     threshold 3 is below the ceiling, so the test can confirm. On the time to a design decision the ceiling is
     the share of that time spent finding failures, taken from the phase ledger of the baseline arm.
   - *Arms:* (1) `nemesis.search --method auto`; (2) card-uniform randomization at equal budget, which is also
     the mechanism's own fallback; (3) where a scenario-language tool is installed, its sampler with the same
     generator and monitors through `--method given`; otherwise recorded `not run`.
   - *Potency control:* a planted trap of probability 10⁻² that both arms must find; an A/A run (arm 2 against
     arm 2 with different seeds) whose lower bound must be below 1.2.
   - *Reported quantity:* the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on the paired ratio R = (steps of arm 2 to
     its first promoted failure in the trap) ÷ (steps of arm 1), pairs formed by trap and seed.
   - *Fallback:* KILL-NEMESIS, as stated.
   - *Reachability:* under the declared alternative R = 6 with the pair-to-pair spread seen in the pilot
     (N26a-06), the probability that the lower bound exceeds 3 at 30 pairs is computed and recorded; if it is
     below 0.8 the experiment is not run as planned and the plan is revised by a recorded decision.
2. **Design.** 30 paired repetitions over the T3 traps of the planted registry (probability 3 × 10⁻⁵, trigger
   box z₁ > 2.3263 and z₂ > 2.7478), ten traps across two interiors, three seeds each. "Found" is a promoted
   failure inside the trap's box with the trap's failure class and binding atom. Steps are simulated steps
   charged to the arm, including its oracle and guard runs. Arm 2 is capped at 10⁵ worlds per repetition (three
   times its expected 33,333) and a capped run enters at the cap, flagged censored, which lowers R.
3. **Script steps.**
   1. `start`: build, registry digest, registration hash, seeds, tier, hardware fingerprint.
   2. Potency: run both arms on the 10⁻² trap; assert both find it; run the A/A pair set; assert bound < 1.2.
   3. For each of the 30 pairs: `frob nemesis search <candidate> --task <task> --legality <profile> --method
      auto --first --budget steps=<B> --seed <s> --key <k> --json`, then the same with `--method card-uniform`;
      wait on the job handles; read `steps` and the first promoted failure; assert the failure lies in the box.
   4. Compute R per pair and the lower bound; compute the share of all reported counterexamples whose oracle
      class is `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible`.
   5. `metric` lines: `ratio_lower_95 = x` against `≥ 3`; `solvable_share = y` against `≥ 0.70`.
   6. One step runs the pair entry point on its synthetic fixture (N26a-10) and one runs the whole script with
      `methods = baseline-only` on three pairs to show the fallback branch works.
   7. Write the gate record; `summary`.
4. **Logging.** `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_search/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript, through the shared helper. Every
   line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-11`), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
   evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), and for `metric` events the value, unit
   and threshold. Per repetition: arm, trap, seed, steps per phase, censoring flag, first failure's world
   identity, class, atom, oracle class, guard outcome, legality and prior blocks. On failure: the first pair
   whose search arm did not find the trap, with the campaign identity needed to replay it. Summary: assertions
   passed and failed, `gates: ["GATE-NEMESIS-02"]`.
5. **Gate record.** Measured lower bound, solvable share, censored count, registry digest, registration hash,
   commit, fingerprint, seeds. A fail is recorded as a fail; one repair cycle is allowed before KILL-NEMESIS is
   read, and the second run is a new record, not an overwrite.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration record with all six parts exists and predates the first measured run.
- [ ] The potency control passes: both arms find the 10⁻² trap; the A/A bound is below 1.2.
- [ ] The script records GATE-NEMESIS-02 with the measured values (TARGET thresholds: lower bound at least 3;
      solvable share at least 70%), pass or fail.
- [ ] Ten synthetic pairs with constant ratio 4 fed to the statistics step return a lower bound of 4.
- [ ] The fallback-branch step produces promoted failures with `method: card-uniform`.
- [ ] Logs contain one line per command and the per-repetition fields; the script exits non-zero when an
      assertion fails and zero when the gate is measured (whatever its outcome, which is recorded as a metric).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`scripts/e2e/tests/nemesis_search_stats.sh`: feeds recorded step counts to the statistics step: constant ratio 4
→ bound 4; a baseline censored at 10⁸ steps in every pair with the search at 10⁷ steps → R = 10 per pair,
bound 10; a mixed set with one
search arm that never finds the trap → that pair enters with R below 1 and the bound drops accordingly.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-661 (the planted solvable trap is found with a causal witness; the baseline at equal budget is its control).
Negative controls: the A/A arm; a search arm silently given twice the budget (the equal-steps assertion fails);
a "found" rule that accepts any failure anywhere (the in-box assertion fails on the baseline's unplanted
failures). Determinism lanes: the 30-pair run repeated on one machine gives identical step counts on one
backend; across backends the gate is re-measured, not compared bit for bit.

## Considerations

- Wall time: 30 capped baseline repetitions are up to 3 × 10⁶ worlds; on the fast tier this is minutes to
  hours depending on measured throughput, and the script prints an estimate first.
- The lower bound is on a ratio of costs to the first failure, which is heavy-tailed (geometric for the
  baseline); the bound routine works on log ratios, and its coverage on geometric data is part of its own test.
- A pass says the search finds planted traps of this kind faster. It does not say that unplanted failures of
  real designs are found faster; the share of design-loop time spent finding failures is reported from the phase
  ledger so the effect on decisions is not overstated.
