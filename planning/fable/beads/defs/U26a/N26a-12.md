## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision at the milestone the gate register names. KILL-NEMESIS is the stop rule of
the adversarial search (bet BET-22: adversarial search inside legality bounds finds failures faster than
randomization). The search is Frontier work whose gain is unmeasured until GATE-NEMESIS-02 is run; if the gain
is not there, the program keeps everything around the search that has value on its own and removes the search.

## Scope

In: reading the gate's measurements, evaluating the trigger literally, recording the decision, setting the
switch, listing what changes downstream.
Out: producing the measurements (BD-273, N26a-11); the fallback pipeline's implementation (N26a-07).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-NEMESIS-02 fails after one repair cycle. The gate (TARGET): on planted traps of nominal
probability at most 3 × 10⁻⁵, in 30 paired repetitions, the search reaches a first promoted failure with at
least 3 times fewer simulated steps than card-uniform randomization at a 95% lower bound, and at least 70% of
reported counterexamples are `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible`. Either clause failing fails the gate.

**One repair cycle** (the program's reading, N40-03): one bounded attempt to fix the cause of a failed
measurement, opened by a log line naming the failed receipt and the hypothesis, then one round of changes to the
search mechanism (methods, shares, defaults), and closed by one second measurement on the same frozen planted
registry with the same threshold and controls, with fresh seeds. The planted registry, the thresholds, the
"found" rule, the solvable-share definition and the budget accounting may not change between the two runs. A
second fail fires the gate; a second repair cycle is a `deferred` decision and needs its own ADR.

**Fallback.** The search methods are removed; Nemesis keeps the legality profile, the solvability oracle, the
artifact guard and shrinking, applied to failures that card randomization finds.

**Milestone.** Decided at M3, inside the milestone's exit review.

**Measurements that decide it.**
1. The paired lower 95% bound of the step ratio from the gate record (first run and, if any, second run).
2. The share of reported counterexamples with a solvability witness.
3. The potency control of the experiment (both arms find the 10⁻² trap; the A/A lower bound does not clear
   1.02, N38a-08's rule): if the potency control failed, the experiment is void and is rerun; a void experiment
   neither passes nor fires the gate.
4. For the record, not for the trigger: the pilot's result (N26a-06) and the share of design-loop time spent
   finding failures.

**Procedure** (the record format and outcomes are those of the kill-gate records, N40-03; the kill row itself,
with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided = "M3"`, `mode = "exit"` and `reads = ["GATE-NEMESIS-02"]`, is created by
the fallback ledger, N40-26).
1. A session that did not implement the search reads the gate records and the registration record. A session
   listed as an implementer of BD-273 or the method beads is refused by the record check.
2. It checks that the registry digest, thresholds and accounting are identical across runs, and that the repair
   note predates the second run.
3. It evaluates the trigger literally and writes the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-NEMESIS-M3.md` (every
   decision record lives in `docs/adr/`; a kill-gate decision is `ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`) with
   N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendments; each
   measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number; the outcome; the fallback in force and the
   name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by
   this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session.
4. It fills the kill row's `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (`receipts`: the hashes of the
   gate receipts the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`, N40-03) with outcome `not-fired` (the trigger
   evaluated and not met; GATE-NEMESIS-02 keeps its `pass` record), `fired` (GATE-NEMESIS-02's failed part takes
   status `killed` with detail "KILL-NEMESIS: card randomization with legality, oracle, guard and shrinking"),
   `not-reached` (the search package was not funded after the pilot N26a-06, so the methods never existed) or
   `deferred` (a second repair cycle, with its ADR); it sets `nemesis.search.methods = all | baseline-only` in
   the configuration record accordingly; and it writes the chapter's owner declaration of KILL-NEMESIS
   (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`, the ledger's text verbatim) where N26b-18 places gate
   declarations, so that N40-30's cross-check finds exactly one.
5. **The unfunded branch (convention 10).** This bead closes whichever way the measurement falls. When the
   search pilot's falsifier holds (`PILOT-NEMESIS-01`, N26a-06's record for the search, verdict
   `falsifier-holds`) or the search methods are not funded,
   the method beads (N26a-08, N26a-09) and the gate's measuring bead N26a-11 close `withdrawn` citing that
   `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7) and the driver BD-273 stands on the baseline arms, which releases this
   bead's dependencies; it then records `not-reached` (or `fired` on the pilot's values) and never waits for
   methods that were not built.

**What changes downstream if it fires.**
- `nemesis.search` remains a verb and runs the fallback pipeline: `--method` accepts `card-uniform` and `given`
  only; other values return FROB-REF-724 (not implemented in this build; exit 5) naming KILL-NEMESIS and the
  ADR. Results report `method: card-uniform`; `frob capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name, not the
  search.
- The method modules (cross-entropy, descent, grammar mutation, the quality-diversity selection) are removed
  from the build (the manifest feature `frontier-search` is dropped and its `contract.toml` row with it); the
  failure archive type stays for the failure-mode catalogue. The bead that carried the mechanism (BD-273) is
  re-read as "stands on the fallback": its driver, score and downstream remain.
- The design-pair reversal search (N26a-10) runs on card-uniform proposals only; the mission subsystem's
  separation query is re-read against its own kill gate.
- Splitting as a search method inside `nemesis.search` is withdrawn; the rare-failure exploration lane is
  governed by its own kill gate, KILL-RARE, and is unaffected by this decision alone.
- The capability is described by the name of its fallback ("randomized failure discovery with shrinking"), and
  the thesis lever "find failures instead of waiting for them" is restated without a search gain.
- The hard-world set, shrinking, the counterexample file format, the oracle, the guard and the legality profile
  are unchanged.

**What changes if it does not fire.** Nothing is removed; the measured ratio (not the target) is the number
cited for the search from then on.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-NEMESIS-M3.md` exists with N40-03's required fields, the measurements with
      their receipt hashes and the literal trigger evaluation.
- [ ] The kill row of KILL-NEMESIS carries `decision.outcome` (one of `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached`,
      `deferred`), the ADR identifier, date, session and the receipts it rests on; on `fired`, GATE-NEMESIS-02's failed part has status
      `killed` with the fallback as detail; N40-30's cross-check passes with exactly one owner declaration.
- [ ] The configuration switch matches the decision; with `baseline-only`, a test shows
      `frob nemesis search --method cross-entropy` refused with FROB-REF-724 naming the kill gate and
      `--method card-uniform` producing promoted failures that `nemesis.shrink` accepts.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a fresh-context session that did not implement the search.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/nemesis_kill_nemesis.sh` reproduces the outcome from the recorded receipts.
- [ ] Trigger, thresholds and fallback are unchanged from the text above; any change is a dated decision record
      before the measurement, not after.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/kill_nemesis_switch.rs`:
- `baseline_only_refuses_methods`: each removed method name is refused; `card-uniform` and `given` run.
- `fallback_pipeline_complete`: with the switch set, a planted trap of probability 10⁻² yields a classified,
  guarded failure that the shrinker accepts.
- `trigger_evaluator`: (first run bound 3.4, share 0.82) → `not-fired`; (2.1, 0.82) then repair then
  (2.6, 0.85) → `fired`; (4.0, 0.65) then (4.1, 0.66) → `fired` (the witness clause); (2.1, 0.82) with no
  second run yet → undecided (no outcome written); a void potency control → undecided; a third run after a
  second repair → refused unless a `deferred` ADR exists.
- `outcome_closed_set`: the evaluator can emit only the four outcomes of N40-03; `pass` and `killed` are gate
  statuses, never kill outcomes.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_kill_nemesis.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; log
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_kill_nemesis/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript):
1. Read GATE-NEMESIS-02's records and the registration record with `frob evidence trace <clm:…>` on the claim
   identifier each receipt carries (the verb takes a claim identifier, not a receipt hash); log each measured value (paired lower bound, solvable share, potency and A/A results, censored count) with
   its threshold and receipt hash.
2. Re-evaluate the trigger from those values and assert that the outcome equals the ADR's and the kill row's.
3. In a scratch configuration with `methods = baseline-only`: `frob nemesis search … --method cross-entropy
   --key kn-ce --json` → assert exit 5, FROB-REF-724, KILL-NEMESIS named; `--method card-uniform --first --key
   kn-cu` on the 10⁻² potency trap → assert a promoted failure; `frob nemesis shrink <world> --key kn-shr
   --json` on it → assert exit 0 or 2 (accepted). The three are run verbs and each carries its own key.
4. On the `fired` branch also assert `frob capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name and not the search.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-12`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time and the envelope's evidence block; on failure the first value that differs between the re-evaluation and
the record, with both receipt paths. A final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed, `gates:
["KILL-NEMESIS"]` and the outcome. N26a-11's fallback-branch step remains the measurement-side check.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative control for the procedure: the evaluator run on a planted record set in which both runs fail must
return `fired` (and GATE-NEMESIS-02's failed part status `killed`); a procedure that cannot return `fired` is not
a gate. The fallback is demonstrated by forcing
the switch.

## Considerations

- The threshold 3 was fixed before any data, with a ceiling under ten; a pass at 3.1 and a fail at 2.9 are both
  small effects on the cost of finding one failure, and the decision record says so.
- The gate measures planted traps. If real campaigns later show the search finding nothing randomization does
  not, the decision can be reopened by a new dated record; it is never reopened silently.
- BET-22's own fallback wording (importance-weighted tail sampling, with failing worlds reported unshrunk)
  covers the case where both this gate and the shrinker's kill gate fire.
