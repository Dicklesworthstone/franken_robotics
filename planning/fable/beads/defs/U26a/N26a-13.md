## Background

ADR-NEMESIS-02: only solvable worlds count. A world is a counterexample to a design only with a witness that the
candidate's own body, actuators and mission constraints could have satisfied the requirement in it. The
solvability oracle classifies every failing world into one of four classes before it counts:

| Class | Witness | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| `solvable-causal` | A controller that uses only the candidate's observation interface satisfies the requirement in this world, within every actuator and mission limit | A counterexample to the candidate's controller or policy |
| `plant-feasible` | A trajectory found with privileged state satisfies the requirement within the body's limits | A counterexample, tagged plant-feasible only: the body can do it; whether the sensors allow it is a separate question for the observation analyses |
| `not-excluded` | Necessary conditions pass and no trajectory was found | Unresolved. Reported, not counted either way |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition fails, with its dual vector or its bottleneck named | Not a counterexample. Counted in the scorecard's infeasible column and offered to the retrofit query |

This bead owns the last two rows' machinery: the necessary conditions. Two of the oracle's three rules bind it.
*An infeasibility verdict needs a checked necessary condition*: a failed search for a witness proves nothing and
yields `not-excluded`. *The oracle runs for the candidate's body, not for a generic point robot*: connectivity
of free space at a clearance radius certifies occupancy only, not steering, traction, reach or perception. The
counterexample that motivates both: a doorway narrower than the body defeats every controller; an oracle that
checks a point robot calls the world solvable and the design is blamed for an impossible trap.

Solid: each condition is an analysis that exists elsewhere (the clearance complex of the scene, the static
stability and actuator-envelope analyses, the energy-budget analysis); the work here is to pose them for one
candidate in one world and to check what they return.

## Scope

In: `oracle::necessary`: three condition families, their certificates, an independent check of each certificate,
the aggregation rule, the retrofit handoff.
Out: witness search (N26a-14); the classification pipeline and the verb `nemesis.oracle` (BD-271); the scorecard
column (BD-254); the retrofit search itself (BD-369).

## Technical approach

1. **A condition must be necessary for every admissible controller and trajectory.** A quasi-static screen is not
   one where dynamics could succeed. Worked case: a driven wheel of radius r = 50 mm meeting an edge of height
   h = 20 mm quasi-statically needs friction of at least √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h) = 4/3, which no floor offers; yet
   a body arriving at √(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the energy to rise 20 mm, and in one recorded planar study 62 of
   156 cases that a sampled static test rejected succeeded dynamically (FACT, source register, to be re-read).
   Every condition therefore declares its kind: `necessary` (may yield infeasibility) or `screen` (may only
   order work; a failing screen leaves the world `not-excluded`).
2. **Condition family 1: a route exists (necessary).** Query the clearance complex of the realized scene with the
   candidate's swept footprint family (every heading and every admissible body configuration, including the
   carried payload): the complex answers `fits` with a route witness, `cannot-fit` with a component cut (the set
   of cells separating start from goal, each too narrow for the footprint) or `unresolved`. `cannot-fit` yields
   a `Bottleneck { feature: FieldPath, required: Length, available: Length }`: for the narrow doorway, the door,
   body width 0.75 m, clear width 0.70 m. Checked by re-evaluating the cut: every cell of the cut is tested
   against the footprint by exact interval arithmetic, and the cut is verified to separate start from goal.
3. **Condition family 2: wrench and torque envelopes suffice (necessary only for events that must be met at
   rest or at a bounded speed).** For each mandatory task event whose requirement fixes the kinematic state
   (hold a payload at rest on a slope for T seconds; push a door whose closer exerts torque τ through a stated
   angle at a speed the task caps), pose the cone program: find contact forces in the friction cones and joint
   torques inside the actuator cards' torque-speed envelopes that produce the required wrench with the body in
   static or bounded-speed equilibrium, over every admissible stance. Infeasibility returns a separating dual
   vector y (a Farkas certificate: y·A = 0 on the equalities, y·b < 0 with the cone inequalities respected).
   Checked by substituting y in exact rational arithmetic. Events without a fixed kinematic state (a lip that
   may be taken at speed) are posed as `screen` only.
4. **Condition family 3: the energy budget closes (necessary).** A lower bound on mandatory work (the potential
   rise m·g·Δh of body and payload along any route; unavoidable rolling or sliding loss on the shortest route
   at the lowest loss coefficient inside the cards' uncertainty; work against fixtures that must be moved) is
   compared with the declared energy at full charge plus any approach kinetic energy the task admits. If the
   lower bound exceeds the available energy the condition fails with `EnergyBound { mandatory_j, available_j,
   terms }`. Checked by recomputing each term in interval arithmetic with outward rounding.
5. **Aggregation.**
   ```rust
   pub enum ConditionResult { Pass, Fail(Certificate), Unresolved { reason }, ScreenFail { reason } }
   pub fn necessary(world, candidate, task) -> NecessaryReport { results: Vec<(ConditionId, ConditionResult)> }
   ```
   The world is `infeasible-for-candidate` exactly when some `necessary` condition returns `Fail` **and** the
   independent check of its certificate accepts. A certificate the check rejects is a defect: the result is
   `Unresolved` and an integrity finding is logged. All `Pass` (with any `Unresolved` or `ScreenFail`) and no
   witness means `not-excluded`.
6. **For the class, too.** The same conditions evaluated over every body of the declared robot class give the
   scorecard's `infeasible-for-class` column: evidence that the scenario should leave a suite by a recorded
   decision, not by attrition.
7. **Retrofit handoff.** An infeasibility report names the building feature at fault (the door, the lip, the
   slope); it is the input the retrofit query takes to ask whether a cheap amendment to the place removes the
   bottleneck.
8. **Determinism and evidence.** Exact or interval arithmetic; class D0 for verdicts. An infeasibility verdict
   is a checked instance-level statement in the declared model; it is reported as `Tested` with its certificate
   attached unless and until the certificate kinds are admitted by the certificate checker, at which point the
   same bytes can be checked there.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The narrow doorway (body 0.75 m, door 0.70 m, legal) returns `Fail` with bottleneck `door`, required
      0.75 m, available 0.70 m; the same world with a point footprint returns `Pass` with a route (so a
      point-robot oracle is demonstrably wrong).
- [ ] Widening the door to 0.80 m returns `Pass` with a route witness.
- [ ] A payload held at rest on a slope with tan θ = 0.6 against a friction coefficient of at most 0.5 over the
      cards' uncertainty returns `Fail` with a dual vector that the exact check accepts; at tan θ = 0.4 it
      passes.
- [ ] A mission whose mandatory potential rise needs 1,200 J against 900 J available fails the energy
      condition with both figures; adding 400 J of admitted approach energy removes the failure.
- [ ] The wheel-and-lip case (r = 50 mm, h = 20 mm) is reported as `ScreenFail` and never as infeasible.
- [ ] A corrupted certificate (one coefficient changed) is rejected by the check and the world is `Unresolved`.
- [ ] On the 100 planted impossible traps the module returns a checked `Fail` for all 100 and on the 100
      solvable traps for none.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/oracle_necessary.rs`:
- `doorway_bottleneck`, `doorway_point_robot_passes`, `doorway_widened_passes`.
- `slope_hold_dual_vector`: the dual vector is checked in rational arithmetic; a perturbed vector fails.
- `energy_bound_terms`: body 20 kg and payload 5 kg raised 4 m: m·g·Δh = 25 × 9.81 × 4 = 981 J. With 900 J
  available: `Fail`. With 900 J plus 100 J of admitted approach energy (1,000 J): `Pass`. The boundary is
  asserted on both sides (980 J available fails, 982 J passes).
- `lip_is_screen_only`: required friction 4/3 for r = 50 mm, h = 20 mm; doubling the radius gives 0.75; a wedge
  with run twelve times its rise needs 1/12; all three values asserted, and the verdict is `ScreenFail`.
- `aggregation_rules`: tables of condition results against the expected class.
- `class_level`: a doorway narrower than every body of the class yields the class-level flag.
- `corrupted_certificate_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised through `frob nemesis oracle` by `scripts/e2e/nemesis_oracle.sh` (N26a-33) and by
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_legality.sh` for the legal-but-impossible fixtures; logged per world: each condition, its
kind, its result, the bottleneck or the dual vector's hash, the check's outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-660: a doorway narrower than the body is `infeasible-for-candidate` with the bottleneck and never a
counterexample; 0 of 100 planted impossible traps counted (GATE-NEMESIS-01); control: an oracle that checks a
point robot calls it solvable. CX-289: any world that defeats the design is a counterexample (it is not). The
closed forms √(h(2r − h))/(r − h) = 4/3, 0.75 and 1/12 are hand-checkable. Negative controls: a module that
treats `ScreenFail` as `Fail` (the lip test catches it); a module that reports `Fail` without running the check.

## Considerations

- A missing condition makes the oracle weaker, never wrong: the world falls to `not-excluded`. A wrong
  condition makes it wrong, which is why every certificate is rechecked by code that did not produce it.
- The clearance complex may answer `unresolved` near its resolution; that is `Unresolved` here, and the world
  goes to witness search.
- INV-NEMESIS-01 (no world without a `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible` witness enters the hard-world set or
  a counterexample count) is enforced downstream; this module's part is never to let an impossible world reach
  that count as solvable.
