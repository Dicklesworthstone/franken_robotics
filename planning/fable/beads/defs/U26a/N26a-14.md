## Background

ADR-NEMESIS-02: a world is a counterexample to a design only with a witness that the candidate's own body,
actuators and mission constraints could have satisfied the requirement in it. This bead finds the witness. There
are two kinds, and the difference is the point:

- `solvable-causal`: a controller that uses **only the candidate's observation interface** satisfies the
  requirement in this world, within every actuator and mission limit. Admitted sources: the baseline controller
  at other gains, another member of the policy family, or a sampling controller on the candidate's sensors. This
  is a counterexample to the candidate's controller or policy: the same body with the same sensors could have
  coped.
- `plant-feasible`: a trajectory found **with privileged state** satisfies the requirement within the body's
  limits. This is a counterexample tagged plant-feasible only: the body can do it; whether the sensors allow it
  is a separate question for the observation analyses (two worlds with the same observation history that need
  different actions cannot be told apart by any policy on those sensors).

The oracle's first rule binds this bead: a witness is itself a simulated run, so it passes the artifact guard. A
witness that succeeds through solver-created energy, or through traction that a linearized friction cone grants
along its diagonals, is not a witness. And the second rule: a failed search for a witness proves nothing; it
yields `not-excluded`, never infeasibility.

Solid: it reuses controllers that exist (the baseline controllers, the policy family, the sampling-based
predictive controller that is the program's fallback controller) and adds bookkeeping.

## Scope

In: `oracle::witness`: the ladder of witness sources, budgets, the causality check, the limit check, the guard
call, the witness record.
Out: the necessary conditions (N26a-13); the classification pipeline, the verb and the gate rows (BD-271); the
artifact guard itself (BD-272); the sampling controller (BD-252).

## Technical approach

1. **Order of work.** Necessary conditions first (cheap; a checked failure ends the oracle with
   `infeasible-for-candidate`). Then the causal ladder; then the privileged search; then `not-excluded`.
2. **The causal ladder (stop at the first success).**
   1. *Baseline at other gains.* The archetype's baseline controller over a fixed grid of its declared gains:
      speed scale {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, with the controller's other gains at their defaults, then the two
      neighbouring settings of each further declared gain (DESIGN; at most 9 runs).
   2. *Another member of the policy family.* If the candidate's policy belongs to a family (other checkpoints,
      other conditioning values), up to 8 members chosen by keyed draw.
   3. *Sampling controller on the candidate's sensors.* The sampling-based predictive controller, run through
      the causal-policy harness so that its state estimate comes from the candidate's sensor suite at the
      declared rates, delays and noise, with a horizon and sample count from its defaults; up to 4 restarts with
      different stream keys.
   Every run uses the failing world's address (same scene, same disturbances, same random streams); only the
   controller differs.
3. **Causality check.** A causal witness's controller may read only channels the candidate's observation
   interface delivers. The harness enforces it by construction (privileged channels are not wired); the record
   lists the channels read; a controller that reads a privileged channel is rejected as a causal witness and
   may at best serve the privileged search.
4. **Limit check.** Over the witness run: joint torques, speeds and currents inside the actuator cards; thermal
   states under their limits; every hard-limit atom of the task and every mission constraint satisfied; the
   requirement's verdict `satisfied` with margin above the boundary guard band. A run that satisfies the
   requirement while saturating beyond a card is not a witness.
5. **The privileged search.** If no causal witness is found: the sampling controller with privileged state
   (true state substituted for the estimate), then, where the task is short enough, a trajectory search over
   open-loop commands by the derivative-free optimizer on the margin. Budget as below. A success yields
   `plant-feasible`.
6. **Guard.** Each successful run is submitted to the artifact guard as a witness: replay one tier up (the
   witness must still satisfy the requirement), the four numerical variants (half step; twice the solver
   iterations; the alternative contact law; a tolerance ten times tighter), and the energy audit (no
   `energy-created`, `contact-active` or `cone-excess` flag beyond the envelope). A witness that fails the guard
   is discarded and the ladder continues; it is written to the fidelity ledger as a tier-sensitive case.
7. **Budget (DESIGN, TARGET).** Per world, the witness search may spend up to 8 times the simulated steps of the
   failing episode on the causal ladder and 8 times on the privileged search, charged to the arm that found the
   failure. On exhaustion without a witness: `not-excluded`, with the sources tried.
8. **Record.** `WitnessRecord { class, controller: ControllerId, source: Gains | FamilyMember | Sampling |
   Privileged, margin, trace: Blake3, channels_read, guard: GuardVerdict, steps }`; on the wire
   `"oracle": {"class": "solvable-causal", "controller": "c:77e1…", "margin": 0.18, "trace": "b3:a9d2…"}`.
   The witness run is stored and replayable.
9. **Determinism.** The ladder's order, grids and stream keys are fixed functions of the world identity and the
   candidate identity, so the oracle gives the same class on a rerun.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a planted solvable trap whose witness is the baseline at speed scale 0.5, the ladder returns
      `solvable-causal` with that controller, a positive margin and a trace digest; the record lists only
      candidate channels.
- [ ] On a world solvable only with privileged state (a planted observation alias: two worlds with identical
      sensor histories needing opposite turns), the result is `plant-feasible`, never `solvable-causal`.
- [ ] A witness that succeeds only under the box-linearized friction cone (a slope with tan θ = 0.6 at friction
      0.5, held only because the diagonal grants √2 × 0.5 = 0.707) is rejected by the guard and does not count.
- [ ] A witness that satisfies the requirement while exceeding a card's peak torque is rejected by the limit
      check.
- [ ] With no witness found within budget the result is `not-excluded` with the list of sources tried; no code
      path turns budget exhaustion into `infeasible-for-candidate`.
- [ ] On the 100 planted solvable traps at least 95 receive a witness (the witness row of GATE-NEMESIS-01,
      recorded by BD-271); on the 100 impossible traps none does.
- [ ] Rerunning the oracle on one world gives the same class and the same witness controller.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/oracle_witness.rs` (real controllers on the CPU reference tier; small scenes):
- `ladder_order_and_stop`: the first successful source ends the ladder; later sources are not run.
- `baseline_half_speed_witness`: grease patch crossed upright at speed scale 0.5 where nominal gain slips.
- `privileged_only_is_plant_feasible` (the alias fixture).
- `causality_enforced`: a controller reading a privileged channel is rejected as causal.
- `limit_violation_rejected`: peak torque 1.2 times the card's limit during an otherwise successful run.
- `guard_rejects_box_cone_witness`; `guard_rejects_energy_pumped_witness` (the penetration-recovery plant:
  1 kg, 1 mm removed in a 2 ms step gives 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event from nothing).
- `budget_exhaustion_is_not_excluded`.
- `same_address_as_failure`: every witness run's world address equals the failing episode's.
- `deterministic_class`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Through `frob nemesis oracle` in `scripts/e2e/nemesis_oracle.sh` (N26a-33): logged per world the sources tried in
order, steps spent per source, the witness class, controller identity, margin, channels read, the guard's
verdict per variant, and for `not-excluded` the reason.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-661: a planted solvable trap of known nominal probability is found with a causal witness attached; at least
95 of 100 planted solvable traps receive a witness (GATE-NEMESIS-01). KA-662's plants serve as false witnesses
here (box-cone traction; energy-pumping penetration recovery). INV-NEMESIS-05: a failure and its witness count
only after the artifact guard (the two guard tests). Negative controls: a ladder that skips the guard (the
false witnesses are then accepted; the tests must fail); a ladder that runs the witness at a fresh address
(the same-address test fails).

## Considerations

- The 95-of-100 threshold admits that a real witness is sometimes not found in budget; such worlds are reported
  `not-excluded` and are not counted either way, which costs yield, not correctness.
- A `plant-feasible` world is a weaker counterexample; reports never drop the tag, and the hard-world set
  records it.
- The witness shows that some controller copes, not that the candidate's controller is badly designed; whether
  the fix is the controller, the body or the place is what the shrunk world and the attribution tools address.
- If KILL-NEMESIS fires the witness search is unchanged: the oracle is one of the pieces the fallback keeps.
