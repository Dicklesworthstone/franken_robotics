## Background

GATE-NEMESIS-06 (known-answer claim; earliest milestone M3; TARGET): 100 of 100 planted artifact failures
labelled, and none in the hard-world set; at least 95 of 100 genuine planted failures survive promotion. The
guard has no verb of its own; a user meets it as a line in a search result ("all three reproduce on the
referee"), as a bucket of failures that were not counted, as a refusal to shrink an artifact candidate and as a
refusal to admit one to a hard-world set. This script drives those surfaces on the real binary with the planted
registry and records the gate.

The guard replays a failure three ways before it counts: one tier up (from the fast tier to the CPU reference
tier in double precision, and to the referee for anything bound for the hard-world set), under four numerical
variants (half the step; twice the solver iterations; the alternative contact law; a solver tolerance ten times
tighter), and against the energy ledger. A failure that vanishes under any of these is labelled
`artifact-candidate` with what removed it.

## Scope

In: the script, its assertions, logs and gate record.
Out: the guard (BD-272); the plants (N26a-15); unit tests.

## Technical approach

1. **Surfaces used.** `frob nemesis search <candidate> --task <task> --legality <profile> --method given
   --worlds <ndjson> --step-plan <plan> --json` evaluates given worlds and runs the standard pipeline (legality,
   evaluation, oracle, guard). `frob nemesis shrink <world>` and `frob nemesis hardset add <world>` are then
   tried on what the guard labelled. `frob fidelity ledger --query tier-sensitive` reads the ledger records.
2. **Steps.**
   1. `start`: build, registry digest, seeds, tiers available, hardware fingerprint.
   2. Artifact failures: submit the 100 planted artifact worlds under their artifact step plans. Assert for
      each: it appears under `not_counted.artifact_candidate`; its guard block has status `artifact-candidate`;
      `removed_by` contains at least the removals in the plant's `truth`; it is absent from `failures`.
   3. For 10 sampled artifact candidates: `frob nemesis shrink` → assert refusal `FROB-REF-564` (shrinking
      requested for a failure that does not replay, or that the guard labelled an artifact candidate), exit 3;
      `frob nemesis hardset add` → assert a refusal naming the guard check; then `frob nemesis hardset show` →
      assert none of the 100 identities is present.
   4. Assert one fidelity-ledger record per artifact candidate, with the tier pair or variant and both outcomes.
   5. Genuine failures: submit the 100 genuine worlds under the production step plan. Assert at least 95 appear
      in `failures` with guard status `passed` and all four variant tags, promoted on the tier requested.
   6. False witnesses: for the 20 false-witness plants run `frob nemesis oracle`; assert none yields
      `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible` on the strength of the planted run.
   7. Negative control of the script: rerun 10 artifact worlds against a build with the test feature
      `nemesis-guard-off`; assert the script's own assertion in step 2 fails (run in a subshell; the outer
      script requires the inner failure).
   8. `metric`: `artifacts_labelled = a of 100` against `100`; `artifacts_in_hardset = 0` against `0`;
      `genuine_survive = g of 100` against `≥ 95`. Write the gate record; `summary`.
3. **Logging.** `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_guard/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript, through the shared helper. Every
   line: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-16`), `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence
   block. Per world: plant id and kind; for each replay its tier or variant tag, verdict, failure class, binding
   atom and failure time; the energy readout (created energy as a share of absolute work in the worst 0.5 s
   window, with its threshold; the two companion flags); the guard status and removals; the expected removals.
   On failure: the first plant whose label or removal set differs from its truth, and the replay identities
   needed to reproduce. Summary: assertions passed and failed; `gates: ["GATE-NEMESIS-06"]`.
4. **Gate record.** The three measured counts, registry digest, commit, hardware fingerprint, seeds, the tiers
   used.
5. **Runtime.** 220 worlds × about 7 episodes each on small fixtures; minutes on the CPU. A device is not
   required: with no device, "the fast tier" is the CPU reference tier in single precision and the tier-up is
   the same tier in double precision, and the record says so.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 on a correct build; non-zero when any assertion fails.
- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-06 is recorded with measured values: artifacts labelled (TARGET 100 of 100), artifacts in the
      hard-world set (TARGET 0), genuine failures surviving promotion (TARGET at least 95 of 100).
- [ ] Shrinking an artifact candidate is refused with FROB-REF-564 and exit 3; adding one to a hard-world set is
      refused.
- [ ] Each artifact candidate has a fidelity-ledger record.
- [ ] The guard-off control makes the script's own check fail (the gate can be failed).
- [ ] The log holds the per-world fields listed above and a final summary line.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`scripts/e2e/tests/nemesis_guard_selftest.sh`: runs the script against the `nemesis-guard-off` build and
requires a non-zero exit with the first wrongly counted artifact named in the log; runs it against a build with
`nemesis-guard-reject-all` and requires the genuine-failure row to fail.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-662 (planted artifacts labelled `artifact-candidate` with the variant that removed each; a genuine planted
failure survives every variant). CX-295. The two self-test builds are the planted defects of the gate: a guard
that does nothing and a guard that rejects everything; each must fail one row. Determinism lanes: the script run
twice on one machine gives identical labels and removal sets; on the second platform the labels must agree and
the replay digests are compared per tier's determinism class.

## Considerations

- "None in the hard-world set" is asserted on a scratch set created by the script and removed at the end, not
  on a project's real set.
- The hard-world set's own admission rules have their own bead and script; this script only proves that the
  guard's label reaches them.
- If the alternative contact law is not yet available on the tier in use, the `law-2` variant is recorded
  `not run` and the gate cannot pass; the record says which variant is missing.
