## Background

Two questions about failures are routinely confused. *Where does this design fail?* is a search, and any method
that finds failures faster is welcome. *Is its failure probability below p⋆?* is a claim, and only estimators
with a valid finite-sample bound may make it. The simplest such estimator is also the benchmark every other must
beat: draw n episodes from the task's nominal randomization and count failures.

With no failure in n independent nominal episodes, p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) at confidence 1 − δ. This is exact, it is
ours (integer counts and outward rounding, no library needed), and it fixes the price of a claim by arithmetic.
With δ = 0.05:

| Target p⋆ | Nominal episodes with no failure | Time at an illustrative 400 episodes per second |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁻⁴ | 29,956 | 75 s |
| 10⁻⁵ | 299,572 | 12.5 min |
| 10⁻⁶ | 2,995,731 | 2.1 h |

Throughput is for the performance chapter to establish; the middle column does not move. Proving absence has a
fixed price that no sampler discounts without assuming where the failures live. Inside the blinded qualification
lane the level is that of the request (request i of a campaign gets α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]), so the tenth request
needs 76,959 clean episodes for 10⁻⁴ instead of 29,956.

What is not built, and why: extreme-value extrapolation (fitting a tail law and reading off a probability beyond
the data). Checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ takes the same nominal episodes that certify directly, so it buys
nothing that can be trusted.

Solid: exact binomial arithmetic. The bound's proof class is `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, never `Certified`,
which is reserved for certificates a checker accepted.

## Scope

In: `rare::zero_failure`: the required count, the bound reached, exact one-sided binomial bounds for k failures
at a fixed n, the cost table and budget check, the qualification-level variant, PO-NEMESIS-04.
Out: sequential stopping (N26a-22); what may be counted as a nominal episode (N26a-23); importance sampling
(BD-276); the decision policy and the verb (N26a-26, BD-276).

## Technical approach

1. **Required count.** n(p⋆, δ) = the least n with (1 − p⋆)ⁿ ≤ δ = ⌈ln δ / ln(1 − p⋆)⌉. Inputs are exact
   rationals (p⋆ = 1/10⁴, δ = 1/20). Computation: enclose ln δ / ln(1 − p⋆) by directed-rounding interval
   arithmetic at 128 bits or more; take the ceiling; certify the answer by enclosing (1 − p⋆)ⁿ and
   (1 − p⋆)ⁿ⁻¹ and checking the first is at most δ and the second is above δ; if an enclosure straddles δ,
   double the precision and repeat. For n up to 10⁵ the result is cross-checked by exact integer arithmetic
   (20·9999ⁿ ≤ 10000ⁿ).
2. **Two approximations that are not used for the count.** ln(1/δ)/p⋆ is 29,957.3 for the first row: rounded
   down it gives 29,957 and rounded up 29,958. Both are sufficient (they exceed the exact count, because
   −ln(1 − p) > p) and neither is the exact count. The construction card writes ⌈ln 20/p⋆⌉; the chapter's table
   uses the exact formula, and the table governs.
3. **Bound reached.** After n clean episodes: p_U(n, δ) = 1 − δ^(1/n), rounded outward (upward) to the printed
   precision. Reported with n, δ and the statement of scope.
4. **k failures at a fixed n (one-sided exact binomial).** Upper bound p_U(k, n, δ): the p solving
   Σ_{i=0..k} C(n, i)·pⁱ·(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ⁱ = δ. Lower bound p_L(k, n, δ): the p solving
   Σ_{i=k..n} C(n, i)·pⁱ·(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ⁱ = δ, and 0 when k = 0. Solved by bisection on the regularized incomplete
   beta function with outward rounding (the upper bound rounded up, the lower bound down). These are valid at a
   sample size fixed before sampling, not under optional stopping.
5. **Curtailment.** A zero-failure plan may stop at its first failure with the outcome "not certified by this
   plan": the probability of certifying when p > p⋆ is still (1 − p)ⁿ < δ, whatever happens after a failure.
   What a stopped plan may not do is issue an upper or lower bound from the truncated sample; that needs the
   sequential bound (N26a-22).
6. **Qualification level.** `required(p⋆, α, i)` uses δ = α/[i·(i + 1)]: α = 0.05, i = 10 gives δ = 0.05/110 and
   76,959 episodes at 10⁻⁴.
7. **Cost and the budget refusal.** `plan(p⋆, δ, tier, budget) -> CertPlan { n, seconds, steps }` reads episodes
   per second from the tier's estimate. If the episodes the claim needs at its level exceed the budget, the
   request is refused at admission with FROB-REF-760 ("the episodes a claim needs at its level exceed the
   budget; the bound reachable within budget is attached"): `observed` = episodes affordable n_b, `required` =
   n, and the attachment `reachable_bound = 1 − δ^(1/n_b)`. Ranked repairs: (1) raise the budget to the stated
   amount; (2) ask for the reachable bound instead; (3) certify a decade higher and report the target as
   `Estimated`. Exit code 4 (the budget family; nothing partial is published as a result).
8. **PO-NEMESIS-04.** Statement: n ≥ ln(1/δ)/p⋆ clean nominal episodes give P(no failure in n) < δ whenever
   p > p⋆, since (1 − p)ⁿ ≤ exp(−p·n). One-line proof on file in `docs/derivations/po-nemesis-04.md`. Class
   sought: a candidate theorem card; `Tested` by exact binomial arithmetic until a checker accepts it.
9. **Scope of every bound (printed with it).** The probability is under the task's nominal randomization, on the
   stated tier, for the frozen requirement. It is a statement about the model at that tier; it is never a claim
   about reality, and a bound obtained on a fast tier is provisional until promoted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `required(10⁻⁴, 0.05)`, `(10⁻⁵, 0.05)`, `(10⁻⁶, 0.05)` return 29,956, 299,572 and 2,995,731 exactly, each
      certified at n and n − 1.
- [ ] `required(10⁻⁴, 0.05, i = 10)` returns 76,959; for i = 1, 2, 5 it returns 36,887, 47,873 and 63,967.
- [ ] `p_U(29,956, 0.05)` ≤ 10⁻⁴ and `p_U(29,955, 0.05)` > 10⁻⁴ (outward rounding never flips the first).
- [ ] The one-sided exact upper bounds at n = 1,000 are 0.002991, 0.004735 and 0.006282 for k = 0, 1, 2 (to four
      significant figures, rounded up).
- [ ] Exact coverage of the upper bound, computed by enumeration (no sampling), is at least 0.95 on a grid of
      (n, p): for example 0.9599 at (1,000, 0.005), 0.9596 at (5,000, 0.001), 0.9510 at (300, 0.01).
- [ ] A request needing 299,572 episodes with budget for 100,000 is refused with FROB-REF-760, exit 4, and
      `reachable_bound` = 1 − 0.05^(1/100,000) = 2.996 × 10⁻⁵ (rounded up).
- [ ] The time table reproduces 75 s, 12.5 min and 2.1 h at 400 episodes per second, and 21 h for 10⁻⁶ at 40.
- [ ] The derivation of PO-NEMESIS-04 is on file and a property test checks the inequality on a grid.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/zero_failure.rs` (pure arithmetic):
- `required_counts_table` (the three values); `required_counts_qualification` (the four levels).
- `approximation_is_not_the_count`: ⌊ln 20/10⁻⁴⌋ = 29,957 and ⌈ln 20/10⁻⁴⌉ = 29,958 differ from 29,956; the
  function never returns either.
- `certified_at_n_and_n_minus_1` (interval enclosures); `exact_integer_crosscheck` for p⋆ = 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴.
- `clopper_pearson_values` (k = 0, 1, 2 at n = 1,000); `lower_bound_zero_at_k0`.
- `exact_coverage_grid`: enumeration over k for 50 (n, p) pairs; minimum coverage at least 0.95.
- `po_nemesis_04_property`: for 10⁴ random (p⋆, δ, p > p⋆): (1 − p)ⁿ < δ with n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p⋆⌉.
- `budget_refusal_760`; `curtailment_keeps_level`: a simulation-free check that P(certify) = (1 − p)ⁿ for the
  stopped plan.
- `outward_rounding_direction`: printed upper bounds are never below the enclosure's upper end.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_bound.sh` (N26a-30) runs `frob nemesis bound … --mode certify --estimate` and logs the
required count against the table, the time estimate, and the FROB-REF-760 refusal with its reachable bound.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-664: the zero-failure rule at δ = 0.05 for p⋆ = 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁶ gives 29,956, 299,572 and 2,995,731 nominal
episodes with no failure; the tenth qualification request needs 76,959 for 10⁻⁴; exact (count); control: an
episode count from ln(1/δ)/p⋆ rounded down. KA-138 (the same bound stated for the construction). CX-290 (a good
sampler discounts the price of proving absence: it does not). Negative controls: floating-point evaluation in
single precision (the n − 1 certification fails at 10⁻⁶); inward rounding of the printed bound. Determinism:
exact, class D0, identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NEMESIS-3 (the compute envelope for a 10⁻⁶ claim, which exceeds the one-hour job rule at the illustrated
  throughput; owner: project lead). Fail-closed default: certify to 10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`. The
  budget refusal implements the default: a 10⁻⁶ request beyond the envelope is refused with the reachable bound.
- The rule assumes independent nominal episodes and a failure predicate frozen before sampling; both are
  enforced where episodes are admitted (N26a-23), not here.
- A screening rung never buys a rare-failure bound: the cost table is why. At 3 × 10⁵ episodes for 10⁻⁵, a
  bound is on the order of one or two training runs.
