## Background

The zero-failure rule answers one question at one sample size: after n clean episodes, is p below p⋆? A real
run is messier. Failures may appear, in which case the claim should be refuted early instead of running to the
end; or the budget may run out, in which case the caller should get the bound reached so far. Looking at a
fixed-sample bound repeatedly and stopping when it looks good invalidates it. A **confidence sequence** is a
sequence of intervals valid at every stopping time simultaneously: the true rate stays inside all of them with
probability at least 1 − δ, so the run may stop whenever it likes.

FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-eproc/src/bernoulli.rs` provides exactly this for a Bernoulli rate
(`BernoulliMixtureCs::new(alpha)`, `observe(success)`, `interval()` returning a lower and an upper bound,
`log_e_value(p)`): with s events and f non-events, the likelihood is integrated against a fixed Beta(½, ½)
mixing distribution, E(p) = B(s + ½, f + ½) / (B(½, ½)·pˢ·(1 − p)ᶠ); under a fixed rate p this is a nonnegative
test martingale, and by Ville's inequality the set {p : E(p) < 1/α} covers at all times. It has a cap of 10⁶
observations (`MAX_BERNOULLI_SAMPLES`), after which it returns a sample-limit error. The mixing distribution is a
tuning choice, not a prior belief, and may not be chosen after seeing data.

The cap matters: a 10⁻⁶ claim needs about 3 × 10⁶ episodes. OPEN-NEMESIS-4 (the sample caps of 10⁶ in
`fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance execution; owner: airlock maintainer) has the
default: our own exact binomial bound for larger counts; upstream work item FD-FSIM-18 (lift or parameterize the
caps) is open and no milestone waits on it.

Solid: a published construction, used through the airlock, plus arithmetic of our own.

## Scope

In: `rare::sequential::NominalSequence`: feeding nominal outcomes to the confidence sequence; the three terminal
states; the plan for counts beyond the cap; checkpointing; the null self-test.
Out: admission of episodes (N26a-23); the zero-failure arithmetic and fixed-n bounds (N26a-21); bounded-weight
observations (BD-276, N26a-25); the policy that decides when to stop exploring (N26a-26).

## Technical approach

1. **Observations.** One bit per admitted nominal episode: 1 if the frozen failure predicate holds, 0 otherwise.
   Episodes are drawn in address order from the certification stream; outcomes are fed in batches. The mixture's
   value depends only on the counts, so the order inside a batch does not matter; stopping is evaluated at batch
   boundaries, which is a stopping rule like any other and keeps validity.
2. **The sequence.** Through the airlock: a two-sided confidence sequence at level δ (so each one-sided
   statement holds with error at most δ; using the two-sided set for both ends is conservative). After each
   batch: `interval() -> (lo, hi)`.
3. **Terminal states.**
   - `Certified`: hi < p⋆. The claim "p < p⋆ at confidence 1 − δ" is issued with n, the counts and hi.
   - `Refuted`: lo > p⋆. Issued with n, the counts and lo. For a refutation only failures that passed the
     artifact guard are counted (dropping a failure lowers the lower bound, so this is conservative); for a
     certification every failure on the stated tier is counted (conservative for the upper bound).
   - `Exhausted`: the budget ended first; the result carries the interval reached. It is a completed result
     with findings, not a refusal: the caller learns the bound that was reachable.
4. **How many clean episodes the sequence needs.** More than the zero-failure rule's 29,956 for 10⁻⁴, because it
   pays for validity at every time. `plan_sequential(p⋆, δ) -> n_clean` computes the first n with hi(0 events,
   n) < p⋆ by evaluating the mixture's upper bound for zero events (a one-dimensional root-find on
   B(½, n + ½)/(B(½, ½)·(1 − p)ⁿ) = 1/δ), and the verb's estimate prints both numbers side by side. The exact
   values are produced by the implementation and then pinned as regression values.
5. **Beyond the cap.** If the plan needs more than 10⁶ observations, the sequence is not used. Instead a
   **fixed-n exact design** from N26a-21 is committed before sampling: n declared in the request record, the
   zero-failure rule with curtailment at the first failure (outcome "not certified by this plan"), and, if
   failures occur, the one-sided exact binomial bounds at the declared n once all n episodes are in. No interval
   is issued from a truncated fixed-n sample. The result names the design used (`sequential` or `fixed-n`).
6. **Checkpoint.** State is the pair of counts plus the request's commitment hash; a killed job resumes from the
   ledger's recorded outcomes and reaches the same interval.
7. **Null self-test.** Before a bound is issued in a session, and in the test suite: 10⁴ synthetic Bernoulli
   streams at a known p, each run to a fixed horizon with stopping at the first exclusion of p; the share of
   streams whose sequence ever excludes p must be at most δ plus three binomial standard errors. A failed
   self-test is a fallback trigger of the decision policy (crude sampling by the fixed-n design alone).
8. **Evidence.** Class `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, scoped to tier, randomization and the frozen predicate;
   never `Certified`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On 10⁴ synthetic streams with p = 10⁻³ and horizon 20,000, the sequence excludes the true p at some time
      in at most 5% of streams (plus three standard errors: at most 565 of 10⁴).
- [ ] With zero events the upper bound falls below p⋆ at the planned n_clean and not one batch earlier; n_clean
      for (10⁻⁴, 0.05) is larger than 29,956 and is pinned once measured.
- [ ] A stream with true p = 10⁻² against p⋆ = 10⁻³ ends `Refuted` in at least 99% of 1,000 runs, and the mean
      stopping time is recorded.
- [ ] A budget below the plan ends `Exhausted` with the interval reached and exit 2 (a result with findings).
- [ ] A plan above 10⁶ observations switches to the fixed-n design, records it, and never calls the upstream
      sequence past its cap (a test drives the count to 10⁶ + 1 and asserts the switch, not an error).
- [ ] A killed and resumed run reaches the same interval as an uninterrupted one.
- [ ] Refutation counts only guard-passed failures; certification counts all failures (asserted on a stream
      with one artifact-labelled failure).

labelled `synthetic`):

- `zero_event_plan`: n_clean certified by evaluating hi at n_clean and at n_clean minus one batch.
- `refutes_planted_rate`; `exhausted_returns_interval`.
- `mixture_value_formula`: with s = 0, f = 10 and p = 0.1 the e-value B(½, 10½)/(B(½, ½)·0.9¹⁰) matches the
  sibling's `log_e_value` to 10⁻¹².
- `cap_switches_to_fixed_n`; `fixed_n_no_truncated_interval`.
- `resume_equals_uninterrupted`.
- `conservative_counting_rules`.
- `peeking_does_not_inflate`: the same stream checked after every observation and after every 1,024 gives
  nested decisions (the coarser rule never certifies earlier than the finer one could).

### End-to-end script and logging

(a task whose failure predicate is a keyed Bernoulli draw of known rate, labelled `synthetic`), logging per
batch n, events, lo, hi against p⋆, the design used and the terminal state.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-141 (on a fixture with a closed-form probability the crude and bounded-weight bounds bracket the truth in at
least 1 − δ of 10⁴ repetitions). The first row of GATE-NEMESIS-04 (over 10⁴ synthetic studies with known p the
95% upper bound covers p in at least 95%) is measured with this module and the fixed-n bounds. Negative
controls: a fixed-n Clopper–Pearson bound recomputed after every batch and stopped at the first success (its
error rate under the null exceeds δ; the anytime test must catch it); a sequence fed exploration episodes (the
admission layer refuses; asserted there). Determinism: decisions are functions of the counts; identical across
platforms given the same outcomes.

## Considerations

- The sequence is wider than a fixed-n bound at any single n; that is the price of stopping freely. When the
  caller knows it will run to a fixed n with no early look, the fixed-n design is cheaper and equally valid.
- When the upstream cap is lifted (FD-FSIM-18), the switch point moves; the fixed-n design stays as the
  fallback and as the cross-check.
- Independence: nominal episodes are independent because their randomness is keyed by distinct addresses; a
  repeated address would break the martingale property and is rejected at admission.
