## Background

ADR-NEMESIS-05: exploration and certification never share data. Search-selected, tilted, forked and
battery-selected episodes find failures. Only nominal draws, or draws with recorded bounded weights, enter a
bound. The reason is selection: an episode that was chosen because it looked likely to fail (or kept because it
did) is not a draw from the distribution the bound is about, and no arithmetic afterwards can undo that. The
decision model for rare failures names this as its primary risk, "exploration data leaking into the bound", and
gives the countermeasure: episodes carry their proposal law; the bound refuses any episode not drawn nominally
(INV-NEMESIS-03: exploration data never enter a certification bound).

The bound's obligation has three more clauses, each of which the firewall makes checkable: independent nominal
episodes; a failure predicate frozen before sampling; a scope fixed to one tier and one randomization. And for
importance sampling the admitted law is narrow: an explicit Gaussian law (in normal germs) with a proposal fixed
before the run, or fitted on a separate completed pilot whose data are never reused.

Solid, and deliberately boring: a commitment record, a typed accumulator and three refusals.

## Scope

In: the bound commitment; the type `AdmittedEpisode`; the admission rules; refusals FROB-REF-560, -563 and -568;
the property test of INV-NEMESIS-03.
Out: the estimators that consume admitted episodes (N26a-21, N26a-22, BD-276, N26a-25); the hard-world set's
discovery predicate (BD-275); the qualification lane's own refusal of discovery worlds (FROB-REF-567).

## Technical approach

1. **Commitment, before the first draw.** A bound request writes one record to the ledger with an idempotency
   key before any certification episode exists:
   ```json
   { "schema": "frob.nemesis.bound.commit/1", "candidate": {…}, "task": "t:…", "requirement": "delivered",
     "predicate": "pred:…", "target": "1/100000", "delta": "1/20", "tier": "batch-cpu",
     "randomization": "rand:…", "stream": "nemesis-certify", "address_range": {"run_seed": 77, "from": 0, "to": 400000},
     "proposal": null | {"id": "prop:…", "epsilon": "1/10", "fitted_on": "campaign:…"},
     "design": "sequential" | "fixed-n", "n_declared": 299572, "ledger_seq": "…" }
   ```
   `predicate` is the hash of the task identity, the requirement and the failure predicate. Everything a bound
   depends on is in the record, and the record precedes the data.
2. **The typed accumulator.**
   ```rust
   pub struct AdmittedEpisode { addr: WorldAddr, failed: bool, weight: Option<BoundedWeight>, guard: Option<GuardStatus> }
   impl BoundAccumulator { pub fn admit(&mut self, ep: &EpisodeRecord) -> Result<AdmittedEpisode, Refusal>; }
   ```
   `AdmittedEpisode` has no public constructor; estimators accept only this type. An exploration episode cannot
   reach an estimator by construction, and `admit` is the one place where the rules below are checked at run
   time (for episodes offered from records, for example `--episodes`).
3. **Admission rules (all must hold).**
   1. `ep.provenance.law` is `Nominal` on the committed randomization and stream, or `DefensiveMixture` with the
      committed proposal and a recorded weight w ≤ 1/ε. Anything else (`Search`, `Tilted`, `Fork`, `Given`,
      `BatterySelected`, `HardSet`) → FROB-REF-560.
   2. The address lies in the committed range and has not been admitted before (independence: one address, one
      observation) and does not appear in the discovery record (an address used by exploration is spent) →
      otherwise FROB-REF-560.
   3. `ep.provenance.predicate` equals the committed predicate → otherwise FROB-REF-568. A change to the task,
      an atom, a scale, a window or the failure definition after the commitment changes the predicate identity;
      the commitment cannot be amended, only superseded by a new bound with new episodes.
   4. `ep.provenance.tier` and numerical profile equal the committed ones (a bound is scoped to its tier).
   5. For bounded-weight episodes: the proposal was committed before the run; it is a Gaussian shift in normal
      germs (with declared monotone transforms to the physical quantities, or the discrete mixture of N26a-24);
      and `fitted_on`, if present, names a completed campaign none of whose episodes are in the committed
      range → otherwise FROB-REF-563.
   6. All episodes of the committed range that were drawn are admitted, failures and non-failures alike: a
      bound over a subset chosen after outcomes is refused (FROB-REF-560, "selected").
4. **Refusals.**
   | Code | Meaning | Ranked repairs | Exit |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | FROB-REF-560 | A bound was offered episodes that were not drawn nominally or with recorded bounded weights | Draw fresh nominal episodes under a new commitment; remove the offered set and let the verb sample; use the offered episodes for exploration only | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-563 | Importance sampling outside its admitted law, or with a proposal fitted on the run it is applied to | Declare the transform to normal germs; fit the proposal on a separate completed pilot and sample with a fresh key; fall back to crude nominal sampling | 3 |
   | FROB-REF-568 | The failure predicate changed after sampling began | Start a new bound under the new predicate with fresh episodes; restore the committed task identity | 3 |
   Each envelope names the first offending episode (`pointer`), its recorded law (`observed`) and the committed
   law (`required`), with `retry: after-change`.
5. **What the firewall also blocks in the other direction.** Certification episodes may be read by exploration
   (they are nominal draws and carry no secret), but a certification address that exploration then forks or
   tilts around is recorded as discovery from that moment and cannot be admitted to a later bound.
6. **Conservative counting.** For an upper bound every failure on the committed tier counts. For a lower bound
   (refutation) only failures that passed the artifact guard count. Both directions are conservative, and
   neither removes an episode from the sample.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-NEMESIS-03 as a property: over 10,000 random mixes of episode records, the accumulator admits exactly
      the nominal and committed bounded-weight episodes; no estimator in the crate has a function that accepts
      an `EpisodeRecord` (compile-level check by a public-API test).
- [ ] A tilted episode offered to the bound is refused with FROB-REF-560 (the verification item the decision
      model names); so are a search-selected, a forked, a battery-selected and a hard-set episode, each named in
      the refusal.
- [ ] A proposal whose `fitted_on` campaign overlaps the committed range is refused with FROB-REF-563; a
      non-Gaussian quantity with no declared transform is refused with FROB-REF-563.
- [ ] Changing one comparator of the requirement after the commitment and offering new episodes is refused with
      FROB-REF-568.
- [ ] An address admitted twice, an address outside the range and an address present in the discovery record
      are each refused.
- [ ] A subset of a drawn range (only its clean episodes) is refused as selected.
- [ ] The commitment's ledger sequence number is lower than that of every admitted episode.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/lane.rs`:
- `inv_nemesis_03_property` (as above); `no_estimator_takes_raw_records` (API surface test).
- `refuses_each_exploration_law` (five laws, five refusals with the law named).
- `refuses_reused_address`, `refuses_out_of_range`, `refuses_discovery_address`.
- `refuses_predicate_change_568`: commit, alter the window of one "always" clause from 2 s to 3 s, offer.
- `refuses_same_run_fit_563`, `refuses_undeclared_transform_563`, `refuses_weight_above_bound`: a recorded
  weight of 10.5 at ε = 1/10.
- `refuses_selected_subset`.
- `commit_precedes_data`.
- `conservative_counts`: with 3 failures of which 1 is an artifact candidate, the upper-bound path sees 3 and
  the lower-bound path sees 2.
- `certification_address_spent_by_fork`: fork a certification episode, then start a second bound over the same
  range: the forked address is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_bound.sh` (N26a-30) offers, through `frob nemesis bound … --episodes`, a tilted set, a
search set, a hard-set run and a changed predicate; logs for each the refusal code, the pointer, the observed
and required law, and that no estimate was produced.

### Known answers and negative controls
The third row of GATE-NEMESIS-04 (non-nominal episodes are refused) is this bead's. CX-290 (a good sampler
discounts the price of proving absence) and CX-294 (the hard-world set as nominal draws for a bound,
FROB-REF-560). Negative controls: an accumulator that checks only the verb that produced an episode, not its
law (a tilted episode routed through the nominal verb is then admitted; the property test must catch it); a
predicate identity that hashes the requirement's name only (the comparator change is then missed).
Determinism: exact; decisions depend only on records.

## Considerations

- The firewall protects a statistical claim from the program's own tools. An agent has every incentive to reuse
  expensive episodes; the typed accumulator makes the honest path the only path, and the refusals say what to
  do instead.
- A failed admission never leaves a partial bound: the accumulator is all-or-nothing per request.
- The same rule in the plan's doctrine (the two lanes never mix) appears in the qualification lane as its own
  firewall; the two are independent mechanisms and neither relies on the other.
- KILL-RARE removes splitting and leaves this bead unchanged; the fallback (crude nominal sampling with the
  binomial bound alone) depends on it.
