## Background

Bounded-weight importance sampling samples worlds from a proposal q that visits the failure region more often
than the nominal law p does, and corrects by the likelihood ratio w = p/q. The construction that keeps it honest
is the **defensive mixture**: q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p_shifted, so w = p/q is at most 1/ε whatever the shifted part
does, and ε·w·1[failure] is an observation in [0, 1] whose mean is ε·p.

What the sibling library provides (FACT, `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`): exactly this
construction for parameters that are independent or jointly Gaussian in normal germs (independent standard-normal
coordinates): q(z) = ε·φ(z) + (1 − ε)·φ(z − μ), configured by a germ shift vector and a defensive fraction, with
weights computed in germ space so that no physical covariance inverse is needed. Importance sampling beyond
Gaussian germs is an open upstream work item (FD-FSIM-17) on which nothing here waits.

What we add on our side of the airlock (DESIGN), and what this bead builds:

1. **Declared monotone transforms** from normal germs to non-Gaussian card distributions, so that a friction
   coefficient that is uniform on an interval or a mass that is lognormal can be searched and weighted in germ
   space.
2. **The same mixture for discrete scene edits** (a film present or absent, a cart in the aisle), where the
   weight bound holds for the same reason.

It is Solid: a change of variables and a one-line inequality. It is also shared: the search's continuous methods
use the same germs.

## Scope

In: `rare::transform` (the germ map and its inverse, per distribution family), `rare::mixture` (the joint
defensive mixture over continuous germs and discrete features; exact weights in log space; the sampler by
address).
Out: the estimator and confidence sequences on the weighted observations (BD-276, N26a-25); admission of the
episodes (N26a-23); fitting a shift (N26a-08).

## Technical approach

1. **Germ map.** For a randomized scalar X with continuous nominal distribution function F, the germ is
   z = Φ⁻¹(F(x)) and x = F⁻¹(Φ(z)). The map is strictly increasing, so events and orderings are preserved. Under
   the nominal law z is standard normal. Supported families and their maps:
   | Family | x from z |
   |---|---|
   | Normal(m, s) | m + s·z |
   | Lognormal(m, s) | exp(m + s·z) |
   | Uniform[a, b] | a + (b − a)·Φ(z) |
   | Truncated normal(m, s; a, b) | m + s·Φ⁻¹(Φ(α) + Φ(z)·(Φ(β) − Φ(α))), α = (a − m)/s, β = (b − m)/s |
   | Triangular(a, c, b) | F⁻¹(Φ(z)) with the closed-form inverse of the triangular distribution function |
   A transform is **declared**: each randomized field names its family in a table hashed into the proposal
   identity; a field with no declared transform cannot be importance-sampled (refusal FROB-REF-563 at
   admission).
2. **Why weights can live in germ space.** For a proposal defined on germs, the density ratio of the physical
   quantity equals the density ratio of the germ at the corresponding point, because the Jacobian of the
   monotone map multiplies numerator and denominator alike: p_X(x)/q_X(x) = φ(z)/q_Z(z). No Jacobian is ever
   evaluated.
3. **Correlated parameters.** Jointly Gaussian germs with a declared correlation are handled upstream (a
   Cholesky factor applied to independent germs; weights still in independent-germ space). Dependence that is
   not jointly Gaussian in germs is outside the admitted law.
4. **Discrete features.** For a discrete quantity E with nominal mass π(e) and a tilted mass π′(e) (for example
   film present: π = 1/100, π′ = 1/2), the factor's density ratio is r_E(e) = π′(e)/π(e). The tilted mass must
   be positive only where π is positive.
5. **The joint mixture (one mixture indicator per episode).** Let x = (z, e) collect all germs and discrete
   features, p(x) the nominal joint law (a product) and p′(x) the shifted joint law (germs shifted by μ, discrete
   features tilted). The proposal is q(x) = ε·p(x) + (1 − ε)·p′(x): per episode, by a keyed draw, the whole world
   is drawn from p with probability ε and from p′ otherwise. Then
   w(x) = p(x)/q(x) = 1 / (ε + (1 − ε)·R(x)),   R(x) = exp(μ·z − ‖μ‖²/2) · Π over discrete features of r_E(e),
   and 0 < w ≤ 1/ε for every x (PO-NEMESIS-02's weight bound: q ≥ ε·p pointwise). Mixing per coordinate instead
   of per episode would only give the bound (1/ε)ᵈ, which is useless; the per-episode indicator is the design.
6. **Numerics.** log R is accumulated in log space; w = exp(−logaddexp(ln ε, ln(1 − ε) + log R)); the scaled
   observation y = ε·w is computed directly as exp(−logaddexp(0, ln((1 − ε)/ε) + log R)) and lies in (0, 1].
   Each episode records `log_R`, `w`, `y`, ε and the proposal identity. Because every draw is addressed, a
   disturbance or parameter drawn from a tilted proposal has an importance weight that is computed, not
   estimated.
7. **Sampler.** `MixtureSampler::draw(addr) -> (World, BoundedWeight)`: the mixture indicator, the germs and the
   discrete draws come from registered streams keyed by the world address; the world is realized through the
   germ maps and the scene generator; legality is checked on the final world. If the legality set truncates the
   nominal law (a legal range narrower than the card's support), the nominal law itself is the truncated one,
   stated in the randomization identity, and both p and p′ are truncated to the same set so the weight formula
   is unchanged up to a recorded normalizing constant.
8. **Unbiasedness (PO-NEMESIS-02, second half).** E_q[w·1[failure]] = ∫ 1[failure]·p = the nominal failure
   probability, for any μ and any tilt with the support condition; tested against closed-form tails.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For each family, 10⁵ germ draws mapped to x pass a goodness-of-fit test against the family at level 0.001,
      and F(x) = Φ(z) holds to 10⁻¹² at 1,000 points (round trip).
- [ ] Every recorded weight is at most 1/ε: with ε = 1/10 and shift 4 the maximum over 10⁶ draws is at most 10,
      and the closed-form values are reproduced: w = 10.000 as z → −∞ (9.9699 at z = 0), w = 1 at z = 2,
      w = 3.727 × 10⁻⁴ at z = 4.
- [ ] The Gaussian tail P(z > 4) = 3.167 × 10⁻⁵ is recovered by the weighted mean with shift 4 and ε = 1/10:
      over 100 replications of 10⁵ draws the mean of the estimates is within three standard errors of the truth.
- [ ] A lognormal mass and a uniform friction coefficient, each with a tail event of known probability
      (P(X > F⁻¹(1 − 10⁻⁴)) = 10⁻⁴), are recovered through the germ map with shifts fitted in germ space.
- [ ] A discrete feature with π = 1/100 tilted to 1/2, with failure exactly when the feature is present,
      recovers 0.01; with a joint event (feature present and z > 2.3263) it recovers 10⁻⁴.
- [ ] With per-coordinate mixing switched on (test feature) the maximum weight in d = 3 exceeds 1/ε and the
      weight-bound test fails.
- [ ] A field with no declared transform is refused for importance sampling (FROB-REF-563, through N26a-23).

sampler replays bit for bit on one backend.

## Considerations

- A defensive mixture protects against a failure mode the shift misses, at a price: a mode reachable only
  through the nominal share is sampled at ε times the nominal rate, so an estimate can cost up to 1/ε times
  crude sampling. ε is therefore a declared trade (default 1/10, TARGET), not a tuning knob to minimize.
- Transforms are exact changes of variables only for the declared family; if a card's distribution is revised
  the proposal identity changes and old weights are void.
- The scope of the admitted law is narrow and is stated in every result: independent or jointly Gaussian germs
  with declared monotone transforms, product discrete features, one mixture indicator per episode.
