## Background

Under a defensive mixture with nominal share ε, every importance weight w is at most 1/ε, so the scaled
observation y = ε·w·1[failure] lies in [0, 1] and its mean is ε·p, with p the nominal failure probability. A
confidence sequence on that mean, divided by ε, bounds p at every stopping time. The sibling library feeds y to a
sequence that uses only the range of the observation (FACT, `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`
with `fs-eproc`'s Gaussian-mixture sequence), and says in its own words that this is much wider than the
descriptive error for rare events. That is to be expected: y is zero in almost every episode and tiny when it is
not, so its variance is orders of magnitude below what a range of one allows.

DESIGN: we add a **variance-adaptive betting sequence** on the scaled observation. A betting sequence tests a
hypothesis about a mean by wagering against it; the wealth of a fair bettor is a nonnegative martingale under the
hypothesis, so by Ville's inequality large wealth is evidence against it at any stopping time. The bet size may
depend on the past in any way without affecting validity, and choosing it from the observed variance is what
makes the sequence tight for low-variance data.

The purpose is narrow. With weights bounded by 1/ε, the upper bound after n runs without a failure cannot fall
below about ln(1/δ)/(ε·n), so importance sampling is never the cheaper way to certify absence. It earns its place
in two cases: **refuting** a target, by a lower confidence bound that exceeds p⋆, and **tightening an estimate**
once failures have been observed. This bead serves those two.

Solid: validity is a one-paragraph martingale argument; only power depends on tuning.

## Scope

In: `rare::betting`: the one-sided lower test, the one-sided upper test, the two-sided sequence on a grid, the
predictable bet rule, numerics, the null self-test.
Out: producing the observations (N26a-24); the Bernoulli sequence for crude sampling (N26a-22); the verb and its
modes (BD-276).

## Technical approach

1. **Setting.** Observations y₁, y₂, … in [0, 1], each with conditional mean m given the past (here m = ε·p,
   constant). Targets are stated on the m scale: m₀ = ε·p⋆.
2. **Lower test (refutation: evidence that m > m₀).** Wealth K⁺_t = Π over i ≤ t of (1 + λᵢ·(yᵢ − m₀)), with
   λᵢ ∈ [0, c/m₀] chosen from y₁ … yᵢ₋₁ only (c = ½, TARGET, keeps every factor at least ½). If the true mean
   is at most m₀, K⁺ is a nonnegative supermartingale with K⁺₀ = 1, so P(K⁺ ever reaches 1/δ) ≤ δ. The target
   is refuted the first time K⁺_t ≥ 1/δ. The lower confidence bound at time t is the largest m₀ on the grid not
   yet rejected at level δ.
3. **Upper test (evidence that m < m₁).** Wealth K⁻_t = Π (1 − λᵢ·(yᵢ − m₁)), λᵢ ∈ [0, c′/(1 − m₁)], with
   c′ = 1 − 2⁻¹⁰ so a factor never reaches zero. Rejected when K⁻_t ≥ 1/δ.
4. **Bet rule (variance-adaptive, predictable; DESIGN).** With running estimates from the past observations and
   one prior pseudo-observation (mean m₀, variance ¼):
   λᵢ = clip( (μ̂ᵢ₋₁ − m₀) / (σ̂²ᵢ₋₁ + (μ̂ᵢ₋₁ − m₀)²), 0, c/m₀ )   for the lower test,
   and the mirror image for the upper test. This approximates the bet that maximizes expected log-wealth for a
   distribution with the estimated mean and variance. Any predictable rule is valid; this one only affects how
   fast the wealth grows.
5. **Two-sided sequence for estimates.** A fixed grid of candidate means, log-spaced from 10⁻¹² to 1 (64 points
   per decade, TARGET; integer powers of ten are grid points), each with its own pair of wealth processes at level
   δ/2. The reported interval is [ℓ, u], where ℓ is the largest grid point that is rejected together with every
   grid point below it (0 if none is), and u is the smallest grid point that is rejected together with every grid
   point above it. Both ends are rejected points, not the outermost unrejected ones, so the interval is always a
   superset of the un-gridded confidence set and conservative by at most one grid step on each side; reporting
   the outermost unrejected points instead would cut off part of the true set and is the negative control of
   `grid_conservative`. Divided by ε it is the interval for p.
6. **The zero-failure sanity value.** With y = 0 for n episodes and the maximal upper-test bet, the wealth is
   (1 + c′·m₁/(1 − m₁))ⁿ, which is (1 − m₁)⁻ⁿ as c′ → 1, so m₁ is rejected once (1 − m₁)ⁿ ≤ δ: the upper
   bound on m is about
   1 − δ^(1/n), the binomial zero-failure bound, and the bound on p is that divided by ε, about
   ln(1/δ)/(ε·n). At ε = 1/10 the 29,956 clean episodes that certify 10⁻⁴ by crude sampling certify only 10⁻³
   here. The module asserts this floor in its tests so that nobody expects importance sampling to certify
   absence cheaply.
7. **Numerics.** Log-wealth is accumulated as a sum of log1p terms in double precision with compensated
   summation; factors are bounded away from zero by the clips; thresholds are compared on the log scale.
   Outputs are rounded outward.
8. **Null self-test.** For the session and in the test suite: 10⁴ synthetic streams with a known mean; the share
   in which the true mean is ever rejected must be at most δ plus three standard errors. A failed self-test is a
   fallback trigger of the decision policy (N26a-26).
9. **Evidence.** A bound from this sequence is `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ under the admitted law (N26a-23,
   N26a-24). The point estimate printed beside it (the weighted mean divided by ε) is descriptive.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Validity under the null: on 10⁴ streams of the scaled Gaussian-tail observation (shift 4, ε = 1/10, true
      m = 3.167 × 10⁻⁶), the true mean is rejected by the lower test at some time in at most 5% of streams (plus
      three standard errors), and likewise by the upper test.
- [ ] Validity for an adversarial bounded distribution: the same test passes for Bernoulli(m) observations and
      for a two-point distribution on {0, 1} with m = ½.
- [ ] Power: on the Gaussian-tail fixture with p⋆ = 10⁻⁵ (true p = 3.167 × 10⁻⁵, above the target), the lower
      test refutes within 5,000 proposal draws in at least 95 of 100 runs; crude nominal sampling needs on the
      order of 10⁵ episodes to see even a few failures. The measured median stopping time is recorded.
- [ ] Width: at n = 10⁵ on the same fixture the two-sided interval for p is narrower than the range-based
      sequence's by at least a factor of 5 (measured; the factor is recorded).
- [ ] The zero-failure floor: with n = 29,956 zero observations, ε = 1/10 and the one-sided upper test at level
      δ = 0.05, the upper bound on p is between 9.9 × 10⁻⁴ and 1.1 × 10⁻³. With the maximal bet of step 6 it is the
      grid point 1.037 × 10⁻³ (one grid step above (1 − δ^(1/n))/ε = 9.99994 × 10⁻⁴; c′ < 1 keeps the grid point at
      exactly 10⁻³ unrejected); with the adaptive rule of step 4 it is at most two grid steps higher (about
      1.075 × 10⁻³ under the reading of step 4 used in the design check) and is pinned as a regression value once
      measured. The two-sided interval at δ/2 per side has a higher upper end (about 1.29 × 10⁻³) and is not
      what this criterion measures.
- [ ] Bets are predictable: a test that perturbs observation i shows λᵢ unchanged and λᵢ₊₁ changed.
- [ ] The gridded interval always contains the interval of a 4-times-finer grid (conservative by construction).

  1.792.
- `bet_is_predictable`; `bet_clipped_to_range`: every factor in [½, ∞) for the lower test.

  with the maximal bet: the bound is within one grid step above the binomial zero-failure bound 1 − δ^(1/n) =
  9.99994 × 10⁻⁵ at n = 29,956, i.e. the grid point 1.037 × 10⁻⁴).
- `narrower_than_range_based`: the same observations fed to the sibling's range-based sequence through the
  airlock.
- `grid_conservative`: on 1,000 random streams the gridded interval contains the interval of a 4-times-finer grid;
  the planted variant that reports the outermost unrejected grid points fails on the zero-failure stream of the
  floor criterion with the maximal bet: it reports 10⁻⁴ on the m scale, while the un-gridded upper end lies
  strictly between 10⁻⁴ and the next grid point 1.037 × 10⁻⁴.
- `outward_rounding`.
- `self_test_trips_on_broken_bets`: a bet rule that peeks at the current observation fails the self-test.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_bound.sh` (N26a-30): `frob nemesis bound --mode refute` and `--mode estimate` on the
Gaussian-tail fixture; logged per batch: n, log-wealth of the lower test against ln(1/δ), the interval for p
against the truth, the range-based interval for comparison, the self-test outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-665 (the Gaussian tail 3.167 × 10⁻⁵ recovered inside the confidence sequence with shift 4 and ε = 0.1).
KA-139 (zero observed failures under a mixture certify only p ≤ ln(1/δ)/(ε·n), never better than crude sampling
on the nominal share). CX-065 (a defensive mixture does not make certifying absence cheaper). Negative controls:
a non-predictable bet (caught by the self-test); a sequence that divides by ε before clipping to [0, 1] (the
observation then leaves the unit interval and validity is lost; the range assertion catches it). Determinism:
the sequence is a deterministic function of the observation order; observations are fed in address order.

## Considerations

- A betting sequence is valid for any distribution on [0, 1]; it needs no Gaussian assumption on y. The Gaussian
  assumption of the admitted law concerns the germs and the weights' exactness, not this module.
- If the proposal misses a failure mode, y is zero more often than it should be for that mode, but the nominal
  share ε still samples it: the bound stays valid and becomes wide. Validity is never traded for width.
- The grid's lower end (10⁻¹²) bounds the smallest reportable mean; below it the result says so.
- If KILL-RARE fires this module is unaffected: bounded-weight estimation is Solid and stays; only splitting is
  removed.
