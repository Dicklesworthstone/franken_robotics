## Background

Asking for a bound on a failure rate is a decision problem with a budget. Exploration (splitting, tilting) finds
failures cheaply and has no bound on its error; nominal sampling certifies and has a fixed price. Spending the
whole budget on exploration yields no claim; spending it all on nominal sampling wastes most of it when the
design fails often enough to be refuted in minutes. The program settles this once, as a **fixed table, not an
adaptive controller**, so that the rule which produced a verdict can be named and replayed.

The decision model, as the program states it:

```text
Decision:          Is the failure probability of finalist F on task T, at tier X, below p⋆?
Policy:            1. Explore to 20% of the budget, or until the unseen-mode mass is at most 0.05.
                   2. If a bounded-weight or nominal lower bound exceeds p⋆: stop, refuted.
                   3. Spend the rest on nominal episodes under the sequential binomial bound.
                   4. End certified (upper bound below p⋆), refuted, or exhausted with the bound reached.
Applies to:        Finalists only. A screening rung never buys a rare-failure bound.
Obligation:        The bound holds at confidence 1 - delta for independent nominal episodes with a failure
                   predicate frozen before sampling, scoped to its tier and its randomization.
Primary risk:      Exploration data leaking into the bound. Countermeasure: episodes carry their proposal
                   law; the bound refuses any episode not drawn nominally (INV-NEMESIS-03).
Failure modes:     An uninformative distress score; forks that carry no fresh randomness.
Fallback:          Crude nominal sampling alone.
Fallback trigger:  Under 1% of forks reach the next level; a failed null self-test of the bound.
Budgeted mode:     Exploration at most 20% of the budget; then a refusal that carries the bound reached.
Baseline:          Crude nominal sampling with the zero-failure rule at the same budget.
Rollback:          A configuration flag to the baseline; bounds already issued stay valid.
Verification:      Coverage of the bound on synthetic studies with known p; planted two-mode failure found;
                   a tilted episode offered to the bound is refused. Shadow first, then default.
```

States: the finalist's failure probability is below the target p⋆ or not. Actions: explore, draw nominal
episodes, stop and certify, stop and refute, stop exhausted. Loss: a false certification costs far more than a
false refutation, which costs more than wasted episodes. This is decision core DC-04 (family: rare-event
simulation; owner `frob-nemesis`; timescale: per finalist; Solid for the bound, Frontier for splitting).

## Technical approach

1. **States and transitions (the whole policy).**
   | State | Does | Leaves when | To |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `Explore` | Splitting where per-step disturbances exist and the distress score is informative; otherwise tilting; failures filed in the catalogue | 20% of the budget is spent, or the Good–Turing unseen-mode mass is at most 0.05 | `RefuteCheck` |
   | `RefuteCheck` | If exploration produced a proposal: a bounded-weight run in mode `refute` on fresh addresses, within a share of the remaining budget (one quarter, TARGET). Else: skipped | The lower bound exceeds p⋆ → `Refuted`; the share is spent → `Nominal` | `Refuted` or `Nominal` |
   | `Nominal` | Nominal episodes under the design that N26a-22's `choose_design` commits on entry, with n_available the remaining nominal share: the sequential binomial bound when its clean count fits the share and the upstream cap of 10⁶, otherwise the fixed-n exact design run to n_declared = `required(p⋆, δ)` (not curtailed) | Sequential: upper bound below p⋆ → `Certified`; lower bound above p⋆ → `Refuted`; budget ends → `Exhausted`. Fixed-n, at n_declared: no failure → `Certified`; exact lower bound above p⋆ → `Refuted`; otherwise `Exhausted` with the exact interval | terminal |
   Terminal results: `Certified`, `Refuted`, `Exhausted` (with the interval reached). Exploration episodes never
   enter either bound; the proposal used in `RefuteCheck` is fitted on exploration and applied to fresh addresses
   only.
2. **Constants (DESIGN defaults, recorded in the policy record; changing one is a new policy version).**
   Exploration cap 20% of budget; unseen-mass threshold 0.05; refute-check share one quarter of the remainder;
   δ from the request; fork-informativeness floor 1%.
3. **Admission.**
   - *Finalists only.* The candidate must be a finalist of a recorded campaign, or the request must carry an
     explicit attestation `--finalist` that is itself recorded. A request made from inside a screening rung is
     refused, exit 3. The plan names no code for this; the slug `rare-bound-not-finalist` (family `5xx`) is a
     row of the allocation ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (N35x-02, listed in N26-REF's table),
     numbered by the ledger's M0 run before this bead lands, and the `nemesis.bound` row lists it under that
     number. This bead raises it under the assigned number from the start, held in one named constant read from
     the ledger by slug (never a literal at the raise site), with the slug as the refusal's `slug`, the pointer
     at the candidate and the repairs "bound a finalist of a recorded campaign; or attest with `--finalist`";
     tests compare the code with the `assigned` field of the slug's ledger row, never with a typed number.
     N26b-14's script pins this behaviour from the user's side.
   - *Budget.* If the nominal episodes the claim needs at its level exceed the nominal share of the budget, the
     request is refused at admission with FROB-REF-760 and the bound reachable within budget attached (exit 4),
     unless the mode is `refute` or `estimate`. The nominal share is that of the acting policy: 80% under
     `table` (exploration may take 20%), 100% under `baseline` and in `shadow` (where the baseline acts). The
     required count and the reachable bound 1 − δ^(1/n_b) are computed by N26a-21's functions with n_b the
     nominal share; the record names which share was used.
   - *Envelope.* A 10⁻⁶ claim beyond the job-length rule is refused the same way, with the standing default:
     certify to 10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.
4. **Fallback (crude nominal sampling alone), and its triggers.** (a) Under 1% of forks reach the next level in
   the first exploration round: the distress score is uninformative. (b) Forks carry no fresh randomness
   (splitting refuses with FROB-REF-569). (c) A failed null self-test of the bound in this session. On any
   trigger the policy skips `Explore` and `RefuteCheck`, runs `Nominal` with the full budget, and records the
   trigger.
5. **Baseline and rollback.** The baseline is crude nominal sampling with the zero-failure rule at the same
   budget: the `Nominal` state alone with the whole budget as its share, so its design is also chosen by
   `choose_design` (fixed-n at `required(p⋆, δ)` when the budget does not afford the sequence's clean count).
   A configuration flag `nemesis.bound.policy = table | baseline` selects it; bounds already issued stay
   valid, since they never depended on exploration. Independently, the policy reads BD-277's switch
   `nemesis.rare.exploration`: with `off` (set by N26a-32 if KILL-RARE fires) the table has no `Explore` state,
   `RefuteCheck` has no exploration proposal and is skipped, and `Nominal` runs with the whole budget; the
   policy record is re-issued as a new version saying so.
6. **Shadow first, then default.** Adoption status `shadow`: the baseline acts; the table's decisions are computed
   and logged beside the baseline's and act on nothing. Status moves to `default` by a recorded decision after
   GATE-NEMESIS-04 and GATE-NEMESIS-05 are recorded and the shadow comparison has been read.
7. **Evidence record per decision.** At every transition: the policy identifier and version, the identities read
   (candidate, task, tier, randomization, predicate), the budget spent, the unseen-mass value, fork statistics,
   the bounds, the action taken, and in shadow the baseline's action. Written to the ledger and referenced from
   the bound's result.
8. **Policy record for the registry.** `registries/cores/dc-04.toml`: decision, owner, timescale, family, the
   table above as `frob.core-table/1` with version and hash, the loss ordering, proof obligations (PO-NEMESIS-02,
   PO-NEMESIS-04, INV-NEMESIS-03), fallback and its three triggers, budgeted mode, adoption status, a replay
   scenario and a demonstration manifest that runs in under 60 s. The certification count the policy's
   admission check uses is the exact binomial one, ⌈ln δ / ln(1 − p⋆)⌉ (29,956 at δ = 0.05, p⋆ = 10⁻⁴); the
   table form ⌈ln(1/δ)/p⋆⌉ (29,958) is the registered upper form that N28a-27 adds to the record at M4 beside
   it, and both are printed there. Selected episodes are refused at the bound with FROB-REF-560 (N26a-23);
   FROB-REF-549 is the cascade's code for the same rule one layer up.
9. **Interference rule with the battery selector.** Bounds use nominal draws only; a bound refuses
   battery-selected episodes. Failures found here flow one way: to attribution, then to the incidence matrix of
   scene selection; nothing flows back inside a campaign.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a synthetic finalist with true p = 10⁻² against p⋆ = 10⁻³ and a budget whose nominal share affords the
      sequence's clean count (10,078 episodes: 80% is 8,062, the planning value of `plan_sequential(10⁻³, 0.05)`),
      the policy commits the `sequential` design and ends `Refuted` using less than 30% of the budget in at least
      95 of 100 seeded runs (the sequence refutes near 500 nominal episodes at this rate). With true p = 10⁻⁷
      against p⋆ = 10⁻⁴ it ends `Certified` in at least 95 of 100 seeded runs both with a budget of 37,445
      (nominal share 29,956: design `fixed-n`, certified at 29,956 nominal episodes) and with a budget of 150,000
      (design `sequential`, certified at the first batch boundary at or after 92,872). With a budget of half the
      required nominal episodes it is refused at admission with FROB-REF-760 and the reachable bound.
- [ ] The committed design and n_declared are in the commitment record and in the result, and equal
      `choose_design`'s answer for the remaining nominal share at the moment `Nominal` was entered.
- [ ] Exploration never exceeds 20% of the budget (asserted from the budget ledger), and stops earlier when the
      unseen mass reaches 0.05.
- [ ] No exploration episode is among the episodes of either bound (asserted from the admitted-episode list).
- [ ] Each of the three fallback triggers, forced in a test, sends the policy to nominal sampling alone and is
      recorded.
- [ ] The rollback flag selects the baseline; a bound issued before the flag changed still validates.
- [ ] In shadow, the table's action and the baseline's action are both in every evidence record and only the
      baseline's is executed.
- [ ] A non-finalist request is refused; a finalist request is admitted.
- [ ] The compiled table regenerates bit-identically from the policy record; the demonstration manifest runs in
      under 60 s.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/policy.rs` (estimators real; episode outcomes from `synthetic` rate fixtures):
- `transitions_table`: each row of the table driven by scripted inputs (budget spent 19% and 20%; unseen mass
  0.06 and 0.05; lower bound just below and just above p⋆).
- `refuted_early`, `certified_clean`, `exhausted_returns_interval`, `refused_760_at_admission`.
- `design_by_share`: the two certification budgets of the acceptance criterion commit `fixed-n` and `sequential`
  respectively; a 10⁻⁵ target with any budget commits `fixed-n` (the sequence's 1,050,021 exceeds the cap); a
  fixed-n plan that sees failures runs to n_declared and reports the exact interval, never a truncated one.
- `admission_share_by_policy`: a request needing 90% of the budget in nominal episodes is refused under `table`
  and admitted under `baseline` and in `shadow`; the reachable bound attached under `table` uses 80% of the
  budget.
- `non_finalist_slot_name`: the refusal's code equals the `assigned` field of the ledger row of slug
  `rare-bound-not-finalist` (read by slug, never typed), with exit 3, that slug and the pointer; the same request
  with `--finalist` is admitted.
- `exploration_cap`; `no_exploration_in_bounds`.
- `fallback_on_uninformative_score` (0.5% of forks reach the next level), `fallback_on_no_fresh_randomness`,
  `fallback_on_failed_self_test`.
- `rollback_flag`; `shadow_logs_both_actions`.
- `exploration_off_is_nominal_only`: with `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` the transition log has no `Explore` or
  `RefuteCheck` state, the admission share is 100%, and the bound equals the baseline's on the same addresses.
- `finalists_only`.
- `table_regenerates_bit_identical`.
- `loss_ordering_documented`: the policy record lists the three costs in order (a metadata test).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_bound.sh` (N26a-30) runs the policy end to end on three synthetic finalists and logs every
transition with its evidence record: state, budget share, unseen mass, fork fraction, bounds against p⋆, action,
baseline action in shadow, trigger if any.

### Known answers and negative controls
The decision model's own verification list: coverage of the bound on synthetic studies with known p (through
BD-276's gate); a planted two-mode failure found (through BD-277's gate); a tilted episode offered to the bound
is refused (through N26a-23). KA-664 (the certification cost table drives the admission check). Negative
controls: a policy that lets `RefuteCheck` reuse exploration addresses (the admitted-list test must catch it); a
policy with an adaptive exploration share (the cap test fails). Determinism: the policy is a deterministic
function of its inputs; a replayed campaign makes the same transitions.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NEMESIS-3: the compute envelope for a 10⁻⁶ claim (owner: project lead). Default as in step 3. If
  throughput is ten times lower than the illustrated 400 episodes per second, the 10⁻⁶ claim costs 21 hours.
- The ceiling of the whole exploration step, stated in advance: a clean bound at 10⁻⁵ costs about 3 × 10⁵
  episodes whatever the explorer does, so a perfect explorer saves at most about a factor of three on a finalist
  (the refutation case) and nothing on other candidates.
- KILL-RARE (N26a-32) reduces the table to its fallback: `Explore` and the splitting branch are removed, tilting
  remains only inside `nemesis.search`, and the policy is nominal sampling with the binomial bound alone.
- The table is deliberately not clever. A value-of-information controller could spend the budget better; it
  would also be a moving part between a user and a certified statement.
- The sequential design costs about 3.1 times the zero-failure count on a clean run at 10⁻⁴ (92,872 against
  29,956; N26a-22) and buys early refutation, certification despite a few failures and an interval at any time.
  With a nominal share below its clean count, a design far above p⋆ is refuted early only by `RefuteCheck`, or
  at n_declared by the fixed-n design; the bound's `--estimate` names the design a budget buys, so the caller
  can choose by budget. At 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁶ the fixed-n design is always used until the upstream cap is lifted
  (FD-FSIM-18, N26b-24).
