## Background

Exploration can run forever. What says it has stopped finding anything new? A count of failures does not: a
thousand failures of one kind are one finding. DESIGN: every failure that passes the artifact guard is filed by
**failure class and binding atom**: the class from the failure taxonomy (fall, stuck, collision, slip, torque
saturation, timeout, film slip, perception dropout and the rest) and the requirement atom that decided the
verdict. That pair is a **mode**. A Good–Turing monitor estimates the probability mass of modes not yet seen as
the number of modes seen exactly once divided by the number of failures seen. It is the stopping signal for
exploration: the decision policy for rare failures explores to a fifth of its budget or until the unseen-mode
mass is at most 0.05.

The intuition behind the estimate: modes seen exactly once are the ones that were nearly missed; the share of
observations they account for is a good guess at the share that the still-missing modes would account for. It is
a stopping signal, not a bound, and it is labelled `Estimated`.

The catalogue has two further readers. The quality-diversity archive of the search bins failures by the same key.
And the scene-by-failure-mode incidence matrix from which covering batteries are selected is built from this
catalogue over a reference set of designs.

Solid: counting.

## Scope

In: `rare::catalogue`: the mode key, the catalogue record and its identity, the monitor with its estimate and
interval, the stopping predicate, the export for the incidence matrix.
Out: the guard that admits failures (BD-272); the archive (N26a-09); the policy that reads the signal (N26a-26);
coverage selection (BD-278).

## Technical approach

1. **Mode key.** `Mode { class: FailureClass, atom: AtomId }`, with the taxonomy version and the task's
   requirement identity in the catalogue's identity. A failure of outcome kind `numerical` (the solver refused or
   faulted) is not a physical failure and is never filed.
2. **Catalogue.** Per (candidate, task, tier, legality profile, taxonomy version):
   `Catalogue { modes: BTreeMap<Mode, ModeRecord { count, first_world, lowest_margin_world, laws: {…} } >,
   failures: u64 }`. Only guard-passed failures are filed (one filing per promoted failure; a failure and its
   replays count once). Each record remembers how its failures were found (the proposal laws), since a mode found
   only by tilting says nothing about its nominal frequency.
3. **The monitor.** With N = failures filed and N₁ = the number of modes with count exactly 1:
   unseen mass M̂ = N₁ / N (defined for N ≥ 1; reported as `undefined` at N = 0, which the policy treats as
   "keep exploring").
   Companion figures: N₂ (modes with count 2) and an approximate standard error
   se = √(N₁ + 2·N₂) / N (DESIGN: Good's variance approximation, with the smaller-order term dropped), giving the
   interval M̂ ± 1.96·se clipped to [0, 1]. The interval is descriptive; the estimate and its interval carry the
   label `Estimated`.
4. **What population the estimate refers to.** The mass is over the stream of failures as exploration produces
   them, not over nominal worlds. It says "the next failure this explorer finds has about this chance of being a
   new mode"; it does not say how probable unseen modes are under the nominal randomization. Results print this
   sentence with the number.
5. **Stopping predicate.** `stop(threshold = 0.05, min_failures = 20)`: true when N ≥ min_failures (TARGET; with
   fewer the estimate is too coarse: at N = 20 one singleton already gives 0.05) and M̂ ≤ threshold.
6. **Updates are order-free.** The catalogue is a commutative merge of filings, so worlds evaluated in any batch
   order or on any number of workers give the same catalogue.
7. **Export.** `incidence_rows(catalogue_set) -> [(scene, mode, exposed: bool)]`: a scene exposes a mode when at
   least one reference design fails in that mode in that scene after the guard. The export carries the
   identities under which it was built (model class, tier, kernel versions, taxonomy), because a change of any
   of them makes a stored incidence stale.
8. **Surface.** The catalogue appears in `nemesis.search` results (`classes`, and the per-mode table on request),
   in `nemesis.bound` results (the unseen-mass value at the end of exploration) and in the dashboard's failures
   panel.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a synthetic catalogue with mode probabilities (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/32), the mean of M̂ over
      10,000 replications of N = 20 failures is 0.0635 ± 0.003 (the exact expectation of N₁/N is
      Σ pᵢ·(1 − pᵢ)¹⁹ = 0.06347), and the mean realized unseen mass is 0.0598 ± 0.003 (exact:
      Σ pᵢ·(1 − pᵢ)²⁰ = 0.05976).
- [ ] At N = 10 and N = 50 the same two means are 0.1393 and 0.1257, and 0.0160 and 0.0154.
- [ ] The realized unseen mass lies inside the monitor's interval in a share of replications that is measured,
      recorded and then pinned (TARGET to set at first measurement; expected near 90% at N = 50).
- [ ] The negative control is caught: a monitor that divides the number of distinct modes by N reports 0.230 at
      N = 20 where the truth is 0.060.
- [ ] Only guard-passed, physical failures are filed; an artifact candidate and a numerical outcome change no
      count.
- [ ] `stop` is false at N = 19 with no singletons and true at N = 20 with one singleton (M̂ = 0.05) and with
      none.
- [ ] The catalogue is identical under permutation of filings and under merging of partial catalogues
      (property test).

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-667: the Good–Turing monitor on a synthetic catalogue with known mode probabilities returns the unseen mass
inside its interval; control: a monitor that counts distinct modes instead of modes seen once. The expectations
above are exact sums and hand-checkable. Metamorphic: relabelling modes changes nothing; duplicating every
failure (N and all counts doubled) sends N₁ to zero and M̂ to zero, which is why replays are filed once.
Determinism: exact integer counts, class D0.

## Considerations

- The estimate is biased upward slightly (E[N₁/N] is the unseen mass after N − 1 failures, not N); the bias is
  the difference between adjacent rows of the table above and is ignored on purpose.
- Mode granularity is a choice: finer modes (adding the scene archetype, say) make the unseen mass larger and
  exploration longer. The key is fixed at class and atom; a finer split is a new catalogue identity.
- An explorer that only ever samples near one mode will drive the unseen mass down while other modes exist
  elsewhere. The signal is about the explorer's stream; the archive's uniform selection over cells and the
  nominal share of a defensive mixture are what counter that.
- The catalogue is discovery data.
