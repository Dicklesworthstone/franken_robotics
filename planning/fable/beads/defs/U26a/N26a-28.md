## Background

Exploration of rare failures (multilevel splitting on forks, cross-entropy tilting) is Frontier: published gains
are real and uneven. The measured gains in the sources read are 2 to 20 times in one driving study and about 5
times in mean-square error on one policy-in-the-loop task (FACT, source register, to be re-read); the program's
TARGET in its surviving form is that, with an informative score, exploration reaches failures of nominal
probability 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁵ with 10 to 100 times fewer episodes than crude sampling. The evidence cuts both ways:
in one published study adaptive splitting with temporal-logic scores estimated 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ for a true
4.8 × 10⁻⁵, with a spread five times the mean, and its authors note that neither splitting nor sampling has a
guarantee on estimation error at a fixed budget; in the same study a cross-entropy sampler returned 7.6 × 10⁻²²
for a true 2.0 × 10⁻³; on a policy-in-the-loop task at p near 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, plain splitting had a relative
mean-square error of 1.02 against 1.09 for naive sampling.

The rule for Frontier work is a paper-scale pilot before the work package is funded, with a falsifier and a
ceiling written first. This is that pilot. Its falsifier is the trigger of kill gate KILL-RARE, which names the
pilot explicitly; the splitting seed bead (BD-277) depends on it.

**Ceiling, stated in advance.** A clean bound at 10⁻⁵ costs about 3 × 10⁵ episodes whatever the explorer does, on
the order of one or two training runs, so a perfect explorer saves at most about a factor of three on a finalist
and nothing on other candidates. Exploration buys discovery speed only; the certification price is unchanged.

## Scope

In: the protocol frozen before execution; the random-walk stage with its harness, the one implementation of that
fixture, its estimators, its interval code and its stream-discipline control; planted failures of known
probability on one quadruped; four arms at equal episode budget; 30 repetitions; the record; the recommendation.
Out: the construction's own pre-funding pilot PILOT-C-27 (N10b-18, M3, which depends on this bead): one planted
trap of probability at most 3·10⁻⁵ in a scene with a wheeled robot, splitting against uniform sampling, the
falsifier "not found at least three times sooner, or fewer than 1% of forks reach the next level", its record
`PILOT-C-27` and GATE-GAUNT-08. That pilot does not rebuild the walk: it runs its walk measurements on this
bead's harness, with this bead's level set. Also out: the product's splitting and bounds (BD-277, BD-276); the gate GATE-NEMESIS-05 at M3 (N26a-31); the kill
decision (N26a-32); the gate rows of the chapter and the test that this pilot's row is complete (N26b-18); the
experiment on distress scores for flat margins, which reuses this harness (N26b-20).

## Technical approach

1. **Stage A, no simulator (host).** The biased random walk with a closed-form answer: a walker starts at level 1
   and steps up with probability 0.4, down with 0.6; the probability of reaching level 20 before level 0 is
   0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴. All four arms run on it first; it validates the pilot's own estimators and
   interval code before any robot runs. Control: a walk with no noise after the fork returns identical children
   and must be refused (children that share the parent's stream key reproduce it bit for bit, are detected by
   equal digests, and the splitting estimate is refused; in the product this is FROB-REF-569, splitting
   requested where no randomness enters after the fork). The walk harness is one module,
   `crates/frob-conformance/src/pilots/splitting_walk.rs` (DESIGN), with one set of splitting levels,
   (2, 4, 8, 16, 20), fixed here because their conditional probabilities are worked below; N continuations per
   level, streams keyed by child addresses. The construction's pilot PILOT-C-27 (N10b-18) uses this module and
   this level set for its own walk measurements (the potency control at N = 10⁴ and the coefficient of
   variation against √(L·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·N))), so the walk exists once.
2. **Stage B, one quadruped.** The reference quadruped with its baseline controller on the CPU referee (or the
   pinned development oracle), on flat ground with a low-friction strip, under per-step push disturbances drawn
   from keyed streams (so that forks carry fresh randomness) and randomized static parameters (friction of the
   strip, payload mass). Requirement: stay upright and cross within the horizon.
3. **Planted failures of known probability.** Fixtures built as trigger boxes in standard-normal germs of
   independent randomized quantities, with analytic probability and a physical mechanism checked by conditional
   sampling inside the box. Two kinds of germ (DESIGN, so that the sentence below is true of every box): a
   *static* germ is that of a parameter drawn once per episode (strip friction, payload mass); a *per-step* germ
   is the standardized sum of the push impulses drawn on the per-step disturbance stream over a stated window
   that starts after the earliest fork tick (standard normal under the nominal law, so the box stays analytic).
   - F4: probability 10⁻⁴: z₁ > 2.3263 (static: strip friction low) and z₂ > 2.3263 (per-step: lateral push
     sum), one mode (slip on the strip under a push).
   - F5: probability 10⁻⁵ with **two separate failure modes** of 5 × 10⁻⁶ each, disjoint in germs and in failure
     class: mode A: z₁ > 2.3263 (static: strip friction) and z₃ > 3.2905 (per-step: lateral push sum in window
     W₁), a slip binding `upright`; mode B: z₄ > 2.3263 (static: payload mass high) and z₅ > 3.2905 (per-step:
     forward push sum in window W₂), a torque saturation binding `delivered`. 0.01 × 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 5 × 10⁻⁶ per
     mode; the union is 10⁻⁵ less the 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ overlap.
   - F2 (potency control): probability 10⁻²: z₁ > 1.2816 and z₂ > 1.2816 (0.1 × 0.1), a slip; every arm must find
     it in every repetition, and a repetition in which any arm misses it is void.
   Because part of each trigger is a per-step disturbance sequence (a cumulative push level), splitting has
   something to act on in every mode; the static part is what subset simulation and the mixture act on. (The
   product-scale fixture of GATE-NEMESIS-05, N26a-04, differs on purpose: its two-mode trap puts one mode on a
   static germ only and the other on a per-step germ only, because there the arm is the lane, splitting and
   tilting together; here each arm runs alone, and a mode with no per-step part would make the splitting arm
   miss it by construction, firing the third clause on the fixture rather than on the method.)
4. **Arms, at equal episode budget** (an episode-equivalent is the simulated steps of one full nominal episode;
   fork fragments are charged by their steps):
   - *Crude sampling:* nominal episodes; exact binomial interval.
   - *Splitting:* fixed levels on the online margin of the requirement, chosen from a separate level pilot;
     fixed effort per level; children re-keyed after the fork tick.
   - *Subset simulation:* Markov-chain resampling of the static germs conditional on each level (a
     component-wise Metropolis step), the standard alternative when forks carry no fresh randomness.
   - *Defensive mixture:* a shift fitted on a separate pilot run, nominal share ε = 1/10, bounded weights; a
     betting confidence sequence.
   Budget per repetition: 3 × 10⁴ episode-equivalents for F4 and 10⁵ for F5 (so crude sampling expects 3 and 1
   failures respectively). 30 repetitions per arm and fixture, seeds paired across arms.
5. **Measurements.** Per arm and repetition: episode-equivalents to the first failure inside a planted box
   (censored at the budget); which modes were found; the estimate of p and, for every arm that offers an
   interval as a bound (crude sampling and the mixture), whether the interval covers the planted probability;
   for splitting, the fraction of forks reaching the next level and the spread of the estimate across
   repetitions.
6. **Falsifier = the trigger of KILL-RARE (any one fires).**
   - the gain (crude episode-equivalents to first failure ÷ exploration's, as a paired lower 95% bound) is below
     10 times at 10⁻⁵; or
   - the coverage of any interval offered as a bound is below 85%; or
   - a planted mode is missed in more than 20% of repetitions.
   DESIGN reading for the pilot: "exploration" in the first and third clauses is the splitting arm, the mechanism
   whose funding the pilot decides; the subset-simulation and mixture arms' gains and missed modes are recorded
   beside it and do not fire the gate. The second clause reads crude sampling's exact interval and the mixture's
   confidence sequence. The paired lower bound, the potency rule and the A/A arm are N38a-08's routines, the same
   ones the M3 gate (N26a-31) calls, so pilot and gate cannot compute the gain two ways.
   Recorded beside it, as the construction card's own reading: whether fewer than 1% of forks reach the next
   level (an uninformative score).
7. **Record.** One entry in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`,
   N38a-09), identifier `PILOT-RARE-01` (DESIGN: the grammar's `PILOT-<SET>-<nn>` form, set `RARE` for
   rare-failure exploration, first of its set): `mechanism` rare-failure exploration (multilevel splitting on
   forks), `tier = "F"`, `owner = "§26.15"`, `bead = "N26a-28"`, `work_package = "WP-55"`, `gated_beads =
   ["BD-277"]`; the protocol path and hash, frozen first; the falsifier; the ceiling above; `fallback = { ships
   = "crude nominal sampling with the binomial bound alone; splitting removed, tilting kept only inside
   nemesis.search", kill_gate = "KILL-RARE" }`; the cost (tens of CPU core-hours on the CPU referee); the tool
   and its pin; `integrated_gate = "GATE-NEMESIS-05"` (the M3 gate that must still measure the integrated lane;
   this result sets no part of it); then the appended result, whose `receipt = "blake3:<hash>"` is committed
   byte-identical at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` in the commit that appends it (N38a-09 rule 2a; otherwise the
   register does not load, `pilot-receipt-uncommitted`). The construction's pilot `PILOT-C-27` (N10b-18) is the
   other record of the same family; the exploration lane starts only if neither falsifier holds.
8. **Recommendation.** Fund the splitting work, or ship the fallback. A work package whose pilot's falsifier
   holds is not funded.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol hash and the ceiling are in the pilot register before the first measured run.
- [ ] Stage A: every arm's estimate of 1.504 × 10⁻⁴ is unbiased within three standard errors over 200
      replications; the no-noise walk is refused.
- [ ] Both fixtures passed their conditional-sampling check (at least 95% of worlds inside each box fail by the
      planted mechanism; none in the shell outside); every box has a static and a per-step germ as listed, and the
      per-step germ's window starts after the earliest fork tick (asserted from the fixture record).
- [ ] The potency fixture F2 is found by every arm in every counted repetition, and the A/A arm's paired lower
      bound does not clear 1.02 (N38a-08's pass rule; one that clears it means a broken harness and voids the
      run); void repetitions are listed with their reason.
- [ ] The record is entered as `PILOT-RARE-01`, loads under the identifier grammar of N38a-09, and names
      `KILL-RARE`, `gated_beads = ["BD-277"]` and `integrated_gate = "GATE-NEMESIS-05"`; its result's receipt is
      committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` and `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- complete
      --json` passes with it.
- [ ] The record holds, per arm and fixture, the 30 repetitions' episode-equivalents to first failure with
      censoring flags, the modes found, the estimates, interval coverage, and for splitting the per-level
      fractions.
- [ ] Each clause of the falsifier is evaluated with its measured value against its threshold (10 times; 85%;
      20%), and the record states the recommendation.
- [ ] The pilot can be rerun from the record alone (seeds, pins, commands).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot harness's self-tests, run first:
- `walk_closed_form`: 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; reach level 2 before 0 from 1: 0.4; level 5: 0.07583;
  level 10: 0.008824.
- `splitting_unbiased_on_walk`: levels (2, 4, 8, 16, 20): conditional probabilities 0.4, 0.3077, 0.1649,
  0.03755, 0.1973, product 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; the estimator's mean over 200 replications within three standard
  errors.
- `duplicate_children_detected`: children created with the parent's stream key have equal digests and the
  estimate is refused; children keyed by their own addresses differ pairwise.
- `constant_ratio_bound`: ten pairs of ratio 12 return a lower bound of 12.
- `aa_arm`: crude against crude (paired seeds, two independent stream keys) gives a paired lower bound that does
  not clear 1.02 (N38a-08's rule).
- `interval_code_coverage`: the binomial interval covers a known rate in at least 95% of 2,000 synthetic runs.
- `box_probabilities`: 1 − Φ(2.3263) = 0.010001, 1 − Φ(3.2905) = 5.0005 × 10⁻⁴, 1 − Φ(1.2816) = 0.099992; F4
  = 1.0003 × 10⁻⁴, each F5 mode 5.0011 × 10⁻⁶ (to five significant figures, the values recorded with the fixture).
- `per_step_germ_standard_normal`: the window sum of 10⁵ nominal push sequences, standardized, passes a normality
  test at level 0.001; a fork before the window gives children whose germs differ, a fork after it gives equal
  germs.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/nemesis_explore.sh` sources the STD-E2E helper, is seed-explicit and resumable by run key, and
writes `target/pilot-logs/nemesis_explore/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript. Every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N26a-28`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the tool pin and the protocol
hash. Per run: the arm, fixture, repetition, seed, episode-equivalents (fork fragments and level pilots
separately), censoring, modes found with class and atom, estimate, interval and coverage flag; for splitting the
level table (hits and trials per level) and duplicate-children checks; for the mixture the maximum weight against
1/ε. `metric` lines give each falsifier's measured value with its threshold (splitting's gain lower bound at 10⁻⁵
≥ 10; coverage per interval-offering arm ≥ 85%; missed-mode share per mode ≤ 20%), the fork-fraction reading
against 1%, and the potency and A/A results. On a failed self-test or a falsifier that fires, the first offending
run with its seed and fixture is logged. A final `summary` lists assertions passed and failed, the clauses fired,
void repetitions and the recommendation. The harness self-tests (Stage A included) run first; a self-test failure
aborts the pilot before any measured arm.
The bead has no `scripts/e2e/` script (convention 11): it delivers nothing a `frob` command can reach (the walk
harness and the arms are pilot code of `frob-conformance`, not linked into `frob`), and its record is read by
the register's own command, `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots -- complete --json`, which the pilot
script runs last and logs. The script keeps its place under `scripts/pilots/`; the name
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_explore.sh` is N26a-31's gate script.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (splitting on a biased random walk: up-probability 0.4, start at level 1, level 20 before level 0 is
1.504 × 10⁻⁴; control: a walk with no noise after the fork returns identical children and is refused). KA-140
(splitting with 6 levels of 0.1 and 10⁴ trials per level has a coefficient of variation near 7%:
√(6 × 0.9/(0.1 × 10⁴)) = 0.0735). CX-292 (forks of a deterministic simulator do not give independent children).
The two-mode fixture is the control for mode collapse: an arm that finds only mode A in most repetitions fails
the third clause.

## Considerations

- Subset simulation is an arm of the pilot only. It is not planned for the product unless forks turn out to
  carry no fresh randomness; the pilot is where that would be seen.
- A pilot on one quadruped and two planted fixtures decides funding, not truth; the M3 gate (GATE-NEMESIS-05)
  repeats the comparison on the product.
- If only the coverage clause fires for the mixture while splitting passes the gain clause, the decision record
  must say which mechanism failed; the kill gate's fallback removes splitting and keeps bounded-weight
  estimation, so a mixture defect is repaired, not killed, and the pilot is rerun.
- The construction card for rare failures words its pilot falsifier as "the trap is not found at least three
  times sooner than by uniform sampling, or fewer than 1% of forks reach the next level"; the kill gate's
  wording (10 times at 10⁻⁵) is the one the gate register carries and governs. Both values are recorded. The
  construction's wording is measured by its own pilot bead N10b-18 on its own fixture (one trap in a scene);
  the exploration lane (BD-277) starts only if neither pilot's falsifier holds, and where the two disagree the
  kill gate's reading decides.
