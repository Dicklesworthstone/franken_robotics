## Background

Multilevel splitting reaches a rare failure in stages: instead of waiting for a one-in-a-million episode, it waits
for a one-in-ten episode that gets "closer to failing", saves it, continues it many times under fresh
disturbances, and repeats. Everything depends on what "closer to failing" means. DESIGN: the distress score is an
**online past-time margin** with **fixed levels chosen before the run**.

A requirement is a bounded-time temporal formula with a dimensionless margin (positive when satisfied, negative
when violated). Its past-time form can be evaluated while the episode runs: formulas over "so far" and "within the
last W ticks" compile into kernels in the last stage of the batched step, with fixed memory (two values per
window sample per operator and world), no data-dependent control flow, and identical bits on every backend. The
online signal has three uses in the program: early termination, an optional added reward term, and this score.

Why the levels must be fixed before the run: with levels adapted to the data the splitting estimate, though still
usable for discovery, loses the simple unbiasedness argument, and published adaptive variants have shown large
errors (one study estimated 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ for a true 4.8 × 10⁻⁵). Fixed levels keep the product-of-fractions
estimator unbiased whatever the score.

The score can be useless, and the program says so in advance. The decision model lists "an uninformative distress
score" as a failure mode with a trigger (under 1% of forks reach the next level), and an open question
(OPEN-NEMESIS-2) covers the case where the requirement margin is flat until shortly before failure.

Frontier, with the splitting it serves (pilot N26a-28, gate GATE-NEMESIS-05, kill gate KILL-RARE).

## Scope

In: `rare::distress`: the score, the level-selection pilot, the frozen level record, crossing detection with
snapshots, the informativeness check and the flat-margin rule.
Out: forking and the estimator (BD-277); the online monitors themselves (BD-255); the policy that reads the
trigger (N26a-26).

## Technical approach

1. **Score.** For a world at requirement tick t: ρ_on(t), the online margin of the past-time compilation of the
   verdict formula (for an "always" clause, the running minimum of its body's margin; for bounded windows, the
   sliding extremum). Distress is the **running minimum** m(t) = min over t′ ≤ t of ρ_on(t′), which is monotone
   non-increasing in t, so "the world has reached level ℓ" (m(t) ≤ ℓ) stays true once true. Failure is m < 0
   after the boundary check.
2. **Levels.** A strictly decreasing sequence ℓ₁ > ℓ₂ > … > ℓ_L = 0 in margin units. Level k is crossed at the
   first tick with m(t) ≤ ℓ_k.
3. **Choosing the levels (before the run, from a separate pilot).**
   1. Run N_pilot nominal episodes (default 2,000, TARGET) on the stated tier; record each episode's final
      running minimum m_end. These episodes are exploration data and are never reused in a bound or in the
      splitting run itself.
   2. Target conditional probability p₀ = 0.1 (TARGET). Set ℓ₁ to the empirical p₀-quantile of m_end (the value
      that one episode in ten falls to or below).
   3. For deeper levels the pilot has too few samples; continue by a short staged pilot: fork the episodes that
      crossed ℓ_k (as the splitting run will) N_stage times (default 500) and set ℓ_{k+1} to the p₀-quantile of
      the children's m_end. Stop when the quantile is at or below 0; set the last level to 0.
   4. Cap L at 8 (TARGET). Freeze `LevelRecord { task, requirement, tier, levels, p0, pilot_addresses, hash }`
      into the ledger before the splitting run starts.
   The planning arithmetic this follows: six levels of conditional probability 0.1 reach 10⁻⁶.
4. **Crossing detection and snapshots.** A device-side predicate m(t) ≤ ℓ_k is evaluated in the monitor stage;
   at the first tick it fires for a world, the world is snapshotted (a complete checkpoint: physical state,
   contact memory, clock phases and packet queues, controller and policy state, actuator states, the step
   counter). The snapshot and the crossing tick are what the splitting stage forks from.
5. **Informativeness check.** During the staged pilot and during the run, for each level: the fraction of forks
   that reach the next level. If it is under 1% at any level, the score is declared `uninformative` for this
   requirement on this tier, splitting is not used, and the caller falls back to crude nominal sampling; the
   declaration is recorded with the level and the fraction.
6. **The flat-margin rule (default of OPEN-NEMESIS-2).** Some requirements give no warning: the margin sits at a
   constant until the instant of failure (a count of chassis strikes declared `:exact` has margin +1 or −1 and
   nothing between; a clearance that is large until a sudden collision). Detection in the level pilot, any of:
   (a) the binding atom is `:exact`; (b) more than 90% of pilot episodes have m_end within 1% of one value;
   (c) the p₀-quantile of m_end is already at or below zero while the failure share is below p₀/10 (the margin
   jumps). Result: `flat`, no splitting for that requirement, crude sampling.
7. **Multiple requirements and atoms.** The score is defined per requirement; when a task has several, the
   caller names the one whose failure probability is in question, and the levels belong to it.
8. **Determinism and evidence.** The online margin is exact min/max arithmetic on atom values and is
   bit-identical across backends for identical atom values; the level record is exact data. Nothing here is
   evidence about a failure rate; levels are a tuning of an explorer.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the biased random walk (up-probability 0.4, start at level 1; "distress" is the walker's height, with
      levels 2, 4, 8, 16, 20), the conditional probabilities between levels are 0.4, 0.3077, 0.1649, 0.03755 and
      0.1973 and their product is 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; the level pilot with p₀ = 0.1 picks levels whose measured
      conditional fractions lie in [0.05, 0.2].
- [ ] The running minimum is monotone: once a level is crossed it is never un-crossed (property test over 10⁴
      random margin traces).
- [ ] The level record's ledger sequence precedes the first splitting episode, and the pilot's addresses are
      disjoint from the run's.
- [ ] A planted uninformative score (a margin independent of the failure mechanism) yields fork fractions under
      1% and the declaration `uninformative`; splitting is not run.
- [ ] A requirement bound by an `:exact` atom, and a planted jump margin (constant 1.0 until failure), are each
      declared `flat`.
- [ ] A snapshot taken at a crossing replays the world's continuation bit for bit when forked with the empty
      change (the null fork).
- [ ] The online score equals the offline margin's running minimum on recorded traces (10⁴ random traces per
      operator, bit for bit).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/distress.rs`:
- `running_min_monotone`; `crossing_tick_first`: a trace (0.9, 0.6, 0.7, 0.3, 0.5, −0.1) with levels (0.65,
  0.35, 0) crosses at ticks 1, 3 and 5.
- `levels_from_quantiles`: 2,000 synthetic m_end values uniform on [0, 1] give ℓ₁ within 0.02 of 0.1.
- `walk_conditional_probabilities` (the five values above; `synthetic`).
- `level_record_frozen_before_run`; `pilot_addresses_disjoint`.
- `uninformative_declared`; `flat_exact_atom`; `flat_jump_margin`; `flat_constant_mass`.
- `online_equals_offline_running_min`.
- `cap_on_levels`: a requirement that would need 12 levels stops at 8 and reports the residual conditional
  probability it could not split.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_explore.sh` (N26a-31): the level pilot's quantiles, the frozen levels with their hash, the
per-level fork fractions against the 1% floor, and any `uninformative` or `flat` declaration with its reason.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (the walk's level probability 1.504 × 10⁻⁴) supplies exact conditional fractions for the level logic.
KA-140 (six levels at 0.1 with 10⁴ trials per level: coefficient of variation near 7%). Negative controls:
levels re-chosen from the splitting run's own data (the freeze test fails); a score using the instantaneous
margin instead of the running minimum (a world can then "un-cross" a level; the monotonicity test fails).
Determinism: identical levels from identical pilot seeds on every platform.

## Considerations

- OPEN-NEMESIS-2 (the distress score when the requirement margin is flat until shortly before failure; owner:
  Nemesis lead; blocks GATE-NEMESIS-05). Fail-closed default, implemented here: no splitting for that
  requirement; crude sampling. A better score (a learned or hand-declared progress variable) is a later decision
  and would enter as a new, declared score with its own level record.
- The level pilot costs episodes that produce no failures and no bound; it is charged to the exploration share
  of the budget.
- A score that is informative for one failure mode can be blind to another; the two-mode planted fixture of the
  gate exists to show that, and the failure catalogue's unseen-mass monitor is the run-time warning.
