## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision at the milestone the gate register names. KILL-RARE is the stop rule of the
exploration lane of rare-failure estimation. Splitting on forks is Frontier: its published gains are real and
uneven, it issues no bound, and it adds snapshots, forks and a distress score to the path between a user and a
statement about failures. If it does not deliver, the program keeps the part that is Solid and exact: crude
nominal sampling with the binomial bound.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's and the gate's measurements; evaluating the trigger literally; the decision record; the
switch; what changes downstream.
Out: producing the measurements (N26a-28, N26a-31, BD-277); the fallback's estimators (N26a-21, N26a-22), which
exist regardless.

## Technical approach

**Trigger (any one clause).** In the exploration pilot:
1. the gain is below 10 times at 10⁻⁵ (crude sampling's episodes to reach a planted failure of probability 10⁻⁵,
   divided by exploration's, as a paired lower 95% bound); or
2. the coverage of any interval offered as a bound is below 85%; or
3. a planted mode is missed in more than 20% of repetitions.

**Fallback.** Crude nominal sampling with the binomial bound alone; splitting is removed and tilting is kept only
inside `nemesis.search`.

**Milestone.** Decided at M3.

**Which measurements are read.** The trigger is worded on the exploration pilot (N26a-28: one quadruped, planted
failures of probability 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one with two separate modes; crude sampling, splitting, subset simulation
and the defensive mixture at equal episode budget; 30 repetitions). The pilot decides whether the splitting work
is funded at all. At M3 the same three quantities are measured again on the product by the script of
GATE-NEMESIS-05 (N26a-31), from the quantities the seed bead for splitting (BD-277) puts in every run; that bead
does not wait for this decision (convention 7), and this decision reads what it delivered. DESIGN reading,
recorded here so the decision cannot drift: the decision at M3 is taken on the product-scale measurements where
they exist, and on the pilot's where the splitting work was never funded; both sets of values are printed in the
decision record, and a disagreement between them is itself recorded.

**Procedure** (the record format and outcomes are those of the kill-gate records, N40-03; the kill row itself,
with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided = "M3"`, `mode = "exit"` and `reads` naming the pilot record of N26a-28 and
GATE-NEMESIS-05, is created by the fallback ledger, N40-26).
1. A session that did not implement splitting reads the pilot record, the GATE-NEMESIS-05 record and the
   interval-coverage metrics of the exploration script. A session listed as an implementer of BD-277, N26a-29 or
   the pilot harness is refused by the record check.
2. It checks the protocol hashes predate the runs, the planted registry digest is the frozen one, the potency
   controls passed (a failed potency control voids a run; a void run neither passes nor fires the gate), and that
   no amendment of the kill row postdates a receipt it reads.
3. It evaluates each clause literally:
   - clause 1 fires when the paired lower bound at 10⁻⁵ is below 10;
   - clause 2 fires when, for any arm that offers an interval as a bound (crude sampling's exact interval; the
     defensive mixture's confidence sequence), fewer than 85% of repetitions cover the planted probability. An
     exploration estimate is not offered as a bound and is not read by this clause; if any surface did offer
     one, that is a defect to fix and the clause is evaluated on it as well;
   - clause 3 fires when either mode of the two-mode fixture is missed in more than 20% of repetitions (more
     than 6 of 30).
4. It writes the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RARE-M3.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill
   gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendments; each measured value per clause, from the
   pilot and from the product-scale script, with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number; the outcome; the
   fallback in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the
   fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; which mechanism each fired clause points at;
   the recording session.
5. It fills the kill row's `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (`receipts`: the hashes of the
   receipts the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`, N40-03) with outcome `not-fired` (no clause
   fires), `fired` (any clause fires, on the product-scale values, or on the pilot's when the splitting work was
   never funded because the pilot's falsifier held; GATE-NEMESIS-05's failed part, if any, takes status
   `killed` with detail "KILL-RARE: crude nominal sampling with the binomial bound alone"), `not-reached` (the
   pilot was never run, so splitting does not exist) or `deferred` (with its own ADR naming the later exit). It
   sets BD-277's switch `nemesis.rare.exploration = splitting | off` in the configuration record accordingly,
   and writes the chapter's owner declaration of KILL-RARE (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`, the
   ledger's text verbatim) where N26b-18 places gate declarations, so that N40-30's cross-check finds exactly
   one.
6. **The unfunded branch (convention 10).** This bead closes whichever way the measurement falls. When the
   pilot's falsifier holds (`PILOT-RARE-01` or `PILOT-C-27` with verdict `falsifier-holds`), or the pilot was
   never run, the lane is not funded: BD-277, N26a-29 and N26a-31 close `withdrawn` citing that pilot's
   `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7), or the absent funding decision, which releases this bead's
   dependencies; it then records `fired` on the pilot's values or `not-reached`, and never waits for a lane that
   was not built.

**What changes downstream if it fires.**
- `rare/splitting.rs` and the distress-level machinery are removed from the build (the manifest feature
  `frontier-splitting` is dropped and its `contract.toml` row with it); `nemesis.search --method splitting`
  returns FROB-REF-724 (not implemented in this build; exit 5) naming KILL-RARE and the ADR, and `frob
  capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name, not splitting. BD-277 is re-read as "superseded by this ADR";
  N26a-29 likewise; N26a-26 "stands on the fallback".
- The decision policy of `nemesis.bound` reduces to its fallback: no `Explore` state; nominal sampling under the
  sequential binomial bound (or the fixed-n design) with the whole budget; the zero-failure table is the price
  list. The policy record gets a new version.
- Cross-entropy tilting stays, only inside `nemesis.search`, as a way to find failures; it no longer feeds an
  exploration step of `nemesis.bound`.
- Bounded-weight importance sampling with the defensive mixture is Solid and stays for refutation and estimates,
  **unless clause 2 fired on the mixture's own interval**. In that case the mixture is not a kill-gate matter
  but a defect in a Solid mechanism: the modes `refute` and `estimate` are withheld until its gate
  (GATE-NEMESIS-04) passes again, and the decision record says so.
- Bounds already issued stay valid: none ever depended on exploration data.
- The upstream work item for a sibling splitting estimator stays open with no consumer; the open question about
  a distress score for flat margins is closed as moot.
- The capability is described by the name of its fallback ("failure-rate bounds by nominal sampling"), and the
  thesis lever is restated: failures are found by search and randomization; rates are certified at the fixed
  price.

**What changes if it does not fire.** Splitting stays in shadow inside the decision policy until the policy's own
adoption decision; the measured gain (not the TARGET) is the number cited from then on.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/kill_rare_switch.rs`:
- `exploration_off_refuses_splitting`; `policy_reduces_to_nominal`: with the switch off, the policy's transition
  log has no `Explore` or `RefuteCheck` state and the bound equals the baseline's.
- `issued_bounds_still_validate`: a bound issued with exploration on re-validates with it off.
- `trigger_evaluator`: (gain 14, coverages 0.97 and 0.93, modes missed 3 and 2 of 30) → `not-fired`;
  (gain 7, …) → `fired`, clause 1; (gain 14, mixture coverage 0.80) → `fired`, clause 2, flagged as a mixture
  defect; (gain 14, coverages fine, mode B missed 7 of 30) → `fired`, clause 3; mode B missed exactly 6 of 30 →
  `not-fired` (the boundary); a void potency control → undecided (no outcome written); no pilot record →
  `not-reached`.
- `outcome_closed_set`: the evaluator can emit only the four outcomes of N40-03; `pass` and `killed` are gate
  statuses, never kill outcomes.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_kill_rare.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; log
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_kill_rare/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript):
1. Read the pilot record and GATE-NEMESIS-05's record with `frob evidence trace <clm:…>` on the claim
   identifier each receipt carries (the verb takes a claim identifier, not a receipt hash); log each
   measured value (gain lower bound at 10⁻⁵, coverage per interval-offering arm, missed-mode share per mode,
   potency and A/A results, void repetitions) with its threshold and receipt hash, from both sources.
2. Re-evaluate the trigger and assert that the outcome equals the ADR's and the kill row's.
3. In a scratch configuration with `nemesis.rare.exploration = off`: `frob nemesis search … --method splitting
   --key kr-3-1 --json` → assert exit 5, FROB-REF-724, KILL-RARE named; `frob nemesis bound … --tier batch-cpu
   --mode certify --key kr-3-2 --json` on a synthetic-rate task → assert the transition log has no `Explore`
   state and the bound equals the one recorded with exploration on for the same addresses (a run under its own
   key, so the comparison is between two runs and not one stored receipt).
4. On the `fired` branch also assert `frob capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name and not splitting.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-32`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time and the envelope's evidence block; on failure the first value that differs between the re-evaluation and
the record, with both receipt paths. A final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed, `gates:
["KILL-RARE"]` and the outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative control for the procedure: the evaluator on a planted record with gain 2 must return `fired`; a
procedure that cannot return `fired` is not a gate. The fallback is demonstrated by forcing the switch. The
certification cost table (29,956; 299,572; 2,995,731 at δ = 0.05) is what the fallback costs and is asserted
unchanged under either decision.

## Considerations

- The three clauses test three different things (speed, honesty of intervals, breadth). Only the first and third
  are about splitting; the second is a tripwire on anything that claims to be a bound. The decision record must
  say which fired, because the repair differs.
- The pilot's ceiling was written first: a perfect explorer saves at most about a factor of three on a finalist
  and nothing on other candidates. A kill here costs little of the program's thesis; it mostly removes moving
  parts.
- If KILL-NEMESIS also fires, tilting leaves `nemesis.search` too, and Nemesis is randomization plus the oracle,
  the guard, shrinking and nominal bounds: the plan's M3 fallback line reads exactly so (card randomization with
  the legality profile, the oracle and the guard; crude sampling with a binomial bound; full failing worlds with
  replay).
