## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision at the milestone the gate register names. KILL-MCW is the stop rule of
minimal counterexample worlds: the claim that a failing world shrinks to a few typed edits that replay bit for
bit (the second half of bet BET-22: each failure shrinks to a world of at most three edits that reproduces bit
for bit and that the design's own body could have coped with). Delta debugging is established for programs; its
yield on physical worlds is unmeasured. Two things can go wrong on real failures that planted ones do not show:
real causes may be diffuse (many small edits, none removable), so shrunk worlds stay large; and replays may
break (a file that does not replay with identical digests is invalid).

The ceiling of the mechanism was stated in advance and is not a speed: a better artifact per failure, measured in
agent repair iterations. Its named fallback at the construction level is the failing world unshrunk, with its
edit list.

## Scope

In: reading the gate's measurements; the literal trigger; the decision record; setting the switch; the fallback's
behaviour as the decision states it; what changes downstream.
Out: producing the measurements (N26a-34, BD-274); the fallback's implementation, which exists before the
decision so that it can be forced and tested whatever the outcome: the contrasts computation `shrink::contrasts`
(N26a-18, over the relation's per-subset margin record of N26a-17), the switch `nemesis.shrink.mode` and its
dispatch in the verb (BD-274), and the file's `contrasts` field and its validation (N26a-19).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (either clause).** On unplanted failures: fewer than half of shrunk worlds have at most three edits;
or fewer than 90% replay.

**Fallback.** Nemesis ships full failing worlds with replay and the single-edit contrasts ranked by margin; the
format stays.

**Milestone.** Decided at M3.

**Measurements that decide it** (from the record of GATE-NEMESIS-03, script N26a-34): on the sample of at least
60 unplanted promoted failures, taken in the order found, (1) the share of shrunk worlds with at most three
atomic edits; (2) the share of written `.fmcw` files that replay with identical state digests on their
originating backend. For the record and not for the trigger: the planted-cause recovery rate, the pilot's result
(N26a-06), the distribution of shrunk sizes, and the share of shrinks that stopped as seed-fragile.

**Procedure** (the record format and outcomes are those of the kill-gate records, N40-03; the kill row itself,
with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided = "M3"`, `mode = "exit"` and `reads = ["GATE-NEMESIS-03"]`, is created by
the fallback ledger, N40-26).
1. A session that did not implement the shrinker reads the gate record and checks that the unplanted sample was
   taken in the order found (campaign identities recorded before shrinking) and that the planted-cause row
   passed (a shrinker that fails its planted row is defective; the unplanted measurement is then void until it
   is repaired, and a void measurement neither passes nor fires the gate). A session listed as an implementer of
   BD-274, N26a-17, N26a-18 or N26a-19 is refused by the record check.
2. It evaluates both clauses literally: clause 1 fires when the share with at most three edits is below 0.5;
   clause 2 fires when the replay share is below 0.9.
3. It writes the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MCW-M3.md` with N40-03's required fields: the kill
   gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendments; the two measured shares with sample
   sizes and receipt hashes beside the trigger's numbers; the outcome; which clause fired; the fallback in force
   and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback" or
   "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session.
4. It fills the kill row's `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (`receipts`: the hashes of the
   gate receipts the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`, N40-03) with outcome `not-fired`, `fired`,
   `not-reached` (the shrinker was not funded after the pilot N26a-06) or `deferred` (with its own ADR). On
   `fired`, GATE-NEMESIS-03 keeps its planted rows (they measure the algorithm on planted worlds and stay on
   record), and its unplanted row's record is annotated with the detail "KILL-MCW: full failing worlds with
   replay and single-edit contrasts ranked by margin". It sets `nemesis.shrink.mode = minimal | contrasts` in the
   configuration record accordingly, and writes the chapter's owner declaration of KILL-MCW (`trigger`,
   `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`, the ledger's text verbatim) where N26b-18 places gate declarations, so
   that N40-30's cross-check finds exactly one.
5. **The unfunded branch (convention 10).** This bead closes whichever way the measurement falls. When the
   pilot's falsifier holds for shrinking (`PILOT-C-28`, verdict `falsifier-holds`) or the shrinker is not funded,
   the gate's measuring bead N26a-34 closes `withdrawn` citing that `[[pilot.result]]` (N41-17, rule 7), while
   BD-274 (in `contrasts` mode), N26a-17, N26a-18 and N26a-19 stand on the contrasts path, which exists before
   this decision; this bead then records `not-reached` (or `fired` on the pilot's values) and never waits for a
   minimal-world shrinker that was not built.

**The fallback, as the decision states it (`mode = contrasts`; implemented by N26a-18 and dispatched by BD-274
before this decision).** `nemesis.shrink <world>` no longer searches subsets. It:
1. verifies the failing world replays (still refusing a failure that does not replay or that the guard labelled
   an artifact candidate, FROB-REF-564);
2. evaluates, for each of the n edits, the world with that single edit removed, on the same 64 paired seeds,
   recording the number of seeds still failing by the original mechanism and the mean requirement margin;
3. ranks the edits by the margin gain of removing them (largest gain first): these are the single-edit
   contrasts, each an interventional contrast between the failing world and a neighbour;
4. writes a `.fmcw` with the full edit list, `minimality: {"kind": "non-minimal(n)", "pairs_exhausted": false,
   "one_minimal_verified": false}`,
   a `contrasts` array (`edit`, `seeds_failing_without`, `margin_gain`) in rank order, and
   `neighbours_passing` listing only those single-removal neighbours that pass (possibly none);
5. keeps every other field and rule of the format: identities, digests, the oracle's witness, legality, prior,
   evidence, the no-authorization flags. A file must still replay with identical digests to be valid; under
   clause 2 the replay defect itself is tracked as a repair item in the replay machinery, and files that do not
   replay are not written.
The cost is n tests of 64 episodes instead of a quadratic search.

**What changes downstream if it fires.**
- The hard-world set lists full failing worlds; its admission rules are unchanged (a witness, promotion on the
  referee, replay); entries are larger.
- The counterfactual debugger receives the ranked contrasts instead of a minimal set; its own attribution does
  the rest.
- The retrofit question receives a full world and the top-ranked building-feature edits.
- The design-pair separation query's acceptance row on minimized edit lists (at most three edits) is re-read by
  its owner against its own kill gate.
- The capability is described by the name of its fallback ("replayable failing worlds with ranked single-edit
  contrasts"); bet BET-22's shrinking half is recorded as not confirmed. The planted-cause recovery result stays
  on record as a property of the algorithm on planted worlds.
- The modules for subset search, bisection and the pair check are removed from the build (the manifest feature
  `frontier-mcw` is reduced to the contrasts path and its `contract.toml` row says so); the test relation, the
  contrasts computation and the file format stay. `frob capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name.

**What changes if it does not fire.** Nothing is removed; the measured shares (not the size TARGET) are what
reports cite about shrinking.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/kill_mcw_switch.rs` (the algorithm's own tests, `contrasts_mode_output` and
`contrasts_cost_is_linear`, are N26a-18's):
- `contrasts_file_validates_and_replays`: through the verb with the switch forced to `contrasts`, the 7-edit
  world whose failure needs edits 2 and 5 yields a file that N26a-19's reader validates, with `non-minimal(7)`,
  two passing neighbours, contrasts ranked 2 and 5 first, and identical digests on replay.
- `refusal_564_unchanged`.
- `trigger_evaluator`: (share 0.62, replay 0.97) → `not-fired`; (0.41, 0.97) → `fired`, clause 1; (0.62, 0.85)
  → `fired`, clause 2; (0.50, 0.90) → `not-fired` (both boundaries); a failed planted-cause row → undecided
  (void, no outcome written); no shrinker funded → `not-reached`.
- `outcome_closed_set`: the evaluator can emit only the four outcomes of N40-03.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_kill_mcw.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; idempotent; log
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_kill_mcw/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript):
1. Read GATE-NEMESIS-03's record with `frob evidence trace <clm:…>` on the claim identifier its receipt carries
   (the verb takes a claim identifier, not a receipt hash); log the two shares with sample sizes,
   thresholds and receipt hash, the planted-cause row's result, and the campaign identities of the unplanted
   sample.
2. Re-evaluate the trigger and assert that the outcome equals the ADR's and the kill row's.
3. In a scratch configuration with `nemesis.shrink.mode = contrasts`: shrink three worlds of the planted-cause
   registry with `frob nemesis shrink <world> --key km-3-<i> --json` and replay each file with `frob sim replay
   <fmcw> --key km-3r-<i> --json`; log the mode, n, the ranked contrasts with seeds failing and margin gain;
   assert each file validates, replays with identical digests and lists only passing single-removal neighbours;
   shrink an artifact candidate (`--key km-3-a`) and assert FROB-REF-564 (exit 3) is unchanged.
4. On the `fired` branch also assert `frob capabilities --json` lists the fallback's name.
Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26a-35`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time and the envelope's evidence block; on failure the first value that differs between the re-evaluation and
the record, with both receipt paths. A final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed, `gates:
["KILL-MCW"]` and the outcome. `scripts/e2e/nemesis_loop.sh` (N26a-20) switches its shrink assertions to the
fallback's when the mode is `contrasts`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Negative control for the procedure: the evaluator on a planted record with shares (0.30, 0.95) must return
`fired`; a procedure that cannot return `fired` is not a gate. The fallback is demonstrated by forcing the
switch. KA-144 (a stored counterexample file reproduces its state hash) holds under either decision.

## Considerations

- Clause 1 measures the world, not the code: if real failures have diffuse causes, no shrinker makes them small.
  Clause 2 measures the replay machinery. The decision record says which fired, because only the second is
  repairable.
- A threshold of one half on "at most three edits" was fixed before data. A kept decision at 0.55 still means
  nearly half of counterexamples are larger than the TARGET; they are kept and labelled `non-minimal(k)`.
- If the search's kill gate also fires, Nemesis is card randomization with the legality profile, the oracle and
  the guard, and full failing worlds with replay: the plan's fallback line for the milestone.
