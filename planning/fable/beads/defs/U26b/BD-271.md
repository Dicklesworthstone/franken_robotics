## Background

Nemesis (crate `frob-nemesis`, layer 5) searches for worlds in which a candidate (a design with its controller or
policy) violates its requirement. An unconstrained adversary wins trivially by building a world no robot could
cope with: a doorway narrower than the body defeats every controller, and a search that reported it as a failure
of the design would blame the design for an impossible trap. ADR-NEMESIS-02 therefore rules: **only solvable
worlds count.** A world is a counterexample to a design only with a witness that the candidate's own body,
actuators and mission constraints could have satisfied the requirement in it. Rejected alternative: counting any
world that defeats the design (the tempting shortcut registered as CX-289).

The solvability oracle is the mechanism that enforces the rule. It classifies a failing world before the world
counts for anything:

| Class | Witness | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| `solvable-causal` | A controller that uses only the candidate's observation interface satisfies the requirement in this world, within every actuator and mission limit: the baseline controller at other gains, another member of the policy family, or a sampling controller on the candidate's sensors | A counterexample to the candidate's controller or policy |
| `plant-feasible` | A trajectory found with privileged state satisfies the requirement within the body's limits | A counterexample, tagged plant-feasible only: the body can do it, and whether the sensors allow it is a separate question for the observation analyses (alias witnesses, observability) |
| `not-excluded` | Necessary conditions pass (a route exists in the scene's clearance complex, wrench and torque envelopes suffice, the energy budget closes) and no trajectory was found | Unresolved. Reported, not counted either way |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition fails, with its dual vector or its bottleneck named | Not a counterexample. Counted in the scorecard's infeasible column and offered to the retrofit query |

Three rules bind the oracle. (1) A witness is itself a simulated run, so it passes the artifact guard: a witness
that succeeds through solver-created energy is not a witness (INV-NEMESIS-05). (2) An infeasibility verdict needs
a checked necessary condition; a failed search for a witness proves nothing and yields `not-excluded`. (3) The
oracle runs for the candidate's body, not for a generic point robot: connectivity of free space at a clearance
radius certifies occupancy only, not steering, traction, reach or perception.

Tier Solid: the oracle is bookkeeping over mechanisms that exist elsewhere (the clearance complex, the static
analyses, the baseline and sampling controllers, the guard). It serves bet BET-22 (its falsifier includes "fewer
than 70% of reported counterexamples carry a solvability witness") and North-Star scenario NS-15 (a failing world
confirmed, by a witness, to be one the candidate's own body and actuators could have completed). If the search's
kill gate KILL-NEMESIS fires, the oracle is one of the pieces the fallback keeps.

## Scope

This seed bead is the capstone of the oracle. Its parts are separate beads that it depends on and composes: the
necessary conditions with their checked certificates (N26a-13, with the mandatory task events of N26a-36) and the
witness search (N26a-14), on the crate foundation (N26a-01) and the world model (N26a-02); its crate-level gate
test runs on the planted trap registries (N26a-04). The artifact guard (BD-272) is reached through the witness
search, which passes every witness through it; this bead does not depend on the guard directly.

In: the classification pipeline that composes the two component modules; the result type and its wire schema; the
single admission function that enforces INV-NEMESIS-01 wherever something is counted; the ledger record and
memoization; the hand-offs (infeasible column, retrofit offer, observation analyses); the verb `nemesis.oracle` on
CLI and MCP; the crate-level gate test for the oracle rows of GATE-NEMESIS-01.
Out: the necessary conditions and their certificate checks (N26a-13); the witness ladder and budgets (N26a-14);
the artifact guard (BD-272); the planted traps (N26a-04); the end-to-end script that records the gate (N26a-33);
the scorecard columns (BD-254); the retrofit search (BD-369); the verb's registry row (N26b-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Modules (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-nemesis/src/oracle/`: `mod.rs` (the pipeline `classify` and
   `admit_counterexample`), `record.rs` (result type, canonical wire form, ledger table), `necessary.rs` (N26a-13),
   `witness.rs` (N26a-14).
2. **Types (DESIGN sketch; fixes intent, not signatures).**
   ```rust
   pub enum OracleClass { SolvableCausal, PlantFeasible, NotExcluded, InfeasibleForCandidate }
   pub struct OracleResult {
       world: WorldId, candidate: Candidate, task: TaskId, requirement: Ident, tier: Tier,
       candidate_verdict: Verdict,              // the candidate's own verdict in this world, after the boundary check
       class: OracleClass,
       witness: Option<WitnessRecord>,          // controller, source, margin, trace hash, channels read, guard verdict
       condition: Option<FailedCondition>,      // condition id, kind, Bottleneck | DualVector | EnergyBound, check: Accepted
       necessary: NecessaryReport,              // every condition with its kind (necessary | screen) and result
       tried: Vec<SourceTried>,                 // witness sources in order, steps spent, outcome
       class_level: Option<ClassInfeasible>,    // set when every body of the declared robot class is excluded
       counts_as: CountsAs,                     // Counterexample | CounterexamplePlantOnly | Unresolved | Infeasible | NotAFailure
       steps: u64, evidence: ClaimProduct,
   }
   ```
   `OracleClass::InfeasibleForCandidate` can be built only from a `CheckedCertificate` (a private constructor in
   `necessary.rs` that the independent check returns). No code path turns an exhausted search into infeasibility:
   rule 2 is a property of the types, not of discipline.
3. **Pipeline `oracle::classify(world, candidate, task, opts) -> OracleResult` (numbered).**
   1. Resolve identities. Check the world against the legality profile on the final world (INV-NEMESIS-02); an
      illegal world or an edit without declared causal paths is refused with the legality codes (FROB-REF-360,
      -361, -362) and nothing is simulated. A world address in the confirmation namespace is refused with the
      sealed-read refusal of the qualification lane (FROB-REF-840).
   2. Establish the failing episode: take the named run if one is given, else evaluate the candidate at the
      world's address on the request's tier. If the candidate's verdict is `satisfied` the world is not a failing
      world: the candidate's own run is submitted to the guard as a witness and, if it passes, the result is
      `solvable-causal` with witness source `candidate` and `counts_as: NotAFailure`. `invalid` (a numerical
      fault) and `undetermined` verdicts are reported as such and classified no further.
   3. Necessary conditions (cheap, CPU, exact or interval arithmetic). If some condition of kind `necessary`
      fails and the independent check of its certificate accepts: class `infeasible-for-candidate`, stop. A
      certificate the check rejects is an integrity finding and the condition is `Unresolved`. A failing
      `screen` condition (a quasi-static test where dynamics could succeed) never excludes.
   4. Causal ladder: the first source whose run satisfies the requirement within every actuator and mission
      limit, reads only candidate channels, and passes the artifact guard as a witness (one tier up; the four
      numerical variants half step, twice the solver iterations, the alternative contact law, a tolerance ten
      times tighter; the energy audit) gives `solvable-causal`.
   5. Privileged search under the same limit and guard checks gives `plant-feasible`.
   6. Otherwise `not-excluded`, with the sources tried and the steps spent.
   7. Record and hand off (step 5 below). Every witness run uses the failing world's address: same scene, same
      disturbances, same random streams; only the controller differs.
4. **The one admission function (INV-NEMESIS-01).** `oracle::admit_counterexample(&OracleResult, &GuardVerdict)
   -> Result<Counted, NotCounted>` returns `Counted` only when the candidate's verdict is `violated`, the guard
   passed the failure, and the class is `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible`. Every site that counts or keeps a
   counterexample calls it and nothing else: the `failures` list and `failures_promoted` count of `nemesis.search`,
   the writer and the reader of the counterexample file `.fmcw`, and `add` on the hard-world set. `NotCounted`
   carries the bucket (`infeasible_for_candidate`, `not_excluded`, `artifact_candidate`, `not_a_failure`).
5. **Record, memo and hand-offs.** Ledger table `nemesis_oracle` (world, candidate, task, tier, kernel versions,
   oracle version, class, witness trace hash, condition id, certificate hash, ledger sequence). A repeat request
   with the same key fields returns the stored result (the ladder's order, grids and stream keys are fixed
   functions of the world and candidate identities, so a rerun gives the same class). Hand-offs:
   `infeasible-for-candidate` feeds the scorecard column of the same name with the certificate attached, and
   writes a retrofit offer `{world, feature, required, available}` naming the building feature at fault (the
   door, the lip, the slope) for the retrofit query; evaluated over every body of the declared class it feeds
   `infeasible-for-class`, the evidence that a scenario should leave a suite by a recorded decision.
   `plant-feasible` results carry a pointer to the observation analyses, never a claim that the sensors suffice.
6. **Wire form, schema `frob.nemesis.oracle/1` (two of the four shapes).**
   ```json
   { "world": "w:9d04…", "candidate_verdict": "violated", "class": "solvable-causal", "counts_as": "counterexample",
     "witness": {"controller": "c:77e1…", "source": "gains", "margin": 0.18, "trace": "b3:a9d2…",
                 "channels_read": ["imu", "encoders", "depth-front"],
                 "guard": {"status": "passed", "tier": "batch-cpu", "variants": ["half-step", "iterations-x2", "law-2", "tol-div-10"]}},
     "tried": [{"source": "gains", "runs": 3, "steps": 61440, "outcome": "witness"}], "steps": 98304 }
   { "world": "w:51b0…", "candidate_verdict": "violated", "class": "infeasible-for-candidate", "counts_as": "infeasible",
     "condition": {"id": "route", "kind": "necessary",
                   "bottleneck": {"feature": "/doors/door[pantry]", "required": "0.75 m", "available": "0.70 m"},
                   "certificate": "b3:44c0…", "check": "accepted"},
     "retrofit_offer": {"feature": "/doors/door[pantry]", "required": "0.75 m", "available": "0.70 m"} }
   ```
   `not-excluded` carries `tried` and the necessary report; `plant-feasible` carries a witness whose `source` is
   `privileged` and the note `sensors: not examined`.
7. **Verb.** `nemesis.oracle`, kind run, first milestone M3: classifies one world for one candidate and returns
   the witness or the failed necessary condition. CLI: `frob nemesis oracle <candidate> --task <task> --world
   <world.json | w:… | run id> --legality <profile> [--tier T] [--class-level] [--budget steps=N] --key <k>
   --json`. MCP tool `nemesis_oracle` in the task cluster `attack`. Scope `nemesis:run`. Idempotent by key. A long
   verb: bounded synchronous wait (to completion on the CLI; 5,000 ms by default and at most 30,000 on MCP), then
   a job handle with progress in witness runs (exit 8). Exit 0 for a completed classification without flags;
   exit 2 when the class is `not-excluded` or an integrity flag is present (a rejected certificate, a witness the
   guard discarded); exit 3 for the refusals of step 1; exit 4 when the estimate exceeds the budget at admission.
   Budget exhaustion inside the witness search is not a refusal: it is `not-excluded`.
8. **Evidence and determinism.** Proof class `Tested` (an infeasibility verdict is a checked instance-level
   statement in the declared model, reported `Tested` with its certificate attached until the certificate kinds
   are admitted by the certificate checker). Tier: the tier that guarded the witness. Class verdicts are exact
   (D0); witness runs inherit the class of their tier. `claims: []`, `reality: no-claim`, the three
   no-authorization flags false. Cost: necessary conditions in seconds on CPU; witness search up to 16 times the
   failing episode's steps (8 causal, 8 privileged; TARGET) plus six guard episodes per accepted witness.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, GATE-NEMESIS-01 (Known answer; M3; TARGET): 0 of 100 planted impossible traps counted
      and at least 95 of 100 planted solvable traps receive a witness, measured by the crate-level test
      `oracle_gate` on the planted registry and recorded with measured counts by the end-to-end script
      (N26a-33), which joins the legality row (100 of 100 planted illegal edits refused) into one gate record.
- [ ] The narrow doorway (body 0.75 m, door 0.70 m, legal) is `infeasible-for-candidate` with the bottleneck
      named, lands in the scorecard's infeasible column and never in a counterexample count.
- [ ] `admit_counterexample` is the only route to a count: a grep-level test finds no other constructor of
      `Counted`, and a property test over random results admits only the two solvable classes.
- [ ] `InfeasibleForCandidate` cannot be constructed without a `CheckedCertificate` (compile-fail test); with the
      necessary conditions disabled, an impossible trap yields `not-excluded`, never infeasible.
- [ ] A witness discarded by the guard does not support a class; the case is written to the fidelity ledger.
- [ ] A candidate that satisfies the requirement in the world yields `counts_as: NotAFailure`.
- [ ] Rerunning the verb gives the same class and witness controller; a repeat by key returns the stored result.
- [ ] The whole pipeline runs with no device: necessary conditions on the host, witness runs and guard replays on
      `batch-cpu` and the referee (the CPU reference path); a device tier only makes the candidate's own
      episode and the witness search faster.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and MCP with schema `frob.nemesis.oracle/1`, an example and a refusal list;
      cancellation tested (request, drain, finalize, no leaked device resources); `CONTRACT.md` lists the verb,
      its classes and INV-NEMESIS-01, -05; outputs carry the claim product.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/oracle_pipeline.rs` (real components on the CPU reference tier; small scenes):
- `pipeline_order`: a checked failed condition ends the pipeline; no witness run is started (zero steps).
- `infeasible_requires_checked_certificate` (trybuild compile-fail) and `search_failure_is_not_excluded`.
- `counts_as_table`: the five `CountsAs` values for (verdict, class) pairs; `plant-feasible` keeps its tag.
- `admit_counterexample_inv_nemesis_01` (property, 10,000 random results): admitted iff verdict `violated`,
  guard `Passed`, class in {`solvable-causal`, `plant-feasible`}.
- `candidate_passes_is_not_a_failure`; `invalid_verdict_not_classified`.
- `illegal_world_refused_before_any_step`: FROB-REF-360 with the bound named; zero episodes recorded.
- `memoized_repeat` and `deterministic_class`.
- `wire_roundtrip_four_classes`: canonical bytes identical after parse and re-serialize.
- `retrofit_offer_written` (doorway: feature `/doors/…`, 0.75 m required, 0.70 m available) and
  `infeasible_for_class_flag` (a door narrower than every body of the class).
- `exit_codes`: 0, 2 (`not-excluded`), 3 (illegal world), 8 (job), per the table above.
- `cancel_drains`: cancel during the ladder; no witness run is left unfinalized.
- `oracle_gate`: 100 impossible traps → 100 checked `infeasible-for-candidate`, 0 counted; 100 solvable traps →
  at least 95 with a guarded witness.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_oracle.sh` (bead N26a-33) drives `frob nemesis oracle` on the planted traps and writes the
gate record. Per world it logs each necessary condition with kind, result and certificate hash, each witness
source tried with steps and outcome, the final class, `counts_as`, the witness controller, margin, trace digest,
channels read and the guard verdict per variant, in the structured log of the shared end-to-end helper.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-660 (a doorway narrower than the body: `infeasible-for-candidate` with the bottleneck, never a counterexample;
0 of 100 counted; control: an oracle that checks a point robot calls it solvable). KA-661 (a planted solvable
trap is found with a causal witness; at least 95 of 100). KA-143 (the impossible-world control of the
construction: rejected for lack of a witness). CX-289. Planted defects the tests must catch: the build feature
`nemesis-oracle-point-robot` (footprint replaced by a point: the doorway becomes solvable);
`nemesis-oracle-skip-guard` (a box-cone or energy-pumped run is accepted as a witness);
`nemesis-mut-count-without-witness` (a `not-excluded` world enters `failures`). Determinism lanes: class and
witness controller identical twice on one machine and across worker counts; class verdicts identical in debug
and release.

## Considerations

- A missing necessary condition makes the oracle weaker (more `not-excluded`), never wrong; a wrong condition
  makes it wrong, hence the independent check of every certificate.
- The 95-of-100 threshold measures the witness search's power. A solvable trap left `not-excluded` costs yield,
  not correctness.
- The oracle answers "could this body have coped", not "what should be changed": the shrunk world, the
  counterfactual debugger and the retrofit query answer that.
- `plant-feasible` is the weaker counterexample; no report drops the tag, and the hard-world set records it.
- RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard instead of the robot, including by moving failures out of the
  denominator): the infeasible column is the obvious route. Two things close it: an infeasibility verdict needs
  a certificate that an independent check accepted, and success rates are printed with two denominators (all
  scenarios; scenarios not shown infeasible), side by side.
- Privacy: a world derived from a private scene keeps its mark; logs never carry scene content.
