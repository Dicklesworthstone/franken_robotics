## Background

A battery (a named, versioned set of scenarios a design is evaluated on) has a budget, and the scenes worth
running are those that expose failure modes other scenes miss. Running the hundred scenes in which designs fail
most often mostly re-tests one failure mode a hundred times. DESIGN: choose scenes by **weighted coverage of
failure modes**, which is a monotone submodular objective, so a greedy algorithm has a classical guarantee and,
more usefully, each selection can carry a certificate computed from its own numbers.

- **Incidence.** A matrix of scenes against failure modes, built from the catalogue of modes over a reference
  set of designs. A failure mode is a pair (failure class, binding requirement atom). A scene exposes a mode
  when at least one reference design fails in that mode in that scene after the artifact guard. Each mode has a
  declared weight: frequency × severity × number of designs affected.
- **Objective.** f(S) is the total weight of modes exposed by at least one scene of S. It is monotone and
  submodular: a scene is worth only what earlier scenes missed.
- **Algorithm.** Lazy greedy selection: keep scenes in a priority queue by their last computed marginal gain and
  recompute only the top, since gains never increase.
- **Certificate.** For a selection S of k scenes, OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k largest remaining marginal
  gains (PO-NEMESIS-03). Greedy also guarantees f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k at fixed cardinality. With unequal scene
  costs the selection ranks by gain per second and the guarantee weakens to the constant of the cost-benefit
  variant; the certificate still applies and is usually far tighter than either constant.
- **Held-out slots.** One slot in five is filled at random from held-out scenes, so the battery cannot consist
  only of yesterday's failures.

Worked instance (illustrative): 40 modes of total weight 100. Greedy gains of 31, 22, 14, 9, 6 and 4 cover 86.
The six largest remaining gains sum to 12, so OPT₆ ≤ 98 and the selection is within 88% of the best six-scene
battery, against a worst-case guarantee of 63%.

The selection fills Arena's battery `covering` ("a scene set chosen by the coverage selection, with one slot in
five drawn at random from held-out scenes"). It is decision core DC-10 of the program ("which scenes form a
battery"; family: submodular coverage; timescale: per battery revision). Rejected alternatives: a battery of the
most frequent failures (redundant); a battery made only of past failures (it overfits them, CX-294); a
calibrated item bank with a response model (considered and not examined further).

Tier Solid: a greedy algorithm with a classical guarantee.

## Technical approach

1. **Decision contract (carried verbatim in substance; it is the specification of the verb).**
   ```text
   Decision:          Which k scenes form the covering battery for robot class C?
   Loss:              Uncovered mode weight against battery runtime.
   Obligation:        Weighted coverage is monotone submodular; the certificate is checked on every
                      selection and stored with the battery.
   Failure modes:     The battery overfits past failures; the taxonomy changes under it.
   Fallback:          The standard battery (about 200 scenarios per robot class, frozen at M2).
   Fallback trigger:  Any identity change (model class, tier, kernel versions, taxonomy) since the incidence
                      was built.
   Budgeted mode:     Any greedy prefix is a valid battery with its own certificate.
   Interference:      A rare-failure bound refuses battery-selected episodes.
   Verification:      Lazy greedy equals plain greedy; brute-force optimum on instances of 20 scenes or fewer
                      lies under the certificate; the worked instance.
   ```
2. **Request.** Robot class C and task family (which fix the catalogues read); the reference set of designs; a
   size, either `--slots K` (cardinality mode) or `--budget-seconds T` (cost mode); optional `--incidence
   <inc:… | file>` to select from a stored or supplied incidence; `--prefixes` to return every prefix's
   certificate; `--algorithm lazy|plain` (default `lazy`; `plain` exists so the equality can be checked from
   the user's side); `--greedy-only` to return K greedy slots with no held-out slot, as a budgeted prefix
   marked `no-held-out-slot` that cannot be adopted as the `covering` battery; `--require-rule table` (input
   `require_rule`, one value `table`; used by canary audits and replay scenarios) to pin the decision to DC-10's
   table, so that a fallback is refused instead of applied (step 0).
3. **Pipeline (numbered).**
   0. The core's table. The verb is governed by decision core DC-10 (policy `nemesis.dc10.cover`). It loads
      DC-10's table against the core's record (`registries/cores/dc-10.toml`) with N28c-02's
      `CoreTable::load`. A table that is missing, whose content hash or version differs from the record, or
      that is bound to other identities yields `CoreState::FallbackInForce { reason }` with FROB-REF-871
      ("a decision-core table that is missing, stale against its contract, or not the registry version").
      N28c-02's surface rule for a governed verb then applies. With `--require-rule table` the request is
      refused outright with FROB-REF-871, exit 3 (`retry: after-change`; repairs: 1. regenerate the table with
      `cargo xtask check-generated --write`; 2. call again without the pin, accepting the fallback), before any
      incidence is read. Without the pin the verb completes on the core's fallback, the standard battery
      (`cover::resolve`'s `Fallback`, N26b-11): exit 2, FROB-REF-871 in the envelope's `warnings`, and the
      decision record and the result's `policy` block say `rule: "fallback"` with the loader's reason. With a
      current table the pin changes nothing: `rule: "table"` and the steps below.
   1. Resolve the incidence: load the named one, or aggregate the stored catalogues of the reference designs
      into one (a cheap, deterministic aggregation; no episode is run: the verb's kind is read).
   2. Staleness: compare the identities the incidence was built under with the identities in force. On any
      difference refuse with **FROB-REF-565** ("coverage selection on a stale incidence"). Envelope: `pointer`
      the incidence; `observed` and `required` the differing identity (for example kernel versions `k:41a0…`
      against `k:7be2…`); `retry: after-change`. Ranked repairs: (1) rebuild the incidence under the identities
      in force (re-run the reference designs' failure discovery, then call again); (2) use the standard battery,
      which is the fallback of the covering selection; (3) pin the request to the identities the incidence was
      built under, to reproduce an earlier selection as a historical record only. Exit code 3.
   3. Slots: with K slots, positions 5, 10, 15, … are held-out slots; the k = K − ⌊K/5⌋ others are greedy slots.
   4. Select the greedy slots by lazy greedy (or by the cost-benefit variant under a time budget).
   5. Compute the certificate from marginal gains recomputed against the final selection, and run the
      independent checker on it. A selection whose certificate fails its check is not returned: the verb
      answers with the fallback and the flag `certificate-check-failed`, exit 2, and logs an integrity finding.
   6. Fill the held-out slots by a keyed draw from held-out scenes that are not rows of the incidence.
   7. Write the decision record of the core (policy identity, identities read, incidence identity, the action
      and, in shadow, the baseline's action) and return the selection.
4. **Result, schema `frob.nemesis.cover.out/1` (the worked instance with seven slots).**
   ```json
   { "schema": "frob.nemesis.cover.out/1", "id": "cov:2b7e…", "robot_class": "quad-mammal",
     "task_family": "payload-transport", "mode": "cardinality", "slots": 7, "greedy_slots": 6, "held_out_slots": 1,
     "incidence": {"id": "inc:90c4…", "scenes": 18, "modes": 40, "total_weight": 100,
                   "built_under": {"model_class": "quad-mammal", "tier": "gpu-cuda/f32", "kernels": "k:41a0…",
                                   "taxonomy": "tax:3", "severity_table": "sev:1", "reference_set": "ref:5d…"}},
     "selection": [ {"slot": 1, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S1", "gain": 31, "covered": 31},
                    {"slot": 2, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S2", "gain": 22, "covered": 53},
                    {"slot": 3, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S3", "gain": 14, "covered": 67},
                    {"slot": 4, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S4", "gain": 9,  "covered": 76},
                    {"slot": 5, "kind": "held-out", "scene": "s:office-88@…"},
                    {"slot": 6, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S5", "gain": 6,  "covered": 82},
                    {"slot": 7, "kind": "greedy", "scene": "S6", "gain": 4,  "covered": 86} ],
     "covered_weight": 86, "uncovered_weight": 14,
     "certificate": {"kind": "top-k-remaining-gains", "k": 6, "remaining_gains": [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2],
                     "opt_upper_bound": 98, "ratio_lower_bound": "43/49", "worst_case_ratio": "1 - 1/e",
                     "check": "accepted"},
     "prefixes": [ {"k": 1, "covered": 31, "opt_upper_bound": 53}, {"k": 2, "covered": 53, "opt_upper_bound": 77},
                   {"k": 3, "covered": 67, "opt_upper_bound": 90}, {"k": 4, "covered": 76, "opt_upper_bound": 91},
                   {"k": 5, "covered": 82, "opt_upper_bound": 94}, {"k": 6, "covered": 86, "opt_upper_bound": 98} ],
     "battery_items": "…", "fallback": null, "discovery": true,
     "policy": {"id": "nemesis.dc10.cover", "version": 1, "rule": "table"} }
   ```
   43/49 = 0.8776: "within 88% of the best six-scene battery", against 1 − 1/e = 0.632.
5. **Verb.** `nemesis.cover`, kind read, first milestone M3: selects a covering scene set and returns it with
   its certificate. CLI `frob nemesis cover --class C --task-family F --slots K | --budget-seconds T
   [--reference R] [--incidence I] [--prefixes] [--algorithm lazy|plain] [--greedy-only] [--require-rule table]
   --json`. With `--incidence` the robot class and the task family are read from the incidence's
   own header and `--class` and `--task-family` may be omitted (the form the worked-instance calls use:
   `frob nemesis cover --incidence cover_worked_40x18.json --slots 7 --prefixes --json`); given together with
   it they must equal the header's, and a difference is FROB-REF-802 naming the option. Without `--incidence`
   both are required. Scope `nemesis:read`. It changes nothing: adoption of a selection into the `covering`
   battery is a recorded membership event of Arena's battery mechanism, which cites the selection identity and
   the certificate. Exit 0 for a selection with an accepted certificate; 2 when the answer is the fallback or
   carries a flag (`held-out-pool-short`, `certificate-check-failed`, fewer scenes than slots because every
   remaining gain is zero, or the DC-10 table not current, with FROB-REF-871 in `warnings`); 3 for FROB-REF-565
   and for FROB-REF-871 under `--require-rule table`. MCP tool `nemesis_cover` in the task cluster `batteries` (the
   plan's cluster table assigned it to none and the `attack` cluster is full at seven tools; the owner of the
   cluster table, NJL-05, added `batteries`, and the verb registry VERBS-nemesis records the row: kind read,
   scope `nemesis:read`, chapter refusals 565 and 871, the second Atelier's code, whose N28-REF row names this
   verb through DC-10; the generator appends 891, 734 and 828 because `--incidence` accepts a file).
6. **Evidence and determinism.** Integer weights and integer arithmetic throughout: class D0, identical bytes on
   every platform. The certificate is a checked arithmetic statement about the incidence, not about the world:
   the evidence block says proof class `Tested`, `claims: []`, and names the incidence identity. Every scene of
   a covering battery is discovery data: episodes run on it are battery-selected and are refused by a
   rare-failure bound and by the qualification lane.

      table the pin changes nothing.
- [ ] The worked instance returns gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4, covered weight 86, certificate 98, ratio 43/49, and
      the six prefix certificates 53, 77, 90, 91, 94, 98.
- [ ] A request on an incidence built under other kernel versions, another tier, another model class or another

- [ ] Seven slots give six greedy slots and one held-out slot at position 5; twenty slots give four held-out
      slots at positions 5, 10, 15 and 20.
- [ ] No selection is returned without an accepted certificate check.
- [ ] The verb writes nothing to the battery registry and runs no episode (asserted from the ledger); the

  incidence read.
- `cover_gate`: 1,000 random instances (the generator of N26b-10) through the library entry point.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_cover.sh` (N26b-12) drives `frob nemesis cover` on the worked instance, on 1,000 random
instances with both algorithms, on a stale incidence and on a live catalogue, and writes the receipt from which
the gate is recorded. Logged
per call: the incidence identity and the identities it was built under, the pick sequence with gains, covered
weight, the certificate with its remaining gains, the check's outcome, the held-out slots with their draw key,
and for a refusal the differing identity.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-669 (the worked instance: 86 covered; OPT₆ ≤ 98; within 88% of the best six-scene battery against a
worst-case guarantee of 63%; control: a selection that double-counts a mode, which reports 132 on this
instance). KA-704 (the same instance as the decision core's known answer; control: a certificate computed from
stale gains, which prints 99). PO-NEMESIS-03 as a property test and by brute force. CX-294 (a battery made of
past failures is a good battery). Metamorphic relations: relabelling scenes or modes changes nothing; adding a
scene that exposes no new mode changes neither the selection nor the covered weight; multiplying every weight by
a constant multiplies every reported weight by it and leaves the ratio unchanged. Determinism lanes: identical
bytes twice on one machine, in debug and release, and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The certificate bounds the best battery *for this incidence*. It says nothing about failure modes no
  reference design has shown; that is what the held-out slots and the catalogue's unseen-mode estimate are for.
- Frequencies in the catalogue come from the discovery stream (search, tilting, forks), not from the nominal
  randomization; a mode found only by exploration is marked so in the incidence and its weight is not a nominal
  frequency.
- Severity has no table in the plan; the incidence bead declares one with a default of 1 per failure class.
- The decision core's owner is listed as the evaluation crate while the selection runs here; the record is
  written where the decision is made and registered by the core-table bead (BD-309). DESIGN: the policy
  identity of DC-10 is `("nemesis.dc10.cover", 1)` in this bead and in BD-309 alike (BD-309 had written
  `arena.dc10.battery`). Reason: the name follows the crate and verb that take the decision and write its
  records, as for `atelier.dc01.promote` and `tether.dc09.dispatch`; the core's `owner` field in the core table
  stays `frob-arena`. BD-309, which depends on this bead, compares the record this verb writes with its DC-10
  row (`dc10_record_matches_contract`); this bead's test pins the identity it writes.
- Fallback is not failure: when the incidence is stale the standard battery is the plan.
