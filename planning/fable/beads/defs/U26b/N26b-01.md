## Background

A design campaign spends cheap stages (a static check, a baseline controller, a shared policy) to discard designs
before expensive ones (training a specialist, the referee). A comparison among survivors says nothing about what
was discarded: an e-value between the eighth and ninth finalists is silent about the design a screen threw away
at the first rung (the shortcut registered as CX-293). ADR-NEMESIS-07 rules: **screens are audited, not trusted.
Whatever a cheap stage discards is sampled, and a known winner is planted.** Rejected alternatives: trusting a
screen because its survivors compare well; calibrating a screen on the designs it advanced (biased toward
agreement). The risk register carries the same point as RISK-37 (a screen discards the winner; severity High).

Nemesis owns the one screening audit of the program (verb `nemesis.audit`, gate GATE-NEMESIS-07); the co-design
cascade applies it stage by stage and defines no second mechanism, verb or bound. The audit has two parts:
sampling the discarded strata (this bead and N26b-02) and planting a winner (N26b-03).

This bead builds the **audit lane**: which discarded designs are run at the next stage anyway. Three properties
make the lane evidence instead of a habit. It is *stratified*: discards are grouped by reason for discard, design
family and score band, so a screen that is wrong only for one family or only near its cut cannot hide in an
average. It is *drawn by a keyed random draw before any score exists*, so no score, and nobody who has seen a
score, can influence which designs are audited. And its runs are *fixed-size and never cancelled by a race*: an
audited design runs at the next stage at a fixed sample size as if it had survived, because an early-stopping
rule that cancels the designs it dislikes would erase exactly the evidence the audit exists to collect.

The lane has a second reader: it is the only calibration source for the rank-agreement measurement between rungs,
because it supplies designs that were not selected by the scores being calibrated.

Tier Solid: stratified sampling.

## Scope

In: `audit::plan` (the pre-score commitment), `audit::priority` (the keyed draw), `audit::strata` (keys and score
bands), `audit::quotas` (the spend share, allocation, merging), `audit::lane` (selection), the rules for running
lane members (same job, fixed n, no cancellation), spend accounting.
Out: the false-discard bound and the verdict per stratum (N26b-02); the planted winner (N26b-03); the audit
record, the label `screen-biased` and the bypass (N26b-04); the verb (N26b-05); the application to the stages of
the cascade and the rule that an unaudited rung is not trusted (seed BD-345, another unit).

## Technical approach

1. **The plan, committed before the stage runs (DESIGN sketch).**
   ```rust
   pub struct AuditPlan {
       campaign: CampaignId, stage: StageId, screen: ScreenId,   // the screen's identity and version
       stream: StreamId,              // the registered stream `nemesis-audit`
       key: Blake3,                   // derived from the campaign seed; never from a score
       share_of_spend: Ratio,         // 1/10 of the stage's spend (TARGET)
       n_audit: u32,                  // the fixed sample size at the next stage
       bands: [Ratio; 2],             // score-band edges, default 1/3 and 2/3
       rates: Option<Vec<(StratumKey, Ratio)>>,   // explicit per-stratum rates, if the manifest fixes them
       m_min: u32,                    // below this a stratum is merged upward; default 30
       delta: Ratio, tolerance: Ratio, top_k: u32, planted: Option<PlantCommitment>,
   }
   ```
   `commitment = BLAKE3(canonical bytes of AuditPlan)` is written into the campaign manifest hash. The ledger
   sequence of the commitment must precede the ledger sequence of the stage's first score record;
   `committed_before_scores` is computed from the ledger, never asserted by the caller. A stage whose plan was
   not committed first cannot be audited: its audit is `not-run` with the reason, which downstream is treated as
   a missing audit.
2. **The keyed draw.** Every design of the stage's population gets a priority
   `u(d) = keyed_draw(stream, key, stage, DesignId(d))`, a 64-bit integer read as a uniform number in [0, 1). It
   is a pure function of the committed key and the design's identity: it exists before any score, does not
   change when scores change, and can be recomputed by anyone holding the plan.
3. **Strata.** After the screen has decided, each discard gets
   `StratumKey { reason: DiscardReason, family: FamilyId, band: ScoreBand }`. `reason` is the screen's typed
   reason (score below the cut; a typed exclusion by an analysis or a contract; a refusal). `family` is the
   design family (the morphology class, or the generator's stratum). `band`: with cut c (the lowest advancing
   score among the designs the stage ranked together with this one: the whole population, or the design's
   allocation stratum where the stage advances per stratum) and y_min the lowest score among the family's
   scored discards, d = (c − y)/(c − y_min);
   `Near` if d < 1/3, `Middle` if 1/3 ≤ d < 2/3, `Far` otherwise; a design excluded without a score is
   `Unscored`. A caller may append further categorical components (the cascade adds the archive cell).
4. **How many.** Audit budget B = `share_of_spend` × the stage's planned spend (the estimate fixed in the
   campaign plan, in the campaign's cost unit). With c_next the estimated cost of one next-stage evaluation at
   `n_audit`, M = ⌊B / c_next⌋ lane members.
5. **Who (numbered).**
   1. *Pre-sample.* With N the population size and r₀ = M/N, every design with u < r₀ is a lane member. This
      set was fixed before any score and is a uniform sample of the whole population. Members the screen
      advances run at the next stage anyway, now at fixed n and uncancellable.
   2. *Quotas for discard strata.* M_d = M − (pre-sampled survivors). With n_s discards in stratum s and N_d
      discards in all: q_s = ⌊M_d·n_s/N_d⌋, the remainder given one each to the largest fractional parts (ties by
      stratum key order), capped at n_s; every non-empty stratum gets at least one when M_d is at least the
      number of non-empty strata. Explicit rates r_s in the plan replace this: q_s = min(n_s, ⌈r_s·n_s⌉), and the
      resulting spend is reported beside the 10% target.
   3. *Merging.* A stratum with q_s < `m_min` is merged upward in a fixed order: drop the score band, then the
      reason, leaving the design family. Merges are recorded. A stratum still under `m_min` is audited with
      what the budget buys; its bound is weak and is reported as it is.
   4. *Selection.* In each stratum the audited discards are the q_s discards with the smallest u (ties by
      design identity). The pre-sampled discards are a prefix of that order, so the two rules agree.
   Each member records `(design, stratum, u, inclusion: Presample | StratumQuota)`.
6. **Running lane members.** An audited design is submitted to the next stage as the same job type with the same
   budget and the same world addresses as a survivor; the evaluating job cannot tell which it is running (the
   audit mark lives only in the sealed lane record). It runs to `n_audit` and is never cancelled or re-weighted
   by a race or an e-process: `AuditLane::is_member(design)` is consulted by every early-stopping controller. A
   member that was cancelled or re-weighted anyway voids the audit of its stratum (`void`, with the episode).
7. **Outputs.** `AuditLane { members, quotas, merges, spend: { stage, audit } }` and, once evaluated,
   `AuditOutcome { design, stratum, lower_score, next_stage_score, n, addresses, cancelled }` per member.
   Selection is exact (class D0); outcomes inherit the determinism class of the next stage's tier. In the
   ladder's allocation record the lane is the field `audit_lane: Vec<DesignId>` ("drawn by a keyed stream
   before any score exists; never cancelled"), and the ladder's calibrated allocation rule is "calibrated on
   the audit lane only"; this module is what fills that field for an audited stage.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `u(d)` is unchanged under any change of scores, decisions or population order (property test), and changes
      for every design when the key changes.
- [ ] A plan committed after the first score record gives `committed_before_scores = false` and an audit that is
      `not-run`; a plan committed before gives `true`.
- [ ] The worked allocation reproduces: stage spend 9,000 GPU-seconds, share 1/10, c_next = 10 GPU-seconds →
      M = 90; population 1,000 → r₀ = 0.09; with 9 pre-sampled survivors M_d = 81; discard strata of sizes 400,
      300, 150, 50 get quotas 36, 27, 14, 4.
- [ ] Within a stratum the selected set is exactly the q smallest priorities; over 10,000 keys each discard of a
      50-member stratum with quota 5 is selected with frequency 0.100 ± 0.012.
- [ ] A stratum under `m_min` is merged band first, then reason, and the merge is recorded.
- [ ] The job specification of a lane member is byte-identical to a survivor's except for the design identity.
- [ ] A race controller cannot cancel a lane member; a forced cancellation marks the stratum `void`.
- [ ] Audit spend and stage spend are recorded separately and their ratio is printed.

### Known answers and negative controls
The allocation and band examples are hand-checkable. CX-293 (a comparison among survivors validates the screen)
is the shortcut this lane exists to block. KA-136 (the ladder's conformal index: n = 39 audit designs and
α = 0.05 select the 38th smallest residual, and fewer than 19 cannot support α = 0.05) is carried by the
ladder's beads and is why the cascade merges strata upward before it falls back. Planted defects the tests
must catch: a draw seeded after scores exist (the commitment test); a lane chosen as "the discards closest to
the cut" (the uniformity test); a lane member stopped by an e-process (the cancellation test); quotas computed
from realized instead of planned spend (the worked example). Determinism lanes: lane membership identical twice
on one machine, across worker counts and across platforms (integer arithmetic only).

## Considerations

- "Before any score exists" and "stratified by score band" are reconciled by fixing the random numbers first and
  the strata afterwards: membership of a stratum depends on the screen, selection inside it does not.
- A tenth of a cheap stage's spend can buy very few evaluations at an expensive next stage (81 designs at 10 s
  each are 13.5 minutes; a tenth is 81 s, less than one 3-minute evaluation). The lane then has M = 0 or 1 and
  every bound is weak; the result says so and the stage is not treated as audited.
- The cascade's own calibration needs at least 19 lane designs per stratum at α = 0.05 and merges strata upward
  to the morphology class first; `m_min` here is about the false-discard bound (30 clean audited discards bound
  the rate at 9.5%).
- Audit episodes are campaign data in the discovery lane; they are never a confirmation sample.
- Atelier's refusal for an audit episode that was cancelled or re-weighted, or for calibration from survivors
  (FROB-REF-546), is raised at the cascade; this module reports the voided stratum that triggers it.
