## Background

`nemesis.audit` is the user-visible surface of the screening audit: the one mechanism by which the program learns
whether a cheap stage of a design campaign threw away a design it should have kept. A comparison among survivors
cannot tell; the audit samples what was discarded, stratum by stratum, runs the sample at the next stage at a
fixed size as if it had survived, bounds the false-discard rate exactly, and checks that a planted winner of
known rank survived. A missed plant or an audited miss rate above the declared tolerance labels the campaign's
results `screen-biased` and bypasses the stage for the affected strata until it is re-qualified.

The verb registry describes it as: "audit a screening stage with stratified discards and a planted winner; the
one screening audit, applied by the cascade". Kind run, first milestone M4. The six-stage co-design cascade calls
it for its screening stage and each stage is also callable alone, so the verb must work both inside a running
campaign and on a self-contained stage record.

Tier Solid. Gate GATE-NEMESIS-07 (Known answer; M4; TARGET): a planted winner removed by a biased screen is
reported in 100 of 100 runs; the stratified bound covers the planted false-discard rate in at least 95% of 1,000
synthetic campaigns.

## Scope

In: the verb in its two phases, its request and result schemas, its exit codes, refusals, estimate, cancellation
and idempotency; the offline form on a stage record; the crate-level gate test.
Out: the lane, bound, plant and record mechanisms (N26b-01 to N26b-04); the synthetic campaigns and screens
(N26b-06); the end-to-end script that records the gate (N26b-07); the cascade's use of the result (BD-345); the
registry row (N26b-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Two phases, one verb (DESIGN).** An audit has a part that must happen before the stage's scores exist and a
   part that can only happen after.
   - `frob nemesis audit prepare <campaign> --stage <n> [--share 1/10] [--n-audit N] [--delta 0.05]
     [--tolerance 0.05] [--top-k K] [--plant <design> --poorly <kind>] --key <k> --json`
     builds the audit plan, admits the plant (refusing with FROB-REF-566 when its rank is not on record from a
     full evaluation), writes the commitment into the campaign manifest, and returns the commitment hash. It
     refuses to prepare a stage that already has a score record: the result is then `status: "not-run"`, reason
     `scores-exist-before-commitment`, exit 2 (the plan names no refusal code for this case).
   - `frob nemesis audit run <campaign> --stage <n> [--reveal <file>] [--budget …] --key <k> --json`
     reads the stage's decisions, selects the lane, dispatches the lane members to the next stage at the fixed
     sample size, waits for them, computes the per-stratum rates, bounds and verdicts, tracks the plant, checks
     the reveal when one is given (otherwise the plant's outcome stays sealed and is completed by a later call
     with `--reveal`), writes the audit record, the label and any bypass, exports the calibration rows, and
     returns the record.
2. **Offline form.** `frob nemesis audit run --stage-record <file> --outcomes <file> [--reveal <file>]` audits a
   self-contained stage record (schema `frob.nemesis.stage/1`: the plan with its commitment, the population with
   families, each design's score and decision with reason, the survivors' next-stage scores) against a table of
   next-stage outcomes, of which only the rows of lane members are read. Nothing is simulated. The record is
   marked `origin: synthetic` unless the stage record resolves to a real campaign in the ledger. This is how the
   gate's synthetic campaigns and the cascade's replayed campaigns are audited by the real binary.
3. **Result.** The audit record `frob.nemesis.audit/1` (strata with size, audited count, false discards, rate,
   upper bound, verdict; merges; the stage-level bound; the plant's outcome; the label; the bypass list; the
   calibration artifact), inside the standard envelope. Evidence block: class `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ,
   the tier of the next stage, the determinism class of the lane's episodes, `claims: []`, `reality: no-claim`.
4. **Exit codes.** 0: completed, every stratum `passed` and the plant `survived`. 2: completed with findings or
   flags: any stratum `screen-biased`, `bound-above-tolerance` or `void`; a discarded winner found; a plant
   `missed` or `not-run`; the label set. 3: refused (FROB-REF-566; a schema failure). 4: the lane's estimated cost
   exceeds the budget at admission (the Tether budget refusal; the estimate is attached). 5 in a build before M4:
   the verb is registered and not implemented (the capability refusal FROB-REF-724). 8: accepted and running.
5. **Long-verb behaviour.** `run` spends compute (M next-stage evaluations at `n_audit`); it takes an
   idempotency key and a budget, accepts the bounded synchronous wait (to completion on the CLI; 5,000 ms by
   default and at most 30,000 on MCP) and otherwise returns a job handle with progress in lane members
   evaluated out of M. `--estimate` prints M, the per-stratum quotas' expected sizes and the cost before
   anything runs. Cancellation drains running evaluations and finalizes; a cancelled audit writes no record and
   voids nothing (its lane members were not cancelled by a race; the audit simply did not finish) and resumes
   by key. A repeated `prepare` with the same key returns the same commitment.
6. **Surface.** Verb `nemesis.audit`; MCP tool `nemesis_audit` in the task cluster `ladder` (with `opt_cascade`,
   `eval_rank_agreement`, `cores_show`, `report_campaign`, `explain_compare`, `predict_report`); scope
   `nemesis:run`, and the campaign administration scope for `--reveal`. Refusal list in the registry:
   FROB-REF-566 and the common budget, scope and schema codes.
7. **What the cascade gets.** The record identity, the label, the bypass list and `bypass_in_force`; it applies
   them (rung `uninformative` in the affected strata, discarded designs re-opened through the lane). The verb
   itself changes no rung.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-07 rows hold in the crate-level test `audit_gate` on the synthetic fixtures (N26b-06): the
      planted winner removed by the biased screen is reported in 100 of 100 runs; the unbiased screen passes in
      100 of 100; the per-stratum bound covers the planted false-discard rate in at least 95% of stratum rows
      and the stage-level bound covers the planted overall rate in at least 95% of 1,000 synthetic campaigns.
      The recorded gate values come from the end-to-end script (N26b-07).
- [ ] `prepare` after a score exists returns `not-run` with its reason; `run` on a stage with no commitment
      returns `not-run`; neither writes a passing record.
- [ ] `prepare` with a plant that has no rank record returns FROB-REF-566, exit 3, with ranked repairs.
- [ ] `run` on the worked stage (stage spend 9,000 GPU-seconds, c_next 10 GPU-seconds, population 1,000) audits
      90 lane members, reports quotas 36, 27, 14, 4 for the four discard strata and a spend ratio of 0.100.
- [ ] The offline form on a synthetic stage record simulates nothing and marks the record `synthetic`; the
      audit's own arithmetic (lane, bounds, verdicts) is host-only, and lane evaluations run on whatever tier
      the next stage uses, CPU tiers included.
- [ ] Exit codes follow the table; a repeated call with the same key returns the stored envelope
      (`idempotent_replay: true`).
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and MCP with schemas, an example and a refusal list;
      cancellation is tested (request, drain, finalize, resume by key); outputs carry the claim product;
      `CONTRACT.md` lists the verb and the two schemas.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-668 (a planted winner removed by a deliberately biased screen is reported; no top-k design among 30 audited
discards bounds the false-discard rate at 9.5% with 95% confidence, and 59 bound it at 5.0%; control: a screen
with no bias passes). CX-293. Planted defects the verb-level tests must catch: an audit that inspects survivors
only (reports nothing on the biased screen); a `run` that draws the lane with a key created at run time (the
commitment check fails); an audit that lets the race cancel lane members (strata become `void`).

## Considerations

- The two phases are DESIGN: the plan names one verb and one sentence of behaviour; a single invocation cannot
  both precede and follow the scores. Inside the cascade `prepare` is called when the campaign manifest is
  sealed and `run` when the stage closes.
- A stage whose commitment is missing has no refusal code in the plan; `not-run` is a completed result that the
  cascade treats as a missing audit. The case is flagged for the owner of the refusal registry.
- Cost is the lane: a tenth of the stage's spend by default, spent at the next stage. The estimate states it.
- Audit episodes are campaign data in the discovery lane, never confirmation samples.
