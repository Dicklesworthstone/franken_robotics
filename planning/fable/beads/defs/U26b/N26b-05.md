## Background

`nemesis.audit` is the user-visible surface of the screening audit: the one mechanism by which the program learns
whether a cheap stage of a design campaign threw away a design it should have kept. A comparison among survivors
cannot tell; the audit samples what was discarded, stratum by stratum, runs the sample at the next stage at a
fixed size as if it had survived, bounds the false-discard rate exactly, and checks that a planted winner of
known rank survived. A missed plant or an audited miss rate above the declared tolerance labels the campaign's
results `screen-biased` and bypasses the stage for the affected strata until it is re-qualified.

The verb registry describes it as: "audit a screening stage with stratified discards and a planted winner; the
one screening audit, applied by the cascade". Kind run, first milestone M4. The six-stage co-design cascade calls
it for its screening stage and each stage is also callable alone, so the verb must work both inside a running
campaign and on a self-contained stage record.

Tier Solid. Gate GATE-NEMESIS-07 (Known answer; M4; TARGET): a planted winner removed by a biased screen is
reported in 100 of 100 runs; the stratified bound covers the planted false-discard rate in at least 95% of 1,000
synthetic campaigns.

There is one screening audit in the system, and the registry holds no second name for it. The adversarial
chapter owns the mechanism; the cascade applies it stage by stage and defines no second mechanism, verb or bound.
The cascade unit's own draft of this verb (N28b-29) is superseded by this bead and its parts are folded into the
five beads of the audit: the bound into N26b-02, the planted winner into N26b-03, the record and the label into
N26b-04, the gate batteries into N26b-06, the verb here.

Rejected alternatives: auditing a sample chosen after scoring (the sample can then depend on the scores through
any code path that sees them; this is why the verb has a phase that precedes the scores); the approximation
ln(1/δ)/m for the bound (10.0% at m = 30 where the exact value is 9.5%; the exact form costs nothing); a planted
winner whose "known rank" is a guess (refused, FROB-REF-566).

## Scope

In: the verb in its two phases, its request and result schemas, its exit codes, refusals, estimate, cancellation and
idempotency; the offline form on a stage record; the crate-level gate test and the Gauntlet entry `GATE-NEMESIS-07`
of the tier target `g1_known_answers` that runs the same batteries. Out: the lane, bound, plant and record
mechanisms (N26b-01 to N26b-04); the keyed draw and the fixed-n evaluation on the cascade's side, which the lane
calls (N28b-13); the synthetic campaigns and screens (N26b-06, a dependency: `audit_gate` and the Gauntlet
entry run on its frozen registry); the end-to-end script whose receipt the gate is
recorded from (N26b-07); the cascade's use of the result (BD-345) and its own end-to-end script (N28b-31); prune
soundness, Atelier's third audit (N28b-30); the design corpus's winners (BD-248); the registry row (VERBS-nemesis,
the registry of record for the cluster's seven verbs, which superseded N26b-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Two phases, one verb (DESIGN).** An audit has a part that must happen before the stage's scores exist and a
   part that can only happen after.
   - `frob nemesis audit <campaign> --action prepare --stage <n> [--share 1/10] [--n-audit N] [--delta 0.05]
     [--tolerance 0.05] [--top-k K] [--plant <design> --poorly <kind>] --key <k> --json`
     builds the audit plan, admits the plant (refusing with FROB-REF-566 when its rank is not on record from a
     full evaluation), writes the commitment into the campaign manifest, and returns the commitment hash. It
     refuses to prepare a stage that already has a score record: the result is then `status: "not-run"`, reason
     `scores-exist-before-commitment`, exit 2 (the plan names no refusal code for this case).
   - `frob nemesis audit <campaign> --stage <n> [--reveal <file>] [--budget …] --key <k> --json` (the default
     action `run`) reads the stage's decisions, selects the lane, dispatches the lane members to the next stage
     at the fixed sample size, waits for them, computes the per-stratum rates, bounds and verdicts, tracks the plant, checks
     the reveal when one is given (otherwise the plant's outcome stays sealed and is completed by a later call
     with `--reveal`), writes the audit record, the label and any bypass, exports the calibration rows, and
     returns the record.
   - *Default action and spellings (DESIGN).* `run` is the default action: `frob nemesis audit <campaign>
     --stage <n> --key <k> --json` with no `--action` is `run` (the registry's example,
     `frob nemesis audit camp:12 --stage 3 --key aud-1 --json`, is this form), and `prepare` is listed in the
     registry row's `actions` and selected only with `--action prepare` (NJL-01 action rule 1; `prepare` is a
     `run` action, scope `nemesis:run`, so it carries `--key`). The default has no name to pass: `--action run`
     is FROB-REF-802, and so is an action word in a positional place (`frob nemesis audit prepare camp:12 …`
     reads `prepare` as the campaign and `camp:12` as a second positional argument). The campaign may also be
     given as `--campaign <id>`; the two spellings are the same argument, and giving both with different values
     is FROB-REF-802. Reason: the cascade and its end-to-end script call the audit as one command per stage
     after the manifest was sealed with the plan, and one verb must serve both callers.
   - *Inputs read by `run`.* From the campaign manifest: the audit plan (strata definition, audit share, fixed
     n, δ, tolerance, k, the planted winner's commitment). From the ledger: the stage's committed draw (the
     `audit-draw` evidence record written at admission, before any score of the stage), the population admitted
     to the stage, the stage's decisions (who advanced, who was discarded and for which typed reason: excluded
     by a named analysis, below the cut, raced out, pruned by a contract), and the next stage's scores of every
     design that ran there, survivors and lane members alike. The draw is a uniform sample of the stage's
     population made before scores existed, so within any stratum defined afterwards it is a uniform sample of
     that stratum; that is what makes the per-stratum bound valid.
   - *Only the committed lane is audited.* The computation admits as audited discards only designs named by the
     stage's committed draw. A design outside it that a caller offers as audited (in a stage record, an outcome
     table or a request field) is refused with **FROB-REF-546** ("calibration from survivors, or an audit episode
     cancelled or re-weighted"; the cascade's code, defined by the Atelier refusal registry N28-REF, whose trigger
     covers a set containing a design not drawn by the keyed audit stream), exit 3; `pointer` the offending design's
     entry, `observed` its identity and `not in committed draw`, `required` a member of the draw with the commitment
     hash; repair "redraw the audit lane by key"; `retry: after-change`. An audit-lane episode in state cancelled,
     or a lane design re-weighted after its score existed, is not refused here: the lane's enforcement points refuse
     the cancellation itself with 546 (N28b-13), and a member cancelled or re-weighted anyway voids its stratum
     (verdict `void`, with the episode, N26b-01 and N26b-02; exit 2), so the other strata are still audited. Outcome
     rows of designs outside the lane are never read. A record that is malformed in itself (missing fields, wrong
     types) remains a schema failure, also exit 3.
2. **Offline form.** `frob nemesis audit --stage-record <file> --outcomes <file> [--reveal <file>] --key <k>
   --json` (the default action `run`, with no campaign) audits a self-contained stage record (schema
   `frob.nemesis.stage/1`: the plan with its commitment, the population with families, each design's score and decision with reason, the survivors' next-stage scores)
   against a table of next-stage outcomes, of which only the rows of lane members are read. Nothing is simulated. The record is
   marked `origin: synthetic` unless the stage record resolves to a real campaign in the ledger. This is how the
   gate's synthetic campaigns and the cascade's replayed campaigns are audited by the real binary.
3. **Schemas and result.** Request schema `frob.nemesis.audit.in/1` (one flat schema with the input `action`,
   whose one value is `prepare` and whose absence runs the default `run`, and the fields of each action, each
   described as belonging to its action; a field given with the other action is FROB-REF-802, NJL-01); output
   schema `frob.nemesis.audit.out/1`, whose payload is the audit record `frob.nemesis.audit/1` (strata with size, audited count, false discards, rate, upper bound,
   verdict; merges; the stage-level bound; the plant's outcome; the label; the bypass list; the calibration
   artifact) for `run`, and the commitment hash for `prepare`, inside the standard envelope. There is one name
   for the record: `frob.nemesis.audit/1` (N26b-04). Evidence block: class `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, the
   tier of the next stage, the determinism class of the lane's episodes, `claims: []`, `reality: no-claim`. The
   evidence is scoped to the tier, the population and the generator identity of the audited stage, all three
   named in the record.
   DESIGN: `claims` is empty. The record bounds a screen's rate of discarded winners on one population; none of
   the seven claims has a permitted wording for that statement (a Design comparison reads "B beats A on a
   metric" or "qualified at level α"), so a report cites the record by its identity and prints the bound with m
   beside it, and never cites it as a Design comparison. The gate GATE-NEMESIS-07 is a Known-answer claim about
   the audit on synthetic fixtures.
   The record is a pure function of the ledger at a frontier: computing it twice, or with different worker
   counts, yields identical canonical bytes.
4. **Exit codes.** 0: completed, every stratum `passed` and the plant `survived`. 2: completed with findings or
   flags: any stratum `screen-biased`, `bound-above-tolerance` or `void`; a discarded winner found; a plant `missed`
   or `not-run`; the label set. 3: refused (FROB-REF-566; FROB-REF-546; a schema failure). 4: the lane's estimated
   cost exceeds the budget at admission (the Tether budget refusal; the estimate is attached). 5 in a build before
   M4: the verb is registered and not implemented (the capability refusal FROB-REF-724). 8: accepted and running.
5. **Long-verb behaviour.** `run` spends compute (M next-stage evaluations at `n_audit`); it takes an
   idempotency key and a budget, accepts the bounded synchronous wait (to completion on the CLI; 5,000 ms by
   default and at most 30,000 on MCP) and otherwise returns a job handle with progress in lane members
   evaluated out of M. `--estimate` prints M, the per-stratum quotas' expected sizes and the cost before
   anything runs. Cancellation drains running evaluations and finalizes; a cancelled audit writes no record and
   voids nothing (its lane members were not cancelled by a race; the audit simply did not finish) and resumes
   by key. A repeated `prepare` with the same key returns the same commitment.
6. **Surface.** Verb `nemesis.audit`; MCP tool `nemesis_audit` in the task cluster `ladder` (with `opt_cascade`,
   `eval_rank_agreement`, `cores_show`, `report_campaign`, `explain_compare`, `predict_report`); scope
   `nemesis:run`, and the campaign administration scope for `--reveal`. Refusal list in the registry
   (VERBS-nemesis): FROB-REF-566 and FROB-REF-546, and the common budget, scope and schema codes.
7. **What the cascade gets.** The record identity, the label, the bypass list and `bypass_in_force`; it applies
   them (rung `uninformative` in the affected strata, discarded designs re-opened through the lane). The verb
   itself changes no rung. Layer: `frob-nemesis`, reading Chronicle; apart from the lane's next-stage
   evaluations, which the campaign machinery dispatches, it runs no simulation of its own.
8. **Measuring GATE-NEMESIS-07.** Two synthetic batteries on fixture scorers (N26b-06), both through the offline
   form of the real handler: (a) 100 runs in which a deliberately biased screen (it under-scores one design
   family by a fixed offset) removes the planted winner; the audit must report the loss in 100 of 100, and the
   unbiased screen must pass in 100 of 100; (b) 1,000 synthetic campaigns with a planted false-discard rate per
   stratum (true top-k designs placed among discards at known rates between 0 and 20%); the stratified bound
   must cover the planted rate in at least 95%. The batteries exist once, as the test `audit_gate`; the
   Gauntlet entry is a row of the known-answer tier target (the gate is a Known-answer claim), run as
   `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-NEMESIS-07` (there is
   no per-gate test target; ADR-GAUNT-01, BD-025): a thin entry that runs `audit_gate` on the frozen registry of
   N26b-06 and prints the four counts, so that the cascade's script `scripts/e2e/screening_audit.sh` (N28b-31)
   can call it as a step beside its own gate's entry. The gate is recorded once, from the receipt of
   `scripts/e2e/nemesis_audit.sh` (N26b-07), by a session that did not implement the audit; the cascade's gate
   receipt (BD-345) cites that record and does not write a second one.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-07 rows hold in the crate-level test `audit_gate` on the synthetic fixtures (N26b-06): the
      planted winner removed by the biased screen is reported in 100 of 100 runs; the unbiased screen passes in
      100 of 100; the per-stratum bound covers the planted false-discard rate in at least 95% of stratum rows
      and the stage-level bound covers the planted overall rate in at least 95% of 1,000 synthetic campaigns.
      The recorded gate values come from the end-to-end script (N26b-07).
- [ ] `prepare` after a score exists returns `not-run` with its reason; `run` on a stage with no commitment
      returns `not-run`; neither writes a passing record.
- [ ] `prepare` with a plant that has no rank record returns FROB-REF-566, exit 3, with ranked repairs.
- [ ] `run` on the worked stage (stage spend 9,000 GPU-seconds, c_next 10 GPU-seconds, population 1,000) audits
      90 lane members, reports quotas 36, 27, 14, 4 for the four discard strata and a spend ratio of 0.100.
- [ ] The offline form on a synthetic stage record simulates nothing and marks the record `synthetic`; the
      audit's own arithmetic (lane, bounds, verdicts) is host-only, and lane evaluations run on whatever tier
      the next stage uses, CPU tiers included.
- [ ] Exit codes follow the table; a repeated call with the same key returns the stored envelope
      (`idempotent_replay: true`).
- [ ] The bare form `frob nemesis audit <campaign> --stage <n> --key <k> --json` and the form with
      `--campaign <id>` both run the `run` phase and return the same envelope; `--action prepare` runs
      `prepare`; `--action run` and an action word in a positional place are FROB-REF-802, exit 3.
- [ ] The audit uses only designs in the stage's committed draw; a design outside it offered as an audited
      discard is refused with FROB-REF-546, exit 3, and no outcome row of a non-member is read.
- [ ] Before the reveal no output of any verb identifies the plant: every envelope the campaign produced is
      scanned for the plant's identity in a distinguishing position (a field, an order, a label or a listing
      that sets it apart from its neighbours) and none is found.
- [ ] The record is identical when computed twice and across worker counts (canonical bytes compared).
- [ ] `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-NEMESIS-07` runs
      the two batteries on the frozen registry and prints the same four counts as `audit_gate`; with
      `--features gauntlet-mutations` it reports the registered controls of N26b-06 caught.
- [ ] The verb is reachable through `frob --json` and the MCP tool `nemesis_audit` with schemas, an example and
      a refusal list; cancellation is tested (request, drain, finalize, resume by key) and a cancelled wait
      leaves no partial record; outputs carry the claim product; `frob-nemesis/CONTRACT.md` lists the verb and
      its schemas and documents the bound, the seal and the label.

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/audit_verb.rs` (the real verb handler on synthetic stage records and a synthetic
next-stage evaluator, both labelled `synthetic`):
- `prepare_then_run_happy_path`; `prepare_after_scores_not_run`; `run_without_commitment_not_run`.
- `plant_refused_566`; `plant_sealed_until_reveal`; `reveal_completes_record`.
- `worked_stage_quotas_and_spend`.
- `exit_code_table` (0, 2 for each finding kind, 3, 4, 8).
- `offline_form_simulates_nothing` (zero episodes in the ledger after the call).
- `idempotent_prepare_and_run`; `cancel_and_resume`.
- `audit_gate` (the four counts of the acceptance criterion, on fixed seeds), including `coverage_simulation`:
  1,000 synthetic campaigns → coverage of the planted rate at least 0.95.
- `default_action_is_run` and `campaign_flag_equals_positional`; `action_spellings`: `--action prepare`
  prepares; `--action run`, `frob nemesis audit prepare camp:12 --stage 3 …` and `--reveal` given with
  `--action prepare` are each FROB-REF-802 with the pointer at the input.
- `non_lane_design_refused_546`: a stage record that marks as audited a design absent from the committed draw
  is refused with FROB-REF-546, exit 3, the design named in `pointer` and the repair "redraw the audit lane by
  key"; the same record with that design removed is admitted (the twin); a lane episode in state cancelled is
  not refused but voids its stratum alone (`cancelled_member_voids_stratum`); a record with a missing field is a
  schema failure, not 546.
- `no_envelope_identifies_plant_before_reveal` (the envelope scan over a whole synthetic campaign) and its
  control: a build that marks the plant in the population listing fails the scan.
- `record_pure_function_of_ledger`: twice, and with 1 and 8 workers, equal bytes.
- `mcp_tool_matches_registry`: the generated tool's schema equals the registry's.

The entry `GATE-NEMESIS-07` in the static table of `crates/frob-conformance/tests/g1_known_answers.rs`: the thin
entry over `audit_gate` on the frozen registry.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_audit.sh` (N26b-07) drives both phases and the offline form with the real `frob` binary and
writes the receipt the gate is recorded from. Every line carries the command, exit code, wall time, the evidence
block, the audit record identity, the label, and per stratum the counts, bound and verdict. For the gate batteries
it logs per run the screen's bias, the plant's fate as reported against its true fate, per stratum m, the false
discards found, the bound and the planted rate, and the coverage tally against 0.95, with the gate named in the
`summary` line. The cascade's script `scripts/e2e/screening_audit.sh` (N28b-31) drives `frob opt cascade` and `frob
nemesis audit` on its own clean and planted seeded populations; it exercises this verb and does not record this
gate.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-668 (a planted winner removed by a deliberately biased screen is reported; no top-k design among 30 audited
discards bounds the false-discard rate at 9.5% with 95% confidence, and 59 bound it at 5.0%; control: a screen
with no bias passes) and its twin on the cascade's side, KA-706 (the same two bounds, to the printed digits).
CX-293 (a comparison among survivors does not validate the screen). Planted defects the verb-level tests must
catch: an audit that inspects survivors only (reports nothing on the biased screen); a `run` that draws the lane
with a key created at run time (the commitment check fails); an audit whose sample is drawn among discards
after scoring by a rule correlated with the score (coverage of the planted rate falls below 95% in battery (b),
which the gate test must detect); an audit that lets the race cancel lane members (strata become `void`); a
plant marked in the population listing (the envelope scan fails). Determinism: the record is a pure function of
the ledger at a frontier; identical twice and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The two phases are DESIGN: the plan names one verb and one sentence of behaviour; a single invocation cannot
  both precede and follow the scores. Inside the cascade `prepare` is called when the campaign manifest is
  sealed and `run` when the stage closes.
- A stage whose commitment is missing has no refusal code in the plan; `not-run` is a completed result that the
  cascade treats as a missing audit. The case is flagged for the owner of the refusal registry.
- Cost is the lane: a tenth of the stage's spend by default, spent at the next stage. The estimate states it.
  The planted winner costs one more design's evaluation through the cascade. Both are charged to the campaign.
- Audit episodes are campaign data in the discovery lane, never confirmation samples.
- RISK-37 (a screen discards the winner; High; owner the Atelier lead): this audit and the cascade's trust rule
  that consumes it (BD-345) are the named mitigations.
- OPEN-ATEL-5: classes with no earlier specialist result have no eligible planted winner; the control is
  `not-run` there, the verb exits 2, and the cascade's gate for those classes stays blocked.
- The cascade-side beads (BD-345, N28b-31) were drafted against the superseded draft and use its spellings. The
  mapping is fixed here and in N26b-04: record `frob.audit/1` is `frob.nemesis.audit/1`; stratum status
  `under-sampled` is the verdict `bound-above-tolerance` and `failed` is `screen-biased`; plant status
  `reached-finalists` is `survived` and `lost` is `missed`; `--campaign <id>` is the positional campaign.
