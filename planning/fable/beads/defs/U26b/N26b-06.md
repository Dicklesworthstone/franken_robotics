## Background

A gate that nothing can fail validates nothing. GATE-NEMESIS-07 (Known answer; M4; TARGET) states: a planted
winner removed by a biased screen is reported in 100 of 100 runs; the stratified bound covers the planted
false-discard rate in at least 95% of 1,000 synthetic campaigns. The chapter's known answer adds the control: a
screen with no bias passes. The verification program lists screening audits among the certifiers that are
themselves certified, with the test "a deliberately biased screen and an unbiased one" and the threshold "the
planted winner's removal is reported; the unbiased screen passes; 30 clean audited discards bound the rate at
9.5%".

None of that can be measured on real campaigns, where nobody knows the true false-discard rate. This bead builds
the planted material: synthetic screening stages in which the truth is known by construction. A *synthetic
campaign* is a population of designs with a hidden final quality, a screen that scores them, and a table of
next-stage outcomes; the number of true top designs among the discards of each stratum is planted exactly. A
*biased screen* is one whose score penalizes a design family whatever its quality, so that it discards a planted
winner of that family every time.

Everything here is `synthetic`: it tests the audit, not any robot. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the generator for synthetic stage records and outcome tables, its example target and the synthetic scratch
campaign; the two screens; the 100 biased-screen runs and 100 unbiased-screen runs; the 1,000 coverage campaigns
with their planted rates; the exact expected coverage of the study, computed by enumeration; the registry entry,
frozen and hashed; the registered mutants. Out: the audit mechanism (N26b-01 to N26b-04); the verb, its test
`audit_gate` and the Gauntlet entry `GATE-NEMESIS-07` of `g1_known_answers` that run the two batteries on this
registry (N26b-05); the script that measures the gate (N26b-07); the cascade's seeded population for its own audit
gate (BD-377, another unit), which the cascade's script (N28b-31) uses. These two batteries are built once, here:
the cascade unit's superseded draft of the audit (N28b-29) described the same 100 biased-screen runs and 1,000
campaigns, and nothing else builds them.

## Technical approach

1. **Where (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/planted/nemesis_audit/`: `generator.rs` (pure, seeded by
   the registered stream `nemesis-audit-fixtures`), `manifest.toml` (seeds, counts, the expected-coverage table,
   the registry hash), and generated files written on demand, never committed: one stage record
   (`frob.nemesis.stage/1`) and one outcome table per campaign.
2. **A synthetic stage (numbered construction).**
   1. Population: N designs in F families (F = 4), each with a hidden final quality q ∈ [0, 1] and a boolean
      `true_top` (membership of the final top band of size k_top).
   2. Screen score: y = q + b(family) + σ·z, with z a standard normal draw from the fixture stream. The screen
      advances the top third of the whole population by y (the fixed halving rule, η = 3) and discards the rest
      with reason `score-below-cut`; a second reason, `typed-exclusion`, discards every design whose attribute
      flag is set, without a score.
   3. Next-stage outcome at the fixed sample size: q plus a small noise (σ_next = 0.005), so that the top band
      at the next stage is the planted top band.
   4. The audit plan with its commitment is generated first; scores are generated afterwards with ledger
      sequences that follow the commitment.
3. **The biased screen and the planted winner.** Family F₄ is the poorly represented family. The population has
   81 designs with qualities on a fixed grid, not drawn: the 54 designs of the other three families have q
   evenly spaced from 0.30 to 0.95, except that the best of them has 0.99; 26 designs of F₄ have q evenly spaced
   from 0.10 to 0.60; the planted winner is the 27th design of F₄ with q = 0.97, known rank 2 of 81, admitted
   with a synthetic rank record. Biased screen: b(F₄) = −0.5, b(other) = 0, σ = 0.01. The cut (the 27th best
   score of 81) is then near 0.631 and the plant's score near 0.47: a margin of 0.16, more than eleven standard
   deviations of the difference, so removal is certain by construction, not by luck. Unbiased screen: b ≡ 0;
   the plant's score is near 0.97 and it advances in every run. 100 seeds each.
4. **The 1,000 coverage campaigns.** Each has four discard strata. For each stratum: size n_s drawn from
   {100, 150, 200, 300, 400}; planted rate ρ_s from {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20}; D_s = round(ρ_s·n_s)
   discards are given `true_top = true` and a quality above every survivor's (so each is a false discard by the
   audit's predicate); costs are set so that the lane's quota is m_s = n_s/10 (10 to 40 audited discards per
   stratum). The planted truth per stratum is D_s/n_s exactly, and per campaign Σ D_s / Σ n_s.
5. **The study's exact expected coverage (so that passing is not a coin toss).** For each (n, D, m) of the grid
   the builder enumerates the hypergeometric distribution of the number of false discards in the lane and the
   bound each count yields. For the correct bound at δ = 0.05 the per-stratum coverage is 1.0000 in 24 of the 30
   grid cells and between 0.9632 and 0.9911 in the other six ((150, 30, 15) 0.9709; (200, 40, 20) 0.9911;
   (300, 30, 30) 0.9644; (300, 60, 30) 0.9632; (400, 40, 40) 0.9883; (400, 80, 40) 0.9771), with a mean of
   0.9952 over the grid. The stage-level bound (level δ/4 per stratum, weights n_s/N_d) covers the planted
   overall rate with probability above 0.999. The manifest stores these numbers; a registry test recomputes
   them. A study designed at exact coverage 0.95 would pass a "95% of 1,000" threshold only about half the
   time, so the grid is chosen with its expected coverage stated in advance.
6. **Registered mutants (negative controls).** Each is a build feature of the audit under test, compiled only
   under the Gauntlet's umbrella feature `gauntlet-mutations` (BD-025), and is the plant of one N26-CX entry
   (CX-293, `nemesis-late-01` to `-03`), from which the seeding tool of N38b-05 makes the control record of
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (this bead writes no row of that file); the assertion each
   must break:
   - `nemesis-audit-mut-point-estimate`: reports the audited rate k/m as the bound. Expected stratum coverage on
     the grid: 0.5537 (enumerated); the coverage row must fail.
   - `nemesis-audit-mut-survivors-only`: audits survivors and ignores discards (the shortcut "a comparison among
     survivors validates the screen"). The biased screen's missed plant goes unreported; the planted-winner row
     must fail.
   - `nemesis-audit-mut-post-score-draw`: draws the lane after scores, preferring discards far from the cut. On
     campaigns whose planted false discards sit near the cut the bound under-covers; the commitment check and
     the coverage row must fail.
   - `nemesis-audit-mut-approximation`: uses ln(1/δ)/m. Still covers (it is looser); it must be caught by the
     known-answer row instead (10.0% printed where 9.5% is expected at m = 30).
7. **The entry point and the scratch campaign (DESIGN).** The crate has no binary (BD-025), so the generator is
   reached through the example target `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example nemesis_audit_fixtures -- --seed S
   --kind biased|unbiased|coverage|scratch-campaign --out DIR`, which writes the stage record and outcome table
   of one fixture. `--kind scratch-campaign --store DIR` instead writes, through the Chronicle ledger API
   (BD-090) into a temporary store, one synthetic campaign (`origin: synthetic`) with a manifest, the
   population of its stage 3 and the stage's `audit-draw` record made by the lane's keyed draw (N28b-13), and no
   score, so that the verb's `prepare` has a campaign to commit into; a second
   call with `--phase scores` appends, with ledger sequences after the commitment, the stage's scores and
   decisions (the biased screen of item 3 on seed S) and the scripted next-stage outcomes of the lane members
   the committed draw names. Nothing is simulated; the end-to-end script (N26b-07) uses it for the two phases.
8. **Freezing.** The seed list (100 + 100 + 1,000 seeds), the grid, the screen parameters and the
   expected-coverage table are hashed into `manifest.toml` before the first measured run of the gate; changing
   any of them is a new registry identity and a recorded event. No seed is chosen after a result is seen.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The generator is a pure function of (registry identity, seed): two runs produce byte-identical stage
      records and outcome tables.
- [ ] In all 100 biased-screen stage records the planted winner is discarded by the screen itself (score below
      the stage's cut by at least 0.10); in all 100 unbiased records it advances.
- [ ] Every coverage campaign plants exactly D_s true-top designs among the discards of stratum s, and the
      audit's false-discard predicate is true for exactly those designs on the outcome table.
- [ ] The expected-coverage table in the manifest is reproduced by enumeration: the 30 grid values, mean
      0.9952, minimum 0.9632; the point-estimate mutant's mean 0.5537.
- [ ] The four mutants are registered with the row each must break; the registry check fails if any mutant is
      missing its expected failure.
- [ ] The registry is frozen and hashed; every generated file is labelled `origin: synthetic`.
- [ ] The example target writes each fixture kind; the scratch campaign's two phases leave scores only after the
      commitment, and outcome rows only for lane members.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/nemesis_audit_planted.rs`:
- `generator_deterministic` (two runs, equal bytes for seeds 0, 1 and 999).
- `biased_screen_always_discards_plant` and `unbiased_screen_always_keeps_plant` (100 seeds each).
- `planted_counts_exact`: for 50 sampled campaigns, the count of `true_top` discards per stratum equals D_s.
- `false_discard_predicate_matches_truth`: on the outcome table, the audit's predicate marks exactly the planted
  designs.
- `expected_coverage_table` (hypergeometric enumeration; the six non-unit cells to four decimals).
- `point_estimate_mutant_expected_coverage` (0.5537 ± 0.0001).
- `commitment_precedes_scores_in_every_record`.
- `scratch_campaign_phases`: after the first call the store holds the campaign and stage 3's population with no
  score; after `--phase scores` every score's ledger sequence exceeds the commitment's, the outcome rows are
  exactly the lane members', and every row is `origin: synthetic`.
- `manifest_hash_frozen`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Consumed by `scripts/e2e/nemesis_audit.sh` (N26b-07), which logs for each fixture file its seed, registry
identity, the planted truth (per stratum D_s and n_s; the plant's family and known rank) and, after the audit,
the observed counts beside the truth.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-668 (a planted winner removed by a deliberately biased screen is reported; no top-k design among 30 audited
discards bounds the false-discard rate at 9.5% with 95% confidence, and 59 bound it at 5.0%; control: a screen
with no bias passes). CX-293. The four mutants above are the negative controls of GATE-NEMESIS-07. Determinism:
the generator uses integer and addressed draws only; files are identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- The fixtures test the audit's arithmetic and plumbing. They say nothing about how often real screens discard
  winners; that is what the audit measures in real campaigns.
- The bias magnitude is deliberately gross. A subtle bias that removes the plant in 60% of runs would make the
  "100 of 100" row a statistical statement; the row is a potency control and must be deterministic.
- The cascade's own audit gate uses a seeded cascade population with planted campaigns of its own; the two sets
  share this generator's stage-record schema so that one reader serves both.
