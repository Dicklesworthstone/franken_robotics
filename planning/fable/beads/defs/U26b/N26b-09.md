## Background

Given an incidence (scenes against failure modes, each mode with an integer weight; N26b-08), the coverage
objective is

    f(S) = Σ { W_m : some scene of S exposes mode m }.

f is **monotone** (adding a scene never lowers it) and **submodular** (the gain of adding a scene to a larger set
is never more than to a smaller one: a scene is worth only what earlier scenes missed). For such objectives the
greedy rule, pick the scene with the largest marginal gain, repeat, is the classical algorithm: at a fixed number
of scenes k it reaches f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k, where OPT_k is the best value of any k scenes.

DESIGN: selection is **lazy greedy**: keep scenes in a priority queue by their last computed marginal gain, and
recompute only the top, since gains never increase. A stale gain is an upper bound on the true gain, so when a
freshly recomputed gain is still on top it is the true maximum. Lazy and plain greedy must return the same
selection; the plain form is kept as the reference the gate compares against.

Two extensions are part of the design. With unequal scene costs the selection ranks by gain per second and the
guarantee weakens to the constant of the cost-benefit variant. And any greedy prefix is itself a valid battery
(the budgeted mode of the decision), because the first j picks of a greedy run are the greedy run for j.

Tier Solid: a greedy algorithm with a classical guarantee.

## Scope

## Technical approach

1. **Objective.** With `covered` a bit set over modes, `gain(s | covered) = Σ W_m over modes in exposes[s] and
   not in covered`, in 64-bit integers (the sum of all weights is required to fit, checked when the incidence is
   loaded). `f(S)` is the weight of the union. No floating point appears anywhere in selection.
2. **Order and ties.** Candidates are ordered by (gain descending, scene key ascending); the scene key is the
   byte order of the scene identifier. The rule is total, so both algorithms are deterministic functions of
   the incidence.
3. **Plain greedy (the reference).** For i = 1 … k: compute the gain of every unselected scene against the
   current coverage; pick the first in the order of point 2; stop early if its gain is zero (remaining scenes
   add nothing; the selection then has fewer than k scenes and says so).
4. **Lazy greedy (numbered).**
   1. For every scene compute g(s) = gain(s | ∅) and push (g(s), key, s, stamp 0) on a max-heap ordered as in
      point 2.
   2. Pop the top entry (g, s, stamp). If stamp equals the current number of selected scenes, g is exact for
      the current coverage: select s, add its modes to the coverage, record (s, g, f so far). Otherwise
      recompute g′ = gain(s | covered), push (g′, s, current count), and repeat this step.
   3. Stop when k scenes are selected or the selected gain would be zero.
   Why it equals plain greedy, ties included: by submodularity every stale entry's g is at least its true gain.
   When a fresh entry (g, a) is on top, every other scene has true gain ≤ its stale bound ≤ g; and any scene
   whose bound equals g with a smaller key would have been ahead of a in the heap and popped first. So a is
   the first in the order of point 2 among true gains, which is plain greedy's pick.
5. **Cost-benefit variant (time budget T, costs c_s > 0 in integer milliseconds).**
   - *Ratio greedy*: among unselected scenes whose cost fits the remaining budget, pick the largest gain/cost;
     ratios are compared exactly by cross-multiplication in 128-bit integers (g₁·c₂ against g₂·c₁), ties by key.
     A scene that does not fit is skipped, not a reason to stop. Ratios also never increase as the selection
     grows, so the lazy queue applies unchanged.
   - *Gain greedy under the budget*: the same loop ranked by gain alone.
   - Return the better of the two by f (ratio greedy on a tie), and say which was returned.
   The guarantee of this combination is the constant ½·(1 − 1/e) ≈ 0.316 of the best selection within the
   budget (FACT from the literature on budgeted submodular maximization, to be re-read at the source before a
   gate or a report cites it); ratio greedy alone has no constant guarantee (see the trap below). The
   certificate (N26b-10) is computed for the returned selection and is usually far tighter than the constant.
6. **Trace.** `GreedyTrace { picks: Vec<Pick { scene, gain, covered_after, cost_ms }>, evaluations: u64,
   algorithm: Plain | Lazy | CostBenefit { returned: Ratio | Gain }, stopped: Full | ZeroGain | Budget }`.
   `prefix(j)` is the first j picks with `covered_after` of the j-th.
7. **Complexity.** Plain: O(k·n) gain evaluations, each O(modes/64). Lazy: n initial evaluations plus the
   recomputations; on the worked instance 36 evaluations against 93. For 10⁴ scenes and a few hundred modes a
   selection of 200 takes milliseconds either way; lazy matters for the cost-benefit loop and for prefixes of
   large batteries.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the worked instance (18 scenes, 40 modes, total weight 100) both algorithms pick S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6
      with gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4 and coverage 31, 53, 67, 76, 82, 86; continued to eight picks they add R1
      and R2 with gains 2 and 2 (coverage 88, 90), the tie among six scenes of gain 2 being broken by key.
- [ ] On that instance plain greedy uses 93 gain evaluations and lazy greedy 36.
- [ ] Lazy and plain greedy return identical traces (scenes, gains, coverage) on 10,000 random instances with up
      to 60 scenes and 80 modes, a third of them built with duplicated scenes and equal weights to force ties.
- [ ] f is monotone and submodular on random instances: f(A) ≤ f(B) for A ⊆ B, and gain(s | A) ≥ gain(s | B).
- [ ] Selection stops when the best remaining gain is zero and reports `ZeroGain`.
- [ ] `prefix(j)` of a k-selection equals the j-selection, for every j ≤ k.
- [ ] The knapsack trap is handled: with budget 100 ms, a scene X of gain 2 and cost 1 ms and a scene Y of gain
      100 and cost 100 ms, ratio greedy alone returns value 2 and the variant returns Y with value 100, saying
      `returned: Gain`.
- [ ] Ratio comparison is exact: 1/3 ranks above 333,333/1,000,000.

`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/cover_greedy.rs` (pure; instances are fixtures or generated, labelled `synthetic`):
- `worked_instance_plain`, `worked_instance_lazy`, `worked_instance_eight_picks`.
- `evaluation_counts_worked` (93 and 36, for the stated key order).
- `lazy_equals_plain_random` (10,000 instances; identical traces).
- `monotone_property`, `submodular_property` (1,000 instances, random nested sets).
- `tie_rule_total_and_stable`: an instance of five identical scenes is selected in key order.
- `zero_gain_stop`; `prefix_is_greedy`.
- `cost_benefit_knapsack_trap`; `cost_benefit_skips_unaffordable`; `ratio_cross_multiplication_exact`;
  `cost_benefit_lazy_equals_plain` (1,000 instances with random costs from 1 to 10,000 ms).
- `equal_costs_reduce_to_cardinality`: with every cost equal and budget k·c the variant returns the plain
  greedy selection of k scenes.
- `no_float_in_selection`: a build-time lint (the module compiles with floating-point types denied).

### End-to-end script and logging
Through `frob nemesis cover --algorithm plain|lazy` in `scripts/e2e/nemesis_cover.sh` (N26b-12), which logs for
each instance the pick sequence with gains and coverage under both algorithms, the evaluation counts, and the
first differing pick if the two ever disagree.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-669 (the worked instance: gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4 cover 86). GATE-NEMESIS-08's first clause (lazy and plain
greedy agree). Planted defects the tests must catch: a lazy queue that accepts the top entry without checking
its stamp (it picks by stale gains: on the worked instance it would pick S2 at its stale gain of 30 and report
coverage wrongly; `worked_instance_lazy` fails on the recorded gain); a gain that double-counts a mode already
covered (coverage 132 for the six worked scenes instead of 86); a tie rule that depends on heap insertion order
(`tie_rule_total_and_stable` fails under a shuffled input). Metamorphic relations: permuting the input order of
scenes or modes leaves the trace unchanged; scaling all weights by a constant scales every gain by it and leaves
the picks unchanged; adding a scene identical to a selected one changes nothing. Determinism: integer
arithmetic; identical traces on every platform and worker count (selection is single-threaded by design).

## Considerations

- Greedy can be beaten: on the worked instance the best six scenes cover 90, not 86 (the pair T1, T2 jointly
  covers what S1 and S2 cover plus two more modes, but each alone looks worse than S1). The guarantee and the
  certificate say how far off greedy can be; they do not say it is optimal.
- The cost-benefit constant comes from published analysis and is not re-derived here; nothing in the product
  depends on it, because every selection carries its own certificate.
- Costs are medians of measured episode times and move with hardware; a cost-mode selection records the
  fingerprint its costs came from, and a cardinality-mode selection is hardware-independent.
- If a future objective adds a term that is not submodular (a diversity bonus across archetypes, say), lazy
  evaluation is no longer valid and the equality test with plain greedy is the alarm.
