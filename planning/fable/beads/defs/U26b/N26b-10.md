## Background

Greedy selection of a covering battery (N26b-09) comes with a worst-case guarantee: at a fixed number of scenes
it reaches at least (1 − 1/e) ≈ 63% of the best possible weighted coverage. That constant is true and nearly
useless: it cannot tell a selection at 64% from one at 99%. The plan therefore requires an **instance
certificate**, computed from numbers the selection already holds:

    PO-NEMESIS-03.  For monotone submodular f and a selection S of k scenes,
                    OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k largest remaining marginal gains,

where OPT_k is the best value of any k scenes and a remaining marginal gain is f(S ∪ {s}) − f(S) for a scene s
outside S. The obligation's class is Tested by brute force on small instances, with the argument on file. The
decision contract says the certificate is checked on every selection and stored with the battery.

Worked instance (illustrative): 40 modes of total weight 100; greedy gains of 31, 22, 14, 9, 6 and 4 cover 86;
the six largest remaining gains sum to 12, so OPT₆ ≤ 98 and the selection is within 88% of the best six-scene
battery (86/98), against the worst-case 63%. It is a known answer of the chapter (control: a selection that
double-counts a mode) and of the decision core (control: a certificate computed from stale gains).

Why the bound holds, in three lines: for any set O of at most k scenes, f(O) ≤ f(O ∪ S) because f is monotone;
f(O ∪ S) ≤ f(S) + Σ over o in O∖S of [f(S ∪ {o}) − f(S)] because f is submodular (adding the scenes of O one at
a time, each adds no more than it would add to S alone); and that sum is at most the sum of the k largest
remaining gains. It holds for any S, greedy or not.

Tier Solid.

## Technical approach

1. **Cardinality certificate.** After a selection S with |S| = k: recompute the marginal gain of every scene
   outside S against the final coverage (fresh values, never the lazy queue's cached ones); take the k largest;
   `opt_upper_bound = min(f(S) + their sum, total weight of the incidence)`; `ratio_lower_bound =
   f(S)/opt_upper_bound` as an exact fraction in lowest terms. Stored:
   `Certificate { kind: TopKRemainingGains, k, f: u64, remaining_gains: Vec<u64> /* the k largest, descending */,
   opt_upper_bound: u64, ratio_lower_bound: (u64, u64) }`.
2. **Budget certificate (cost-benefit selections).** For a time budget T: sort the scenes outside S by
   gain/cost descending (exact cross-multiplication); take whole scenes while their total cost stays within T
   (the full budget, since the best selection may be any set of cost at most T); with C the cost taken and
   (g, c) the next scene, add ⌈(T − C)·g / c⌉. `opt_upper_bound = min(f(S) + that sum, total weight)`. This is
   the fractional-knapsack relaxation of the same argument.
3. **Checker (independent code path).** `cover::check_certificate(&Incidence, &[SceneKey], &Certificate) ->
   Result<(), CertError>`: recompute f(S) from the incidence by set union; recompute every remaining gain;
   verify the listed gains are the k largest, the sum, the bound, the fraction. It shares no code with the
   selection's gain routine (a second, simple implementation over sorted index lists). `CertError` names the
   first disagreeing quantity. The verb runs it before returning any selection.
4. **The greedy guarantee, checked exactly where OPT is known.** For cardinality-mode greedy the sharp classical
   bound is f(S_k) ≥ (1 − (1 − 1/k)^k)·OPT_k, which is at least (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k. In integers:
   f(S_k)·k^k ≥ (k^k − (k − 1)^k)·OPT_k (128-bit; k ≤ 10 in the gate's instances).
5. **The worked instance as a fixture (`cover_worked_40x18`, synthetic).** Modes and weights: a1…a8 = 8, 6, 5,
   4, 3, 2, 2, 1; b1…b7 = 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1; c1…c5 = 5, 4, 2, 2, 1; d1…d4 = 4, 2, 2, 1; e1…e3 = 3, 2, 1;
   f1…f3 = 2, 1, 1; g1…g10 = 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 (40 modes, total 100). Scenes: S1 = all a; S2 = all b
   and a1; S3 = all c, b1, a2; S4 = all d, a3, b2; S5 = all e, c2, a4; S6 = all f, d1, b3; T1 = a1, a3, a5, b1,
   b2, g1; T2 = a2, a4, a6, a7, a8, b3, b4, b5, b6, b7, g2; R1 = g1, a5; R2 = g2, b4; R3 = g3, c3; R4 = g4, d2;
   R5 = g5, e2; R6 = g6, f2; R7 = g7; R8 = g8; R9 = g9; R10 = g10 (18 scenes). Single-scene values: 31, 30, 26,
   19, 14, 12, 29, 28, 5, 5, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1, 1, 1, 1.
   - Greedy: S1 … S6, gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4, coverage 86.
   - Remaining gains against the final selection: T1 2, T2 2, R1 2, R2 2, R3 2, R4 2, and 1 for R5 … R10. The six
     largest sum to 12: certificate 98, ratio 43/49.
   - True optimum by enumeration of the 18,564 six-subsets: OPT₆ = 90, attained by {S3, S4, S5, S6, T1, T2}.
   - Prefixes k = 1 … 6: coverage 31, 53, 67, 76, 82, 86; certificates 53, 77, 90, 91, 94, 98; optima 31, 57,
     71, 80, 86, 90.
6. **Brute-force oracle (conformance crate, test-only).** `frob-conformance` binary `cover-oracle <incidence>
   --k K` enumerates every K-subset of at most 20 scenes with bit-set unions (at most C(20, 10) = 184,756
   subsets) and prints OPT_K and one optimal subset. It is written independently of `frob-nemesis`.
7. **Random instance generator (stream `nemesis-cover-gate`; 1,000 instances; frozen and hashed).**
   600 uniform (scenes 4 to 20, modes 5 to 60, exposure density from {0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4}, weights 1 to 20, k
   from 1 to min(scenes, 10)); 150 with duplicated scenes and unit weights (ties); 150 greedy traps (two or
   three scenes that partition a block which one decoy scene covers by halves, as T1 and T2 do above); 100 edge
   cases (k = number of scenes; k = 1; scenes with empty exposure; one mode; all scenes identical).
8. **Registered mutants (negative controls of the certificate).**
   - `nemesis-cover-mut-no-remaining-term` (bound = f(S)): 86 < 90 on the worked instance.
   - `nemesis-cover-mut-top-one-gain` (adds only the largest remaining gain): 88 < 90.
   - `nemesis-cover-mut-stale-gains` (reads the lazy queue's cached gains): prints 99, not 98.
   - `nemesis-cover-mut-double-count` (sums scene values without removing overlap): reports coverage 132.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked instance yields the certificate 98 with remaining gains [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2] and ratio 43/49; the
      checker accepts it; the oracle reports OPT₆ = 90 ≤ 98; the six prefix certificates are 53, 77, 90, 91, 94,
      98 and each is at or above its optimum 31, 57, 71, 80, 86, 90.
- [ ] PO-NEMESIS-03 on all 1,000 generated instances: the oracle's optimum is at or below the certificate, and
      greedy satisfies f·k^k ≥ (k^k − (k − 1)^k)·OPT_k (so it is at or above (1 − 1/e) of the optimum).
- [ ] The tight two-scene instance (four unit modes; A₁ = {m1, m2}, A₂ = {m3, m4}, a decoy {m1, m3} first in key
      order) gives greedy 3, optimum 4, certificate 4, and equality in the guarantee check (3·4 = 3·4).
- [ ] The checker rejects a certificate with one remaining gain altered, a wrong sum, a wrong f, or a selection
      that is not the one certified, naming the quantity.
- [ ] Each of the four mutants fails its stated assertion; the registry check fails if a mutant passes.
- [ ] The budget certificate on the example (budget 4; remaining scenes with (gain, cost) = (6, 2), (5, 5),
      (3, 1)) adds 3 + 6 + ⌈1·5/5⌉ = 10; on 1,000 random budgeted instances with at most 16 scenes the
      enumerated optimum within the budget is at or below the certificate.
- [ ] The oracle shares no source with `frob-nemesis` (checked by the dependency lint).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_cover.sh` (N26b-12) compares, for each generated instance, the certificate returned by
`frob nemesis cover` with the oracle's optimum and logs k, f(S), the remaining gains, the certificate, OPT_k and
both ratios; on failure the first instance (seed and family) whose optimum exceeds its certificate.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-669 (worked instance: 86 covered; OPT₆ ≤ 98; within 88% of the best six-scene battery against a worst-case
guarantee of 63%; control: a selection that double-counts a mode). KA-704 (the decision core's row: six gains
cover 86 of 100; bound 98; control: a certificate computed from stale gains). PO-NEMESIS-03 by property test and
by brute force. Metamorphic relations: the certificate is invariant under relabelling; scaling all weights by c
scales f and the bound by c and leaves the ratio unchanged; adding a scene with empty exposure changes nothing.
Determinism: integers only; identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- A certificate from cached (stale) gains is still an upper bound, since a stale gain is at least the true one;
  it is refused as the stored certificate because it is looser and not reproducible from the selection alone.
- The certificate can be loose when many scenes overlap heavily outside S (the sum of individual gains
  overstates what k of them can add together). It is still the tightest statement available without solving
  the problem, and the ratio it yields is a lower bound: the selection may be better than it says (95.6% of
  the true optimum on the worked instance, against a certified 87.8%).
- The certificate is about the incidence. It is not a statement about failure modes absent from the catalogue.
