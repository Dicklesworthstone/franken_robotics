## Background

Coverage selection (N26b-09) picks the scenes that expose the most failure-mode weight, and its certificate
(N26b-10) says how close that is to the best possible. Both statements are about the incidence, which is built
from failures already found. A battery assembled only from them has a known disease: **a battery made of past
failures overfits them** (the shortcut registered as CX-294). Designs tuned against it learn its worlds, and it
cannot show a failure mode that no reference design has exhibited yet. The plan's countermeasure is structural:
**one slot in five is filled at random from held-out scenes, so the battery cannot consist only of yesterday's
failures.**

Three further rules of the decision ("which k scenes form the covering battery for robot class C?", decision
core DC-10) are assembled here:

- *Fallback.* The standard battery (about 200 scenarios per robot class, frozen at M2 and read by the fidelity
  ledgers). *Trigger:* any identity change (model class, tier, kernel versions, taxonomy) since the incidence
  was built.
- *Budgeted mode.* Any greedy prefix is a valid battery with its own certificate.
- *Interference.* A rare-failure bound refuses battery-selected episodes: bounds use nominal draws only.

Tier Solid: bookkeeping around a selection.

## Scope

In: the slot layout; the held-out pool and its keyed draw; the item list in battery form; the selection

## Technical approach

1. **Slot layout (DESIGN).** A covering battery of K slots has held-out slots at positions 5, 10, 15, … (every
   fifth) and greedy slots elsewhere, filled in greedy order. So K slots hold h = ⌊K/5⌋ held-out scenes and
   k = K − h greedy scenes, and every prefix of the battery is itself laid out by the same rule: a prefix of K′
   slots has the first K′ − ⌊K′/5⌋ greedy picks, which are a greedy prefix with their own certificate. Twenty
   slots give four held-out slots; seven give one (position 5) and six greedy picks. A battery of fewer than
   five slots has no held-out slot: it is permitted as a budgeted prefix only and carries the flag
   `no-held-out-slot`; the default and the minimum for adoption as the `covering` battery is five.
2. **The held-out pool.** Scenes of the robot class's scene split that are on the held-out side (the mechanical
   separation of training, development and held-out scenes, with building-level holdouts), that passed scene
   acceptance for the class, and that are **not rows of the incidence** (no reference design's failure there
   contributed to the selection). "Held out" therefore means held out twice: from training, and from the
   evidence the greedy slots were chosen on.
3. **The draw.** Slot j (a multiple of 5) takes the pool scene with the smallest keyed priority
   `keyed_draw(nemesis-cover-heldout, key, j, SceneId)`, without replacement, where `key` is derived from the
   incidence identity, the robot class and the task family. The draw is a pure function of those identities:
   the same request gives the same held-out scenes, and a rebuilt incidence gives new ones. If the pool has
   fewer scenes than held-out slots, the unfilled slots stay empty (they are not back-filled with greedy
   picks) and the selection carries the flag `held-out-pool-short` with the counts.
4. **What is certified.** `covered_weight`, the certificate and the ratio refer to the greedy slots only. A
   held-out scene has no row in the incidence, so its exposure is unknown; it is listed with `kind: held-out`
   and contributes nothing to the certified numbers. Coverage is monotone, so held-out slots can only add.
5. **Items.** Each slot becomes a battery item in the manifest's canonical item form (task, world with scene
   identity and world addresses, disturbance law, initial-condition family): a greedy slot uses the incidence
   row's item (so the world that exposed the modes is the world re-run); a held-out slot uses the task
   family's default item for the drawn scene, at world addresses derived from the same keyed draw. The
   selection document `frob.nemesis.cover/1` carries the item list with the header `selection = covering-ref`
   and its own identity `cov:…`; a covering manifest refers to it. Adoption (writing or changing the `covering`
   manifest of a class) is a membership event of the battery mechanism, with a reason that cites the selection
   identity, covered weight and certificate; this module never writes a manifest.
6. **Resolution and fallback.** `cover::resolve(class, family, in_force) -> Resolved`:
   - `Covering { selection }` when a selection exists whose incidence is not stale under the identities in
     force and whose certificate check was accepted;
   - `Fallback { battery: standard, reason }` with reason `stale { field, built, in_force }` (the plan's four
     triggers: model class, tier, kernel versions, taxonomy; and the two DESIGN additions of the incidence),
     `no-incidence`, or `certificate-check-failed`.
   The evaluation verb's resolution of the battery name `covering` calls this and echoes the outcome in the
   envelope's `bound` block, so a run can always tell which population it counted. A fallback is a new
   resolution, never a silent substitution.
7. **Interference.** Episodes run from a covering battery (greedy or held-out slots alike) carry the proposal
   law `BatterySelected { battery }`. That law is in the exploration lane, so the bound's admission refuses
   them with FROB-REF-560 ("a bound was offered episodes that were not drawn nominally or with recorded bounded
   weights"); the cascade raises its own code for the same rule at its boundary (FROB-REF-549). Covering items
   are discovery data: offered as a confirmation sample they are refused with FROB-REF-567.
8. **Decision record (the core's contract).** Policy identity `("nemesis.dc10.cover", 1)`. Every selection and
   every resolution writes an evidence record: the identities read, the incidence identity (the table version),
   the ledger frontier, the action (a selection identity, or the fallback with its trigger) and, while the core
   is in shadow, the action of the baseline (the standard battery's identity). The parameters "one slot in
   five", "minimum five slots" and the trigger list are DESIGN defaults recorded in the contract; changing one
   is a new policy version. Inputs supplied for the core table: the loss model (uncovered mode weight against
   battery runtime), the obligation (PO-NEMESIS-03, checked per selection), the fallback and its triggers, the
   budgeted mode, a replay scenario (the worked instance) and a demonstration manifest (commands that run in
   under 60 s with checksums).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] K = 20 gives held-out slots at 5, 10, 15, 20 and 16 greedy slots; K = 7 gives one held-out slot at 5 and
      six greedy slots; K = 4 gives none and the flag `no-held-out-slot`; adoption of a battery under five slots
      is refused by the assembly function.
- [ ] Every prefix of an assembled battery equals the battery assembled for that many slots.
- [ ] No held-out slot holds a scene that is a row of the incidence or that is on the training side of the
      scene split (property test over random pools).
- [ ] The held-out draw is identical for identical identities and changes when the incidence identity changes.
- [ ] A pool of 2 scenes for 4 held-out slots leaves 2 slots empty and sets `held-out-pool-short`; no greedy
      scene is placed in a held-out position.
- [ ] `resolve` returns the fallback with the differing field for each of the four plan triggers, with
      `no-incidence` when none exists, and with `certificate-check-failed` on a planted bad certificate; it
      returns the covering selection when identities match.
- [ ] Each trigger is demonstrated by forcing it (the core's fallback demonstration), and the shadow record
      holds both actions.
- [ ] Episodes of a covering-battery run offered to a bound are refused with FROB-REF-560; a covering item
      offered as a confirmation sample is refused with FROB-REF-567 (checked where those lanes exist).

- `pool_short_leaves_slots_empty`.
- `certified_numbers_ignore_held_out_slots`: the worked instance with seven slots reports coverage 86 and

- `resolve_fallback_each_trigger`, `resolve_no_incidence`, `resolve_bad_certificate`, `resolve_fresh`.
- `items_are_valid_battery_items` (each item parses under the battery manifest's item schema and hashes to a
  stable `ItemId`).
- `decision_record_fields`; `shadow_record_has_baseline_action`.
- `law_is_battery_selected` (an episode run from a covering item carries the law and the exploration lane).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_cover.sh` (N26b-12): asserts the slot layout from the verb's output, forces a stale identity
and reads the fallback from `frob eval run --battery covering` (the `bound` block), and offers covering-run
episodes to `frob nemesis bound`. Logged: K, k, h, each held-out slot with its draw key and scene, the pool
size, the resolution with its reason, the decision record's identity, and the refusal envelopes.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-294 (a battery made of past failures is a good battery: it overfits them; one slot in five of a covering
battery is drawn from held-out scenes). The decision core's interference test: a bound refuses battery-selected
episodes. Planted defects the tests must catch: an assembly that back-fills an empty held-out slot with the next
greedy pick; a pool that admits incidence rows (the battery is then all past failures); a resolution that keeps
serving a stale selection; a held-out draw seeded from the wall clock (the determinism test). Determinism:
integer and addressed draws only; identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- "Held-out scenes" is not defined further in the plan. The double reading here (held out by the scene split and
  absent from the incidence) is the strictest one that still leaves a pool; if the split's held-out side is
  reserved for another use, the pool is the development side with the same exclusion, by a recorded change of
  the policy version.
- RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard instead of the robot) names held-out scenes among its mitigations: a
  design tuned against the greedy slots still meets scenes nobody selected.
- Held-out slots are random, not adversarial; their job is to notice what the catalogue has not seen. New
  failures they expose enter the catalogue and, at the next battery revision, the incidence.
- The core's owner is listed as the evaluation crate; the selection and its record live in `frob-nemesis`
  because the catalogue and the verb are here. The core table names both.
- Timescale: per battery revision. Nothing in a campaign reads a covering battery that is being revised; a
  revision is a new battery identity.
