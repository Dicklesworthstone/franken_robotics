## Background

GATE-NEMESIS-08 (claim: Known answer; earliest milestone M3; TARGET): **coverage: lazy and plain greedy agree; on
1,000 random instances of at most 20 scenes the brute-force optimum is at or below the certificate and greedy is
at or above (1 − 1/e) of it.** It is one of the Nemesis gates the M3 milestone exit must have on record.

The capability behind it is `nemesis.cover`: select the scenes of a covering battery so that together they expose
as much failure-mode weight as possible, and return the selection with a certificate. The objective f(S), the
total weight of failure modes exposed by at least one scene of S, is monotone and submodular; lazy greedy
selection picks scenes by marginal gain; for a selection S of k scenes, OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k largest
remaining marginal gains (PO-NEMESIS-03), and greedy guarantees f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k. One slot in five of the
battery is a randomly drawn held-out scene; a stale incidence is refused and the standard battery is the
fallback; a rare-failure bound refuses battery-selected episodes.

This script measures the gate from the user's side on the real `frob` binary, against an optimum computed by an
independent brute-force tool.

## Scope

In: the script, assertions, logs, the gate record, the self-test against the certificate's registered mutants.
Out: the mechanism (N26b-08 to N26b-11); the verb (BD-278); the instance generator and oracle (N26b-10); DC-10's
contract record and committed table (BD-309, a dependency: without it every normal call answers the fallback).

## Technical approach

1. **Surface exercised.** `frob nemesis cover … --incidence <file> --slots K --algorithm lazy|plain --prefixes
   [--greedy-only] [--require-rule table] --json` (a read verb, no key), governed by DC-10's committed table
   (BD-309); the conformance crate's example targets (it has no binary, BD-025; N26b-10)
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example cover_gate_instances -- --out DIR` (the 1,000 frozen instances)
   and `--example cover_oracle -- <incidence> --k K` (the brute-force optimum); `frob eval run <design>
   --battery covering`; `frob nemesis bound … --episodes <run set>`; `frob eval qualify` for the confirmation
   probe (rows of VERBS-nemesis and VERBS-eval). Every run-verb call carries its own `--key nc-<step>-<i>`, so a
   rerun returns stored receipts and no two compared runs share a key.
2. **Steps.**
   1. `start`: build identity, the generator's registry hash, hardware fingerprint.
   2. *Worked instance.* `frob nemesis cover --incidence cover_worked_40x18.json --slots 7 --prefixes`. Assert:
      picks S1 … S6 in slots 1 to 4, 6 and 7 with gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4; slot 5 is `held-out`; covered
      weight 86; certificate 98 with remaining gains [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2]; ratio 43/49; prefix certificates 53,
      77, 90, 91, 94, 98; `certificate.check` is `accepted`. The oracle reports OPT₆ = 90: assert 90 ≤ 98 and
      86·6⁶ ≥ (6⁶ − 5⁶)·90.
   3. *Lazy against plain, 1,000 instances.* For each instance and its k: run the verb with `--algorithm lazy`
      and `--algorithm plain` (greedy slots only: `--slots k --greedy-only`, which returns a battery prefix
      marked `no-held-out-slot` that cannot be adopted). Assert identical pick sequences, gains and coverage.
      Metric `lazy_equals_plain = a of 1,000` against `1,000`.
   4. *Certificate and guarantee, 1,000 instances.* For each: OPT_k from the oracle. Assert OPT_k ≤
      `opt_upper_bound`, and f(S)·k^k ≥ (k^k − (k − 1)^k)·OPT_k (which implies f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k). Metrics
      `optimum_under_certificate = b of 1,000` against `1,000`; `greedy_meets_guarantee = c of 1,000` against
      `1,000`; also recorded, without a threshold: the mean and minimum of f(S)/OPT_k and of
      f(S)/`opt_upper_bound`, and the share of instances where greedy is optimal.
   5. *Slots.* `--slots 20` on a 60-scene synthetic instance with a held-out pool: assert held-out slots at 5,
      10, 15, 20, none of them a row of the incidence; rerun: identical held-out scenes.
   6. *Stale incidence.* Rerun the worked request under a scratch configuration whose kernel-version identity
      differs from the incidence's: assert `FROB-REF-565` (coverage selection on a stale incidence), exit 3,
      `observed` and `required` naming the two kernel identities, three ranked repairs. Repeat for tier, model
      class and taxonomy version.
   7. *Fallback.* With the stale identity in force, `frob eval run <reference design> --battery covering --class
      <C> --tier batch-cpu --estimate --key nc-7-1 --json`: assert the `bound` block names the standard battery's
      identity and the reason `stale`.
   8. *Interference.* Run a small covering battery on the fast tier (`frob eval run <design> --battery covering
      --tier batch-cpu --key nc-8-1 --json`); offer its episodes to `frob nemesis bound <design> --task <t> --tier
      batch-cpu --target 1e-3 --mode certify --episodes <run> --key nc-8-2 --json`: assert `FROB-REF-560` (a bound
      was offered episodes that were not drawn nominally or with recorded bounded weights), exit 3. Offer one
      covering item to `frob eval qualify`: assert `FROB-REF-567`, exit 3.
   9. *Live incidence.* Build an incidence from the stored catalogues of the reference designs of one robot
      class (after a search campaign has filed failures) and select 10 slots: assert a non-empty selection, an
      accepted certificate, the identity block, and that no episode was run by the verb (ledger episode count
      unchanged). Where no catalogue exists yet the step is recorded `not run` with the reason.
   10. *Read-only.* Assert the battery registry's files and the ledger's battery-event table are unchanged by
       every `nemesis cover` call of the script.
   11. *DC-10's table (BD-278 step 0).* With the committed table (BD-309), the worked request of step 2 with
       `--require-rule table` returns the same bytes as without it and `policy.rule` is `table`. Against a
       scratch copy of `registries/cores/` in which DC-10's table is removed, and again with one cell changed
       (the form N28c-05 uses for DC-01): the worked request completes on the standard battery, exit 2, with
       `FROB-REF-871` in `warnings` and `policy.rule` `fallback` with the loader's reason; the same request with
       `--require-rule table` is `FROB-REF-871` (a decision-core table that is missing, stale against its
       contract, or not the registry version), exit 3, with the two ranked repairs.
   12. Write the receipt the gate is recorded from (item 5); `summary`.
3. **Self-test.** `scripts/e2e/tests/nemesis_cover_selftest.sh` runs the script against builds with each
   registered mutant and requires a non-zero exit at the named step: `nemesis-cover-mut-no-remaining-term` and
   `nemesis-cover-mut-top-one-gain` → step 2 (the optimum 90 exceeds the printed bound 86 or 88);
   `nemesis-cover-mut-stale-gains` → step 2 (99 printed for 98); `nemesis-cover-mut-double-count` → step 2
   (coverage 132); and a lazy queue without the stamp check → step 3.
4. **Logging.** The script sources the shared helper of the end-to-end harness and writes
   `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_cover/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript beside it. Every line: `ts`,
   `script`, `bead` (`N26b-12`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
   `evidence` block. Per instance: seed and family, scenes, modes, k, the pick sequence with gains under both
   algorithms, f(S), the remaining gains, the certificate, OPT_k, both ratios, and the selection's identity
   digest. On failure: the first instance whose algorithms disagree (with the first differing pick) or whose
   optimum exceeds its certificate, with the instance file path. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed;
   `gates: ["GATE-NEMESIS-08"]`. Instances are synthetic; the live step logs identities and counts only, never
   scene content.
5. **Gate record.** `lazy_equals_plain`, `optimum_under_certificate`, `greedy_meets_guarantee`, the recorded
   ratio statistics, the generator's registry hash, commit, command, fingerprint, in the script's receipt; the
   gate's record is entered from it by a session that did not implement the capability, with `cargo run -p
   frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-NEMESIS-08 --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19; no script
   writes a gate record).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `scripts/e2e/nemesis_cover.sh` exits 0 on a correct build and non-zero on any failed assertion; it is
      idempotent and seed-explicit.
- [ ] Lazy and plain greedy agree on 1,000 of 1,000 instances; the brute-force optimum is at or below the
      certificate on 1,000 of 1,000; greedy meets the guarantee on 1,000 of 1,000 (TARGETs of the row; the
      measured counts are recorded).
- [ ] The worked instance prints 86, 98 and 43/49.
- [ ] FROB-REF-565 is produced for each of the four identity changes; the fallback names the standard battery.
- [ ] FROB-REF-560 is produced for battery-selected episodes offered to a bound.
- [ ] With DC-10's table removed or changed, a normal call falls back to the standard battery with
      FROB-REF-871 in `warnings` (exit 2) and `--require-rule table` is refused with FROB-REF-871, exit 3; with
      the committed table the pin changes nothing.
- [ ] The self-test shows every mutant failing its named step.
- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-08 is recorded with measured values, commit and fingerprint.
- [ ] The log has the per-instance fields above and a final summary line.

### Known answers and negative controls

identical metric lines and selection digests.

## Considerations

- Cost: 2,000 read-verb calls and 1,000 enumerations of at most 184,756 subsets; a few minutes on CPU cores.
- Steps 7 and 8 depend on other subsystems' verbs; where one is absent at the time of the run the step is
  recorded `not run` with the reason. The gate's three clauses (steps 3 and 4) depend only on this chapter.
- The ratio statistics are recorded because the certificate's practical value is how much tighter it is than
  63%; a mean certified ratio near the constant would mean the instance family is unrepresentative.
