## Background

The chapter states six invariants and four proof obligations. An invariant is a property that must hold on every
path through the subsystem, not only on the path a mechanism's own test happens to walk. Each is stated once,
with the decision it protects:

- **INV-NEMESIS-01.** Only solvable worlds count: no world without a `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible`
  witness enters the hard-world set or a counterexample count. (ADR-NEMESIS-02.)
- **INV-NEMESIS-02.** Every world Nemesis evaluates is inside the legality profile, checked on the final world.
  (ADR-NEMESIS-01: two individually legal obstacles can narrow a passage below its minimum width.)
- **INV-NEMESIS-03.** Exploration data never enter a certification bound. (ADR-NEMESIS-05.)
- **INV-NEMESIS-04.** A `.fmcw` replays with identical digests on its originating backend. (ADR-NEMESIS-04: a
  counterexample that does not replay is a story, not an object.)
- **INV-NEMESIS-05.** A failure and its witness count only after the artifact guard. (ADR-NEMESIS-03.)
- **INV-NEMESIS-06.** Hard-world and search-selected worlds are discovery data. (ADR-NEMESIS-06.)

| Obligation | Statement | Class sought |
|---|---|---|
| PO-NEMESIS-01 | The shrinker's output is 1-minimal under its test relation | Tested: property test on random monotone and non-monotone predicates |
| PO-NEMESIS-02 | Under a defensive mixture with nominal share ε every weight is at most 1/ε, and the weighted estimator is unbiased under the admitted law | Tested against closed-form tails; the weight bound is a one-line inequality on file |
| PO-NEMESIS-03 | OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the k largest remaining marginal gains, for monotone submodular f | Tested by brute force on small instances; the argument is on file in `docs/derivations/` |
| PO-NEMESIS-04 | n ≥ ln(1/δ)/p⋆ clean nominal episodes give P(no failure in n) < δ whenever p > p⋆, since (1 − p)ⁿ ≤ exp(−p·n) | A candidate theorem card; Tested by exact binomial arithmetic until a checker accepts it |

The mechanism beads each test the invariant nearest to them. What they cannot do is show that *no other path*
violates it: a new verb that counts a world without asking the oracle, a helper that evaluates a world without
the legality check, a convenience that feeds a search episode to a bound. This bead closes that gap in three
ways: a binding ledger (every identifier names the tests and the code site that carry it, machine-checked);
crate-wide tests that drive whole pipelines and then audit the ledger for violations; and registered mutants
for every invariant, planted violations that the tests must catch (the program's rule: a gate that nothing can fail
validates nothing).

Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the binding ledger and its check; the type-level enforcement points; the crate-wide tests; the mutants;
the obligations' test bindings.
Out: the mechanisms themselves; the derivation documents and the candidate theorem card (N26b-16); the
known-answer battery (N26b-17).

## Technical approach

1. **Binding ledger (DESIGN).** The invariants section of `crates/frob-nemesis/CONTRACT.md` holds a table: one
   row per identifier with its statement, the enforcement point (type or function), the unit tests, the
   crate-wide test, the mutant feature and the end-to-end step. A test `invariant_bindings` parses the table
   and fails if a named test, function or feature does not exist, or if an identifier of the chapter has no
   row. The contract check of the build tooling runs it.
2. **Enforcement by construction (each a narrow gate in the type system, so a new path must go through it).**
   - INV-NEMESIS-01: `oracle::Counted` can be produced only by `oracle::admit_counterexample`; the search
     result's failure list, the counterexample-file writer and reader, and hard-set admission take `Counted`.
   - INV-NEMESIS-02: `legality::LegalWorld` can be produced only by the final-world legality check; the one
     function that submits worlds for evaluation (`eval::submit`) takes `LegalWorld`. Witness runs, shrink
     subsets, splitting children, held-out and battery items, and bound draws all pass through it. Nominal
     draws are checked like any other world: a task randomization whose support leaves the profile is reported
     at profile load.
   - INV-NEMESIS-03: the bound's accumulator takes `AdmittedEpisode`, produced only by the lane firewall from
     an episode whose proposal law is nominal or a defensive mixture with a recorded weight at most 1/ε.
   - INV-NEMESIS-04: the counterexample-file writer publishes only after the replay check returns identical
     digests; the hard-set `add` and the regression loader re-run the check.
   - INV-NEMESIS-05: `guard::Promoted<Failure>` and `guard::Guarded<Witness>` are produced only by the guard;
     `admit_counterexample` and the oracle's solvable classes take them.
   - INV-NEMESIS-06: every writer of a world under an exploration law, of a counterexample file, of a hard-set
     entry and of a covering selection writes the discovery record in the same transaction;
     `is_discovery_world` is the single reader.
3. **Crate-wide tests (`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/invariants.rs`; real pipelines on small fixtures, CPU tiers).**
   - `inv01_no_unwitnessed_count`: run a search campaign on a fixture mixing 5 planted solvable traps, 5
     impossible traps and 5 worlds whose witness search is starved of budget; then audit the ledger: every world
     in a failure list, in a `.fmcw` and in the hard-world set has an oracle record of a solvable class; the
     buckets hold 5, 5, 5.
   - `inv02_every_evaluated_world_legal`: run a campaign with a mutation method configured to propose 30%
     illegal worlds (including pairs of legal obstacles that jointly close a passage); then re-check every world
     in the discovery record and every episode's world: zero illegal; the count of refused proposals is positive.
   - `inv03_bound_taint_free`: in one campaign run exploration, a pilot fit and a certification bound; then, for
     every episode identity in the bound's accumulator, assert a certification-lane law, a commitment sequence
     earlier than the episode, and the committed predicate.
   - `inv04_all_files_replay`: every `.fmcw` produced by the test campaigns is replayed on its originating
     backend with identical digest chains; a file with one altered digest is detected.
   - `inv05_nothing_counts_unguarded`: every counted failure and every witness has a guard verdict `Passed`
     recorded before the count's ledger sequence.
   - `inv06_discovery_mark_complete`: every world written by search, splitting, shrinking, the hard-world set
     and a covering selection answers true to `is_discovery_world`; a nominal certification draw answers false;
     offering any of the former to the qualification lane's intake predicate is refused. With
     `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` (KILL-RARE fired) the splitting writer is absent and the test audits the
     remaining four, recording `splitting: removed`.
   Property tests accompany each (random outcome tables through the pure parts of each gate, 10,000 cases).
4. **Mutants (registered in the negative-control registry; each a build feature; each must fail its test).**
   Where a mechanism's own script already defines a test feature for the same defect it is reused under its
   name. INV-01: `nemesis-mut-count-without-witness` (a `not-excluded` world enters the failure list). INV-02:
   `nemesis-broken-legality` (the checker that looks at each edit and not at the final world) and
   `nemesis-mut-evaluate-unchecked` (a helper that submits a world without the check). INV-03:
   `nemesis-lane-open` (the bound's admission check disabled). INV-04: `nemesis-mut-fmcw-skip-replay` (the
   writer publishes without the replay check). INV-05: `nemesis-guard-off` (failures counted unguarded) and
   `nemesis-oracle-skip-guard` (witnesses accepted unguarded). INV-06: `nemesis-mut-discovery-mark-skipped`
   (the shared discovery-record write is skipped for every writer: search results, fork children, shrink
   output, hard-set entries and covering selections; so the mutant keeps a target if KILL-RARE removes
   splitting, and `inv06_discovery_mark_complete` fails on the first writer it audits). A harness test builds the
   crate with each of the eight features and requires the crate-wide test of its invariant to fail.
   Their control records, in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (BD-025's schema; one plant, one
   record). Two are the plants of catalogue entries, `nemesis-lane-open` (CX-290) and `nemesis-guard-off`
   (CX-295), whose records the seeding tool of N38b-05 derives: this bead lists those two and writes none for
   them. The other six are stated by no catalogue entry's `plant` and no known-answer row's control cell
   (`nemesis-mut-count-without-witness` is CX-289's second plant, which N26-CX leaves to the bead that owns it;
   `nemesis-broken-legality` and `nemesis-oracle-skip-guard` are the test features of the legality and oracle
   scripts, reused here under their names). This bead appends one record for each of the six, at the next free
   `NC-` number above the file's highest, under BD-025's rules: `seed` the invariant the mutant violates
   (`INV-NEMESIS-01` for count-without-witness; `INV-NEMESIS-02` for broken-legality and evaluate-unchecked;
   `INV-NEMESIS-04` for fmcw-skip-replay; `INV-NEMESIS-05` for oracle-skip-guard; `INV-NEMESIS-06` for
   discovery-mark-skipped), `kind = "mutation"`, `feature` the feature name, `target` the enforcement point of
   item 2, `defect` the planted violation, `expect` the failing crate-wide test, and `gate` the gates the seed
   resolves to through `registry/rule_gates.toml`, with GATE-NEMESIS-01 for the per-edit-only checker and the
   oracle that does not guard (the controls N26b-18's row of that gate names). For INV-NEMESIS-06, which no
   Nemesis gate measures (the case of CX-294), the record takes the gates that exercise FROB-REF-567 and -560
   as the join resolves them. The run that shows each of the six caught is the harness test
   `nc_<nnnn>_inv_nemesis_<nn>_caught` (STD-TEST's prefix for a control).
5. **Obligations' bindings.** PO-NEMESIS-01: the delta-debugging property test over random monotone and
   non-monotone predicates (the output satisfies the predicate and no single-edit removal does). PO-NEMESIS-02:
   the weight-bound test (every weight ≤ 1/ε) and the unbiasedness test on the Gaussian tail P(z > 4) =
   3.167 × 10⁻⁵ with shift 4 and ε = 0.1 (every weight at most 10). PO-NEMESIS-03: the brute-force test of the
   coverage certificate (N26b-10). PO-NEMESIS-04: the exact binomial test (for p⋆ = 10⁻⁴ and δ = 0.05,
   ln(1/δ)/p⋆ = 29,957.3, so n = 29,958 suffices and (1 − p⋆)ⁿ < δ; the smallest sufficient n is 29,956). Each
   obligation's row also names its derivation file (N26b-16) and its evidence class, `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ledger has ten rows (six invariants, four obligations); `invariant_bindings` passes and fails when a
      row is removed or a named test is renamed.
- [ ] `Counted`, `LegalWorld`, `AdmittedEpisode`, `Promoted` and `Guarded` have no public constructor outside
      their gate (compile-fail tests, one per type).
- [ ] The six crate-wide tests pass on a correct build, with the bucket counts 5, 5, 5 in the first.
- [ ] Each of the eight mutant features makes the crate-wide test of its invariant fail; the negative-control
      registry lists all eight, at least one per invariant: the two seeded from CX-290 and CX-295, and the six
      records this bead appends with `INV-NEMESIS-nn` seeds, which pass BD-025's loader validation.
- [ ] Each obligation is bound to a passing test and to its derivation file; PO-NEMESIS-04's test asserts
      29,958 sufficient and 29,956 minimal at p⋆ = 10⁻⁴, δ = 0.05.
- [ ] The M3 exit review can read, from one table, which test carries which identifier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/invariants.rs`: the six crate-wide tests and their property companions named above;
`invariant_bindings`. `crates/frob-nemesis/tests/compile_fail/`: `counted_not_constructible.rs`,
`legal_world_not_constructible.rs`, `admitted_episode_not_constructible.rs`, `promoted_not_constructible.rs`,
`guarded_not_constructible.rs`. `crates/frob-conformance/tests/nemesis_mutants.rs`: `each_mutant_is_caught`
(eight builds, eight expected failures), the six `nc_<nnnn>_inv_nemesis_<nn>_caught` tests of the records this
bead appends, and `registry_lists_one_per_invariant`.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own: what this bead adds is tests, a ledger table and test features, and its type gates sit on
the mechanisms' own paths, which their scripts already drive. The chapter's scripts assert the invariants from the user's side: the oracle script
(INV-01, -05), the legality script (INV-02), the bound script (INV-03), the shrink script (INV-04), the loop
script (INV-06), and the refusal script (N26b-14) for the refusals that guard them. Each logs the invariant
identifier beside the assertion that carries it, so a log search by identifier finds its evidence.

### Known answers and negative controls
The eight mutant features are the negative controls. Known answers used: KA-660 (impossible trap never
counted), KA-659 (illegal edit refused), KA-665 (Gaussian tail with every weight at most 10), KA-663
(planted causes recovered), KA-664 (zero-failure counts), KA-669 (coverage certificate). Counterexamples the
invariants block: CX-289 (INV-01), CX-290 and CX-291 (INV-03), CX-295 (INV-05), CX-294 (INV-06). Determinism
lanes: the crate-wide tests run twice on one machine and across worker counts with identical ledger audits.

## Considerations

- Type gates catch a new path at compile time; the ledger audits catch a path that goes around the types (raw
  ledger writes). Both are needed, and neither replaces the mechanism's own tests.
- INV-NEMESIS-02 has a subtle scope: "every world Nemesis evaluates". Worlds evaluated by other subsystems for
  Nemesis (a witness run by the sampling controller, a referee replay) are submitted by Nemesis and are covered.
- If a kill gate fires (KILL-NEMESIS, KILL-RARE, KILL-MCW) the invariants stay; the ledger rows for removed
  modules are kept with the status "module removed" and their mutants are retired by a recorded change.
