## Background

Four statements in the Nemesis chapter are proof obligations: claims the code relies on that are mathematical,
not empirical. The chapter says what class of evidence each is to have, and for three of them it says an
argument is to be "on file":

| Obligation | Statement | Class sought |
|---|---|---|
| PO-NEMESIS-01 | The shrinker's output is 1-minimal under its test relation | Tested: property test on random monotone and non-monotone predicates |
| PO-NEMESIS-02 | Under a defensive mixture with nominal share ε every weight is at most 1/ε, and the weighted estimator is unbiased under the admitted law | Tested against closed-form tails; the weight bound is a one-line inequality on file |
| PO-NEMESIS-03 | OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the k largest remaining marginal gains, for monotone submodular f | Tested by brute force on small instances; the argument is on file in `docs/derivations/` |
| PO-NEMESIS-04 | n ≥ ln(1/δ)/p⋆ clean nominal episodes give P(no failure in n) < δ whenever p > p⋆, since (1 − p)ⁿ ≤ exp(−p·n) | A candidate theorem card; Tested by exact binomial arithmetic until a checker accepts it |

The project's proof classes are ordered, and the words are not interchangeable: `Proved` (a proof a proof
checker accepted), `Certified` (a certificate an independent checker accepted), `Tested`, `Estimated`. A
statistical bound issued by Nemesis is `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ and never `Certified`. A derivation on file
does not raise a class; it makes a `Tested` claim reviewable, and it is what a future certificate or theorem
card would be built from. The program's rule for derivations: one file per known answer or obligation, stating
the model, the derivation, the numeric values with their digits, and the error budget of the test.

A second rule of this chapter is served here: a value that follows from a stated formula is a derived value and
a known answer. The derivation files are where each such value is shown to follow.

Tier Solid. Subsystems: Nemesis, with Lemma (theorem cards and proof classes) as the reader of the candidate.

## Technical approach

1. **`po-nemesis-01-one-minimal.md`.** Statement: delta debugging over an edit list, with `test(S)` true exactly
   when the relation returns "fails", terminates with a set C such that `test(C)` holds and `test(C ∖ {e})` does
   not hold for any e in C. Argument: the loop ends only in a round where the granularity equals |C|, every
   part is a singleton and no complement failed; the complements of singletons are exactly the single removals.
   Termination: each reduction strictly shrinks C or raises the granularity, both bounded. What is *not*
   claimed, with the counterexample: with edits listed (a, c, d, e, b) and failure when {a, b} or {c, d, e} is
   present, the tests in order are ac, deb, a, c, d, eb, cdeb, ceb, cd, e, b, cdb, cde, de, ce (15 distinct
   tests), ending at {c, d, e}, while {a, b} fails with two edits; the pair check for lists of at most 12 edits
   adds at most 12 + 66 = 78 tests. Inadmissible subsets (illegal, or not closed under edit dependencies) count
   as "does not fail", so 1-minimal means: every single removal passes or is inadmissible.
2. **`po-nemesis-02-weight-bound.md`.** With nominal density p, shifted density p_s and proposal
   q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p_s for 0 < ε ≤ 1: since (1 − ε)·p_s ≥ 0, q ≥ ε·p, so w = p/q ≤ 1/ε wherever p > 0 (the one
   line). Unbiasedness: q > 0 wherever p > 0, so E_q[w·1(failure)] = ∫ (p/q)·1(failure)·q = P_p(failure). The
   scaled observation ε·w·1(failure) lies in [0, 1] with mean ε·P_p(failure), so a confidence sequence on a
   bounded mean applies and, divided by ε, bounds the probability at every stopping time. Consequence stated
   with numbers: with no failure observed in n runs the upper bound cannot fall below about ln(1/δ)/(ε·n); at
   ε = 0.1 the 29,956 clean episodes that certify 10⁻⁴ under crude sampling certify only 1.0 × 10⁻³. The same
   three lines hold for discrete scene edits with p and q as probability mass functions. Known answer worked:
   standard normal z, failure z > 4, shift 4, ε = 0.1: P = 3.167 × 10⁻⁵; every weight at most 10.
3. **`po-nemesis-03-coverage-certificate.md`.** For f monotone and submodular on scenes, S any selection, O any
   set of at most k scenes: f(O) ≤ f(O ∪ S) (monotone) ≤ f(S) + Σ_{o ∈ O∖S} [f(S ∪ {o}) − f(S)] (submodular,
   adding the scenes of O one at a time) ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k largest remaining marginal gains. Why
   weighted coverage is monotone submodular (a mode counted once however many scenes expose it). The budget
   form by the fractional-knapsack relaxation. The greedy guarantee f(S_k) ≥ (1 − (1 − 1/k)^k)·OPT_k ≥
   (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k, cited with its standard proof sketch. Why a certificate from stale (cached) gains is still
   a bound and is still refused as the stored certificate. The worked instance in full (40 modes, 18 scenes:
   coverage 86, remaining gains 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, bound 98, ratio 43/49 = 0.8776, true optimum 90).
4. **`po-nemesis-04-zero-failure.md`, with `audit-bound.md` as its corollary.** Statement: for an integer
   n ≥ 1 and 0 < p⋆ < 1, 0 < δ < 1 with n ≥ ln(1/δ)/p⋆: for every p in (p⋆, 1], (1 − p)ⁿ < δ. Proof:
   1 − p ≤ exp(−p) for all real p, so (1 − p)ⁿ ≤ exp(−p·n) < exp(−p⋆·n) ≤ δ. Reading: if n independent nominal
   episodes with a frozen failure predicate show no failure, a failure probability above p⋆ is rejected at
   level δ. The exact form: the smallest n with (1 − p⋆)ⁿ ≤ δ is ⌈ln δ / ln(1 − p⋆)⌉, slightly below
   ⌈ln(1/δ)/p⋆⌉.

   | p⋆ | Exact smallest n (δ = 0.05) | Sufficient n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p⋆⌉ |
   |---|---|---|
   | 10⁻⁴ | 29,956 | 29,958 |
   | 10⁻⁵ | 299,572 | 299,574 |
   | 10⁻⁶ | 2,995,731 | 2,995,733 |

   At the level of the tenth qualification request, 0.05/110, the exact count for 10⁻⁴ is 76,959. Inverting for
   a fixed sample gives the audit's bound: with no top-k design among m audited discards the false-discard rate
   is at most 1 − δ^(1/m), which is 0.0950 at m = 30 and 0.0495 at m = 59; the approximation ln(1/δ)/m gives
   0.0999 at m = 30 and is not used.
5. **The candidate theorem card (handed to Lemma; DESIGN of the hand-off, not an admitted card).**
   - *Statement.* As in point 4, for rational p⋆ = a/q and δ = c/d and an integer n.
   - *Route A (exact power).* The checker decides (q − a)ⁿ·d ≤ c·qⁿ in big-integer arithmetic; monotonicity of
     xⁿ on [0, 1] gives the statement for every p > p⋆. No analytic assumption. It decides the exact counts
     (true at n = 29,956 and false at 29,955 for 10⁻⁴; true at 299,572 and false at 299,571 for 10⁻⁵). Cost: the
     integers have about n·log₂ q bits, 7.5 megabytes at p⋆ = 10⁻⁶.
   - *Route B (truncated series).* With x = n·a/q, the checker decides Σ_{i=0..m} xⁱ/i! ≥ d/c in rational
     arithmetic (m = 16 suffices for the three sufficient counts of the table: the sums are 20.00135, 20.00015
     and 20.00001; at one episode fewer they are 19.99935, 19.99995 and 19.99999 and the check fails, as it
     should for a count below ln(1/δ)/p⋆). Two named analytic assumptions carry it to the
     statement: 1 − p ≤ exp(−p), and exp(x) is at least each partial sum of its series for x ≥ 0. Cheap at any
     n; decides only the sufficient counts.
   - *What the card would and would not license.* It is arithmetic about a binomial model. Independence of the
     episodes, a failure predicate frozen before sampling, and nominal draws are assumptions of the bound, not
     of the card; with a card admitted, a bound's class stays `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, and what becomes
     `Certified` is only the arithmetic step from "n clean episodes" to "rejected at level δ".
   - *Interim posture.* Until Lemma rules, the obligation is `Tested` by exact binomial arithmetic.
6. **Numbers under test.** `docs/derivations/nemesis/numbers.toml` lists every number the four files print
   (each with its formula identifier and inputs); the test `derivations_numbers` recomputes each in exact or
   outward-rounded arithmetic and fails on any mismatch, so a file cannot drift from the code's known answers.

## Considerations

- Three formulas for the zero-failure count circulate (the exact ceiling, the sufficient ceiling, and the
  approximation rounded down). The files state all three and which one each known answer uses, so that a
  two-episode difference is never mistaken for a defect.
- Admitting the card is Lemma's call. If it is not admitted, nothing changes downstream: the obligation remains
  a test, which is where the chapter leaves it.
- These files are also the references a reviewer needs for the exit review; they contain no result that
  depends on a simulator.
