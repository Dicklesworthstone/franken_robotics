## Background

A gate is a numeric threshold with a recorded measurement. The program's rules: a gate passes only with a
recorded measurement against its threshold, as a row of `registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the
command, its output and a receipt hash, and that resolves in the artifact graph; a gate with no record has the
status `not run`, which is not a pass; statuses are `not run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and
`blocked: ⟨open question⟩`. Each gate names exactly one of the seven claims. Every convergence gate has a
registered negative control that it catches; a gate whose control has not been shown to fail has not passed. A
kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded
as an architecture decision at the milestone the register names; a trigger, threshold or fallback changes only
by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan. Where a chapter and the gate register differ the register governs; the
register's threshold column is a brief, and a clause the brief leaves out is not waived. A milestone exits when
every gate with a part due there has a record that is a pass or a kill decision with its fallback adopted. The
project's honest-status table is generated from this registry.

Nemesis defines eight convergence gates and three kill gates, measured and decided across many beads. The record
of each is created by the register bead that owns it (Scope names each). This bead supplies the chapter's side
of all eleven: the full criterion of each gate, so that a threshold cannot be lost or softened between its
definition and its measurement, its controls and its measuring or deciding bead; and it states what the
chapter's three records in the pilot register must contain. All thresholds are TARGETs until a gate records a
measured value.

## Scope

This bead is the Nemesis chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the eight gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as
  the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 created; the chapter's
  owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead and script.
- For the three kill gates: the map from each to its deciding bead, and the check that each has its ledger
  record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The required content of the three pilot records (`PILOT-NEMESIS-01`, `PILOT-C-28`, `PILOT-RARE-01`) and the
  test that they are complete; the registry tests; the milestone views.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-NEMESIS-01 to -08 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02.
- The kill records of KILL-NEMESIS, KILL-RARE and KILL-MCW, with trigger, fallback, decision point (M3), mode
  and what each trigger reads: the fallback ledger, N40-26.
- The measurements (the scripts named per row) and the kill decisions (their decision beads).
- Writing the three pilot records and their results, which the pilot beads (dependencies) do at M2 when they
  freeze their protocols: N26a-06 writes `PILOT-NEMESIS-01` (the search; KILL-NEMESIS) and `PILOT-C-28` (minimal
  counterexample worlds; KILL-MCW) from its one run, and N26a-28 writes `PILOT-RARE-01` (exploration;
  KILL-RARE).

Bead keys behind the rows. Scripts: `nemesis_legality.sh` N26a-05; `nemesis_oracle.sh` N26a-33;
`nemesis_search.sh` N26a-11; `nemesis_shrink.sh` N26a-34; `nemesis_bound.sh` N26a-30; `nemesis_explore.sh`
N26a-31; `nemesis_guard.sh` N26a-16; `nemesis_audit.sh` N26b-07; `nemesis_cover.sh` N26b-12. Decisions:
KILL-NEMESIS N26a-12; KILL-RARE N26a-32; KILL-MCW N26a-35. Each gate declaration's `measured_by` names the
script's bead, and each kill gate's deciding bead is the key given here.

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-NEMESIS-01 to -08 are
created by N40-02, with the fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section` (26.15), `consumers`,
`milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`, `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`.
The three kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-26) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`
and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers, as registered in
   `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, of the negative controls in the last column of the table below,
   and the tiers the gate is measured on. The `NC-` identifiers are the ones the seeding tool of N38b-05 makes
   from the chapter's catalogue entries (N26-CX, a dependency: each entry's `rule_ids` names the gate whose row
   below carries its plant) and from the stated controls of its known-answer rows (N26-KA, a dependency). A
   control that no catalogue entry and no known-answer row states has its record written, under BD-025's rules,
   by the bead that owns it: the invariant mutants by N26b-15 (seed `INV-NEMESIS-nn`; among them GATE-NEMESIS-01's
   per-edit-only legality checker and its oracle that does not guard its witnesses), and every other by the bead
   that measures its gate (seed `GATE-NEMESIS-nn`), after BD-025's reuse check against the seeded records: the
   potency control and the A/A arm of GATE-NEMESIS-02 by N26a-11; the first-round-only shrinker and the file with
   one altered digest of GATE-NEMESIS-03 by N26a-34; the crude baseline arm of GATE-NEMESIS-05 by N26a-31; the
   guard that rejects everything of GATE-NEMESIS-06 by N26a-16 where KA-662's control cell does not already seed
   it; the certificate without its remaining-gain term and the lazy queue without its stamp check of
   GATE-NEMESIS-08 by N26b-12. A gate lists each control by its `NC-` identifier; a control whose record or
   plant does not exist is listed with state `pending` and its owning bead (the form N22c-49 uses), keeps its
   gate ineligible to pass, and is never dropped.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of `frob-nemesis` (`registries/gate_declarations.toml` until that block is agreed with the
   owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record has no
   field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full threshold of the table below, of which
   the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the bead key and script path.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its deciding bead as data of the
   registry tests. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is
   written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30; the
   decision's ADR is named in the kill record's `decision` (N40-03).

The Claim, Earliest, Trigger and Fallback columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the register
governs. They were compared with N40-02 and N40-26 when the beads were reconciled, and they agree. If the two
ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is
never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`,
`killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); on record a part carries `commit`, `command`, `receipt` (the
hash of the receipt file, which names the output) and `measured` (values, never targets). A kill decision's
outcome is `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gates_nemesis.rs`: `gates_nemesis_rows` (as above); `claims_match_register`;
`milestones_match_register`; `kill_rows_complete`; `pilot_rows_complete`; `status_table_prints_open`;
`pass_without_receipt_rejected`; `copied_target_rejected`; `threshold_text_hash` (the hash of each declaration's
`criterion` string is pinned, so an edit is a visible registry change with a dated reason);
`one_record_per_identifier` (a planted second record for GATE-NEMESIS-01 fails the load with `duplicate-id`).

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. Each measuring script writes a hash-named receipt through the shared end-to-end helper. A
recording session that did not implement the capability then enters the part's record, citing that receipt (no
script and no verb writes a gate record: N40-01). The validator refuses a `pass` whose metrics do not meet the
gate's threshold or whose negative controls were not caught in the same run. The record's `measured` values are
those of the script's `metric` log lines, so a registry row can be traced to the log that produced it.

### Known answers and negative controls
The registry's negative controls are the three planted bad rows. The gates' own controls are listed per row and
live in the counterexample and mutant registries of the chapter. Known answers the thresholds lean on: KA-659 to
KA-669.

## Considerations

- Several thresholds are deliberately stricter than a calibrated procedure can meet by luck alone (95% coverage
  of a 95% bound; 100 of 100 for a potency control). The measuring beads design their studies so that passing
  is not a coin toss (expected coverage stated in advance; potency controls deterministic by construction);
  the registry keeps the threshold text unchanged.
- KILL-RARE's trigger is worded on the pilot, while the product-scale gate GATE-NEMESIS-05 is measured later;
  the decision bead reads both where they exist. The ledger's row keeps the register's wording.
- A threshold changes only by a dated amendment before the measurement it governs; the pinned hash of each
  threshold string is what makes a silent softening visible.
