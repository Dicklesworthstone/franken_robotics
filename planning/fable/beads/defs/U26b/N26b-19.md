## Background

Nemesis searches for worlds in which a design fails, and an unconstrained adversary wins by building worlds no
robot is meant to face. ADR-NEMESIS-01 therefore confines the search to a **legality set** and keeps priors
apart from admissibility: legality is a set (may this world be used at all?), the nominal randomization is a
distribution on part of it (how often would it arise?). The set is declared in a legality profile, schema
`frob.legality/1`, which lists a bound for every editable quantity with a provenance class: `cited` with its
source, `card` when the bound is the uncertainty of a component card, or `assumed`.

**OPEN-NEMESIS-1. Sources for the legality bounds of interiors.** Owner: the project lead, with OPEN-HAB-1 (which
published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions of the interior generators). It blocks
claims that a counterexample is *realistic*, not its *validity*. **Default (fail closed, in force until
decided): bounds are `assumed` and counterexamples say so.** Dimensional norms for buildings come from cited
sources once that question is decided; until then every counterexample that touches an assumed bound carries
the fact in its legality block, and a request that demands cited bounds is refused (FROB-REF-361: the request
demands `cited` bounds and the profile has an `assumed` bound on a touched quantity).

Why it is an owner decision: choosing sources is a choice of jurisdiction and building types, has licence
consequences for quoting standards, and fixes what the project is willing to call a realistic adversarial
world. The program-level list of owner questions groups it with dimensional norms, retrofit prices, card prices
and the parts catalogue as "one cited source each"; the cost of the default is stated there: no claim about
realism in a published report. The M3 milestone lists the Habitat half among the owner inputs wanted.

## Scope

In: preparing the decision (the inventory of bounds and what governs each; the options); recording the ruling;
wiring it to the source register and the legality profiles; the statistic that keeps the default visible; tests
that the default holds mechanically.
Out: the legality check, the profile format and the two refusals (the legality seed bead, BD-270); the search
verb whose result gains the two shares of point 6 (BD-273, a dependency: this bead adds the fields
`legality_shares.counterexamples_touching_assumed` and `legality_shares.bounds_assumed`, each a numerator and
denominator, to its result); the Habitat decision itself (N19c-30); retrofit prices and legality of amendments
(a separate open question of the scene chapter).

## Technical approach

1. **Inventory for the owner.** Every bound of the default legality profiles, by archetype (home, apartment,
   office, warehouse, manufacturing plant, retail store, commercial kitchen), in three groups:
   - *Dimensional bounds shared with the hazard registries* (door clear widths, sill and threshold heights,
     stair risers, ramp slopes, aisle widths, edge radii): the 59 hazard rows of the seven registries. A norm
     plausibly governs most of them; one ruling under OPEN-HAB-1 serves both uses.
   - *Bounds on fixtures, materials and optics that the hazard registries do not carry as norms* (door-closer
     torque, for example 5 to 20 N·m in a commercial kitchen; conveyor speed; floor friction by surface and
     contaminant; film presence; light positions and reflectances): governed, if at all, by product data
     sheets, test standards or published surveys.
   - *Bounds on clocks, links and disturbances* (sensor latency, dropout bursts, packet jitter, push impulses,
     payload shifts): no building norm applies; the bound is `card` where a sensor or link card states it and
     otherwise `assumed` until the task's author cites a source.
   For each bound: its present range, unit, provenance class, the failure modes it can provoke, and whether a
   norm, a data sheet, a survey or only a measurement could source it.
2. **Options to rule on.**
   - (a) *Reuse.* Whether the sources chosen under OPEN-HAB-1 for dimension distributions also define the
     legality bounds of the same quantities (one source, two uses), or whether legality is deliberately wider
     than the norm: a norm states what is permitted, and old or non-conforming buildings exist.
   - (b) *Kinds of source admitted as `cited`* for the second group: standards only; standards and
     manufacturers' data sheets; published surveys as well.
   - (c) *Wording.* What a report may say about a counterexample whose touched bounds are all `cited`: "inside
     cited bounds" (recommended wording), "code-conforming", or "realistic".
   - (d) *Scope of the first pass.* All bounds, or the bounds touched by the M3 task families first (doors,
     thresholds, floor patches, door closers, sensor latency).
3. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-NEMESIS-1.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`) with the ruling on (a) to
   (d), cross-referenced from the Habitat decision record; one row per chosen source in the source register
   (identifier, title, clause, date read, licence note, the bounds it governs). Sources are re-read from the primary
   document before a gate cites them.
4. **Effect of a ruling.** For each governed bound the profile's provenance changes from `assumed` to `cited`
   with the source's identifier, and the bound's range is set from the source where the ruling says so. A
   profile is a versioned, hashed object: the change is a new profile identity. New counterexamples record the
   new provenance in `legality.touched`. Existing counterexample files keep what they recorded (their bytes
   and identities do not change); a re-check against the profile in force reports which of them now lie inside
   cited bounds, and which fall outside a narrowed bound and are therefore no longer legal under the new
   profile (they stay valid under the profile they name).
5. **Until decided (the default, enforced by code that exists).** Every bound not backed by a card is
   `assumed`; every counterexample that touches one says so; `--require-cited` and any report claiming realism
   are refused with FROB-REF-361, whose ranked repairs are: rerun without the demand and accept the label;
   record a source for the bound in the source register; restrict the search to quantities with `cited` or
   `card` bounds. The search does not discard a rare but admissible world: validity never depended on sources.
6. **Statistic kept visible.** Every search result and every milestone exit review reports the share of
   promoted counterexamples that touch at least one `assumed` bound, and the share of bounds per profile that
   are `assumed`. At the default both are near 100% for building quantities; the number is the honest measure
   of how much "inside the legality set" currently means.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The inventory of legality bounds per archetype exists, in the three groups, with a provenance class and a
      "what could source it" mark for every bound, and is attached to the decision record.
- [ ] The owner's ruling on reuse, admitted kinds of source, wording and scope is recorded; or, absent a ruling
      at the M3 exit, the record states that the default is in force and that realism claims are refused.
- [ ] Each chosen source has a row in the source register with clause, date read and licence note, and names
      the bounds it governs.
- [ ] After a ruling, governed bounds show `cited` in the profile and in the `legality.touched` block of new
      counterexamples; a request with `--require-cited` that touches only `cited` and `card` bounds is
      admitted; one that touches a remaining `assumed` bound is still refused with FROB-REF-361.
- [ ] The two shares of point 6 are printed by `nemesis.search` results and logged as `metric` lines by this
      bead's script, from which the M3 exit review reads them (the review is a later bead; this bead does not
      wait for it).
- [ ] No counterexample's verdict, class, witness or digests change when only a provenance class changes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/legality_sources_decision.rs` (exercising the profiles and the promotion path with a
source row labelled `synthetic`):
- `default_bounds_assumed_or_card`: in every default profile no bound is `cited`.
- `promotion_changes_only_governed_bounds`: a synthetic source governing `closer_torque` changes that bound in
  the profiles that have it and no other; the profile identity changes.
- `require_cited_refused_while_any_touched_bound_assumed` and `require_cited_admitted_when_all_cited_or_card`.
- `validity_independent_of_provenance`: the same world's legality verdict, the candidate's verdict and the
  state digests are equal before and after a provenance-only promotion.
- `narrowed_bound_recheck`: a stored counterexample at 19 N·m is reported outside a profile narrowed to
  [5, 18] N·m and remains valid under the profile it names.
- `shares_reported`: a result with three counterexamples, two touching assumed bounds, prints 2 of 3.
- `source_row_requires_clause_and_date`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/legality_sources_decision.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): `frob nemesis search … --require-cited --key
ls-1 --json` on a planted solvable trap whose trigger touches the closer torque (expect exit 3, FROB-REF-361, the
three repairs); the same without the demand under `--key ls-2` (admitted; the result's `legality.touched` shows
`assumed`); then, in a scratch registry with a synthetic source registered for that bound, the demand under `--key
ls-3` is admitted (three keys, so no call returns another's stored envelope). Every NDJSON line, through the shared
helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26b-19`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the
envelope's evidence block; per step the refusal block (code, pointer, observed, required, repairs), the
`legality.touched` entries with their provenance classes, the profile identity before and after the synthetic
promotion, and the two shares with numerator and denominator. On failure: the first assertion whose observed value
differs, with both values and the paths of the world, profile and scratch registry needed to reproduce it. Final
`summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: []`. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/legality_sources_decision/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: at the default, 0 bounds are `cited`. Negative controls: a profile edited to say `cited` with no
source row must be rejected by the profile loader; a report path that reads the provenance of the profile's
bounds in general instead of the touched ones must be caught (a world touching only card-backed bounds must be
admitted under `--require-cited` even while other bounds are assumed).

## Considerations

- An owner decision that blocks a class of claims is one step more urgent than its milestone.
- The decision cannot make a counterexample more valid, only more citable. A design that fails in an
  assumed-legal world still fails there; the question is whether anyone must care.
- Narrowing a bound to a norm can remove counterexamples from the legal set; they are not deleted, and the
  hard-world set re-promotes its members against the new profile before they are used again.
- If the two questions are ruled on separately, the records must agree on shared quantities; the profile
  loader refuses a bound whose cited source differs from the hazard registry's source for the same quantity.
