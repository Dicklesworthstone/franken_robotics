## Background

Nemesis explores for rare failures by **splitting**: a world that has reached a level of distress is
snapshotted and continued many times under fresh per-step disturbances, so that compute concentrates on worlds
that are already in trouble. The distress score is an online, past-time requirement margin (the dimensionless
distance of the episode so far from violating its requirement; for an "always" clause the running minimum of
the clause's margin), with fixed levels chosen before the run. Splitting earns its keep only if the score is
*informative*: worlds that cross a level must be much likelier to fail than worlds that have not. The decision
policy for rare failures names "an uninformative distress score" as a failure mode and falls back to crude
nominal sampling when under 1% of forks reach the next level.

Some requirements defeat the margin as a score by construction. A count that is exact (zero chassis strikes) has
margin +1 or −1 and nothing between. A clearance stays large until a sudden collision. A deadline requirement
("delivered within 20 s") has a margin that says nothing until the deadline passes. For these the margin is flat
until shortly before failure and gives splitting nothing to climb.

**OPEN-NEMESIS-2. The distress score when the requirement margin is flat until shortly before failure.** Owner:
the Nemesis lead. It blocks GATE-NEMESIS-05 (exploration reaches planted failures with at least 10 times fewer
episodes than crude sampling at 10⁻⁵, and finds both modes in at least 80% of repetitions). **Default (fail
closed, in force until decided): no splitting for that requirement; crude sampling.** The default is already
enforced by the flat-margin detection of the distress-score module: a requirement declared `flat` is never
split.

This bead is the experiment that can retire the default for some classes of requirement, or confirm it. It is an
experiment, not a mechanism: its output is a decision record and, where a candidate passes, a registered
alternative score for a named class of requirement. Frontier, like splitting itself; the ceiling of anything it
enables is discovery speed only (the price of certifying absence is unchanged).

## Scope

In: the candidate scores; the flat-margin fixtures; the registered experiment (arms, metric, decision rule fixed
before data); the decision record; the registry of admitted scores per requirement class, and the distress-score
module's reading of it (no other bead reads `distress_scores.toml`; the arms of point 3 run through this reader).
Out: the splitting mechanism and the margin-based score (seed BD-277, N26a-29; both dependencies, since the arms
run through them); the search verb that runs the arms (BD-273, a dependency); the product-scale gate (N26a-31,
which depends on this bead's decision record for the classes it excludes); the kill decision KILL-RARE
(N26a-32).

## Technical approach

1. **Candidate scores (each an online, per-world signal computable in the monitor stage; DESIGN).**
   - S0, the baseline: the running minimum of the requirement's online margin (declared `flat` on these
     fixtures by construction; it is the arm that must lose).
   - S1, sub-formula margins: the minimum over the requirement's *non-binding* atoms and over declared
     precursor atoms the task author attaches to a requirement (for "zero chassis strikes": the smallest
     chassis-to-scene clearance, a continuous quantity that falls before a strike).
   - S2, a progress residual for deadline requirements: remaining distance to the goal divided by the distance
     the body can still cover in the remaining time at its declared top speed (above 1 means the deadline can
     no longer be met).
   - S3, first-anomaly monitors: the earliest anomaly indicator of the failure taxonomy's monitors (slip ratio,
     torque saturation fraction, tilt against the support margin), normalized to [0, 1] and combined by the
     maximum.
   - S4, short look-ahead: the margin of a brief open-loop continuation of the current command (0.5 s) on the
     same tier, evaluated every k ticks. The only candidate with a compute cost of its own, charged to its arm.
   Every candidate is monotonized as a running extremum, so that "reached level ℓ" stays true once true.
2. **Fixtures (three planted flat-margin cases with known or calibrated nominal failure probability).**
   - F1, exact-count: the quadruped of the exploration pilot under per-step pushes, requirement "zero chassis
     strikes", planted probability near 10⁻⁴ (calibrated by crude sampling to a recorded interval).
   - F2, deadline: a wheeled base on a low-friction strip with per-step traction noise, requirement "docked
     within T", planted probability near 10⁻⁴.
   - F3, a synthetic control with a closed form: a biased random walk (up-probability 0.4, start at level 1,
     absorbing at 0 and 20; truth 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴) observed only through a flat score (1 until
     absorption at 20) and through an informative one (the walker's level). It shows what the experiment's
     metric reads when the score is known to be flat and when it is known to be ideal.
3. **Arms and budget.** For each fixture: crude nominal sampling, and splitting under each candidate score, at
   equal simulated-step budget (level pilots and S4's look-ahead charged to the arm), 30 repetitions with
   addressed seeds fixed in the registration.
4. **Metrics.** (a) Per level, the fraction of forks that reach the next level (the policy's fallback trigger
   is under 1%). (b) Steps to the first promoted failure, as a paired ratio against crude sampling with its 95%
   lower bound. (c) Whether the splitting estimate's interval covers the planted probability (share of 30).
   (d) A flatness diagnostic for the record: the median lead time, in control ticks, between the score first
   crossing its highest level and the failure.
5. **Decision rule (fixed before data; registered with the experiment).** A candidate score is *admitted for a
   requirement class* (exact-count; deadline) when, on that class's fixture: at least 1% of forks reach the
   next level at every level; the paired lower bound of the step ratio against crude sampling is at least 3
   (the construction's pilot falsifier: not found at least three times sooner than by uniform sampling); and
   its interval covers the planted probability in at least 85% of repetitions (the coverage clause of
   KILL-RARE). On F3 the flat observation must fail the rule and the informative one must pass it, or the
   experiment itself is void. If no candidate is admitted for a class, the default stands for that class.
6. **Record and effect.** `docs/adr/OPEN-NEMESIS-2.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`): the
   registration, the measured values per arm, the
   literal evaluation of the rule, and the decision per requirement class. Admitted scores are entered in
   `registries/nemesis/distress_scores.toml` (requirement class → score identifier, version, the fixture and
   record that admitted it); the distress-score module reads the registry and uses an admitted score in place
   of the margin for requirements of that class; everything else about splitting (fixed levels chosen from a
   separate pilot, the informativeness check, the refusal when no randomness enters after a fork, rates
   labelled `Estimated`) is unchanged. A requirement class with no admitted score keeps the default, and the
   exploration gate is measured only on requirements with a graded margin or an admitted score, with the
   excluded classes named in its record.
7. **What the experiment may not do.** It may not change a requirement, a verdict or a failure predicate: a
   score orders exploration and never decides whether an episode failed. It may not feed a bound: all its
   episodes are exploration data.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The experiment is registered before its first run with its six parts (question, arms, metric, decision
      rule with thresholds, potency control, ceiling and fallback) and its seed list.
- [ ] On F3 the flat observation is rejected (fork fraction to the next level under 1%, or no gain over crude
      sampling) and the informative observation is admitted, with the estimate's interval covering
      1.504 × 10⁻⁴ in at least 85% of repetitions; otherwise the run is void and says so.
- [ ] For F1 and F2, every candidate's four metrics are recorded per repetition and in summary, with the arms'
      step accounting showing equal budgets within one batch.
- [ ] The decision record states, per requirement class, `admitted: <score>` or `default stands`, by literal
      evaluation of the rule; no threshold was changed after data.
- [ ] If a score is admitted, the registry row exists, the distress-score module uses it for that class, and a
      splitting run on that class's fixture no longer ends in the `flat` declaration.
- [ ] If none is admitted, a splitting request on a flat-margin requirement still falls back to crude sampling
      with the reason, and the decision record names the excluded classes in the form the exploration gate's
      measuring bead (N26a-31, which depends on this bead) cites in GATE-NEMESIS-05's record; this bead does
      not wait for that record (convention 7).
- [ ] The record is written by a session that did not implement the candidate scores.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/distress_candidates.rs` (pure signal tests on recorded or synthetic traces):
- `candidates_are_monotone_running_extrema` (S1 to S4 on 100 recorded traces).
- `s1_precursor_clearance_falls_before_strike`: on a planted strike trace the clearance score crosses 0.5 at
  least 5 control ticks before the strike.
- `s2_progress_residual_closed_form`: 3 m to go, top speed 0.5 m/s, 4 s left → 1.5 (deadline unreachable);
  8 s left → 0.75.
- `s3_normalized_to_unit_interval`; `s4_cost_charged_to_arm`.
- `flat_observation_of_walk_is_flat`: the absorption-only score triggers the flat-margin detection.
- `decision_rule_evaluator`: (fork fraction 0.04, ratio lower bound 3.4, coverage 27/30) → admitted;
  (0.008, …) → rejected; (0.04, 2.6, …) → rejected; (0.04, 3.4, 24/30) → rejected.
- `registry_row_roundtrip`.
- `mapped_class_uses_registered_score` and `unmapped_class_keeps_margin`: with a scratch `distress_scores.toml`
  mapping the deadline class to S2, a deadline requirement's level record is S2's and an exact-count
  requirement keeps the margin (and its `flat` declaration).
- `experiment_rows_only_in_scratch_store`: a row marked `experiment: OPEN-NEMESIS-2` is read from a scratch store
  and rejected by the loader of the production store, so an unadmitted score can never reach a real search.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_distress_experiment.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): runs the arms on F3 (always), and on F1
and F2 where the tier exists, with the options of the verb rows only (VERBS-nemesis): each splitting arm as
`frob nemesis search <candidate> --task <fixture> --legality <profile> --method splitting --tier <t> --budget …
--key nd-<fixture>-<arm>-<rep> --json` under a scratch configuration whose `distress_scores.toml` maps the
fixture's requirement class to that arm's candidate (rows marked `experiment: OPEN-NEMESIS-2`, read only in the
scratch store; the verb gains no option for an unadmitted score), and the crude arm as `frob nemesis bound
<candidate> --task <fixture> --tier <t> --mode estimate --budget … --finalist --key nd-<fixture>-crude-<rep>
--json`, as in the exploration script (N26a-31; `--finalist` because a fixture's candidate is no finalist of a
recorded campaign, and the finalists-only rule of N26a-26 refuses every such bound request without it); every arm and repetition has its own key, so paired arms are never one stored
receipt. It logs per repetition and arm the level record, the per-level fork fractions, steps to first
promoted failure, the estimate with its interval and the planted truth, the flatness diagnostic, and at the end
the literal evaluation of the rule per class. Every NDJSON line, through the shared helper, carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N26b-20`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's
evidence block; each measured value carries its unit and the threshold of the rule it is compared with (1%,
3, 85%); the registration hash, fixture identities and seed list are logged at `start`. On failure (a void F3
run, or an arm whose step accounting differs from the batch's budget): the first repetition and arm at fault,
with both values and the seed and fixture needed to reproduce it. Final `summary`: per class `admitted: <score>`
or `default stands`, arms run, repetitions void; `gates: []`. Logs under
`target/e2e-logs/nemesis_distress_experiment/`; no gate is recorded.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (the biased random walk: 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; control: a walk with no noise after the fork returns identical
children and is refused). The potency control of this experiment is F3's pair of observations: the flat one must
lose and the informative one must win. Negative control of the rule: a planted candidate that is a monotone
function of episode time alone (it crosses every level and predicts nothing) must be rejected by the ratio
clause. Determinism lanes: the walk fixture is exact arithmetic on addressed draws; fixtures F1 and F2 inherit
the determinism class of their tier and compare counts and intervals only.

## Considerations

- A score built from task-specific precursors (S1) needs the task author to declare them; that is a change to
  the task file's optional fields, not to its requirement, and it is recorded in the task's identity.
- S4 spends steps to decide where to spend steps; it is admitted only if its gain survives its own cost, which
  is why costs are charged to the arm.
- An admitted score is an explorer's tuning. It carries no evidence class, and a bound refuses every episode
  it helped to select.
- If KILL-RARE fires, splitting is removed and this experiment's registry is retired with it; the record stays.
- Owner of the question: the Nemesis lead, who signs the decision record.
