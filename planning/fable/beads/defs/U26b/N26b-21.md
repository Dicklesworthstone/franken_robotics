## Background

"Is this design's failure probability below p⋆?" is a claim, and only estimators with a valid finite-sample
bound may make it. The cheapest such bound is the zero-failure rule: after n independent nominal episodes with
no failure, p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) at confidence 1 − δ. The cost of a claim is therefore fixed by arithmetic. With
δ = 0.05:

| Target p⋆ | Nominal episodes with no failure | Time at an illustrative 400 episodes per second |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁻⁴ | 29,956 | 75 s |
| 10⁻⁵ | 299,572 | 12.5 min |
| 10⁻⁶ | 2,995,731 | 2.1 h |

Throughput is for the performance work to establish; the middle column does not move. Proving absence has a
fixed price that no sampler discounts without assuming where the failures live: exploration by splitting or
tilting finds failures sooner and issues only rates labelled `Estimated`.

The program inherits a machine rule as its fail-closed default: no run above 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores, one hour
of wall time and one device without written approval (envelope class E0). A 10⁻⁶ claim takes 2.1 hours at the
illustrated throughput and 21 hours at a tenth of it.

**OPEN-NEMESIS-3. The compute envelope for a 10⁻⁶ claim, which exceeds the one-hour job rule at the illustrated
throughput.** Owner: the project lead. **Default (fail closed, in force until decided): certify to 10⁻⁵ and
report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.** What the default costs: the lowest certified rate is 10⁻⁵. It blocks any certified
rate below 10⁻⁵ and nothing else. The owner's question list carries it with a latest useful answer at M3; the M4
milestone lists it again among owner inputs wanted; the work-package table asks for class E2 (a design campaign,
48 device-hours) for it, with the default as the outcome if not approved.

## Scope

In: preparing the decision (the arithmetic at measured throughput; the options); recording the ruling; wiring it
to the envelope in force; tests that the default holds mechanically and that a ruling takes effect.
Out, with the bead that has it: the bound and its planner (the certification seed bead BD-276 and its component
beads); the request itself as registered at M0, row R5 of the envelope request register (WP-55, "a certified
failure rate of 10⁻⁶", class E2, fallback "certify to 10⁻⁵; report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`"), and the list of all
requests put to the owner (N44-01; this bead depends on it and fills that row's ruling, keeping no second copy);
the envelope classes, the record's file and loader (N21c-35); the admission check, the device-hour meter and
refusal `FROB-REF-761` (N36-20); the sample caps of the sibling's sequences (OPEN-NEMESIS-4, N26b-22).

## Technical approach

1. **The arithmetic the owner needs, recomputed at measured throughput.** For each reference robot class and
   tier, from the measured episodes per second R of the task in question (the estimate verb's calibrated model,
   not the illustrative 400):
   - seconds for a 10⁻⁶ certification = 2,995,731 / R; at R = 400 it is 7,489 s (2.08 h), at R = 40 it is
     74,893 s (20.8 h);
   - the throughput at which the claim fits one hour is 833 episodes per second; two hours (the per-run limit
     of class E1) needs 417; 48 device-hours (class E2) needs 18;
   - what one hour buys at R: n = 3,600·R episodes and the reachable bound 1 − 0.05^(1/n); at R = 400 that is
     1,440,000 episodes and 2.08 × 10⁻⁶;
   - inside the qualification lane the level is that of the request (α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]), and the counts
     grow: the tenth request needs 76,959 episodes for 10⁻⁴, 769,618 for 10⁻⁵ and 7,696,209 for 10⁻⁶.
2. **Options to rule on.**
   - (a) *Keep the default.* The lowest certified rate is 10⁻⁵; 10⁻⁶ is reported `Estimated` from exploration.
   - (b) *Approve a named envelope for 10⁻⁶ certification of a finalist*: class E2 (48 device-hours) as the
     work-package table asks, or class E1 (two hours per run, 12 device-hours per gate) where the measured
     throughput is at least 417 episodes per second; per finalist, per task, with the estimate attached to the
     approval.
   - (c) *Permit accumulation across runs under class E0*: one certification under one commitment, executed
     as several resumable jobs of at most one hour each. The bound's validity is unaffected (independent
     nominal episodes under a predicate frozen before sampling; the zero-failure rule and the sequential
     binomial bound hold at any stopping time), so the question is only whether the one-hour rule is about a
     single run or about total spend; that is the owner's to say.
   - (d) *A cap on how many finalists may buy a 10⁻⁶ bound per campaign*, if (b) or (c) is chosen. A screening
     rung never buys a rare-failure bound in any case.
3. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-NEMESIS-3.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`): the table of point 1 at
   measured throughput, the ruling on (a) to (d), its date, and the envelope class it names. The ruling takes effect
   through the one record of envelope classes and approvals that the estimate verb and the admission check read,
   `registries/compute_envelope.toml` (file and loader N21c-35; request rows N44-01): row R5 of its `[[request]]`
   table gets `status`, `approved_by`, `date`, `decision` (the path above), `token` (its hash) and `expires`. Under
   (a) the status is `declined` and the row's fallback is in force. Under (b) the row is `approved` (class E2) or
   `amended` (class E1 where the measured throughput is at least 417 episodes per second) and its `admits` list
   names one scope per approved certification, `campaign:nemesis-certify/<finalist>/<task>`, with the estimate
   attached to the decision note; the cap of (d) is the number of such scopes the owner allows per campaign. Under
   (c) nothing above E0 is approved: the row stays `declined` for class E2 and the decision note records that
   accumulation across resumable E0 runs is permitted, which the certification job reads as a flag of its own
   (`accumulate_across_runs`, in the `nemesis.bound` configuration, DESIGN), since no envelope class is exceeded by
   any single run.
4. **Mechanics that enforce the default (existing behaviour, pinned here by tests).** `nemesis.bound --mode
   certify` computes n and its seconds at admission. If the seconds exceed the envelope in force the request is
   refused before anything runs, with the envelope refusal (FROB-REF-761: a job exceeds the compute envelope in
   force); if the episodes exceed the caller's budget, with FROB-REF-760 (the episodes a claim needs at its
   level exceed the budget; the bound reachable within budget is attached). Either refusal lists the repair
   "certify a decade higher and report the target as `Estimated`". A result that reports 10⁻⁶ from exploration
   carries class `Estimated` and the sentence that it is not a bound.
5. **Effect of a ruling.** (b): requests for 10⁻⁶ on a named finalist are admitted when their estimate fits the
   approved class; the result is a bound of class `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, scoped to its tier and
   randomization. (c): the certification job may be resumed by key across runs; each run is at most one hour;
   the accumulator's commitment (predicate, nominal stream, level) is unchanged across runs, and a changed
   predicate is refused. In every case counts above 10⁶ use the project's own exact binomial bound, since the
   sibling's sequential routine stops at 10⁶ samples.
6. **Statistic kept visible.** Every rare-failure result prints the lowest rate that is certifiable in the
   envelope in force at the measured throughput, so a reader sees what the default costs on their own task.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record holds the arithmetic at measured throughput for at least one reference task per robot
      class in use at M3, and the ruling; or, absent a ruling at the M3 exit, the statement that the default is
      in force.
- [ ] Under the default, a 10⁻⁶ certification request whose estimate exceeds one hour is refused at admission
      with the envelope refusal and the decade-higher repair; a 10⁻⁵ request that fits is admitted.
- [ ] A 10⁻⁶ figure produced by exploration is labelled `Estimated` in the result, the report and the scorecard,
      never `Tested`.
- [ ] With a scratch envelope record approving the named class, the same 10⁻⁶ request is admitted and its
      planned count is 2,995,731.
- [ ] Accumulation across runs exists behind `accumulate_across_runs` (off by default; set in the real
      configuration only under ruling (c)): with it set in a scratch configuration, a 10⁻⁶ certification is
      admitted as runs of at most one hour each, and resumed by key across three runs (the resume of BD-276) it
      reaches the same bound as one uninterrupted run with the same addresses; with it unset the same request is
      refused at admission with the envelope refusal.
- [ ] Every rare-failure result prints the lowest certifiable rate in the envelope in force.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/envelope_decision.rs`:
- `plan_seconds_at_throughput`: R = 400 → 7,489 s; R = 40 → 74,893 s; R = 833 → 3,597 s (fits one hour).
- `one_hour_buys`: R = 400 → 1,440,000 episodes, reachable bound 2.08 × 10⁻⁶.
- `qualification_level_counts`: tenth request → 76,959, 769,618 and 7,696,209.
- `default_refuses_1e6_above_one_hour` (envelope refusal with the repair) and `default_admits_1e5`.
- `estimated_label_never_tested` (a 10⁻⁶ splitting rate passed through result, report and scorecard formatters).
- `approved_class_admits` (scratch envelope record).
- `accumulation_equals_single_run`: three resumed runs on the synthetic rate task against one run; equal counts
  and equal bound; a changed predicate between runs is refused.
- `accumulate_flag_off_by_default`: with the flag unset a plan longer than one hour is refused at admission;
  with it set, the plan is split into runs of at most 3,600 s each.
- `lowest_certifiable_rate_printed`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_envelope_decision.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): `frob nemesis bound <candidate> --task
<synthetic rate task> --tier batch-cpu --mode certify --target 1e-6 --delta 0.05 --estimate --key ne-1 --json`
(expect the planned 2,995,731 episodes, the fixed-n design of N26a-22, and the seconds at measured throughput),
then without `--estimate` under the default envelope (`--key ne-2`; expect exit 4, the exit code the refusal
registry gives `FROB-REF-761`, with the repair), then `--target 1e-5` (`--key ne-3`; admitted), then with a
scratch copy of `registries/compute_envelope.toml` in which row R5 is approved for the task's scope (`--key
ne-4`; admitted), then, under the default envelope and a scratch configuration with `accumulate_across_runs`
set, the 10⁻⁶ request with `--estimate` (`--key ne-5`; the plan is split into runs of at most one hour each).
Each call has its own key, so no call returns another's stored envelope.
Every NDJSON line, through the shared helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26b-21`), `step`, `event`, the exact
command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block; per step the planned count and seconds, the
measured throughput R the plan used, the envelope class in force and the envelope record's hash, the lowest
certifiable rate printed, and for a refusal the refusal block (code, observed, required, repairs). On failure:
the first step whose count, seconds, exit code or admission differs from the expected, with both values and the
scratch envelope file needed to reproduce it. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: []`. Logs
under `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_envelope_decision/`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-664 (29,956, 299,572 and 2,995,731 episodes at δ = 0.05; 76,959 for the tenth qualification request at
10⁻⁴). CX-290 (a good sampler discounts the price of proving absence: it does not). Negative controls: a planner
that reads the illustrative 400 episodes per second instead of the measured throughput (the admission decision
changes on a slow task); a formatter that prints an exploration rate without `Estimated`.

## Considerations

- An owner decision that blocks a class of claims is one step more urgent than its milestone.
- The decision is about compute, not about validity: nothing the owner chooses changes what a bound means.
- A 10⁻⁶ claim is a statement about the model at one tier under the task's nominal randomization; it is never a
  Reality claim, and a bound obtained on a fast tier is provisional until promoted. The envelope spent on it
  buys exactly that and no more.
- The exit code of the envelope refusal follows the refusal registry's row for it, which is exit 4 (budget:
  refused at admission); this bead does not fix it and its tests read the code from the registry.
- Linked question: the sibling's 10⁶ sample caps (OPEN-NEMESIS-4) do not limit a 10⁻⁶ certification, because
  the project's own exact bound serves larger counts; they limit which routine runs.
