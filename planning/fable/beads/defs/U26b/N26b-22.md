## Background

Nemesis reaches the sibling numerical library FrankenSim only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, and uses
two of its statistics routines for rare-failure work. FACT (read at plan time; to be re-read by the admission
test of this bead): `frankensim/crates/fs-eproc/src/bernoulli.rs` provides a confidence sequence for a Bernoulli
rate as a Beta(½, ½) mixture, with a cap of 10⁶ samples; `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`
provides bounded-weight importance sampling on Gaussian germs with checkpoints and a cap of 10⁶ evaluations.
FrankenSim has no splitting estimator; splitting on forks is implemented in `frob-nemesis`.

The caps matter because the price of a claim is fixed by arithmetic: at δ = 0.05 a zero-failure certification
of 10⁻⁵ needs 299,572 nominal episodes (under the cap) and of 10⁻⁶ needs 2,995,731 (three times over it).

**OPEN-NEMESIS-4. The sample caps of 10⁶ in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance
execution, and the absence of splitting upstream.** Owner: the airlock maintainer. **Default (in force): our own
exact binomial bound for larger counts; upstream work items FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 are open and no milestone
waits on them.** It blocks nothing.

The program's rule for sibling repositories shapes what this bead may do. Every sibling is the owner's, and this
program changes it where it needs changing, by an *upstream work item*: a change made in the sibling's own
repository under that repository's rules, recorded in `registries/upstream.toml` with five things (the change, a
priority, the interim posture here, the switch-over rule, and one reproducible failing fixture). No milestone
waits on an upstream change landing, and no local patch or fork stands in for one. A gap found in a sibling
becomes an admission test here and an upstream work item there, in that order: the admission test records what
the pinned commit does.

## Scope

In: the admission tests that record the two caps and the absence of splitting at the pinned commit; the test
that the interim posture holds at the boundary; the two rows of the upstream registry with their failing
fixtures; the decision record.
Out: the changes inside FrankenSim (the upstream work items FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 themselves); the
switch-over when they land (N26b-24); the sequential path and the exact bound (N26a-22); splitting (BD-277).

## Technical approach

1. **Admission tests (what the pinned commit does; `crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission_stats_caps.rs`).**
   - `bernoulli_sequence_cap`: through the airlock trait, feed the sibling's Bernoulli sequence 10⁶ samples
     (accepted) and one more (the cap's error, recorded verbatim with the commit hash).
   - `importance_execution_cap`: request an importance run of 10⁶ evaluations (accepted) and of 10⁶ + 1 (the
     cap's error, recorded).
   - `no_splitting_estimator`: the airlock's capability listing of the `stats` group contains no splitting
     entry (a listing test, so the fact is re-established at every pin instead of remembered).
   Each records `{sibling commit, crate, routine, cap, observed error}` in the admission record. A pin whose
   behaviour differs (a lifted cap, a new estimator) fails the test by design; that failure is the signal that
   starts the switch-over.
2. **Interim posture, pinned by tests.**
   - Sequential certification uses the sibling's sequence while the planned or realized count is at most 10⁶
     and the project's own exact binomial bound (integer counts, outward rounding) beyond; the routing decision
     is made at admission from the planned count and is recorded in the result (`bound.routine: "fs-eproc" |
     "exact-binomial"`). The two routines must agree at the boundary: for n = 10⁶ − 1, 10⁶ and the same zero
     failures, the exact bound 1 − 0.05^(1/n) (2.9957 × 10⁻⁶ at n = 10⁶) is at or below the sequence's upper
     bound, since a bound valid at every stopping time is wider than a fixed-n bound.
   - Importance sampling runs are refused above 10⁶ evaluations at admission, with the budget refusal's shape
     and the repair "split the estimate into independent stages under separate commitments, or use crude
     nominal sampling": no silent truncation of a run at the cap.
   - Within the qualification lane the level shrinks with each request (α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]): for a 10⁻⁵
     target the count passes 10⁶ at the 33rd request (995,793 at the 32nd; 1,001,856 at the 33rd). The routing
     handles it; the record states it so nobody meets it by surprise.
   - Claims are certified to 10⁻⁵ by default and reported as `Estimated` beyond (the default of OPEN-NEMESIS-3);
     where the owner approves an envelope for 10⁻⁶ the exact binomial routine carries it.
   - Splitting stays in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture (a biased random walk: up-probability 0.4,
     start at level 1; the probability of reaching level 20 before level 0 is 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴).
3. **The two upstream rows (`registries/upstream.toml`).**

   | Item | Repository | The change | Priority | Interim posture here | Switch-over rule | Reproducible failing fixture |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | FD-FSIM-18 | frankensim | Lift or parameterize the 10⁶ sample caps in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance execution | P3 | Our own exact binomial bound for larger counts; claims certified to 10⁻⁵ and reported as `Estimated` beyond | Re-run GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count | `bernoulli_sequence_cap` and `importance_execution_cap` at 10⁶ + 1 |
   | FD-FSIM-19 | frankensim | A splitting estimator (adaptive multilevel splitting) beside the existing importance sampler | P3 | Splitting is implemented in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture | Differential test of the two estimators on the random-walk fixture, then choose one | `no_splitting_estimator`, with the random-walk known answer as the fixture the upstream estimator must pass |

   Upstream changes are additive (a new mode, entry point or parameter, never a change to existing behaviour)
   and follow the sibling's own rules; a pin then advances through the compatibility train (move the pin, write
   a new receipt, rerun the admission and parity gates, relock).
4. **Decision record.** `docs/open/OPEN-NEMESIS-4.md`: the two caps and the absence as observed at the pinned
   commit; the default; the airlock maintainer's ruling on the one real choice the question holds, namely
   whether the project asks upstream for both changes or keeps its own routines permanently (the default asks
   for both at priority P3 and waits on neither); the pointer to the switch-over bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three admission tests pass at the pinned commit and record the caps (10⁶ and 10⁶) and the absence of a
      splitting estimator in the admission record with the sibling's commit hash.
- [ ] A certification planned at 2,995,731 episodes is routed to the exact binomial bound and says so; one
      planned at 299,572 is routed to the sibling's sequence; at n = 10⁶ − 1 and 10⁶ the exact bound is at or
      below the sequence's bound.
- [ ] An importance run requested above 10⁶ evaluations is refused at admission with the repair; nothing is
      truncated silently.
- [ ] The 33rd-request crossing is asserted (995,793 and 1,001,856) and routed correctly.
- [ ] `registries/upstream.toml` has rows FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 with all five fields; the registry loader
      rejects a row without a failing fixture.
- [ ] The decision record exists with the maintainer's ruling or the statement that the default is in force.
- [ ] No milestone gate lists either upstream item as a prerequisite (a registry test over `registries/gates.toml`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission_stats_caps.rs`: the three admission tests of point 1.
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/sample_caps.rs`:
- `routing_by_planned_count` (299,572 → sibling sequence; 2,995,731 → exact binomial; 10⁶ → sibling; 10⁶ + 1 →
  exact).
- `routines_agree_at_boundary`: zero failures at n = 999,999 and 1,000,000: exact bounds 2.99573 × 10⁻⁶ and
  2.99573 × 10⁻⁶ (to five digits), each at or below the sequence's value.
- `importance_above_cap_refused_with_repair`.
- `qualification_level_crossing_at_request_33`.
- `result_names_routine`.
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/upstream_rows_nemesis.rs`: `fd_fsim_18_and_19_complete`;
`no_gate_waits_on_upstream`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_sample_caps.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): `frob nemesis bound --mode certify --target
1e-6 --delta 0.05 --estimate` and `--target 1e-5 …` on the synthetic rate task, asserting the planned counts
and the routine named in each plan; `frob doctor --json` asserting the admission record of the `stats` group
lists the two caps. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, planned count, routine, the
sibling commit. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_sample_caps/`; no gate is recorded.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-664 (29,956, 299,572 and 2,995,731 at δ = 0.05). KA-666 (the random walk: 1.504 × 10⁻⁴). Negative controls:
a router that sends every count to the sibling's sequence (the 2,995,731 plan then fails at the cap in the
middle of a run: the routing test catches it at admission); an importance run that clips itself to 10⁶ and
reports the clipped estimate as the requested one. Determinism: the routing and the exact bound are integer and
outward-rounded arithmetic, identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- The caps are not defects: a library is entitled to bound its work. The question is only whether this program
  asks for a parameter. Hence priority P3 and "no milestone waits".
- The switch-over rule in the upstream table names the exploration gate for the cap item; the certification
  gate's coverage row is the natural second check and is added in the switch-over bead.
- If KILL-RARE fires, splitting is removed here and FD-FSIM-19 loses its consumer; the row is then closed by a
  recorded decision, not left open.
- Other agents work in the sibling repository; the upstream change itself reserves its files there and follows
  the sibling's coordination rules. Nothing in this bead edits the sibling.
