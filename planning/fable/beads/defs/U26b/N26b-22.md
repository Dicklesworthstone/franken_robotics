## Background

Nemesis reaches the sibling numerical library FrankenSim only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, and uses
two of its statistics routines for rare-failure work. FACT (read at plan time; to be re-read by the admission
test of this bead): `frankensim/crates/fs-eproc/src/bernoulli.rs` provides a confidence sequence for a Bernoulli
rate as a Beta(½, ½) mixture, with a cap of 10⁶ samples; `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`
provides bounded-weight importance sampling on Gaussian germs with checkpoints and a cap of 10⁶ evaluations.
FrankenSim has no splitting estimator; splitting on forks is implemented in `frob-nemesis`.

The caps matter because the price of a claim is fixed by arithmetic: at δ = 0.05 a zero-failure certification
of 10⁻⁵ needs 299,572 nominal episodes (under the cap) and of 10⁻⁶ needs 2,995,731 (three times over it); the
sibling's sequence, which pays for its freedom to stop at any time, would need about 1,050,021 clean episodes
already at 10⁻⁵ (N26a-22).

**OPEN-NEMESIS-4. The sample caps of 10⁶ in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance
execution, and the absence of splitting upstream.** Owner: the airlock maintainer. **Default (in force): our own
exact binomial bound for larger counts; upstream work items FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 are open and no milestone
waits on them.** It blocks nothing.

The program's rule for sibling repositories shapes what this bead may do. Every sibling is the owner's, and this
program changes it where it needs changing, by an *upstream work item*: a change made in the sibling's own
repository under that repository's rules, recorded in `registries/upstream.toml` with five things (the change, a
priority, the interim posture here, the switch-over rule, and one reproducible failing fixture). No milestone
waits on an upstream change landing, and no local patch or fork stands in for one. A gap found in a sibling
becomes an admission test here and an upstream work item there, in that order: the admission test records what
the pinned commit does.

## Scope

In: the admission tests that record the two caps and the absence of splitting at the pinned commit; the tests
that the interim posture holds at the boundary; the `admission_test` entries of the two upstream rows, with the
check that both rows are complete; the decision record.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the two rows of `registries/upstream.toml` and the form of their failing fixtures: BD-037 (a dependency) writes
  the rows of the whole register, these two among them, and opens one fixture per item under
  `tests/upstream/<bead key>/`. This bead writes no row and no fixture; it names its admission tests in the rows.
- the changes inside FrankenSim, and keeping the fixtures `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-18/` and
  `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-19/` current: FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 (related, at M4). A gap found in a sibling
  becomes an admission test here first and an upstream work item there second; the milestones give that order,
  and neither upstream bead is blocked on this one.
- the switch-over on this side when a change lands (rewritten admission tests, differential tests, routing,
  gate re-runs, the rows' status `adopted`): N26b-24 for FD-FSIM-18 (the lifted caps) and N26bp1-01 for
  FD-FSIM-19 (the choice between two splitting estimators).
- the sequential path and the exact bound: N26a-22. Splitting on forks: BD-277.

## Technical approach

1. **Admission tests (what the pinned commit does; `crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission_stats_caps.rs`).**
   - `bernoulli_sequence_cap`: through the airlock trait, feed the sibling's Bernoulli sequence 10⁶ samples
     (accepted) and one more (the cap's error, recorded verbatim with the commit hash).
   - `importance_execution_cap`: request an importance run of 10⁶ evaluations (accepted) and of 10⁶ + 1 (the
     cap's error, recorded).
   - `no_splitting_estimator`: the airlock's capability listing of the `stats` group contains no splitting
     entry (a listing test, so the fact is re-established at every pin instead of remembered).
   Each records `{sibling commit, crate, routine, cap, observed error}` in the admission record. A pin whose
   behaviour differs (a lifted cap, a new estimator) fails the test by design; that failure is the signal that
   starts the switch-over.
2. **Interim posture, pinned by tests.**
   - The routing is the design choice of the sequential path, `choose_design` of N26a-22, made at admission
     and recorded in the result (`bound.routine: "fs-eproc" | "exact-binomial"`): the sibling's sequence when
     the clean count it needs, `plan_sequential(p⋆, δ)`, fits both the budget and the cap of 10⁶; otherwise the
     project's own exact binomial bound (integer counts, outward rounding) on the fixed-n design at
     `required(p⋆, δ)`. At δ = 0.05 a 10⁻⁴ target goes to the sequence when the budget affords its 92,872
     clean episodes and to the exact bound at 29,956 when it does not; 10⁻⁵ (the sequence would need about
     1,050,021) and 10⁻⁶ always go to the exact bound at 299,572 and 2,995,731. A sequential run is admitted
     with at most 10⁶ observations and ends `Exhausted` at the cap rather than passing it. The two routines
     must agree in direction at the boundary: for n = 10⁶ − 1, 10⁶ and zero failures, the exact bound
     1 − 0.05^(1/n) (2.9957 × 10⁻⁶ at n = 10⁶) is at or below the sequence's upper bound, since a bound valid
     at every stopping time is wider than a fixed-n bound.
   - Importance sampling runs are refused above 10⁶ evaluations at admission, with the budget refusal's shape:
     the uncoded refusal `importance-over-sibling-cap` of the Nemesis refusal registry (N26-REF; exit 4, as for
     FROB-REF-760 a count refused at admission with the reachable alternative attached; tests assert slug, exit
     code and pointer, and the number once the allocation ledger assigns one), `observed` the planned
     evaluations, `required` at most 10⁶, the bound reachable at 10⁶ evaluations attached, and the repairs
     "split the estimate into independent stages under separate commitments; use crude nominal sampling": no
     silent truncation of a run at the cap.
   - Within the qualification lane the level shrinks with each request (α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]): for a 10⁻⁵
     target the count passes 10⁶ at the 33rd request (995,793 at the 32nd; 1,001,856 at the 33rd). The exact
     bound carries it (a 10⁻⁵ target never routes to the sibling's sequence); the record states it so nobody
     meets it by surprise.
   - Claims are certified to 10⁻⁵ by default and reported as `Estimated` beyond (the default of OPEN-NEMESIS-3);
     where the owner approves an envelope for 10⁻⁶ the exact binomial routine carries it.
   - Splitting stays in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture (a biased random walk: up-probability 0.4,
     start at level 1; the probability of reaching level 20 before level 0 is 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴).
3. **The two upstream rows (`registries/upstream.toml`, schema `frob.upstream/1`).** The rows are written by
   BD-037 with the rest of the register; they are restated here because this bead's tests are named in them.

   | Item | Repository | The change | Priority | Interim posture here | Switch-over rule | `fixture` (fails at the pin) | `admission_test` (passes at the pin; this bead) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | FD-FSIM-18 | frankensim | Lift or parameterize the 10⁶ sample caps in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance execution | P3 | Our own exact binomial bound for larger counts; claims certified to 10⁻⁵ and reported as `Estimated` beyond | Re-run GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count | `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-18/`: the ignored integration test `upstream_fd_fsim_18` asks for 3·10⁶ failure-free trials and for 10⁶ + 1 importance evaluations and receives the cap refusals (kept current by FD-FSIM-18) | `bernoulli_sequence_cap` and `importance_execution_cap` at 10⁶ + 1 |
   | FD-FSIM-19 | frankensim | A splitting estimator (adaptive multilevel splitting) beside the existing importance sampler | P3 | Splitting is implemented in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture | Differential test of the two estimators on the random-walk fixture, then choose one | `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-19/`: the ignored integration test `upstream_fd_fsim_19` asks the `stats` group for a splitting estimator and finds none; the random-walk known answer is what the upstream estimator must pass (kept current by FD-FSIM-19) | `no_splitting_estimator` |

   The two kinds of test are different artifacts and are never merged: an admission test passes at the pin by
   recording what the pinned commit does; the fixture fails at the pin by asking for what is missing. What
   this bead does to the rows: it sets each row's `admission_test` field to the tests above, and each of the
   three admission tests carries the entry `upstream = "FD-FSIM-18"` or `upstream = "FD-FSIM-19"`, so that
   the registry's cross-check can find the probe from the row and the row from the probe. Row `status` values
   are `open`, `landed` (merged on the sibling's `main`) and `adopted` (switch-over run here); both rows are
   `open` when this bead closes.

   Upstream changes are additive (a new mode, entry point or parameter, never a change to existing behaviour)
   and follow the sibling's own rules; a pin then advances through the compatibility train (move the pin, write
   a new receipt, rerun the admission and parity gates, relock).
4. **Decision record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-NEMESIS-4.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`): the two caps and
   the absence as observed at the pinned commit; the default; the airlock maintainer's ruling on the one real choice
   the question holds, namely whether the project asks upstream for both changes or keeps its own routines
   permanently (the default asks for both at priority P3 and waits on neither); the pointer to the switch-over bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three admission tests pass at the pinned commit and record the caps (10⁶ and 10⁶) and the absence of a
      splitting estimator in the admission record with the sibling's commit hash.
- [ ] Routing follows `choose_design` and the result says which routine ran: 10⁻⁴ with a budget of 120,000
      episodes (a nominal share of 96,000 under the `table` policy and of 120,000 under `baseline` and in
      `shadow`, N26a-26: either affords the 92,872 clean episodes) → the sibling's sequence; 10⁻⁴ with 50,000 →
      the exact binomial bound at 29,956; 10⁻⁵ (299,572)
      and 10⁻⁶ (2,995,731) → the exact binomial bound; at n = 10⁶ − 1 and 10⁶ the exact bound is at or below
      the sequence's bound.
- [ ] An importance run requested above 10⁶ evaluations is refused at admission with the repair; nothing is
      truncated silently.
- [ ] The 33rd-request crossing is asserted (995,793 and 1,001,856) and routed correctly.
- [ ] `registries/upstream.toml` has rows FD-FSIM-18 and FD-FSIM-19 (written by BD-037) with all five things the
      rule asks for; each row's `admission_test` names this bead's tests and each of those tests carries the
      matching `upstream` entry; the registry loader rejects a row without a failing fixture.
- [ ] The decision record exists with the maintainer's ruling or the statement that the default is in force.
- [ ] No milestone gate lists either upstream item as a prerequisite (a registry test over `registries/gates.toml`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission_stats_caps.rs`: the three admission tests of point 1.
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/sample_caps.rs`:
- `routing_follows_design` ((10⁻⁴, budget 120,000) → `fs-eproc` under either policy's nominal share; (10⁻⁴,
  budget 100,000) → `fs-eproc` with the whole budget as share and `exact-binomial` under `table`'s 80,000;
  (10⁻⁴, 50,000) → `exact-binomial` at 29,956;
  (10⁻⁵, 10⁶) → `exact-binomial` at 299,572; (10⁻⁶, 3·10⁶) → `exact-binomial` at 2,995,731; a sequential run
  given a budget of 2·10⁶ is admitted with 10⁶ observations and ends `Exhausted` at the cap). Negative control:
  a router by planned count alone (299,572 ≤ 10⁶ → sibling) is caught, because the sequence ends `Exhausted`
  on a clean run of 299,572 at 10⁻⁵.
- `routines_agree_at_boundary`: zero failures at n = 999,999 and 1,000,000: exact bounds 2.99573 × 10⁻⁶ and
  2.99573 × 10⁻⁶ (to five digits), each at or below the sequence's value.
- `importance_above_cap_refused_with_repair`: 10⁶ + 1 planned evaluations → exit 4, slug
  `importance-over-sibling-cap`, `observed` 1,000,001, `required` at most 1,000,000, the bound reachable at 10⁶
  attached, both repairs, zero episodes in the ledger; 10⁶ → admitted.
- `qualification_level_crossing_at_request_33`.
- `result_names_routine`.
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/upstream_rows_nemesis.rs`: `fd_fsim_18_and_19_complete` (both rows have the
change, the priority, the interim posture, the switch-over rule and a `fixture` path under `tests/upstream/`,
and their `admission_test` fields name the three tests of point 1); `admission_tests_carry_upstream_entry`;
`no_gate_waits_on_upstream`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_sample_caps.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): `frob nemesis bound <candidate> --task
<synthetic rate task> --tier batch-cpu --mode certify --target 1e-6 --delta 0.05 --finalist --estimate --key sc-1
--json` (every `nemesis bound` call of the script carries `--finalist`: the synthetic candidate is no finalist of
a recorded campaign, and the finalists-only rule of N26a-26 refuses such a request without it), the same with
`--target 1e-5` (`--key sc-2`) and with `--target 1e-4` at budgets of 120,000 and 50,000 episodes (`--key sc-3`,
`sc-4`), asserting the planned counts and the routine named in each plan (exact, exact, sequence, exact; 120,000
leaves the sequence's 92,872 clean episodes inside the nominal share of either policy, where 100,000 would not
under `table`); `frob nemesis bound … --mode estimate --proposal <p> --budget episodes=1000001 --key sc-5` on
the same task (each bounded-weight episode is one evaluation of the sibling's importance execution; expect
exit 4, slug `importance-over-sibling-cap`, zero episodes) and its twin at `episodes=1000000` with `--estimate`
(`--key sc-6`; admitted); `frob doctor --json` (a read verb) asserting the admission record of the `stats`
group lists the two caps. Every NDJSON line, through the shared helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N26b-22`),
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block; per step the
planned count, the routine, the sibling commit and the stack-receipt hash, and for a refusal its block. On
failure: the first step whose count, routine or exit differs from the expected, with both values. Final
`summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: []`. Logs under `target/e2e-logs/nemesis_sample_caps/`; no
gate is recorded.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-664 (29,956, 299,572 and 2,995,731 at δ = 0.05). KA-666 (the random walk: 1.504 × 10⁻⁴). Negative controls:
a router that sends every count to the sibling's sequence (the 2,995,731 plan then fails at the cap in the
middle of a run: the routing test catches it at admission); a router by planned count alone (a clean run of
299,572 at 10⁻⁵ then ends `Exhausted`); an importance run that clips itself to 10⁶ and
reports the clipped estimate as the requested one. Determinism: the routing and the exact bound are integer and
outward-rounded arithmetic, identical across platforms.

## Considerations

- The caps are not defects: a library is entitled to bound its work. The question is only whether this program
  asks for a parameter. Hence upstream priority P3 for both items and "no milestone waits" on them. This bead
  itself is priority 2: the M3 exit review (BD-319, priority 2) waits on its admission tests and decision
  record, and a bead a review waits on is not ranked below the review.
- The switch-over rule in the upstream table names the exploration gate for the cap item; the certification
  gate's coverage row is the natural second check and is added in the switch-over bead.
- If KILL-RARE fires, splitting is removed here and FD-FSIM-19 loses its consumer; the row is then closed by a
  recorded decision, not left open.
- Other agents work in the sibling repository; the upstream change itself reserves its files there and follows
  the sibling's coordination rules. Nothing in this bead edits the sibling.
