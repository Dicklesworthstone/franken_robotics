## Background

Two upstream work items in the sibling library FrankenSim concern rare-failure estimation. Each has, by the
program's rule for upstream work, an interim posture (what this project does until the change lands) and a
switch-over rule (what must be re-run when it does):

| Item | The change | Interim posture | Switch-over rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| FD-FSIM-18 | Lift or parameterize the 10⁶ sample caps in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance execution | Our own exact binomial bound for larger counts; claims certified to 10⁻⁵ and reported as `Estimated` beyond | Re-run GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count |
| FD-FSIM-19 | A splitting estimator (adaptive multilevel splitting) beside the existing importance sampler | Splitting is implemented in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture | Differential test of the two estimators on the random-walk fixture, then choose one |

Both are priority P3, and no milestone waits on either: the interim posture is complete and tested (N26b-22).
This bead is what happens on the day one of them lands and the pin moves. It exists so that the switch-over is a
planned, tested step and not an untracked edit made when somebody notices the pin changed. It is backlog work:
it cannot start before its upstream item exists, and nothing else depends on it.

Tier Solid for the cap switch-over; the splitting comparison concerns a Frontier mechanism and inherits its kill
gate (if KILL-RARE has fired, the second half of this bead is void and is closed by a recorded decision).

## Scope

In: the two switch-over procedures, each independently executable; the differential tests; the re-runs the
switch-over rules name; the decision records; updates to the admission tests and the upstream registry rows.
Out: the upstream changes themselves (FD-FSIM-18, FD-FSIM-19); the interim routines (N26a-22 for the exact
bound; BD-277 for splitting), which stay in the tree as the references the differential tests compare against.

## Technical approach

1. **Trigger.** The admission tests of the `stats` group fail at a new pin by design: `bernoulli_sequence_cap`
   or `importance_execution_cap` no longer sees the cap error at 10⁶ + 1, or `no_splitting_estimator` finds an
   entry. The compatibility train stops there and this bead's matching procedure runs before the pin is relocked.
2. **Procedure A: FD-FSIM-18 has landed (caps lifted or parameterized).**
   1. Rewrite the two admission tests to record the new behaviour (the parameter's name, default and maximum).
   2. Differential test of the sibling's sequence against the project's exact binomial bound, with zero
      failures and with planted failure counts, at n = 999,999, 1,000,000, 1,000,001, 2,995,731 and 7,696,209
      (the tenth qualification request at 10⁻⁶): the sibling's upper bound must be at or above the exact
      fixed-n bound at every n (a bound valid at every stopping time cannot be tighter than the exact fixed-n
      bound), and within a recorded factor of it; coverage over 10⁴ synthetic studies with known p at
      n = 2,995,731 must be at least the nominal level.
   3. Routing: the planned-count router may now send counts above 10⁶ to the sibling's sequence for the
      sequential mode; the fixed-n zero-failure rule stays on the exact routine (it is exact and cheaper).
      The result still names the routine used.
   4. Re-runs named by the switch-over rule: the exploration gate at the larger count (GATE-NEMESIS-05's script
      with importance runs allowed above 10⁶ evaluations), and, as the natural second check, the certification
      gate's coverage row (GATE-NEMESIS-04's script) with the sibling's path at counts above 10⁶. Both gates'
      records are appended with the new pin; a regression blocks the relock.
   5. Update the upstream registry row to `landed` with the sibling commit; keep the exact binomial routine as
      the reference of the differential test.
3. **Procedure B: FD-FSIM-19 has landed (an upstream splitting estimator exists).**
   1. Admission test of the new entry through the airlock: the estimator runs on the airlock's fork interface
      with child streams keyed by child world addresses; the duplicate-children control (forks that share the
      parent's streams) must be detected or refused by it as it is by ours.
   2. Differential test on the random-walk fixture (up-probability 0.4, start at level 1, absorbing at 0 and 20;
      truth 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴), 10⁴ repetitions at equal fragment budget for both estimators:
      mean of each within its own standard error band of the truth (unbiasedness); the truth inside each
      estimator's interval in at least 85% of repetitions (the coverage clause of KILL-RARE); relative
      mean-square errors recorded side by side.
   3. The same comparison on the product fixture of the exploration gate (planted failures of probability
      10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one with two separate modes), 30 repetitions: episodes to first failure and the share of
      repetitions finding both modes.
   4. *Then choose one* (decision rule fixed here, before data): adopt the upstream estimator if it passes
      steps 1 and 2, and is not worse than ours on step 3 (paired lower bound of the ratio of episodes to first
      failure at least 1, and both modes found at least as often); otherwise keep ours. Either way the losing
      implementation leaves the default path, the decision is an architecture decision record, and rates stay
      labelled `Estimated`: adopting an upstream estimator changes no evidence class and feeds no bound.
   5. If adopted: `frob-nemesis`'s splitting module becomes a thin caller of the airlock trait; the fixed-level
      distress score, the informativeness check and the refusal when no randomness enters after a fork
      (FROB-REF-569) stay here; our estimator is kept under a test-only feature as the differential reference.
4. **Records.** `docs/adr/ADR-NEMESIS-SWITCH-18.md` and `-19.md` with the differential results and the choice;
   updated admission records; updated upstream registry rows; appended gate records with the new pin.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Procedure A, when run: the admission tests record the new cap behaviour; the differential test passes at
      the five counts; routing and its tests are updated; GATE-NEMESIS-05 and the coverage row of
      GATE-NEMESIS-04 are re-run at the new pin and recorded; the upstream row is `landed`.
- [ ] Procedure B, when run: the upstream estimator passes the airlock admission test including the
      duplicate-children control; the random-walk differential test records both estimators' means, coverage
      and mean-square errors; the choice is made by the stated rule and recorded; the default path holds
      exactly one estimator.
- [ ] Neither procedure changes an evidence class: bounds stay `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ; splitting rates
      stay `Estimated`.
- [ ] If KILL-RARE has fired before FD-FSIM-19 lands, Procedure B is closed by a recorded decision ("no
      consumer") and the upstream row is updated to say so.
- [ ] Until its upstream item lands, each procedure's tests exist as ignored tests with the reason "awaiting
      FD-FSIM-18" or "awaiting FD-FSIM-19", so that the work is visible in the test listing.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/switch_fd_fsim_18.rs` (ignored until the pin moves):
`differential_exact_vs_sibling_at_five_counts`; `sibling_bound_not_tighter_than_exact`; `coverage_at_2995731`
(10⁴ synthetic studies).
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/switch_fd_fsim_19.rs` (ignored until the pin moves): `upstream_splitting_admission`;
`duplicate_children_control`; `random_walk_differential` (10⁴ repetitions; truth 1.504 × 10⁻⁴).
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/switch_choice.rs` (runs today, on synthetic result tables): `choice_rule_evaluator`:
(passes admission, unbiased, coverage 0.91, ratio lower bound 1.2, modes 28/30 against 27/30) → adopt;
(coverage 0.80) → keep ours; (ratio lower bound 0.8) → keep ours; (fails duplicate-children control) → keep
ours.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_switchover.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): detects which upstream item is present at the
pin (from the admission record), runs the matching procedure's `frob` steps (`frob nemesis bound --mode certify
--target 1e-6 --estimate` to show the routine chosen; `frob nemesis search --method splitting` on the random
walk fixture with each estimator), and re-invokes the two gate scripts. Logged: the sibling commit, the routine
per count, per estimator the mean, interval, coverage and mean-square error, the literal evaluation of the
choice rule, and the gate scripts' summaries. With neither item present the script exits 0 after logging "no
upstream item landed; interim posture in force" and asserting the interim tests of N26b-22 pass.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (random walk, 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; control: a walk with no noise after the fork returns identical children and
is refused). KA-664 (2,995,731 at 10⁻⁶; 7,696,209 at the tenth request's level). KA-141 (the crude and
bounded-weight bounds bracket the truth in at least 1 − δ of 10⁴ repetitions on the known-probability fixture).
Negative controls: an "upstream" estimator stub that reuses the parent's streams must fail the admission test;
a sibling sequence patched to be tighter than the exact bound must fail `sibling_bound_not_tighter_than_exact`.

## Considerations

- Appendix-level priority P3 and "no milestone waits" translate to backlog priority here; the bead becomes
  ready only when its upstream dependency closes.
- The two procedures are independent; one upstream item landing does not wait for the other. If the lead
  prefers, the bead can be split in two at that time without loss.
- A pin move is never a silent behaviour change: the admission tests are written to fail when the sibling's
  behaviour changes, and this bead is the planned response.
