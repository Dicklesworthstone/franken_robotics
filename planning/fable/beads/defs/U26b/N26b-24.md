## Background

Two upstream work items in the sibling library FrankenSim concern rare-failure estimation. Each has, by the
program's rule for upstream work, an interim posture (what this project does until the change lands) and a
switch-over rule (what must be re-run when it does):

| Item | The change | Interim posture | Switch-over rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| FD-FSIM-18 | Lift or parameterize the 10⁶ sample caps in `fs-eproc`'s Bernoulli sequence and in `fs-uq`'s importance execution | Our own exact binomial bound for larger counts; claims certified to 10⁻⁵ and reported as `Estimated` beyond | Re-run GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count |
| FD-FSIM-19 | A splitting estimator (adaptive multilevel splitting) beside the existing importance sampler | Splitting is implemented in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture | Differential test of the two estimators on the random-walk fixture, then choose one |

Both are priority P3, and no milestone waits on either: the interim posture is complete and tested (N26b-22).
This bead is what happens on the day FD-FSIM-18 lands and the pin moves; the switch-over for FD-FSIM-19 is
N26bp1-01. The two procedures are independent: each bead depends only on its own upstream item, so one item
landing never waits for the other (a single bead for both would show ready only when both items had closed,
and FD-FSIM-19 can stay open indefinitely or close as "no consumer" if KILL-RARE fires). It exists so that the
switch-over is a planned, tested step and not an untracked edit made when somebody notices the pin changed. It
is backlog work: it cannot start before its upstream item has landed, and nothing else depends on it.

Tier Solid: the cap switch-over concerns the certification lane, which no kill gate removes.

## Scope

This bead is the switch-over on this side for FD-FSIM-18, and the only place where it is specified. The
upstream bead ends when its change is on the sibling's `main` (registry status `landed`); this bead takes the
item from `landed` to `adopted`.

In: the switch-over procedure (Procedure A); the rewritten admission tests of the two caps; the cap passed
through the airlock; the differential test of the sibling's sequence against the exact binomial bound; the
routing above 10⁶; the re-runs the switch-over rule names (GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count, and the coverage
row of GATE-NEMESIS-04); the decision record; the registry row's status `adopted` with the receipt of the pin
move.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the upstream change itself, inside FrankenSim, with its tests there, the failing fixture
  `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-18/`, and the script `scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_18.sh`: FD-FSIM-18, a dependency.
  This bead runs phase `post` of that script; it does not write it.
- the switch-over for FD-FSIM-19 (the upstream splitting estimator, its differential test and the choice rule):
  N26bp1-01.
- the admission tests as they stand before the change, the tests of the interim posture and the decision
  record of OPEN-NEMESIS-4: N26b-22, a dependency.
- the interim exact routine (N26a-22, a dependency), which stays in the tree as the reference the differential
  test compares against.
- the train itself (pin move, receipt, relock, train log): the compatibility train's pin-advance procedure
  (N33b-04, a dependency). The scripts that measure the two gates: N26a-30 and N26a-31.

## Technical approach

1. **Trigger.** The admission tests of the `stats` group fail at a new pin by design: `bernoulli_sequence_cap`
   or `importance_execution_cap` no longer sees the cap error at 10⁶ + 1. The same pin makes the item's fixture
   `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-18/` flip from failing to passing, which is how the train sets the row to `landed`.
   The compatibility train stops there and this procedure runs before the pin is relocked. The train's own
   steps are its frame: (1) the sibling's pin in `franken-stack.lock` moves to a clean commit that contains the
   change (a pin only moves forward; a sibling's newer clean head is never rolled back locally); (2) `cargo
   xtask check-stack` writes a new resolution receipt (`frob.stack-receipt/1`) under `receipts/`; (3) the
   sibling's admission gates, the parity gates and the gates named below are re-run; (4) relock (`Cargo.lock`
   regenerated and committed, builds `--locked` on the one toolchain T) and append the record, with the receipt
   hash, to `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`. Only then does the row's `status` become `adopted` and the interim
   posture end. No PASS is recorded on a dirty sibling tree, and neither a pin nor T moves while a ten-fold
   tournament is running. If a gate named here does not exist yet when the change lands, the pin still moves
   with the gates that do exist, the row stays `landed`, and the gate's first run is made against the new pin.
   If FD-FSIM-19 lands in the same pin, both switch-overs run inside one train stop and the relock waits for
   both; each bead records its own procedure.
2. **Procedure A: FD-FSIM-18 has landed (caps lifted or parameterized).**
   1. Rewrite the two admission tests to record the new behaviour (the parameter's name, default and maximum;
      the upstream contract states a tested maximum, TARGET 10⁸).
   2. The airlock passes the configured cap through to both routines. A count above the configured cap is
      still a refusal by name, never a silent truncation (configured cap 2·10⁶, 2·10⁶ + 1 trials: the named
      refusal; for an importance run this is the uncoded refusal `importance-over-sibling-cap` of N26-REF, exit
      4, now read against the configured cap instead of 10⁶).
   3. Differential test of the sibling's sequence against the project's exact binomial bound, with zero
      failures and with planted failure counts, at n = 999,999, 1,000,000, 1,000,001, 2,995,731, 3,000,000
      and 7,696,209 (the tenth qualification request at 10⁻⁶): the sibling's upper bound must be at or above
      the exact fixed-n bound at every n (a bound valid at every stopping time cannot be tighter than the
      exact fixed-n bound), and within a recorded factor of it; at n = 3·10⁶ with zero failures the exact
      bound is 1 − 0.05^(1/n) = 9.986·10⁻⁷ and the sibling's bound must be within a factor of 3 of it (TARGET
      for the sequence's width); coverage over 10⁴ synthetic studies with known p at n = 2,995,731 must be at
      least the nominal level.
   4. Routing: the design choice `choose_design` of N26a-22 reads the configured cap in place of 10⁶, so the
      sequential design (the sibling's sequence) is chosen whenever its clean count `plan_sequential(p⋆, δ)`
      fits both the budget's nominal share (N26a-26) and the configured cap: at δ = 0.05, 10⁻⁵ (about 1,050,021
      clean episodes) becomes sequential with a nominal share and a cap above that, while 10⁻⁶ (about 11,705,893) stays fixed-n below a cap of
      that size. The fixed-n design stays on the exact routine (it is exact and needs between a quarter and a third
      of the episodes: 299,572 against 1,050,021 at 10⁻⁵, 2,995,731 against 11,705,893 at 10⁻⁶). The result still names the routine used (`bound.routine: "fs-eproc" | "exact-binomial"`).
   5. Re-runs named by the switch-over rule: the exploration gate at the larger count (GATE-NEMESIS-05's script
      with importance runs allowed above 10⁶ evaluations: on planted failures of probability 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one
      with two separate modes, at equal episode budget and 30 repetitions, exploration needs at least 10 times
      fewer episodes than crude sampling at 10⁻⁵ and finds both modes in at least 80% of repetitions), and, as
      the natural second check, the certification gate's coverage row (GATE-NEMESIS-04's script) with the
      sibling's path at counts above 10⁶: the certification that follows the exploration is re-run with a
      budget above 10⁶ episodes on the planted 10⁻⁵ failure and on a failure-free fixture sized for 10⁻⁶.
      Both gates' records are appended with the new pin; a regression blocks the relock.
   6. Labels after the switch-over: a bound stays class `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ (never `Certified`,
      which is reserved for certificates a checker accepted). A 10⁻⁶ claim carries such a bound only if the
      owner approved the envelope (OPEN-NEMESIS-3) and the bound was obtained; otherwise it is reported as
      `Estimated`. Lifting the cap makes the claim expressible, not affordable.
   7. Run `scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_18.sh --phase post` (written by FD-FSIM-18): the item's fixture now
      passes, and `frob nemesis bound --json` on the failure-free fixture with a budget of 3,750,000 episodes on
      `batch-cpu` returns the bound with its label (the budget whose nominal share affords 2,995,731 episodes
      under either policy: 80% of it under `table`, all of it under `baseline`, N26a-26).
   8. Update the upstream registry row to `adopted` (it was set to `landed`, with the sibling commit, when the
      change reached the sibling's `main`), with the receipt of the pin move; keep the exact binomial routine
      as the reference of the differential test.
3. **Records.** `docs/adr/ADR-NEMESIS-SWITCH-18.md` with the differential results; updated admission records;
   the upstream registry row at `adopted` with the receipt hash; the record appended to
   `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`; appended gate records with the new pin.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The admission tests record the new cap behaviour; the airlock passes the configured cap through, and a
      count above it is refused by name, never truncated.
- [ ] The differential test passes at the six counts, with the sibling's bound at n = 3·10⁶ and zero failures no
      smaller than the exact 9.986·10⁻⁷ and within a factor of 3 of it (TARGET); coverage at n = 2,995,731 over
      10⁴ synthetic studies is at least the nominal level.
- [ ] Routing and its tests are updated; the result names the routine.
- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-05 at the larger count and the coverage row of GATE-NEMESIS-04 are re-run at the new pin and
      recorded; a regression blocks the relock.
- [ ] Phase `post` of `upstream_fd_fsim_18.sh` passes; the upstream row is `adopted`.
- [ ] `cargo xtask check-stack` reports the sibling on-pin and clean under the new receipt, and the record in
      `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` cites that receipt; nothing in this repository patches, forks or vendors the
      sibling.
- [ ] No evidence class changes: bounds stay `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ, and a 10⁻⁶ claim carries one only if
      the envelope was approved and the bound obtained (`Estimated` otherwise).
- [ ] Until this bead is taken up, the pending work is visible through FD-FSIM-18's ignored fixture
      `upstream_fd_fsim_18` and the row's status in `registries/upstream.toml`; this bead becomes ready only
      when the change has landed, so its own tests are written un-ignored.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/switch_fd_fsim_18.rs`:
`differential_exact_vs_sibling_at_six_counts`; `sibling_bound_not_tighter_than_exact` (the sequence's bound is
never below the fixed-n exact bound at the same n); `sibling_bound_within_factor_3_at_3e6`;
`coverage_at_2995731` (10⁴ synthetic studies); `over_cap_refuses_by_name` (configured cap 2·10⁶, 2·10⁶ + 1
trials through the airlock: the named refusal; 2·10⁶ + 1 importance evaluations: `importance-over-sibling-cap`,
exit 4).
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/sample_caps.rs` (N26b-22's file; this bead adds): `zero_failure_bound_closed_form`:
δ = 0.05; the exact bound 1 − 0.05^(1/n) is 9.999991·10⁻⁶ at n = 299,572 (at or below 10⁻⁵) and
1.0000024·10⁻⁵ at n = 299,571 (above it); 9.986·10⁻⁷ at n = 3·10⁶; `routing_above_cap_after_switch` (configured
cap 2·10⁶; budgets chosen so that the nominal share of either policy, 80% under `table` and 100% under
`baseline`, gives the same route: (10⁻⁵, budget 1,350,000) → sequential, `fs-eproc`; (10⁻⁵, budget 500,000) →
fixed-n at 299,572, `exact-binomial`; (10⁻⁶, budget 3,750,000) → fixed-n at 2,995,731, `exact-binomial`, the
sequence's clean count exceeding the cap).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_switchover.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): checks from the admission record that FD-FSIM-18 is
present at the pin; runs `frob nemesis bound <candidate> --task <synthetic rate task> --tier batch-cpu --mode
certify --target 1e-6 --finalist --estimate --key sw-1 --json` and the same at `--target 1e-5` with a budget of
1,350,000 episodes (`--key sw-2`) to show the routine chosen, and `frob nemesis bound … --mode certify --target
1e-6 --finalist` on the failure-free fixture with a budget of 3,750,000 episodes (`--key sw-3`; each call its own
key). Every bound call carries `--finalist`, since the synthetic candidate is no finalist of a recorded campaign
and the finalists-only rule (N26a-26) refuses such a request without it; the budgets leave the sequence's
1,050,021 clean episodes at 10⁻⁵ and the 2,995,731 at 10⁻⁶ inside the nominal share of either policy (1,200,000
and 3·10⁶ would not be under `table`, which takes 80%); re-invokes the two gate
scripts (N26a-30, N26a-31), whose receipts at the new pin a recording session enters (N38b-19); and calls phase
`post` of `scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_18.sh` (written by FD-FSIM-18). Every NDJSON line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N26b-24`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block; logged
per step: the sibling commit, the stack-receipt hash, the routine per count, the exact and sibling bounds at each of
the six counts with their ratio and the factor-3 threshold, the coverage share with its nominal level, and the gate
scripts' summaries. On failure: the first count whose sibling bound is below the exact bound or above three times it
(both values), or the first gate row that regressed, with the receipt hash needed to reproduce. With FD-FSIM-18 not
present the script exits 0 after logging "FD-FSIM-18 not landed; interim posture in force" and asserting the interim
tests of N26b-22 pass. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: ["GATE-NEMESIS-04",
"GATE-NEMESIS-05"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-664 (2,995,731 at 10⁻⁶; 7,696,209 at the tenth request's level). KA-141 (the crude and bounded-weight bounds
bracket the truth in at least 1 − δ of 10⁴ repetitions on the known-probability fixture). Negative controls: a
sibling sequence patched to be tighter than the exact bound must fail `sibling_bound_not_tighter_than_exact`;
an airlock that clips a count above the configured cap to the cap must fail `over_cap_refuses_by_name`; a
router that sends a fixed-n plan to the sequence must fail `routing_above_cap_after_switch`.

## Considerations

- Appendix-level priority P3 and "no milestone waits" translate to backlog priority here; the bead becomes
  ready only when its upstream dependency closes.
- Division of work with the upstream bead: FD-FSIM-18 is done in the sibling's repository under its rules and
  ends at `landed`; everything that happens in this repository afterwards is here. A change to a switch-over
  step is made in this bead only.
- A pin move is never a silent behaviour change: the admission tests are written to fail when the sibling's
  behaviour changes, and this bead is the planned response.
- Lifting the cap does not lift the compute envelope: OPEN-NEMESIS-3 (N26b-21) still decides whether a 10⁻⁶
  certification may run.
