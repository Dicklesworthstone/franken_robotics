## Background

Nemesis explores for rare failures by **splitting**: a world that has reached a level of a distress score is
snapshotted and continued many times under fresh per-step disturbances, so that compute concentrates on worlds
already in trouble; the estimate of the failure rate it yields is labelled `Estimated` and never feeds a bound.
The sibling numerical library FrankenSim has no splitting estimator, so splitting on forks is implemented in
`frob-nemesis` (BD-277), with its unbiasedness fixture: a biased random walk with up-probability 0.4, started at
level 1 and absorbed at 0 and 20, whose probability of reaching 20 first is 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴.

The program changes a sibling only by an upstream work item, recorded in `registries/upstream.toml` with the
change, a priority, the interim posture here, the switch-over rule and a reproducible failing fixture:

| Item | The change | Interim posture | Switch-over rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| FD-FSIM-19 | A splitting estimator (adaptive multilevel splitting) beside the existing importance sampler | Splitting is implemented in `frob-nemesis` with its unbiasedness fixture | Differential test of the two estimators on the random-walk fixture, then choose one |

The item is priority P3 and no milestone waits on it: the interim posture is complete and tested (N26b-22). This
bead is what happens on the day it lands and the pin moves, so that the switch-over is a planned, tested step
and not an untracked edit. It was split from N26b-24 in polishing round 1: the two upstream items are
independent, and one bead depending on both would have held each procedure until both items closed. N26b-24
keeps the switch-over for FD-FSIM-18 (the lifted sample caps). This bead is backlog work: it cannot start before
FD-FSIM-19 has landed (or closed by a recorded decision), and nothing else depends on it.

Tier Frontier: it concerns splitting, a Frontier mechanism, and inherits its kill gate. KILL-RARE (decided at
M3 by N26a-32, a dependency) removes splitting and switches `nemesis.rare.exploration` to `off`; if it has
fired, this bead's procedure is void and the bead closes by a recorded decision ("no consumer"), with the
upstream row updated to say so.

upstream bead ends when its change is on the sibling's `main` (registry status `landed`); this bead takes the
item from `landed` to `adopted`, or closes it as "no consumer" after KILL-RARE.

## Technical approach

1. **Trigger.** The admission test `no_splitting_estimator` of the `stats` group fails at a new pin by design:
   the capability listing now has a splitting entry. The same pin makes the item's fixture
   `tests/upstream/FD-FSIM-19/` flip from failing to passing, which is how the train sets the row to `landed`.
   The compatibility train stops there and this procedure runs before the pin is relocked. The train's own
   steps are its frame: (1) the sibling's pin in `franken-stack.lock` moves to a clean commit that contains the
   change (a pin only moves forward); (2) `cargo xtask check-stack` writes a new resolution receipt
   (`frob.stack-receipt/1`) under `receipts/`; (3) the sibling's admission gates, the parity gates and
   GATE-NEMESIS-05 are re-run; (4) relock (`Cargo.lock` regenerated and committed, builds `--locked` on the one
   toolchain T) and append the record, with the receipt hash, to `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`. Only then does the
   row's `status` become `adopted`. No PASS is recorded on a dirty sibling tree, and neither a pin nor T moves
   while a ten-fold tournament is running. If FD-FSIM-18 lands in the same pin, both switch-overs run inside one
   train stop and the relock waits for both; each bead records its own procedure. First step of all: read
   `nemesis.rare.exploration`; if it is `off` (KILL-RARE fired), skip to step 2.8's closing decision.
2. **Procedure B: FD-FSIM-19 has landed (an upstream splitting estimator exists).**
   1. Admission test of the new entry through the airlock: the estimator runs on the airlock's fork interface
      with child streams keyed by child world addresses; the duplicate-children control (forks that share the
      parent's streams) must be detected or refused by it as it is by ours (FROB-REF-569 on our side).
   2. Differential test on the random-walk fixture (up-probability 0.4, start at level 1, absorbing at 0 and 20;
      truth 0.5/(1.5²⁰ − 1) = 1.504 × 10⁻⁴), 10⁴ repetitions at equal fragment budget for both estimators:
      mean of each within 3 of its own standard errors of the truth (unbiasedness); the truth inside each
      estimator's replica-based interval in at least 85% of repetitions (the coverage clause of KILL-RARE);
      the two means agreeing within the combined standard error (agreement statistic
      |m₁ − m₂| / √(se₁² + se₂²) below 3, where mᵢ is estimator i's mean over the repetitions and seᵢ its standard
      error); the variance per simulated step of each (the variance of the estimate times the simulated steps
      one estimate consumed, so that estimators with different costs are compared fairly) and the relative
      mean-square errors recorded side by side.
   3. The same comparison on the product fixture of the exploration gate (planted failures of probability
      10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one with two separate modes), 30 repetitions: episodes to first failure and the share of
      repetitions finding both modes.
   4. *Then choose one* (DESIGN; the rule is fixed here, before data, and stated in FD-FSIM-19 as well): adopt
      the upstream estimator if it passes steps 1 and 2, its variance per simulated step on the random walk is
      no larger than ours, and it is not worse than ours on step 3 (paired lower bound of the ratio of
      episodes to first failure, ours over theirs, at least 1, and both modes found at least as often);
      otherwise keep ours. Reason: the register says only "then choose one"; the estimator on the default path
      has already passed the kill gate on this project's forks, so replacing it must be justified both where
      the truth is known in closed form and on the measure the exploration lane is judged by. Either way the
      losing implementation leaves the default path and stays as the differential check, the decision is an
      architecture decision record, and rates stay labelled `Estimated`: adopting an upstream estimator
      changes no evidence class and feeds no bound.
   5. *Selecting the estimator (DESIGN; the configuration value FD-FSIM-19's phase `post` asks for, since the
      search verb has no option for it and gains none).* This bead adds the configuration value
      `nemesis.splitting.estimator = ours | upstream` beside BD-277's switch `nemesis.rare.exploration`. Its
      default is written by the decision record of step 4: `upstream` if adopted, `ours` if kept. Both
      estimators are in every build, so one binary serves both values: the switch-over script and phase `post`
      select an estimator per call with a scratch configuration, never with a separate build. The splitting
      module reads the value at admission of each run; the fixed-level distress score, the informativeness check
      and the refusal when no randomness enters after a fork (FROB-REF-569) stay in `frob-nemesis` and run before
      either estimator is called, and under `upstream` the module is a thin caller of the airlock trait. The
      value is part of the run's recorded configuration, and the result's method block names the estimator that
      ran (`estimator: "frob-nemesis" | "fs-uq"`, with the sibling commit for `fs-uq`), so two calls differing
      only in the value are distinguishable in their envelopes. `upstream` at a pin whose `stats` listing has no
      splitting entry answers FROB-REF-724 naming FD-FSIM-19, before any episode. With
      `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` the value is not read. The estimator that loses the choice stays
      reachable only by setting the value away from its default, as the differential reference.
   6. Re-run GATE-NEMESIS-05 with the chosen estimator (planted failures of probability 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁵, one with
      two separate modes, equal budget, 30 repetitions: at least 10 times fewer episodes than crude sampling
      at 10⁻⁵; both modes found in at least 80% of repetitions) and append its record with the new pin; a
      regression blocks the relock.
   7. Run `scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_19.sh --phase post` (written by FD-FSIM-19): the item's fixture now
      passes, and `frob nemesis search … --method splitting --first --json` on the planted-trap fixture is run
      once per estimator (`nemesis.splitting.estimator` set to `ours` and to `upstream` in a scratch
      configuration, each call under its own key), logging episodes to first discovery and the result's
      `estimator` for each. Then set the registry row to `adopted` with the receipt of the pin
      move.
   8. Fallback if the comparison cannot be completed, or the item never lands: our estimator alone, checked on
      the closed-form fixture; if it fails its own falsifier (the planted trap of probability at most 3·10⁻⁵
      is not found at least three times sooner than by uniform sampling, or fewer than 1% of forks reach the
      next level), crude sampling alone. If KILL-RARE has fired: no procedure runs; the decision "no consumer"
      is recorded in the ADR and in the upstream row, and the bead closes.
3. **Records.** `docs/adr/ADR-NEMESIS-SWITCH-19.md` with the differential results and the choice; the updated
   admission record; the upstream registry row at `adopted` (or closed as "no consumer") with the receipt hash;
   the record appended to `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`; the appended GATE-NEMESIS-05 record with the new pin.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The upstream estimator passes the airlock admission test including the duplicate-children control.
- [ ] The random-walk differential test records both estimators' means, coverage, variances per simulated step,
      mean-square errors and the agreement statistic; the product-fixture comparison records episodes to first
      failure and the both-modes share per estimator.
- [ ] The choice is made by the stated rule, evaluated literally, and recorded as an architecture decision
      record; the default of `nemesis.splitting.estimator` names the chosen estimator, and the other remains
      selectable only through that value, as the differential check.
- [ ] One build serves both values: a splitting run under each value calls the named estimator, its result
      names it in `estimator`, FROB-REF-569 is raised in `frob-nemesis` under either, and `upstream` without a
      splitting entry at the pin answers FROB-REF-724 naming FD-FSIM-19 with zero episodes.
- [ ] GATE-NEMESIS-05 passes with the chosen estimator at the new pin and is recorded.
- [ ] Phase `post` of `upstream_fd_fsim_19.sh` passes; the upstream row is `adopted`.
- [ ] `cargo xtask check-stack` reports the sibling on-pin and clean under the new receipt, and the record in
      `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` cites that receipt; nothing in this repository patches, forks or vendors the
      sibling.
- [ ] Splitting rates stay `Estimated` and are never offered as a bound, whichever estimator is chosen.
- [ ] If KILL-RARE has fired before FD-FSIM-19 lands, the procedure is closed by a recorded decision ("no
      consumer") and the upstream row is updated to say so.
- [ ] Until this bead is taken up, the pending work is visible through FD-FSIM-19's ignored fixture
      `upstream_fd_fsim_19` and the row's status in `registries/upstream.toml`; its own tests are written
      un-ignored once the item has landed, except `choice_rule_evaluator`, which needs no pin.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/switch_fd_fsim_19.rs`: `upstream_splitting_admission`; `duplicate_children_control`
(children that share the parent's streams are reported as duplicates); `random_walk_differential` (10⁴
repetitions; truth 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; each mean within 3 standard errors of it; coverage of each interval at least
85%; the agreement statistic of the two means below 3; both variances per simulated step recorded).
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/switch_choice.rs` (on synthetic result tables, independent of any pin):
`choice_rule_evaluator`: (passes admission, unbiased, coverage 0.91, agreement statistic 1.1, variance-per-step
ratio upstream to ours 0.9, ratio lower bound 1.2, modes 28/30 against 27/30) → adopt; (coverage 0.80) → keep
ours; (ratio lower bound 0.8) → keep ours; (variance-per-step ratio 1.3) → keep ours; (agreement statistic 3.4)
→ keep ours; (fails duplicate-children control) → keep ours; (modes 26/30 against 27/30, all else passing) →
keep ours. `killed_rare_closes_procedure`: with `nemesis.rare.exploration = off` the procedure writes the "no
consumer" decision and runs no differential test.
`crates/frob-nemesis/tests/splitting_routing.rs`: `adopted_routes_to_airlock` (with
`nemesis.splitting.estimator = upstream`, a splitting run calls the airlock trait and the result names
`fs-uq`); `kept_routes_to_ours` (with `ours`, our estimator runs and the result names `frob-nemesis`);
`default_follows_decision_record` (with the value unset, the estimator is the one the record names);
`refusal_569_stays_here` (a fork with no fresh randomness is refused by `frob-nemesis` before the airlock is
called, under either value); `upstream_without_entry_refused` (a scratch airlock listing with no splitting
entry: FROB-REF-724 naming FD-FSIM-19, zero episodes); `estimator_value_ignored_when_exploration_off`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/nemesis_switchover_splitting.sh` (shared end-to-end helper): checks from the admission record that
FD-FSIM-19 is present at the pin; runs `frob nemesis search <candidate> --task <random-walk fixture> --legality
<profile> --method splitting --tier batch-cpu --budget … --json` with each estimator, the verb having no option
that picks one: once with `nemesis.splitting.estimator` unset (the chosen estimator, `--key sp-1`) and once
with a scratch configuration setting it to the other value (`--key sp-2`), on the same binary, two keys so
neither run is the other's stored receipt, asserting each result's `estimator`; re-invokes the GATE-NEMESIS-05 script (N26a-31), whose receipt at the new pin a recording session
enters (N38b-19); and calls phase `post` of
`scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_19.sh` (written by FD-FSIM-19). Every NDJSON line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N26bp1-01`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block;
logged: the sibling commit, the stack-receipt hash, per estimator the mean, interval, coverage, variance per
simulated step and mean-square error against the truth 1.504 × 10⁻⁴, the agreement statistic against its
threshold 3, episodes to first discovery, the literal evaluation of the choice rule clause by clause, and the
gate script's summary. On failure: the first clause of the rule or the first gate row that failed, with both
values and the seeds needed to reproduce. With FD-FSIM-19 not present the script exits 0 after logging
"FD-FSIM-19 not landed; interim posture in force" and asserting the interim tests of N26b-22 pass; with
KILL-RARE fired it logs "no consumer" and exits 0. Final `summary`: the choice, assertions passed and failed;
`gates: ["GATE-NEMESIS-05"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-666 (random walk, 1.504 × 10⁻⁴; control: a walk with no noise after the fork returns identical children and
is refused). Negative controls: an "upstream" estimator stub that reuses the parent's streams must fail the
admission test; a choice evaluator that looks at one measure only (variance alone, or episodes to first failure
alone) must fail the two `keep ours` rows of `choice_rule_evaluator` that differ in the other measure; an
estimator stub biased by a factor 1.5 must fail the unbiasedness clause of `random_walk_differential`.
Determinism: the random-walk fixture is exact arithmetic on addressed draws; the product fixture inherits the
determinism class of its tier and compares counts and intervals only.

## Considerations

- Appendix-level priority P3 and "no milestone waits" translate to backlog priority here; the bead becomes
  ready only when its upstream dependency closes.
- Division of work with the upstream bead: FD-FSIM-19 is done in the sibling's repository under its rules and
  ends at `landed`; everything that happens in this repository afterwards is here. The choice rule is stated in
  both beads; a change to it is made here and mirrored there by a request.
- Keeping two estimators costs maintenance; the loser stays only as a differential check, selectable by
  `nemesis.splitting.estimator` and never the default. It stays in every build rather than behind a test-only
  feature because FD-FSIM-19's phase `post` compares the two on the one installed binary.
- An adopted upstream estimator changes nothing about evidence: splitting rates are exploration data, refused by
  every bound, whichever implementation produced them.
