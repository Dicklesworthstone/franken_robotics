## Background

`frob explain` orders the anomalies of a failed episode in time and calls the earliest one the first cause; that
ordering is a heuristic and is labelled `Estimated`. To test a cause the system must be able to restore the world
to a moment before the failure, change one thing and run forward. The operation that does this is the **fork**: a
parent episode, a fork tick and a change set. This bead delivers the fork as a mechanism and as the verb
`run.fork` at milestone M2, a milestone before the attribution verbs that consume it, because three other things
need it first: the null-replay and classification gates, rare-event splitting, and the planning worlds of the
sampling controller.

Ownership is split and both halves meet here. The Chronicle subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`, layer 6) owns the
mechanism: snapshots, restore, the shared prefix and the verb. The Autopsy chapter owns the semantics: the five
classes and the earliest tick at which a change may be applied. The mechanism implements exactly those five
classes and adds none. Autopsy registers no fork verb of its own; the attribution verbs start from a fork handle
made here.

Design decisions that bind this bead:

- **ADR-CHRON-06 / ADR-AUTOPSY-02: a fork is classified before any state is restored.** Restoring first and
  discovering later that the change invalidated the restored state produces a trajectory of no design.
- **Forks by indexed copy, not copy-on-write.** Rejected alternative: copy-on-write device state, so a fork costs
  nothing until it diverges. Refutation: state is write-dense (every step rewrites every world's configuration,
  velocity, contacts and warm starts), so a fork has copied everything after one step and pays an indirection on
  every read; and the whole copy is small: 8,192 worlds × 30 kB = 0.25 GB, about a millisecond at an RTX 4090's
  memory bandwidth, at most a quarter of one 4.1 ms batch step at 2.0 M world-steps/s. What survives: copy the
  chosen rows into free slots, with warm template environments kept resident.
- **A world does not depend on its batch.** One failing world is forked K times into a batch of K children, and
  that is sound only because a world's trajectory is the same bits alone, in any batch, at any slot.
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-04: attribution reports a flip rate at round-off scale; double precision is the default for one
  failure.** Closed-loop legged control amplifies round-off enough to flip marginal outcomes in single precision
  over long windows; single precision on the originating tier is admitted only by a measured rule. For the fork
  verb this means two checks at the fork point of a single-precision run: the null fork on the originating
  backend, and a replay of the same checkpoint in double precision with a decision-level comparison. The
  rejected alternative is to attribute on whatever tier recorded the failure without either check.

Counterfactual replay from restorable state is established practice and incumbent simulators expose restorable
state and opt-in run-to-run determinism on one GPU (FACT, source register). Nothing here is claimed as new. Tier:
Solid.

## Scope

In: the implementation of `ForkService` in `frob-chronicle`; the indexed copy into child slots; batches of K
children including children that are different model variants; the null fork run at fork-point creation; the
double-precision decision check for single-precision parents as it stands at M2 (the comparison replay at the
fork point, the per-world decision comparison, the result stored with the fork point and printed as
`double_precision`); the verb `run.fork` with its schema, exit codes and refusals; the ledger record of fork
points and children.
Out: the classifier (N27a-02), fork points (N27a-03), identity and streams (N27a-04), restore handling (N27a-05),
the gate measurements (N27a-06, N27a-07), the end-to-end script (N27a-08), interventions with a declared
mechanism, placebo plans and attribution (BD-279, BD-280), the first-divergence search (BD-206); and what an
attribution does with the stored check from M3: the precision route record, the repeat of the double-precision
run, the hand-off to the single-precision lane and the refusal FROB-REF-584 (N27b-07, which reads the result
stored here and runs no second comparison at the fork point).

This seed bead is the capstone of the fork service. It depends on its components N27a-01 to N27a-05; its three
gates are measured and recorded against it by N27a-06 and N27a-07, and from the user's side by N27a-08, all of
which depend on it.

## Technical approach

**Flow of one call.**

1. Check the session scope (`run:run`, the capability of the verb's row in the verb registry, VERBS-run; scope
   names are proposals until the capability bead BD-258 fixes the scope registry) and that the run is not a
   sealed confirmation run (FROB-REF-840 otherwise).
2. `classify(request)` (N27a-02): licence, tick resolution, model differences, classes. Nothing is restored.
   If the request names a minimal counterexample world and gives no change set, the candidate changes are the
   inverses of that world's edits (each edit's `to` set back to its `from`), one change per edit.
3. With `--classify-only`: no child carrying a change is restored. The fork point is still opened (step 4), so
   that the caller holds a verified handle. Without it, a change set whose class is structural at a tick > 0 is
   FROB-REF-581, and a request that grafts state across models is FROB-REF-582.
4. `open`: resolve the fork point (N27a-03), derive its `ForkId` (N27a-04) and run the **null fork**: one child
   with the empty change set and shared streams, on the originating backend and profile, from the fork tick to
   the end of the parent's record inside the fork horizon. Its chain digests are compared with the parent's
   recorded ones. Result `null_replay: bitwise`, or `mismatch` with the first differing tick and stage (found by
   re-running that tick with per-stage digests). A fork point is opened once; a repeated call returns the stored
   result. A fork point whose null replay is a `mismatch` is unsound: it is kept and reported, and no child is
   ever spawned from it (step 6).
5. For a parent recorded in single precision: replay the same checkpoint in double precision on the CPU tier
   (values widened exactly) and apply the per-world decision comparison: the same verdict, the same failure class
   and the same binding atom, with the failure time inside the envelope recorded for "time of first failure" in
   the fidelity ledger's stratum of the run. Result `double_precision: decision-equivalent | not-equivalent |
   unresolved`, with both failure times. `unresolved` (the double-precision replay ends in a numerical outcome,
   or the stratum has no recorded envelope) is treated as not equivalent, fail-closed, with the reason
   recorded. For a double-precision parent the field is `same-precision` and no comparison runs. The replay is
   the cross-profile restart of `sim.replay --tier` (BD-204), labelled a comparison replay and never a
   continuation. The result is stored with the fork point; from M3 the precision routing of attribution
   (N27b-07) reads it there.
6. `spawn` (when children are requested: the request's own `--change` set without `--classify-only`, and
   every specification of a `--children` plan): refuse with FROB-REF-583 when the fork point's null replay is
   a `mismatch`; otherwise build one `ChildSpec` (N27a-01) per requested child, order them canonically by
   child key, keep one of any that share a key (one child, run once), restore each by its class (N27a-05) and
   run to the horizon. `ForkService::spawn` applies the same check for every caller.
7. Write the fork point and every child to the ledger with their manifests; return the envelope.

**The indexed copy.** Children run in free slots of a resident environment of the parent's shape class. The copy
is a gather: for child slot j, every state column of the checkpoint row `src[j]` is copied to row j. The column
list is the snapshot kernel's own (physical state, contact memory and warm starts, clocks and packet queues,
controller, filter and policy states, actuator states, scene state, command cursor), so a field added to the
checkpoint is added to the copy by construction. On the CPU tier this is a row copy; on devices it is the
snapshot blit with an index vector. The copy never writes the source row. The sampling controller's planning
worlds use the same gather on live state.

**Batches.** K children of one world occupy K slots of one batch. Children whose change sets alter a per-world
parameter (a surface property, a sensor law) are parameter variants in their own slots; children whose changes
alter the model table use the mixed batches of the variant mechanism. Batch size, slot order and co-tenants do
not enter any child's address, stream keys or digests.

**The verb.** `run.fork`, kind `run`, first milestone M2.

```text
frob run fork <run> --world <w>  ( --at <tick or time> | --before failure [--dt <lead>] )
              [--change '<factor> := <value>']…  [--reuse <checkpoint field>]…  [--classify-only]
              [--children <plan file>] [--co-tenants <seed>] [--horizon <time> | --to-horizon]
              [--determinism <profile>] [--precision <profile>] [--on-degraded <policy>]
              --key <idempotency key> --json
```

`--key` is required, as for every `run` verb (a call without it is FROB-REF-805); the generator adds the other
common properties of the kind (the budget, `--estimate`, the synchronous wait, the cancellation mode). The row
also lists `--field-reuse auto|require|off` and `--no-field-reuse`, which the Moonshot field-reuse bead N19c-35
implements from M6; until then this handler answers each with FROB-REF-724.

**Numerical options on a fork.** `--determinism {reference-replay|device-replay|cross-backend|fast-training}`,
`--precision {default|stiff|f32|f64}` and `--on-degraded {refuse|continue-flagged}` are parsed and admitted by
the shared parser and admission step of N35-04, which this handler calls before step 2. Their defaults on a fork
are not N35-04's per-tier defaults but the parent's recorded `ProfileRecord`: its determinism profile, its
precision profile and width, and its degraded-world policy (DESIGN: a bare fork must stay a continuation, and the
per-tier default would not for a parent run under `stiff`, whose snapshot is bound to that profile). An explicit
value that restates the parent's profile changes nothing. One that resolves to another determinism profile,
precision profile or width (another `pins_digest` or step plan identity) cannot open a fork point, because every
fork point runs its null fork, which is a continuation: the request is
FROB-REF-894 and the repair is the comparison replay of BD-204 (`frob sim replay … --tier <t> [--precision
f32|f64] --comparison`, the options of `sim.replay`'s row).
`--on-degraded` is not part of the pins and may differ from the parent's: under `refuse` a child world that is
ill-conditioned beyond its precision profile is FROB-REF-248 with nothing partial published; under
`continue-flagged` that child runs and carries the flag `Degraded: ill-conditioned`. Admission order: 245, 247
and 604 (N35-04's steps 1 and 2), then the licence (562, N35-07; on this verb the class check of N35-04's step 4
is 562, by N35-REF's division), then the profile comparison (894), then classification.

The repeated `--change` options together form **one** change set (classified change by change; the set's
class is the maximum); without `--classify-only` that set is run as one child with shared streams and no
perturbation. `--children <plan file>` (DESIGN) is how a batch of *different* children is requested, which is
what GATE-AUTOPSY-01's batch of 512 and a splitting stage need. The file is a child plan, schema
`frob.fork.children/1`: a JSON array of child specifications in the canonical JSON of `ChildSpec` (N27a-01).

```json
[ { "changes": [], "perturbation": "none", "series": "null", "index": 0, "streams": "shared" },
  { "changes": [], "series": "placebo", "index": 3,
    "perturbation": {"state": {"coord": "q[7]", "relative": "1/1000000000000", "sign": "+"}} },
  { "changes": ["scene./floor/patch[galley-2]/friction := 0.3"], "series": "replicate", "index": 0 },
  { "changes": [], "series": "replicate", "index": 5, "streams": "rekey-disturbances" } ]
```

Omitted fields take the defaults: no change, no perturbation, series `null`, index 0, `shared` streams, the
request's horizon. Every specification is classified in step 2 before any child is restored. A specification
that would be refused alone (FROB-REF-581, -582) is listed under `refused` in the result with its code and
fields while the others run (exit 2); when none can run, the first refusal is the envelope's (exit 3). Two
specifications with the same child key are one child. The plan's size is bounded by the free slots of the
resident environment and by the budget, through the ordinary admission estimate (FROB-REF-701 otherwise). The
file is read through the path jail, like every file parameter. `--co-tenants <seed>` (the composition option
that `sim.replay` has, BD-203) places the children in a slot order permuted by the seed among as many
unrelated filler worlds as there are children; it changes no address, stream key or digest, and exists so
that batch independence can be checked from the command line.

`--reuse` (the request's `reuse` list, empty by default) names checkpoint fields the caller asks to keep across
the change, for example `checkpoint.contact.memory`. It is how a warm-start graft is requested and therefore how
FROB-REF-582 is reached: naming a field that the change set invalidates is refused, with the field in `observed`.
Naming a field the change leaves valid is accepted and changes nothing, since that field is restored anyway.

Result (DESIGN; the output schema is `frob.run.fork.out/1`, as the verb's registry row names it; the string
`frob.fork/1` is only the domain tag of the fork-identity hash, N27a-04):

```json
{ "fork": "f:a41c…", "parent": {"run": "r:19e2…", "world": 311}, "requested": "failure − 1.0 s",
  "tick": "10.30 s", "source": "ring", "class": "continuation",
  "null_replay": "bitwise", "double_precision": "decision-equivalent",
  "failure_time_s": {"parent": "11.30", "f64": "11.31"},
  "classified": [
    {"do": "scene./floor/patch[galley-2]/film := dry", "class": "contact-affecting",
     "earliest_valid_tick": "10.30 s", "forced_by": ["contact memory"], "labels": ["contact-memory-discarded"]},
    {"do": "payload.tray.fill := 0.50", "class": "structural", "earliest_valid_tick": "0 s",
     "forced_by": ["payload mass and slosh state", "policy history", "contact memory"]} ],
  "children": [], "horizon_s": "2" }
```

The `forced_by` list of the structural change is abbreviated here to three of its live fields; the classifier
returns every live field in its fixed order (N27a-02).

Envelope evidence: class `Tested` on the parent's tier, with the parent's determinism class and identities and an
empty `claims` list (a fork is a replayable experiment, not a claim about a design). Exit 0 when complete with nothing to flag; exit 2 when `null_replay`
is `mismatch` (on a call that asked for no child), when the double-precision check is `not-equivalent` or
`unresolved`, or when some children of a plan were refused and the others ran (completed with findings); exit 8 with a job
handle when children are still running at the end of the bounded wait; exit 3 on the refusals below except
FROB-REF-604, which is exit 6. In every refusal block, `pointer`, `observed` and `required` have the shapes of the
code's registry row (N27-REF for 580 to 583; N35-REF, N18-REF, N21-REF, N29-REF and N39-REF for the others).

| Code | Refusal | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|
| FROB-REF-580 | A continuation whose physical, clock, sensor or policy contract does not match the checkpoint (`pointer` the checkpoint; `observed` the contract at fault with its two identities, or the missing field) | Fork from the nearest snapshot whose four contracts match (the refusal names it); reconstruct the fork point by replay from the world address to the tick; if the contract was changed on purpose, declare the change as an intervention in the change set, which is then classified, or request a comparison replay (the order of the registry row, N27-REF) |
| FROB-REF-581 | A structural or design-parameter change requested at a mid-episode tick; the earliest valid tick (zero) and the forcing fields are returned | `autopsy.rerun` from the episode start; fork at the earliest valid tick returned; the labelled diagnostic forced fork where admitted (the order of the registry row, N27-REF). `--classify-only` reads the classification without raising this refusal |
| FROB-REF-582 | State from one model grafted onto another: a `--reuse` field the change set invalidates, or a checkpoint of a run under another `ModelId` (`pointer` the change set; `observed` the field whose reuse is invalid) | Fork at the earliest valid tick returned; or restart and settle |
| FROB-REF-583 | Children requested from a fork point whose null fork does not reproduce its parent (`pointer` the fork; `observed` the first divergent tick and stage with both digests) | `frob telemetry divergence <parent> <null fork>`; fork from another stored snapshot or by replay from the address; re-record under a replay profile and file the determinism violation |
| FROB-REF-562 | A counterfactual fork requested below determinism class D2 (the source run's class is D3, D4 or unrecorded, or its device family's batch-independence record is not current) | Teleport the world to `batch-cpu` and fork there; re-run the episode under `device-replay` on a family with a current record; `frob spark check --sweeps all` to renew the record (N35-REF's order) |
| FROB-REF-894 | A snapshot restored under another numerical profile and labelled a continuation (here: options that resolve to a determinism profile, precision profile or width other than the parent's) | Request a comparison replay |
| FROB-REF-245 | A half-precision value of `--precision` (N35-04's admission step 1) | None; not offered (`retry: never`) |
| FROB-REF-247 | An N35-04 option not applicable to a fork: `--determinism fast-training`, which only training verbs take, or `cross-backend` without two named placements | Drop the option, or use one of the applicable profiles the refusal lists |
| FROB-REF-248 | A child world ill-conditioned beyond its precision profile under `--on-degraded refuse` (κ̂·u above the limit, default 0.1); nothing partial is published | `--precision stiff`; the world on `batch-cpu` in double precision or on the referee; soften the contact impedance; `--on-degraded continue-flagged` (N35-REF's order) |
| FROB-REF-604 | A `--precision` width the parent's tier has no step for (`f64` on a device tier; `f32` or `stiff` on the referee); exit 6, zero worlds stepped | Omit `--precision` (the parent's width is the default); for another width, teleport the world to `batch-cpu` and fork there (N35-04's first repair, `--tier batch-cpu`, as a fork reads it) |
| FROB-REF-840 | The run is a sealed confirmation run | None from a discovery session |

The registry entry of `run.fork` in `registries/verbs.toml` is made at M1 by VERBS-run and lists only the codes
already entered then (245, 247, 248, 562, 604, 840, 894), because the registry lint RL-06 makes a code absent from
`refusals.toml` a load-time error. N27-REF enters 580, 581, 582 and 583 at M2; this bead's change appends those
four to the row's `refusals` list, so that the generated refusal table and the binding test of N27-REF list them
against `run.fork`.

A null-replay mismatch found when a fork point is opened is a result, not a refusal: the call that opened it (a
bare open, or `--classify-only`) completes with exit 2, the mismatch is filed in the divergence ledger with both
digests, and the fork point is kept so that it can be read again and passed to `telemetry.divergence`. What is
refused is building on it (DESIGN): a call that asks for children from that fork point is FROB-REF-583 and
restores nothing, and from M3 every attribution verb refuses the fork with the same code. Reason: the record is
unsound, so its children would be evidence of nothing and would still be charged to the budget; and the verb's
registry row lists the code among those `run.fork` raises.

Cancellation: request, drain, finalize; child slots are returned to the environment and no device resource leaks.
Idempotency: one key yields one fork job and one charge.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition. GATE-CHRON-05 (TARGET): the five classes are assigned correctly on 100 planted change
      sets; the warm-start graft is refused 100 of 100; the empty intervention equals replay on 1,000 worlds.
      GATE-AUTOPSY-01 (TARGET): null forks of 1,000 failing worlds replay with identical digests on the
      originating backend; a world forked alone and in a batch of 512 children gives identical digests.
      GATE-AUTOPSY-02 (TARGET): 40 catalogued pairs of intervention and state are classified correctly; the
      warm-start graft and a mid-episode gear change are refused with the right fields. Each is recorded with
      measured counts by N27a-06 and N27a-07 against this implementation.
- [ ] `--classify-only` restores no child carrying a change (store instrumentation) and returns the class,
      earliest valid tick and forcing fields for every requested change.
- [ ] The indexed copy leaves the source row and the parent's record unchanged (hashes before and after).
- [ ] A batch of children with three different parameter variants runs in one batch and each child's digests
      equal those of the same child run alone.
- [ ] A planted single-bit corruption of a checkpoint yields `null_replay: mismatch` naming the tick, exit 2,
      and a divergence-ledger row; a call that asks for children from that fork point is FROB-REF-583, exit 3,
      and restores no child.
- [ ] A `--children` plan of specifications with different change sets, perturbations and stream modes runs as
      one batch; each child's address equals the one `ForkService::spawn` derives for the same `ChildSpec`,
      whatever the order of the file; `--co-tenants` changes no address and no digest.
- [ ] `frob run fork … --json` writes exactly one envelope; every refusal above maps to exit 3 except 604 (exit
      6); a cancelled job leaves the environment's free-slot count where it started.
- [ ] Without numerical options a fork records the parent's `ProfileRecord` on the fork point (a parent run under
      `stiff` keeps `stiff`, and its null fork is bitwise); options that resolve to another determinism profile,
      precision profile or width are FROB-REF-894; 245, 247 and 604 are raised through N35-04's admission before anything is classified.
- [ ] After this bead's change the `run.fork` entry of `registries/verbs.toml` lists 580, 581, 582 and 583 beside
      its M1 codes, and `cargo test -p frob-verbs --test cluster_run -- refusal_lists` passes.
- [ ] `frob-chronicle`'s `CONTRACT.md` documents forks under inputs, outputs, invariants (INV-CHRON-07,
      INV-AUTOPSY-04), refusals and determinism class; a CPU reference path exists (the CPU tier is one of the
      backends) and the device path has a retained differential check against it.

## Considerations

- A call with `--classify-only` still opens the fork point and runs the null fork. The plan says classify-only
  "restores nothing" and its worked session shows a classify-only call returning a fork handle with a null-replay
  result; this bead reads the first as "restores no changed child".
- Far-field field reuse across forks (sharing a parent's field tiles with a child and recomputing only dirty
  ones) is a Moonshot mechanism of the scene subsystem that reports through this verb and adds none; the result
  reserves the field `field_reuse` for it and this bead does nothing else for it.
- The sampling controller and splitting depend on this bead; their requirement that the controlled world's
  digest not change with the fork count is the source-row rule above.
- Memory: K children of the reference quadruped cost K × about 35 kB; 689 children are about 24 MB.
- Risk RISK-50 (attribution names a cause where none is stable) is controlled downstream; here the control is
  that a fork point without a bitwise null replay cannot be attributed from.
