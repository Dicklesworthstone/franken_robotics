## Background

The counterfactual debugger (the subsystem Autopsy) lets an agent test a proposed cause of a simulated failure by
intervention: fork the failed episode shortly before the failure, change one declared thing, and see whether the
failure still happens, net of a placebo; re-run design edits from the episode start; get back labelled contrasts
and minimal repair sets. Its mechanisms are established technique. One claim about it is not established: that
an agent repairs designs in fewer turns with these tools than with logs. Everything else in the subsystem is
gated by known answers; this claim is a HYPOTHESIS and is gated by a comparison of agents.

GATE-AUTOPSY-08 (claim: Design comparison; milestone M4; TARGET): **on the planted-defect designs of the corpus,
agents given Autopsy reach an accepted repair in at most one third of the serial turns of agents given logs and
`explain` alone, pre-registered, with an anytime-valid verdict.**

What rides on it. The bet that interventional autopsy is stable enough to act on (BET-23) lists as one of its
falsifiers that an agent with autopsy needs more than one third of the repair turns of an agent with logs. The
program's lever "explain by intervention" carries the TARGET of at least 3 times fewer repair turns than an agent
given logs, on planted causes, with the note that no published figure for agent repair turns exists. The
release criteria say the effect on an agent's repair turns is measured and reported, whichever way it falls.

What does not ride on it. The ceiling of the benefit was stated before any measurement: the output is an
accuracy, not a time; it can shorten a design decision only by removing agent turns spent on wrong repairs, so
its ceiling is the share of turns that diagnosis takes in the phase ledger. And the consequence of failure was
stated in advance: if the gate fails, the tools stay, because they are cheap and exact, and the claim about
turns is withdrawn. The subsystem does not claim to be a differentiator of the engine either: an incumbent
simulator with restorable state and a thin layer can host the same method, and that arm belongs to the
program's ten-fold protocol.

This bead runs the registered experiment and records the gate. The protocol, the arms, the turn definition, the
acceptance rule, the ceiling, the potency controls and the statistical rule are fixed and hashed beforehand by
the pre-registration bead; nothing in them may change here.

## Scope

In: the campaign harness run on the test subset; the adjudication of submissions; the e-process and its
verdict; the gate record with every disclosed figure; the withdrawal or statement of the turn claim in the
documentation and the bets register.
Out: the protocol and its registration (N27a-19); the corpus (BD-248); the autopsy verbs and the agent surface;
the studio-wide surface A/B and its gate; the ten-fold protocol's composed-incumbent arm.

## Technical approach

1. **Preconditions, checked by the harness before the first campaign.** The registration record exists and its
   hash matches the committed one; the corpus label file's hash matches the one in the record; the agent model
   and revision are the pinned ones; the build of `frob`, `frobd` and `frob-mcp` is one commit, recorded; the
   single-cause, coalition, abstention and re-run gates of the subsystem have recorded results at that commit
   (an agent must not be handed a debugger whose known answers fail). A mismatch stops the run.
2. **Campaigns.** For each test design and each of its two agent seeds, one campaign per arm:
   - arm A: the full agent surface, including `run.fork`, `autopsy.intervene`, `autopsy.attribute`,
     `autopsy.repair`, `autopsy.rerun`, `autopsy.show`;
   - arm B: the same surface without those six verbs (logs, telemetry, `explain`, simulation, edits, comparison);
   - arm F: arm B plus `autopsy.rerun` (the subsystem's own fallback), reported beside the verdict.
   Arm order is randomized and counterbalanced by the registered seed; each campaign has a fresh context, a
   session and namespace of its own, the same brief template, the same documentation, and the same caps (40
   serial turns; the registered token, tool-call and compute budgets). No human intervenes. A campaign that hits
   a harness fault is voided and re-run once with the fault recorded; an agent's own failure is never voided.
3. **Adjudication.** Each submission (a sequence of typed design edits) is checked by the harness: no diagnostic
   factor among the edits; `design check` passes; on sealed world clusters the exact one-sided 95% lower bound on
   the requirement pass rate is at least the task's threshold. Accepted or rejected is returned to the agent
   with no detail about the sealed worlds.
4. **Measurement.** Serial turns to the accepted submission (censored at the cap), per campaign. Also recorded
   per campaign: the first named cause and whether it matched the planted defect; the number of submissions; the
   phase tags of every turn; simulations run; tokens; tool calls; device-seconds; and for arm A which autopsy
   verbs were called and the labels they returned.
5. **Verdict.** Campaign pairs (arm A, arm B) are fed in the registered order to the registered rule: paired
   quantity d = b/3 − a; betting e-process on the bounded mean; confirmed when E ≥ 20 (α = 0.05); the mirrored
   process decides "refuted"; the budget's end decides "not confirmed". The harness computes nothing that the
   registration did not name, and writes the e-value trajectory.
6. **Disclosure in the record.** Restricted mean turns per arm with the paired ratio and its confidence-sequence
   bounds; censoring counts per arm; first-cause accuracy per arm; the share of arm-A campaigns in which the
   debugger abstained and what the agent did next; the ceiling measured in pre-registration against the ratio
   observed; arm F's ratio against arm B; the A/A and potency figures from pre-registration; every voided
   campaign with its reason; cost per arm. The record names the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seeds and
   the registration hash.
7. **Consequences, applied by this bead.**
   - *Confirmed*: the turn claim may be printed in the permitted wording for a design comparison, with the
     e-value and the study identifier, scoped to planted-defect designs of the corpus and to the pinned agent.
   - *Not confirmed* or *refuted*: the claim is withdrawn: the lever's TARGET line, the bet's status and the
     documentation are updated to say the tools are kept as exact diagnostic instruments with no claim about
     repair turns. The tools are not removed. The bet's fallback text (single-factor paired rewind; then
     first-anomaly ordering, labelled `Estimated`) is not triggered by this gate alone; it belongs to the kill
     gate on planted-cause accuracy.
   In every case the result is published as measured.
8. **Exit review.** The record is reviewed by a fresh-context session that did not implement the harness, which
   re-derives the verdict from the stored campaign records and the registration.

Harness location: `crates/frob-conformance/src/experiments/autopsy_ab.rs`, driven by
`scripts/e2e/gate_autopsy_08.sh`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/autopsy_ab_run.rs`:
- `refuses_without_registration`: the harness stops when the registration hash is absent or differs.
- `refuses_on_corpus_relabel`: a changed label-file hash stops the run.
- `verdict_from_records`: on a stored set of 60 synthetic campaign pairs with a = 3 and b = 15 the rule returns
  confirmed and the ratio bound is below 1/3; with a = 6 and b = 12 it returns refuted or not confirmed, never
  confirmed (labelled `synthetic`; these check the rule's wiring, not agents).
- `voiding_rules`: a harness fault voids and re-runs once; an agent timeout does not.
- `allowlist_audit`: a transcript containing `autopsy_attribute` in arm B fails the audit.
- `consequence_is_applied`: for each verdict the documentation patch produced by the harness matches the
  expected text.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/gate_autopsy_08.sh` launches the daemon and the `frob-mcp` server, opens one session per campaign
with the arm's scopes, runs the agent through the registered harness, adjudicates submissions and computes the
verdict. Log (newline-delimited JSON at `target/e2e-logs/gate_autopsy_08/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`, with a
human-readable transcript beside it): `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`BD-375`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`,
`assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`); for harness commands the exact command line, exit code and wall time;
per campaign: design, defect kind, arm, agent seed, order position, turns, censored, submissions and their
adjudications, first named cause and whether it matched, autopsy verbs called, tokens and tool calls against
caps, device-seconds, the session's evidence blocks by reference; after each pair the paired quantity and the
running e-value; metrics `turn_ratio` with its bounds against 1/3, `e_value` against 20, `first_cause_accuracy`
per arm, `censored` per arm. A final `summary` line: campaigns run and voided, the verdict, `gates:
["GATE-AUTOPSY-08"]`. Sealed-cluster content and world addresses of the acceptance battery never appear in the
log; agent transcripts are stored as artifacts and referenced by hash. The script is restartable: campaigns are
keyed by (design, seed, arm) and a finished campaign is not re-run.

### Known answers and negative controls
The potency control and the A/A control are run in pre-registration and their figures are carried into this
record: an arm told the planted defect must show at least the three-fold effect, and arm B against itself must
not. The planted defects themselves are the known answers for first-cause accuracy. The ceiling arithmetic: a
diagnosis share s bounds the ratio below by 1 − s. Negative controls on the harness: an attempt to change the
cap, the rule or the unit order after registration (the hash check fails); an acceptance decided on worlds the
agent could read (the sealed-read audit fails). Determinism lanes: the harness's own computations (order,
adjudication, e-process) are deterministic given the campaign records; agent behaviour is the random element
and is what the statistics are for.

## Considerations

- A failed or unconfirmed result is the expected outcome if diagnosis is a small share of repair turns on these
  tasks. That is not a failure of the bead; it is the result, and the claim is then withdrawn as written.
- The result is specific to the pinned agent model and to planted defects. A later agent that diagnoses better
  from logs alone would shrink the effect; a re-run is a new registration.
- Agents may ignore the debugger in arm A. The record reports the share of arm-A campaigns that called it at
  all; an arm-A agent that never forks is still arm A.
- Compute and model spend are bounded by the caps; the campaigns need the envelope requested with the
  registration, and without it the number of units is reduced and the reachable verdicts are restated first.
- The studio-wide surface A/B shares the harness; if both run in the same week, arm B transcripts with identical
  briefs are reused only where the registration said so.
