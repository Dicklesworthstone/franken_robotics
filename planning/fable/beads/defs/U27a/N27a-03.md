## Background

A fork needs a complete state of the world at the fork tick. Where that state comes from decides what a fork
costs and whether it is possible at all for a world nobody thought to snapshot. DESIGN: a fork can start

- at any stored snapshot;
- at any tick held in the per-world ring buffer of recent full states;
- at any tick of any replayable episode, by replaying the world from its address up to that tick.

**The ring buffer is an optimization, not a requirement.** The reason is arithmetic. A per-world state is tens of
kilobytes (the design byte model gives about 35 kB for the reference quadruped and about 97 kB for the reference
humanoid in single precision). Twenty snapshots covering two seconds for each of 8,192 worlds would take
20 × 8,192 × 35 kB ≈ 5.7 GB for the quadruped: several gigabytes on a 24 GB card that also holds the batch. So
full-state rings are kept for a reservoir of worlds, and every other failing world is reconstructed by replay, at
the cost of one episode prefix on the CPU. Replay is possible because a world is determined by its address, its
model, scene, task and policy identities and its kernel versions.

A fork also needs a place to stop. Counterfactual debugging forks shortly before a failure and runs shortly past
it; the choice of "shortly" is bounded by how fast small differences grow in closed-loop contact dynamics.

This bead is the resolver that turns a request ("world 311 of run r:19e2, one second before its failure") into a
restorable state at a committed tick, and the rule for the horizon. Crate `frob-chronicle`. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

the tier and precision a fork's children run on (the precision routing of attribution, N27b-07, under seed

## Technical approach

1. **Request forms.**
   - `at: Tick(t)`: an explicit tick, or a time with a unit that is converted exactly by the run's rational clock.
   - `at: BeforeFailure { lead }`: the tick of the terminal failure event of that world minus the lead. A world
     with no failure event is refused as an invalid argument (there is nothing to be "before"): FROB-REF-802
     with the pointer at `before`, `observed` "no terminal failure event in world <w>", and the repair
     `--at <tick or time>`.
2. **Snapping.** A fork starts at a committed tick (a tick at which the step's commit fence published state).
   The requested tick is snapped to the committed tick at or before it; the result reports both. Worked case: a
   fall recorded at 11.30 s, lead 1.0 s, gives a fork at 10.30 s.
3. **Source selection**, cheapest first:
   1. a stored snapshot at exactly the tick: restore it;
   2. the per-world ring, if it holds the tick: restore from the ring;
   3. otherwise the nearest stored snapshot before the tick, replayed forward to the tick;
   4. otherwise replay from the episode start (the authored initial condition and the reset-cache pose drawn by
      address).
   The source is recorded on the fork point (`source: snapshot | ring | replay-from-snapshot | replay-from-start`)
   with the number of ticks replayed.
4. **Where the reconstruction runs.** A fork point is always reconstructed on the originating backend and
   numerical profile, because a snapshot is bound to its numerical profile and only there is the prefix the
   parent's, bit for bit. The reconstructed state's chained digest must equal the recorded chain value at the
   fork tick; a mismatch is the null-replay failure and nothing proceeds. A child that will run in double
   precision on the CPU tier starts from that same checkpoint with its single-precision values widened exactly
   to double precision; restoring a checkpoint under another profile is a comparison replay, not a
   continuation, and the child is labelled so (the decision-level comparison that licenses it is made by the fork verb, BD-205).
5. **The lead ladder** (TARGET): 0.25 s, 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s. DESIGN default lead when none is given: 1 s. The ladder
   is what an escalation walks when no intervention at the first fork tick rescues. Leads longer than 2 s are
   accepted with the warning `lead-beyond-default-horizon`, since the usable horizon is set by the growth of
   small differences, which is not yet measured for our own controllers.
6. **Horizon.** By default a fork runs to one second past the parent's failure time (TARGET), or to the episode
   horizon when asked (`horizon: ToEpisodeEnd`). With the default lead the fork window is 2 s. A child that
   fails earlier stops at its own terminal event; a child that does not fail runs to the horizon and its verdict
   is read there.
7. **What the resolver can count on from the rings (the rings themselves are N29a-11's).** Which worlds keep
   a full-state ring is decided there, as a pure function of the run manifest and the addresses (the worlds
   of the trace sample, and a ring reservoir chosen by address hash under the stream identifier
   `ring-reservoir`), so replay recomputes the choice and two runs on the same addresses keep rings for the
   same worlds. The reservoir size per device is set there from the budget estimator (OPEN-AUTOPSY-4), and
   the ring depth default is 2 s with a snapshot every 0.1 s (OPEN-CHRON-2). This resolver assumes none of
   it: a failing world whose ring was not kept is reconstructed by replay, and a run with no ring at all
   resolves every fork point by replay with the same digests.
   What the default ring serves, by arithmetic on those defaults (DESIGN arithmetic, stated identically in
   N29a-11): a failure capture is the 2 s window that ends 0.2 s after the terminal event, with a snapshot
   every 0.1 s: 20 snapshots, the newest 0.2 s after the failure and the oldest 1.7 s before it (at 50 Hz
   control and a terminal event at tick 500, the window covers ticks 411 to 510 and the snapshots are at 415,
   420, …, 510). The leads 0.5 s and 1 s land on a ring snapshot (`source: ring`). The lead 0.25 s lies
   between two snapshots: it is reached from the snapshot 0.3 s before the failure by at most 0.05 s of replay
   (`replay-from-snapshot`). The lead 2 s (tick 400) lies 0.3 s before the oldest ring snapshot and is
   reconstructed from an earlier stored snapshot or from the episode start. The default lead of 1 s is
   therefore served without replay; the escalation to 2 s costs a replay unless the ring holds a snapshot 2 s
   before the failure, which takes a 2.3 s ring (23 snapshots, ticks 400 to 510; a 2.2 s ring's oldest
   snapshot is at tick 405, still 0.1 s short). That depth is an input to the open question on ring depth and
   is not decided here.
8. **Retention.** Opening a fork point pins the chunks and snapshots of its window (from the source snapshot to
   the horizon) while the fork point is cited by an open attribution; the pin is released when the session closes.
   A fork point whose source has been collected is refused with the tombstone's reason (Chronicle's dangling-
   evidence refusal), and the repair is replay from the start.
9. **Cost report.** Every fork point records `reconstruct_ticks`, `reconstruct_wall_ms`, the source and the
   backend, so the estimate for a batch of forks can be made before it is bought.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three sources give the same state: for 100 worlds and 3 ticks each, the chained digest at the fork tick
      is identical whether the state came from a stored snapshot, from the ring, or from replay-to-tick on the
      originating backend.
- [ ] `BeforeFailure { lead: 1 s }` on a world that fails at 11.30 s resolves to 10.30 s; each lead of the ladder
      resolves to failure time minus the lead, snapped down to a committed tick.
- [ ] The default horizon is failure time plus 1 s; `ToEpisodeEnd` runs to the episode horizon.
- [ ] A world outside the reservoir is forked by replay and its fork point records `replay-from-start` or
      `replay-from-snapshot` with the tick count.
- [ ] The resolver assumes no ring: with the ring reservoir set to zero in the manifest, every fork point of
      the same 100 worlds resolves by replay to the same chained digests. (That the reservoir set is a pure
      function of the addresses, identical under 100 slot assignments and shard counts, is accepted in
      N29a-11, which owns the rule.)
- [ ] On the default ring (2 s deep, a snapshot every 0.1 s, ending 0.2 s after the terminal event) the leads
      0.5 s and 1 s resolve with `source: ring`, the lead 0.25 s with `replay-from-snapshot` and at most
      0.05 s of replayed ticks, and the lead 2 s by replay from before the ring.
- [ ] A fork point whose window was collected is refused with the tombstone's reason; a pinned window survives a
      collection cycle.
- [ ] The memory arithmetic that justifies replay as a source (5.7 GB for 20 × 8,192 quadruped states) is
      reproduced from the byte model by the ring's own test (`ring_bytes_formula`, N29a-11), so a change to
      the byte model changes the stated default knowingly; this bead adds no second copy of it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-chronicle/tests/fork_point.rs`:
- `sources_agree`: digest equality across snapshot, ring and replay, on the CPU tier in both precisions.
- `before_failure_ladder`: failure at tick 22,600 of a 2,000 Hz physics clock (11.30 s); leads 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 s
  give ticks 22,100, 21,600, 20,600 and 18,600 before snapping; with a commit fence every 20 physics ticks all
  four are committed ticks already.
- `snap_down`: a request at tick 20,607 snaps to 20,600 and reports both.
- `no_failure_refused`: `BeforeFailure` on a passing world is refused with FROB-REF-802 pointing at `before`.
- `default_horizon`: fork at 10.30 s of a world failing at 11.30 s runs to 12.30 s.
- `resolves_without_any_ring`: reservoir size zero; every fork point is `replay-from-start` or
  `replay-from-snapshot`, digests unchanged.
- `ladder_against_default_ring`: terminal event at control tick 500 (50 Hz), ring snapshots at ticks 415,
  420, …, 510: lead 1 s → tick 450, `ring`; lead 0.5 s → 475, `ring`; lead 0.25 s → 12.5 control ticks
  before the failure, snapped down to the committed tick at or before it, `replay-from-snapshot` from the
  snapshot at tick 485 with the replayed ticks reported (at most 0.05 s); lead 2 s → 400, 15 control ticks
  (0.3 s) before the oldest ring snapshot at 415, replay. With the ring made 2.3 s deep (snapshots 400 to
  510) the lead 2 s resolves with `source: ring`; with 2.2 s (oldest 405) it still needs replay.
- `pin_and_release`; `collected_window_refused`.
(The tests of the reservoir rule and of the ring's bytes, `reservoir_is_address_function` and
`ring_bytes_formula`, are N29a-11's.)

### End-to-end script and logging
Covered by `scripts/e2e/run_fork.sh` (N27a-08): forks one world from a ring tick and one from outside the
reservoir, and logs the source, the reconstructed tick count, the wall time and the digest at the fork tick.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-671 (null fork: identical digests, all tiers) depends on this resolver giving the exact state. Negative
controls: a resolver that snaps up instead of down (the fork tick is then after the request and
`before_failure_ladder` fails); a resolver that treats a ring snapshot near the tick as the tick (the
0.25 s lead then reports `ring` and its digest differs from replay); the control of a reservoir chosen by
arrival order belongs to the ring's bead (N29a-11).
Metamorphic relation: forking at tick t from the snapshot at t₀ < t and from the snapshot at t₁ with t₀ < t₁ < t
gives the same digests. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; on each backend against
its own record.

## Considerations

- OPEN-AUTOPSY-4 (the size of the snapshot reservoir on each device; owner: Chronicle lead; blocks nothing).
  Fail-closed default: a reservoir sized by the budget estimator, with every other fork point reconstructed by
  replay.
- OPEN-AUTOPSY-3 (Lyapunov exponents of our own controllers and policies on our tiers, which set the usable fork
  horizon; owner: Cortex lead; measured at M3; blocks nothing). Default: leads of at most 2 s, with the growth of
  placebo separation reported in every attribution. FACT from the literature the plan cites: learned continuous-
  control policies in closed loop have measured maximal exponents of 0.03 to 0.16 per control step on walker and
  cheetah tasks; at 0.16 per step over 80 steps a perturbation grows by about 3.6 × 10⁵.
- Replay-to-tick of a GPU world costs one episode prefix. For a batch of failing worlds the prefixes are
  replayed together in one batch on the originating backend.
- A rejected alternative was copy-on-write device state so that every world is always forkable for free. State
  is write-dense (every step rewrites configuration, velocity, contacts and warm starts), so a fork has copied
  everything after one step and paid an indirection on every read; and the whole copy is small (8,192 worlds at
  30 kB are 0.25 GB).
