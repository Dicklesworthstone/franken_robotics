## Background

Counterfactual debugging reports, for a failed episode, which interventions remove the failure. When no single
intervention does and a pair does, the cause is labelled `joint`; when several single interventions each do, the
causes are labelled `alternative`. The two cases call for opposite repairs (fix both factors; fix either one),
and a summary by Shapley values cannot tell them apart: both give (½, ½). The gate that checks the coalition
machinery therefore needs failures whose causal structure is known in advance.

GATE-AUTOPSY-04 (claim: Known answer; milestone M3; TARGET): **planted joint and alternative causes are labelled
correctly in at least 95 of 100; the Shapley identities hold exactly; the negative-value case is reproduced.**

A gate is only as good as its plants. A "planted joint cause" that is in truth a single cause plus a bystander
would pass a broken attributor and fail a correct one. This bead builds the 100 cases with a procedure that
establishes each case's truth table by direct simulation of its four corner worlds, before any attribution code
sees it, and freezes the labels with a hash. The cases join the design corpus's planted material, which is the
potency control for every analysis: an experiment that cannot see a planted effect is silent about a real one.

Naming, since it is easy to invert. Let A and B be two adverse factors present in the failing world, and write
¬A for the intervention that removes A.

| Kind | Failure occurs when | do(¬A) alone | do(¬B) alone | do(¬A, ¬B) | Label | Repair sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint | A or B is present (each is sufficient) | still fails | still fails | passes | `joint` | {A, B} |
| Alternative | A and B are both present (each is necessary) | passes | passes | passes | `alternative` | {A} or {B} |

Tier: Solid. Everything simulated here is labelled `synthetic`: the worlds are constructed, not measured.

## Scope

In: the planting procedure; the 100 simulated cases; the host tables for the exact cases; the registry file and
its hash; the checks that keep the plants honest.
Out: running attribution on the cases and recording the gate (BD-281, with the script of N27a-18); planted
single causes for the single-factor gate (the design corpus, BD-248); planted chaotic and no-cause failures
(N27a-14).

## Technical approach

1. **Factor classes.** The five cause classes of the planted-cause suite: friction drop (a low-friction patch on
   the path), torque cap (a reduced joint torque limit), payload shift (a displacement of the payload at a stated
   time), latency (added sensor latency from a stated time) and terrain step (a step of stated height on the
   path). Each adverse factor is realized as a typed world edit or scheduled disturbance that first acts after
   the intended fork tick (one second before the failure), so that removing it at the fork tick is an exogenous,
   contact-affecting, information or diagnostic intervention and never a structural one.
2. **Case grid.** The 10 unordered pairs of classes × 5 instances × 2 kinds = 100 cases. Robot: the reference
   quadruped under the baseline controller and under the trained flat-terrain policy (cases alternate). Tasks:
   velocity tracking, low-friction crossing and payload transport. Each instance draws its base world address,
   path and factor timing from a seed listed in the registry.
3. **Planting one case** (on the CPU tier in double precision under the strict step profile; the referee for a
   20-case subset as a cross-check).
   1. Choose a base world w₀ in which the candidate passes with margin at least 0.2 of the requirement's scale.
   2. For each factor alone, find by bisection on its magnitude the threshold a* (respectively b*) at which the
      world begins to fail, within 2% of the factor's legal range. A factor with no threshold inside its legal
      range is replaced.
   3. *Joint kind*: set a = 1.5·a* and b = 1.5·b*, clamped to the legal range. *Alternative kind*: start from
      a = 0.7·a*, b = 0.7·b* and scale both by a common factor s ≥ 1 (bisection on s) until the world with both
      fails while each alone still passes; if no s works, discard the instance and draw another.
   4. Run the four corner worlds (neither, A only, B only, both) and record verdict, failure class, binding
      atom, failure time and margin. The truth table must match the kind's row above, with the failure in the
      both-present world of the same class and binding atom as in each single-factor failing corner (joint kind).
   5. *Robustness screen.* Each corner is re-run under 8 perturbations of one state coordinate at relative 10⁻⁷
      at the intended fork tick. All 8 must keep the corner's verdict. A case that fails the screen is discarded
      and counted. Reason: the gate tests the coalition logic; marginal cases are the abstention gate's subject.
   6. *Fork-tick check.* The intended fork tick is one second before the failure of the both-present world;
      neither factor may have acted before it (first-dependence tick later than the fork tick).
4. **Distractors.** Each case declares ten candidate factors: the two planted ones and eight that are present in
   the world and irrelevant to the failure (another patch off the path, a second sensor's latency, a closed
   door's torque, an unused joint's limit, and so on). For each distractor the planting run verifies that
   removing it alone leaves the failure in place. With ten factors the coalition plan is 10 singles and 45 pairs,
   55 coalitions.
5. **Host tables** (exact, no simulator), each a tiny synthetic system with two or three Boolean inputs and a
   deterministic verdict, run through the same fork-and-count code by the host-fixture trait:
   - `host:joint-pair`: v(∅) = 0, v({1}) = 0, v({2}) = 0, v({1, 2}) = 1. Expected label `joint`, repair set {1, 2},
     Shapley values (½, ½).
   - `host:alternative-pair`: v(∅) = 0, v({1}) = v({2}) = v({1, 2}) = 1. Expected `alternative`, repair sets {1}
     or {2}, Shapley values (½, ½).
   - `host:negative-value`: v(∅) = 0, v({1}) = 1, v({2}) = 0, v({1, 2}) = 0. Expected: factor 1 `but-for`; the
     pair does not rescue; Shapley values (½, −½), summing to 0; a single-factor repair {1} exists.
6. **Registry.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/autopsy-plants-coalitions.toml`: per case the identities
   (design, scene, task, controller or policy), the base address, the two planted edits with class and
   magnitude, the eight distractors, the four-corner table with margins, the fork tick, the expected label and
   repair sets, the seed, and the planting tier. The file's BLAKE3 hash is committed before any attribution
   result on these cases exists; a relabel changes the registry identity. Discarded draws are listed with the
   reason, so the yield of the procedure is on record.
7. **What keeps the plants honest.** The expected label of a case is derived from its recorded truth table by a
   ten-line function of this bead, never from attribution output. A unit test recomputes each table by re-running
   the four corners from the registry.

Modules: `crates/frob-conformance/src/plants/autopsy_coalitions.rs` (generator and checker) and
`crates/frob-autopsy/src/fixtures/host_tables.rs` (the three host systems, labelled `synthetic`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registry holds 100 simulated cases (50 joint, 50 alternative; 10 per pair of classes) and the three
      host tables, each with its four-corner table, and its hash is committed.
- [ ] Re-running the four corners of every case from the registry reproduces the recorded verdicts, failure
      classes and binding atoms (bitwise on the planting tier).
- [ ] Every case passes the robustness screen (8 of 8 at relative 10⁻⁷ on each corner) and the fork-tick check.
- [ ] Every distractor leaves the failure in place when removed alone.
- [ ] The 20-case referee subset agrees with the CPU tier on all four corners; a disagreement removes the case.
- [ ] The label-from-table function returns `joint` for the first table row and `alternative` for the second on
      all 100, and a planted mislabelled case (kinds swapped) is caught by the recomputation test.
- [ ] The number of discarded draws and the reasons are in the registry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/autopsy_plants_coalitions.rs`:
- `truth_table_kinds`: (fail, fail, fail, pass) for (both, A only, B only, neither) is `joint`; (fail, pass,
  pass, pass) is `alternative`; any other table is rejected as not a plant of either kind.
- `registry_recomputes`: the four corners of each case, re-run.
- `robustness_screen_holds`.
- `fork_tick_precedes_both_factors`.
- `distractors_do_not_rescue`.
- `swapped_kind_is_caught`: a copy of the registry with one case's kind flipped fails `registry_recomputes`.
- `bisection_thresholds`: on a `synthetic` one-parameter system that fails iff its magnitude exceeds 0.37, the
  bisection returns a* within 2% of the range.
`crates/frob-autopsy/tests/host_tables.rs`: the three tables return the stated coalition values when run
through the fork-and-count code with 8 replicates and 64 placebo forks (all counts are 0 or full).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_coalitions_abstention.sh` (N27a-18) reads this registry, runs the attribution verbs on each
case and logs expected against observed labels and repair sets; the planting itself is reproduced by
`cargo test -p frob-conformance autopsy_plants_coalitions`, whose output lists per case the thresholds a*, b*,
the magnitudes used and the four margins.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-673 (joint and alternative pairs: labels `joint` and `alternative`; Shapley values (½, ½) in both; repair sets
{1, 2}, and {1} or {2}; control: a summary that reports only Shapley values cannot tell them apart). KA-674 (the
negative-value table). KA-146 and CX-069, CX-296, CX-297. The planted-effect and A/A requirements of a method
experiment: these plants are the planted effect; the A/A side is a case with no adverse factor, in which no
coalition may be reported as rescuing (10 such control worlds are added to the registry and must yield no named
factor). Determinism lanes: the generator is seed-explicit and gives the identical registry twice on one machine
and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The joint kind is causal overdetermination. A reader who expects "joint" to mean "both needed for the failure"
  has the table inverted; the registry stores the table itself so that no one relies on the word.
- Planting is done in double precision on the CPU tier. A case whose truth table changes on a single-precision
  tier is still a valid plant for double-precision attribution; the single-precision lane has its own gate.
- Yield: the alternative kind needs a region where two sub-threshold factors add up to a failure. Class pairs
  whose effects do not combine (for example latency with a terrain step on a slow walk) may have low yield; the
  procedure then widens the task (a faster commanded speed) before giving up on a pair, and records it.
- Cost: 100 cases × (two bisections of about 7 runs, a scaling bisection, 4 corners, 32 screen runs) is about
  5,500 episodes of 20 s on the CPU tier, hours on one machine, run once and cached by content hash.
