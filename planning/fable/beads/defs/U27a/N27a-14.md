## Background

The most expensive mistake a causal debugger can make is to name a cause where there is none. Naming a wrong
cause sends an agent to edit the wrong thing, which costs a design iteration and its compute; declining costs one
fallback to the heuristic ordering of anomalies. Two situations must end in "no factor named".

**Chaos.** A closed-loop legged robot in contact amplifies small differences. Restore a failing world, change
anything, and some outcomes change; that is sensitivity, not cause. The protection is the **placebo fork**: a
change no physical cause could depend on (one state coordinate perturbed by a relative 10⁻¹²). The placebo flip
rate ρ₀ is the fraction of placebo forks whose verdict differs from the parent's, and above 0.2 the failure is
labelled `chaotic: unattributable` and no factor is named. The reference case: a doubling map with failure
defined on its fortieth iterate amplifies a 10⁻¹² placebo by 2⁴⁰ ≈ 1.1 × 10¹², so about half of placebo forks
flip; the same map over five iterates amplifies by 32 and attributes normally.

**A cause outside the list.** An attribution is scoped to the declared factor list. A failure whose cause is not
among the declared factors must yield no named factor, not the nearest bystander.

GATE-AUTOPSY-05 (claim: Known answer; milestone M3; TARGET): **Abstention: a planted chaotic failure and a planted
no-cause failure name no factor in 100 of 100.** This is the control for risk RISK-50 (attribution names a cause
where none is stable).

This bead builds the planted cases. As with every planted set, the truth is established by direct simulation
before any attribution code sees the case, and the labels are frozen by hash. Everything here is `synthetic`.
Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: 100 chaotic cases (50 on the host map, 50 simulated) and one referee knife-edge fixture; 100 simulated
no-cause cases; the non-chaotic and in-list controls; the registry and its hash; the checks that keep the plants

(the design corpus).

## Technical approach

1. **Chaotic, host (50 cases).** The map x ← 2x mod 1 in 64-bit fixed point (exact), started from x₀ drawn by
   seed; the requirement is violated iff the fortieth iterate is at least ½; only seeds whose parent is violated
   are kept. Each case declares three candidate factors that change nothing (they set unused parameters). A
   placebo of relative size in [1, 2) × 10⁻¹² moves x₀ by that much and the fortieth iterate by 2⁴⁰ times as
   much, between 1.1 and 2.2, which covers the circle: about half of placebo forks flip. Expected: ρ₀ between
   0.35 and 0.65, outcome `chaotic: unattributable`, no factor named.
2. **Chaotic, simulated (50 cases).** A real failing episode of the reference quadruped (a slip on a
   low-friction patch, a fall after a push) on the M3 task families, with one test-only requirement atom added
   under the conformance feature: `chaos_probe`. Its definition: at the committed tick 0.1 s after the intended
   fork tick, y₀ is the fractional part of Σ over joints of (joint angle in radians); y_{k+1} is the fractional
   part of 2·y_k in 64-bit fixed point; the atom is violated iff y₄₀ ≥ ½. The episode's requirement is the
   conjunction of the task's own requirement and the probe, and cases are kept where the probe is the binding
   atom. Every state coordinate couples to the joint angles within 0.1 s through the closed loop, so a placebo
   on any coordinate moves y₀ by about 10⁻¹² and y₄₀ by order one. Each case declares ten candidate factors (real
   ones: patch friction, the push, a torque limit, a latency, and so on). Expected: ρ₀ above 0.2, outcome
   `chaotic: unattributable`, no factor named, even though several declared factors will show rescue rates near
   one half.
3. **Chaotic, referee fixture.** `fixture:knife-edge`: one rigid pole of length 1 m on a frictionless pivot,
   released at 10⁻¹³ rad from upright with zero rate; the requirement is "the pole is on the positive side at
   1.5 s". A placebo of ±10⁻¹² rad or ±10⁻¹² rad/s decides the side by its sign, so about half of placebo forks
   flip. This is the case "a fixture where every nudge flips the outcome", on the trusted tier.
4. **No-cause, simulated (100 cases).** A failing episode with one planted adverse factor F* (a low-friction
   patch, a torque cap, a payload shift, added latency, a terrain step: 20 cases of each class) and ten declared
   candidate factors that do **not** include F*. Planting procedure, on the CPU tier in double precision under
   the strict step profile:
   1. Choose a base world in which the candidate passes with margin at least 0.2 of the requirement's scale; add
      F* at 1.5 times its failure threshold (found by bisection on its magnitude, within 2% of its legal range).
   2. Verify F* is the cause: removing F* alone makes the world pass.
   3. Verify the declared list is innocent: removing each of the ten declared factors alone (10 runs) and each
      pair (45 runs) leaves the failure in place, with the same failure class and binding atom.
   4. Robustness screen: the failing world and the world without F* each keep their verdict under 8
      perturbations of one state coordinate at relative 10⁻⁷ at the intended fork tick. A case that fails the
      screen is discarded and counted: a marginal no-cause failure belongs to the chaotic set, not here.
   Expected: every contrast `none`, outcome `none`, no factor named, ρ₀ at most 0.2 (in fact 0 on a robust case).
5. **Controls** (cases whose right answer is *not* abstention, so that an attributor that always abstains fails):
   - 10 host cases of the same map over five iterates with one real factor that removes the failure: expected
     ρ₀ = 0 and that factor `but-for`;
   - 10 copies of no-cause cases with F* added to the declared list: expected F* named `but-for`;
   - 10 non-chaotic worlds with a quadruped standing still: a placebo flips nothing (ρ₀ = 0).
6. **Registry.** `crates/frob-conformance/registry/autopsy-plants-abstention.toml`: per case its kind,
   identities, base address, seed, the planted factor (for no-cause), the declared factors, the verification
   runs with verdicts and margins, the intended fork tick, the expected outcome and the expected ρ₀ band. The
   file's BLAKE3 hash is committed before any attribution result on these cases exists. Discarded draws are
   listed with reasons.
7. **Honesty checks.** Expected outcomes are derived from the verification runs by a function of this bead. For
   chaotic cases the generator itself measures the flip fraction of 64 perturbed re-runs with plain simulation
   (no attribution code) and keeps the case only if the fraction is at least 0.3, so that a case sitting near
   the 0.2 threshold cannot make the gate a coin toss.

Modules: `crates/frob-conformance/src/plants/autopsy_abstention.rs`; the probe atom in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/plants/chaos_probe.rs`, compiled only under the conformance feature.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registry holds 100 chaotic cases (50 host, 50 simulated), the knife-edge fixture, 100 no-cause cases
      (20 per cause class) and the 30 controls, and its hash is committed.
- [ ] For every chaotic case the generator's own flip fraction over 64 perturbed plain re-runs is at least 0.3
      and is recorded.
- [ ] For every no-cause case: removing F* passes; none of the 10 singles and none of the 45 pairs of declared
      factors passes; the robustness screen holds.
- [ ] Re-running the verification runs from the registry reproduces every recorded verdict bitwise on the
      planting tier.
- [ ] The probe atom is absent from every build without the conformance feature (a build test).
- [ ] The five-iterate control has ρ₀ = 0 computed from first principles (amplification 32 moves the iterate by
      at most 6.4 × 10⁻¹¹), and its real factor removes the failure.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/autopsy_plants_abstention.rs`:
- `doubling_amplification`: 2⁴⁰ = 1,099,511,627,776; a shift of 10⁻¹² becomes 1.0995; 2⁵ = 32.
- `host_chaotic_flip_fraction`: over the 50 host cases, each has a plain-simulation flip fraction in [0.35, 0.65].
- `probe_atom_definition`: for joint angles summing to 3.25 rad, y₀ = 0.25 and y₁ = 0.5, y₂ = 0; the probe at
  two iterates would be violated at k = 1 and not at k = 2 (checks the fixed-point doubling and the threshold).
- `probe_is_binding_in_every_kept_case`.
- `knife_edge_sign_decides`: on the referee, a release at +10⁻¹³ rad ends positive; with −10⁻¹² rad added it ends
  negative; with +10⁻¹² rad added it ends positive.
- `no_cause_verification_runs`: F* removed passes; 10 singles and 45 pairs fail, for each of the 100 cases.
- `no_cause_robustness_screen`.
- `controls_are_not_abstentions`: the three control kinds have expected outcomes other than abstention.
- `registry_recomputes`; `probe_absent_without_feature`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_coalitions_abstention.sh` (N27a-18) reads this registry and runs the attribution verbs on
every case. The planting itself is reproduced by `cargo test -p frob-conformance autopsy_plants_abstention`,

seed-explicit and produces the identical registry twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- The simulated chaotic cases use an artificial amplifier because physical chaos over a two-second fork window
  is not strong enough to be a reliable plant: at the published closed-loop exponent of 0.16 per control step,
  100 control steps grow a perturbation by about 9 × 10⁶, which turns 10⁻¹² into 10⁻⁵, far below most margins. The
  amplifier makes the planted property exact. Real marginal failures are the single-precision lane's subject.
- A no-cause case in which some unplanted pair of declared factors does rescue would be a true joint cause, and
  a correct attributor would name it. Step 4.3 is what excludes that; without it the gate would punish
  correct behaviour.
- The gate's row says "100 of 100" once; this bead supplies 100 cases of each kind so that each kind can be
  held to 100 of 100 separately.
- Cost: no-cause planting is about 100 × (7 bisection runs + 1 + 55 + 16) ≈ 8,000 episodes on the CPU tier, run
  once and cached by content hash.
