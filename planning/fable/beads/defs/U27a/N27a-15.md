## Background

When a design parameter is changed and the failing worlds are re-run from the episode start, something has to
drive the edited robot. The policy's reaction is part of the effect of an edit: a change of gear ratio changes
what torque a given command produces, and a controller that has never seen the new body may fail on it for
reasons that have nothing to do with whether the edit is a good repair. If that failure were recorded as "the
edit does not help", the edit would be blamed for the controller's ignorance.

DESIGN: the controller must be able to act on the edited body without retraining. Three kinds can: the baseline
controller re-synthesized from the edited model, a family policy (one policy pretrained over a class of
designs), or a sampling controller (a predictive controller that plans by forking worlds of whatever model it is
given). **A policy trained for the original body alone is refused for design factors (FROB-REF-587)**, since its
failure on the new body would be attributed to the edit.

The same section of the plan keeps one escape hatch. Applying a design change to a mid-episode state answers a
different question from "would this design have avoided the failure": it asks what happens to a robot that lived
its history under the old design and is then changed. That is usually the wrong experiment, and a structural or
design-parameter intervention is never applied to a mid-episode state (INV-AUTOPSY-03: no structural or design-
parameter intervention starts from a mid-episode state, except in the labelled diagnostic mode). But it is
occasionally the question someone means to ask (what if the gearbox were swapped on a hot motor?), so a **forced
fork** of a design parameter remains available in a diagnostic mode, labelled as such.

ADR-AUTOPSY-06 governs both halves: **diagnostic interventions are not repairs.** Switching a torque limit off
locates a cause; it is not an edit anyone can build. The two kinds are typed apart, and a forced fork is on the
diagnostic side of that line. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the eligibility decision for a (controller, edit) pair; FROB-REF-587 with its repairs; the rule that keeps
the pair of arms comparable when the controller kind is switched; the admission rule and labelling of the
diagnostic forced fork.
Out: the re-run itself (BD-283); controller synthesis, family policies and the sampling controller (their own
beads); restoring state under an edited model (the hook of N27a-05); the verb flags (`--diagnostic-force` on
`autopsy.intervene`, BD-279, which applies this bead's admission rule and class; `--controller` on
`autopsy.rerun`, BD-283); the rule that a `diagnostic-forced` child is never labelled (N27b-06).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** The parent run's controller reference (a `ControllerId` of a synthesized baseline, or a `PolicyId`
   with its `frob.policy/1` manifest, or a sampling-controller configuration) and the edit's model difference
   (the invalidation set the typed edit returns: which compiled products change, and whether the `ModelId`
   changes).
2. **Decision table** `eligibility(controller, diff, override) → Eligible { plan } | Refused(587)`.

   | Controller of the parent run | Edit changes the `ModelId` | Decision |
   |---|---|---|
   | Any | No (for example a rename, a budget, a symmetry assertion) | Keep the controller unchanged |
   | Baseline controller synthesized from the model | Yes | Re-synthesize from the edited model with the same synthesis configuration; record `resynthesized: true` and the new `ControllerId`. If synthesis does not succeed for the edited model, the re-run is refused with the synthesis verb's own outcome (not FROB-REF-587) |
   | Family policy | Yes | Admit if the edited design is inside the class the policy was pretrained on and every channel of its mapping table binds by role on the edited model (roles, dimensions, signs, units and rates are matched by role, never by position); otherwise FROB-REF-587 |
   | Sampling controller | Yes | Admit, with the edited model as its planning model and the same planning configuration |
   | Specialist policy (trained on the original body, no family, `donor` null or a single-design lineage) | Yes | FROB-REF-587 |
   | The controller is itself what the edit changes (gains, controller memory, policy weights) | n/a | Admit: the edited controller is the factor |

3. **FROB-REF-587.** Meaning: a design-parameter re-run with a policy that cannot act on the edited body without
   retraining. Refusal block: `pointer` the controller reference; `observed` the policy's `trained_on` design;
   `required` "a controller that acts on the edited body without retraining"; `retry: after-change`; exit 3.
   Ranked repairs, in the order of the refusal registry row (N27-REF):
   1. `--controller resynth`: run both designs under baseline controllers synthesized per design;
   2. `--controller family:<policy>`: name a family policy whose class contains both designs;
   3. `--controller mpc`: run both designs under the sampling controller;
   4. inherit or retrain the policy for the edited design and compare the two systems with `eval.compare`,
      declaring the controller arm (that is a study, not an autopsy).
   Until a family policy exists, repair 2 is printed with the note that no registered family policy covers
   the two designs, so an agent passes to repair 3 without a wasted call.
4. **Keeping the pair comparable.** If the controller kind used for the edited design differs from the kind that
   produced the recorded failure (an override, or a repair above), the *original* design is re-run under the new
   kind as well, on the same world addresses, and the failing set is re-established under it. Worlds that no
   longer fail under the new controller are reported as `not-reproduced-under-controller` and are excluded from
   the rescued count and included in the totals. Reason: otherwise the comparison confounds the change of body
   with a change of controller. The report's `controller_arm` says which case holds: `unchanged`,
   `resynthesized-per-design`, `family:<id>`, `sampling`, each a declared arm in the sense used by design
   comparisons (a shared controller isolates the change of body; controllers adapted with equal resources compare
   the best systems).
5. **The diagnostic forced fork.** `forced_fork_admissible(diff) → Admitted | Refused(582) | NotNeeded`.
   - *Admitted* when the edit leaves the kinematic structure and the collision geometry digests unchanged: a
     gear ratio, an actuator card parameter, a controller gain, a mass or inertia value. The restored state is
     then a point of the edited model's state space. Contact memory is still discarded and the first solve runs
     cold with the raised iteration cap; history-carrying state (winding temperature, battery state, filter and
     policy states) is carried, which is the point of the mode.
   - *Refused* with FROB-REF-582 when the structure or the geometry changes (a wheel radius, an added limb, a
     moved mount): the copied configuration is not a configuration of the edited model.
   - The child carries the fork label `ForcedFork` of the fork vocabulary and the class `diagnostic-forced`
     in the fork ledger and on the wire (the spelling the intervention verb BD-279, the label function N27b-06
     and the auditor N27b-20 read). Its outcome is printed in a block of its own
     (`diagnostic_forced`), with the sentence "this is the continuation of a robot that lived its history under
     the original design; it does not answer whether the edited design avoids the failure". It never contributes
     a label, never enters a named set, a repair set or a `next_action` as a repair, and the controller-
     eligibility rule above applies to it unchanged.
6. **Types.**

   ```rust
   pub enum ControllerPlan { Keep, Resynthesize { config: SynthConfigId }, Family { policy: PolicyId },
                             Sampling { config: PlannerConfigId }, EditedController }
   pub struct Eligibility { plan: ControllerPlan, arm: ControllerArm, rerun_original: bool }
   ```
   Module `crates/frob-autopsy/src/eligibility.rs`. Pure functions of manifests and digests: class D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each row of the decision table is exercised and returns the stated decision.
- [ ] A specialist policy with a gear-ratio edit is refused with FROB-REF-587, exit 3, and the four ranked
      repairs in order.
- [ ] A family policy is admitted for a design inside its class and refused with FROB-REF-587 for one outside
      it or one whose mapping table has an unbound role.
- [ ] When the controller kind is switched, the original design is re-run under the new kind and worlds that no
      longer fail are reported under `not-reproduced-under-controller`; the rescued count excludes them.
- [ ] A forced fork is admitted for a gear-ratio edit and labelled; it is refused with FROB-REF-582 for a wheel-
      radius edit.
- [ ] A forced child's outcome appears only in `diagnostic_forced`; a property test over generated attributions
      shows it in no label, named set, repair set or repair action.
- [ ] INV-AUTOPSY-03 as a property: every child whose change set is structural either starts at tick 0 or
      carries the class `diagnostic-forced`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_rerun.sh` (N27a-17) exercises the refusal from the user's side (a specialist policy with
`--edit 'regear *.knee 16'`), each ranked repair, and the forced fork on the thermal fixture; it logs the
refusal block, the `controller_arm` and the `diagnostic_forced` block.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-675's negative control: a tool that reports the forced fork's result as the answer to the design question;
here the control is that the forced child's verdict can reach no label or repair. CX-071: a design-parameter
change tested by forking at a snapshot just before the failure; the rule protected includes "a fork is refused
when the controller cannot adapt without retraining". CX-298. Planted defects the tests must catch: eligibility
that admits a specialist policy (a planted failure of the policy on the new body is then reported as the edit
not helping); a switched controller without re-running the original design; a forced fork whose result is
merged into the contrasts. Determinism lanes: decisions are identical twice, in debug and release.

## Considerations

- A baseline controller re-synthesized for the edited body may be better or worse tuned than the original was
  for its body. That is why the result of a re-run is labelled `Estimated` and why a verdict on the two designs
  comes from a study with a declared controller arm, not from the autopsy.
- Family policies arrive at a later milestone than this bead. Until one exists the family row is tested on a
  synthetic manifest and marked not yet exercised on a real policy in the contract's known gaps.
- Mass and inertia edits are admitted for a forced fork by the structure-and-geometry rule, although the
  restored momenta then belong to another mass; the label covers it, and the printed sentence says what the
  experiment is.
