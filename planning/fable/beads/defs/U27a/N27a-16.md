## Background

An agent wants to know whether a different gear ratio would have prevented a thermal trip. Applying the new ratio
one second before the trip answers a different question: what happens to a motor that was heated by the old
ratio's currents and is then geared differently. Winding temperature, battery state of charge, wear, estimator
bias, a policy's hidden state and the contact memory all carry the history of the old design, and none has a
canonical image under the edit. This is the reason design-parameter interventions are re-run from the episode
start (ADR-AUTOPSY-03: history-dependent state would otherwise answer a different question).

The claim is carried by one hand-checkable counterexample, and this bead turns it into a fixture that can fail.

**The counterexample.** One thermal node with time constant 60 s, ambient 25 °C and a trip at 115 °C. At a gear
ratio of 12 the steady-state rise for the task's torque is 192 K, so the motor trips at 60·ln(192/102) = 37.95 s
of a 45 s task. Heating scales with the inverse square of the ratio, so at 16 the rise is 108 K.

| Experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| Ratio 16 from the episode start | Peak temperature 25 + 108·(1 − e^(−0.75)) = 82.0 °C at 45 s. The task passes with 33 K in hand |
| Ratio 16 applied at 36.95 s, one second before the trip | The winding is already at 113.3 °C and still heading for 133 °C. It trips at 42.4 s. The fork says the edit does not prevent the failure |

The fork is not wrong about the experiment it ran. It ran the wrong experiment.

GATE-AUTOPSY-06 (claim: Known answer; milestone M3; TARGET): **the thermal counterexample: all four numbers within
0.1 s and 0.1 K; the mid-episode request is refused and the re-run passes.** The four numbers are 37.95 s,
113.3 °C, 42.4 s and 82.0 °C. Tier: Solid. The fixture is `synthetic`: explicit values chosen so that the closed
form holds, registered so that an implementer can build it and a result can falsify it.

## Technical approach

1. **Derivation** (`docs/derivations/thermal-regear.md`). One node with heat capacity C and thermal resistance
   R_th to ambient T_a: C·dT/dt = P − (T − T_a)/R_th, time constant τ = R_th·C. For constant loss P the rise
   above ambient is ΔT(t) = ΔT_ss·(1 − e^(−t/τ)) + ΔT(0)·e^(−t/τ) with ΔT_ss = P·R_th. A winding of resistance R
   carrying current i dissipates P = R·i². Holding an output torque τ_L through a lossless gear of ratio N with
   torque constant k_t needs i = τ_L/(N·k_t), so P ∝ 1/N² and ΔT_ss(16) = ΔT_ss(12)·(12/16)² = 192 × 0.5625 =
   108 K.
   - Trip time at ratio 12, from ambient: 192·(1 − e^(−t/60)) = 90 ⇒ t = 60·ln(192/102) = 37.951 s.
   - Peak at ratio 16 from the start: 25 + 108·(1 − e^(−45/60)) = 25 + 108 × 0.527633 = 81.984 °C; margin to the
     trip 33.0 K.
   - Temperature at 36.95 s under ratio 12: 25 + 192·(1 − e^(−36.95/60)) = 113.283 °C.
   - Forced fork: from 113.283 °C the node relaxes toward 25 + 108 = 133 °C: T(t) = 133 − 19.717·e^(−(t − 36.95)/60);
     it reaches 115 °C at t = 36.95 + 60·ln(19.717/18) = 42.415 s.
2. **Reference values** (computed by the derivation, committed as constants with their formulas; tolerance of
   the gate in brackets): trip 37.951 s [±0.1 s]; temperature at the fork 113.283 °C [±0.1 K]; forced-fork trip
   42.415 s [±0.1 s]; peak from the start 81.984 °C [±0.1 K].
3. **Simulated realization** `fixture:thermal-regear` (DESIGN; every value explicit).

   | Part | Value |
   |---|---|
   | Mechanism | One revolute joint with a horizontal axis; a rigid massless arm of 1.000 m carrying a point mass of 12/9.81 = 1.22324 kg, held horizontal: gravity torque 12.000 N·m at g = 9.81 m/s² |
   | Actuator | Torque constant 0.1 N·m/A; winding resistance 0.96 Ω with its temperature coefficient set to zero in this fixture; inductance 1 mH; no current limit below 20 A; lossless gear; no joint friction |
   | Thermal network | One node: heat capacity 30 J/K, thermal resistance 2.0 K/W to ambient at 25 °C (τ = 60 s); initial winding temperature 25 °C; trip at 115 °C, absorbing, failure class `thermal-limit` |
   | Design A | Gear ratio 12: motor torque 1.000 N·m, current 10.00 A, loss 96.0 W, steady rise 192 K |
   | Design B | Design A after the typed edit `regear` to ratio 16: motor torque 0.750 N·m, current 7.50 A, loss 54.0 W, steady rise 108 K |
   | Controller | The baseline joint controller holding the horizontal pose, re-synthesized per design; the settled reset pose carries the holding current, so the loss is at its steady value from the first tick |
   | Task | Hold for 45 s. Requirement: the winding temperature stays below 115 °C and the arm stays within 2° of horizontal, always. Clocks: physics 1/2000 s, requirement 1/100 s |
   | World | A single world; no randomization is needed, and the address is fixed in the registry |

   Checks of the realization against the closed form: the electrical time constant L/R ≈ 1 ms and the 10 ms
   requirement tick are both far inside the 0.1 s tolerance; with a zero temperature coefficient the loss is
   constant, which is the closed form's assumption.
4. **What the fixture must show**, each a row of the registry entry with its expected value:
   1. Design A from the start: verdict violated, failure class `thermal-limit`, failure time 37.95 s.
   2. A fork point one second before the failure: tick 36.95 s; winding temperature in the checkpoint 113.3 °C.
   3. A request to apply `gear_ratio := 16` at that fork point: refused with FROB-REF-581, earliest valid fork
      tick 0, forcing fields including the actuator thermal state.
   4. The same request in the labelled diagnostic mode (a forced fork): the child trips at 42.4 s; the block
      that reports it says it is not the answer to the design question.
   5. Design B re-run from the episode start on the same address: verdict satisfied, peak temperature 82.0 °C
      at 45 s.
5. **Variant that is not a known answer.** With the card's real temperature coefficient of resistance the loss
   grows with temperature and no row above holds; the fixture pins the coefficient to zero and says so in its
   card provenance. A second fixture variant with the copper coefficient (`thermal-regear/copper/`, header kind
   `recorded`) exists only as a regression record (values captured, labelled `Estimated`, never cited by the
   gate or by a known-answer row).
6. **Files and the known-answer row.** The fixture is `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/autopsy/thermal-regear/`
   (header kind `synthetic`): the design files of A and B, the task file, and `scenario.toml` with the five checks
   of step 4, their expected values and tolerances, the reference constants with their formulas and the
   derivation's hash. The known-answer row is KA-675 of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml`,
   entered by N27-KA with `fixture = "thermal-regear"` and `derivation = "docs/derivations/thermal-regear.md"`.
   This bead writes no registry row and no registry file of its own (a chapter's private registry file is a
   lint failure, STD-TEST); a test holds the scenario's numbers to the row's truth and tolerance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The derivation file exists; a test evaluates its four formulas in exact-to-double arithmetic and obtains
      37.951 s, 113.283 °C, 42.415 s and 81.984 °C to three decimals.
- [ ] On the referee and on the CPU tier in double precision, design A's winding temperature follows
      25 + 192·(1 − e^(−t/60)) within 0.05 K at every requirement tick up to the trip, and trips within 0.1 s of
      37.95 s.
- [ ] Design B from the start peaks within 0.1 K of 82.0 °C at 45 s and passes.
- [ ] The checkpoint at 36.95 s holds a winding temperature within 0.1 K of 113.3 °C.
- [ ] The fixture's card declares the zero temperature coefficient with provenance `synthetic`, and a test shows
      that with the copper coefficient the trip time differs by more than the gate's tolerance (so the pin is
      load-bearing and visible).
- [ ] The fixture's `scenario.toml` carries its `synthetic` header and the five checks with their tolerances;
      its four reference numbers and its tolerances equal KA-675's truth and tolerance in the known-answer
      registry, and the derivation hash it records is the hash of the committed derivation file.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/thermal_regear.rs`:
- `closed_form_values`: the four reference numbers; also 192 × (12/16)² = 108 and 115 − 81.984 = 33.016.
- `steady_state_losses`: 96.0 W and 54.0 W; rises 192 K and 108 K.
- `design_a_trajectory`: sampled at 5, 10, 20, 30 s: 40.35, 54.48, 79.43, 100.55 °C (25 + 192·(1 − e^(−t/60))),
  each within 0.05 K.
- `design_a_trip_time`; `design_b_peak`; `checkpoint_temperature`.
- `copper_coefficient_breaks_the_closed_form`.
- `mass_gives_12_newton_metres`: 1.22324 kg × 9.81 m/s² × 1.000 m = 12.000 N·m.
- `scenario_matches_ka_675`: the scenario file carries the kind header STD-TEST requires (`synthetic`), and its
  numbers and tolerances equal the KA-675 row read through the known-answer loader (N38a-12); a copy of the
  scenario with the forced-fork trip changed to 42.6 s fails the test, naming the row.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_rerun.sh` (N27a-17) runs the five rows from the user's side; it logs each measured number
beside its reference value and tolerance, and the gate row is recorded from the receipt of the gate's tier run
by a session that did not implement the re-run, as N27a-17 states.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-675: thermal re-run, one thermal node, time constant 60 s, ambient 25 °C, trip at 115 °C, steady-state rise
192 K at gear ratio 12 and 108 K at 16, 45 s task: trip at 60·ln(192/102) = 37.95 s; ratio 16 from the start
peaks at 25 + 108·(1 − e^(−0.75)) = 82.0 °C; ratio 16 applied at 36.95 s finds the winding at 113.3 °C and trips
at 42.4 s; tolerance 0.1 s and 0.1 K; tier: referee; negative control: a tool that reports the forced fork's
result as the answer to the design question. CX-298 and CX-071: "apply a design edit at a mid-episode fork to
see whether it prevents the failure". Planted defects the tests must catch: a thermal integrator with a 1%
error in the time constant (the trip moves by about 0.4 s); heating scaled with 1/N instead of 1/N² (the rise at
16 becomes 144 K and design B would trip only at 58.8 s, outside the task, with a peak of 101.0 °C at 45 s instead of
82.0 °C). Determinism lanes: the trajectories are bitwise repeatable on each tier and agree between the referee
and the CPU tier within the stated tolerances.

## Considerations

- The fixture is a holding task with no contact, so nothing but the thermal state carries history. That is
  deliberate: the counterexample isolates one history-carrying state. The general statement (battery, wear,
  filter bias, policy memory, contact memory) is covered by the classifier's forcing fields.
- If the actuator model's thermal network cannot represent a single node (only a multi-node network), the
  fixture uses a network whose other nodes have negligible capacity and the realization check of step 3 is the
  acceptance test for that reduction.
- The same fixture serves the actuator chapter's sustained-duty gate as a cross-reference; it is run once.
