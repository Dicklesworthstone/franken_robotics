## Background

Every mechanism of the counterfactual debugger is established technique except one claim: that an agent repairs
designs in fewer turns with these tools than with logs. That claim is a HYPOTHESIS with its own gate.

GATE-AUTOPSY-08 (claim: Design comparison; milestone M4; TARGET): **on the planted-defect designs of the corpus,
agents given Autopsy reach an accepted repair in at most one third of the serial turns of agents given logs and
`explain` alone, pre-registered, with an anytime-valid verdict.**

It is the third clause of the falsifier of the bet behind the subsystem (BET-23, "interventional autopsy is
stable enough to act on": falsified if top-1 accuracy on planted causes is below 90%; or the placebo flip rate
exceeds 0.2 on more than half of the reference failures; or an agent with autopsy needs more than one third of
the repair turns of an agent with logs). The program's list of levers gives the same figure as a TARGET (at
least 3 times fewer repair turns than an agent given logs, on planted causes) and notes that no published figure
for agent repair turns exists.

Two things are stated in advance. **The ceiling:** the debugger's output is an accuracy, not a time. It can
shorten a design decision only by removing agent turns spent on wrong repairs, so its ceiling is the share of
turns that diagnosis takes in the phase ledger of a design loop. **The consequence of failure:** if the gate
fails, the tools stay, because they are cheap and exact, and the claim about turns is withdrawn. There is no kill
gate on this result.

A comparison of methods is admitted as evidence only if six parts are written down before the first run:
ceiling, competing arms, potency control, paired lower bound, pre-declared fallback and reachability
pre-analysis. This bead writes them, measures the ceiling, and registers the experiment. The reasons for the
rule are on record: a test whose ceiling is below its success threshold cannot confirm anything; an arm against
a weak baseline measures the baseline; an experiment that cannot see a planted effect is silent about a real
one. Tier: the experiment tests a hypothesis about a Solid mechanism.

## Scope

In: the protocol document; the unit and the pairing; the arms and how their tool surfaces are enforced; the
definition of a serial turn and of an accepted repair; the ceiling measurement on a development subset; the
potency controls; the statistical rule and its pre-analysis; the registration record and its hash.
Out: running the registered experiment and recording the gate (BD-375); the corpus (BD-248); the surface A/B of
the studio as a whole, which is another gate with its own bead; the composed-incumbent arm, which belongs to the
program's ten-fold protocol.

## Technical approach

1. **Units.** The planted-defect designs of the design corpus: designs with a planted defect of known kind (an
   undersized knee actuator, an inertia that breaks static stability, a wheelbase too long for an aisle, and the
   corpus's other kinds), each with a task on which it fails and labels frozen before any analysis was tuned.
   The corpus is split by a seeded draw, stratified by defect kind, into a development subset (one quarter, for
   the ceiling and the potency checks) and a test subset (the rest; at least 30 designs, or the gate is recorded
   `not run: too few units`). A campaign is one (design, agent seed) pair; each test design gets 2 agent seeds.
2. **Arms** (each campaign runs every arm; order randomized and counterbalanced; a fresh context per campaign;
   one agent model at one pinned revision; equal token and tool-call caps; no human in the loop).

   | Arm | Surface | Role |
   |---|---|---|
   | A | The full agent surface, including `run.fork` and the five autopsy verbs | The mechanism |
   | B | The same surface without `run.fork` and the autopsy verbs: logs, telemetry, `explain`, simulation, design edits, comparison | The strongest conventional alternative at equal resources |
   | F | Arm B plus `autopsy.rerun` only | The mechanism's own fallback (single-factor paired re-runs from the episode start); reported, not part of the verdict |
   | O | Arm B with the planted defect stated in the brief | Potency control: a planted effect the experiment must detect |
   | B′ | Arm B again with another agent seed | A/A control: a difference the experiment must not find |

   Enforcement: each arm's session token carries only its scopes, and the harness's tool allowlist for the arm is
   part of the registration; a call outside the allowlist is a refusal the agent sees. An incumbent with
   restorable state and a thin layer could host the same method; that arm is run by the ten-fold protocol and is
   named here only so that nobody reads this gate as a claim about the engine.
3. **The task given to the agent.** "This design fails this task. Find a repair: a sequence of typed design
   edits after which the design meets the task. Submit when you believe it does." The brief, the documentation
   (`robot-docs`) and the budget are identical across arms except for the lines that list available tools.
4. **Serial turn.** One model response that reads results and emits the next requests. Parallel tool calls
   inside one response are one turn. Turns are counted from the first response to the response that makes the
   accepted submission. A campaign with no accepted submission within the cap (DESIGN: 40 turns, or the token or
   compute budget, whichever comes first) is censored at the cap and counted as the cap.
5. **Accepted repair.** Decided by the harness, never by the agent: (a) the submission is a sequence of typed
   design edits, none of them a diagnostic factor; (b) `design check` passes on the edited design; (c) on sealed
   world clusters that no session of the campaign could read, the exact one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on
   the requirement pass rate is at least the task's threshold. A rejected submission costs the turn and the
   campaign continues.
6. **Ceiling** (measured before registration, on the development subset, arm B only). From the transcripts, each
   turn is tagged by phase; the diagnosis share s is the fraction of turns before the accepted submission that
   were spent locating the defect or testing a repair that was later abandoned. If every such turn were removed,
   the turn ratio would be 1 − s. The gate's threshold is a ratio of 1/3, so the hypothesis is confirmable only if
   s ≥ 2/3. The protocol records s with an exact interval. If the upper end of the interval for 1 − s is above
   1/3 (the ceiling may be too low), the registration says so, and the experiment still runs as a measurement
   whose only reachable verdicts are "not confirmed" and "refuted".
7. **Statistic and rule.** For campaign j, with turns a_j in arm A and b_j in arm B (each in 1 … 40), the paired
   quantity is d_j = b_j/3 − a_j, bounded in [−39⅔, 12⅓]. Null hypothesis: E[d] ≤ 0 (arm A needs more than one
   third of arm B's turns on average). The verdict is anytime-valid: a betting e-process for the mean of a
   bounded variable, E_n = ∏ (1 + λ_j·d_j / 39⅔) with a predictable bet 0 ≤ λ_j ≤ 1 fixed by the registered rule,
   confirms when E_n ≥ 1/α with α = 0.05. Units are consumed in a registered order. Reported beside the verdict:
   the restricted mean turns per arm, the paired ratio with a lower and an upper confidence bound from the
   confidence sequence, the censoring counts, and the share of campaigns in which the first submitted repair was
   accepted. The reported quantity is a bound on the paired ratio, never a ratio of means alone.
8. **Potency and A/A.** Before registration, on the development subset: arm O against arm B must cross the
   threshold (the harness can see a three-fold effect when one exists); arm B′ against arm B must not, and its
   paired ratio's interval must contain 1. A harness that fails either is repaired before anything is registered.
9. **Reachability pre-analysis.** Using the development subset's turn distributions, a simulation of the rule
   gives the number of campaigns at which the e-process crosses with probability 0.8 under declared alternatives
   (true ratios 1/5 and 1/4) and the probability that it never crosses within the test subset. If the test
   subset is too small for power 0.8 at ratio 1/5, the registration states that "confirmed" is reachable only
   with low probability.
10. **Pre-declared outcomes.** *Confirmed*: the e-process crosses; the turn claim may be stated with its bound.
    *Not confirmed*: the budget ends first; the claim is withdrawn; the tools stay. *Refuted*: the mirrored
    e-process for the null E[d] ≥ 0 crosses; the claim is withdrawn and the record says an agent with autopsy
    needed more than one third of the turns. In no case are the tools removed by this gate.
11. **Registration.** One record in the method-experiment register: the six parts, the unit lists with their
    split seed, the arm allowlists, the agent model and revision, the caps, the brief template's hash, the rule
    with its constants, the pre-analysis output, the ceiling measurement, and the hash of the corpus label file.
    The record's hash is committed before any test campaign runs; BD-375 refuses to start without it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol document and the registration record exist, the record validates against the register's
      schema, and its hash is committed before the first test campaign.
- [ ] All six parts are present; the register's admission check passes; a copy with the ceiling removed is
      rejected by the check.
- [ ] The ceiling s is measured on the development subset from at least 10 arm-B campaigns, with its exact
      interval, and the registration states which verdicts are reachable given it.
- [ ] The potency control is detected and the A/A control is not, on the development subset, with the numbers
      in the record.
- [ ] The pre-analysis reports the crossing budget at ratios 1/5 and 1/4 and the power within the test subset.
- [ ] The test subset has at least 30 designs, stratified by defect kind, disjoint from the development subset.
- [ ] Each arm's allowlist is enforced: a scripted call to `autopsy.attribute` from an arm-B session is refused.
- [ ] The turn counter and the acceptance check are implemented in the harness and unit-tested on recorded
      transcripts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/autopsy_ab_protocol.rs`:
- `turn_counter`: a recorded transcript with 7 responses, of which the 3rd contains four parallel tool calls and
  the 7th the accepted submission, counts 7.
- `censoring`: no accepted submission in 40 turns counts 40 and is flagged censored.
- `paired_quantity_bounds`: a = 1, b = 40 gives d = 12⅓; a = 40, b = 1 gives d = −39⅔.
- `e_process_constant_ratio`: ten pairs with a = 2, b = 12 (ratio 1/6) give d = 2 each; the e-process is
  increasing and the confidence sequence's upper bound on the ratio falls below 1/3; ten pairs with a = 4,
  b = 12 (ratio 1/3) give d = 0 and E stays at 1.
- `aa_does_not_cross`: 1,000 simulated A/A experiments from the development distribution cross in at most 5%.
- `acceptance_rejects_diagnostic_edits`; `acceptance_uses_sealed_clusters` (the agent's session cannot read the
  clusters: the read is refused).
- `split_is_seeded_and_stratified`.
- `allowlist_enforced`.
- `registration_admission`: the complete record is admitted; each of six records with one part removed is not.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_ab_dev.sh` runs the development-subset campaigns for arms B, O and B′ through the real
agent harness and the `frob-mcp` server, and writes the ceiling, potency and A/A figures. Log (newline-delimited
JSON under `target/e2e-logs/autopsy_ab_dev/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`, with a transcript beside it): `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N27a-19`), `step`, `event`; per campaign: design, defect kind, arm, agent seed, turns, censored, the
phase tags per turn, submissions with their acceptance results, token and tool-call counts against caps, the
session's scopes; metrics `diagnosis_share` with its interval, `potency_ratio` with bounds, `aa_ratio` with
bounds; a final `summary` line with assertion counts and `gates: ["GATE-AUTOPSY-08"]` marked `pre-registration`.
Sealed clusters and agent prompts' private content are not logged; transcripts are stored as artifacts and
referenced by hash.

### Known answers and negative controls
The arithmetic of the ceiling: with a diagnosis share s the best attainable ratio is 1 − s, so a threshold of 1/3
needs s ≥ 2/3; at s = 0.5 the best ratio is 0.5 and the hypothesis cannot be confirmed. The paired-bound check of
method experiments: ten pairs of constant ratio r must return the bound r. Negative controls: the A/A arm (must
not cross); a harness that counts tool calls instead of turns (the turn-counter test fails on the parallel-call
transcript); an acceptance check on worlds the agent could read (the sealed-read test fails). Determinism
lanes: the split, the allowlists and the rule are deterministic given the seed; agent behaviour is not, which is
why units are campaigns with seeds and the rule is statistical.

## Considerations

- The diagnosis share may well be below 2/3 on these tasks. Finding that out before the experiment is the point
  of the ceiling rule; the gate is then recorded as a measurement, and the turn claim is withdrawn without
  spending the full budget.
- Agent revisions change. The record pins one; a re-run on a later revision is a new registration.
- The surface A/B of the studio (structure against reward alone) uses the same harness and corpus; the two
  experiments share transcripts of arm B where their briefs coincide and are otherwise separate.
- Compute: a campaign is bounded by its caps; the test subset at 30 designs × 2 seeds × 3 arms is 180 campaigns.
  The budget request is made with the registration.
