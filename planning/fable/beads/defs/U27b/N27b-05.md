## Background

After `autopsy.intervene` has run its forks, each intervention has counts: how many of its replicates no longer
fail, how many placebo forks changed their verdict, how many replicates changed their verdict under a
perturbation alone. This bead turns counts into the three numbers Autopsy reports and into the one decision it
makes about each factor: may this factor be *named*.

**ADR-AUTOPSY-01. A cause is what an intervention removes, net of a placebo.** A factor is named only when
removing it prevents the failure more often than an irrelevant perturbation does. Rejected alternative: naming
any intervention that flips the outcome. On a system where every nudge flips the outcome (the doubling-map
counterexample), every intervention would be named. The placebo measures that floor, and a cause is whatever
beats it.

Invariant **INV-AUTOPSY-05**: a factor is named only when its contrast is tested above the placebo at a stated
level and the placebo flip rate is at most 0.2. The test is an exact one-sided test at the fixed replicate count,
or an e-process when forks are bought one at a time, with the level divided by the number of factors.

Over a set of failing worlds the rescue rate estimates the **probability of necessity**,
PN(c) = P(no failure under do(¬c) | failure). Tier Solid; crate `frob-autopsy`, layer 5; construction C-29;
decision core DC-06; bet BET-23; risk RISK-50 (attribution names a cause where none is stable).

## Scope

In: the three reported quantities per intervention and per coalition; v(∅); the exact one-sided test; the
e-process; the division of the level; the reachability pre-analysis of the fixed rule; the cluster-level
estimate of PN; exact arithmetic; a null calibration test.
Out: counting flips and rescues (N27b-03); the labels drawn from these statistics (N27b-06); coalition

## Technical approach

1. **Quantities.** For one failing world, an intervention c with R replicates of which k are rescued (the
   requirement is not violated), and P placebo forks of which f flipped:
   | Quantity | Definition |
   |---|---|
   | Rescue rate | r_c = k / R |
   | Placebo flip rate | ρ₀ = f / P |
   | Contrast | r_c − ρ₀ |
   | Flip rate under perturbation | The fraction of replicates whose verdict differs from the unperturbed fork of the same intervention, at 10⁻¹² and at 10⁻⁷ (the second is `null` when that series did not run) |
   The value of the empty coalition is the placebo rate: v(∅) = ρ₀. (This is what makes a pair's interaction
   v({i, j}) − v({i}) − v({j}) + v(∅) come out as −0.34 for rescue rates 1, 1 and 3/8 with ρ₀ = 2/64.)
   All rates are kept as exact rationals (integer numerator and denominator) and printed as decimals.
2. **The fixed-count test (DESIGN: Fisher's exact test, one-sided).** Null hypothesis: the rescue probability of
   do(c) is at most the placebo flip probability. With K = k + f successes among N = R + P forks,
   p = Σ over x from k to min(R, K) of C(R, x)·C(P, K − x) / C(N, K). The factor is *tested above the placebo*
   when p ≤ α / m, where m is the number of single factors in the family and α is the stated level (DESIGN
   default α = 1/20, recorded in every attribution). The family is the fork's: the caller passes the
   `family_size` of the set's ledger header, the distinct single factors run from the fork up to and including
   the request (BD-279), which for a first request is the number of its single factors; this module takes the
   divisor as given. Computed in exact integer arithmetic; the comparison
   p·m ≤ α is a comparison of rationals, so no rounding decides a label. The divisor is a parameter of the
   function: for single factors it is m; the coalition bead (BD-281) calls the same test for a coalition with
   the number of admissible coalitions of that size (sizes are separate families, each controlled at α).
   Worked values (R = 8, P = 64): 8 of 8 against 2 of 64 gives p = 3.76 × 10⁻⁹; 3 of 8 against 2 of 64 gives
   p = 0.00839, which passes at m = 3 (level 0.0167) and fails at m = 12 (level 0.00417).
3. **Reachability pre-analysis.** Before any fork runs, the rule is enumerated at its (R, P, m, α): for each
   possible f, the least k that is tested above the placebo, and the least k that also reaches a contrast of 0.5.
   Known table at R = 8, P = 64, α = 0.05:
   | f (placebo flips) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 6 | 13 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | least k, m = 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
   | least k, m = 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
   | least k, m = 12 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
   | least k with contrast ≥ ½ as well (every m above) | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 6 |
   A rule at which no k can pass is reported as `unreachable` and the request is not admitted without
   `--allow-unreachable` (the flag of `autopsy.intervene`, BD-279); the table's hash is stored with the
   attribution. Smallest cases at P = 64: R = 1 is unreachable at m = 12 (the best case, 1 of 1 against 0 of 64,
   has p = 1/65 ≈ 0.0154 > 0.05/12 ≈ 0.00417), while R = 2 is reachable at m = 12 (2 of 2 against 0 of 64 has
   p = 1/2145 ≈ 4.7 × 10⁻⁴). Reason: a frozen decision rule is enumerated before sampling, so that a verdict that
   cannot occur is never promised.
4. **The sequential test (forks bought one at a time).** Forks arrive in pairs: one replicate of do(c) with
   outcome X_i ∈ {0, 1} (rescued) and one placebo fork with outcome Y_i ∈ {0, 1} (flipped), with independent
   keyed perturbations. d_i = X_i − Y_i. The betting e-process is E_n = ∏ (1 + λ·d_i) with the fixed bet λ = ½
   (DESIGN). Under the null hypothesis E[d_i | past] ≤ 0, so E_n is a nonnegative supermartingale and, by
   Ville's inequality, the chance that it ever reaches 1/α′ is at most α′. The factor is tested above the placebo
   when E_n ≥ m / α. A pair (rescued, placebo unflipped) multiplies E by 1.5; the reverse pair halves it; equal
   outcomes leave it unchanged. Least number of clean pairs to cross: 8 at m = 1 (threshold 20), 11 at m = 3
   (60), 14 at m = 10 (200) and at m = 12 (240). Stopping: on crossing, or at the fork budget (not named).
5. **One type per attribution.** The ledger header of an intervention set declares `test: fixed` or
   `test: sequential` before a fork runs. Reading a fixed set sequentially, or computing a fixed p-value at the
   moment a sequential test stops, is refused (FROB-REF-553: a comparison that mixes study types, or re-reads
   fixed-n data as a sequential study; exit 3).
6. **The gate on naming.** `may_name(c) = (5·f ≤ P) ∧ tested(c)`: the first clause is ρ₀ ≤ 0.2 in integers
   (at P = 64, 12 flips pass and 13 do not).
7. **Clusters.** For a cluster of W failing worlds with one unperturbed fork of do(c) and one placebo fork per
   world: PN̂(c) = (worlds rescued under do(c)) / W, ρ₀ = (worlds whose placebo flipped) / W, and the same exact
   test with R = P = W. Worked card: of 200 failing worlds, removing the torque limit prevents the failure in
   164, nominal friction in 38 and a placebo in 12: rescue rates 0.82, 0.19 and 0.06, contrasts 0.76 and 0.13,
   p = 6.9 × 10⁻⁶⁰ and 5.9 × 10⁻⁵. With a placebo rate of 30 of 200 against 38 the test gives p = 0.176 and
   nothing is attributed.
8. **Types and layout (DESIGN).**
   ```rust
   pub struct ContrastStat { rescued: (u32, u32), placebo: (u32, u32), contrast: Ratio,
                             flip_rate: FlipRates, test: TestResult, level: Ratio, tested: bool }
   pub enum TestResult { Fixed { p: BigRatio }, Sequential { e: BigRatio, pairs: u32 } }
   ```
   `crates/frob-autopsy/src/stats.rs`, `preanalysis.rs`. Determinism class D0: integer and rational arithmetic
   only. Evidence: a contrast is a replayable experiment, class Tested on its tier.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every worked value of steps 2, 3, 4 and 7 is reproduced exactly (p-values to the printed digits, both rows
      of the table of least k, the unreachable case R = 1 and the reachable case R = 2 at m = 12, the pair counts
      8, 11, 14, 14).
- [ ] INV-AUTOPSY-05 as a property: over generated count tables, `named ⇒ tested ∧ ρ₀ ≤ 0.2`.
- [ ] Null calibration: over 10⁴ synthetic attributions with m = 10 factors whose rescue probability equals the
      placebo probability (0.05, 0.1 and 0.2), the share of attributions that name any factor is at most α = 0.05
      for the fixed test, and for the sequential test under optional stopping at every pair.
- [ ] A planted effect is detected: with rescue probability 0.8 against placebo 0.05 at R = 8, P = 64, m = 10,
      the factor is tested above the placebo in at least 95% of 10⁴ synthetic attributions.
- [ ] A mixed-type request is refused with FROB-REF-553; an unreachable rule is reported before any fork runs.
- [ ] No floating-point comparison decides `tested` (a test replaces the rational comparison with `f64` and shows
      a boundary case that flips).

### Known answers and negative controls
The worked card GB-34 (164, 38 and 12 of 200; contrasts 0.76 and 0.13; a placebo of 30 of 200 leaves nothing to
attribute). CX-070 (an intervention that flips the outcome is not thereby a cause) and CX-300. Negative controls:
a test that ignores the placebo (names the factor at 38 against 30); a level that is not divided by m (the null
calibration with ten factors exceeds α: expected family-wise rate about 1 − 0.95¹⁰ = 0.40); a sequential test
that recomputes a fixed p-value at stopping (the calibration under optional stopping exceeds α). Determinism:
exact arithmetic, identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- Fisher's test treats the intervention and placebo forks as two independent samples. In the cluster form the
  two forks of one world are paired, and an exact test on discordant worlds would be sharper. It is not built:
  one rule for both forms is easier to audit. Revisit with the pilot's data if the cluster test proves too
  conservative to reach 90% on planted causes.
- The level α and the bet λ are DESIGN defaults recorded in the decision policy of the attribution core;
  changing either is a new policy version.
- OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 (replicate counts) changes R and P and therefore the reachability table; the table is recomputed
  and its hash changes, which is intended.
- Exact binomial coefficients for clusters of a few thousand worlds need arbitrary-precision integers; the cost
  is negligible beside one fork.
