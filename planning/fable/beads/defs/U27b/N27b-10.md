## Background

Autopsy's mechanisms are Solid (established technique applied carefully), but the Autopsy specification still names a pilot,
because one question is open and cheap to answer before money is spent on the coalition machinery: on realistic
closed-loop failures, is attribution by forks accurate and stable at all, and do the cheap one-at-a-time
contrasts already give the answer that an exhaustive coalition analysis gives?

The pilot, as the program states it: the planted-cause test runs at paper scale before the coalition machinery
is funded: one trained quadruped policy on a development oracle outside the product or on the CPU referee, 200
planted failures in five cause classes with ten candidate factors, one-at-a-time contrasts against exact Shapley
over 1,024 coalitions, each repeated under 20 snapshot perturbations at 10⁻⁷ and at 10⁻¹². It takes about a day
on a 32-core machine. Its falsifier is the trigger of KILL-AUTOPSY, and it also answers whether ten forks
suffice.

The ceiling of the benefit is stated in advance: the output is an accuracy, not a time. It can shorten a design
decision only by removing agent turns spent on wrong repairs, so its ceiling is the share of turns that
diagnosis takes in the phase ledger of the design loop. If the later agent comparison fails, the tools stay,
because they are cheap and exact, and the claim about turns is withdrawn.

A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome that is not
known in advance; a pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot. A result obtained on the development oracle funds or
kills a work package and supports no gate; when the same pilot is re-run on the CPU referee and the verdict
differs, the referee's verdict stands.

## Scope

In: the pilot's frozen protocol, its small program (outside the product workspace, under `tools/pilots/`), the
registered record with falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool and pin, the run, and the recorded result with
its consequences.
Out: the product suite and gate (N27b-08, N27b-09); the kill decision (N27b-11); the decision on replicate
counts and on whether pairs are optional (N27b-13), which reads this pilot's result.

## Technical approach

1. **Register before running.** A record in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`:
   mechanism "interventional attribution on forks, with coalitions" (Solid; Autopsy); protocol hash; falsifier;
   ceiling; fallback and kill gate; cost; tool and pin; and, after the run, the result.
   - *Falsifier* (the trigger of KILL-AUTOPSY): top-1 accuracy on planted causes below 90%, or the top factor
     changes in more than 30% of cases under 10⁻⁷ perturbation.
   - *Ceiling*: the share of serial agent turns charged to the `diagnose` phase in the recorded design-loop
     ledger. If diagnosis is a share s of turns, removing every wrong-repair turn saves at most s of the turns;
     the record states s with the ledger it came from, or `unmeasured` with the consequence that no claim about
     turns may be made from this pilot.
   - *Fallback*: single-factor paired re-runs from the episode start, with the first-anomaly ordering labelled
     `Estimated`; coalitions and Shapley summaries are not built.
   - *Cost*: about one day on a 32-core machine (8.2 × 10⁶ forks of about two seconds at about 190
     world-seconds per second).
2. **Protocol (frozen by hash before execution).**
   1. Plants: 200 failures, 40 in each class (friction drop, torque cap, payload shift, latency, terrain step),
      on one quadruped under one trained policy; each with ten candidate factors of which one is the rescuer;
      each plant checked from the episode start to be a single cause (baseline passes, plant fails, no decoy
      rescues alone), with 20 further out-of-list controls.
   2. For each failure, restore a snapshot one second before the failure. Verify the empty intervention replays
      the failure exactly on the tool; a failure whose null replay is not exact is excluded and counted.
   3. Evaluate all 2¹⁰ = 1,024 coalitions of the ten factors from the snapshot (one fork each). From this table
      read: the one-at-a-time result (the ten singletons); the pairs table (55 coalitions); exact Shapley values
      φᵢ = Σ over S ⊆ N∖{i} of |S|!·(10 − |S| − 1)!/10! · [v(S ∪ {i}) − v(S)], with v(S) ∈ {0, 1} the rescue
      indicator of the fork; and 64 placebo forks for ρ₀.
   4. Repeat step 3 from 20 perturbed snapshots at relative 10⁻⁷ and from 20 at 10⁻¹² (one state coordinate, sign
      and size factor in [1, 2) by a keyed draw). That is 200 × 1,024 × 40 = 8,192,000 forks.
   5. Compute, per stability scale: top-1 accuracy of (a) one-at-a-time, (b) singles and pairs with the label
      rules, (c) the factor with the largest Shapley value; the share of (failure, perturbation) pairs whose top
      factor differs from the unperturbed table's; the agreement between (a) and (b), and between (a) and (c);
      ρ₀ per failure and the share of failures with ρ₀ above 0.2; the share of failures the label rules abstain
      on.
3. **Reading the result.**
   | Outcome | Consequence |
   |---|---|
   | Accuracy of (b) ≥ 90% and change rate ≤ 30% at 10⁻⁷ | The coalition work is funded; defaults stand |
   | Falsifier holds for (b) | The coalition work is not funded; the fallback ships; KILL-AUTOPSY is recorded as fired at the pilot |
   | (a) agrees with (b) in at least 95% of failures | Evidence that ten forks suffice: input to the decision that makes pairs optional |
   | More than half of failures have ρ₀ above 0.2 | The bet's second falsifier holds: most failures are unattributable at this lead; the lead ladder is re-examined at 0.25 and 0.5 s before any funding |
4. **Arithmetic.** Exact rational arithmetic for Shapley values (integer rescue counts; the values sum to
   v(N) − v(∅) exactly). The pilot program is small, has no dependency on product crates, and is kept with its
   pin under `tools/pilots/autopsy_planted_cause/`.
5. **Record.** The result, negative or positive, goes into the pilot register and the review log, and the
   mechanism's scope is revised to match. If the run used the development oracle, every figure is labelled
   `development-oracle` and the pilot is queued for re-run on the CPU referee when a quadruped steps there.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record exists with protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool and pin before the
      first fork is run (timestamps and commit order show it).
- [ ] The run reports the figures of step 2.5 with denominators, including exclusions for inexact null replay.
- [ ] Potency controls pass: a planted method that always names the rescuer scores 100%; the out-of-list controls
      name nothing under the label rules; on a synthetic table with a known joint pair the pairs arm finds it
      and the one-at-a-time arm does not.
- [ ] Shapley identities hold exactly on every table (efficiency: the values sum to v(N) − v(∅)).
- [ ] The result and its consequence (funded, or fallback) are written to the pilot register and the review log;
      the coalition bead is released or withdrawn accordingly.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In the pilot's own directory (plain tests, no product crates): `shapley_exact_small` (the two-factor tables:
joint and alternative both give (½, ½); the table v(∅) = 0, v({1}) = 1, v({2}) = 0, v({1, 2}) = 0 gives
(½, −½) summing to 0); `shapley_efficiency_random` (1,000 random 0/1 tables on five factors);
`top_change_rate_arithmetic`; `protocol_hash_stable`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`tools/pilots/autopsy_planted_cause/run.sh` (a pilot script, outside `scripts/e2e/` because it runs outside the
product). It writes newline-delimited JSON in the same line format as the end-to-end helper: `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N27b-10`), `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, tool and pin, seed, and `metric` lines
with thresholds: `accuracy_oat`, `accuracy_pairs` (0.90), `accuracy_shapley_top`, `top_change_rate_1e-7` (0.30),
`top_change_rate_1e-12`, `agreement_oat_pairs`, `share_rho0_above_0.2` (0.5), `excluded_null_replay`. A final
`summary` line gives the verdict against the falsifier.

### Known answers and negative controls
The hand-checkable Shapley tables above (CX-296, CX-297). KA-148 as the pilot's target (planted cause recovered
first in at least 90% of cases). Negative controls: the out-of-list set; an A/A check in which the "intervention"
is a second placebo (it must be named in at most 5% of failures). Determinism: the pilot is run twice on the
pinned tool; identical tables are required, and a tool that cannot repeat a fork exactly is unfit for the pilot
(recorded as such).

## Considerations

- OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 (default replicate count and perturbation sizes) is decided by this pilot. Until then: 8
  replicates at 10⁻¹², and 8 at 10⁻⁷ for interventions that change the outcome.
- The pilot uses single forks per coalition (v ∈ {0, 1}) and gets its stability from the 40 perturbed snapshots;
  the product uses replicates per coalition. Both protocols are named in their reports, and the pilot's change
  rate is the more demanding of the two.
- An incumbent that restores state but cannot replay one world outside its batch can still host this pilot with
  one world per batch; that cost is part of the record.
- Risk: the policy trained on the development oracle is unusually robust or unusually brittle. The record states
  the policy's recipe and return, and the referee re-run uses the reference policy.
