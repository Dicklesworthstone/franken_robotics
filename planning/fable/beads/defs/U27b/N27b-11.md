## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at the milestone the gate register names. A kill gate that fires is
not a failure of the program: the fallback is what the program then does.

**KILL-AUTOPSY** (decided at M3). Trigger: GATE-AUTOPSY-03 fails after one repair cycle: planted-cause accuracy
below 90%, or the top factor changes in more than 30% of cases at 10⁻⁷. Fallback: single-factor paired re-runs
from the episode start, with the first-anomaly ordering labelled `Estimated`; coalitions and Shapley summaries
are removed.

It guards bet **BET-23** (interventional autopsy is stable enough to act on; falsifier: top-1 accuracy on
planted causes below 90%; or the placebo flip rate above 0.2 on more than half of the reference failures, so
that most are unattributable; or an agent with autopsy needs more than one third of the repair turns of an agent
with logs) and risk **RISK-50** (attribution names a cause where none is stable; early signal: the placebo flip
rate on reference failures). The reason the gate exists: contact dynamics amplify small differences, so a top
factor can change under a perturbation at round-off scale; if it does so often, a ranked cause is noise with a
confident label, which costs an agent more than no answer. The strong form of the idea (a single top cause from
forks taken just before the failure, design parameters included) does not hold, and was narrowed to
interventional contrasts with a flip rate and a placebo, design parameters re-run from the start, and this kill
gate.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's result and the gate's measured figures; applying the trigger as written; recording the
decision as an ADR and in the gate ledger; if it fires, specifying exactly what is removed, what remains and
what each dependent bead does; if it does not fire, recording that the coalition machinery stands.
Out: producing the measurements (N27b-09, N27b-10); implementing the re-run verb the fallback uses (seed bead
BD-283) and the heuristic ordering (`explain.run`, seed bead BD-112).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** (a) The pilot record (N27b-10): accuracy of the pairs arm and the top-factor change rate at 10⁻⁷
   on the pilot's tool. (b) The gate row of GATE-AUTOPSY-03 after at most one repair cycle (N27b-09): accuracy
   over 200 planted failures in double precision, and the share of cases whose top factor changes at 10⁻⁷.
2. **Two decision points, one rule.**
   - *At the pilot (before the coalition work is funded).* If the pilot's falsifier holds, the gate is recorded
     as fired at the pilot: the coalition bead is not funded and the fallback ships. If the pilot ran on the
     development oracle and its falsifier holds, the pilot is re-run on the CPU referee as soon as a quadruped
     steps there, and the referee's verdict stands.
   - *At M3 (on the product).* Fired when, after one repair cycle, accuracy < 180 of 200, or the change rate
     > 30%. Not fired otherwise. The comparison is on the recorded counts; no rounding and no re-scoping of the
     suite, the thresholds or the candidate lists inside the cycle.
3. **If it fires: what changes downstream.**
   | Item | Change |
   |---|---|
   | `autopsy.intervene` | `--coalitions pairs` is removed; forks from snapshots remain for diagnostic and exogenous interventions and are labelled `Estimated`, without labels of cause |
   | `autopsy.attribute` | Reports per-factor rescue counts and flip rates as a description; `pairs`, `shapley`, and the labels `joint` and `alternative` are removed; no factor is labelled `but-for` from forks |
   | The primary answer to "which repair first" | `autopsy.rerun`: for each candidate edit, a paired re-run from the episode start on the same world addresses (failing worlds and a sample of passing ones), one factor at a time, compared by a declared study |
   | Ordering of candidates | The first-anomaly ordering of `explain.run`, labelled `Estimated` |
   | `autopsy.repair` | Returns single-factor repairs confirmed by re-run only; no shrinking over coalitions |
   | Coalition and Shapley bead | Closed as `killed: fallback adopted`; its code is not shipped |
   | The single-precision lane | Withdrawn with the fork-based attribution it accelerates; its kill gate is recorded as moot |
   | Agent comparison at M4 | Still run, on the fallback toolset, and reported whichever way it falls |
   | Reports and documentation | Autopsy is described by the name of the fallback: "single-factor paired re-runs from the episode start" |
4. **If it does not fire.** The ADR records the measured accuracy and change rate, the pilot's figures and the
   one-at-a-time agreement, and states that singles, pairs and the budgeted Shapley summary stand.
5. **The ADR** (`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUTOPSY.md`): the trigger as written; the measured values with the gate row
   and receipt hashes; the repair cycle used, if any, and what was repaired; the decision; the downstream
   changes; the date and the session that recorded it (not the session that implemented the attribution). The
   gate ledger row for KILL-AUTOPSY takes the status `pass` (not fired) or `killed: fallback adopted`.
6. **A trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated amendment** to the register, with its reason,
   made before the measurement it governs. This bead refuses to record a decision against thresholds that were
   edited after the first gate run (it compares the register's history with the gate row's commit).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with the measured values, cites the gate row and the pilot record, and states one of the
      two outcomes.
- [ ] The gate ledger shows KILL-AUTOPSY as decided at M3, and the M3 exit lists it among the kill gates
      decided, with its fallback: single-factor paired re-runs from the episode start.
- [ ] If fired: each row of the table in step 3 is carried out or filed as a bead change, the verb registry no
      longer advertises `--coalitions`, and the end-to-end session script passes on the fallback toolset.
- [ ] If not fired: the coalition bead's gate (labelling of planted joint and alternative causes) is unblocked.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a fresh-context session that did not implement the attribution.
- [ ] A dry run on synthetic gate rows produces the right decision at the boundary: 180 of 200 with 30% → not
      fired; 179 of 200 → fired; 180 of 200 with 31% → fired.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_autopsy.rs` (the decision rule as a pure function over gate rows):
`boundary_180_30_not_fired`, `accuracy_179_fired`, `change_31_percent_fired`, `second_cycle_refused` (a third
measurement after one repair cycle is rejected as input), `edited_threshold_refused` (register history newer
than the gate row).

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. It reads the log and gate row of `scripts/e2e/autopsy_attribute_planted.sh` and the pilot's
log; the ADR quotes their `summary` lines (accuracy with threshold 0.90, top-change rate with threshold 0.30) and
their receipt hashes. If the gate fires, `scripts/e2e/autopsy_session.sh` is re-run with `--toolset fallback` and
its log is attached.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-670 (accuracy at least 90%; the kill trigger below that). The boundary cases above are the negative controls
of the decision rule: a rule that rounds 179/200 = 89.5% up to 90% is caught. Determinism: the rule is exact
integer arithmetic.

## Considerations

- The second falsifier of BET-23 (placebo flip rate above 0.2 on more than half of the reference failures) is
  not part of this trigger. If it holds while accuracy on planted causes passes, the gate does not fire, the
  share of unattributable failures is reported in every campaign, and the fork lead ladder is re-examined; the
  ADR records that figure either way.
- The third falsifier (agent repair turns) is read at M4 by its own gate. If that gate fails, the tools stay,
  because they are cheap and exact, and the claim about turns is withdrawn; that is not a kill of this
  mechanism.
- The fallback costs more compute per question (a full episode from the start for failing worlds and a sample of
  passing ones, per candidate) and answers fewer kinds of question (no diagnostic interventions as causes), but
  every answer it gives is one a study can confirm.
- What would make us revisit after a kill: a measured Lyapunov exponent of our own closed loops well below the
  published 0.03 to 0.16 per control step, or a shorter fork lead at which stability passes.
