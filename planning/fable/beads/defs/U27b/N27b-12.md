## Background

Attribution is a decision problem, and the program writes each recurring decision down as a small policy with a
loss model, obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode, so that the rule which produced an answer
can be named and replayed. For attribution the decision model is:

```text
Decision:          Which repair edit should the agent try first for this failure?
Artifacts:         Typed interventions; a fork ledger; the placebo flip rate; a rescue rate per factor and
                   per pair; minimal repair sets; one label from the label table.
Obligations:       The empty intervention replays the failure bit for bit, so the starting point is exact.
                   Each intervention changes one declared mechanism (model difference on file).
                   A cause is named only when its contrast beats the placebo under a test at a stated level.
Failure modes:     Chaos makes every nudge look like a cause. The cause precedes the snapshot.
                   The failure does not survive double precision.
Fallback:          The first-anomaly ordering of the telemetry subsystem, labelled Estimated.
Fallback trigger:  Null-replay mismatch; placebo flip rate above 0.2; failed equivalence in double precision.
Budgeted mode:     12 factors, singles and pairs, 8 replicates, 64 placebo forks: 689 forks. A Shapley
                   summary only if no single and no pair changes the outcome.
Baseline:          The heuristic ordering; and one-at-a-time contrasts without coalitions, to measure
                   whether ten forks answer as well as seven hundred.
Rollback:          A configuration flag to the heuristic; recorded forks remain valid experiments.
Verification:      Planted single and joint causes; a planted chaotic failure; the thermal counterexample.
```

States: the failure has a single removable cause, a joint cause, or none that can be attributed. Actions: buy a
fork, name a factor or a set, or decline. Loss: naming a wrong cause sends an agent to edit the wrong thing,
which costs a design iteration and its compute; declining costs one fallback to the heuristic ordering. Naming a
cause in a chaotic regime sends the agent after noise, which costs more than saying nothing.

This is decision core **DC-06** (which cause, and which repair edit, ranks first after a failure; family:
counterfactual forks with a placebo; owner `frob-autopsy`; timescale: per failure cluster; tier Solid). It sits
in a one-way pipeline: failures found by the rare-failure core feed attribution, attributed modes feed the
incidence matrix of battery selection, and nothing flows back inside a campaign. Methods considered and not
built for this core, with the reasons kept so the question is not reopened without new evidence:
coalition-value attribution as the primary output (values can be negative and cannot establish necessity; kept
as a budgeted description); copy-on-write device state for forks (wrong bottleneck: copying 8,192 worlds at
30 kB is 0.25 GB); observational causal adjustment (exact intervention is available).

## Scope

In: `policy::attribute`: the stage machine and its constants; the compiled guard table; the fallback and how it
is delivered; the rollback flag; the two baseline arms and shadow mode; the evidence record per decision; the
policy's record for the decision-core registry with its demonstration manifest.
Out: the statistics, labels and precision routing it calls (N27b-05, N27b-06, N27b-07); coalition and Shapley
stages (BD-281); the abstention gate on planted chaotic and no-cause failures, GATE-AUTOPSY-05, with its planted
sets (seed bead BD-282, which measures this policy's `Decline` path); registry-wide checks of all decision cores
(seed beads BD-349, BD-350).

## Technical approach

1. **Stages (the whole policy).**
   | Stage | Does | Leaves when | To |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `Start` | Reads the null replay of the fork | Not bitwise | `Fallback(null-mismatch)` |
   | `Precision` | Reads the precision route | Not decision-equivalent in double precision and not admitted to the single-precision lane | `Fallback(precision)` |
   | `Placebo` | Reads ρ₀ from P placebo forks | 5·flips > P (ρ₀ above 0.2) | `Decline(chaotic)` with the fallback offered |
   | `Singles` | Rescue counts of at most 12 factors, R replicates each; exact test at level α/m | One factor rescues → `Name(factor)`; several → `Name(set, alternative)`; none → `Pairs` | terminal or `Pairs` |
   | `Pairs` | All pairs of the factors | A pair rescues → `Name(set, joint)`; none → `Summary` | terminal or `Summary` |
   | `Summary` | A Shapley summary, exact for at most 5 factors and sampled otherwise, only because no single and no pair changed the outcome; labelled a description | Always | `Decline(none)` with the summary attached |
   Terminal actions: `Name(factor)`, `Name(set)`, `Decline(reason)`, `Fallback(trigger)`. "Rescues" is the
   rescue criterion: contrast at least 1/2, tested above the placebo, flip rate at 10⁻¹² at most 1/10.
2. **Constants (DESIGN defaults in the policy record; changing one is a new policy version).** At most 12
   factors; R = 8; P = 64; second series of 8 at 10⁻⁷ per coalition that changed the outcome; α = 1/20; chaos
   threshold 1/5; rescue thresholds 1/2 and 1/10; budget 689 forks plus 8 per coalition that changed the outcome.
3. **Compiled guard table** (`frob.core-table/1`). For the constants in force, an integer table indexed by
   (f, k, flips at 10⁻¹²) → {`chaos`, `tested`, `rescues`}, generated from the exact test; the runtime looks up
   and never recomputes a p-value. The generator is deterministic and the table regenerates bit-identically from
   the policy record.
4. **Loss model, as declared.** States {single removable cause, joint cause, none attributable}; actions {buy a
   fork, name a factor, name a set, decline}; costs in order: a wrong name (one design iteration and its
   compute) > a decline (one fallback to the heuristic ordering) > a fork. The program gives no exchange rate
   between them; the level α and the chaos threshold stand in for it and are the TARGETs the planted gates test.
5. **Fallback and its delivery.** The fallback is the first-anomaly ordering (monitors timestamp their first
   anomaly; the earliest anomaly before the terminal event is listed first), labelled `Estimated`. Triggers and
   delivery:
   | Trigger | Delivered as |
   |---|---|
   | Null-replay mismatch | Refusal FROB-REF-583 whose repairs include `frob explain run <run> --budget 600` |
   | Failed equivalence in double precision (and no lane admission) | Refusal FROB-REF-584 with the same repair |
   | Placebo flip rate above 0.2 | A result with outcome `chaotic: unattributable`, every factor `none`, and a `fallback` block holding the ordering inline with its label |
   Every delivery records the trigger in the evidence record.
6. **Rollback.** Configuration `autopsy.policy = table | heuristic` (default `table` once adopted). Under
   `heuristic`, `autopsy.attribute` returns only the ordering, labelled `Estimated`, with `policy: "heuristic"`.
   Recorded forks remain valid experiments: their ledgers stay readable and replayable.
7. **Baselines and shadow.** Baseline H: the heuristic ordering, with its first anomaly mapped to the declared
   factor nearest in mechanism. Baseline O: one-at-a-time contrasts without coalitions (one unperturbed fork per
   factor). Adoption status `shadow`: the policy's action is computed and logged beside both baselines' actions,
   and `explain.run` keeps offering the heuristic's edit first; status moves to `default` by a recorded decision
   after the planted-cause and abstention gates are recorded and the shadow comparison has been read. The
   agreement of baseline O with the table is the figure that answers "do ten forks suffice".
8. **Evidence record per decision.** Decision-policy identifier and version (`("autopsy.dc06.attribute", 1)`);
   identities read (run, world, fork, model, scene, policy, tier, precision); the table's hash; counts read
   (f, P, and per factor k, R, flips); the stage path; the action; the trigger if any; in shadow, both baseline
   actions. Written to the ledger and referenced from the attribution record as `policy`.
9. **Record for the registry.** `registries/cores/dc-06.toml`: decision, owner, timescale, family, the table
   with version and hash, the loss ordering, obligations by identifier (INV-AUTOPSY-01, INV-AUTOPSY-02,
   INV-AUTOPSY-05, PO-AUTOPSY-01), the fallback and its three triggers, the budgeted mode, the adoption status, a
   replay scenario (one planted failure of each terminal action) and a demonstration manifest whose commands run
   in under 60 s with checksums and the claim identifiers they support.
10. **Layout.** `crates/frob-autopsy/src/policy.rs`, `policy_table.rs`. Determinism class D0 over recorded counts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each row of the stage table is exercised by a scripted input and takes the stated transition, including
      the boundaries (12 and 13 placebo flips of 64; a contrast of exactly 1/2; a p-value exactly at the level).
- [ ] Each of the three fallback triggers, forced in a test, delivers the fallback in the stated form and is
      recorded.
- [ ] The rollback flag returns the heuristic ordering only; a fork ledger recorded before the flag changed still
      replays.
- [ ] In shadow, every evidence record holds the policy's action and both baselines' actions, and `explain.run`
      is unchanged.
- [ ] The Shapley summary is produced only on the path where no single and no pair changed the outcome.
- [ ] The compiled table regenerates bit-identically; the demonstration manifest runs in under 60 s.
- [ ] The decision model's verification list is on record through the gates that measure it: planted single
      causes, planted joint causes, a planted chaotic failure, the thermal counterexample.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-autopsy/tests/policy.rs` (statistics and labels real; counts from `synthetic` ledgers):
- `stage_transitions`: one case per row, with the boundary values above.
- `fallback_null_mismatch`, `fallback_precision`, `decline_chaotic_offers_fallback`.
- `summary_only_when_nothing_rescues`: a single rescues → no summary computed (asserted by a call counter in the
  real coalition stage, not a mock).
- `rollback_flag_heuristic_only`; `ledger_survives_rollback`.
- `shadow_records_three_actions`.
- `budget_689`: 12 factors → 689 forks planned; a thirteenth factor is refused upstream.
- `table_regenerates_bit_identical`; `table_matches_exact_test` (every cell against N27b-05).
- `loss_ordering_in_record` (metadata).
- `evidence_record_complete` (every field of step 8 present; property over generated decisions).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_session.sh` (N27b-18) logs the evidence record of each attribution: stage path, counts,
action, trigger, baseline actions in shadow. `scripts/e2e/autopsy_attribute_planted.sh` (N27b-09) records the
agreement of baseline O with the table over the 200 planted failures.

### Known answers and negative controls
The worked card GB-34 (164, 38 and 12 of 200: contrasts 0.76 and 0.13; a placebo of 30 of 200 against 38 leaves
nothing to attribute; 12 factors with pairs at 8 replicates and 64 placebo forks is 689 forks). KA-672 through
the `Decline(chaotic)` path. Negative controls: a policy that names the largest contrast without the placebo
stage (names a factor on the doubling map); a policy that computes the summary first and names its largest value
(fails the joint and alternative pair); a fallback that is silent (no trigger recorded). Determinism: a replayed
ledger makes the same stage path and writes the same evidence record.

## Considerations

- RISK-50 (attribution names a cause where none is stable; likelihood medium; early signal: the placebo flip
  rate on reference failures; mitigations: abstention, double precision, the kill gate; owner: Autopsy lead).
- If KILL-AUTOPSY fires the policy reduces to its fallback and to single-factor re-runs from the start; the
  stage table loses `Pairs` and `Summary`, and the registry record says so.
- The per-cluster timescale means the policy is applied to each failing world of a cluster and the pooled table
  is read beside them; it never pools forks of different worlds into one rescue count for a single world.
- OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 and OPEN-AUTOPSY-2 change constants of step 2 (replicate counts; whether `Pairs` is entered by
  default); each change is a policy version.
- Overlap to resolve at load time: the seed bead for the decision model with abstention measures
  GATE-AUTOPSY-05; this bead supplies the policy mechanics it measures. If both carry the stage table, this one
  is the reference for constants and the other for the planted abstention sets.
