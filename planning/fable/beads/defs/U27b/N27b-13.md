## Background

An open question in this program has an owner, a deadline expressed as the gate or pilot that decides it, and a
fail-closed default that holds until it is decided. Autopsy has four. Two of them set constants that every
attribution uses and are decided by measurements this unit produces; this bead records those two decisions. The
other two are measured or owned elsewhere and are listed here so that all four can be found in one place.

| Question | What is open | Owner | Decided by | Default until decided |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 | Default replicate count and perturbation sizes | Autopsy lead | The planted-cause pilot | 8 replicates at 10⁻¹², and 8 at 10⁻⁷ for interventions that change the outcome |
| OPEN-AUTOPSY-2 | Whether one-at-a-time contrasts suffice, which would make pairs optional | Autopsy lead | GATE-AUTOPSY-03 | Singles and pairs |
| OPEN-AUTOPSY-3 | Lyapunov exponents of our own controllers and policies on our tiers, which set the usable fork horizon. No measurement exists for trained quadruped policies in any engine that could be found | Cortex lead | Measured at M3; blocks nothing | Leads of at most 2 s, with the growth of placebo separation reported in every attribution |
| OPEN-AUTOPSY-4 | The size of the snapshot reservoir on each device | Chronicle lead | Nothing blocks on it | A reservoir sized by the budget estimator; every other fork point is reconstructed by replay |

Why these two matter. The replicate count R and the placebo count P decide which labels are reachable at all:
with R = 8, P = 64 and twelve factors, a factor needs at least 3 rescued replicates of 8 to be tested above a
clean placebo, and `but-for` needs all 8. Fewer replicates make labels unreachable; more cost forks linearly
(each added replicate is one fork per coalition: 78 more forks for twelve factors with pairs). Pairs are the
larger cost: twelve factors are 12 singles and 66 pairs, so pairs are 528 of the 689 forks (77%). If ten one-at-a-time
forks name the same top factor as the full table in nearly every planted failure, pairs should be bought only
when no single rescues. Neither question blocks work: the defaults are implemented first and the decision can
only change constants in the policy record.

## Scope

In: the decision procedure for OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 and OPEN-AUTOPSY-2 from recorded measurements; the two ADRs; the
resulting change of constants in the attribution policy record and verb defaults; the status of all four
questions in the register.
Out: the measurements (N27b-10 the pilot; N27b-09 the gate script; N27a-18 the coalition gate's labels of
planted joint and alternative causes, which the on-demand rule must meet); measuring growth of our own closed loops
(N27b-22, which answers OPEN-AUTOPSY-3 for the reference policies); the reservoir size (Chronicle).

## Technical approach

1. **OPEN-AUTOPSY-1: replicate counts and sizes.** Inputs from the pilot: per failure, the outcome of every
   coalition under 20 perturbed snapshots at 10⁻⁷ and 20 at 10⁻¹².
   1. For R ∈ {4, 8, 16} and P ∈ {16, 32, 64}, resample the pilot's perturbed outcomes (without replacement,
      by keyed draws, 1,000 resamples per failure) to emulate an attribution with those counts, and compute
      top-1 accuracy, the abstention rate and the reachability table of the exact test at twelve factors. The
      pilot records 20 perturbed snapshots per scale and 64 placebo forks per failure, so R above 20 or P above
      64 cannot be emulated without replacement; a larger count is decided only after a dated amendment of the
      pilot that records more.
   2. Choose the smallest (R, P) at which accuracy is within one percentage point of the best configuration and
      at least 90%, and at which `but-for` is reachable. Ties go to the cheaper configuration (fork count
      78·R + P + 1 for twelve factors with pairs).
   3. Sizes: keep 10⁻¹² and 10⁻⁷ unless the pilot shows either that perturbations at 10⁻¹² change a
      coalition's outcome in fewer than 1% of (coalition, snapshot) pairs (then the perturbed replicates at
      10⁻¹² add little beyond the unperturbed fork, and R at 10⁻¹² may be lowered as far as the reachability
      table at twelve factors allows), or that the pilot's tool cannot represent 10⁻¹².
   4. If the pilot could not run or its tool could not repeat forks exactly, the default stands and the ADR says
      so: 8 at 10⁻¹², and 8 at 10⁻⁷ for interventions that change the outcome; 64 placebo forks.
2. **OPEN-AUTOPSY-2: are pairs optional.** Input from the gate script: over the 200 planted single causes, the
   agreement between the ten-fork one-at-a-time arm and the full table, with its one-sided 95% exact lower bound;
   and from the coalition gate (planted joint and alternative causes), how often singles alone mislabel.
   - Pairs become *on demand* (`--coalitions` defaults to `none`; the policy enters its pairs stage only when no
     single rescues) when the lower bound on agreement is at least 0.95 on single causes **and** the on-demand
     rule labels the coalition gate's 100 planted causes (50 joint, 50 alternative, N27a-12) correctly in at
     least 95 of 100, the gate's threshold: a joint cause has no rescuing single, so the on-demand rule reaches
     the pairs stage for it by construction.
   - Otherwise pairs stay on by default.
   - Planted *alternative* causes are found by singles alone under either rule.
   The smallest counterexample that keeps this from being decided by cost alone: two factors that rescue only
   together have v = 0 for each single and v = 1 for the pair; a singles-only table names nothing and a
   singles-only *method* (no pairs stage at all) can never find the repair. On-demand pairs find it; removing
   pairs does not. The decision is therefore between "always" and "on demand", never "never".
3. **Recording.** One ADR per question (`docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-AUTOPSY-1.md`, `-2.md`): the default that held, the
   measurement with its record hashes, the decision, the constants changed, and the policy version that results.
   The open-question register marks each as decided with the ADR's identifier. The verb defaults
   (`--replicates`, `--placebo`, `--coalitions`) and the policy record are changed in the same commit, and the
   reachability table's hash changes with them.
4. **OPEN-AUTOPSY-3 and -4.** Their status lines are updated from the records of the beads that measure or own
   them: the growth figures G(W) of the reference quadruped and humanoid policies at 0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s (the
   fork-lead default of at most 2 s stands unless those figures say otherwise), and the reservoir default.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two ADRs exist, each quoting the measurement it rests on by record hash, or stating that the default
      stands and why.
- [ ] The register shows OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 and OPEN-AUTOPSY-2 as decided, and OPEN-AUTOPSY-3 and OPEN-AUTOPSY-4
      with their current status and defaults.
- [ ] After the decision, `frob autopsy intervene --plan-only` on twelve factors prints the fork count implied by
      the decided constants, and the attribution policy record carries a new version if any constant changed.
- [ ] The resampling analysis is reproducible: the same pilot record and seed give the same table of accuracy by
      (R, P).
- [ ] The on-demand rule, if adopted, passes the planted joint and alternative sets at the coalition gate's
      threshold; the decision is reverted to "always" if it does not.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_autopsy.rs`:
- `fork_count_formula`: (R, P) = (8, 64) → 689; (4, 32) → 345; (16, 64) → 1,313 for twelve factors with pairs.
- `resample_is_keyed`: same seed, same table; `resample_refuses_beyond_record`: R = 32 or P = 128 is refused
  against a pilot record of 20 snapshots and 64 placebo forks.
- `choose_smallest_within_one_point` on a `synthetic` accuracy table: {(4, 32): 0.86, (8, 64): 0.93,
  (16, 64): 0.935} → (8, 64).
- `pairs_decision_rule`: lower bound 0.96 and 97 of the 100 joint and alternative causes correct → on demand;
  lower bound 0.93 → always; 90 of 100 correct → always.
- `joint_pair_needs_pairs_stage`: on the table v({1}) = v({2}) = 0, v({1, 2}) = 1, the on-demand rule reaches
  the pair and a singles-only rule names nothing.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. The ADRs quote the `summary` lines of the pilot's log (`accuracy_pairs`,
`top_change_rate_1e-7`, `agreement_oat_pairs`) and of `scripts/e2e/autopsy_attribute_planted.sh`
(`oat_agreement` with its lower bound). After a change of defaults, `scripts/e2e/autopsy_attribute_planted.sh`
is re-run and must still pass.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fork-count arithmetic above. KA-673 (the joint pair) as the case that forbids removing pairs outright.
Negative control: a decision procedure that picks the cheapest configuration regardless of accuracy is caught by
`choose_smallest_within_one_point` (it would pick (4, 32) at 0.86).

## Considerations

- A decision here must not be tuned on the same 200 failures the gate then reads. The resampling uses the
  pilot's failures; the gate script's suite is read once per repair cycle. If the pilot and the product suite
  share plants, the ADR says so and the gate's figure is labelled as measured after tuning on overlapping data.
- Fewer replicates lower cost and also widen the set of count tables at which no factor can be named; the
  reachability table is the guard.
- If KILL-AUTOPSY fires, OPEN-AUTOPSY-2 is moot (no coalitions) and is closed with that reason.
