## Background

Autopsy's default is to attribute one failure in double precision on the CPU (`batch-cpu` in `f64`, or the
referee). The single-precision lane is the alternative path: attribution on the tier where the failure happened
(`gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`, or `batch-cpu` in `f32`), in single precision, for failures that a measured admission
rule accepts. It amends the decision **ADR-AUTOPSY-04** (attribution reports a flip rate at round-off scale;
double precision is the default for one failure): double precision is the default because it is cheap for one
failure, not because the evidence requires it. What the evidence supports is a flip rate measured under
perturbations at or above round-off scale, and that can be measured where the failure happened.

Two benefits are claimed and both are stated with their ceilings before any test. The first is wall time of
*bulk* attribution, bounded by the ratio of device to CPU world-seconds per second: a cluster of 200 failures
with pair interactions (55 interventions × 32 replicates × 2 s) is 7.0 × 10⁵ world-seconds, about 3 minutes on a
device producing 4,000 world-seconds per second against 12 minutes to 2 hours on `batch-cpu` at 1,000 to 100.
For one failure with ten factors and 16 replicates (160 forks of 2 s, 320 world-seconds, seconds on either tier)
the lane saves nothing. The second is attribution of *tier-specific* failures, which the double-precision rule
could not attribute at all.

Tier Frontier. Crate `frob-autopsy`. Pilot: N27b-22 (must be on record first). Kill gate: KILL-ENSEMBLE
(agreement with double precision below 90%; or fewer than half of the reference failures admitted at any window
of 0.5 s or more; or a bulk speedup below 3), with the fallback: the decision as first written, attribution in
double precision only, and a failure not reproduced there goes to the artifact guard unattributed.

## Scope

In: the `--lane` option on `autopsy.intervene` (where forks are run) and on `autopsy.attribute` (where a fork
set is read); the lane's fork plan; the `auto` rule and its record; the attribution record's lane fields;
statistics and labels at the lane's counts; refusals; behaviour after a kill.
Out: admission (N27b-23); numerical-variant interventions and `tier-specific` (N27b-25); the cross-check
against double precision (N27b-26); bulk batching and its cost model (N27b-27); the gate (N27b-28).

## Technical approach

1. **Where the option lives.** The program states the option on the read verb: `autopsy.attribute --lane {f64,
   tier-f32, auto}`; `auto` applies the admission rule and records which lane ran and why. Forks, however, are
   run by `autopsy.intervene`, and a read verb runs nothing. DESIGN: both verbs take `--lane`.
   - `frob autopsy intervene <fork> --lane f64|tier-f32|auto …` decides where the forks run (and under
     `tier-f32` and `auto` runs admission first).
   - `frob autopsy attribute <fork> --lane f64|tier-f32|auto` selects which recorded fork set to read. `auto`
     reads the set the `auto` rule chose at intervene time and prints the rule's record; if the requested lane
     has no fork set, the result is empty with the flag `no-interventions-run` and the matching `intervene`
     command as the next action.
2. **The lane's fork plan for one failure.** After admission at window W (N27b-23): the R₀ = 32 placebo forks at
   10⁻⁷ that admission ran are the attribution's placebo forks (same child addresses; not re-run). For every
   intervention and coalition: at least 16 replicates at 10⁻⁷ (DESIGN default 16; replicate 0 unperturbed, the
   rest perturbed at 10⁻⁷ with a size factor in [1, 2), raised to one unit in the last place where larger).
   There is no 10⁻¹² series: it is not representable in single precision and the record prints
   `"1e-12": null` with the reason. Counts: ten factors without pairs: 1 + 32 + 160 = 193 forks; with pairs (55
   coalitions): 1 + 32 + 880 = 913.
3. **Statistics and labels in the lane.** Contrasts are tested against the placebo exactly as in double
   precision: the exact one-sided test with R = 16 and P = 32 at level α divided by the number of factors.
   Reachability at α = 1/20 (least rescued count that is tested above the placebo):
   | Placebo flips f of 32 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | one factor | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
   | ten or twelve factors | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
   The rescue criterion reads the flip rate at 10⁻⁷ (at most 1/10: one flip of 16). The chaos clause cannot fire
   in the lane: admission already required ρ₀ ≤ 0.1.
4. **The `auto` rule (DESIGN; the choice and its reason are recorded per failure).**
   | Situation | Lane that runs | Recorded reason |
   |---|---|---|
   | The failure was recorded in double precision | `f64` | `native` |
   | Single failure recorded in single precision, reproduced in double precision (decision-equivalent) | `f64` | `single failure: double precision costs little` |
   | A cluster (more than one failure in the request) recorded in single precision | `tier-f32` for every admitted failure; `f64` for the others that are decision-equivalent; `marginal` or `precision-sensitive` for the rest | per failure: `admitted at W`, `not admitted: flips`, `not admitted: growth`, `escalated` |
   | Recorded in single precision and not reproduced in double precision | `tier-f32` with the numerical-variant interventions first | `tier-specific candidate` |
   `--lane tier-f32` forces the lane and fails with the admission record if the failure is not admitted (outcome
   `marginal`, or reason `growth` with the escalation offered); `--lane f64` forces double precision.
5. **Record.** The attribution carries `tier: <backend>`, `precision: "f32"`, and
   `"lane": {"requested": "auto", "ran": "tier-f32", "why": "admitted at 1.0 s", "admission": {…}}`; the scope
   block carries the same tier and precision, so a lane attribution is never mistaken for a double-precision
   one. Evidence class as elsewhere: contrasts `Tested` on their tier, the attribution `Estimated`.
6. **Refusals.** FROB-REF-562 when the device family has no current class-D2 record (licence, at admission).
   FROB-REF-584 when neither lane admits the failure. FROB-REF-583 on a null mismatch on the originating
   profile. FROB-REF-222-style placement refusals are not involved: the lane runs the `lockstep` profile the
   failure was recorded under.
7. **After a kill.** If KILL-ENSEMBLE fires, `--lane` accepts only `f64`; `tier-f32` and `auto` are removed from
   the schema (a new schema version), existing lane attributions stay readable and are labelled
   `lane: withdrawn`, and they may not enter a report.
8. **Layout.** `crates/frob-autopsy/src/lane/run.rs`, `lane/auto.rs`; schema additions in the verb registry
   entries. Determinism: class D2 on one device family; a lane attribution is replayable from its ledger on the
   same family.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On an admitted failure, `--lane tier-f32` runs 1 + 32 + 16 per coalition forks on the originating tier,
      reuses the admission's placebo forks, and the record carries the tier, `precision: "f32"` and the lane
      block.
- [ ] The reachability table of step 3 is reproduced exactly and stored with the attribution.
- [ ] `auto` chooses as the table of step 4 states in each of the four situations and records the reason.
- [ ] The pendulum control is not attributed in the lane: outcome `marginal`.
- [ ] A failure admitted to neither lane is refused with FROB-REF-584; a device family without a class-D2 record
      is refused with FROB-REF-562 before any fork.
- [ ] On the planted suite with windows of at most 1 s the lane names the planted factor first in at least 90%
      of admitted cases (the lane clause of GATE-AUTOPSY-03; measured by N27b-28).
- [ ] `frob autopsy attribute --lane tier-f32` on a fork with only a double-precision set returns the empty
      result with its next action, and changes nothing.
- [ ] Until this bead lands, `tier-f32` and `auto` are refused with FROB-REF-724; after a kill they are absent
      from the schema.
- [ ] The conditions of "implemented" for a Frontier capability hold: the lane's behaviour is in the crate's
      `CONTRACT.md` (determinism class, evidence class, validity domain: windows and tiers); a CPU reference path
      exists (`batch-cpu` in `f32` runs the same lane); cancellation of a lane job drains and leaks no device
      resource; the pilot record and the kill-gate decision are on record before the lane is described as
      implemented.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-autopsy/tests/lane_run.rs` (real forks on fixture 2 in `f32` on `batch-cpu`, and on a device where
present; host fixtures `synthetic`):
- `lane_fork_counts`: 193 and 913.
- `placebo_forks_reused`: the admission's child addresses appear once in the ledger.
- `no_1e12_series_in_f32`: the field is `null` with reason `not representable`.
- `reachability_r16_p32`: the table of step 3.
- `auto_rule_table`: one case per row of step 4.
- `forced_lane_unadmitted_returns_marginal`; `forced_f64_ignores_admission`.
- `scope_carries_f32`.
- `refuse_562_without_d2_record`, `refuse_584_neither_lane`.
- `after_kill_schema_rejects_tier_f32` (with the kill flag set in a test configuration).
- `lane_matches_f64_on_wide_margin`: on a wide-margin planted failure the lane and double precision give the
  same top factor, label and contrast signs.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_lane_f32.sh` (N27b-28) drives `frob autopsy intervene --lane auto` and
`frob autopsy attribute --lane auto` over the planted suite and the reference failures, and logs per failure:
lane requested and run, reason, tier, precision, admission figures, fork counts, the table and labels.

### Known answers and negative controls
The lane's two known answers (linear map growth; the pendulum, `marginal`). The cost arithmetic: 160 forks of
2 s is 320 world-seconds; 200 × 55 × 32 × 2 s is 7.0 × 10⁵ world-seconds: 176 s at 4,000 per second, 704 s at
1,000, 7,040 s at 100. Negative controls: a lane that attributes without admission (attributes the pendulum); a
lane whose record omits the precision (the scope test fails); an `auto` that silently changes lanes without a
recorded reason. Determinism lanes: a lane attribution twice on one device; across worker counts; alone and in a
batch; against class D2 on each device family.

## Considerations

- A lane attribution and a double-precision attribution of the same failure are two experiments on two
  arithmetic profiles. They are compared by the cross-check, never merged into one table.
- The lane runs under the profile the failure was recorded under. Changing step, iterations or contact law is an
  *intervention* (a numerical variant), handled by its own bead, not a property of the lane.
- For a single failure the lane has no speed benefit by the cost arithmetic above; `auto` therefore prefers
  double precision there, and the lane's value is bulk work and tier-specific failures.
- OPEN-AUTOPSY-1's decision on replicate counts applies to the double-precision path; the lane's minimum of 16
  is its own default, kept in the lane's policy record.
