## Background

Some failures exist only on the fast tier: replayed in double precision on the CPU, the robot does not fall.
Under the decision as first written, such a failure was refused attribution and sent to the artifact guard, so
the one class of failure a user most needs explained ("why does it fail on the GPU and not on the CPU?") was
the one Autopsy would not touch. The single-precision lane changes that: **a failure that double precision does
not reproduce is no longer refused outright. It is attributed in the lane with the numerical-variant
interventions first: half step, doubled solver iterations, the alternative contact law, the stricter step
profile. A rescue by one of them gives the label `tier-specific: <variant>` and passes the world to the artifact
guard. Only when the lane is also not admitted is the failure labelled `precision-sensitive` and attribution
refused.**

The numerical variants are the perturbations that should not matter to a physical failure. The artifact guard
uses the same four to decide whether a failure counts; here they are used as *interventions*, with replicates
and a placebo, so that "the failure goes away when the solver iterates twice as long" is a tested contrast and
not a single replay. A failure of the simulator is also a finding, and this is how it gets a name.

Tier Frontier (part of the single-precision lane). Crate `frob-autopsy`. Pilot: N27b-22. Kill gate:
KILL-ENSEMBLE; on a kill, a failure not reproduced in double precision goes to the artifact guard unattributed.

## Scope

In: the four numerical variants as intervention factors (namespace `numerics`); their fork handling; the
"variants first" procedure; the label `tier-specific: <variant>`; the hand-off to the artifact guard; the
condition flag on any further attribution of such a failure; the planted solver artifact used by the gate.
Out: the step-plan variants themselves (Kinesis and the model-form check); the guard's three checks and its
ledger record (BD-272); admission (N27b-23); lane execution (N27b-24).

## Technical approach

1. **Factors.** The vocabulary gains the namespace `numerics` and four registered diagnostic factors (rows of
   the diagnostic-factor registry, kind `numerical-variant`, `realizable: false`, never buildable):
   | Tag | Assignment | Mechanism | What it changes (physical parameters held) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `half-step` | `numerics.step := half` | `numerics.step` | Half the physics step; the requirement's clock and every sensor and control clock are unchanged |
   | `iterations-x2` | `numerics.solver_iterations := x2` | `numerics.iterations` | Twice the solver iterations of the profile |
   | `law-2` | `numerics.contact_law := alternative` | `numerics.contact-law` | The second qualified compliant contact law in place of the default |
   | `tol-div-10` | `numerics.profile := stricter` | `numerics.tolerance` | The stricter solver-effort profile: where the tier has a solver tolerance, a tolerance ten times tighter |
   Each is a distinct step-plan identity; its model difference touches exactly its own mechanism, so the
   one-mechanism rule holds. A variant with no current determinism record on the tier is not run: it is listed
   under `refused` with FROB-REF-584 (uncontrolled determinism class). A variant the tier does not carry (for
   example no second contact law on that backend) is listed as `unavailable` with the reason.
2. **Fork handling.** `iterations-x2` and `tol-div-10`: the checkpoint is restored whole (warm starts remain
   valid inputs to a longer solve). `half-step` and `law-2`: positions and velocities restored, contact memory
   discarded, first solve cold with a raised iteration cap, as for any contact-affecting intervention. All four
   show the class `diagnostic`.
3. **Procedure for a failure recorded in single precision that double precision does not reproduce.**
   1. Admission on the originating tier (placebo forks at 10⁻⁷; ρ₀ ≤ 0.1; G(W)·u ≤ 10⁻⁴). Not admitted at any
      usable window: outcome `precision-sensitive`, FROB-REF-584, the world to the artifact guard. Stop.
   2. Run the four variants as single interventions, 16 replicates each at 10⁻⁷, against the admission's placebo
      forks (1 + 32 + 64 = 97 forks). Test each contrast at level α/4.
   3. If at least one variant rescues (contrast at least 1/2, tested above the placebo, at most one flipped
      replicate of 16): outcome **`tier-specific: <variant>`**, naming the rescuing variant; if several rescue,
      the label names the first in the fixed order `half-step`, `iterations-x2`, `law-2`, `tol-div-10` and
      `variants_rescuing` lists all. The declared physical factors are not run unless the caller passes
      `--continue-after-variant`.
   4. If no variant rescues: the declared physical factors are attributed in the lane as usual, and the
      attribution carries the condition flag `tier-sensitive` (the failure exists on this tier only), which
      travels with every result built on it and cannot be dropped by a consumer.
4. **Hand-off to the artifact guard.** On `tier-specific`, the failing run is submitted to the guard in the role
   of a failure. The guard's verdict is attached (`artifact-candidate` with what removed the failure, or
   `Passed`), the world is excluded from counterexample counts and from the hard-world set by the guard's own
   rule, and the guard writes a fidelity-ledger record of a tier-sensitive case. The attribution's
   `next_actions`: the first-divergence command between the recorded run and its double-precision replay (to
   name the stage where the tiers part), and the guard record.
5. **Record additions.**
   ```json
   "outcome": "tier-specific: iterations-x2", "variants_rescuing": ["iterations-x2", "tol-div-10"],
   "variants": [ {"tag": "half-step", "rescued": "2/16", "contrast": 0.09, "flip_rate": {"1e-12": null, "1e-7": 0.125}},
                 {"tag": "iterations-x2", "rescued": "16/16", "contrast": 0.97, "flip_rate": {"1e-12": null, "1e-7": 0.0}},
                 {"tag": "law-2", "status": "unavailable", "reason": "no second law on this backend"},
                 {"tag": "tol-div-10", "rescued": "16/16", "contrast": 0.97, "flip_rate": {"1e-12": null, "1e-7": 0.0}} ],
   "guard": {"status": "artifact-candidate", "removed_by": ["iterations-x2"], "ledger": "…"},
   "conditions": ["tier-sensitive"]
   ```
   (Illustrative numbers. In this example two variants rescue; the label names `iterations-x2`, first in the
   fixed order among those that rescue.)
6. **The planted solver artifact** (for the gate; a conformance build option, never in a product build). A
   10 kg block rests on a slope with tan θ = 0.45 on friction 0.5: demand m·g·sin θ = 40.26 N against capacity
   μ·m·g·cos θ = 44.73 N, so it must hold. The plant caps the contact solver's iterations on the single-precision
   tier at half of what convergence needs on this fixture, so the friction force is under-resolved and the block
   creeps past the requirement "holds position within 5 mm for 2 s". Double precision with the profile's count
   holds. Expected label: `tier-specific: iterations-x2`. Where the artifact guard's planted artifacts exist on
   the tier they are used as further cases (the box-linearized cone, removed by `law-2`).
7. **Layout.** `crates/frob-autopsy/src/lane/variants.rs`; registry rows under `registries/autopsy/`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The planted under-converged solve, which double precision does not reproduce, is labelled
      `tier-specific: iterations-x2`; the same fixture without the plant does not fail.
- [ ] A genuine failure recorded in single precision and reproduced in double precision never enters this
      procedure (the precision route sends it to double precision).
- [ ] A failure that double precision does not reproduce and that no variant rescues is attributed over its
      physical factors with the condition `tier-sensitive` on the record and on every result that consumes it.
- [ ] A failure that is not admitted is `precision-sensitive` with FROB-REF-584; nothing is attributed.
- [ ] Every `tier-specific` world has an artifact-guard verdict attached and a fidelity-ledger record; it is not
      in the hard-world set.
- [ ] Each variant's model difference touches exactly its own mechanism; a variant with no determinism record is
      refused, and an unavailable one is listed, neither silently skipped.
- [ ] No numerical variant can appear in a repair set (type-level).
- [ ] The procedure is documented in the crate's `CONTRACT.md`; it runs on `batch-cpu` in `f32` as its CPU
      reference path; cancellation between variants drains cleanly; the pilot record and the kill-gate decision
      are on record before the capability is described as implemented.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_lane_f32.sh` (N27b-28) runs the planted artifact through `frob run fork`,
`frob autopsy intervene --lane auto` and `frob autopsy attribute --lane auto`, asserts the label and the guard
record, and logs per variant: tag, status, rescued, contrast, flip rate at 10⁻⁷, level; the outcome; the guard's
status.

### Known answers and negative controls
The gate's clause: a planted solver artifact that double precision does not reproduce is labelled
`tier-specific` with the right variant. The hand-checkable slope numbers (24.23°, 40.26 N, 44.73 N). The guard's
artifacts as further cases: a friction cone linearized as a box grants √2 times the friction coefficient along
its diagonals; penetration recovery can pump energy into a hopping body. Negative controls: a procedure that
labels any single-precision-only failure `tier-specific` without a rescuing variant (the "no variant rescues"
case catches it); a procedure that runs variants as single replays with no placebo (a marginal failure would be
called tier-specific: the pendulum control must stay `marginal`). Determinism: each variant step plan under its
own class-D2 record; results identical twice on one device.

## Considerations

- The Autopsy specification names the fourth variant "the stricter step profile" and the guard names it "a solver tolerance
  ten times tighter". They are treated as one variant with the guard's tag `tol-div-10`; on a tier whose solver
  runs a fixed iteration count with no tolerance, the stricter solver-effort profile is used and the record says
  which realization ran.
- All four variants share one contact-formulation family; an artifact common to both contact laws is not
  removed by any of them and will be attributed to a physical factor with the flag `tier-sensitive`. The flag is
  the honest residue.
- A `tier-specific` label says where the simulator's answer depends on its numerics. It does not say which
  answer is right; the higher tier is more trusted, not ground truth.
