## Background

The attribution decision is taken per failure *cluster*: an evaluation of thousands of worlds produces tens or
hundreds of failures of one class, and the useful question is what, across them, would have prevented the
failure. For one failure the single-precision lane saves nothing: ten factors with 16 replicates are 160 forks
of 2 s, 320 world-seconds, seconds on either tier. Its speed benefit exists only in bulk: a cluster of 200
failures with pair interactions (55 interventions × 32 replicates × 2 s) is 7.0 × 10⁵ world-seconds, about 3
minutes on a device producing 4,000 world-seconds per second, against 12 minutes to 2 hours on `batch-cpu` at
1,000 to 100 (the throughputs are TARGETs of the performance program, not measurements). The ceiling of the
benefit, stated before any test: the wall time of bulk attribution, by the ratio of device to CPU world-seconds.
The lane's gate asks for at least 3 times, and its kill gate fires below 3.

This bead builds what makes a cluster one job: a planner that packs the children of many failing worlds into
device batches, a cost model that predicts the job before it runs, and the measurement protocol under which
"three times faster" can be claimed at all. Tier Frontier (part of the lane). Crate `frob-autopsy`. Pilot:
N27b-22, whose record holds the ceiling computed in advance.

## Scope

In: the cluster form of `autopsy.intervene` (many fork handles, one factor list, one job); batch planning across
parents; fork-point reconstruction for worlds without snapshots; the order admission → lane → escalation; the
cost model and `--plan-only` estimate; chunked, idempotent execution; the pooled result; the registered speed
experiment (ceiling, arms, potency control, paired lower bound) that the gate script runs.
Out: per-failure attribution logic (BD-280, N27b-24); admission (N27b-23); cross-check (N27b-26); mixed-variant
batches on devices (the device-variant bead BD-183); the estimate and budget service (BD-219, BD-297); the gate
record (N27b-28).

## Technical approach

1. **Surface (DESIGN).** `frob autopsy intervene --forks <f1,f2,…> | --cluster <run>[:<failure class>]
   --do … [--coalitions pairs] [--lane auto] [--plan-only] --key <k> --json` (`--forks` and `--cluster` are
   inputs this bead adds to the verb in place of the single fork handle; they are carried by the verb's
   registry row, VERBS-autopsy, from this bead on; the idempotency key is the common flag `--key`). With `--cluster`, the
   fork handles are made first (one `run.fork --before failure --dt <lead>` per failing world of the class, in
   world-address order). Factor paths are resolved per world; a factor that does not exist in a world (a patch
   that world's scene lacks) is recorded as `not-applicable` for that world, not refused for the cluster.
   `frob autopsy attribute --forks …` returns the per-failure attributions and the pooled table.
2. **Stages of the job.**
   1. *Fork points.* Worlds with a stored snapshot at the fork tick restore from it. The others are
      reconstructed by replay from their addresses to their fork ticks, as one batch on the originating tier
      (cost: one episode prefix per world). Null forks are checked for all (INV-AUTOPSY-01); a world whose null
      fork is not bitwise is dropped from the job with FROB-REF-583 recorded for it.
   2. *Admission batch.* For every failure, 32 placebo forks at 10⁻⁷ over the window (33 children per failure).
      Failures are split into admitted, admitted at a shorter window, and not admitted.
   3. *Lane batches.* For admitted failures: C coalitions × R replicates children each, packed into device
      batches up to the batch capacity; children of different parents and different variants share a batch (a
      world's trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in).
   4. *Escalation.* Failures not admitted and decision-equivalent in double precision run on `batch-cpu` in
      `f64` concurrently with stage 3. The rest (neither admitted nor reproduced in double precision) are
      `precision-sensitive`, with FROB-REF-584 recorded per failure (N27b-23's precedence); `marginal` occurs
      only for a reproduced failure that the caller forced into the lane with `--lane tier-f32`. Admitted
      failures that double precision does not reproduce run the numerical variants first (N27b-25).
   5. *Cross-check.* One lane attribution in eight is repeated in double precision.
   6. *Pooling.* Per factor across the cluster: worlds rescued by the unperturbed fork out of the worlds where
      the factor applies; the cluster placebo rate; the exact test; the pooled label. The pooled rescue rate is
      the estimate of the probability of necessity for the cluster. Lanes are pooled separately and the table
      says which lane each row came from; a pooled row never mixes precisions.
3. **Chunking.** The job is a list of chunks (one device batch each) keyed by the hash of their child addresses;
   a chunk is idempotent, so a cancelled or preempted job resumes without re-running finished chunks, and a
   budget stop publishes nothing partial as a result (the finished chunks stay in the store as replayable forks).
4. **Cost model.** With F failures, C coalitions, R replicates, placebo R₀, window W, horizon H (fork tick to
   one second past the failure) and prefix lengths Lᵢ:
   world-seconds = Σᵢ Lᵢ (reconstruction, only for worlds without snapshots) + F·(1 + R₀)·W (admission, counting
   the null fork) + F_adm·C·R·H (lane) + F_esc·(C·8 + 64 + 1)·H (escalation in double precision) + (F_adm/8)·
   (C·8 + 64 + 1)·H (cross-check) + 8·H per double-precision coalition whose outcome changes (the second series
   at 10⁻⁷, in escalation and cross-check). That last term is unknown before phase A of each double-precision
   attribution, so the estimate's interval spans 0 to C such coalitions per failure; after the run the exact
   count comes from the ledgers.
   wall = world-seconds(tier) / r(tier, batch shape) + T_fixed, with T_fixed = variant compilation + checkpoint
   restores + result reading. `--plan-only` returns the breakdown with the throughput figures it used, their
   source (measured for this hardware fingerprint, or TARGET) and an interval; an estimate whose upper end
   exceeds the remaining budget is refused at admission (FROB-REF-701) with the estimate attached.
   Worked instance: F = 200, C = 55, R = 32, H = 2 s gives 200 × 55 × 32 × 2 = 704,000 world-seconds for the
   lane stage: 176 s at 4,000 per second; 704 s at 1,000; 7,040 s at 100.
5. **The ceiling.** Before any speed comparison is run, `ceiling = T_cpu / T_dev` is computed from the cost
   model with measured throughputs for both arms and each arm's measured T_fixed, and stored in the speed
   experiment's record (step 7). That record is refused with FROB-REF-866 when it is registered, by the
   register's admission check (N38a-07) through N36-05's `admit_experiment`, the one function that raises the
   code: a record without its computed ceiling, with a ceiling filed after its first run, or with a ceiling
   below its goal of 3. The code is never raised by a call of `autopsy.intervene`: a bulk request carries no
   ceiling and runs or is refused on its own admission alone.
6. **The speed experiment (registered before it runs; six parts).**
   | Part | Content |
   |---|---|
   | Ceiling | As step 5, from a phase breakdown measured on the baseline arm |
   | Competing arms | A: the lane on a device (`--lane auto`), cross-check included. B: double precision on `batch-cpu` with every available core (the strongest conventional alternative at equal hardware). The mechanism's own fallback is arm B |
   | Potency control | An A/A run (B against B) must not clear 1.02; a planted slowdown of the device arm by a factor of 2 (a sleep per chunk) must be detected |
   | Paired lower bound | The 200 failures are split into ten blocks of 20 by address; each block is attributed by both arms; the reported figure is a one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on the paired ratio of wall times, never a ratio of means. Ten pairs of constant ratio r must return r |
   | Pre-declared fallback | Double precision only (KILL-ENSEMBLE) |
   | Reachability | With ten paired blocks, a lower bound of 3 is reachable only if every block's ratio exceeds 3 by the spread observed; the pre-analysis states the smallest mean ratio at which the bound can exceed 3 given the A/A spread |
   Every figure carries the disclosure block.
7. **Layout.** `crates/frob-autopsy/src/lane/bulk.rs`, `lane/cost.rs`; the experiment's registration is one
   record of the method-experiment register, `crates/frob-conformance/registry/experiments.toml` (schema
   `frob.gauntlet.experiment/1`, whose admission check is N38a-07's), not a file of this bead's own.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A cluster of 200 planted failures with ten factors and pairs runs as one job with `--lane auto`; every
      failure ends in exactly one of: lane attribution, double-precision attribution, `marginal`,
      `precision-sensitive`, or dropped with FROB-REF-583; the counts sum to 200.
- [ ] Children of different parents share device batches, and each child's digest equals the digest of the same
      child run alone (sampled: 64 children).
- [ ] The cost model's world-second count equals the count of world-seconds actually stepped (exact, from the
      fork ledgers); the wall-time estimate's interval contains the measured wall time on the gate hardware.
- [ ] The worked instance is reproduced by the model: 704,000 world-seconds; 176 s, 704 s and 7,040 s.
- [ ] A cancelled job resumes and re-runs no finished chunk; a budget stop publishes no partial result.
- [ ] The pooled table never mixes precisions and states its denominators (worlds where the factor applies).
- [ ] The speed experiment is registered with its six parts before it runs; the A/A control stays below 1.02;
      the planted slowdown is detected; ten pairs of constant ratio return that ratio.
- [ ] The speed experiment's record without a stored ceiling, or with a ceiling below 3, is refused with
      FROB-REF-866 at its registration (N38a-07's check) and no block of it runs; a bulk `autopsy.intervene`
      call is never refused with 866.
- [ ] The bulk job is reachable through `frob autopsy intervene --json` and the MCP tool; cancellation is tested
      (request, drain at a chunk boundary, finalize, no leaked device resource); the same job runs on
      `batch-cpu` in `f32` as the CPU reference path; the cost model and the speed experiment's protocol are in
      the crate's `CONTRACT.md`.

  through `autopsy.intervene` is not refused.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_lane_f32.sh` (N27b-28) runs the bulk job and the registered speed experiment and logs: the
plan's breakdown (world-seconds per stage, throughputs and their source, T_fixed, estimate with interval), the
ceiling, per block the wall time of each arm and the paired ratio, the lower bound against 3, the A/A figure
against 1.02, the disclosure block's identifier, the partition of the 200 failures and the admitted share.

### Known answers and negative controls
The cost arithmetic above; the ceiling arithmetic of the pilot. Negative controls: the A/A arm; the planted
slowdown; a planner that lets children share a reduction across worlds (the alone-versus-batch digest check
fails); a speed figure computed as a ratio of means over unpaired runs (refused: a performance ratio without a
paired lower bound). Determinism lanes: the bulk job's fork digests twice on one device and across two batch
packings (different chunk sizes), which must be identical.

## Considerations

- If the measured ceiling on the gate hardware is close to 3 (for example a fast CPU at 1,000 world-seconds per
  second and realistic fixed costs), the speed clause has little power and is likely to fail for reasons that
  are not defects. The pilot's record flags this before the gate is run; the response is a dated amendment or a
  kill, never a re-scoped measurement afterwards.
- Reconstruction by replay costs prefixes on the device, small beside the fork stage for clusters of failures
  late in long episodes; it is in the model so that it is not a surprise.
- Device memory bounds batch capacity; the planner sizes chunks from the estimate service and never from trial
  and error on the device.
- OPEN-AUTOPSY-4 (reservoir size on each device; owner: Chronicle lead) decides how many fork points need
  reconstruction; the default is a reservoir sized by the budget estimator.
