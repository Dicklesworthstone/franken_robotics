## Background

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate with a fallback named in advance, and the fallback is a mechanism of a
lower tier that the program already contains, so that losing the item costs a capability and never the critical
path. The single-precision lane is such a mechanism: attribution on the tier where a failure happened, in single
precision, admitted by a measured rule, with a cross-check against double precision. Its kill gate:

| Kill gate | Trigger | Fallback |
|---|---|---|
| KILL-ENSEMBLE | Agreement with double precision below 90%; or fewer than half of the reference failures admitted at any window of 0.5 s or more; or a bulk speedup below 3 | The precision decision as first written: attribution in double precision only, and a failure not reproduced there goes to the artifact guard unattributed |

Each clause kills a different reason for having the lane. Low agreement means the admission rule does not
protect the answer. Low admission means our closed loops amplify round-off faster than the published exponents
suggest, so the lane rarely applies. A speedup below 3 means bulk attribution, the only case where the lane
saves time (for one failure it saves nothing: 320 world-seconds is seconds on either tier), does not repay its
complexity. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is what the program then does, and the fallback
here is the Solid default that every attribution already supports.

## Scope

In: reading the pilot's record and the gate's figures; applying the three clauses as written and as
pre-registered; the ADR; the gate ledger entries; what is removed and what remains if it fires; the handling of
the one benefit that is not a speed (tier-specific failures).
Out: the measurements (N27b-22, N27b-28, N27b-26's ledger); the double-precision path, which is unaffected.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** (a) The pilot record (N27b-22): the admitted share per window on the pilot's tool, the agreement
   on its planted cases, the ceiling computed in advance. (b) The row of GATE-AUTOPSY-09 after at most one repair
   cycle (N27b-28), per device family: top-factor agreement with double precision over admitted, comparable
   planted cases; the admitted share of the reference failures (quadruped and humanoid, 50 each) at each of
   0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s; the one-sided 95% lower bound of the paired bulk speedup with its ceiling. (c) The
   cross-check ledger's running agreement, if the lane has been in use.
2. **The rule (exact comparisons on recorded counts).**
   - *Agreement clause* fires when agreeing cases × 10 < comparable cases × 9.
   - *Admission clause* fires when, at **every** window of 0.5 s or more (0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s), fewer than half of the
     reference failures are admitted; that is, there is no window of at least 0.5 s at which at least half are
     admitted. (Reading fixed before measurement: "fewer than half … at any window of 0.5 s or more" is taken as
     "at whichever such window is examined", since the lane needs only one usable window; 0.25 s does not
     count.) The clause is read per robot class and fires if it fires for the quadruped.
   - *Speed clause* fires when the lower bound of the paired bulk speedup is below 3, or when the stored ceiling
     is below 3.
   - KILL-ENSEMBLE fires when any clause fires on every device family measured. If it fires on one family and
     not another, the lane is withdrawn for the family where it fired and the ADR says so.
3. **Two decision points.** At the pilot: if the pilot's falsifier holds (fewer than half admitted at 0.5 s;
   agreement below 90%; ceiling below 3), the lane's work is not funded and the gate is recorded as fired at the
   pilot, subject to re-measurement on our tiers if the pilot ran on the development oracle. At M3: on the
   gate's figures, as above.
4. **If it fires: what changes.**
   | Item | Change |
   |---|---|
   | `--lane` on `autopsy.intervene` and `autopsy.attribute` | `tier-f32` leaves the schema (a new schema version). `auto`, the default, stays and is again the double-precision procedure, as in a build without the lane; `f64` stays as its explicit form |
   | Admission, lane execution, numerical-variant interventions, cross-check, bulk planner | Not shipped; their beads close with reason `fallback` and the fallback's name (the typed close of N41-17) |
   | A failure not reproduced in double precision | Labelled `precision-sensitive`, attribution refused (FROB-REF-584), the world passed to the artifact guard, unattributed |
   | The label `tier-specific: <variant>` and the label `marginal` | Removed from the label set; the artifact guard's own label `artifact-candidate` with the variant that removed the failure remains the only statement about such a world |
   | GATE-AUTOPSY-03 | Its row stays the one its recorder (N27b-09) wrote with the lane clause on this fallback (`fallback: double precision only`), now in force; the gate passes or fails on its double-precision clauses, and the ADR cites the row |
   | Existing lane attributions | Stay readable, are marked `lane: withdrawn`, and may not enter a report |
   | The 10⁻⁷ flip rate in double-precision attributions | Kept: it was never part of the lane |
   | Bulk attribution | Runs in double precision on `batch-cpu` (the 12 minutes to 2 hours of the cost arithmetic for 200 failures with pairs) |
5. **A partial outcome that is allowed, and one that is not.** If only the speed clause fires, the owner may
   keep a *reduced* lane for the single purpose of attributing failures that double precision does not
   reproduce (the benefit that is not a speed), but only by a dated amendment to the register made before the
   gate measurement it governs; decided afterwards it would be a quiet re-scoping, which the register forbids.
   Absent such an amendment on file before the first gate run, a fired speed clause kills the whole lane.
6. **If it does not fire.** The ADR records the three figures per device family, the pilot's figures, the fork
   lead adopted for the lane (the largest window with at least half admitted), and the cross-check's alarm rule
   as the standing guard. After the decision entry is in the register, this bead lifts GATE-AUTOPSY-03, whose
   recorder (N27b-09) wrote the lane clause on the fallback because no lane existed when it recorded: it runs
   `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations --
   GATE-AUTOPSY-03` at its own commit, the entry reading the lane clause from N27b-28's GATE-AUTOPSY-09 receipt
   (and the fallback for a device family where the decision fired), and records the part once more with
   `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-AUTOPSY-03 --receipt <hash>`: the one
   further recording N38b-19 allows the bead that lifts a row recorded against a fallback named in advance,
   by this bead's recording session, which implemented neither the lane nor N27b-09.
7. **The ADR** (`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ENSEMBLE-M3.md`: every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`, and a kill-gate
   decision is named by its gate and its milestone), with the required fields of the kill-gate record (N40-03):
   the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered, the pre-registered readings of step 2 and any
   dated amendment applied; each measured value with its gate-row and receipt hashes, set beside the trigger's
   number (agreement against 90%; admitted share against one half per window; speed bound and ceiling against
   3); the repair cycle used, if any; the outcome per device family; the fallback now in force and the name
   the capability carries from now on ("attribution in double precision only"); the beads re-read, each
   marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR" (the rows of step 4); the downstream changes;
   the date; the recording session, which is not a session that implemented the lane. The kill row of the gate
   register takes `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03; `receipts` the gate-row and
   receipt hashes the ADR quotes) with outcome `not-fired` or `fired` (`fired`
   also when it fires at the pilot, step 3; `not-reached` when KILL-AUTOPSY fired first and the lane was never
   measured), one entry per device family where the outcomes differ (`scope = "backend:<tier>"`, N40-03's kind
   `backend`: `backend:gpu-cuda`, `backend:gpu-metal`); when the outcome
   is `fired`, GATE-AUTOPSY-09 takes the status `killed` with the detail "KILL-ENSEMBLE: attribution in double
   precision only", through the register's own propagation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists, quotes the three measured figures per device family with their hashes and states one
      outcome per family.
- [ ] The decision rule gives the right answer at each boundary on synthetic gate rows: agreement 90 of 100 not
      fired, 89 of 100 fired; admitted 25 of 50 at 1 s (and fewer at 0.5 s and 2 s) not fired, 24 of 50 at every
      window fired; speed bound 3.00 not fired, 2.99 fired; ceiling 2.98 fired.
- [ ] If fired: every row of the table in step 4 is carried out or filed as a bead change; the verb registry no
      longer advertises `tier-f32`, and `auto` runs the double-precision procedure; the refusal script still
      passes with FROB-REF-584 raised directly for a failure not reproduced in double precision.
- [ ] If not fired: the lane's adoption status and fork lead are recorded, the alarm of the cross-check is
      active, and GATE-AUTOPSY-03 is recorded once more from a receipt at this bead's commit with its lane clause
      read from N27b-28's receipt; if fired, GATE-AUTOPSY-03's row is the one N27b-09 recorded on the fallback.
- [ ] The M3 exit record lists KILL-ENSEMBLE as decided, with its fallback.
- [ ] A reduced lane exists only if its amendment predates the first gate run (checked against the register's
      history).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_ensemble.rs` (the rule as a pure function over gate rows):
`agreement_boundary` (90/100, 89/100), `admission_boundary` (25/50 at one window; 24/50 at all),
`admission_ignores_quarter_second_window`, `speed_boundary` (3.00, 2.99), `ceiling_below_three_fires`,
`per_family_outcomes`, `late_amendment_refused`, `third_measurement_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own. It reads the `summary` line of `scripts/e2e/autopsy_lane_f32.sh` (`agreement_top` against
0.90, `admitted_share` per window against 0.5, `speedup_lower_bound` and `ceiling` against 3) and the pilot's
log; the ADR quotes them with receipt hashes. If it fires, `scripts/e2e/autopsy_refusals.sh` and
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_attribute_planted.sh` are re-run on the fallback and their logs attached.

### Known answers and negative controls
The boundary cases above are the negative controls of the rule (a rule that reads "any window" as "some window
fails" would kill a lane admitted at 1 s but not at 2 s; the `admission_boundary` test pins the reading). The
arithmetic behind the admission clause as a sanity check on the measured figures: at 0.16 per step and a 25 ms
period the growth bound of 1,666.7 (10⁻⁴ / 6 × 10⁻⁸) is reached after 46.4 steps, about 1.16 s, so windows of 0.5 s and 1 s are
expected to pass the growth clause and 2 s to fail it; a measurement far from this in either direction is
reported against OPEN-AUTOPSY-3. Determinism: exact integer and rational comparisons.

## Considerations

- KILL-ENSEMBLE depends on nothing in the coalition machinery, and KILL-AUTOPSY does not depend on the lane. If
  KILL-AUTOPSY fires first, fork-based attribution is reduced to a description and the lane that accelerates it
  is moot; this gate is then recorded with the outcome `not-reached` and the reason `moot: KILL-AUTOPSY` (the
  "recorded as moot" of N27b-11) and no measurement.
- The name of the kill gate comes from the idea of an ensemble of perturbed forks on the originating tier; its
  register row (N40-29) is decided at M3 and reads GATE-AUTOPSY-09.
- What would make us revisit after a kill: measured exponents of our loops falling with better controllers; a
  double-precision device tier (which would make the question disappear); a much larger ratio of device to CPU
  throughput on the fork workload.
