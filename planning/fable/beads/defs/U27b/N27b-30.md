## Background

A closed bead is not acceptance. FACT (recorded experience behind this rule): a tracker once showed 87 beads
closed within 12 seconds with one shared reason, while the figures came from a stand-in. Here a bead closes with
a typed reason that cites its own test, a gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold, and
exit reviews are done by a fresh-context session that did not implement the work. This bead is that review for Autopsy at M3: one
session, reading only records and re-running only scripts, that confirms every gate and kill gate the Autopsy specification
defines is on record with measured values, and that the capability may be described as implemented in the
program's sense, or says exactly what is missing.

The gates and kill gates of the Autopsy specification (thresholds are TARGETs until a gate records a measured value; claims as
the register gives them):

One classification is shared with the telemetry subsystem: the five fork classes are one set, defined by Autopsy
and implemented by the fork mechanism, with one refusal code for a warm-start graft (FROB-REF-582); the review
checks there is one implementation and one code, not two.

## Scope

In: the review protocol; the checklist; re-running the end-to-end scripts in a clean checkout; reading the gate
ledger, the ADRs and the bead logs; the report of status per gate; the honest-status statement for Autopsy; the
note carried forward to M4.
Out: fixing anything (findings become beads); the program-wide M3 exit review, which consumes this one; the M4
gate.

## Technical approach

1. **Who and how.** A session with fresh context that implemented none of the Autopsy beads, working from a
   clean checkout at the candidate commit, with read access to the gate ledger, the ADRs, the pilot register,
   the bead logs and the store. It may run scripts; it may not edit code, thresholds, suites or records.
2. **For each gate due at M2 or M3** (01 to 07 and 09): (a) the row in `registries/gates.toml` exists, cites a
   commit of this repository, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and its claim identifiers resolve in
   the artifact graph; (b) the status is `pass`, or `killed: fallback adopted` with the ADR; `not run`, `fail`
   and `blocked` are reported as such; (c) the measured values, not the targets, are in the row; (d) the gate's
   registered negative control was exercised and caught in the same run; (e) re-running the gate's script at the
   candidate commit reproduces the figures (digests and counts exactly; wall times inside the recorded band).
3. **For each kill gate**: the ADR exists at the milestone the register names, quotes the measurement it rests
   on, states the outcome, and was recorded by a session other than the implementer; if it fired, the fallback
   is what the verbs, schemas, documentation and reports now describe, by the name of the fallback.
4. **The eight conditions of "implemented"**, checked for the capability as a whole: code in `frob-autopsy`; a
   `CONTRACT.md` with ten sections (purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants, refusals, determinism class, evidence
   class of outputs, validity domain, oracles, known gaps); known-answer tests passing against derivations that
   were never regenerated from the candidate; outputs carrying the claim product; a CPU reference path (the
   default path is the CPU in double precision); reachability through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp` with
   documented schemas and exit codes for all five verbs; cancellation tested for the three `run` verbs; gates
   recorded with measured values. For the Frontier lane additionally: its pilot and its kill-gate decision are
   on record.
5. **Audits run by the reviewer.** The invariant auditor over every fork ledger produced by the scripts in this
   review (no violation of the six invariants); the known-answer completeness check (twelve rows bound, each
   with derivation and control); the refusal sweep (eight codes raised with their fields); the verb registry
   check (exactly five verbs under `autopsy`; no fork verb and no divergence verb); the citation guard (an
   attribution cited as established is refused); a spot check that three randomly chosen attributions replay
   from their transcripts to identical digests.
6. **Checks against false progress.** No Autopsy bead is closed with a reason that cites another bead's test;
   no gate row predates the commit that introduced its script; no threshold, suite or candidate list changed
   between a gate's first run and its recorded run except through one declared repair cycle; the planted suite's
   label-file hash equals the corpus's committed hash.
7. **Output.** `docs/delivery/reviews/autopsy-m3.md` and one line in the bead log: per gate the status and
   measured values; per kill gate the outcome; the eight conditions with any that fail named; findings as new
   beads; and the honest-status sentence: Autopsy is `implemented`, or `partial` with the missing items named,
   or described by the name of a fallback.
8. **Carried to M4.** GATE-AUTOPSY-08 is not due at M3. The review records that its fixture (the session script
   on planted-defect designs) exists and has not been tuned, and that, by the program's rule, if that gate
   fails at M4 the tools stay, because they are cheap and exact, and the claim about turns is withdrawn.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The review document exists, written by a session that implemented no Autopsy bead (the bead log's
      `recorded_by` differs from every implementing session).
- [ ] Each of GATE-AUTOPSY-01 to -07 and -09 has a status with measured values, or is listed as `not run`,
      `fail` or `blocked` with what is missing; none is reported as passed without a resolving row.
- [ ] KILL-AUTOPSY and KILL-ENSEMBLE each have an ADR and a ledger status.
- [ ] The five audits of step 5 ran in the review and their logs are attached.
- [ ] The eight conditions are answered one by one; the honest-status sentence follows from them.
- [ ] Re-run figures match the recorded ones as step 2(e) requires, or the mismatch is a finding.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None. The review's checklist is itself data (`docs/delivery/reviews/autopsy-m3.checklist.toml`) and a small test
in `frob-conformance` asserts that every gate and kill gate of the Autopsy specification appears in it exactly once and that
the document addresses every checklist item.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_exit_review.sh` runs, in a clean checkout: `autopsy_intervene.sh`,
`autopsy_attribute_planted.sh`, `autopsy_session.sh`, `autopsy_refusals.sh`, `autopsy_lane_f32.sh` (where a
device is present), then the audits of step 5, and compares each script's `summary` line with the gate ledger.
Its own log (`target/e2e-logs/autopsy_exit_review/<utc>.ndjson`) has one `assert` line per gate (row resolves;
status; measured values equal on re-run; negative control caught), one per kill gate, one per condition of
step 4, and a `summary` with gates touched (all ten due) and the honest-status sentence. On a mismatch: the gate,
the recorded value, the re-run value and both receipt hashes.

### Known answers and negative controls
The review is tested against planted defects in a rehearsal before it is trusted: a gate row whose claim
identifier does not resolve; a gate row older than its script; a kill gate with no ADR; a verb registry with a
sixth verb; a bead closed citing another bead's test. Each must appear as a finding in the rehearsal's report.
Determinism: the review's re-runs use the scripts' own lanes (twice on one machine; across worker counts).

## Considerations

- GATE-AUTOPSY-01, -02 and -07 are M2 gates carried by the fork and divergence beads; they are re-read here
  because Autopsy's M3 results rest on them, and because a regression between M2 and M3 would otherwise be
  invisible.
- If a device is absent on the review machine, GATE-AUTOPSY-09 is reviewed from its record only and the review
  says so; a record-only review of a gate is weaker than a re-run and is labelled.
- The review is deliberately redundant with the program-wide M3 exit review; it exists so that the subsystem's
  owner sees its own status before the program does.
