## Background

Autopsy tests a proposed cause of a simulated failure by intervention: it restores the failed world to a moment
before the failure (a fork point), applies one declared change `do(factor := value)`, runs forward, and counts
how often the failure no longer happens. A factor may be *named* only when its rescue rate is tested above the
placebo flip rate (the rate at which a fork with no intervention and one state coordinate perturbed at a
relative 10⁻¹² changes its verdict), at a stated level divided by the number of factors (invariant
INV-AUTOPSY-05). The program allows two tests for that: "an exact one-sided test at the fixed replicate count,
or an e-process when forks are bought one at a time". The verb `autopsy.intervene` (BD-279) builds the first: a
fixed table of R replicates per factor against P placebo forks. This bead builds the second.

Why a second test type exists. A fixed table spends R forks on every factor whether the answer is obvious after
three or still open after eight, and an agent that looks at a fixed table, buys more replicates and tests again
has raised its chance of naming a null factor above the stated level (optional stopping). An e-process is a
running product that may be inspected after every fork and stopped at any time without that inflation: under
the hypothesis "this factor rescues no more often than a placebo flips" it is a nonnegative supermartingale, so
the chance that it ever reaches 1/α′ is at most α′ (Ville's inequality). Rejected alternative: letting the
fixed test be repeated at growing R. The null calibration of the statistics bead shows the family-wise rate of
naming a null factor then exceeds α.

**ADR-AUTOPSY-01. A cause is what an intervention removes, net of a placebo.** The sequential mode keeps the
placebo inside every step: each bought fork of an intervention is paired with a placebo fork of its own.

Tier Solid (a betting e-process for a difference of two Bernoulli outcomes is established technique; construction
C-15 is the program's use of e-processes). Crate `frob-autopsy`, layer 5. This bead was split from BD-279 in
polishing: the verb with both test types was more than one piece of work.

## Scope

In: the test type `sequential` of `autopsy.intervene`: the options `--test sequential` and `--max-pairs`; the
plan in rounds of pairs; the two series and their child addresses; the per-factor e-process update and the
closing rule; the stop conditions; ledger rows that make the stopping time replayable; what the mode refuses
(coalitions, the 10⁻⁷ series); the refusal FROB-REF-553 for a factor requested under one test type after it was
run under the other, in both directions; the result fields; how `autopsy.attribute` reads a sequential set.
Out: the arithmetic of the e-process, its calibration under optional stopping and the reachability
enumeration (N27b-05, called here); admission, batch execution, the ledger and its header, intervention sets and
the factor family of a fork (BD-279, used unchanged); labels and ranking (N27b-06); the attribution record
(BD-280); the independent recomputation of the stopping rule on stored ledgers (the auditor, N27b-20); the
end-to-end steps (N27b-04, step 8; N27b-19, neighbour case); the verb's registry row (VERBS-autopsy, which
already lists `test`, `max_pairs` and FROB-REF-553 for the verb).

## Technical approach

1. **Surface.** `frob autopsy intervene <fork> --test sequential [--max-pairs 64] --do '<assign>' … --key <k>
   --json` (MCP properties `test: "sequential"`, `max_pairs`). Every other option of the verb keeps its meaning
   (BD-279). `--coalitions` defaults to `none` in this mode and `pairs` is refused; `--series-1e-7` is fixed at
   `never`; `--replicates` and `--placebo` are not read (the mode buys by rounds) and giving either is refused
   with FROB-REF-802 pointing at it, so that no caller believes a count was honoured.
2. **Admission.** The verb's admission order holds (licence, fork handle and null replay, precision route,
   parse and resolve with at most 12 factors, model difference, classification). At the place of its test-type
   step this bead adds:
   - *Type recorded first.* The ledger header of the set is written with `test: "sequential"`, α, the bet λ and
     the fork's factor-family size m (BD-279: the distinct single factors run from this fork up to and
     including this request) before any fork runs.
   - *FROB-REF-553* (a comparison that mixes study types, or re-reads fixed-n data as a sequential study). A
     request any of whose interventions, by canonical string, is already in an intervention set of this fork
     under the other test type is refused, exit 3, before any fork runs: `pointer` = `test`; `observed` = the
     stored type and the identifier of its ledger; `required` = "one test type per factor on a fork"; `retry:
     after-change`; ranked repairs: (1) `frob autopsy attribute <fork> --ledger <id>` to read the stored set;
     (2) repeat the request with the stored type; (3) run the other type from a fork point opened at another
     lead (`frob run fork <run> --world <w> --before failure --dt 0.5s`). The check runs for a `fixed` request
     as well as for a `sequential` one. Reason: forks bought until a stopping rule fires and a fixed table of
     the same factor are two looks at one hypothesis, and choosing the kinder of the two afterwards voids both
     levels.
   - *Reachability.* The least number of clean pairs (replicate rescued, placebo not flipped) at which a factor
     can close is ⌈ln(m/α) / ln(1.5)⌉: 8 at m = 1, 11 at m = 3, 14 at m = 10 and at m = 12. A request whose
     `--max-pairs` is below that number cannot close any factor and is refused with FROB-REF-802 pointing at
     `max_pairs`, with the least admissible value as the first repair, unless `--allow-unreachable` is given.
3. **Rounds of pairs.** The null fork runs first (child `null`, as in the fixed plan). Then, in rounds
   n = 1, 2, …, every factor still open receives one pair: one replicate of do(c), in the series `seq-rep`, and
   one placebo fork of its own, in the series `seq-placebo`, both with index n and both keyed by the factor's
   change-set hash, with independent keyed perturbations at a relative 10⁻¹² (the generator of N27b-03). The
   replicate of round 1 is the unperturbed fork of the intervention, the reference of its flip rate. All pairs
   of a round run as one mixed batch through the fork service, exactly as a phase of the fixed plan does.
4. **Update and closing rule.** After each round, per factor: X_n = 1 if the replicate is rescued (the
   requirement is satisfied), else 0; Y_n = 1 if the placebo fork's verdict differs from the parent's, else 0;
   an `unknown` outcome counts as not rescued for X and as flipped for Y (fail-closed, as in the fixed counts).
   E_n = E_{n−1}·(1 + λ·(X_n − Y_n)) with E_0 = 1 and the fixed bet λ = ½ (N27b-05), in exact rationals. A
   factor closes as *tested* at the first n with E_n ≥ m/α (α = 1/20 by default: thresholds 20, 60, 200 and 240
   at m = 1, 3, 10 and 12) and receives no further pair. A clean pair multiplies E by 3/2, an adverse pair
   (not rescued, placebo flipped) by 1/2, any other pair by 1.
5. **Stopping.** The run stops when every factor is closed, or after `--max-pairs` rounds (DESIGN default 64),
   or at the budget (a budget stop publishes no partial result, FROB-REF-702; the rounds already run stay in
   the store as replayable forks). A factor open at the stop is *not tested*: it is never reported as "nearly"
   and its label is `none`. Upper bound on forks, returned by `--plan-only`: 1 + 2·(number of factors)·
   `max_pairs` (1,537 for 12 factors at the default).
6. **Ledger.** Rows carry the series, the round and the factor; the header carries the test type, α, λ, m and
   `max_pairs`. A stored set therefore replays to the same stopping rounds on any machine of the tier, and the
   auditor (N27b-20) recomputes each E_n from the stored pairs without calling this code.
7. **Result.** The verb's result with, in place of the fixed series counts, per factor: `rounds`, `rescued`
   (count of n), `placebo_flips` (count of n), `e_value` as an exact fraction, `threshold`, `state` (`tested`
   or `open`), `closed_at` (the round, or null).
8. **How `autopsy.attribute` reads a sequential set.** The record's `test` block is `{"type": "sequential",
   "alpha", "lambda", "factors": m, "max_pairs"}`; each contrast row carries `rounds`, `rescued` as k/n,
   `contrast` = k/n − f/n with the factor's own placebo count f, `e_value` and `tested`. The flip rate at 10⁻¹²
   is the share of rounds 2 … n whose replicate verdict differs from round 1's, over n; the flip rate at 10⁻⁷ is
   `null` (the series does not exist in this mode). The placebo rate of the chaos clause is pooled over all
   factors' placebo forks. Labels then follow the label table unchanged (N27b-06): a closed factor with
   contrast at least 1/2 and a flip rate of at most 1/10 rescues; `joint` cannot be reached (no coalition is
   run) and the record says `coalitions: not-run`.
9. **Layout.** `crates/frob-autopsy/src/sequential.rs` (plan and loop), with the 553 check in `intervene.rs`
   and the reading rules in `attribute.rs`. Determinism: arithmetic class D0; children class D2 of their tier.
   Evidence: a sequential contrast is a replayable experiment, class `Tested` on its tier; an attribution drawn
   from it is `Estimated`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a fixture with a strong factor (every replicate rescued, no placebo flip) and null factors (never
      rescued, no placebo flip): the strong factor run alone (m = 1) closes as tested after exactly 8 rounds,
      and beside two null factors (m = 3) after exactly 11 (1.5⁸ = 25.6 ≥ 20 > 1.5⁷ = 17.1; 1.5¹¹ = 86.5 ≥ 60 >
      1.5¹⁰ = 57.7); the null factors are still open at `--max-pairs` with E = 1 and label `none`.
- [ ] A replay of the stored set stops at the same rounds and yields the same e-values as exact fractions.
- [ ] FROB-REF-553 is raised, before any fork runs and with the fields of step 2, when a factor already run as
      a fixed table is requested sequentially, and when a factor already run sequentially is requested as a
      fixed table; a different factor under the other type on the same fork is admitted.
- [ ] `--coalitions pairs`, `--series-1e-7 always`, `--replicates` and `--placebo` are each refused in this
      mode with FROB-REF-802 pointing at the option.
- [ ] `--max-pairs 7` at m = 1 is refused as unreachable (8 clean pairs are the least) and admitted with
      `--allow-unreachable`.
- [ ] `autopsy.attribute` on a sequential set prints the `test` block of step 8, names the strong factor
      `but-for` and no null factor, and prints `null` for every 10⁻⁷ flip rate.
- [ ] Null calibration on real forks: on a failing split-grip world of fixture 2 with ten decoy factors (the
      friction of patches off the route), no factor closes within 64 rounds and every label is `none`.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob autopsy intervene --json` and the MCP tool with equal envelopes; cancellation
      drains at a round boundary; `CONTRACT.md` documents the mode and its stopping rule.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-autopsy/tests/sequential.rs` (real forks on fixture 2, the quadruped on the split-grip landing;
host fixtures `synthetic`):
- `sequential_rounds_to_cross`: the 8-round and 11-round cases of the first criterion; `e_values_are_exact`
  (after 8 clean pairs E = 6561/256).
- `adverse_pair_costs_two_rounds`: one adverse pair among clean ones at m = 1 closes at round 11 (ten clean
  pairs and one adverse give 1.5¹⁰·0.5 = 28.8 ≥ 20, while nine clean and one adverse give 19.2).
- `sequential_open_at_cap`: a null factor ends `open` with E = 1 at round 64 and is labelled `none`.
- `sequential_replays_stop`: the stored set replayed in a fresh state root stops at the same rounds.
- `sequential_refuses_pairs`, `sequential_refuses_second_series`, `sequential_refuses_counts`.
- `fixed_reread_as_sequential_553` (a factor run as fixed, then requested with `--test sequential`) and
  `sequential_reread_as_fixed_553` (the reverse); `other_factor_other_type_admitted`.
- `max_pairs_below_reach_refused`: m = 1 with 7; m = 12 with 13.
- `unknown_is_fail_closed`: an `unknown` replicate counts as not rescued, an `unknown` placebo as flipped.
- `attribute_reads_sequential_set`: the `test` block, k/n and f/n per factor, the labels, `null` at 10⁻⁷.
- `decoys_name_nothing_in_64_rounds` (ten decoy factors on a failing split-grip world).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_intervene.sh` (N27b-04, step 8) runs one sequential request on a failing split-grip world
through the real binary with `--key`, asserts the header's test type, the round at which each factor closed
against m/α, the replay to the same rounds, and FROB-REF-553 for the same factors requested as a fixed table.
`scripts/e2e/autopsy_refusals.sh` (N27b-19) raises FROB-REF-553 in the other direction. Logged per factor as
`metric` lines of the shared helper: `rounds_to_close`, `e_value` against the threshold m/α, `rescued`,
`placebo_flips`; the refusal line carries code, pointer, observed type and ledger, and the first repair.

### Known answers and negative controls
The least numbers of clean pairs to cross: 8, 11, 14 and 14 at m = 1, 3, 10 and 12 (1.5¹³ = 194.6 < 200 ≤
1.5¹⁴ = 291.9). Negative controls (planted defects the tests must catch): a loop that closes a factor one round
early (round 7 at m = 1: E = 17.1 < 20; also the auditor's mutant); a loop that keeps a closed factor's
placebo forks for the other factors (the per-factor pairing is lost and the null factors' E moves); a mode
that computes a fixed p-value at the stopping round (the calibration under optional stopping of N27b-05
exceeds α); a 553 check that compares request strings and not canonical strings (the same factor spelled with
other whitespace passes). Determinism lanes: the same set twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and
release on `batch-cpu`.

## Considerations

- The mode buys two forks per round and factor, so a strong single factor closes after 16 forks where the
  fixed table spends 8 on it and 64 on the placebo: for a few factors the modes cost about the same, and the
  sequential mode pays off when the caller wants to stop as soon as the answer is clear or to keep buying on a
  budget. It answers single-factor questions only; a joint cause needs the fixed table with pairs.
- The bet λ = ½ and the level α are constants of the decision policy record (N27b-12); changing either is a new
  policy version, and the header of every set records the values it ran under.
- A factor open at the cap is not evidence that it is not a cause. The record says `open`, the label is `none`,
  and the next action offers more rounds (a new request on the same fork continues from the stored rounds,
  whose children are read by address) or the fixed table from a fork point at another lead.
- If KILL-AUTOPSY fires (decision N27b-11), fork tables are described and not labelled: the mode stays as a way
  to buy forks in pairs, its e-values are printed as description, and no factor is labelled from them.
- Open question OPEN-AUTOPSY-1 (replicate counts and perturbation sizes; owner: Autopsy lead; default 8
  replicates at 10⁻¹²) does not set this mode's counts, which follow from α, λ and m; its perturbation size
  does apply.
