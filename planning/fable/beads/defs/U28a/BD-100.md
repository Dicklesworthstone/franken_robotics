## Background

Two of the studio's static analyses, run in seconds before any simulation, both inside `design.check`.

**A-02 Mass roll-up** answers: what are the mass, the centre of mass and the inertia of every subtree, and are
the numbers physically possible? A rigid body's ten inertial parameters are equivalent to the 4×4 pseudo-inertia
matrix 𝖯 = [[Σ, h], [hᵀ, m]], with Σ = ∫ x·xᵀ dm = ½·tr(Ī)·I − Ī the second moment of mass (Ī the rotational
inertia about the frame origin), h = m·c the first moment, m the mass. The parameters are physically consistent
exactly when 𝖯 is positive definite. Positivity is necessary and not sufficient for a declared envelope: a body
whose mass lies inside a ball of centre o and radius ρ also satisfies the mass-support bound
‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ², whose left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o (I_C the inertia
about the centre of mass). By default inertias are not parameters at all: they are computed from geometry,
fill and material, so an agent designs shapes; `declared` bodies (imports) are the ones this check polices.

**A-08 Static stability** answers: what are the support margins, the tip-over angles on slopes, and the margin
of the contact-wrench cone with payload? Static stability is a cone program in the contact forces. Its result is
typed `exact`, `inner` or `outer` and names its load-sharing model, because the same body gives different
answers under different authority: with independently commanded foot forces one stance needs 155 N at the worst
foot; with equal passive springs the same stance loads the feet (175, 115, 85, 25) N against a 160 N rating.

Rejected shortcut, kept as a negative control: support polygon plus total actuator capacity as a stance screen.
In an exhaustive enumeration of 11,520 body-and-load cases it accepts 1,596 bodies that the exact classifier
rejects (FACT, plan-time pilot; first example: inverse half-sizes u = v = 2 per metre, foot ratings
(80, 80, 80, 160) N, load vertex (60, 30) N·m). Per-contact force limits must enter the program.

Seed done-condition: known answers; a planted top-heavy design is flagged; a point-foot biped is reported, not
refused.

## Scope

In: both analyses as implementations of the analysis contract (N28a-04), their findings and repairs, their
registration for preflight stages 2 and 6. For A-02 that is: wrapping the three body checks of `frob-model`
as findings, the solid-fill finding that wraps N13-04's plausibility verdict (FROB-REF-168), and the report
of the roll-up per subtree (centre of mass,
inertia about the centre of mass, principal moments), derived from summaries this bead reads and does not
compute. Out: the three body checks themselves (`declared::consistency` with its nearest consistent matrix,
`support::check` over every enclosing set of the Region, `massless::check`) and the codes FROB-REF-104 and
FROB-REF-106 they raise: N14a-13, a dependency, written once there; A-02 calls them and holds no second copy.
Likewise the plausibility rule and its code FROB-REF-168: N13-04, a dependency.
Also out: the fold itself, that is, the per-subtree summaries of mass, first moment and pseudo-inertia
(with actuator, battery and sensor card masses at their mounts), their memoization, and the comparison with
the design's budgets that raises `FROB-REF-115`: the budget functor of `frob-model` (N14a-10, a dependency).
There is one fold over the tree in the workspace, and A-02 holds no second one. Also out: the
geometry-to-inertia functor and the fill rule (BD-062); the cone trait and solver (BD-050, BD-115); the inertia cone and mass-support predicate primitives (BD-051); the
exhaustive stance battery (N28a-06); the whole-family stance query of the mission subsystem (BD-130); co-design
closure and actuator envelope (BD-103). Order of work: the cone program of A-08 is posed on the `Cone` trait
of BD-050 and the contract of the contact wrench cone, both dependencies; the dense solver BD-115 is a
higher-numbered seed, so it is a related bead and not a dependency, and the A-08 acceptance lines that need a
solve are closed when both beads exist.

## Technical approach

**A-02, numbered.**
1. Per body, read the pseudo-inertia from the model (exact rationals where the geometry is rational).
2. Positivity: call `frob_model::geom::declared::consistency` (N14a-13): exact LDLᵀ of 𝖯 without pivoting, all
   four pivots positive. On failure wrap its result as finding `A-02.<body>.inconsistent`, refusing code
   FROB-REF-104, carrying what the function returns: the index and value of the offending pivot, the smallest
   eigenvalue (double precision, for the message) and the nearest consistent matrix (𝖯 with its eigenvalues
   below ε·tr(𝖯) raised to that value, ε = 10⁻⁶, converted back to mass, centre of mass and inertia and offered
   as a typed edit the user must accept; never applied in silence). Equivalent hand check reported with it:
   principal moments obey the triangle inequalities I₁ + I₂ ≥ I₃ and permutations.
3. Mass support, for bodies that declare a mass envelope (a Region; never a collision proxy): call
   `frob_model::geom::support::check` (N14a-13), which tests tr(𝖯·Q) ≥ 0 exactly for every enclosing set Q
   of the Region (the sphere or ellipsoid itself; for a box the circumscribed sphere and the three slabs; for
   a cylinder the circumscribed sphere, the end slab and the tube; for a capsule the sphere of radius L/2 + r
   and the tube; for unions, differences and meshes the bounding sphere and bounding box). For a sphere of
   centre o and radius ρ the test reads ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ ρ². A violation is wrapped as
   `A-02.<body>.mass-support`, code FROB-REF-106, naming the enclosing set that failed, with the required and
   allowed mean squared distance. A body with no mass Region is reported `mass-support: not checked`.
4. Roll-up by subtree, leaves to root: m = Σ mᵢ; c = Σ mᵢ·cᵢ/m; pseudo-inertias add after transport to the
   subtree frame (𝖯 transforms as G·𝖯·Gᵀ under a rigid transform G, so the sum is exact). The sums are not
   formed here: they are the per-subtree summaries in the budget section of the compiled model (N14a-10:
   `BudgetSection.per_subtree`, each with mass, first moment h and pseudo-inertia in the subtree root frame,
   and with the card masses of actuators, batteries and sensors included at their mounts). A-02 reads a
   summary and derives its outputs from it: c = h/m; the rotational inertia about the frame origin from the
   pseudo-inertia (Ī = tr(Σ)·I − Σ), moved to the centre of mass by the parallel-axis rule; its principal
   moments. Output per subtree: mass, centre of mass, inertia about the centre of mass, principal moments,
   with the summary's provenance and any moment bound carried through. Because the summaries include card
   masses, the roll-up's total is the same number the mass budget is checked against.
5. Massless chains: call `frob_model::geom::massless::check` (N14a-13). A joint with at least one degree of
   freedom whose moved subtree has zero composite mass, or zero inertia sᵀ·I·s about one of its screws s,
   makes the joint-space inertia singular; it is wrapped as `A-02.<chain>.massless` and refuses at stage 2
   with FROB-REF-104 naming the joint (the format's rule: stage 2 refuses it), with the repairs ranked as
   N14a-13 ranks them (give the distal body a fill: `set-fill`; `set-material`).
6. Solid-fill plausibility: read the verdict of N13-04's check (computed in `frob-model` after each body's
   mass, under the rule decided in N13-03) and wrap it; A-02 holds no second copy of the rule. When it fires (a
   `solid` fill without a statement of intent, the design declares `mass_max`, and m_solid > mass_max/4,
   decided exactly), the finding `A-02.<body>.solid-fill` has severity `Refusing(FROB-REF-168)` and carries the
   body path, m_solid, the bound mass_max/4, `mass_max`, the illustrative 2.5 mm shell mass, and N13-04's
   ranked repairs (`{shell: t}`, `{lattice: φ}`, `{solid: {intended: true}}`, change material or Region). An
   intended solid leaves N13-04's report line and no finding. Without `mass_max` the verdict is
   `plausibility: not-evaluated`, and the same kind is raised at Low as the density warning of the fill rule,
   with the shell alternative and its mass, for a solid primitive body whose 2.5 mm shell would weigh less
   than a quarter of its solid mass (DESIGN: the rule's factor of four applied to the shell hint the check
   already computes, so the warning fires on enclosures and not on bars: the 0.4 × 0.3 × 0.2 m box, 3.45 of
   64.8 kg, is warned; a 0.02 × 0.02 × 0.3 m bar, whose shell keeps 45% of its mass, is not).
7. Budget feed: total mass against the mass budget. The comparison and its finding are the budget functor's
   (N14a-10), called by preflight stage 6, to which the budget refusal FROB-REF-115 belongs; A-02 prints the
   total beside the budget and raises nothing of its own for it.
Evidence `Tested` (exact), `Certified` when the LDLᵀ certificate is emitted; determinism D0; stage budget 50 ms
(TARGET, 95th percentile).

**A-08, numbered.** Inputs: the model at its reference stance (or stances the task declares), contact sites with
friction μ and per-contact normal-force limits cᵢ (from foot ratings, or from actuator torque through the leg
Jacobian at the stance), the payload set, the slopes the task requires, and the authority model.
1. Support geometry: project contacts and the centre of mass along gravity; the support polygon is the convex
   hull. Support margin d = signed distance from the projected centre of mass to the nearest edge (positive
   inside). Tip-over angle about edge e: θ_e = atan(d_e/h_e), with d_e the horizontal distance to the edge and
   h_e the height of the centre of mass above it. On a slope of angle φ across that edge the margin is θ_e − φ.
   With payload (m_p, c_p) the centre of mass is (m·c + m_p·c_p)/(m + m_p).
2. Wrench feasibility, authority `independent-normal-force`: find contact forces fᵢ with ‖f_t,i‖ ≤ μ·f_n,i,
   0 ≤ f_n,i ≤ cᵢ, Σ fᵢ = −W, Σ pᵢ × fᵢ = −c × W, where W is the weight (with payload). A second-order cone
   program in the shared solver. Reported: feasible or not; the load margin λ* (largest factor by which the
   weight can be scaled and still be balanced; the margin is λ* − 1); the friction utilization
   minimax ‖f_t,i‖/(μ·f_n,i); the least achievable worst-contact utilization min max f_n,i/cᵢ; and a witness
   allocation. The feasible allocations form a set, not a point (four normal forces under three balance
   equations leave a segment), so the witness is fixed by rule, not by the solver's path (DESIGN, so that the
   reported forces are reproducible across solvers): take the binding contact (the one with the largest
   utilization at the min-max allocation), solve for the minimum and the maximum of its normal force over the
   feasible set (ties broken lexicographically by contact index), and report both extreme allocations and
   their midpoint as the witness. On the stance fixture at b = 1/4 m the ++ force ranges over [150, 160] N,
   the extremes are (150, 140, 110, 0) N and (160, 130, 100, 10) N, the witness is (155, 135, 105, 5) N, and
   the least worst-foot load is 150 N. Infeasibility returns the dual separating vector, re-checked in exact
   rationals for flat ground (where the program is linear).
3. Authority `passive-equal-spring`: forces are not free. For contacts on rigid planar ground with equal
   springs the deflections are affine in position, f_n,i = k·(α + β·xᵢ + γ·yᵢ); solve the three balance
   equations for (α, β, γ), then check 0 ≤ f_n,i ≤ cᵢ. For four feet at (±a, ±b) this is the compatibility
   equation f₊₊ + f₋₋ = f₊₋ + f₋₊.
4. Typing: with exact rational data and a decided program the result is `exact` for the static model under the
   stated authority; with outward-rounded contact-site uncertainty it is `inner` (feasible for all sites) or
   `outer`. The authority model is always in the result. A result is never transported between authorities.
5. Findings: `A-08.margin` when a static margin the task requires is not met (refusing code FROB-REF-124 inside
   `check`), with the binding edge or contact; repairs ranked by the contract: widen the stance (`move-mount`),
   lower mass (`set-fill`, `set-material`), move the payload (`move-payload`), raise a foot rating
   (`swap-actuator`, `regear`).
6. Classes with no static stability: when the support polygon has zero area in every declared stance (a
   point-foot biped in single or double support), A-08 reports `A-08.no-static-stability` at severity Info
   with the polygon's dimension, and does not refuse: the archetype `biped-point` declares no static margin
   requirement, and refusing would reject every dynamic walker.
Stage 6 budget 600 ms shared with A-10 and A-11 (TARGET); a stance set that exhausts it returns `undecided`.

## Considerations

- A static projection certifies neither run-up nor landing nor recovery; an `outer` exclusion is an exclusion of
  the static requirement in the declared stance only (the enclosure rule of the contract applies).
- General lateral friction on slopes makes the program conic, not linear; exact re-checking is then by the
  solver's dual certificate with outward rounding, and the result is `inner` or `outer`, not `exact`.
- KILL-STATIC (decided at M3): if static verdicts do not predict simulated failure on the design corpus, the
  refusing severity of `A-08.margin` becomes an advisory lint.
- RISK-32 (common-mode card error): foot ratings from `assumed` cards make the result `Estimated`; the finding
  says which card fields it rests on.
