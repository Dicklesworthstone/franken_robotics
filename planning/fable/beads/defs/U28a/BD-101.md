## Background

A-01 answers: how many degrees of freedom does a mechanism really have, and is any loop over-constrained? For an
open tree the answer is the sum of joint freedoms. For closed loops (a parallel knee linkage, a steering
linkage, a Sarrus lift) the textbook Grübler–Kutzbach count M = 6·(n − 1 − j) + Σ fᵢ (n links, j joints, fᵢ the
freedoms of joint i) is wrong exactly for the linkages designers like to use:

| Linkage | n | j | Σ fᵢ | Count | True mobility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sarrus (two limbs of three parallel revolutes each, in planes with different normals) | 6 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 1 |
| Bennett (four revolutes with skew axes satisfying a metric condition) | 4 | 4 | 4 | −2 | 1 |
| Planar four-bar (four parallel revolutes), counted spatially | 4 | 4 | 4 | −2 | 1 |

A count of zero or less would make `design.check` refuse a working mechanism; a count that is too high would
pass a locked one. A-01 therefore computes mobility from the constraint Jacobian in exact arithmetic [Solid],
and is later cross-checked against the structural answer of the subgroup lattice [Frontier; N13-28 at M3,
which fills a field this bead defines]. Configuration space is a
product of Lie groups; a joint whose screws are closed under the bracket is a connected subgroup of SE(3) in one
of twelve conjugacy classes (identity; line translation; rotation about an axis; helical; planar translation;
cylindrical; spatial translation; planar gliding SE(2); spherical SO(3); translating screw; Schönflies; SE(3)).
Intersections and products in that lattice give the right mobility for exceptional over-constrained linkages
whose mobility follows from subgroup structure (Sarrus) and return no verdict for paradoxical ones (Bennett,
Bricard), whose mobility rests on metric conditions and is decided by the rank test alone.

Rejected: the count alone (wrong as above); floating-point rank with a tolerance as the primary method (a rank
decision at a tolerance is a guess near a singular pose; exact rationals are affordable at this size).

Seed done-condition: Sarrus 1, Bennett 1, planar four-bar 1.

## Scope

In: the exact rank method, the result field and call site through which the lattice route reports when it
exists, findings and repairs, registration in preflight stage 3. Out: the Hervé classification of a screw basis
(BD-046); the lattice route itself [F]: lattice operations, the common-subgroup and limb rules, lattice
mobility and the agreement rule between lattice and rank (N13-28, at M3, behind its pilot N13-27, which
depends on this bead); singularities (A-06, BD-105); self-collision (A-13, BD-104); bounded exact linkage
synthesis (BD-361). The rank method is Solid and ships at M1 whatever the pilot decides.

## Technical approach

1. **Loops.** From the model's kinematic graph take a fundamental cycle basis (one cycle per closure, by the
   spanning tree the compiler already built). For cycle ℓ with joints k = 1…n_ℓ, each joint contributes its
   screws S_k,1…S_k,f_k expressed in one common frame at the assembled configuration q.
2. **Constraint Jacobian.** The closure condition differentiates to Σ_k Σ_i S_k,i(q)·θ̇_k,i = 0 per cycle, with
   signs by traversal direction. Stacking all cycles gives A(q) (6·L rows for L cycles, one column per joint
   coordinate); joints shared by two cycles share columns.
3. **Mobility.** M(q) = (number of joint coordinates) − rank A(q), plus 6 for a floating base when the caller
   asks for the unconstrained count. The reported mobility is the minimum of M(q) over the sample points
   (the generic rank is the maximal one; a singular sample over-reports).
4. **Exact sample points.** At least three assembled configurations: the reference configuration of the design
   (closure constructors emit an exactly assembled reference) and two more reached by exact motion: joint
   angles are represented by tangent half-angles t = tan(θ/2) ∈ ℚ, so cos θ = (1 − t²)/(1 + t²) and
   sin θ = 2t/(1 + t²) are rational; for a one-degree-of-freedom loop the dependent half-angles are solved
   exactly in ℚ or in a declared real quadratic extension. Rank by fraction-free Gaussian elimination. Where no
   exact assembled point can be produced within budget, the rank is computed in outward-rounded interval
   arithmetic on a numerically assembled point and the result is classed `Tested` with its tolerance and the
   flag `numeric-rank`.
5. **Lattice field [F hook].** The result carries `lattice: not-run | decided(n) | undecided(reason)`. At M1
   it is always `not-run`: the call site is compiled behind feature `frontier-lattice-mobility` of `frob-lie`
   (off by default) and the lattice computation, its rules (a loop whose joints all lie in one subgroup H of
   dimension d has mobility Σ fᵢ − d; parallel limbs whose bonds are subgroups give dim of their meet plus
   idle mobilities; otherwise `undecided`) and the agreement rule applied when a decided lattice answer
   differs from the rank are N13-28's, which fills this field at M3. This bead fixes the field's schema and
   guarantees that with the feature off the output is exactly the rank result. The rank is always computed
   and is always the arbiter.
6. **Outputs.** Per loop and in total: mobility, the Grübler–Kutzbach count for comparison, the number of
   redundant constraints (mobility − count, when positive: the linkage is over-constrained and needs geometric
   accuracy to assemble), the null-space basis of A (the instantaneous motions), and `first_order: true` (the
   rank test is first-order; a shaky linkage with infinitesimal but no finite mobility is reported with this
   caveat when sample points disagree).
7. **Findings and refusals (stage 3).** Mobility below one for a closure the design declares as moving:
   `A-01.<loop>.locked`, refusing code FROB-REF-110 (a closure that over-constrains the mechanism), repairs
   ranked: remove the closure (`closure-remove`), replace a revolute by a joint of higher freedom, correct the
   axis that breaks the special geometry (with the nearest geometry at which the rank drops, when one exists
   within the tolerance class of the design). Over-constrained but mobile: `A-01.<loop>.over-constrained`
   (Info), with the redundant constraints named, because such a linkage binds under tolerance. Mobility above
   the number of actuated coordinates: `A-01.under-actuated` (Info). A joint whose declared class disagrees with
   its screw basis is FROB-REF-101, reported by the classification this analysis calls.
8. **Class and budget.** D0; `Tested` (exact), stage budget 400 ms shared with A-06 and A-13 (TARGET).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sarrus: mobility 1 where the count gives 0; Bennett: mobility 1 where the count gives −2; planar four-bar:
      mobility 1 (spatial count −2), each at three exact sample points.
- [ ] The result carries `lattice: not-run` on every fixture at M1, and the report prints the
      Grübler–Kutzbach count beside the rank mobility, labelled as a count that is wrong for over-constrained
      linkages (the lattice values Sarrus 1, four-bar 1, Bennett `undecided` are N13-28's acceptance).
- [ ] A Bennett linkage with one twist angle perturbed off the metric condition has mobility 0 and is refused
      with FROB-REF-110, with the repair that restores the condition.
- [ ] An open tree reports mobility equal to its joint freedoms and no loop findings.
- [ ] An Ackermann steering linkage reports closure mobility 1. The `ackermann` archetype is an M3 template
      (N14b-03), so the M1 test uses a committed synthetic fixture (`crates/frob-analyze/fixtures/a01/
      ackermann-steer.fbot`, labelled `synthetic`: axle beam, two steering knuckles and a tie rod, a four-bar
      closure), and N14b-03 re-runs the assertion on the template when it lands.
- [ ] Results carry `first_order`, the count, the redundant-constraint number and a null-space basis; the
      analysis meets the contract (derived memo key; D0) and its stage budget on this bead's fixtures (the
      per-archetype stage-3 time is BD-110's).
- [ ] `frob analyze mobility <design> --json` is schema-valid (exit 2 on the perturbed Bennett: a verb never
      refuses on a finding); `A-01.<loop>.locked` is registered for stage 3 with severity
      `Refusing(FROB-REF-110)`, so `design.check` stops at stage 3 with exit 3 (asserted end to end by
      N28a-29's script once the runner N28ap1-01 and BD-110 assemble the stages).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/a01_mobility.rs`:
- `sarrus_rank_1_count_0`, `bennett_rank_1_count_minus_2`, `four_bar_rank_1`.
- `bennett_perturbed_locked_110`: twist angle changed so that sin α/a ≠ sin β/b → mobility 0.
- `open_chain_sum_of_freedoms`: a 3R arm → 3.
- `shared_joint_two_loops`: a planar Watt six-bar (six links, seven revolutes, two cycles that share joints):
  mobility 1; a planar five-bar (one cycle, five revolutes): mobility 2.
- `singular_sample_does_not_lower_mobility`: a parallelogram four-bar sampled at its flattened change-point
  pose (all four joint axes coplanar and collinear in the plane) has M(q) = 2 there; the minimum over samples
  is 1.
- `numeric_rank_flagged`: a loop with no exact assembled point returns `Tested` and `numeric-rank`.
- `lattice_field_not_run_at_m1`: with the feature off, every fixture reports `lattice: not-run` and a result
  byte-identical to the rank-only result (the lattice tests, including the planted meet-as-join defect, are
  N13-28's).
- Property: mobility is invariant under a change of the common frame and under reversing a cycle's traversal.

The `design check` leg on the perturbed Bennett (stage 3, exit 3, code 110) needs the runner and the assembly
and is logged `e2e_skip 'needs BD-110'`; N28a-29 asserts it. NDJSON at

`script`, `bead` = BD-101, `step` = `a01_mobility`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's `evidence` block); metric lines carry per loop the count, the mobility against its known value, the
sample points used, the `lattice` field and `first_order`, and the analysis's wall time against its 400 ms

as the mobility, which the Sarrus step must catch.

### Known answers and negative controls

## Considerations

- The lattice route is Frontier and is not built here: its pilot (N13-27: Sarrus and Bennett by hand, and one
  over-constrained leg linkage, against the exact rank; falsifier: one wrong mobility) must pass before N13-28
  starts; if it fails, the fallback named in advance is the rank alone, which is what this bead ships. Its
  ceiling is a correct structural explanation where the count is wrong; no speed is claimed.
- First-order analysis cannot distinguish a shaky structure from a mechanism; the report says `first_order`
  and offers the referee simulation of the loop as the next action when samples disagree.
- Closed loops on the lockstep physics tiers are compliant rows; a mechanism this analysis calls
  over-constrained will carry internal forces there, which the fidelity ledger measures.
