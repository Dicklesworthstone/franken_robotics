## Background

A-07 answers, for a wheeled or tracked base and before any simulation: which motions can it produce, how
expensive is a small sideways correction, how much room does a turn need, and will it pass a door or an aisle?

Rolling without slipping gives Pfaffian constraints A(q)·q̇ = 0 on the configuration q, which define a
distribution 𝒟 = ker A of allowed velocities. The flag 𝒟 ⊂ 𝒟 + [𝒟, 𝒟] ⊂ … (adding Lie brackets of allowed
vector fields) has a growth vector, the list of its dimensions. It decides controllability (the base can reach
every configuration exactly when the flag reaches the full dimension; Chow–Rashevskii) and cost: a direction
first reached at bracket depth w needs path length of order ε^(1/w) to move a distance ε (ball-box theorem).
So a differential drive, growth vector (2, 3), can move sideways but pays a square root: small corrections are
relatively the most expensive.

Why the growth vector and not another test. A defect such as a mecanum base assembled with all four roller axes
parallel is a rank drop in the map from body twist to wheel rates, from 3 to 2: the wheels command translation
along one direction and rotation, and the base slides freely along the other. Its controlled distribution has
growth vector (2, 3) where an omnidirectional base must have (3). A connection-curvature test applied to the
same base passes, as it passes a differential drive, because the missing direction is still reached by a
bracket. Curvature (analysis A-09) is the right test for a snake-like crawler and the wrong one for a wheel
layout; preflight stage 4 therefore runs the growth vector on wheeled and tracked bases.

Tier: Solid for the growth vector, the turning footprint and the closed-form shift cost; Frontier for the
maneuverability index as a ranking signal and for bracket motion primitives (feature
`frontier-maneuverability-index`). Seed done-condition: the growth vectors of the rolling-distribution
construction; a mecanum base with parallel rollers reports (2, 3) where (3) is required.

## Scope

In: the distribution from the model's wheel and roller contacts, exact bracket computation, the growth vector,
the task-class requirement check with refusal FROB-REF-118, turning footprint, sideways cost, scene-free
passability widths, the index behind its feature. Out: frictional wheel contact and slip (the contact
subsystem); the wheeled baseline controller (BD-117); connection curvature (BD-243); route feasibility in a
scene (BD-233, BD-244).

## Technical approach

1. **Fields.** From the kinematic model build, for the planar base state q = (x, y, θ, steering and trailer
   angles…), the vector fields g₁…g_m that span 𝒟: one per independent wheel-group input, obtained as a basis of
   ker A(q) with A assembled from each wheel's no-slip rows (a plain wheel: no lateral slip and rolling; a
   roller wheel: no slip along the roller's constrained direction only). For omnidirectional layouts first
   compute the 3-column map J from body twist ξ = (v_x, v_y, ω) to wheel rates; rank J is reported.
   Representation: polynomial fields in the coordinates and in (c, s) = (cos, sin) of each angle with
   c² + s² = 1, coefficients in ℚ; tan of a steering angle enters as s/c.
2. **Brackets.** [f, g] = Dg·f − Df·g, computed symbolically on the polynomial representation.
3. **Flag and growth vector.** 𝒟₁ = span{gᵢ}; 𝒟_{k+1} = 𝒟_k + [𝒟₁, 𝒟_k]; n_k = rank 𝒟_k evaluated at exact
   rational sample points (angles by rational half-angle tangents), by fraction-free elimination. Stop when the
   rank reaches dim Q or stops growing. The growth vector is (n₁, n₂, …). At least three sample points must
   agree (a regular point); a sample at which the rank is lower is reported as a singular configuration.
4. **Requirement check.** Each archetype declares the growth vector its class requires (omnidirectional: (3);
   differential drive: (2, 3); car-like: (2, 3, 4)); a task class may require a direction outright (a lateral
   docking move). When the computed vector falls short, finding `A-07.motion-missing`, refusing code
   **FROB-REF-118** (the base cannot produce a motion the task class requires) inside `check`, with the missing
   direction (a vector in the body frame spanning the uncontrolled complement) and ranked repairs: the roller
   edit (`set-param` on roller angle), the steering edit, or the joint edit that restores the rank, each
   verified by re-running steps 1 to 3 on the edited model.
5. **Turning footprint.** Differential drive and omnidirectional: the radius of the disc swept by the footprint
   in an in-place turn about the wheel-axis midpoint, max over footprint vertices of the distance to that point.
   Car-like: minimum turning radius R = L/tan δ_max for wheelbase L and maximum steer angle δ_max, with the
   swept annulus between the inner and outer footprint radii.
6. **Cost of a sideways shift** (differential drive, half-track b, lateral shift d; ideal rolling). The
   four-move manoeuvre rotate by α, drive, rotate back, reverse costs wheel travel 2·α·b + d·cot(α/2); for
   small α this is 2·α·b + 2·d/α, least at α = √(d/b) with cost 4·√(d·b). The analysis reports the exact
   minimum over α (a one-dimensional minimization in closed form derivative) and the small-angle value. It is
   an upper bound on the optimum (one admissible path). An omnidirectional base pays d for d.
7. **Passability widths without a scene.** Straight passage: footprint width plus the declared clearance.
   In-place turn: twice the turning-footprint radius. These are necessary conditions, typed `outer`: a corridor
   narrower than the straight-passage width excludes; wider proves nothing. With a scene bound the question
   goes to route feasibility (A-18) and its two-sided answer.
8. **The index [F].** I(d) = (least wheel travel among registered bracket manoeuvres that realize a
   displacement d in the worst required direction)/d, at reference shifts d ∈ {0.01, 0.10} m; 1 for growth
   vector (3). It is labelled a screening signal and may be used to rank bases only after its pilot passes.
9. **Honest limits written into the output.** (a) A bracket direction is infinitesimal and assumes reversible
   inputs: with ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1] the rank is one and negative x is still unreachable, so a finite mission needs an
   integrated witness path; the result carries `reversible_inputs: assumed`. (b) Ideal rolling is a regime: a
   four-wheel fixed-axis base with axles at ±a satisfies lateral no-slip only if v_y + a·ω = 0 and
   v_y − a·ω = 0, which forces ω = 0; its yaw exists only under a separately qualified slip model, so A-07
   reports growth vector (1) under ideal rolling with the note `turns-only-by-slip` and does not refuse a
   skid-steer archetype.
10. **Class and budget.** D0, `Tested`; stage 4 budget 200 ms (TARGET).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Growth vectors: differential drive (2, 3); car-like with steering (2, 3, 4); car with one trailer
      (2, 3, 4, 5); ideal omnidirectional base (3).
- [ ] The mecanum template reports rank J = 3 and (3); the same base with all four roller axes parallel reports
      rank J = 2 and (2, 3), and `design.check` refuses it at stage 4 with FROB-REF-118, naming the missing
      lateral direction and a roller-angle repair that, applied, restores (3).
- [ ] Sideways cost for b = 0.20 m: 0.18 m of wheel travel for a 0.01 m shift (18 times the shift); 0.57 m by
      the small-angle form and 0.55 m exact (α = 0.72 rad) for a 0.10 m shift.
- [ ] Ackermann: turning radius L/tan δ for the template's L and δ_max.
- [ ] The four-wheel fixed-axis base reports `turns-only-by-slip` and is not refused.
- [ ] The index is absent unless its feature is on, and is labelled a screening signal when present.
- [ ] Contract conformance (derived memo key, D0), `frob analyze maneuverability --json`, stage budget met on the
      wheeled templates.

half turn in one second gives the chord (0, 2/π) m). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- The index's falsifier (it ranks four bases differently from measured wheel travel in doorway scenes) and its
  fallback (growth vector plus the turning-footprint check) are fixed before the pilot runs; its ceiling is a
  static screen for wheeled bases.
- Retired when the drive slips outside the ideal-rolling regime: the kinematic prediction is withdrawn for that
  operating region and the frictional model answers; the finding says so instead of extrapolating.
- Tracked bases turn by slipping; they are handled like the fixed-axis base until the track law of the contact
  subsystem qualifies a slip model.
