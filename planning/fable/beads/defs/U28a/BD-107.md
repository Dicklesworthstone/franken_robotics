## Background

A-12 answers: what are the natural frequencies and mode shapes of the robot standing at a stance, and is
actuator bandwidth sufficient for them? A leg-and-body system on compliant feet, belts or series springs has
low-frequency modes (bounce, pitch, roll, a twisting mode) that a controller must damp and that a gait can
excite. Knowing them before simulation tells an agent whether a stiffness, a mass distribution or an actuator
is the limit. A-12 is a separate verb and not part of `design.check`, because it needs a choice of stance and
costs more than the preflight budget allows.

Symmetry makes the answer cheaper and, more usefully, labelled. Every design has a mechanical symmetry group
Γ_mech: the automorphisms of its labelled kinematic tree realized by an isometry of the body that preserve
link geometry, inertias, joint screws, actuator cards and gear ratios. At a symmetric stance the mass matrix M
and the stiffness matrix K commute with the action of Γ_mech ∩ Stab(configuration), so in a symmetry-adapted
basis both are block diagonal, one block per irreducible representation, and each mode belongs to one block.
Which group applies matters: block-diagonal mass and stiffness matrices need only the mechanical group
intersected with the stabilizer of the configuration; sensors are irrelevant here, unlike weight tying in
policies, which needs the smaller observation-preserving group. Rejected: using the observation-preserving
group for A-12 (a forward lidar would needlessly destroy the fore-aft block structure of a purely mechanical
question); a dense eigen-solve with no labels (correct, but an agent cannot tell "roll mode" from "pitch mode").

Seed done-condition: modes of a symmetric two-mass model.

## Scope

In: linearization at a stance, stiffness assembly, the stabilizer subgroup, block-diagonalization, the
eigen-solve per block, labels, the bandwidth finding, the verb. Out: the groups themselves (BD-102, BD-048);
structural (finite-element) modes of links (structure subsystem, BD-402); symmetric controller synthesis
(BD-327); closed-loop sampled stability (A-22, N28a-09).

## Technical approach

1. **Stance.** The reference stance or one the caller names (`--stance`): a configuration q₀ with a contact
   set, in static equilibrium under gravity (checked: the generalized gravity force is balanced by joint
   holding torques and contact forces within the static-stability result; otherwise refuse the request with the
   unbalanced residual).
2. **Coordinates.** Floating base plus joints, n generalized velocities. Contacts are either compliant (their
   stiffness enters K) or bilateral for the linearization (a constraint Jacobian J_c reduces the coordinates to
   its null space; the reduction basis is recorded).
3. **M and K.** M = the joint-space mass matrix at q₀ from the model. K = K_joint + K_contact + K_geo:
   K_joint = diag of each actuated joint's positional stiffness (the declared position-loop stiffness of the
   stance controller, or the series or transmission stiffness of the card when the joint is held rigidly;
   the source is recorded per joint); K_contact = Σ J_cᵀ·k_c·J_c for compliant contacts; K_geo = the Hessian of
   gravitational potential plus the configuration derivative of J_cᵀ·f₀ at the equilibrium contact forces f₀.
   K is symmetrized; its asymmetry before symmetrization is reported.
4. **Stabilizer.** G = {g ∈ Γ_mech : ρ_q(g)·q₀ = q₀ and g maps the contact set to itself}. Each g acts on
   generalized velocities by a signed permutation ρ(g) (polar and axial components by the reflection parity
   rule: under an isometry with orthogonal part P, polar vectors map by P and axial vectors by det(P)·P).
5. **Symmetry defect.** d_M = ‖M − R(M)‖/‖M‖ and d_K likewise, with R the Reynolds projection
   R(X) = |G|⁻¹·Σ_g ρ(g)·X·ρ(g)ᵀ. If either exceeds 10⁻⁹ (DESIGN) the stance is not symmetric under G: the
   analysis drops the offending elements, recomputes G, and in the limit runs the full unblocked problem. The
   reduced calculation is replaced by the full one without an author's edit, and the output says which element
   was dropped and why (for example a one-sided payload).
6. **Blocks.** For each real irreducible χ of G, the isotypic projector P_χ = (d_χ/|G|)·Σ_g χ(g)*·ρ(g) (summed
   with its conjugate for complex type) gives an orthonormal basis Q_χ of its component; Q = [Q_χ₁ … ]. Then
   QᵀMQ and QᵀKQ are block diagonal. Assert the off-block norms are below 10⁻¹² of the matrix norm.
7. **Eigen-solve.** Per block, K_b·φ = ω²·M_b·φ by Cholesky reduction of M_b and the cyclic Jacobi method in
   double precision (in-house; no external numerical library is admitted in the core). Modes are M-normalized.
   Rigid-body modes of an unconstrained base (ω = 0) are separated and counted.
8. **Output.** Per mode: frequency in Hz, the irreducible label with a readable name where the group is C₂,
   C₂ × C₂ or dihedral (for a quadruped: all-symmetric "bounce", fore-aft antisymmetric "pitch", left-right
   antisymmetric "roll", doubly antisymmetric "twist"), the shape as joint and base participation, strain
   energy share per joint, and the residual ‖K·φ − ω²·M·φ‖.
9. **Bandwidth finding.** For each mode and each actuated joint with participation above 10% of strain energy:
   r = f_mode / f_bw, with f_bw the card's closed-loop torque bandwidth. A mode with r > 1/3 (DESIGN default,
   recorded in the contract) is `A-12.<mode>.bandwidth` (Low: the loop cannot damp it); repairs ranked by the
   contract: lower the mode (`set-param` on the stiffness source, `set-material`, mass redistribution) or raise
   bandwidth (`swap-actuator`). A card with no bandwidth field yields `Estimated` and says so.
10. **Class.** Deterministic double precision on the host with a fixed sweep order (class D2 on one platform;
    the group computations inside are D0); evidence `Tested`. Budget 1 s for n ≤ 60 (TARGET).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The symmetric two-mass model (two masses m = 1 kg, each tied to ground by k = 100 N/m and to each other by
      k_c = 50 N/m; group C₂ exchanging the masses) returns two 1 × 1 blocks: the symmetric mode at
      ω = √(k/m) = 10 rad/s with shape (1, 1)/√2 and the antisymmetric mode at
      ω = √((k + 2·k_c)/m) = 14.142 rad/s with shape (1, −1)/√2, to 10⁻⁹ relative.
- [ ] The blocked and the unblocked solves agree on every frequency to 10⁻⁹ relative on the four first
      archetypes at their reference stances; off-block norms are below 10⁻¹².
- [ ] Adding a one-sided payload to the quadruped drops the left-right mirror from the stabilizer; the analysis
      reports the dropped element and still returns all modes.
- [ ] The free-free two-inertia fixture (J₁, J₂ joined by stiffness k) returns one rigid mode and the resonance
      √(k·(J₁ + J₂)/(J₁·J₂)).
- [ ] A mode above one third of the participating actuator's bandwidth produces the bandwidth finding with
      ranked repairs.
- [ ] Contract conformance; `frob analyze modes <design> [--stance S] --json` schema-valid; not part of `check`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/a12_modes.rs`:
- `two_mass_symmetric_modes`: 10 and 14.142 rad/s; labels `A` (symmetric) and `B` (antisymmetric) of C₂.
- `two_inertia_free_free`: J₁ = 0.01, J₂ = 0.04 kg·m², k = 100 N·m/rad ⇒ ω = √(100·0.05/0.0004) = 111.80 rad/s.
- `blocks_match_dense`: random symmetric M ≻ 0 and K ⪰ 0 projected by the Reynolds operator for C₂ × C₂ and D₃.
- `off_block_zero`, `m_orthonormal`, `residual_small`.
- `payload_breaks_block`: the stabilizer shrinks; frequencies still match the dense solve.
- `complex_type_block`: C₃ acting on three identical legs: one 1-dimensional block and one doubly degenerate
  frequency; a parameterization that treats the two-dimensional block as real type is rejected by the
  degeneracy check.
- `parity_on_mirrored_axis`: under the sagittal mirror P = diag(1, −1, 1) a pitch axis along y keeps its sign
  and roll and yaw axes change theirs; a sign table that flips all three fails the commutation ρ·M·ρᵀ = M.
- `unbalanced_stance_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (bead N28a-29) runs `frob analyze modes quad-mammal --json` and the two-mass
fixture, and logs per mode the frequency (Hz), the irreducible label, the residual, the symmetry defects d_M
and d_K, the stabilizer order, and any bandwidth finding with its ratio against 1/3.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the two-mass model (the seed's condition) and the two-inertia resonance (also KA-335 of the
actuator registry). Negative controls: the complex-type block treated as real (C₃); the wrong parity table;
a mutant that uses the observation-preserving group (on a quadruped with a forward lidar it finds fewer blocks
than the mechanics allow, which the block-count assertion catches). Metamorphic: relabelling the legs by a group
element permutes shapes and leaves frequencies unchanged. Determinism: same bytes twice on one machine and
across worker counts; cross-platform within 10⁻¹² relative.

## Considerations

- A linearized operator that commutes with the group preserves the isotypic blocks; the nonlinear dynamics need
  not decouple. A-12 says nothing about large motions.
- Stiffness is the weakest input: a stance controller's gains are a choice, and cards often lack measured
  stiffness. The source of every stiffness is in the output, and `assumed` values class the result `Estimated`.
- The same bases serve symmetric controller synthesis at M4; this bead exposes the basis Q and the block list
  for that consumer.
- Series-elastic and belt compliance as a simulated mechanism is Frontier elsewhere; A-12 uses card stiffness
  statically and does not depend on it.
