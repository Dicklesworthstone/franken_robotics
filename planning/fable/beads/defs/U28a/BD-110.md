## Background

`frob design check` is the first stage of the studio loop and the cheapest: static analysis in seconds, before
any controller is synthesized or any world is stepped. It is also rung R0 of the evaluation ladder: it may
refuse a design or exclude it with a typed bound, and it never ranks survivors. Its purpose is feedback an agent
can act on at once: each refusal carries ranked repairs expressed as typed edits, which become the envelope's
`next_actions`.

The preflight has eight stages. Stage 1 is the model compile; stages 2 to 8 call the studio's analyses and live
in `frob-analyze`. This bead delivers the runner and stages 1 to 6 (stages 7, place, and 8, information, need
scenes and alias search and land at M3). The stages, what each refuses, and their time targets:

| Stage | What runs | Analyses | What it refuses (code) | 95th-percentile target (TARGET) | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Form | Parse either view; canonicalize; expand constructors and modules; unit and dimension algebra; fills; levers and locks | — (feeds A-02) | Unknown or misspelt field (103); unit or dimension error (102); port mismatch (101); missing fill or a solid written as a shell (105); lever out of bounds or a locked field (111); module digest or signature mismatch (114); missing contact law (121); sensor without frame, rate or clock (126); a card that fails its own checks (130, 134) | 100 ms | [S] |
| 2 Mass | Mass roll-up by subtree; pseudo-inertia positivity by exact LDLᵀ; mass support for declared bodies; massless chains | A-02 | Inconsistent inertia, with the offending pivot and the nearest consistent matrix (104); mass-support violation (106) | 50 ms | [S] |
| 3 Mechanism | Hervé class of each screw basis; loop assembly; mobility; singularity at the reference pose; self-collision and ground clearance over the declared ranges | A-01, A-06 (reference pose), A-13 | Class and basis disagree (101); empty range (107); over-constrained closure (110); self-collision or interpenetration (123) | 400 ms | [S]; [F] for lattice mobility |

The figures are budgets per stage for any archetype on the reference host with an empty memo graph. They are
not predictions and are not added into a headline. The one total committed to is that stages 1 to 6 complete
within 2 s at the 95th percentile on the archetypes; a stage that misses its own budget is a finding even when
the total passes. Rejected designs: one monolithic validator (no stage attribution, no per-stage timing, no
partial runs); warnings instead of refusals (an invalid design is refused with a stable code, never repaired in
silence).

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-02 for the included analyses; GATE-SKEL-04 for stages 1 to 6.

## Scope

In: the stage runner, the stage-to-analysis table, aggregation of findings, global repair ranking, per-stage
timing and budgets with `undecided`, the stage records the loop-order rule reads, the verb with its schema and
exit codes, the two gate measurements. Out: the analyses (BD-100 to BD-106 and their owners); the compile
(stage 1's content); A-20 to A-23 (registered into stage 6 by BD-108); stages 7 and 8 (BD-244); the memo graph
(BD-111); design lints, which are advisory and separate (BD-064).

## Technical approach

1. **Command.** `frob design check <design> [--task T] [--scene S] [--stages a-b] [--all] [--strict-data]
   [--time-budget ms] --json`. Stages run in order and stop at the first refusing finding, unless `--all` asks
   for every finding. `--stages 2-4` runs a sub-range (stage 1 always runs: nothing can be analysed without a
   model). Registry row: cluster `design`, kind `read`, tier S, capability `design:read`, schemas
   `frob.design.check.in/1` and `frob.design.check.out/1`, idempotent, cost model "static: under 2 s for
   template-sized designs", first milestone M1, phase `check`.
2. **Stage table as data** (`registries/preflight.toml`): per stage its analyses, the refusal codes it owns, its
   budget, and per finding kind the `check` policy (refusing with code, or reported). An analysis joins a stage
   through the registration interface of the analysis contract; an analysis marked "when bound" joins only when
   the request names the scene, budget or skill it needs.
3. **Runner.** For stage s: run its analyses (independent ones in parallel on the job context's pool; results
   placed by index so order is deterministic), collect `AnalysisResult`s, apply policy. A refusing finding ends
   the run after the stage completes (all findings of the refusing stage are reported, not only the first).
   Each stage returns its evidence class and the model identity.
4. **Budgets.** Each analysis receives the remainder of its stage budget; on exhaustion it returns `undecided`
   with what it had established. `undecided` is never a pass and never a refusal: the stage is reported
   `undecided`, later stages still run, the exit code is 2, and the stored stage record says `undecided`.
5. **Aggregation.** The result carries: `summary` (total mass, closure status, symmetry groups, mobility,
   growth vector where applicable); `stages[]` with status (`passed`, `refused`, `undecided`, `not-run`),
   evidence class, wall time and budget; `findings[]` merged across stages, each with its stage; and
   `not_run[]` for analyses awaiting a binding, with the flag that binds them.
6. **Global repair ranking.** Repairs from all findings are merged: first those attached to refusing findings,
   in stage order; then by the contract's rank key; a repair that appears under several findings is listed
   once with all the findings it addresses, and ranked by the number of refusing findings it clears. The top
   entries become `next_actions` as runnable `frob design edit …` commands, each with a one-line reason.
7. **Refusal envelope.** On a refusal: `ok: false`, exit 3, `refusal {code, message, pointer (field path),
   observed, required, unit, affected_claims, retry: after-change, repairs}`; the envelope still carries the
   stage list and timings. With findings and no refusal: exit 2. Clean: exit 0.
8. **Stage record.** Written to the ledger per (`ModelId` restricted to the fields the stage read, analysis
   versions, bindings): verdict, codes, finding ids, timings. The loop-order rule and `explain.design` read it.
9. **Evidence.** The envelope's class is the weakest among the stages run (an exact analysis over an `assumed`
   card is `Estimated`); tier `static`; determinism `exact` when every analysis run was D0; `claims: []`.
10. **Timing measurement (GATE-ATEL-02).** Per archetype, 200 cold runs (empty memo graph, fresh process state
    for caches), recording median and 95th percentile of every stage and of the total, with the commit,
    hardware fingerprint and seed. A stage over its budget at the 95th percentile produces a `budget-miss`
    entry in the gate row even if the total is under 2 s.
11. **Defect measurement (GATE-SKEL-04, stages 1 to 6).** Run the corpus of N28a-12: every defect must be
    refused; at least 95 of 100 by the stage and code that own them; zero refusals on the archetypes present
    and on the 10³ generated valid designs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The six stages run in order, stop at the first refusing stage by default and continue under `--all`;
      `--stages` selects a range; a stage that exhausts its budget is `undecided` and the exit code is 2.
- [ ] Every refusal carries a code from the stage's owned list, a field pointer and at least one typed-edit
      repair, or an explicit `no_repair_reason`.
- [ ] `next_actions` are runnable: for each of the eight first-rejection fixtures, applying the first action
      and re-running the check clears that refusal.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-02, M1 part: each included analysis (A-01, A-02, A-03, A-06 at the reference pose, A-07, A-08,
      A-10, A-11, A-13) passes its known-answer tests through `check`; stages 1 to 6 finish in under 2 s at the
      95th percentile on `quad-mammal`, `humanoid`, `diff-drive` and `mecanum`; per-stage medians and 95th
      percentiles are in the gate row; budget misses are listed.
- [ ] GATE-SKEL-04, stages 1 to 6: 100 of 100 seeded defects refused; at least 95 by the owning stage and code;
      zero refusals on the four archetypes and on 10³ random valid designs.
- [ ] The point-foot biped is reported and not refused; the parallel-roller mecanum is refused at stage 4.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` with the documented schema and exit codes; a second call with unchanged
      inputs returns identical result bytes; cancellation mid-run returns no partial stage record.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-analyze` documents the stage table and the policy; both gates have recorded rows
      with measured values.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/preflight.rs`:
- `stops_at_first_refusing_stage`; `all_flag_reports_every_stage`; `stage_range_always_runs_stage_1`.
- `refusing_stage_reports_all_its_findings`.
- `undecided_is_exit_2_and_recorded`: an analysis with a 0 ms budget.
- `owned_codes_only`: a stage that emits a code it does not own fails the test.
- `repair_merge_and_rank`: one repair clearing two refusing findings outranks one clearing one.
- `next_actions_runnable`: each command parses and applies.
- `stage_record_key_ignores_untouched_fields`: renaming a body leaves every stage record valid; a mass edit
  invalidates stages 2 and 6 and not stage 4.
- `evidence_min_over_stages`: an `assumed` card field makes the envelope `Estimated`.
- `deterministic_bytes_parallel`: 1, 4 and 16 workers give identical result bytes.
- `cancel_leaves_no_partial_record`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (bead N28a-29) drives the real `frob` binary: checks the four templates (exit
0 or 2), the eight first-rejection fixtures (exit 3, expected code and stage), applies the first
`next_action` and re-checks, then runs the two gate measurements through the conformance runner. Logged per
call: command line, exit code, wall time, per-stage status and time against budget, the refusal code and
pointer, the first three repairs, the evidence block; a summary with assertions passed and failed and the gates
touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers by analysis: Sarrus, Bennett and four-bar mobility 1 (KA-037, KA-038); the growth vectors and the
parallel-roller mecanum (KA-058, KA-268, KA-681); the stance values (KA-682); the symmetry of the quadruped
with a forward lidar (KA-680); the mass fixtures of the inertia construction; the co-design and envelope
fixtures of the actuator beads; KA-291 for the corpus. Negative controls: the 100 seeded defects; a mutant
runner that continues past a refusing stage without `--all`; a mutant that reports `undecided` as passed (the
loop-order tests then let a simulation through, which they detect). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine;
across worker counts; debug and release; across platforms (D0 analyses byte-identical).

## Considerations

- KILL-STATIC (decided at M3 on the design corpus): if static verdicts predict simulated failure no better than
  a mass-and-torque heuristic, `design check` verdicts are downgraded to advisory lints and the pruning claim is
  withdrawn. The policy table is where that switch lives; nothing else in the runner changes.
- The measured ceiling of static checks as a pruning lever is modest (pruning trivial failures cuts trainings by
  at most about 1.19 times; FACT, source register); their value is feedback in seconds and typed repairs.
- Targets for a warm check after a one-parameter edit (0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 s at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile)
  belong to the memo-graph gate, not to this bead.
- An analysis added later must fit its stage's budget or become a separate verb; the gate re-measures the total
  whenever a stage's analysis list changes.
