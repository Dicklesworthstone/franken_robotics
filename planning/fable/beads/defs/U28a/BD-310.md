## Background

The studio never scores a design by the most expensive method first. It uses a ladder of six rungs (static
check; a shared policy with no adaptation on a fast tier; a fine-tuned policy; a specialist policy; the referee
tier; a rare-failure bound), each about an order of magnitude dearer than the one below, and lets a cheap rung
discard designs so that only a few are trained. The whole saving depends on one unmeasured quantity: whether a
cheap rung orders designs as the trained, referee-promoted rung does. No published measurement of rank
agreement between a shared policy and trained specialists was found. The nearest neighbours (FACT, source
register, every figure from other engines): a shared policy's value predictions correlate with rollouts of the
same policy at a rank correlation of 0.40 overall and 0.50, 0.60 and 0.83 on three robots over 17 designs; in
architecture search with shared weights, sparse Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three benchmarks; a
universal controller reaches up to 80% of individually trained controllers; in the 17-design study the top
design was the same under both scores. So agreement of 0.7 or better near the top is not supported, top-1
agreement at modest correlation is plausible, and the default until a stratum is measured is that the shared
policy rung neither selects nor discards.

This is "the decisive early experiment of the program". It is run first as a pilot at the third milestone, on
an incumbent engine or on the CPU reference tier `batch-cpu`, before the family-policy and adaptive-ladder work
of the fourth milestone is funded. The pilot has two parts: (A) the rank-agreement measurement, and (B) one
logged population on which allocation rules are replayed with no further simulation, which is the pilot and
falsifier of the ladder's two adaptive layers (conformal survivor sets, and value of information at the
finalist stage).

Seed done-condition: the pilot record of GATE-CORTEX-12 exists with τ, top-10 recall and intervals per class;
pilot rows of GATE-ATEL-07 and GATE-ATEL-08.

## Scope

In: part A in full (the design set, the three scores per design, the statistics, the modes, the pilot records
of GATE-CORTEX-12 and GATE-ATEL-07); of part B, the simulations and the log (step 5: 64 designs × 3 rungs × 32
addresses, stored as a content-addressed dataset) and the protocol of the two replays (steps 6 and 7), stated
here so that the pilot is specified in one place; the pilot records.

Shared with N28b-18, which depends on this bead: the replays themselves add no simulation and are run by the
replay engine of N28b-18 (the log's frozen format, six choosers behind one interface, the common
identification rule, the metrics, the zero-value fixture, the controls). N28b-18 computes the pilot row of
GATE-ATEL-08 (step 6) and the chooser comparison that KILL-VOI reads first (step 7) from this bead's log and
appends them as results of its own record in the pilot register (convention 3); the acceptance lines below on
those rows are closed by that run.

Out: the pre-registration (BD-145); tier-pair rank agreement (BD-265); the experiment on this system's own
engine and policies and the full gates (BD-328, BD-329, with the gate run N28b-19); the kill decisions
(N28b-20 for KILL-LADDER, N28c-17 for KILL-VOI); the value-of-information core itself (BD-349, BD-351).

## Technical approach

**Part A: rank agreement between rungs.**
1. Design set: 40 designs in one morphology class (quadruped first), drawn from the corpus by the keyed stream
   and strata of the pre-registration; the list hash and the thresholds 0.7 and 0.5 are already in the ledger.
2. Three scores per design, on common world addresses: R1, a shared policy with no adaptation on the fast tier
   (on an incumbent engine: its multi-embodiment policy or, failing that, one policy trained on the class
   template and applied unchanged; on `batch-cpu`: the synthesized baseline controller, which is a scorer like
   any other); R2, a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps; R3 promoted, a specialist trained per design and
   evaluated on the highest-fidelity tier available. Per-episode outcomes on 256 paired addresses per design
   are kept.
3. Statistics per rung pair (R1, R3) and (R2, R3), per stratum:
   - Kendall τ = (concordant − discordant pairs)/C(n, 2) on the top band, the designs in the top m of either
     rung with m = max(10, 3k), k the number of finalists wanted (k = 3);
   - top-10 recall of the lower rung against the higher; Spearman ρ over the stratum;
   - the mean signed residual per region of design space (the bias of the cheap rung);
   - the per-episode cross-tier correlation ρ_ep on the 256 paired addresses;
   - the conformal margin q̂ at α = 0.05 on the designs that ran both rungs (the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest
     residual |y_hi − ĝ(y_lo)|, ĝ a monotone map fitted on a disjoint part);
   - the containment rank k_c(m) and the regret of a cut;
   - 95% percentile bootstrap intervals from 2,000 seeded resamples of designs. Every threshold is applied to
     the lower bound.
4. Mode assigned per stratum by the fixed rule of the rank-agreement ledger, every condition on the lower
   bootstrap bound: `selective` at τ ≥ 0.7 with the winner check passed and the calibration valid (q̂ from at
   least 19 designs that ran both rungs, exchangeability martingale below 20); `discard-only` at
   0.5 ≤ τ < 0.7; `uninformative` below 0.5, unmeasured, or with the planted winner lost. The third condition of
   `selective` in the ledger (audited miss rate at most α) needs a campaign's audit lane, which part A does not
   have; the record states it `not-measured`, and a pilot `selective` is a default for M4, never a mode a
   campaign may act on before its own audit lane confirms it. The tier correction is marked unusable where
   ρ_ep < 0.7 on at least 200 paired addresses.

**Part B: the ladder log and the chooser replay.**
5. Log once: 64 designs × 3 rungs × 32 addresses (about one GPU-day, TARGET), every (design, rung, address)
   score with its measured cost in GPU-seconds, as the dataset `frob.ladderlog/1`. True ranks are defined by
   the top rung's mean over all 32 addresses.
6. Layer-2 replay (pilot rows of GATE-ATEL-08). On the log, run the fixed table (successive halving with η = 3:
   the top ⌈n/3⌉ advance at each transition) and the conformal survivor rule with a tenth of rung spend as
   audit lane, over 100 keyed replays (permuted address order and audit draws). Report: held-out coverage of
   the margin; audited top-k recall of each rule; GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision with a paired 95%
   lower bound on the ratio; whether a planted over-scored design (one design's lower-rung scores inflated by a
   declared amount) is caught at the next rung; whether synthetic drift (a shift added to later designs) trips
   the exchangeability alarm at martingale value 20.
7. Chooser replay (the measurement behind KILL-VOI and GATE-ATEL-15). Four choosers pick (design, rung,
   address) evaluations sequentially from the log: an agent reading the log so far; the knowledge-gradient
   chooser; e-race elimination; uniform allocation. Metric: evaluations (and GPU-seconds) until a true top-3
   design is identified at 95% confidence, over 100 replays. The agent arm's transcripts are stored.
8. **Records.** The pilot is `PILOT-RANK-01` in the one pilot register,
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; convention 3),
   registered before the first score with its frozen protocol `docs/pilots/PILOT-RANK-01.md`, `bead =
   "BD-310"`, the falsifier (top-band τ below 0.5 at the lower bound on every class tested), the ceiling, the
   fallback (cheap rungs discard only; the fixed halving table) with `kill_gate = "KILL-RANK"`, and
   `integrated_gate = "GATE-CORTEX-12"`. Part A's results are appended as `[[pilot.result]]` tables, one per
   class (never edited): engine and pinned revision (with the incumbent disclosure block), design-list hash,
   every statistic with its interval, the mode per stratum, costs, the ladder-log hash, and the values the rows
   of GATE-CORTEX-12 and GATE-ATEL-07 will measure, labelled `pilot: incumbent` or `pilot: batch-cpu` (a pilot
   result never sets a part of a gate, N38a-09; the gates' rows are BD-328's and BD-329's at M4). Part B's
   replay results are N28b-18's and go to its own record in the same file.

**What the pilot decides.** Funding and defaults, not the gates' final rows: if top-band τ is below 0.5 at the
lower bound, the shared-policy rung is `uninformative` by default at M4 and the family-policy spend is decided
again; if layer 2 does not reach halving's recall at lower cost on the log, the adaptive layer starts M4 behind
its fallback; if the knowledge-gradient chooser is under 1.5 times better than the agent (the paired lower
bound on the ratio below 1.5), value of information starts as a stop rule only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot record of GATE-CORTEX-12 exists: τ on the top band, top-10 recall and bootstrap intervals per
      class and task family, ρ_ep on 256 paired episodes per design, and the mode the rule assigns.
- [ ] Pilot rows of GATE-ATEL-07: for each class tested, τ against 0.7 and 0.5 and ρ_ep against 0.7, all at
      the lower bootstrap bound, on 40 designs.
- [ ] Pilot rows of GATE-ATEL-08 (computed by the replay engine of N28b-18 on this bead's log): coverage
      against 1 − α − 0.03, audited top-k recall of layer 2 against halving's, the paired lower bound on the
      GPU-seconds ratio, the planted over-scored design, the drift alarm.
- [ ] The chooser replay (run by N28b-18 on this bead's log) reports, for the four choosers, the restricted
      mean, the median and the interval of evaluations to a true top-3, and the ratio of the agent chooser to
      the knowledge-gradient chooser with its paired one-sided 95% lower bound, which is the quantity the
      KILL-VOI decision reads against 1.5.
- [ ] The ladder log is stored as a content-addressed dataset that later beads can replay.
- [ ] `PILOT-RANK-01` is registered in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before the first score,
      and part A's results are appended there as `[[pilot.result]]` tables; no other pilot file is written.
- [ ] The design list and thresholds precede the first score in the ledger; the compute approval is on record,
      or the records say `not run` with the default in force.
- [ ] Certifier check: on planted populations with known top-band rank correlation on each side of 0.7 and of
      0.5 the measured τ covers the planted value in its interval and the mode assigned is the prescribed one;
      a lost planted winner stops the rung from discarding in that stratum.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/rank_pilot.rs`:
- `kendall_top_band`: forty designs where the cheap rung reproduces the true order except that the true best
  is ranked third: among the true top ten 2 of 45 pairs are inverted, τ = 41/45 ≈ 0.911; top-1 recall at k = 2
  is 0; containment rank 3 for m = 1; positive regret at k = 2.
- `top_band_m`: k = 3 gives m = 10; k = 5 gives m = 15.
- `conformal_index_38_of_39`; `fewer_than_19_falls_back`.
- `mode_rule`: lower bounds 0.72 → selective (winner check passed), 0.72 with the winner check failed →
  discard-only, 0.72 with q̂ from 18 designs (calibration invalid) → discard-only, 0.72 with the martingale at
  20 → discard-only, 0.55 → discard-only, 0.45 → uninformative, no record → uninformative; a `selective` pilot
  record carries `audited_miss_rate: not-measured`.
- `halving_schedule_81`: 81 → 27 → 9 → 3 at η = 3.
- `ladder_cost_worked_case`: 81 designs with rungs of 10 s, 3 min and 30 min: all trained 40.5 h; halving
  13.5 + 81 + 270 min ≈ 6.1 h; with q̂ = 0.04 and a third-best lower-rung score of 0.74 the cut is 0.66, six
  survive, and with three finalists the cost is 13.5 + 18 + 90 min ≈ 2.0 h, about 2.2 h with the audit tenth.
- `replay_is_pure`: the same log and key give the same chooser trace.
- `planted_overscore_caught`, `drift_trips_alarm_at_20`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rank_ladder_pilot.sh` sources the STD-E2E helper. There is no `frob conformance` command
(ADR-GAUNT-01, BD-025: the lint `no_conformance_command` fails a script that contains it), and at M3 no verb
computes a rung-pair row from a log (`eval.rank-agreement` of BD-265 measures tier pairs by running; rung pairs
reach a verb with BD-328 at M4), so the pilot statistics run as the gate's Cargo entry. Steps:
1. On a small `synthetic` log (12 designs × 3 rungs × 8 addresses, generated from a planted model with a known
   top-band τ, committed as a fixture with a `synthetic` kind header), run
   `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test <tier named by GATE-CORTEX-12's record in
   registries/gates.toml> -- GATE-CORTEX-12`, whose pilot entry reads the fixture log; assert that the planted τ
   is covered by the interval and that the modes are the prescribed ones (including `not-measured` for the
   audited miss rate).
2. Run the same entry with `--features gauntlet-mutations` and the planted controls of the section below
   (calibration from the advanced designs; the lost planted winner); each must fail its row.
3. Validate the ladder log: the stored dataset parses as `frob.ladderlog/1`, its content hash is the same when
   it is written twice from the same simulations, and every (design, rung, address) cell has a score and a cost.
   The replays on it (GATE-ATEL-08 rows and the chooser comparison) are N28b-18's run and its script
   `scripts/e2e/chooser_replay_pilot.sh`, not this one.
4. When the real datasets exist, run `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilots` (the register's loader
   rules: `PILOT-RANK-01` loads, its registration and protocol digest precede its first `[[pilot.result]]`),
   print that record's results, and assert that the design list and thresholds precede the first score in
   the ledger.
It writes `target/e2e-logs/rank_ladder_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`BD-310`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines
carry the design-list hash, n, τ with its interval against 0.7 and 0.5, top-10 recall, ρ_ep against 0.7, q̂,
the mode per stratum, the ladder-log hash; on failure the first statistic that differs from the planted value
and the fixture path; a final `summary` line with assertion counts and the gates touched as `pilot` rows
(GATE-CORTEX-12, GATE-ATEL-07).

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-696 (the 38th residual at n = 39), the winner-check fixture (τ = 41/45), the worked ladder costs. Negative
controls: calibration computed from the designs that advanced (it under-covers on a fixture where the residual
correlates with the cheap score, and the coverage check catches it); the planted over-scored design; synthetic
drift; CX-318 (rank agreement near the top assumed without measurement: an unmeasured stratum must come out
`uninformative`). Determinism: the statistics and replays are seeded and identical across runs; the logged
simulations carry whatever class their engine provides, stated in the record.

## Considerations

- A pilot on an incumbent engine measures the question, not this system: its numbers fund or defund work and
  set defaults; the gates' own rows come from this system's engine at M4.
- RISK-27 (rank agreement too low for cheap rungs to eliminate designs) and RISK-10 (no compute envelope
  approved) both land here. With the default envelope the pilot runs the `batch-cpu` variant on a smaller set
  and reports wider intervals; it never reports a mode it did not measure.
- Cheap rungs are biased as well as noisy: early evaluation underestimates slow learners, and a population
  scored under one shared controller drifts toward designs that controller steers easily. The per-region signed
  residual is therefore reported and is the input to per-bucket calibration later.
- OPEN-ATEL-2 (what a stratum is): default one stratum per morphology class. OPEN-ATEL-6 (whether 5% is the
  right fine-tune rung): decided by this experiment; default one rung at 5%.
- If GATE-ATEL-07 later fails for every class tested, KILL-RANK fires (decided with the M4 experiment): the
  shared-policy rung is `uninformative` everywhere and the phase ledger records which factor was lost.
