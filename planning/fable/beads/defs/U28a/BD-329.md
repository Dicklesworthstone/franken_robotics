## Background

The evaluation ladder lets cheap rungs discard designs so that few are trained. Its baseline allocation rule,
successive halving, advances a fixed third at every rung whether or not the rung can tell the designs apart.
This bead adds layer 2 of the allocation rule, which owns the population stage: advance exactly the designs
the lower rung cannot rule out, with a margin that is measured on a lane of designs set aside before any score
exists. It is Frontier; its pilot is the logged replay of BD-310; it ships in shadow behind the fixed table,
which is its fallback on every trigger; and its kill gate is KILL-LADDER.

The idea: a rung cannot separate designs that are closer than twice its own error. Measure that error (the
residual between a design's lower-rung score and its higher-rung score) on an audit set drawn by keyed
randomness before scores exist; take the split-conformal quantile q̂; and send up every design within 2·q̂ of
the k-th best lower-rung score. For a design exchangeable with the audit set the residual is at most q̂ with
probability at least 1 − α, so a pair separated by more than the margin is misordered with probability at most
2α. Calibrating on the designs that advanced would be biased toward agreement and is forbidden.

Two decision cores govern it (a decision core is a recurring decision compiled ahead of time into a small
versioned table with a loss model, obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode):
DC-02, which designs advance at a rung transition; and DC-03, how wide the survivor margin is for a rung pair
and stratum, and which mode the rung is in.

Rejected for these decisions, with reasons kept so the question is not reopened without new evidence:
Gaussian-process surrogates over designs (no validated kernel on typed designs of varying topology); a learned
correction of the fast tier or a learned step surrogate as a screen (it hides exactly the artifacts the trust
ladder exists to expose); bandit allocation by upper confidence bounds or posterior sampling (regret is the
wrong objective for identifying the best design); adaptive-fidelity stopping as a decision procedure (kept
only as a scheduling heuristic that orders work without touching validity).

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-08; KILL-LADDER decided (the decision is recorded by N28a-25 from this bead's
measurement).

## Technical approach

**Layer 2, for each rung pair (lo, hi) and stratum, per generation.**
1. *The audit lane.* A tenth of the rung's spend (DESIGN default) goes to designs drawn by a keyed random
   stream (key: campaign, stratum, rung pair, generation) before any score for them exists. Each is evaluated
   at the higher rung at a fixed n and is never cancelled by an e-process. The audit lane is the only source of
   calibration. It is blinded: an audit design and a survivor are the same job type with the same budget, and
   the evaluating job cannot tell which it is running. INV-ATEL-03: no audit-lane episode is cancelled,
   re-weighted or selected after any score for its design exists.
2. *Calibration.* With n audit designs that have both scores, split them by key into a fitting part and a
   calibration part. Fit a monotone map ĝ (isotonic regression) from lower-rung to higher-rung score on the
   fitting part. On the calibration part compute residuals rᵢ = |y_hi,i − ĝ(y_lo,i)|. The margin q̂ is the
   ⌈(n_c + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest residual, n_c the calibration count. At α = 0.05 this needs n_c ≥ 19; with
   fewer the stratum falls back to layer 1.
3. *The survivor set.* With k finalists wanted, every design whose lower-rung score is within 2·q̂ of the k-th
   best lower-rung score advances.
4. *The trust check.* If at most 3k designs survive, the rung separates the top band and is trusted there. If
   more survive: buy more lower-rung episodes (which shrinks q̂), or skip the rung for the top band, or, when
   the budget forbids both, truncate to 3k by lower-rung score and label the campaign `budget-truncated`.
5. *The alarm.* A test martingale on the audit designs' conformal p-values watches exchangeability: for the
   i-th audit design, pᵢ = (number of earlier residuals ≥ rᵢ, plus a keyed uniform tie-break)/i, and the
   martingale is the mixture power martingale Mₙ = ∫₀¹ ∏ᵢ ε·pᵢ^(ε−1) dε (DESIGN choice of betting function,
   recorded in the core's contract). At a value of 20, which is 1/α at α = 0.05, the stratum's calibration is
   void, the stratum reverts to layer 1, and calibration restarts inside the new regime.
6. *Bias by region.* Where the cheap rung systematically under-scores a region (bodies that are hard to steer
   with a shared controller), calibration is per bucket; a bucket below the calibration floor refuses instead
   of extrapolating.

**Obligations.** Coverage of at least 1 − α for designs exchangeable with the audit set, on average over
calibration draws (with n_c = 39 the coverage averages 0.95 and falls below 0.885 in one calibration draw in
twenty: the coverage follows the Beta(38, 2) law of split-conformal prediction); two designs separated by more
than the margin at the lower rung are misordered at the higher rung with probability at most 2α; top-k recall
is measured by the audit lane and never claimed from the theory.

**DC-02: which designs advance.**
- *Artifacts.* The bracket schedule of the fixed table; per rung pair and stratum the survivor rule with its
  margin; the one-way handover marker to the finalist core DC-05; the label `budget-truncated`.
- *Loss model.* Eliminating a true top-k design, against the rung cost of promoting a poor one. The first is
  far dearer.
- *Obligations.* Those above; top-k recall is measured by the audit lane; no design is served by two layers
  (INV-ATEL-04: no design receives allocation requests from both the population layer and the finalist layer).
- *Fallback and triggers.* Halving at η = 3. Triggers: under 19 calibration points; martingale at or above 20;
  audited miss rate above α; survivors above 3k for two generations; a change of identity or scope.
- *Budgeted mode.* Audit lane at a tenth of rung spend; survivors capped at 3k, then truncation by lower-rung
  score with the label.

**DC-03: the survivor margin and the mode of a rung.**
- *Artifacts.* The table of margins q̂ by (rung pair, stratum, α); the keyed list of audit draws; the
  exchangeability martingale; the mode automaton with hysteresis: a mode changes only at a generation boundary,
  and a stratum needs two consecutive generations of valid calibration to become `selective`.
- *Loss model.* As DC-02, plus the cost of the audit lane itself.
- *Obligations.* Coverage at least 1 − α for designs exchangeable with the audit set; exchangeability of the
  audit draws by construction, because they are drawn by key before scores exist; a pair separated by the
  margin is misordered with probability at most 2α; the automaton never changes inside a generation.
- *Fallback and triggers.* `discard-only` with the fixed table. Triggers as DC-02.
- *Budgeted mode.* The audit lane's tenth of rung spend. When that buys fewer than 19 audit designs in a
  stratum, strata are merged upward to the morphology class before the stratum falls back.
- *Failure modes.* Calibration from survivors only; drift of the optimizer toward a region the audit set does
  not cover; starved strata. Each is detected by the martingale or by the calibration floor.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* n_c = 39, α = 0.05: q̂ is the 38th smallest residual, say 0.04; the third-best
  lower-rung score is 0.74; the cut is 0.74 − 2 × 0.04 = 0.66.

**Layer-3 handover.** When the survivors of the last population rung number at most 64 (DESIGN, the budgeted
mode of DC-05), allocation passes, one way, to the finalist core: this bead writes the handover marker for
those designs and refuses any later population-layer request for them. Layer 3 orders work; every verdict still
comes from the promotion rule and the comparison rules.

**Refusal.** **FROB-REF-546**: calibration from survivors, or an audit episode cancelled or re-weighted
(repair: redraw the audit lane by key). Raised when a calibration set contains a design not in the keyed audit
list, when a cancel request targets an audit episode, or when an audit design's weight or n is changed after
its first score.

**Adoption.** Shadow first: the fixed table acts, and every `AllocationDecision` records what layer 2 would
have advanced. Canary on one stratum, then default, only after GATE-ATEL-08 and the core-level gate.

**The ceiling, stated before any speed test.** The ladder cannot cost less than one R1 score for every design,
the audit lane, and R3 to R5 for the finalists. Illustrative: 81 designs with rungs of 10 s, 3 min and 30 min
cost 40.5 h if every design is trained and about 6.1 h under halving; with q̂ = 0.04 six designs survive and
with three finalists the ladder costs about 2.0 h (13.5 + 18 + 90 min), about 2.2 h with the audit tenth. With
q̂ = 0.10 about twenty designs survive, more than 3k = 9, and layer 2 gives back most of its advantage.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ATEL-08, layer 2 against the fixed table on the same population and addresses: held-out coverage at
      least 1 − α − 0.03 per stratum; audited top-k recall no lower than halving's; fewer GPU-seconds to the
      same accepted decision at a 95% paired lower bound above 1; a planted over-scored design is caught at the
      next rung; synthetic drift trips the alarm.
- [ ] PO-ATEL-03: over 10⁴ simulated calibration draws at n_c = 39 and α = 0.05 the mean coverage is 0.95
      within Monte Carlo error and the 5th percentile is 0.885 within 0.005.
- [ ] The audit list is in the ledger before any score of its designs (ordering asserted); no audit episode is
      ever cancelled, in any test or campaign replay; a cancel request for one is refused (546).
- [ ] Calibration from survivors is refused (546), and the under-coverage it would cause is demonstrated on the
      fixture where the residual correlates with the cheap score.
- [ ] Each DC-02 and DC-03 trigger, forced in turn, puts the stratum on the fixed table within the same
      generation boundary and records the trigger.
- [ ] The mode automaton changes only at generation boundaries and needs two consecutive valid generations for
      `selective` (property test over random calibration histories).
- [ ] No design receives requests from both the population layer and the finalist layer in a replayed campaign.
- [ ] In shadow, every decision records both the action taken (fixed table) and layer 2's action.
- [ ] The measurements KILL-LADDER reads (recall of both rules, the cost ratio with its bound, after at most
      one repair cycle) are recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/ladder_conformal.rs`:
- `q_hat_index`: n_c = 39 → the 38th smallest; n_c = 19 → the 19th; n_c = 18 → fallback.
- `survivor_rule_worked`: scores with third best 0.74 and q̂ = 0.04 → cut 0.66; six survive on the fixture.
- `trust_check_3k`: 20 survivors with k = 3 → not trusted; the three responses in order of budget.
- `beta_coverage_law`: 10⁴ draws; mean 0.95; 5th percentile 0.885.
- `misorder_bound_2alpha`: on exchangeable synthetic pairs separated by more than 2·q̂ the misorder rate is at
  most 2α within Monte Carlo error.
- `martingale_alarm_on_drift`: residuals whose scale doubles after design 40 push the martingale past 20;
  exchangeable residuals stay below 20 in at least 95% of 10³ runs.
- `survivor_calibration_undercovers` (the forbidden shortcut, demonstrated) and `refused_546`.
- `audit_episode_cancel_refused_546`; `audit_draw_before_scores`.
- `automaton_hysteresis`; `triggers_force_fallback` (five triggers).
- `handover_one_way`.
- `monotone_map_isotonic`: ĝ is non-decreasing and fitted on the disjoint part only.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_cascade.sh` (bead N28a-32) runs the seeded population twice on the same addresses, once
with `--allocation fixed` and once with `--allocation conformal`, and the gate measurement through
`frob conformance run --gate GATE-ATEL-08 --json`. Logged: per stratum n_c, q̂, the cut, survivors against 3k,
the martingale value against 20, the mode before and after, coverage against 1 − α − 0.03, recall of both
rules, GPU-seconds of both with the paired bound, the planted design's fate, triggers fired, and the shadow
record pairs.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-696 (n = 39, α = 0.05: the 38th ordered residual). Negative controls: calibration from survivors only; the
planted over-scored design; synthetic drift; an audit episode cancelled by a racing e-process (the interference
test "no audit episode was stopped by an e-process"); a mutant automaton that switches mode mid-generation.
Determinism: audit draws, ĝ, q̂ and the martingale are pure functions of keyed draws and scores; identical
across worker counts and completion orders.

## Considerations

- KILL-LADDER: GATE-ATEL-08 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance: the fixed halving table
  is the allocation rule, and the audit lane is kept as measurement.
- Conformal coverage holds on average over calibration draws and moves by several points at twenty calibration
  designs; the report prints n_c beside every margin.
- Exchangeability against drift is reconciled by construction: the alarm ends the regime and calibration
  restarts inside the new one. The optimizer drifting toward a region the audit set does not cover is the
  expected way this fails.
- The size of q̂ is exactly what the rank-agreement ledger measures, which is why that experiment comes first.
- OPEN-ATEL-1 does not block this bead (its loss constants are those of DC-01 and DC-05).
