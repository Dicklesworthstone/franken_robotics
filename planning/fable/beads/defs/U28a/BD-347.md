## Background

An edit is an event that puts evidence in question. The studio does not ask "is the cache still warm"; it asks
which verdicts survive, which are revoked, and what the cheapest set of reruns is.
`frob result revalidate <verdict or claim> --after-edit <edit>` answers with the surviving obligations and the
licence for each, the invalidated regions, and the required reruns with their cost. The mission subsystem's
revalidation verb is this same engine applied to a mission answer.

The governing decision (ADR-ATEL-05): a hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck. A
content hash says that an input or a result is the same object. It says nothing about whether a verdict
survives a different input. Reuse of a verdict needs a dependency argument or an implication argument,
recorded and independently checkable.

Every stored verdict has this shape (`frob.verdict/1`):

```
verdict record
  claim        claim_id · claim type · statement · quantifier prefix
  kind         inclusion (a witness exists) | exclusion (no witness can exist) | comparison | optimum | estimate
  assumptions  disturbance set · world set · hypothesis support · actuator limits · requirement ·
               policy class · information contract (channels and their instants) · catalog and prices ·
               model identity · tier · kernel versions · effort profile
  obligations  feasibility obligations   (the witness is valid)
               optimality obligations    (every cheaper rival is excluded; the cost bound; catalog membership)
               each with: evidence_id · dependency support (the exact fields it read) · checker
  provenance   evidence closure · decision-policy id · trace ids
```

Four grades of reuse:

| Grade | Licence to skip the recheck | What is recorded | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Identity | The obligation's full key is unchanged | The memo hit | Solid |
| 2 Dependency | Every changed field lies outside the obligation's recorded dependency support | A mask over the obligations, checkable against the supports without rerunning anything | Solid |
| 3 Implication | A changed field lies inside the support, and a polarity rule of the assumption lattice preserves the obligation | The rule's name, the old and new assumption sets, the order relation between them and its check | Frontier |
| 4 None | — | The rerun and its cost estimate | — |

Grades 1 and 2 exist from the third milestone (bead BD-292). This bead adds grade 3 behind a flag and grade 4's
rerun planner, and measures the whole on the frozen 30-amendment campaign. Grade 2 remains the default: on
small cases a correctly keyed dependency graph ties a semantic receipt, and what decides safety is the
completeness of the key (delivery time is a dependency of a decision).

Rejected and recorded: reuse of a verdict by sensitivity margin (withdrawn for want of a certificate; a matched
dependency graph duplicated every saving).

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-11; KILL-REUSE decided. The gate is measured at M4 by N28b-35 on a freshly
sealed campaign and the decision is recorded by N28b-36 from that receipt; both depend on this bead, and this
bead depends on neither (a mechanism does not depend on its own gate run or on the decision about it).

## Scope

This bead is the capstone of grades 3 and 4. Three component beads build parts of it, and it depends on
them: N28b-32 is the verb's surface (registry entry, input and output schemas, per-obligation grades and
licences, the split verdict, exit codes; it lands at M1 in reduced form and fixes that `--grades` names the
highest grade the caller allows); N28b-33 is the grade-4 rerun planner (the node set to recompute, the choice
among checkers, costing and ordering, the plan record, launching it as jobs); N28b-34 is the content of the
polarity table (sixteen registered rules with side conditions and derivations, the five governing rules as
executable checks, fixture families, the exhaustive finite-game check of PO-ATEL-06). The pilot N28a-23 runs
first.

In this bead: the grade-3 evaluation path for non-mission verdict kinds in production (the purity check,
licences and their independent checker, the obligation split, joint-edit handling, rule disabling on a false
reuse), refusal 870 at every consumer, the verb's full M4 output with the planner's plan in it, the
regression run of the arms on the development campaign, and the acceptance of the assembled capability. The
Technical approach states the components' parts in brief; each component governs its own part in detail,
and the rule registry file is the engine's, `registries/revalidation-rules.toml`.

Out: grades 1 and 2, the rule registry's mechanics and the mission-side verb (BD-292); the memo graph
(BD-111, BD-220); the pilot (N28a-23); the gate run on the sealed M4 campaign with its four arms and the cost
figures it hands on (N28b-35); the kill decision (N28b-36).

## Technical approach

1. **Rule registry** (`registries/revalidation-rules.toml`, the engine's registry; a rule is data plus a
   checker; the rows are entered and tested by N28b-34):
   `{name, component, direction, preserves: inclusion | exclusion, side_conditions[], derivation, status:
   enabled | disabled(reason)}`. The rules are the "preserved" cells of the lattice:

   | Component | Change | Inclusion | Exclusion |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Disturbance or world set | Narrowed | Preserved, same witness | Recheck |
   | | Enlarged | Recheck | Preserved, if the policy class and the information contract are unchanged |
   | Hypothesis support | Enlarged | Recheck | Preserved |
   | Actuator limits | Relaxed | Preserved, if the old command stays realizable and the plant still realizes the old dynamics | Recheck |
   | | Tightened | Recheck | Preserved |
   | Requirement | Relaxed | Preserved | Recheck |
   | | Tightened | Recheck | Preserved |
   | Policy class | Enlarged | Preserved | Recheck |
   | | Shrunk | Preserved only if the witness policy is still in the class | Preserved |
   | Information contract | Refined (more, earlier or better observations, the old ones still recoverable) | Preserved | Recheck |
   | | Coarsened or delayed | Recheck | Preserved |
   | Catalog and prices | Part added or price lowered | Preserved | Recheck: every cheaper rival must be excluded again |
   | | Part removed | Preserved unless the witness uses the part | Preserved; an optimum that used the part is void |
   | Free space of the scene | Enlarged | Preserved, if the witness trace does not touch the changed region | Recheck every exclusion that used the removed obstacle |

2. **Five rules that govern the table, each enforced.**
   1. *A rule applies only to a pure change in its direction.* The model diff of the edit must write only
      fields of the one component, and the new value must be comparable to the old in that component's order
      (set inclusion; pointwise order of limits; refinement of information). A larger motor relaxes a torque
      limit and also changes mass and rotor inertia: not pure; grade 4.
   2. *Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations.* The engine evaluates each obligation on its own
      support. Widen a doorway that excluded a cost-5 relay while a cost-9 cart was the certified minimum: the
      cart's feasibility obligations survive; "every cheaper rival is excluded" read the doorway and is
      revoked. The verdict splits into `feasible: preserved` and `optimal: revoked, recheck`. A cheaper, newly
      feasible rival always revokes an incumbent's optimality claim, including when an amendment adds a
      catalog part.
   3. *Joint edits need a joint check.* Polarity rules compose only when the edited components have disjoint
      dependency supports for the obligation; otherwise grade 4.
   4. *Improving something can destroy a result.* The "refined information" rule preserves inclusions only; an
      impossibility that rested on two worlds being indistinguishable is rechecked.
   5. *Unsafe reuse is a false accept*, even if a later change restores feasibility. One false reused verdict
      (found by shadow recomputation or by the gate) sets the licensing rule to `disabled` until repaired.
3. **Grade-3 licence record.** `{obligation, rule name and version, old assumption set, new assumption set,
   order relation, purity evidence (the diff's write set), side-condition evidence ids}`. An independent
   checker (`revalidate::check_licence`) re-derives the order relation from the two assumption sets and rejects
   an unknown rule name, a disabled rule, or a changed quantifier prefix, even when every hash is consistent.
4. **Refusal FROB-REF-870:** reuse of a verdict with no licence, or under a rule the registry does not know.
   Raised whenever any consumer (a report, a comparison, the cascade's reuse stage) presents a verdict after
   an edit without a grade and a licence (INV-ATEL-07). Repair: recheck (`frob result revalidate <id>`).
   The check is one function, `revalidate::require_licence(verdict, edit)`, that every consumer calls before
   it presents a verdict carried across an edit: a grade-1 key, a grade-2 mask that checks against the
   supports, or a grade-3 licence that `check_licence` accepts passes; anything else is 870 with the verdict's
   id as pointer. The consumer verbs that raise it from M4, the bead that wires each and the test that holds
   it: `report.design` and `report.mission` (BD-302's verbs, wired by this bead: `report_design_unlicensed_870`,
   `report_mission_unlicensed_870`); `study.compare` (N28a-21's verb, for an absolute verdict a comparison
   record cites across an edit; wired by this bead: `study_compare_unlicensed_verdict_870`); `opt.cascade`'s
   reuse stage (stage 2) and `report.campaign` (BD-352, which depends on this bead, wires them and holds
   `stage2_unlicensed_reuse_870` and `report_campaign_unlicensed_870`). VERBS-report, VERBS-study and VERBS-opt
   are asked to list 870 on those rows and N28-REF to name these verbs in its raised-by column.
5. **Grade 4: the rerun planner.** For the obligations left without a licence: the minimal set of reruns that
   re-establishes them (obligations that share an evaluation are grouped; an optimum's recheck lists the
   rivals to exclude again), each with the cost interval from the estimator, ordered cheapest first, and the
   total. The verb returns the plan; it runs nothing unless `--run` is given.
6. **Verb output.** `{surviving[{obligation, grade, licence}], revoked[{obligation, reason: field path}],
   invalidated_regions (cells, world addresses, design regions), reruns[{what, cost}], split verdicts}`; exit 0
   when everything survives, 2 otherwise. Kind `run` (it may evaluate side conditions); the flag `--grades`
   names the highest grade the caller allows, as the surface N28b-32 fixes it: `--grades 2` (default) or
   `--grades 3` (only with the feature flag `frontier-revalidate-implication`).
7. **Arms (from the same cache state and dirty fraction).** The gate run on the sealed M4 campaign is
   N28b-35's, with a fourth arm (the separately built incremental incumbent); this bead runs the three arms
   below on the development campaign as its regression. On the frozen campaign of
   30 amendments: (a) fresh solving of every verdict; (b) grades 1 and 2; (c) grades 1 to 3. Every decision of
   (b) and (c) is replayed against (a). Recorded: every incorrectly inherited verdict (unsafe reuse), every
   unnecessary invalidation (a verdict rechecked that fresh solving shows unchanged), the recheck cost each arm
   left, and the stale-key mutant's fate.
8. **Class.** Licences and masks are D0 data; evidence class of a reused verdict is unchanged from the original
   and its claim product gains the licence in its receipts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ATEL-11 (the seed's done-condition; measured and recorded by N28b-35 on a freshly sealed campaign,
      and reproduced by this bead's script on the development campaign) on the frozen 30-amendment campaign:
      every decision equals full recomputation; zero unsafe
      reuse; the stale-key mutant (a key without delivery time) is caught; unnecessary invalidations are at
      most 20% of unaffected verdicts, counted and reported.
- [ ] The four-state camera fixture (decisions commit at ticks 2 and 4; packet at tick 1; then a display label
      renamed; delivery delayed to tick 2; delivery delayed to tick 3): 2, 0, 0, 1 scene-pair evaluations and
      1, 0, 0, 1 verdict recomputations, each verdict equal to a fresh replay.
- [ ] Widening the doorway: the cost-9 cart stays feasible, its optimality is revoked; the output shows the
      split.
- [ ] Enlarging the docking fixture's hypothesis support by gain 33/32: every inclusion is rechecked and all
      34,590 positive decisions on each test grid are withdrawn; no rule preserves them.
- [ ] PO-ATEL-06: each enabled rule has a written derivation, a positive and a negative fixture, and passes
      the exhaustive check on the finite-game fragment (every amendment of the small docking game in each
      direction, compared with fresh solving).
- [ ] A motor swap (limit relaxed, mass changed) is classified impure and goes to grade 4.
- [ ] A planted false reuse disables its rule; a licence naming an unknown rule, or with a changed quantifier
      prefix and consistent hashes, is rejected; a verdict reused with no licence is refused (870) by each
      consumer verb this bead wires (`report.design`, `report.mission`, `study.compare`).
- [ ] The rerun plan's total cost is within its stated interval when the plan is executed on the campaign.
- [ ] The saving of grade 3 over grade 2 (recheck cost avoided as a share of what grade 2 leaves) is recorded
      for the kill decision.

- Consumer check, on the real verbs over a store holding a parent version's verdict and a child version made by
  a typed edit: `report_design_unlicensed_870` (`frob report design <child> --json` presenting the parent's

  mask); `report_mission_unlicensed_870` (an amended mission's answer without revalidation);

- Property (finite-game fragment): for 10³ random amendments of the docking game, every verdict the engine
  keeps equals the fresh verdict.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_revalidate.sh`, this bead's own script: applies each of the 30 amendments, a design
amendment with `frob design edit <design> <edit> … --expected-parent <id> --key ra-ed-<i> --json` and a
mission-side amendment through result.revalidate's own `--amendment field=value,…` (no stored edit needed),
calls `frob result revalidate <verdict> --after-edit <edit> --grades 2 --key ra-g2-<i> --json` and the same
with `--grades 3 --key ra-g3-<i>` under the session flag `frontier-revalidate-implication` (the two arms are
compared, so their keys differ), runs the fresh arm, and compares; then the consumer check on the real verbs:
`frob report design <child> --json` before revalidation (870, exit 3) and after it (presented with its grade),
and `frob study compare` on a record citing the parent's verdict for the child (870); then runs the gate's
known-answer test in the Gauntlet's form
(ADR-GAUNT-01: there is no `frob conformance` command): `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-ATEL-11` (the tier is the gate record's `tier` field,
`g1_known_answers` for this Known-answer gate; the mutation feature because the stale-key control runs), on the
development campaign, and logs the hash-named receipt it writes.
It sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and writes `target/e2e-logs/atelier_revalidate/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time
and the envelope's `evidence` block. Logged per amendment: the edit class, obligations by grade, licences
(rule names), revoked obligations with field paths, the fresh verdict, `unsafe_reuse` and
`unnecessary_invalidation` booleans, recheck cost per arm; then the totals against zero and 20%, the stale-key
mutant's result, the consumer refusals with code and pointer, and the grade-3 saving ratio against 1/5; on
failure the first amendment whose decision differs from the fresh arm; a final `summary` line naming
GATE-ATEL-11.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-699 (camera fixture), KA-700 (doorway), KA-701 (33/32). Negative controls: CX-315 (a memo key that omits
delivery time keeps a stale promotion at the fourth state; the registered mutant must be caught), CX-316 (an
optimum kept after a cheaper rival became feasible), CX-314, and a dependency graph with one edge omitted,

the three arms start from one snapshot of the store.

## Considerations

- KILL-REUSE: any unsafe reuse in GATE-ATEL-11, or grade 3 saving less than a fifth of the recheck cost grade
  2 leaves (TARGET). Fallback: grades 1 and 2 only. KILL-AUGUR-REUSE (standing since M3) removes a single rule
  on one incorrectly inherited verdict; the two are consistent: a rule can be lost without losing the grade,
  and the grade can be lost as a whole.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete memo key serves a stale verdict) is the failure this whole section exists to prevent;
  the shadow recomputation of the memo graph is its production detector.
- Training avoided by a warm start is not reuse in this sense; it is reported in its own column of the reuse
  ledger and credited to inheritance once.
- OPEN-ATEL-4 (owner: Tether lead; blocks the verb registry freeze at M3): whether `result.revalidate` and the
  mission verb stay two verbs over one engine. Default: two verbs, one engine; this bead adds no third.
