## Background

Two things close here: the last three analyses of the catalogue (A-24 task-wrench attainability, A-25
identifiability, A-26 regime atlas), which the seed names together, and the gate that covers the later half of
the analysis catalogue.

The three analyses are built by their own beads, on which this bead depends: A-24 by N28ap1-02 (the cone
program with a separating dual witness, FROB-REF-448, the verb `analyze.wrench`), A-25 by N28a-13 (sensitivity
rank over a proposed excitation, with regime coverage), A-26 by N28a-16 (the regime atlas, built on the branch
the KILL-ATLAS decision N28a-15 chose after its pilot N28a-14). The other analyses of the later half are
implemented by the subsystems that own their mathematics: A-15 structural margin (BD-401, M5), A-16
sensitivity (N33b-18, the verb `analyze.sensitivity`), A-17 observability and aliasing (BD-120, BD-239, BD-240), A-18 clearance and route
feasibility (BD-233), A-19 rank robustness (BD-346). This bead collects their rows.

**GATE-ATEL-05**: each analysis A-15 to A-26 passes its known answers (the studio's table of known answers and
the owning chapters') and its negative control fails; the analyses marked for `check` keep `design check` under
2 s. Its rows for A-20 to A-23 form the gate's M1 part, measured by BD-108 and recorded by N28a-29; the rest
form the M4 part, which this bead measures and records (the register keeps one part per milestone, N28c-25).

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-05; KILL-ATLAS decided (the decision is N28a-15, on which the A-26 bead
depends; this bead records the decision's identifier beside the A-26 row).

## Scope

In: the gate's M4 measurement and rows for A-16 to A-19 and A-24 to A-26, the A-15 row recorded
`not-yet-due (M5)`, the `check` timing with every analysis marked for it, and the M4 section of the gate's
end-to-end steps. Out: A-24 (N28ap1-02); A-25 and A-26 (N28a-13, N28a-16); the KILL-ATLAS pilot and decision
(N28a-14, N28a-15); A-15 to A-19 (their owners); the rows for A-20 to A-23 (the M1 part: BD-108 measures them,
N28a-29 records them); the stage table and its
revised budgets (N14bx-01); skill artifacts (BD-336) and the cone solver (BD-115).

## Technical approach

**Gate completion (M4).**
1. Rows collected by running the gate in the form ADR-GAUNT-01 fixes (BD-025: there is no `frob conformance`
   command, verb or binary, and BD-025's lint `no_conformance_command` fails a script that keeps the literal
   form): `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test <tier> --
   GATE-ATEL-05`, with `<tier>` the test target named by the gate record's `tier` field (written by N28c-25),
   and recorded with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-ATEL-05 --receipt
   <hash> --part M4` (N38b-19; the M1 part is N28a-29's recording). One row per analysis, each with its known answer's measured value against its truth, the negative control's
   identifier and `caught: true`, the timing, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed, written in the gate record
   form (N40-01) under the Atelier rows. A row carries measured values, never targets.

   | Analysis | Owner bead | Known answer | Negative control that must fail |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | A-15 Structural margin | BD-401 (M5) | Its owner's beam and margin fixtures (due M5) | Its owner's |
   | A-16 Sensitivity | N33b-18 | Its owner's fixtures | Its owner's |
   | A-17 Observability and aliasing | BD-120, BD-239, BD-240 | Seven independent readings at 0.75: 3807/4096 ≈ 0.929; fully shared error: 0.75 | A redundancy score computed from per-reading accuracy |
   | A-18 Clearance and route feasibility | BD-233 | A doorway narrower than every orientation of the footprint returns an impossibility cut that names the doorway; widened past the footprint it returns a witness path | A clearance test on a bounding ball for a rectangular footprint |
   | A-19 Rank robustness | BD-346 | A finalist whose advantage comes from diagonal traction on a pyramidal cone (up to √2·μ) is typed `model-conditional` | The same campaign with the rank-robustness stage skipped reports it as the winner |
   | A-24 Task-wrench attainability | N28ap1-02 | A task wrench outside the attainable set returns a dual vector that separates it from every admissible actuation (the two-link arm: c = (1, 0), support 25/3 N against 10 N) | An infeasible wrench reported as `not-synthesizable` with no witness |
   | A-25 Identifiability | N28a-13 | Zero sensitivity to μ at 1 A and 2 A; 4 N against 8 N at 3 A under 10 N | A fit that reports μ from the first two runs |
   | A-26 Regime atlas | N28a-16 | A run with one planted mode switch is partitioned into two regions with the boundary at the switch tick | A gradient reported as trustworthy across the switch |

2. Each analysis's row is written only when its own bead's known-answer test passes through the binary; a row
   whose owner has not landed is `not-yet-due (<milestone>)` with the owner's key, never a pass.
3. KILL-ATLAS: the A-26 row records which branch the decision N28a-15 chose (validity label kept, or A-26 as a
   descriptive report with no validity label); the A-26 negative control applies on the first branch, and on
   the second the row asserts that no validity label is printed.
4. Timing: `design check` with every analysis marked for it (now including A-17's structural rank in stage 8,
   A-18 and A-24 when bound, A-20 to A-23) stays under 2 s at the 95th percentile on the archetypes for stages
   1 to 6 (stage 6 within N14bx-01's revised 875 ms when A-20 and A-24 are bound); stages that bind a scene
   (7, 8) are measured separately and carry no total. 200 cold runs per archetype, empty memo graph.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/gate_atel05_m4.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-analyze --test gate_atel05_m4`:
- `rows_cover_a15_to_a26_once`: with the M1 part's four rows (A-20 to A-23), the union is A-15 to A-26 with no
  duplicate.
- `not_landed_is_not_yet_due`: a row for an analysis whose bead is absent from the landed set is
  `not-yet-due`, never `pass`.
- `measured_not_target`: the register check rejects a copied target.
- `atlas_branch_recorded`: on each KILL-ATLAS branch the A-26 row asserts the matching behaviour.
- `timing_row_shape`: per-stage medians and 95th percentiles with budgets and `budget-miss` entries.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the gate steps to the M4 section of `scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (the script is owned by
N28a-29, convention 11; `--section m4`). The section first runs as stages the M4 analysis beads' own scripts,
each failing the section when it fails: `analyze_wrench.sh` (N28ap1-02), `analyze_identifiability.sh` (N28a-13)
and `analyze_regimes.sh` (N28a-16). For A-16 to A-19, whose owners live in other subsystems, it runs each known-answer
fixture through its verb (`frob analyze sensitivity`, `observability`, `route --scene …`, `rank-robustness
<campaign>`, each with `--json`, in the form of the row's registered example in VERBS-analyze with the bindings
it names) and its negative-control mutant through the Gauntlet, then `FROB_TEST_SEED=$FROB_E2E_SEED cargo
test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test <tier> -- GATE-ATEL-05` (the form above; the
receipt hash is logged; the M4 part is then recorded by this bead with `record_gate … --part M4`, never by the
script) and the
`check` timing on the four M1 archetypes with a skill and a budget bound. The script sources the STD-E2E helper
and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/atelier_check/<utc>.ndjson`; each line
carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block, per analysis the measured value against its truth with units, the control's identifier and
whether it was caught, the KILL-ATLAS branch, the 95th-percentile `check` time per stage against its budget;
on failure the first differing value and the paths to reproduce; a final `summary` line naming GATE-ATEL-05.

## Considerations

- Milestone mismatch, recorded: the gate's remaining rows are due at M4, while A-15's mechanism and its own
  gate are scheduled at M5. This bead closes the gate at M4 for the analyses that exist and leaves the A-15 row
  open with its owner; the gate register shows the part as open, and the M5 exit review closes it.
- The rows depend on beads in five subsystems; a late owner delays only its own row, which is recorded
  `not-yet-due`, so the M4 exit review sees exactly what is missing.
- KILL-STATIC, if fired at M3, leaves the known-answer rows unchanged (they test the analyses' mathematics) and
  changes only whether `check` refuses.
