## Background

Two things close here: the last static analysis that the studio itself implements, and the gate that covers
the later half of the analysis catalogue.

**A-24 Task-wrench attainability** answers: can this body produce the wrench a skill or a task needs, inside
torque, speed, friction and support limits? A skill is a behaviour stated at the task boundary (push a cart
handle with 40 N; hold a tray level), independent of the body that performs it. Before any controller is
synthesized or any policy trained, a cone program says whether the body can exert the required wrench at the
states the skill visits. If it cannot, the answer is not "synthesis failed": it is a separating dual vector,
which names the direction in task space the body cannot reach, by how much it falls short, and which actuator
or contact binds. A refusal that cites a separating vector tells the agent which constraint to edit. The
program is the one the contact solver and the control programs already share, so no second solver enters.
Tier Solid; in `check` when a skill or task wrench is bound.

**GATE-ATEL-05**: each analysis A-15 to A-26 passes its known answers and its negative control fails; the
analyses marked for `check` keep `design check` under 2 s. Its rows for A-20 to A-23 closed at the first
studio milestone (bead BD-108); the rest close here. A-25 and A-26 are their own beads (N28a-13, N28a-16);
the others are implemented by the subsystems that own their mathematics, and this bead collects their rows.

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-05; KILL-ATLAS decided (the decision is N28a-15, on which the A-26 bead
depends).

## Technical approach

**A-24, numbered.**
1. *Inputs.* The design; a wrench requirement: a `.fskill` phase with its wrench profile, or a task's declared
   wrench set at a task frame; the contact mode m for each phase; the states to test (the reference stance, the
   skill's sampled reference states, or a declared operating box); the minimum friction μ_min the skill
   assumes; actuator cards.
2. *Map.* For a morphology and a contact mode, the equations of motion M·v̇ + h = Sᵀ·u + J_cᵀ·f_c with the
   mode's contact constraints give, after a constrained mass solve that eliminates v̇ and f_c, an affine map
   from actuator effort u to the task-space wrench: w = w₀(q, v) + B_m(q)·u. For a floating-base robot the map
   must come from this contact-consistent solve; a fixed-base pullback τ = Jᵀ·F is wrong and is a registered
   mutant.
3. *Program.* Minimize ‖B_m·u − (w* − w₀)‖² plus declared secondary costs, subject to: actuator boxes from
   the torque–speed envelope at the state's joint speeds; contact forces in their friction cones (with μ_min
   for forces the robot commands); unilateral support limits. A cone program in the shared solver.
4. *Result.* Either a feasible-allocation witness (u, f_c) that replays on the referee; or an infeasibility
   witness: a direction c in task space, the support value h(c) = max cᵀ(w₀ + B_m·u) over admissible u, which
   is below cᵀ·w*, the gap, and the binding actuator or contact. The dual certificate is re-checked in exact
   rationals (strict separation only; a non-strict witness yields `unknown`).
5. *Typing.* Infeasibility at a state is an exclusion typed `outer` for the instantaneous model at that state
   and mode, with the authority model named (independently commanded contact forces unless a passive
   suspension is declared). Feasibility at every sampled state is necessary and not sufficient: the result is
   `instantaneously-attainable`, never `inner`; multi-step feasibility (a capture-point recovery, a
   momentum-assisted push) is outside the map and the output says so.
6. *Findings.* `A-24.<phase>.unattainable` with the witness; inside `check`, when the request binds a skill,
   the refusing code is FROB-REF-448 (a skill's task wrench lies outside the body's attainable set). Repairs
   ranked by the contract and re-solved: `regear` or `swap-actuator` on the binding joint, `reshape` or
   `move-mount` to change the lever, or change the wrench profile. A finding never says `not-synthesizable`
   without its witness.
7. *Budget and class.* One small program per (state, mode); 64 states inside 200 ms (TARGET). Double precision
   solve with exact certificate check; evidence `Certified` when the certificate is emitted, else `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Planar two-link arm, links 0.4 m and 0.3 m, joint angles 0 and π/2: tool point (0.4, 0.3) m, point
      Jacobian [[−0.3, −0.3], [0.4, 0.0]] m/rad; a tool force of (10, 0) N needs torques (−3, −3) N·m; with
      each actuator limited to 2.5 N·m the analysis returns infeasible with direction c = (1, 0), support value
      25/3 N against the requirement 10 N, and binding actuator joint 2; at 3.5 N·m it returns a witness.
- [ ] The dual certificate re-checks in exact rationals; a perturbed multiplier is rejected.
- [ ] The fixed-base pullback mutant gives the wrong answer on a floating-base fixture and is caught; so is the
      mutant that takes the lower rows of a space-frame screw Jacobian in place of the point Jacobian.
- [ ] Feasible results are labelled `instantaneously-attainable` and never `inner`.
- [ ] With a skill bound, `design check` refuses an unattainable wrench with FROB-REF-448 and the witness; with
      none bound, A-24 is listed `not-run: unbound`.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-05, M4 part: the rows for A-16, A-17, A-18, A-19, A-24, A-25 and A-26 pass with their controls
      caught; the A-15 row is recorded `not-yet-due (M5)` with its owner bead; `design check` stays under 2 s
      at the 95th percentile.
