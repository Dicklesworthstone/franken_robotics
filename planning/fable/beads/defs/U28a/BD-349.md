## Background

Eleven decisions recur across the studio, and each one moves compute or changes what is reported as
established: promote a result, advance a design, widen a margin, certify a failure rate, buy an experiment,
rank a cause, issue a reality band, choose solver effort, schedule a job, pick a battery, accept a kernel
rewrite. ADR-ATEL-06: decision rules are compiled to tables. Every adaptive decision is compiled ahead of time
into an integer guard table, an automaton or a monitor with a version, stored under `registries/cores/`,
regenerated in continuous integration and diffed. The runtime evaluates tables. No decision reads the wall
clock, device load or arrival order. The reason: replay, audit, and a rollback that is a configuration flag.
ADR-ATEL-09, the controller gate: no adaptive controller is enabled by default without a replay scenario,
evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback; adoption is always shadow (report both
rules, act on the old), then canary, then default, and the incumbent in each shadow run is the system's own
reference path. ADR-ATEL-11: the cores are named DC-01 to DC-11.

The eight mathematical families sit in three planes of at most three: evidence (sequential tests, conformal
prediction and risk control, value of information), failure (rare-event simulation, counterfactual forks,
submodular coverage) and platform (queueing, certified rewrites). Successive halving is a fixed table, not a
family.

DC-01, DC-09 and DC-10 were recorded at the third milestone (bead BD-309). This bead delivers the compiler

and the gate GATE-ATEL-13 for the eight: each core's table regenerates bit-identically; its null calibration
holds; its fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger; its shadow run records both actions. The records
of DC-04 and DC-06 are bead N28a-27; those of DC-07, DC-08 and DC-11 are N28a-28.

## Scope

In: `frob-opt::cores` (compile, load, decide), the evaluation rules every core obeys, the shadow harness, the
records and tables of DC-02, DC-03 and DC-05, the gate's M4 rows. Out: the runtime logic of the ladder cores
(BD-329, N28a-33) and of experiment suggestion (BD-351); the other cores' records (N28a-27, N28a-28); the
interference tests between cores (BD-350); the contract check itself (BD-309).

## Technical approach

1. **Compiler.** `cores::compile(contract: &CoreContract) -> CoreTable`: a deterministic generator per core
   that turns the contract's constants into `frob.core-table/1`: a header (core id, policy version, family,
   input schema, content hash of the contract) and a body that is one of: an integer guard table (inputs
   quantized to declared integer grids → action code), a finite automaton (states, guarded transitions,
   outputs), or a monitor (fixed-point update rule with thresholds). Canonical bytes; BLAKE3 content hash;
   `xtask check-generated` regenerates every table and compares.
2. **Evaluation rules (all cores).** `CoreTable::decide(inputs, frontier) -> Action` is a pure function of the
   table version, the ledger state at a pinned frontier and addresses (INV-ATEL-06); integer or exact-rational
   arithmetic only; no clock, no device load, no arrival order. A table that is missing, stale against its
   contract hash, or not the registry version is refused at use with **FROB-REF-871** (repair: regenerate; the
   fallback is in force meanwhile). Every decision writes an evidence record: the identities read, the table
   version, the ledger frontier, the action taken and, while in shadow, the action the baseline took, with
   the core's decision-policy identifier and version, so any verdict can be traced to the exact rule that
   produced it.
3. **Adoption state machine.** `shadow → canary → default`, stored in the record; a transition requires the
   core's gate row with a pass and, for Frontier cores, its kill decision `not fired`. A registry check
   refuses a status that its rows do not support. For a Frontier core the fallback is also its kill fallback.
4. **Shadow harness.** `cores::shadow_replay(log, core)` replays a recorded campaign log (the ladder log of
   the pilot, and later real campaigns) through a core's table and its baseline and writes the paired action
   records; it needs no consumer verb, so a core's shadow run is its pilot.
5. **DC-02 · Which designs advance.** Artifacts: the bracket schedule of the fixed table; per rung pair and
   stratum the survivor rule with its margin; the one-way handover marker to DC-05; the label
   `budget-truncated`. Loss model: eliminating a true top-k design, against the rung cost of promoting a poor
   one (the first is far dearer). Obligations: coverage and misordering bounds of the conformal layer; top-k
   recall measured by the audit lane; no design served by two layers. Fallback: halving at η = 3. Triggers:
   under 19 calibration points; martingale at or above 20; audited miss rate above α; survivors above 3k for
   two generations; a change of identity or scope. Budgeted mode: audit lane at a tenth of rung spend;
   survivors capped at 3k, then truncation by lower-rung score with the label. Compiled form: an automaton
   over (mode, calibration count, martingale bucket, survivor-count bucket) → `fixed-table`, `conformal`,
   `truncate`, `handover`.
6. **DC-03 · The survivor margin and the mode of a rung.** Artifacts: the table of margins q̂ by (rung pair,
   stratum, α); the keyed list of audit draws; the exchangeability martingale; the mode automaton with
   hysteresis (a mode changes only at a generation boundary; two consecutive generations of valid calibration
   to become `selective`). Loss model: as DC-02, plus the cost of the audit lane. Obligations: coverage at
   least 1 − α for designs exchangeable with the audit set; exchangeability of the audit draws by
   construction; a pair separated by the margin misordered with probability at most 2α; the automaton never
   changes inside a generation. Fallback: `discard-only` with the fixed table; triggers as DC-02. Budgeted
   mode: the audit tenth; when that buys fewer than 19 audit designs in a stratum, strata are merged upward to
   the morphology class before the stratum falls back. Worked example: n = 39, α = 0.05: q̂ is the 38th
   smallest residual, say 0.04; the third-best lower-rung score is 0.74; the cut is 0.66.
7. **DC-05 · Which experiment next, or stop.** Artifacts: an experiment table (simulation at a rung, a bench
   measurement, a real batch) with cost in GPU-second equivalents at a declared exchange rate, a closed-form
   expected value of sample information and their ratio; a Gaussian posterior on the finalists' score
   differences with sampling, tier-bias and reality-gap variance components read from the ledgers; the stop
   rule: no ratio clears the budget price. Loss model: opportunity loss of the accepted design plus experiment
   cost. Obligations: the value is zero when no outcome can change the choice; its intervals are scored
   against registered predictions; it never issues a verdict. Fallback: the fixed order check, simulate,
   train, referee. Trigger: 80% intervals cover outside 65 to 92% over 30 experiments. Budgeted mode: 64
   candidates, closed form only. Formula: for a difference Δ ~ N(m, s²) between the leader and a rival, an
   experiment that removes variance s̃² has EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃ (φ and Φ the standard
   normal density and distribution). Worked example, illustrative: m = 0.03, s² = 0.03² (sampling) + 0.04²
   (tier bias); per-pair noise 0.3 on the fast tier and 0.1 on the referee. Two hundred more fast-tier pairs
   shrink only the sampling component (0.0009 → 0.0003, so s̃² = 0.0006) and are worth 0.0013 for 1
   GPU-second; eight referee pairs observe Δ itself with variance 0.1²/8 (s̃² = 0.0025²/(0.0025 + 0.00125) =
   0.00167) and are worth 0.0055 for 27; the fast tier wins sixfold per unit cost. A second fast batch is
   worth 10⁻⁵, so the referee goes next: cheap episodes are bought until only tier bias is left, which only
   the referee can shrink. At m ≥ 0.15 no experiment can change the choice: stop and decide. Compiled form: a
   guard table over quantized (z, s̃/price·cost) → `buy <experiment>` or `stop`, plus the calibration monitor.
8. **Rejected methods, recorded with the cores they were considered for.** Gaussian-process surrogates over
   designs (DC-02, DC-05): no validated kernel on typed designs of varying topology; continuous sub-problems
   already have a Bayesian optimizer. A learned correction of the fast tier, or a learned step surrogate as a
   screen (DC-01, DC-02): it hides exactly the artifacts the trust ladder exists to expose. Bandit allocation
   (DC-02, DC-09): regret is the wrong objective for identifying the best design, and a bandit on dispatch is
   a second controller on shared telemetry. Expected information gain (DC-05): it buys knowledge that cannot
   change the choice; the estimator is doubly intractable and fails under misspecification. Adaptive-fidelity
   stopping as a decision procedure (DC-01, DC-02): stopping a pair early on cheap labels does not compute the
   sealed fixed-n decision; kept as a scheduling heuristic that orders work without touching validity. Convex
   dual prices: one scalar, already the DC-05 threshold.
9. **Gate rows.** For each of DC-02 to DC-08 and DC-11: `regenerates: bit-identical` (hash), the
   null-calibration result with its obligation id (PO-ATEL-03 for DC-02 and DC-03; the zero-value and interval
   obligations for DC-05; PO-ATEL-10 for DC-08; the others from N28a-27 and N28a-28), `fallback demonstrated`
   per trigger, `shadow records: both actions`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cores::compile` is deterministic: two builds on both development platforms give identical bytes for
      every table; `check-generated` fails on a hand edit.
- [ ] `decide` links no clock symbol and passes a purity property test (same frontier and inputs, same action;
      different arrival orders, same action).
- [ ] A missing, stale or non-registry table is refused at use (871) and the fallback acts.
- [ ] The DC-05 worked example reproduces 0.0013, 0.0055 and about 10⁻⁵; the value is exactly zero when s̃ = 0
      and when no outcome can change the choice; `stop` is returned at m = 0.15.
- [ ] DC-05's calibration monitor trips when 80% intervals cover 60% of 30 scored predictions, and the fixed
      order takes over.
- [ ] Each trigger of DC-02 and DC-03 (five each) forces the fallback at the next boundary, recorded.
- [ ] Shadow replay over the pilot's ladder log produces paired action records for DC-02, DC-03 and DC-05.
- [ ] No adoption status beyond `shadow` exists without its gate row; a Frontier core whose kill gate fired is
      pinned to its fallback and the registry says so.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-13 has recorded rows with measured values for DC-02 to DC-08 and DC-11 (the DC-07 shadow row
      marked `not-yet-due (M5)`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_cores.sh`: `frob cores show DC-02|DC-03|DC-05 --json`; each demonstration manifest
(commands under 60 s, checksums); `cargo xtask check-generated`; a forced-trigger pass
(`frob conformance run --gate GATE-ATEL-13 --cores DC-02,DC-03,DC-05 --force-triggers --json`); the shadow
replay over the ladder log. Sourcing the shared helper of STD-E2E, it logs per core: table hash against the
registry, calibration values against bounds, each trigger with the fallback observed, the count of shadow
records and the share on which the core and its baseline disagreed, the adoption status, and the gate rows.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-696 (the conformal index 38 of 39) and the value-of-information arithmetic above; KA-695, KA-703 and KA-704
belong to the cores recorded earlier and are re-run by the script to show the shared compiler did not disturb
them. Negative controls: a table edited by hand; a core that reads a clock (planted `Instant::now` in a test
build); a status set to `default` with no rows; a value-of-information number placed in a verdict field
(compile failure; and at run time FROB-REF-872 when a consumer cites one as a verdict). Determinism: tables
and decisions are D0.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-1 (owner: studio lead; blocks the full gate): the loss constants of DC-01 (C_fp, C_fn, the pair
  cost c) and the exchange rate between GPU-seconds and real experiment cost in DC-05. Fail-closed default:
  the fixed 30-pair rule and the fixed experiment order. With the default in force DC-05's row records
  `fallback by open question`, which is a legitimate recorded state, not a pass.
- Every core is re-examined against the first profile of the second milestone; a decision whose share of
  compute turns out negligible keeps its fallback and loses its adaptive form.
- Two tensions are reconciled by construction: conformal exchangeability against drift (the alarm ends the
  regime and calibration restarts inside the new one), and a parametric model beside distribution-free tests
  (the parametric model never issues a verdict).
- Timescales, slowest first: DC-11 at build; DC-10 per battery revision; DC-08 at ledger refresh; DC-07 per
  real batch; DC-03 per generation; DC-02 per rung transition; DC-05 per experiment; DC-04 per finalist; DC-06
  per failure cluster; DC-01 per episode batch; DC-09 per quantum. Each loop treats every slower one as a
  constant, and no loop writes a quantity a faster loop is reading inside its own period.
