## Background

An agent designing a robot chooses topology, leg count and actuator families; underneath, something has to
turn a brief and a few hundred candidate designs into a small set of finalists with evidence. ADR-ATEL-08:
agents are the discrete optimizer; the studio supplies fast continuous optimization, population evaluation and
honest comparison underneath. The co-design cascade is that machinery in one verb:
`frob opt cascade --spec <mission or task> --budget …` runs from a specification to a ranked, typed result
set, streaming progress with denominators. It is the evaluation ladder applied to a population, with a control
built into every stage, because a pipeline that screens can throw away the winner and nothing downstream will
notice. It is orchestration over the studio's other verbs and is judged as such: every number in its answer
comes from the verbs it calls, and each stage is also callable alone.

| Stage | Content | Rungs | Output | Control built into the stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Deduplicate and check | One canonical representative per orbit under the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup; the mission-observable digest; `design.check` with the bound analyses | R0 | Representatives; typed exclusions | Two designs that must merge and two that must split |
| 2 Reuse and synthesize | Memo hits for analyses, controllers, step plans and episodes; module contracts reused where the edit is local; bounded exact linkage synthesis when the brief asks for a mechanism | R0 | Reuse receipts; candidate set | A mirror swap must reuse; a shared-bus edit must invalidate |
| 3 Screen | Gait-orbit screening (A-04), family policy with no adaptation, task-wrench attainability (A-24) | R1 | Survivor set with the mode of each stratum | The blinded stratified audit of discards and the planted winner |
| 4 Race | A mixed-morphology race on paired addresses; fine-tuning for survivors, specialists for finalists | R1 to R3 | Finalists, `provisional` | A null race keeps the best with probability at least 1 − α |
| 5 Rank robustness | A-19: the top-k and the runners-up re-evaluated under model-form variants, with the created-energy audit | R3, R4 | Each finalist typed `model-robust` or `model-conditional` | Planted cone and energy-pumping exploits must be caught |
| 6 Qualify | Sealed confirmation on fresh world clusters with pre-committed requests; promotion on the referee; the rare-failure bound | R4, R5 | A qualified design comparison with receipts | False-promotion rate at most α under adaptive retries on no-signal worlds |

The co-design inner loop is a controller that needs no per-design training (the synthesized baseline
controller, which costs seconds, or the family policy of the design's morphology class); fine-tuning is spent
only on survivors and specialist training only on finalists.

The cascade as a whole is Frontier with Solid controls; each adaptive piece has its own kill gate and fallback
(stage by stage), so the cascade always has a fixed-table form to fall back to. The agent-facing one-call
pipeline `solve.run` calls `opt.cascade` and adds no mechanism; there is one surface A/B, not two, and if
agents with the one-verb form do no better than agents driving the verbs by hand, it stays a convenience and
is not counted as a lever.

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-16; NS-06 demonstrated.

In: the campaign manifest, the stage driver and its state machine, each stage's wiring to the verbs that do the
work, the six controls and the rule for a control that cannot run, honest early endings, the result set, the

(BD-372); the surface A/B (BD-374).

## Technical approach

1. **Manifest (`frob.campaign/1`, written to the ledger before anything runs).** The specification (task or
   mission id); the population source (designs supplied by the agent, an archive, a generator with its split);
   strata; the number of finalists k; the budget and its split by stage; the allocation rule per rung pair
   (fixed table by default; conformal where adopted); audit rates by stratum; the planted winner as a hash
   (sealed until close); stage-5 variants; the qualification campaign; seeds; the decision-policy versions in
   force. The manifest hash is the campaign id.
2. **Driver.** A state machine over stages with a commit at each stage boundary, so a campaign resumes after a
   crash or a cancel at the last committed boundary with identical results. Progress events carry
   denominators (`designs_in`, `designs_out`, `episodes_done/planned`, GPU-seconds spent and budgeted).
   The driver's decisions are the ladder's and the cores'; it reads no clock.
3. **Stage 1.** Call campaign quotienting (one representative per orbit under the observation-preserving
   subgroup; the mission-observable digest), then `design.check` with the bound analyses. Outputs:
   representatives with the members they stand for; typed exclusions (only `outer` or `exact`
   infeasibilities exclude). Arithmetic the stage must reproduce on its fixture: ten leg types on six slots
   with dihedral symmetry give (10⁶ + 2·10 + 2·10² + 10³ + 3·10⁴ + 3·10³)/12 = 86,185 representatives, an
   11.6-fold reduction; with only a sagittal mirror left because a sensor broke the rest,
   (10⁶ + 10³)/2 = 500,500, a 2.0-fold reduction. Control: a planted pair that must merge (mirror images under
   the observation-preserving group) and a planted pair that must split (equal first moment and different
   second moment of a ballast, or equal under the mechanical group and distinguished by a sensor).
4. **Stage 2.** Serve memo hits; call revalidation for verdicts inherited from parent designs (dependency
   grades by default); synthesize baseline controllers; call linkage synthesis when the brief asks. Control: a
   mirror swap of a leaf module must reuse its siblings' results; an edit to the shared bus must invalidate.
5. **Stage 3.** R1 scoring per stratum under the mode gating of the ladder; orbit screening and A-24 as
   screening signals; where adopted, the controller-budget escalation rule. Control: the audit lane's
   stratified, blinded audit of discards, and the planted winner (see 9).
6. **Stage 4.** A mixed-morphology race on paired addresses with mid-evaluation cancellation of the dominated;
   fine-tuning for survivors (R2), specialists for finalists (R3). Finalists are `provisional`. Control: a null
   race (a planted set of identical candidates) keeps the designated best with probability at least 1 − α
   over the control's replays.
7. **Stage 5.** Rank robustness: the top-k and the runners-up re-evaluated under the variants (a) the
   alternative contact law where the hard-contact batch law is available; (b) the circular against the
   pyramidal friction cone; (c) contact stiffness at one half and at twice its value; (d) half the step; (e)
   one tenth of the solver tolerance. The stage costs at most a tenth of campaign compute (TARGET). A finalist
   is `model-robust {variants, top-k overlap, energy audit}` when the top-k set and the order of the top two
   survive every variant and no finalist's advantage coincides with created energy or cone excess; otherwise
   `model-conditional`, the report shows both orders as a fidelity-dependent ranking, and two things are
   blocked: the result cannot be reported as a design comparison without its condition, and it cannot spend
   prototype budget, so a build-packet request is refused (FROB-REF-557). A design is preferred only if it
   wins under both qualified contact laws. Control: planted cone-excess and energy-pumping exploits must be
   typed `model-conditional`.
8. **Stage 6.** Pre-committed requests to the blinded qualification lane on fresh sealed world clusters at the
   level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] of the i-th request; promotion on the referee by the promotion rule; the
   rare-failure bound for finalists. Control: on no-signal worlds, adaptive retries produce a false promotion
   at a rate at most α.
9. **Screening audits, as applied here.** (i) The stratified audit of discards: strata by stage, reason for
   discard, morphology class and archive cell, at rates fixed in the manifest; an audit design and a survivor
   are the same job type with the same budget; with no true top-k design among m audited discards the rate of
   discarded winners is at most 1 − δ^(1/m) at confidence 1 − δ (9.5% at m = 30 and δ = 0.05; 5.0% at m = 59).
   (ii) The planted winner: a design known from an earlier specialist-and-referee result to belong in the top
   k, inserted in a form the screen represents poorly (an unusual gait, or a morphology class with few
   examples in the family policy's training set), its identity sealed in the manifest hash and revealed at
   close. It must reach the finalists; if it does not, the results are labelled `screen-biased`, the offending
   rung becomes `uninformative` in that stratum and the campaign re-opens. A stage whose audit is missing is
   treated the same way. (iii) Prune soundness: pruned designs are sampled and run at R1 or above; one
   feasible pruned design suspends that pruning rule for the campaign and files the case as a negative-control
   seed.
10. **A control that cannot run.** A stage that cannot run its control (no planted winner available, no second
    contact law admitted) reports the control as `not-run`, and the result carries that; finalists are then
    "k survivors".
11. **Honest endings.** Every stage may end the campaign: "every admitted alternative fails", "tied within the
    margin" and "inconclusive at this budget" are results, returned with exit 2 and the evidence.
12. **Result set.** Finalists in rank order where the modes allow ranking (otherwise as an unordered survivor
    set), each with its comparison records, the mode of every rung that selected it, its rank-robustness type,
    its qualification receipt if it has one, its rare-failure bound, and its claim product; the exclusions and
    discards by stage with reasons; the control outcomes; the fallbacks that fired.
13. **`frob report campaign <id>`.** On top of the design report's contents it adds the phase ledger, the
    screening audit, the planted-winner outcome and the list of fallbacks that fired; it prints the mode of
    every rung and stratum, the three reuse columns where recorded, and describes finalists chosen under any
    mode other than `selective` as "k survivors". A report is generated from the artifact graph and prints
    nothing it cannot resolve; the same graph produces the same bytes; a losing campaign is reported with the
    same completeness as a winning one.
14. **Verb.** `opt.cascade`: kind `run`, first milestone M4, idempotency key and budget, exit 8 with a job
    while running; `--stage n` runs one stage on a stored campaign; `--allocation fixed|conformal`;
    `--estimate` returns the cost model per stage without running.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ATEL-16: on the seeded population the cascade runs end to end; all six stage controls run and pass;
      the report prints every mode, every fallback and the phase ledger; merging two agents' archives in both
      orders gives identical bytes.
- [ ] Stage 1 reproduces 86,185 and 500,500 on the counting fixture, merges the planted mergeable pair and
      splits the planted pair; deduplication under the mechanical group when a sensor breaks it is caught.
- [ ] Stage 3: the planted winner reaches the finalists; in a variant where the screen is sabotaged so that it
      is discarded, the results are labelled `screen-biased`, the rung is set `uninformative` in that stratum
      and the campaign re-opens; no audit episode is cancelled.
- [ ] Stage 5: the finalist whose advantage comes from diagonal traction on a pyramidal cone (up to √2·μ) and
      the energy-pumping finalist are typed `model-conditional`; honest winners are `model-robust`; the stage
      spends at most 10% of campaign compute; a build-packet request on a `model-conditional` result is
      refused (557).
- [ ] A stage with no control available reports `not-run` and the finalists are described as survivors.
- [ ] A campaign cancelled in stage 4 and resumed gives the same finalists and digests as an uninterrupted one.
- [ ] A campaign in which every design fails ends in stage 1 or 3 with "every admitted alternative fails", exit
      2, and a complete report.
- [ ] NS-06 is demonstrated and recorded: thirty design-check-simulate-explain-edit cycles on a wheel-leg
      hybrid in one hour on the reference GPU host, each cycle dispatching a family of at least ten variants in
      one batch (at least three hundred designs decided), every version content-addressed, every comparison a
      declared study, and `design bisect` finding the edit that broke stair climbing; the phase ledger of the
      hour is attached.
- [ ] `report.campaign` is byte-identical on regeneration and resolves every number through the artifact graph.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_cascade.sh` (bead N28a-32) runs the seeded population through `frob opt cascade` on
`batch-cpu` and, where present, a device; then `frob report campaign <id>`; then the gate through the
conformance runner. Logged per stage: designs in and out, typed exclusions, modes per stratum, control name
and outcome, spend against the stage budget, fallbacks fired; then the finalists with their types and
receipts, the report's digest on two regenerations, the archive-merge digests, and the gate row.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-697 (86,185 and 500,500), KA-692 (the pyramidal-cone finalist is `model-conditional`), KA-706 (the audit
bounds), KA-698 (the ballast digest: 735,471 placements give 24,546 classes, and equal first moment with
different second moment must split). Negative controls: the same campaign with stage 5 skipped reports the
exploit design as the winner (must be detected by the gate); deduplication under the mechanical group; a
sabotaged screen; a planted unsound prune; a cancelled audit episode. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine;
resumed against uninterrupted; across worker and shard counts; under schedule fuzz (digests and verdicts
identical).

## Considerations

- Stage 1 is cheap and its factor is modest: it is worth having and is not a large factor once sensors break
  the symmetry. Exact reuse in stage 2 is bounded by about a tenth of compute after a dynamics-affecting edit.
  The cascade's benefit, if any, comes from stages 3 and 4 training few designs, which depends on measured
  rank agreement.
- RISK-37 (a screen discards the winner) is what the three audits exist for; GATE-ATEL-09 (owned by the audit
  bead) requires that the studio does not trust a rung whose audit is missing or failed.
- Kill gates that reshape the cascade without removing it: KILL-STATIC (stage 1 stops pruning on static
  findings), KILL-RANK (R1 `uninformative` everywhere), KILL-LADDER (fixed table), KILL-VOI (fixed order among
  finalists), KILL-REUSE (dependency grades only), KILL-GAIT (no orbit screening), KILL-SOLVE (the one-verb
  form is a convenience). The report's fallback list names each that fired.
- Compute: a design campaign for this gate exceeds the default envelope (class E2 per campaign); if not
  approved, the campaign is sized to the default with fewer designs and wider intervals, and says so.
- The scenario's unit is designs decided per hour; the cycle count alone is worth only about 3.0 to 3.5 times
  against an incumbent quadruped loop.
