## Background

Atelier is the design studio: the loop in which a design is checked, given a controller, simulated, explained,
edited and compared, together with every rule that decides how evaluation effort is spent inside that loop. It is
two crates, both at layer 4 of the workspace (a crate depends only on lower layers, or on an earlier row of the
same layer; the order inside layer 4 is `frob-spec`, `frob-control`, `frob-gait`, `frob-analyze`, `frob-learn`,
`frob-arena`, `frob-opt`):

- `frob-analyze` (tier Solid; scope cap 20 thousand lines including tests) holds the static analyses A-01 to A-26
  and the preflight stages 2 to 8 behind `design.check`. Each analysis returns a typed finding with ranked repairs.
- `frob-opt` (Solid and Frontier; cap 14 thousand lines) holds paired comparison, lineage queries, optimization,
  the evaluation ladder and its allocation rules, the co-design cascade, revalidation, experiment suggestion,
  registered predictions, reports, and the compiler for the decision-core tables.

This bead creates the two crates as dependency targets (a crate becomes one only after its contract lands), fixes
in writing what the studio owns and what it only uses, enforces the rule that keeps decisions out of the physics,
and files the eleven architecture decisions of the studio as records with the test that enforces each. It exists
because the studio touches almost every other subsystem, and without a written boundary the same mechanism gets
built twice (comparison in the task crate and in the studio; revalidation in the mission crate and in the studio).

## Scope

In: `crates/frob-analyze` and `crates/frob-opt` with manifests, layer and rank metadata, empty module trees,
`CONTRACT.md` (ten sections) and `contract.toml`, `tests/conformance.rs` skeletons; the ownership table as a
checked document; the two-planes check; `docs/adr/ADR-ATEL-01.md` … `-11.md`. Out: every mechanism (the analysis
contract is N28a-04; comparison is BD-124, BD-262 and N28a-18 to N28a-21; lineage BD-113; the rest in their beads).

## Technical approach

**Module layout (DESIGN).** `frob-analyze/src/`: `finding.rs`, `bound.rs`, `repair.rs`, `registry.rs`,
`preflight/` (stage runner), one module per analysis `a01_mobility.rs` … `a26_atlas.rs`. `frob-opt/src/`:
`compare/` (pairing, anytime, fixed_n, multi), `lineage/` (graph, diff, blame, bisect, merge), `ladder/`,
`cascade/`, `revalidate/`, `suggest/`, `predict/`, `report/`, `cores/` (the table compiler). Frontier modules are
behind `frontier-*` features, off by default (workspace rule: no default build enables a Frontier mechanism).

**`CONTRACT.md`, ten sections each:** purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants, refusals, determinism class, evidence
class of outputs, validity domain, oracles, known gaps. Both `contract.toml` files declare `plane = "decision"`
(layer 4 decides what to run; layers 1 to 3 are the compute plane, N34-08). For `frob-analyze`: determinism D0
(exact) for the exact analyses; outputs carry evidence class and model identity; refusals are the registered
design codes its preflight stages 2 to 8 and its analyses raise, as the design refusal rows name them (the block
FROB-REF-101 to -133 has no rows 117 and 127 to 129, so it is never written as a range), plus 551. For
`frob-opt`: statistics are pure functions of per-address outcomes in address order; refusals are the studio's
codes it raises (N28-REF): 542, 543, 545, 546, 548, 549, 550, 553, 555, 556, 557, 870 and 871 (544, 547, 552 and
554 belong to other crates, and 872 is raised by `frob_verbs::require_establishable`); no verb of the crate
reads a confirmation world of the blinded qualification lane.

**The ownership table, as a checked document** (`docs/atelier/OWNERSHIP.md`, generated into both contracts):

| Atelier owns | Atelier uses and does not own |
|---|---|
| The order of the studio loop and the right of each stage to refuse the next | Design terms, typed edits and the model compiler |
| Analyses A-01 to A-26 and the aggregate verb `design.check` | The three physics tiers, the trust ladder and effort profiles |
| Paired comparison in two declared study types | Family batches and campaign deduplication |
| Lineage queries: diff, blame, bisect, merge | Baseline controllers, gait orbits and skills; family policies and inheritance |
| The ladder of evaluation rungs, its allocation rule and the rank-agreement policy | Tasks, requirements, batteries and the blinded qualification lane |
| The six-stage co-design cascade and its screening audits | Mission queries and family certificates |
| Revalidation of verdicts after an edit (`result.revalidate`) | Adversarial worlds and rare-failure estimation; interventional attribution |
| The memo policy: what may be served from the memo graph, and when | Every ledger and the memo store, which are tables of the record-keeping subsystem |
| Experiment suggestion, registered predictions, reports | Calibration against reality; the daemon and its queue |
| The decision-core contract and the eleven decision rules DC-01 to DC-11 | The mechanisms those rules govern, each in its own crate |

Rule for shared mechanisms: where a decision core governs a mechanism another crate owns, the studio fixes the
decision rule (the table, its loss model, its obligations, its fallback) and the owning crate fixes the mechanism.
The check: every verb in the registry names one owner crate; a verb listed under two owners fails
`check-contracts`.

**The two planes (ADR-ATEL-10; workspace rule L-11).** Kernels, step plans and physics never depend on the
dispatcher, the memo policy or any estimator. The decision plane reads results and writes requests; it cannot
change what a world computes. Enforcement: `xtask check-layers` already refuses an upward edge; this bead adds a
named assertion that no crate of layers 1 to 3 (`frob-lie`, `frob-algebra`, `frob-kir`, `frob-actuate`,
`frob-design`, `frob-model`, `frob-io`, `frob-scene`, `frob-kernels`, `frob-contact`, `frob-dyn`, `frob-phylum`,
`frob-sense`) depends on `frob-opt`, `frob-analyze`, `frob-arena` or the daemon, and a source lint that no such
crate reads a wall clock (`Instant::now`, `SystemTime`) outside its timing module.

**The eleven decision records.** Each file states the decision, the reason, the alternatives rejected, and the
test or check that enforces it.

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-ATEL-01 | The order of the loop is a rule: check before you simulate, simulate before you train, train before you claim | Each stage is cheaper than the next by roughly an order of magnitude (HYPOTHESIS; settled by the phase ledger) and each can refuse the next | N28a-02 |
| ADR-ATEL-02 | Two study types, never mixed inside one study: an anytime-valid e-process study, or a fixed-n exact study with a pre-analysis of reachable verdicts | Time-uniform inference and a sealed fixed-n rule compute different decisions; data collected under one is not re-read under the other | N28a-18, BD-124 |
| ADR-ATEL-03 | Successive halving is the fixed baseline and the fallback of the ladder; every adaptive rule ships in shadow behind it and reverts on a named trigger | An adaptive rule can fail in a way that costs validity or the best design; the fixed table can only cost compute. One asymmetry governs every rule: reporting an artifact-dependent result as established, or eliminating the best design, costs far more than a wasted higher-rung episode | BD-329 |
| ADR-ATEL-04 | A cheap rung discards; it does not select, except in strata where rank agreement has been measured | No published measurement of rank agreement between a shared policy and trained specialists exists, and the nearest neighbours are weak | BD-329, BD-310 |
| ADR-ATEL-05 | A hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck: reuse of a verdict needs a dependency argument or an implication argument, recorded and independently checkable | A content hash says an input or result is the same object; it says nothing about whether a verdict survives a different input | BD-292, BD-347 |
| ADR-ATEL-06 | Decision rules are compiled to tables (integer guard table, automaton or monitor, versioned, under `registries/cores/`, regenerated in CI and diffed); no decision reads the wall clock, device load or arrival order | Replay, audit, and a rollback that is a configuration flag | BD-349 |
| ADR-ATEL-07 | Parametric models rank experiments; distribution-free tests issue verdicts | A wrong value-of-information model then costs efficiency and never validity | BD-351 |
| ADR-ATEL-08 | Agents are the discrete optimizer (topology, leg count, actuator families); the studio supplies fast continuous optimization, population evaluation and honest comparison underneath | Division of labour that matches what each side is good at | BD-352 |
| ADR-ATEL-09 | The controller gate: no adaptive controller is enabled by default without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback; adoption is shadow (report both rules, act on the old), then canary, then default | The incumbent in each shadow run is the system's own reference path | BD-349 |
| ADR-ATEL-10 | Two planes (above) | Physics must not depend on scheduling or estimation | This bead |
| ADR-ATEL-11 | Decision cores are named DC-01 to DC-11 | The letter D already names determinism classes D0 to D4 and doctrine rules; "D2" would be ambiguous exactly where both appear | A registry lint: no identifier matching `^D[0-9]+$` names a core |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both crates build empty, declare `layer = 4` and their ranks, and pass `xtask check-layers` and
      `check-contracts`; each `CONTRACT.md` has the ten sections and each `contract.toml` validates.
- [ ] `docs/atelier/OWNERSHIP.md` exists and the same table is embedded in both contracts by generation
      (`check-generated` passes).
- [ ] The two-planes assertion fails on a planted edge (a dev-branch `frob-dyn → frob-opt` dependency) and on a
      planted `Instant::now()` in `frob-kernels`.
- [ ] Eleven ADR files exist, each with decision, reason, rejected alternatives and an "enforced by" line naming a
      test or check that exists or the bead that will add it.
- [ ] The core-naming lint rejects a core record named `D2`.
- [ ] No Frontier feature is enabled by `cargo build -p frob-opt` with default features.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-analyze/tests/contract.rs` and `crates/frob-opt/tests/contract.rs` (layer, sections, features)
and `xtask/tests/atelier_boundary.rs` (planes, wall clock, ADR files, core names, owners); DESIGN paths:
- `layer_metadata_present` (both crates): manifest metadata equals layer 4 and the declared rank.
- `two_planes_no_upward_edge`: parses `cargo metadata`; asserts the thirteen lower crates have no path to
  `frob-opt`, `frob-analyze`, `frob-arena`.
- `no_wall_clock_in_lower_layers`: source scan with an allow-list of timing modules.
- `contract_sections_ten`: each `CONTRACT.md` has exactly the ten headings in order.
- `adr_files_complete`: eleven files; each has the four parts.
- `core_name_lint`: `D2` rejected; `DC-02` accepted.
- `verb_has_one_owner`: a verb registered under `frob-opt` and `frob-arena` fails `check-contracts`.
- `default_features_have_no_frontier`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_contracts.sh`: runs `cargo xtask check-layers`, `check-contracts`, `check-generated` and the
two planted-defect variants (in a scratch copy of the manifests), and asserts pass, fail, fail. It sources the
STD-E2E helper and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/atelier_contracts/<utc>.ndjson`; every line carries `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step` (`clean`, `planted-edge`, `planted-clock`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code,
wall time, and for the planted cases the offending edge or file and line; on failure the first unexpected exit
code and the scratch directory (kept on failure); a final `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls are the planted upward edge, the planted wall-clock read, a contract with nine sections, an ADR
without an "enforced by" line, and a verb registered under two owners. No numeric known answer applies.

## Considerations

- The intra-layer rank matters: if rows inside a layer were unordered, `frob-arena` could depend on `frob-opt`
  for comparison while `frob-opt` depends on `frob-arena` for batteries, and Cargo would reject the cycle only
  after both were written. The study types live in `frob-arena`; the studio's comparison layer sits above them.
- A scope cap is a trigger for a conversation at 115%, not an estimate; `frob-analyze` holds 26 analyses in 20
  thousand lines, so analyses whose mathematics another crate owns (clearance, observability, structure, gait
  orbits, wrench attainability) are thin adapters here.
- The ADR "enforced by" column points at beads that land later; the file is updated when each lands, and the M1
  exit review reads the table to see which decisions are still unenforced.
