## Background

The project's central claim is about time: an agent using this system reaches an accepted design answer in a
tenth of the restricted mean time of the strongest composed rival. That claim is measured from a phase ledger: a
record, for every run of every arm, of where the clock went. The studio's loop (check, synthesize, simulate,
explain, edit, compare, train, promote) is "measured, not assumed": every verb records its phase and its cost in
the ledger that the ten-fold test reads.

One rule of arithmetic follows and binds every speed statement about a stage: **a phase that accounts for less
than nine tenths of elapsed time cannot deliver a ten-fold improvement alone, even if its cost falls to zero.**
(If a share f of the time is removed entirely, the speed-up is 1/(1 − f), which reaches 10 only at f = 0.9.) So
the ledger is read before any speed claim is made about a stage. This bead makes the verbs emit the phase
intervals and adds the check that turns the arithmetic into a refusal of unsupported claims.

Rejected alternatives: instrumenting only the benchmark harness (the product's own sessions would then have no
ledger, and the cascade's campaign report could not print one); wall-clock sampling by an external profiler
(cannot attribute an agent's blocked time to the job it waits for).

## Scope

In: the `phase` field of every verb's registry row, emission of phase intervals with resource costs by the verb
executor, the mapping from the studio's seven phases to the ledger's thirteen, the nine-tenths check as a
library function used by reports. Out: the ledger tables, their closure rule and the ratio arithmetic (the
ten-fold harness and `frob-chronicle`); the incumbent's ledger (BD-143); the ceiling-before-speed check on
registered speed comparisons (BD-354), which calls the function defined here.

## Technical approach

1. **Studio phases.** Each verb declares exactly one of: `authoring`, `check`, `synthesis`, `simulation`,
   `training`, `promotion`, `review`. Registry validation refuses a verb with no phase.
2. **Mapping to the ledger's phases** (the ledger has thirteen: `author`, `propose`, `check`, `synthesize`,
   `compile`, `screen`, `train`, `evaluate`, `qualify`, `certify`, `diagnose`, `wait`, `adjudicate`):

   | Studio phase | Ledger phase | Verbs (examples) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `authoring` | `author` | `design.new`, `design.edit`, `design.patch`, `task.new` |
   | `check` | `check` | `design.check`, `analyze.<name>`, `explain.design` |
   | `synthesis` | `synthesize` | `control.synth` |
   | `simulation` | `screen` | `sim.run`, `eval.run` on a cheap rung |
   | `training` | `train` | `train.start` and its descendants |
   | `promotion` | `evaluate` | `eval.compare`, `eval.promote`, `study.submit` |
   | `review` | `propose` for the agent's own turn; `diagnose` for `explain.run`, `explain.compare`, `telemetry.*`, lineage queries | — |

   Compilation and cache lookup inside any verb are charged to `compile`, queueing to `wait`; the executor
   splits a verb's wall time accordingly, so one verb call can emit several intervals.
3. **The interval record (DESIGN).** `{job_or_call_id, session, agent, verb, phase, ledger_phase, t_start,
   t_end, resource: {cpu_core_s, gpu_s, device}, tokens_in, tokens_out (agent turns only), model_id, campaign}`,
   times as integer nanoseconds from the daemon's monotonic clock. Written through the evidence sink trait of
   `frob-core`, so no lower crate depends on the ledger. The record is telemetry about the run, not an input to
   any decision (decision cores never read a clock; this is the only place the executor does).
4. **Derived tables** are the ledger owner's: the exclusive table assigns every instant to one phase (an agent
   turn in progress owns its instants; while the agent is blocked on jobs, the job that finishes last among
   those it waits for owns them), and the resource table sums device-seconds and core-seconds per phase. This
   bead supplies the intervals those tables are built from and a test that the intervals of a scripted session
   close to within 2% of the session's clock.
5. **The nine-tenths function.** `phase_ceiling(share f, overhead h) = 1/(1 − f + h)`: the largest speed-up
   obtainable by removing a phase of share f entirely. `claim_supported(phase, claimed_factor, ledger)` returns
   false when `claimed_factor > phase_ceiling(f, 0)`. Reports and the campaign report call it; a speed statement
   about a stage whose ledger share does not support it is printed with the ceiling beside it and the label
   `ceiling-bound`.
6. **Envelope.** Every verb's `cost` block gains `phase` and, for long jobs, the per-phase split.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every verb in the registry at M1 has a phase; registry validation fails on a verb without one.
- [ ] A scripted session (edit, check, synth, sim, explain, edit, compare) produces intervals whose exclusive sum
      is within 2% of the session clock, with the remainder reported as `unattributed`.
- [ ] `phase_ceiling(0.5, 0) = 2`, `phase_ceiling(0.9, 0) = 10`, `phase_ceiling(0.96, 0) = 25`; a claim of 10
      against a phase of share 0.5 is reported `ceiling-bound`.
- [ ] Compile time inside `sim.run` is charged to `compile`, not `screen`; queue time to `wait`.
- [ ] Intervals survive a daemon restart (written through the ledger's crash-consistent path) and are visible
      through `evidence.trace`.
- [ ] No crate of layers 1 to 3 gains a dependency by this change.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `every_verb_has_phase` (registry).
- `mapping_total`: each studio phase maps to a ledger phase; `review` splits by verb.
- `interval_split_compile_wait`: a synthetic verb with 100 ms queue, 300 ms compile, 600 ms run emits three
  intervals with those durations (monotonic test clock).
- `phase_ceiling_values`: 2, 10, 25, and with overhead h = 0.05 at f = 0.9: 1/(0.15) = 6.67.
- `claim_supported_false_below_ceiling`.
- `intervals_close_within_two_percent` on a recorded synthetic session.

### End-to-end script and logging

Part of `scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (bead N28a-29): after its session it calls
`frob evidence trace --session <id> --phases --json` and asserts that phases `author`, `check`, `synthesize`,
`screen`, `diagnose` are present, that the exclusive sum closes within 2%, and logs per phase the seconds, the
share, and `phase_ceiling(share)`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the ceiling arithmetic above. Negative controls: a verb with its phase removed from the registry
(validation fails); a mutant executor that charges compile time to the run phase (the split test fails); a
session with a 5% gap between intervals (reported `ledger-open`, not silently closed).

## Considerations

- Timing records are the one sanctioned wall-clock read in the verb executor; they never feed a decision table,
  a memo key or an estimator, and a lint in N28a-01 checks that lower layers hold none.
- The share that matters for a ten-fold statement is the share in the rival's loop as well as ours; the mapping
  table is therefore the same one the incumbent harness uses when it tags the rival's processes.
- Published incumbent loops put training near 96% of an illustrative campaign where every candidate is trained;
  information features that do not remove training cap near 1.04 times (FACT, source register). The function
  here is what prints that cap beside any stage-level claim.
- Privacy: interval records carry identities and costs, never scene content.
