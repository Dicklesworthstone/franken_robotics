## Background

Every static analysis of the studio is reachable two ways: inside the aggregate `design.check`, which runs the
analyses marked for it and stops at the first refusal, and as its own verb, because some analyses cost more or
need a choice from the caller (a gait atlas, modes, full-range workspace, sensitivity). The verb registry holds
one cluster `analyze` with one verb per analysis A-01 to A-26, kind `read`, first milestones M1 to M4 as the
analyses land. A-19 (rank robustness) and A-26 (regime atlas) read recorded runs and never join `check`.

The registry (`registries/verbs.toml`) is the single source from which the CLI, the daemon protocol, the MCP tool
definitions, the schemas, the refusal table and the agent documentation are generated, so surfaces cannot drift.
This bead makes the `analyze` cluster a generated artifact of the analysis registry of `frob-analyze`: adding an
analysis adds its verb, with its schema and refusal list. The twenty-six rows are first entered in the registry
as data by the cluster's registry bead (VERBS-analyze), at M1 and before any analysis exists, so that the
surfaces and the agent guide are stable from the start; from the moment this bead lands those rows are held
equal to the generator's output by `check-generated`, so the registry cannot drift from the implementations
and a new analysis reaches the registry by running the generator. Rejected alternative: hand-written verb rows
per analysis with nothing tying them to the code (26 rows that drift from 26 implementations).

## Scope

In: the generator from `Analysis` registrations to the twenty-six verb rows, and the check that its output
equals the rows on file; the handler binding of every analysis verb; the common shape of the input and output
schemas and the per-verb schema files generated from it; the names (which this bead proposes and the registry
adopts); exit codes; the behaviour of a verb whose analysis is not implemented or whose binding is missing; the
memo wrapping.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the twenty-six rows as registry data of record in `registries/verbs.toml`, with their machine fields
  (capabilities, schema names, chapter refusals, task clusters, examples, owner, purpose), the cluster's
  surface checks (parity of the CLI and MCP against the rows, the end-to-end coverage lint, the help-schema
  snapshots) and the script `scripts/e2e/verbs_analyze.sh`: VERBS-analyze, on which this bead depends. The rows
  are entered there once, at M1, before any analysis exists. This bead writes no row by hand: from the moment
  it lands, the `analyze` block of the registry is under `check-generated`, the generator derives the same
  rows from the `Analysis` registrations, and the block on file must equal its output byte for byte;
- the count of the family in the whole registry (247 verbs once complete; cluster `analyze` holds these 26
  and, from M3, the three late rows `analyze.sweep-monotone`, `analyze.constraint-graph` and
  `analyze.regressor-identity`, which are not numbered analyses and are registry data of record of NJLx-51)
  and the reconciliation of registry rows against the crates' contracts: NJL-03 and NJLx-51; the task clusters
  the verbs sit in: NJL-05;
- the analyses; `design.check` (BD-110); MCP tool clusters (BD-138 and its successors).

   computation); A-19 and A-26 read runs. `S / F` is a Solid core with Frontier extensions: for A-03 the
   symmetry-event findings of N25d-02 (M6), for A-05 the certified inverse kinematics of N10ax-01 (M4).

   The milestone column is the milestone at which the analysis lands. The registry row's `first_milestone` is
   the verb appendix's value, which gives the family M1 to M4: it equals the column for every analysis except
   A-15, whose row reads M4 (entered with `foundry.size`) while the structure analysis lands at M5; until then
   `analyze.structure` answers the not-implemented refusal. The generator therefore takes `first_milestone`
   from the analysis's declared registry milestone, a field of the registration distinct from the milestone at
   which its executor exists, and the milestone histogram of the registry (NJL-03) is unchanged by it.

   Where another chapter registers a verb for the same mathematics under its own cluster (`sense.observability`,
   `contact.clearance`, `gait.atlas`, `foundry.size`, `skill.check`, `ground.excite`), the `analyze` verb is the
   studio's finding-shaped view of the same computation and calls it; it adds no second implementation.
2. **Registry row, generated and equal to the row on file.** The generator emits, per registered `Analysis`,
   one `[[verb]]` entry with every field of the registry's entry schema: `name`, `cluster = "analyze"`,
   `kind = "read"`, `tier` from the analysis (an analysis printed `S / F` is registered `tier = "S"`, because the
   field takes one of S, F, M; its Frontier extensions are listed by `frob capabilities` when their handlers are
   in the build), `capabilities = ["analyze:read"]`, `input_schema = "frob.analyze.<name>.in/1"`,
   `output_schema = "frob.analyze.<name>.out/1"`, `refusals` = the analysis's declared list plus FROB-REF-551,
   `idempotent = true`, `cost_model` from the analysis's declared budget, `first_milestone`, `task_clusters`,
   `examples`, `owner = "frob-analyze"` and `purpose`, and `estimable = true` for an analysis whose
   registration declares an estimate (A-26 only: `--estimate` prices the paired perturbation runs of its local
   models without running them; the field then adds `estimate_only` by NJL-01's rule). Its output
   for the twenty-six analyses must equal the rows of the numbered analyses in the `analyze` block of
   `registries/verbs.toml` that VERBS-analyze entered; a difference in either direction fails `check-generated`
   and names the row and the field. A row of cluster `analyze` that no `Analysis` registration produces fails
   the check unless an `[[addition]]` record of the registry lists it as a row of record (NJLx-51's three late
   rows from M3, whose handlers are ordinary verbs of `frob-analyze`, `frob-mission` and `frob-synth`); DESIGN, so that a
   later non-numbered `analyze` verb is a recorded addition and never a silent hand-written row. A verb
   whose analysis is not yet implemented is registered with its milestone and answers exit 5 (capability not
   implemented, `FROB-REF-724`) with the milestone in the message; it is never absent from the listing.
   Four fields were drafted differently here and in the registry bead; each now has one value (DESIGN, with
   the reason, stated in both beads):
   - `capabilities`. This bead had `["design:read"]`, plus `run:read` for A-19 and A-26; the registry bead
     has `["analyze:read"]`. The scope catalogue has one read scope per cluster, which grants that cluster's
     `read` verbs, and the entry form of the verb appendix follows it (`design.check` needs `design:read`).
     The analysis verbs are the read verbs of the cluster `analyze`, so they need `analyze:read` and nothing
     else; what a session may see of another namespace or of a private scene is decided by the namespace and
     private-scope rules, not by a second cluster's scope on the row.
   - `output_schema`. This bead had one name for all, `frob.analyze.out/1`; the registry bead has one per verb.
     Per verb, so that the help-schema snapshot and the breaking-change classification are per verb. The
     shape is still common: each `frob.analyze.<name>.out/1` is generated from one template, the
     `AnalysisResult` of item 4 with `output` specialized to the analysis's typed output.
   - `refusals`. FROB-REF-551 (an exclusion from an analysis whose model does not enclose a declared state or
     energy source) is in every analysis verb's list, as here: any analysis that excludes can raise it. The
     registry bead had it on `analyze.energy-budget` only and now carries it cluster-wide.
   - `phase`. An earlier draft of this item listed `phase = "check"` as a row field; the entry schema has no
     such field. The phase is the `cost.phase` of the output envelope (item 4).
3. **Input.** `design` (a name or `DesignId`; resolved to immutable identities echoed in `bound`), analysis
   parameters, optional `task`, `scene`, `budget`, `skill`, `run` bindings, `--time-budget`. An analysis marked
   "when bound" is skipped by `design.check` when its binding is absent (recorded as not run, never a refusal of
   the design); called as its own verb without the binding, it returns exit 3 with the missing binding named as
   the first repair.
4. **Output.** The common envelope with `result = AnalysisResult` (`output`, `findings`, `bound`, `authority`,
   `screening`, `status`), `evidence` (class, tier `static`, model id, determinism `exact` for D0 analyses,
   `claims: []`, `reality: no-claim`), `cost` with `phase: check`, and `next_actions` = the ranked repairs as
   commands. Exit 0 with nothing to flag, 2 with findings, 3 refused (bad input, missing binding, 551, or a
   code the analysis raises itself as a refusal of the request: 215 on `analyze.symmetry` and
   `analyze.workspace`, 308 on `analyze.route`, 263 and 113 on `analyze.sensitivity`, 440 on
   `analyze.deadlines`, 167 and 169 on `analyze.structure`, 431 on `analyze.gait`, and the stratum refusal
   `connection-outside-stratum` on `analyze.curvature`, as VERBS-analyze lists them), 5 not implemented. A verb never refuses because of a finding: refusing on findings is
   `design.check` policy, and the other codes on a row are the codes its findings carry there as
   `Refusing(code)`.
5. **Listing.** An agent sees what exists at this build through the capability report: `frob capabilities
   --json` lists the twenty-six verbs of the cluster with kind, milestone and implementation status, and
   `frob robot-docs --json` prints their purposes, both from the registry. DESIGN: an earlier draft of this
   item added a CLI form `frob analyze --list --json`. That form would be a command on the CLI with no
   registry row and no MCP tool, which the cluster's parity check (VERBS-analyze: registry, CLI and MCP each
   count 26 and every difference list is empty) and the surface invariant forbid, and a second spelling for
   what `capabilities` already reports; it is not built.
6. **Memo.** The executor is wrapped by the memo graph; a repeated call with unchanged read fields is a hit and
   says so in `cost` (`memo: hit`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The `analyze` cluster is generated: adding a toy analysis in a test build adds exactly one verb row, one
      input schema, one output schema and one CLI subcommand; `check-generated` passes.
- [ ] The generator's output for the twenty-six analyses equals the rows of the numbered analyses in the
      `analyze` block of `registries/verbs.toml` entered by VERBS-analyze, field for field
      (`capabilities = ["analyze:read"]`, per-verb output schema names, FROB-REF-551 in every refusal list,
      `first_milestone` M4 for `analyze.structure`); a hand edit of one row fails `check-generated`, and so does
      an extra row of the cluster that no `[[addition]]` record lists.
- [ ] All 26 verbs are listed at M1 by `frob capabilities --json`; those not implemented answer exit 5 with
      their milestone. No CLI command of the cluster exists without a registry row.
- [ ] For a toy analysis registered in a test build, and for every analysis registered when this bead lands,
      `frob analyze <name> <design> --json` returns a schema-valid envelope with findings and ranked repairs.
      The analyses of M1 (A-01, A-02, A-03, A-05, A-06, A-07, A-08, A-10, A-11, A-12, A-13, A-17 structural
      rank, A-20 to A-23) are built by beads that depend on this one (A-13's BD-104 among them), so their verbs
      are exercised by each analysis bead's own tests and, assembled, by N28a-29's script; this bead's
      acceptance does not wait for them.
- [ ] A missing binding returns exit 3 naming the binding; a finding alone never produces exit 3.
- [ ] No verb name contains an underscore; the MCP name map (`.` and `-` to `_`) is one-to-one.
- [ ] A repeated call reports a memo hit and identical result bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (bead N28a-29) reads the cluster's listing from `frob capabilities --json` and
calls each implemented verb on the `quad-mammal` and `mecanum` templates, validating each envelope against its
schema. The cluster's surface conformance (parity, snapshots, the registry example of every verb on both
surfaces) is `scripts/e2e/verbs_analyze.sh` of VERBS-analyze and is not repeated here. Logged per verb on NDJSON
lines with the STD-E2E fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead` = N28a-05, `step` = `analyze_verbs`, `event`): the exact
command line, exit code, wall time against the analysis's budget, the evidence block, the number of findings,
memo status, schema validation result; unimplemented verbs logged with exit 5 and their milestone; on failure the
schema path of the first invalid field and the design path.

### Known answers and negative controls

The verbs carry the known answers of their analyses (registry N28-KA and the owner beads). Negative controls
here are surface-level: a hand-edited verb row that disagrees with the generator (`check-generated` fails); a
second spelling of a verb (registry lint fails); a verb that returns exit 3 on a finding (test fails).
Determinism: byte-identical envelopes on repeat, apart from the `cost` block.

## Considerations

- The surface is large: 26 verbs in one cluster. Agents meet them through `design.check` findings and
  `next_actions`, and MCP exposes them in task clusters of at most seven tools; RISK (an agent surface too large
  to navigate) is handled there, not by hiding analyses.
- Names other than `analyze.symmetry` are DESIGN defaults for the verb-registry freeze at M3; the registry admits
  one name per mechanism, so a rename is a registry change with a deprecation window of zero (no aliases).
- A Frontier analysis verb is behind its `frontier-*` feature and reports exit 5 when the feature is off.
- Frontier analyses and their pilots: A-04, A-17's alias witness, A-18, A-24's witness and A-25 are piloted and
  gated by the subsystems that own their mathematics (control, sensing, scenes, control, calibration); A-15 by
  the structure subsystem; A-26 is piloted in the studio (kill gate KILL-ATLAS). A-09 remains among the first
  items to be cut if its payoff is not measured; its verb row then stays registered and answers exit 5.
