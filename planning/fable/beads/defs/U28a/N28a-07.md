## Background

A-20 answers two questions from a design, a task segment and a declared budget, with no simulation: can the
mission's mandatory work be paid from the declared energy and power budget, and can the body deliver more
energy into a contact than a safety budget allows? The first is a cheap, sound way to exclude designs (a cart
that must lift itself over a sill needs at least m·g·h of work, whatever the controller does); the second is
the static face of the energy-budget safety tier.

The analysis exists in this exact form because of a demonstrated failure. A quasi-static work cut for a passive
sill crossing of an 8 kg body over a 12 mm rise requires 8 × 9.81 × 0.012 = 0.94176 J; against a 0.90 J budget
it excludes the crossing; 0.05 J of approach kinetic energy, absent from the budget, removes the exclusion
(0.95 J ≥ 0.94176 J). The rule that follows binds every static or energy argument in the studio: **an exclusion
encloses every state and every energy source.** A-20 therefore always includes kinetic energy at the start of
the segment, and its result is `outer` (everything it reports infeasible is infeasible) only for the model whose
sources it enclosed. Static rejection is a candidate for a dynamic test until the model it uses contains the
dynamics' resources.

Tier: Solid for the potential-rise bound against energy and power; Frontier for bounds on unavoidable
dissipation and for the contact-energy half bound to a `.fenergy` budget (those are piloted with the
energy-budget tier, not here).

## Scope

In: the mandatory-work lower bound, the available-energy upper bound over every declared source, the power
form, the typed result, the refusal when a source is unbound, the request-parameter form of the contact-energy
cap. Out: the `.fenergy` format, its monitor kernel and its proof kind (BD-337); the energy ledger of a run
(record-keeping subsystem); battery and bus models (BD-075); joining `check` (BD-108).

## Technical approach

Inputs: the model (mass m by A-02, wheel and rotor inertias), the task segment (start height, the mandatory
high point or rise h, path length d, an optional deadline T, the admitted approach-speed interval [v_lo, v_hi]),
the budget (usable stored electrical energy E_stored and peak power P_peak from the design's `budgets` block and
the bus and battery cards; any injected energy E_inj the scene declares; elastic storage E_el), and the list of
energy sources the request declares present. All arithmetic in exact decimals-as-rationals; g is the declared
constant 9.81 m/s² = 981/100.

1. **Enclosure check first.** The analysis encloses: stored electrical, injected electrical, gravitational
   potential, elastic, kinetic energy at the start of the segment (translation and wheel or rotor spin), and
   declared external work. If the request declares a source outside this list, or declares approach motion
   without binding the speed interval, any exclusion is refused with FROB-REF-551 (repairs: bind the missing
   source; accept `unresolved`).
2. **Mandatory work, a lower bound.** W_min = m·g·max(0, h) + D_min, where D_min is the sum of dissipation
   terms that have a proven lower bound for every admissible motion (a declared rolling-resistance floor
   c_rr,min·m·g·d; zero when nothing is declared). Terms without a proof are not added; W_min must never
   overstate.
3. **Available energy, an upper bound.** E_avail = η_max·(E_stored + E_inj) + E_el + T₀, with η_max ≤ 1 the best
   path efficiency the cards allow (1 when unknown, which keeps the bound sound) and
   T₀ = ½·m·v_hi² + Σ ½·J_k·ω_k,hi² the largest kinetic energy at the start of the segment over the admitted
   approach speeds.
4. **Energy verdict.** If W_min > E_avail: an exclusion, typed `outer`, authority not applicable, with the
   finding `A-20.energy` (observed E_avail, required W_min, the shortfall, each source's contribution) and
   repairs ranked by the contract: raise stored energy (`swap-battery`, `set-bus`), lower mass (`set-fill`,
   `set-material`), reduce the rise the body must make (a wheel or geometry edit that lowers h). Otherwise the
   result is `unresolved` with the slack E_avail − W_min: a necessary condition holds; nothing is proved
   feasible. A-20 never returns `inner`.
5. **Power form.** If a deadline T forces the rise within T, the mean mandatory power is at least
   m·g·h/T minus T₀/T; compare with P_peak (and with the bus ceiling E²/(4·R) for a source of open-circuit
   voltage E and series resistance R). Shortfall is `A-20.power`, same typing.
6. **Contact-energy cap (the second question).** The energy a body can deliver into a contact is at most
   E_deliver = ½·m_cap·v² + ∫ max(P_elec, 0) dt + E_stored,mech + E_gravity + E_other + E_reserve over the
   approach (m_cap the capture mass, v the approach speed bound, the integral bounded by P_peak times the
   segment duration). Compared with a cap W_cap given as a request parameter at M1 (`--contact-energy-cap`),
   and with the absorber's minimum work envelope of a bound `.fenergy` from M4. E_deliver > W_cap is the finding
   `A-20.contact-energy` (High): the design can exceed the safety budget. This direction is a sufficient
   condition for a violation being possible, not a proof that one occurs.
7. **In `check`.** "✓ when bound": the analysis joins `design.check` only when the request names a budget or a
   task segment; the refusing code for `A-20.energy` and `A-20.power` there is FROB-REF-115 (a design budget
   exceeded: energy or peak power).
8. **Class.** D0; evidence `Tested`, or `Estimated` when a card field it read is `assumed`. Budget 20 ms.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 8 kg, 12 mm, 0.90 J stored, no approach motion declared: W_min = 0.94176 J, exclusion typed `outer`.
- [ ] The same request declaring approach motion with 0.05 J of kinetic energy bound: E_avail = 0.95 J, no
      exclusion, result `unresolved` with slack 0.00824 J.
- [ ] The same request declaring approach motion and not binding it: refused with FROB-REF-551, first repair
      "bind the approach speed".
- [ ] The cart fixture: raising the front axle of design A by 12 mm takes F₀·h = 39.24 N × 0.012 m = 0.471 J;
      kinetic energy at 0.30 m/s is 0.360 J plus 0.018 J of wheel spin; with no electrical source declared the
      analysis excludes the passive crossing, and with the 24 V bus declared it does not.
- [ ] A-20 never returns `inner` or "feasible" (asserted over 10³ random requests).
- [ ] The contact-energy finding fires on a fixture with E_deliver above the cap and is absent below it.
- [ ] Contract conformance (declared reads, derived key, D0); `frob analyze energy-budget --json` schema-valid.

source's joules, E_avail, the verdict type, the refusal code, the first repair.

## Considerations

- A-20 is a relaxation: it allows any controller and any contact force. That is why its exclusion is sound and
  why its non-exclusion means little. The fixture's own analysis shows momentum, impact and the drive transient
  decide the real outcome; in a planar pilot of 156 design-condition rows, 62 passed a dynamic scorer and failed
  a quasi-static screen (FACT, plan-time pilot), which is the reason the enclosure rule exists.
- The prune-soundness audit of the cascade samples designs this analysis excluded and runs them; one feasible
  excluded design suspends the rule for the campaign and files a negative-control seed.
- KILL-STATIC, if it fires at M3, turns the refusing use of `A-20.energy` into an advisory lint.
