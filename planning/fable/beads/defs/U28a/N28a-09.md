## Background

A-22 answers: is each actuator's current and speed loop stable at the declared control period, feedforward law
and reflected inertia? A digital controller samples, computes and then holds its command for a whole control
period. Between ticks the plant evolves under a constant command. Stability of that sampled loop is a property
of the one-tick map (state at tick k ↦ state at tick k + 1), and it depends on the hold, the period, the
electrical time constant and the inertia the motor sees. A loop that is comfortably stable as a continuous
design can be unstable at 100 Hz on a light wheel.

The demonstrated failure. A 75 mm wheel with total reflected inertia 0.00107890625 kg·m², a rate loop sampled
at 10 ms and a current target held between ticks has one-tick spectral radius 1.4820 with target-current
feedforward and 0.9207 with measured-current feedforward: one law is unstable and the other stable. An
instantaneous-current model of the drive (current equal to its target at once) gives 2.6917 for both with the
fixture's shaft damping (2.7075 without it) and so misses the stable case entirely (FACT, executed pilot;
re-derived here from the fixture). In a planar pilot, changing only that feedforward term changed 24 of 156
task outcomes. A comparison that leaves rotor inertia and controller rate unpinned is a comparison of loop
stability, which is why this analysis runs in `check`.

Rejected: a continuous-time gain or phase margin (silent about the hold); the instantaneous-current model
(above); simulation at a fine step as the only test (it finds the instability after the fact and names no cause).

## Scope

In: the loop model per actuated joint, the exact one-tick map, the spectral radius, the comparison of
feedforward laws, the hook for a tier-integrator radius, findings and repairs. Out: actuator simulation levels
(BD-070, BD-071); the wheel-loop fixture as a physics test (BD-123); the lockstep integrator and its gate (the
dynamics unit, M2); exploitation detectors that flag rate-sensitive results in training (BD-267).

## Technical approach

1. **Loop model per joint (linearized about an operating point).** State x = (ω, i): shaft speed and winding
   current. Between ticks:
   J·ω̇ = k_t·i − b·ω − τ_load,   L·di/dt = v − R·i − k_e·ω,
   with J the total inertia at the shaft (load inertia reflected through the gear ratio N as J_load/N², plus
   rotor and any added inertia), b viscous damping, k_t and k_e the torque and back-EMF constants, R and L the
   winding resistance and inductance. The continuous inner driver applies
   v = FF + k_e·ω + K_i·(i_ref − i), with FF = R·i (measured-current feedforward) or FF = R·i_ref
   (target-current feedforward). The sampled outer loop sets i_ref = K_ω·(ω_ref − ω_k) at tick k and holds it
   for the period h. A constant load torque drops out of the deviation dynamics.
2. **Deviation system.** ẋ = A·x + B·i_ref with
   measured FF: A = [[−b/J, k_t/J], [0, −K_i/L]], B = (0, K_i/L)ᵀ;
   target FF:   A = [[−b/J, k_t/J], [0, −(R + K_i)/L]], B = (0, (R + K_i)/L)ᵀ.
3. **Exact hold.** A_d = e^{A·h}, B_d = (∫₀ʰ e^{A·s} ds)·B, computed together as the exponential of the
   augmented matrix [[A, B], [0, 0]]·h (closed form for this triangular A; scaling-and-squaring with a Padé
   approximant for loops of up to six states, in double precision with a recorded error bound). The one-tick
   map is F = A_d + B_d·K_fb with K_fb = (−K_ω, 0) for this loop; in general K_fb is the row of sampled feedback
   gains, and a compute delay of d ticks augments the state with d held commands.
4. **Spectral radius.** ρ(F) = max |eigenvalue|. `stable` iff ρ < 1; the result also reports the contraction
   per tick and the number of ticks to shrink a deviation by 100, ln(100)/ln(1/ρ).
5. **Law comparison.** Where the controller card admits several feedforward or gain options, the analysis
   evaluates each and lists them, so the repair "change the feedforward law" comes with its radius.
6. **Tier radius (the second evaluation).** The analysis evaluates each loop twice: with the hold modelled
   exactly, and with a named integrator at the task's decimation (number of physics steps per control tick),
   through a small trait `StepModel` that returns the one-tick map of that integrator for the same linear
   loop. It reports both radii and does not label a loop stable on a tier where the second exceeds one. At M1
   the trait has two implementations: exact, and semi-implicit Euler (velocity, then position) with d
   substeps; the lockstep tiers register theirs at M2.
7. **Findings.** `A-22.<joint>.unstable` (High) when ρ_exact ≥ 1; `A-22.<joint>.tier-unstable` (High) when
   ρ_exact < 1 ≤ ρ_tier (the loop is fine and the numerical step is not); `A-22.<joint>.slow` (Low) when the
   contraction is too slow for a declared settling deadline (codes are analysis, subject, kind; `check`'s policy
   rows are `A-22.unstable`, `A-22.tier-unstable` and `A-22.slow`, reported, not refusing, since no code is
   registered: N14bx-01, BD-108). Repairs ranked by the contract, each re-evaluated:
   switch the feedforward law, lower K_ω, shorten the control period (`retime`), add reflected inertia
   (`regear`, `swap-actuator`), raise the decimation for the tier case.
8. **Class.** Double precision on the host, deterministic; evidence `Tested`; values compared "to the printed
   digits". Budget 1 ms per loop.

**The fixture, with every number.** J = 8.7890625 × 10⁻⁴ (the 75 mm wheel's spin inertia) + 2 × 10⁻⁴ (added
rotor inertia) = 0.00107890625 kg·m²; b = 0.002 N·m·s/rad; k_t = 0.2 N·m/A; k_e = 0.2 V·s/rad; R = 1 Ω;
L = 10 mH; K_ω = 2 A·s/rad; K_i = 4 Ω; h = 10 ms. Then F ≈ [[−1.9661, 0.3628], [−1.9865, 0.0067]] with
ρ = 1.4820 (target-current feedforward) and F ≈ [[−1.7947, 0.4485], [−1.9634, 0.0183]] with ρ = 0.9207
(measured-current feedforward). Instantaneous-current model: ω_{k+1} = [e^{−b·h/J} − (1 − e^{−b·h/J})·k_t·K_ω/b]·ω_k,
magnitude 2.6917; without damping |1 − h·k_t·K_ω/J| = 2.7075.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The fixture returns 1.4820 and 0.9207 to the printed digits, labels the first `unstable` and the second
      `stable`, and offers "measured-current feedforward" as the first repair for the first.
- [ ] The held-command slider (state x, w = h·v, command u = −(3/2)·(x + w) held): exact hold
      x′ = x + w + u/2, w′ = w + u has radius 1/2; one semi-implicit step (x′ = x + w + u) has radius
      (1 + √3)/2 ≈ 1.366; two steps of h/2 (x′ = x + w + 3u/4) have (5 + √57)/16 ≈ 0.784; d steps of h/d
      (x′ = x + w + (d + 1)·u/(2d)) have 1/2, 1/4, 0.395, 0.451, 0.476 for d = 3, 4, 8, 16, 32.
- [ ] For the slider at decimation 1 the analysis reports the exact radius 1/2 and the tier radius 1.366 and
      withholds the stability label for that tier (`tier-unstable`).
- [ ] The mutant integrator "explicit Euler at a 0.5 ms step, current updated before wheel speed" gives 1.854
      and 1.497 on the wheel fixture, turning the stable case unstable; the analysis reports it through the
      tier radius.
- [ ] Doubling J or halving h in the fixture brings the target-feedforward loop's radius below one, and the
      corresponding repairs are verified.
- [ ] Contract conformance; `frob analyze loop-stability --json`; each loop of the fixtures within 1 ms. That
      every actuated joint of the first four archetypes is evaluated inside 5 ms total is measured on the
      templates by step 9 of N28a-29 into the GATE-ATEL-05 row (the templates are BD-109, which BD-108, a lower
      seed that depends on this bead, may not wait for).

## Considerations

- An ideal slider or a single wheel has no actuator limit, no contact and no sensor path; a robot's loop needs
  its own evaluation. The analysis linearizes about declared operating points (standing, nominal gait) and
  says which; saturation and contact switching are outside it and are labelled so.
- A step-refinement study must hold every clock fixed in seconds and change only the physics step; a study that
  halves the control period together with the step compares two controllers. This analysis is what tells the
  two apart, and a deadline requirement is compared across decimations by its verdicts, case by case, not by a
  stability label.
- Card fields `assumed` (inductance is often missing) class the result `Estimated`; the sensitivity of ρ to L
  is printed so the agent knows whether the missing field matters.
