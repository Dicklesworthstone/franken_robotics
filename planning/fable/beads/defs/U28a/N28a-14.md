## Background

Analysis A-26, the regime atlas, partitions the recorded operation of a design by contact mode, slip state,
saturation state and derivative validity, and attaches to each region a local model, an error estimate and the
shortest replayable failure. Its promise is a label: "inside this region a gradient is trustworthy". An
optimizer or an agent that follows a design gradient across a contact-mode change is following noise, because
paired worlds decouple under contact (a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degrees is reported to diverge after 50
steps; FACT, source register).

The atlas is Frontier, and its ceiling is stated before any test: it computes nothing new about the robot; its
whole value is fewer wrong gradient steps and fewer misread rankings. So before the mechanism is funded, a
paper-scale pilot must show that the label carries information. This bead is that pilot. Its falsifier decides
the kill gate KILL-ATLAS (decision bead N28a-15): the label predicts no better than "trust nowhere" at a 95%
lower bound; fallback: A-26 is a descriptive report with no validity label.

## Scope

In: an offline partition script sufficient for the pilot, the three measurements per unit, the statistic, the
record. Out: the production analysis (N28a-16); the decision (N28a-15); paired finite-difference design
gradients as a mechanism and their own pilot and kill gate (the family-batch unit, BD-353).

## Technical approach

1. **Units.** The reference quadruped on its flat velocity task and on one terrain task. Twelve design
   parameters (three link lengths, two hip offsets, three masses, gear ratio, motor mass, body length and
   width). For each parameter θ_j and each of 20 world addresses: one unit (j, w); 480 units over the two
   tasks. The unit list, the perturbation sizes (2% of each parameter's range) and the thresholds are
   pre-registered.
2. **Recorded runs.** The nominal design's episodes at the 20 addresses with telemetry that keeps the contact
   mode, per-contact stick or slip, per-joint saturation flags and the event stream, under a D2 profile, from
   the current specialist policy.
3. **Offline partition (pilot script).** Segment each episode into maximal intervals with a constant regime
   key (active contact set; stick or slip per contact; saturation flags; derivative-valid flag). A unit's
   label is `trust` iff, for the metric's decision window in that world, (a) the nominal and both perturbed
   runs traverse the same sequence of regime keys, (b) no interval in the window is derivative-invalid, and
   (c) the local linear model of the metric in θ_j fitted from the two perturbed runs has a residual below the
   registered bound; otherwise `distrust`.
4. **Smoothed gradient estimate.** ĝ(j, w) from the sensitivity analysis (A-16) on the fast tier at the
   nominal policy: the sign of the smoothed estimate of ∂metric/∂θ_j, read through `frob analyze sensitivity
   <design> --mode smoothed-gradient --json` (N33b-18's verb). Which estimator stands behind that mode is
   settled by KILL-FDGRAD (decision N17b-20, a dependency): funded, it is the paired finite-difference design
   gradient of BD-353 (a dependency; it closes `fallback` if the gate fired); fired, the mode returns the
   finite-difference stencil as a report labelled `finite-difference`, and the pilot uses that. The record names
   which branch supplied ĝ.
5. **Specialist-checked finite difference.** For each parameter, the designs θ ± δ_j are given their own
   specialists (fine-tuned from the nominal specialist to matched return; one per perturbed design and task,
   24 designs × 2 tasks = 48 trainings), and the central
   difference of the metric at address w is evaluated on the highest tier available. Its sign is the
   reference. Units whose reference difference is inside the paired noise band are excluded and counted.
6. **Agreement.** a(j, w) = 1 if sign ĝ = sign of the reference, else 0.
7. **Statistic.** acc_label = the share of units with (label = trust and a = 1) or (label = distrust and
   a = 0). acc_never = the share with a = 0, the accuracy of "trust nowhere". Skill s = acc_label − acc_never,
   with a one-sided 95% lower bound from 2,000 seeded bootstrap resamples clustered by world address. Also
   reported: the agreement rate inside `trust` and inside `distrust`, the share labelled `trust`, and the
   excluded units.
8. **Falsifier.** The lower bound of s is at or below 0: the label predicts no better than "trust nowhere".
   A second reading is recorded and does not decide: whether agreement inside `trust` exceeds 0.8.
9. **Record.** One `[[pilot]]` row in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
   (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; there is no private pilot file): `id = "PILOT-ATLAS-01"` (DESIGN, the
   register's mnemonic form), `mechanism` the A-26 validity label, `tier = "F"`, `bead = "N28a-14"`,
   `work_package` the package that funds A-26, `protocol` the frozen pre-registration
   `docs/pilots/PILOT-ATLAS-01.md` with its BLAKE3 digest, `falsifier` as in step 8, `ceiling` (fewer wrong
   gradient steps and fewer misread rankings; nothing new about the robot), `fallback = { ships = "A-26 as a
   descriptive report with no validity label", kill_gate = "KILL-ATLAS" }`, `cost` as below, and an appended
   `[[pilot.result]]` with the unit-list hash, engine and versions, the statistic with its bound, the branch
   that supplied ĝ, and the receipt. The three per-unit values are stored as a dataset beside the protocol and
   cited by digest from the result.
10. **Compute.** 48 specialist fine-tunings (24 designs × 2 tasks) plus the evaluation runs: about 6 to 9
    device-hours (TARGET, scaled from the envelope register's figure of 5 to 7.5 device-hours for forty
    trainings that reuse a specialist). This is above the default class E0 (one device-hour), so the pilot needs
    class E1 for `gate:KILL-ATLAS`. Its row is reserved: N44-01's reservation table holds identifier **R19**
    for this pilot (package WP-11, which carries A-26 and the KILL-ATLAS bullet). Before the first run above E0
    this bead files R19 as a `[[request]]` row of `registries/compute_envelope.toml` (step 6 of N44-01's
    procedure: a bead named in the reservation table files its reserved identifier, never a new one): class E1,
    scope `gate:KILL-ATLAS`, the estimate of 6 to 9 device-hours with the cost breakdown above, and the
    not-approved fallback of the reservation row; in the same commit WP-11's Envelope bullet names R19. The pilot
    is not ready until R19's approval is recorded (the readiness rule of N41-17). If R19 is declined or left
    unanswered, this bead closes `withdrawn` (N41-17, rule 2), its result reading `not run: no envelope` and
    citing R19 and its decision record; no perturbed run exists; KILL-ATLAS is recorded `not run` with that
    reason, never as a pass; and the decision N28a-15 takes its fail-closed branch (A-26 ships as a descriptive
    report with no validity label).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pre-registration (units, perturbations, thresholds, bootstrap seed) is in the ledger before the
      first perturbed run, and the row PILOT-ATLAS-01 is registered with its frozen protocol digest.
- [ ] Reserved row R19 (class E1, scope `gate:KILL-ATLAS`, its estimate and not-approved fallback) is filed in
      `registries/compute_envelope.toml` before the first run above E0, and WP-11's Envelope bullet names it.
- [ ] Every run above E0 cites R19, approved, whose scope admits `gate:KILL-ATLAS`; with R19 declined or
      unanswered, the bead closes `withdrawn` citing R19 and its decision record, the result reads `not run: no
      envelope`, no perturbed run exists, and N28a-15 takes its fail-closed branch.
- [ ] All 480 units have a label; the excluded (inside-noise) units are counted and at most half of the total,
      otherwise the pilot reports `underpowered` and proposes larger perturbations as a new registration.
- [ ] acc_label, acc_never, s and its lower bound are recorded, with the two conditional agreement rates.
- [ ] On a synthetic unit set where the label is constructed to predict agreement with accuracy 0.8 against a
      base agreement rate of 0.5, the pipeline returns s ≈ 0.3 with a positive lower bound; with a random
      label it returns a lower bound at or below 0.
- [ ] The record states the falsifier's outcome and the recommendation, and is produced by a session that will
      not implement the analysis.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`tools/pilots/regime_atlas/tests/`:
- `segments_constant_key`: a scripted event stream with one mode switch at tick 120 yields two intervals with
  the boundary at 120.
- `label_requires_same_regime_sequence`: a perturbed run that lifts a foot one tick earlier flips the label to
  `distrust`.
- `skill_statistic`: 100 units, 60 labelled `trust` with 54 agreeing, 40 `distrust` with 12 agreeing:
  acc_label = (54 + 28)/100 = 0.82; acc_never = 34/100 = 0.34; s = 0.48.
- `bootstrap_clustered_by_world`, `seeded`.
- `synthetic_predictive_label` and `synthetic_random_label` (the fourth acceptance item).
- `noise_band_exclusion_counted`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/regime_atlas_pilot.sh`: runs the pipeline on the synthetic unit set through the real telemetry
readers (`frob telemetry query` on a recorded synthetic run), then prints the registry record when the real
dataset exists. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/regime_atlas_pilot/<utc>.ndjson`; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`
(`synthetic_predictive`, `synthetic_random`, `record`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time;
the measured values are units, excluded units, share labelled `trust`, both agreement rates, acc_label,
acc_never, s with its one-sided 95% lower bound against 0 (and the synthetic expectation s ≈ 0.3), the bootstrap
seed, the branch that supplied ĝ (from `frob analyze sensitivity … --mode smoothed-gradient`), the envelope
request row (R19) and its status, compute spent in device-hours against R19's estimate, and the recommendation; on failure the first differing value and the dataset and record
paths; a final `summary` line naming KILL-ATLAS and the falsifier's outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the one-switch stream partitions at the switch tick (the atlas's registered known answer,
KA-691, in its pilot form); the skill arithmetic above. Negative controls: a random label (must not pass); a
label that marks everything `trust` (its skill equals the agreement rate minus the disagreement rate and is
reported as uninformative when the share labelled `trust` is 1). Determinism: the partition and the statistic
are pure functions of recorded data and seeds.

## Considerations

- The reference (a specialist-checked finite difference) is itself noisy and costs trainings; the exclusion
  band and the clustered bootstrap are there so that noise in the reference is not read as failure of the
  label.
- A positive pilot shows the label is informative on one robot and two tasks; the production analysis still
  ships its label as a screening signal that may discard under the ladder's rules and may not establish a
  comparison.
- If the family-batch gradient mechanism was killed (KILL-FDGRAD), ĝ is the finite-difference stencil report of
  the sensitivity analysis; the pilot still measures whether the label predicts its sign agreement, and the
  record says so.
