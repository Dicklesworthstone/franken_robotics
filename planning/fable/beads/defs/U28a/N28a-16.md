## Background

A-26 answers, from recorded runs: in which contact modes, slip states and saturation states did the design
operate, where is a gradient trustworthy, and where does a ranking change? A legged or wheeled robot's
behaviour is piecewise: a metric responds smoothly to a design parameter while the sequence of contact modes
stays the same, and jumps when a foot lands a tick earlier or a wheel starts to slip. An agent that is shown
one average and one gradient has no way to know which regime produced them. The atlas makes the pieces
visible: a partition of recorded operation by mode, slip, saturation and derivative validity, and per region a
local model, an error estimate and the shortest replayable failure.

A-26 reads runs, so it never joins `design.check`. It is Frontier. Its regions are a screening signal: they may
discard under the evaluation ladder's rules and may not establish a comparison. Its one strong claim, the
validity label, was put to a pilot first; this bead is built in the form the kill-gate decision selected:
with the label if the pilot showed it informative, otherwise as a descriptive report with no validity label.
Its ceiling: the atlas computes nothing new about the robot; its whole value is fewer wrong gradient steps and
fewer misread rankings.

## Scope

In: the regime key, segmentation, regions across worlds, occupancy, the shortest replayable failure, the
ranking-change regions for a comparison, and on the "label kept" branch the local model, its error estimate
and the validity label; the verb `analyze.regimes`. Out: telemetry capture (the record-keeping subsystem); the
sensitivity analysis A-16 (N33b-18); the KILL-ATLAS pilot and decision (N28a-14, N28a-15); the regime-wise comparison explanation and the canonical contact signature
(N28a-17).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** One run or a set of runs of one design (and optionally a comparison), with the telemetry streams
   that carry: contact mode per site, stick or slip per site, saturation flags per joint (torque, speed,
   thermal), the event stream, and derivative-validity marks. A run that lacks a needed stream is reported per
   stream as `not recorded`, with the telemetry level that records it; nothing is inferred.
2. **Regime key.** (sorted active contact sites; stick or slip per active site; the set of saturated joints by
   kind; derivative-valid: true or false). The first three parts are `regime::contact_signature`, the canonical
   function the regime-wise comparison report defines (N28a-17, M3); this analysis adds the derivative-validity
   bit, so its regions refine that report's regimes. Derivative-valid is false in any step that contains a grazing
   event, near-simultaneous untied events, or a change of active set, and inside the declared margin around
   them.
3. **Segmentation.** Per world, maximal intervals of constant key; the boundaries are event ticks. Intervals
   shorter than a declared minimum (default two control ticks) are kept and flagged `transient`, never merged
   silently.
4. **Regions.** Intervals with the same key across worlds form a region; a region may be split by scene region
   where the scene pack declares regions. Per region: number of visits, worlds visited, share of operating
   time, mean dwell, the transitions into and out of it with counts (a regime graph).
5. **Shortest replayable failure.** For each region in which a failure's first-cause event occurred: the
   shortest episode prefix that ends in that failure (world address, tick range), with its replay handle and
   the fork class of restarting there. The region with no failure says so.
6. **Ranking-change regions (with a comparison bound).** Using the paired episodes of the comparison: regions
   in which the sign of the paired difference differs from the overall sign, with counts; these are the
   regimes where the ranking would change if the mission's mix of conditions shifted.
7. **Label-kept branch only.**
   - *Local model.* For a named metric and each design lever, a linear response fitted from paired
     perturbation runs restricted to the visits of the region (the same addresses, the lever moved by its
     declared step up and down), with the residual and the number of visits as its error estimate.
   - *Validity label.* `trust` for a (region, lever) iff the perturbed runs traverse the same regime sequence
     through the region's visits, the region is derivative-valid, and the local model's residual is under the
     registered bound; else `distrust` with the reason (`sequence-changed`, `derivative-invalid`,
     `residual-high`, `too-few-visits`). The label is a screening signal.
8. **Output** `frob.analyze.regimes.out/1`, the schema the verb registry VERBS-analyze names for this verb; on
   the fallback branch the row and the schema keep their names and the fields `validity` and `local_model` are
   absent from the registered schema (the decision N28a-15 selects which form is registered): the regions, the
   regime graph, per-region records, handles; `evidence.class: Estimated`, `screening: true`, `claims: []`.
   Verb `analyze.regimes <run…> [--compare <id>] [--metric M] [--from-study <study>] [--budget N] [--estimate]
   --json` (`--estimate` prints the episode cost of the local models without running them); `--budget N` is a
   token budget on the output, as the explain verbs take, never an episode budget (a `read` verb carries none);
   exit 2 when any region is `distrust` or holds a failure.
   **How a read verb gets its perturbation runs** (label-kept branch; the pattern of N33b-18's Morris mode).
   The verb reads runs and never submits episodes, so the local models take two calls. (a) With `--metric` and
   without `--from-study` it returns the descriptive atlas plus the perturbation plan: per (region, lever) the
   lever's declared step up and down, the world addresses of the region's visits, each perturbed design's typed
   edits and prospective `DesignId` (a content address; nothing is stored), the cost (batches, episodes,
   device-seconds; the same numbers `--estimate` prints), and as `next_actions`, in order: one `frob design
   patch <base> --file <edits-i> --expected-parent <DesignId> --key regimes-<plan>-<i> --json` per perturbed
   design, `frob study plan` on a manifest whose `designs` are those `DesignId`s and the base on the visited
   world set (`fixed_n`, paired by world address), and `frob study submit <plan> --key … --json` (a `run` verb
   with its own budget admission and job handle). Each region's `validity` is `distrust` with reason
   `too-few-visits` until the study is read, never `trust`. (b) With `--from-study <study>` it reads the
   completed study's per-address results, restricts each perturbed run to the base region's visits, fits the
   local models and assigns the labels. A study still running is answered with the job's progress and no
   labels; a missing perturbed run leaves its (region, lever) `distrust`, `too-few-visits`. DESIGN:
   `--from-study` is the spelling N33b-18 uses on `analyze.sensitivity`, and VERBS-analyze's row of
   `analyze.regimes` carries it with this two-call form. On the fallback branch `--from-study` is 802 (pointer
   at the option; there is no local model), as that row states.
9. **Class.** A pure function of the recorded streams (D0 over integers and event ticks); the local models are
   double precision and deterministic.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A run with one planted mode switch is partitioned into two regions with the boundary at the switch tick.
- [ ] (Label kept) A gradient across that switch is labelled `distrust` with reason `sequence-changed`; inside
      either region it is `trust` when the residual bound holds. (Fallback) the output has no validity field.
- [ ] The time shares of the regions of a run sum to 1; every failure of the run is assigned to exactly one
      region, and its handle replays to the same failure.
- [ ] With a comparison bound, the ranking-change regions' pair counts sum to the traced pairs.
- [ ] A missing stream is reported as `not recorded`; no region is fabricated from the remaining streams.
- [ ] The result is byte-identical on repeat and independent of the order in which worlds were stored.
- [ ] The analysis is marked a screening signal in the envelope and the claim product; the comparison layer
      refuses to cite it as the basis of a verdict.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/analyze_regimes.sh`, this bead's own script (convention 11; BD-348 runs it as a stage of the M4
section of N28a-29's `atelier_check.sh`). It sources the STD-E2E helper and records a short referee run of the
quadruped with a scripted foothold that forces one mode switch, from the registered fixture this bead ships
(`assets/fixtures/a26-one-switch/`, N24b-03's format: the `quad-a` design, the foothold scene, the task and the
controller): `frob sim run --fixture a26-one-switch --tier referee --worlds 1 --telemetry T2 --key "$(e2e_key
a26-switch)" --json`. It runs `frob analyze regimes <run> --json`, asserts two regions and the boundary tick,
replays the shortest failure handle when one exists, and asserts the output against the branch KILL-ATLAS
selected (read from `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-ATLAS-M4.md`): label kept, `frob analyze regimes <run> --metric <m>
--json` returns the plan with its `next_actions`, the script runs them in order (each mutate call with a key
from `e2e_key`), waits on the job, then `frob analyze regimes <run> --metric <m> --from-study <study> --json`
labels the gradient across the switch `distrust` with reason `sequence-changed`; fallback (outcome `fired`, or
`not-reached` when the pilot's envelope row R19 was not approved, N28a-15 rule 4), the output validates
against the registered schema and carries no `validity` and no `local_model`, and `--from-study` answers 802.
Then the two planted mutants run through the Gauntlet (`FROB_TEST_SEED=$FROB_E2E_SEED cargo test -p
frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test g1_known_answers -- KA-691`) and must be caught.
Logged on NDJSON lines at `target/e2e-logs/analyze_regimes/<utc>.ndjson` (transcript beside it) with the
STD-E2E fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead` = N28a-16, `step` = `a26`, `event`, the
exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block): regions with keys, shares and visits,
the boundary tick against the planted one, labels and reasons, the schema in force and the KILL-ATLAS branch,
each mutant with `caught`, the plan's cost against the episodes the study ran; on failure the first differing
region or tick and the run's replay handle; a final `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-691: one planted mode switch gives two regions with the boundary at the switch tick. Negative control: a
mutant that reports a gradient as trustworthy across the switch (label-kept branch); a mutant that merges
transient intervals into their neighbours (the boundary moves, and the test catches it). Determinism: identical
bytes across runs, worker counts and platforms for the same recorded run.

## Considerations

- KILL-ATLAS fallback, named in advance: a descriptive report with no validity label. Both branches are
  specified here so the decision changes a schema, not a plan.
- The atlas depends on telemetry that is sampled by address; regions are statements about the sampled worlds,
  and the output states how many worlds were traced of how many ran.
- Per-region local models need paired perturbation runs, which cost episodes; the verb reports that cost before
  running them (`--estimate`), the default call computes the descriptive part only, and the runs themselves
  are submitted by the caller through the `next_actions` of call (a), never by this verb.
