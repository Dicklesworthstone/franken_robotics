## Background

`frob eval compare A B --task T` compares two designs honestly by pairing: both designs meet the same scenes,
the same disturbances and the same randomization draws, episode for episode, so that the difference between
them is not drowned by the difference between worlds. Pairing is worth a great deal (the variance of a paired
difference scales with one minus the correlation between arms) and is easy to get wrong in ways that leave the
statistics looking fine. The rules below are requirements on every paired study; violating one is a refusal,
not a warning. A comparison also declares its study type before it draws an episode (ADR-ATEL-02: an
anytime-valid e-process study, or a fixed-n exact study with a pre-analysis of reachable verdicts; never mixed,
because time-uniform inference and a sealed fixed-n rule compute different decisions, and data collected under
one is not re-read under the other).

1. **Pair by (world, replicate).** A counter that depends on training progress is not part of an address.
   Counterexample: seeds keyed by (world, epoch) on a 64-wide batch pair only morphologies whose indices agree
   modulo 64.
2. **Exogenous fields are indexed by physical coordinate, not by event order.** Two robots on different paths
   must read one space-indexed material field at their own contact points; two tyres on different paths need
   not receive equal friction samples at corresponding ticks, and a comparison that pairs "the k-th friction
   draw" has paired nothing.
3. **Each design is settled separately.** The settled equilibrium is recomputed per design; a design that
   starts in penetration makes the pair `unscored`; a buildable design that stalls is a failed trial, not a
   missing one.
4. **Pairing imposes no minimum number of episodes per design.**
5. **The accounting identity holds:** a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned, each an integer,
   and the verdict states its denominator. An agent cannot optimize failures out of the denominator.
6. **Paired statistics are refused under an uncontrolled determinism class.** Both arms run at class D2
   (bitwise per backend, independent of the batch) or better on one backend, or at D3 (tolerance) with
   decision-level equivalence recorded for the task. A D4 (unspecified) run never enters a comparison.

Why refusals: under class D4 nothing about an arm can be replayed, so its verdict could not be reproduced,
promoted or attributed; a pairing keyed by event order silently produces independent arms and a valid-looking
interval ten times too wide or, worse, a correlated artifact.

## Scope

In: admission checks for the six rules at plan time, the five refusals with repairs, the `PairCounts` type and
its identity enforced at every update, the planted-violation fixtures of the gate. Out: the two study types,
the e-process and the fixed-n rule themselves (BD-124); running paired episodes on devices (BD-262); the
comparison record (N28a-21); the exact pair relation as a mission query (BD-359).

## Technical approach

1. **Admission function.** `pairing::admit(plan: &StudyPlan) -> Result<PairingPlan, Refusal>`, called by
   `eval.compare` and by `study.plan` for any paired study before any episode exists. Checks, in order:
   1. *Study type* (FROB-REF-553): the plan declares exactly one type; a request that names both, or that asks
      to evaluate an e-process on the stored outcomes of a fixed-n study (or a fixed-n interval at the stopping
      time of an anytime study), is refused. Repairs: declare one type; open a new study.
   2. *Reachability* (FROB-REF-555), fixed-n only: the declared verdict set is compared with the reachable set
      of the pre-analysis at the declared n, margin and level; an unreachable declared verdict refuses unless
      the request carries `acknowledge_unreachable`. Repairs: raise n to the first value at which the verdict
      is reachable (computed and reported, for example 86 for a practical tie at a five-point margin), or
      acknowledge.
   3. *Span* (FROB-REF-543), anytime only: each metric must have a bound on its paired difference declared in
      the task before the first observation (a `LossSpan`); it is never estimated from the study's own data.
      Repair: declare the span in the task.
   4. *Address keys* (FROB-REF-556): `seed_protocol.pair_by` must be exactly world and replicate (with the
      cluster unit where episodes share a generator draw, such as one building). Any key that names an epoch,
      an iteration, a step count or another counter that depends on training progress refuses. Every randomized
      exogenous field the task or scene declares must be indexed `space` (by physical coordinate) or `address`
      (one draw per world); a field indexed `event` (by draw order) refuses. Repairs: space-indexed fields;
      (world, replicate) addresses.
   5. *Determinism* (FROB-REF-542): from the backend request and the recorded classes: both arms D2 or better
      on one device family → `paired-exact`; both D3 with a current decision-level equivalence record for the
      task → `paired-by-address`, labelled, with the realized pair correlation to be reported beside the
      verdict; any arm D4, of unrecorded class, or arms in different classes without that record → refused.
      Repair: rerun both arms on a D2 profile.
   6. *Settling* : both designs must have a settled-pose entry for the task; a missing entry is the task
      layer's refusal (529) passed through.
2. **`PairCounts`.** `{a_only, b_only, both, neither, unscored, planned}` as `u32`. Constructed only through
   `PairCounts::update(address, outcome_a, outcome_b)`; after every update and at close the identity
   a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored + pending = planned is asserted, and at close pending = 0.
   Classification of one pair: `unscored` if either arm's initial state was in penetration, if either arm's
   outcome kind is `numerical` (the solver refused or faulted) or `budget`, or if an arm's episode is missing;
   otherwise by the two success bits. A stall is a physical failure and is scored. Unscored pairs are reported
   per arm and per reason, with the worst-case range of the effect if every unscored pair went against the
   candidate.
3. **No minimum.** A plan with any n ≥ 1 is admitted; the pre-analysis or the e-process says what it can
   conclude.
4. **Planted violations (GATE-ATEL-06).** A generator makes 100 plans per refusal, each violating one rule in
   a different way (for 556: (world, epoch), (world, step), event-indexed friction, event-indexed pushes, …);
   every one must refuse with its code.
5. **Record.** The admitted `PairingPlan` (keys, classes, label, spans, the pre-analysis hash) is written to
   the ledger before the first episode; the comparison's evidence cites it. Determinism D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] FROB-REF-553, 555, 542, 543 and 556 each fire on their planted violations, 100 of 100, and none fires on
      100 valid plans of each study type.
- [ ] A 555 refusal reports the first n at which the verdict becomes reachable: for a practical tie at margin
      0.05 with two-sided 97.5% exact intervals, n = 86.
- [ ] The (world, epoch) plan on a 64-wide batch is refused with 556; the test also demonstrates the failure it
      prevents: with those keys only designs whose indices agree modulo 64 share draws.
- [ ] A plan with one arm recorded D4 is refused (542); two D3 arms with an equivalence record are admitted and
      labelled `paired-by-address`; two D2 arms on one family are `paired-exact`.
- [ ] `PairCounts` satisfies the identity after every update in a property test over 10⁴ random outcome
      streams, including numerical faults and penetrating starts; no constructor lets a caller set a count.
- [ ] A design that stalls counts in `a_only`, `b_only` or `neither`, never in `unscored`.
- [ ] The admitted pairing plan is in the ledger before the first episode (ordering asserted).

Known answers: n = 86 for the first reachable tie (KA-693); the modulo-64 pairing failure (CX-311); the
event-order pairing failure (CX-312). Negative controls: the 500 planted violations; a mutant `PairCounts` that
drops numerical faults from `planned` (the identity test fails); a mutant admission that checks only
`pair_by` and not the field index kinds. Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- Overlapping codes: the study layer has its own refusals for a mixed-type request (552), a missing or
  unreachable pre-analysis (532) and an uncontrolled class (533), and the numerics layer has 561 for a D4 arm.
  This layer issues 553, 555 and 542 when the request comes through `eval.compare`. A caller may therefore see
  either code for the same defect, depending on the verb; the refusal registry's owner is asked to mark the
  pairs as equivalent. Fail-closed either way.
- "Can this replacement make a formerly successful delivery fail?" is a question about the pair, and a
  statistical comparison answers it only in distribution. The exact form (reachability of the outcome pair
  (success, failure) in the product of the two closed loops driven by the same exogenous inputs) is a mission
  query, Frontier, owned by the mission subsystem. This bead's pairing discipline is the precondition without
  which neither form means anything.
- RISK-15 (agents optimize the scorecard by moving failures out of the denominator) is what rule 5 exists for.
- Pairing by address shares seeds; it does not make two robots that take different paths meet the same friction
  at the same tick. That is rule 2, and it depends on scenes declaring their fields honestly.
