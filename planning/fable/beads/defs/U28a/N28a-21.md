## Background

A comparison's output is read by agents that will act on it, by later comparisons (bisect), by the evaluation
ladder and by reports. If it is a bare "B wins, p = 0.03", every reader has to guess what was held fixed, how
many episodes were dropped, and whether either design actually meets the mission. This bead defines what a
comparison returns, as one stored record, and the read verb that returns it.

**What a comparison returns.**
- The verdict: `settled`, `tied`, `inconclusive`, `incomparable` or `infeasible`. All five are legitimate
  dispositions; "inconclusive at this budget" is a result, not a failure.
- The statistic of its study type (the e-value and confidence sequence, or the counts and exact interval).
- The pairs used, with the accounting identity a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned and the
  stated denominator.
- The tier it ran on.
- The first divergent event between the arms, when one exists.
- Each design's absolute hard-limit verdict beside the paired verdict, because a relative result does not
  establish that either design meets the mission.
- A rank-robustness field: `model-robust`, `model-conditional` or `unchecked`.
- A verdict from a fast tier is `provisional` until promoted on the referee by the trust ladder's promotion
  rule; a promoted verdict also carries the estimate at referee fidelity from the tier-correction ledger.

**What a comparison does not control.** One comparison is valid under optional stopping. It does nothing about
an agent that submits fifty candidates to the same held-out worlds until one passes: with independent attempts
at 5% each, fourteen null submissions give a 51% chance of at least one false promotion. That is the job of the
blinded qualification lane, with fresh sealed world clusters and the error budget α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] for the
i-th submission. A design comparison that is to be reported as a claim goes through that lane; a comparison on
discovery worlds is a development result, and the record says so in a field no consumer can miss.

## Scope

In: the record schema and its writer, the mapping from each study type's outcome to a disposition, the absolute
verdict block, the labels, the `study.compare` verb, the development-result marking, the firewall check that
the studio's verbs never read a sealed confirmation world (INV-ATEL-11). Out: computing the statistics (BD-262,
N28a-19, N28a-20); promotion (BD-263); rank robustness (BD-346); explanation (N28a-17); qualification (BD-259).

## Technical approach

1. **Record `frob.comparison/1` (DESIGN; canonical JSON in the ledger, content-addressed).**

   ```
   comparison
     study        plan id · study type · rule id and version · level · margin · pre-analysis hash (fixed-n)
     arms         A, B: design id · model id · controller arm · settled-pose id · determinism class
     task, scene  ids · battery or split · address list hash · unit of inference (world or cluster)
     pairing      label: paired-exact | paired-by-address (with realized pair correlation)
     per metric   disposition · direction (A | B | none) · statistic · effect estimate with interval
                  counts {a_only, b_only, both, neither, unscored, planned} · unscored by arm and reason
                  worst-case effect range if every unscored pair went against the leader
     absolute     per arm: hard-limit verdict (met | violated | undetermined), with the requirement id and
                  the counts of episodes violating each hard limit
     divergence   first divergent event between arms on the earliest discordant address: tick, stream, event
     tier         the tier run · provisional: true|false · promotion {referee pairs, DC-01 table version,
                  referee-fidelity estimate with interval} when promoted
     robustness   rank_robustness: model-robust | model-conditional {condition} | unchecked
     standing     development-result | qualified {campaign, index, level, receipt hash}
     evidence     claim product · decision-policy ids · trace ids · no-authorization flags (all false)
   ```
2. **Dispositions.**

   | Disposition | When |
   |---|---|
   | `settled` | Anytime: an e-process crossed 1/α (direction given). Fixed-n: `promote` (direction B) or `inferior` (direction A) |
   | `tied` | Anytime: the confidence sequence lies inside ±margin. Fixed-n: `practical tie` |
   | `inconclusive` | The budget or n ended without the above; the evidence so far is attached |
   | `incomparable` | The study ran and cannot be scored as a comparison: the worst-case effect range over unscored pairs spans both directions and more than a declared share (default 10%) of pairs is unscored; or the arms are labelled `capacity-biased`; or the arms' classes lost their pairing licence during the run |
   | `infeasible` | Neither design meets the mission's hard limits on the scored pairs (both absolute verdicts `violated`), or a design could not be settled at all |

   A `settled` verdict in favour of a design whose absolute verdict is `violated` is still reported as settled,
   with `absolute` beside it and the warning `winner-violates-hard-limit`; promotion and qualification refuse
   such a candidate.
3. **Hard feasibility in the fixed-n rule.** The promote condition requires that the candidate violates no hard
   limit in a condition where the baseline does not; the record lists the conditions checked.
4. **`provisional`.** Set whenever the tier is not the referee. Cleared only by the promotion verb writing the
   `promotion` block; the comparison writer has no way to clear it.
5. **`standing`.** `development-result` unless a qualification receipt whose decision is `promoted` is
   attached. The envelope's `evidence.claims` contains the design-comparison claim only for `qualified`
   records; citing a development result as qualified is the lane's protocol refusal (845). A
   `model-conditional` record cited without its condition, or offered to a build packet, is refused (557) by
   the report and build verbs; the field and its condition text are what they check.
6. **`study.compare <study> [--metric M] --json`.** Kind `read`, first milestone M3, cluster `study`,
   capability `study:read`, phase `review`; the row of record is VERBS-study's (inputs a study, optional
   `--metric`; output schema `frob.comparison/1`). Returns the record's per-metric block with intervals,
   failures by class, the first divergent event and the pair counts; exit 0 for `settled` or `tied`, 2 for
   `inconclusive`, `incomparable`, `infeasible` or any warning; a sealed confirmation study read by a session
   without `qual:admin` before its campaign closes is `FROB-REF-840`, exit 5 (the one Arena code with exit 5,
   N24-REF: it refuses a session outside its scopes); an unknown study or job identifier is the surface's
   `FROB-REF-802` with pointer `study`, exit 3, the convention of `job.cancel` for an unknown job (N32a-13); a
   re-read of fixed-n data as a sequential study is `FROB-REF-553`, exit 3. Idempotent; byte-identical on
   repeat. A study still running returns its progress and no verdict. The row lists 553 and 840 as chapter codes
   (802 is surface-wide), so every code this verb raises is listed and the conformance test passes. Pair counts
   that fail the identity are never printed: the writer refuses such a record, and a read that met one would be
   the internal fault `FROB-REF-990`, exit 9 (N32a-03), as the row states.
7. **Firewall (INV-ATEL-11).** The record writer and every studio verb resolve world addresses through an
   accessor that refuses sealed clusters for sessions without the admin scope (840). A test enumerates the
   studio's verbs and calls each with a sealed study.
8. **First divergence.** For the earliest address in address order where the arms' outcomes differ, call the
   first-divergence search over the two arms' digest chains (they differ from the start, being different
   designs, so the search runs on the task-event stream: the first event present in one arm and absent or
   later in the other); store the tick and event class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record validates against its schema and round-trips byte-identically; its identity is the hash of its
      canonical bytes.
- [ ] The worked fixed-n result (b = 19, a = 2, 40 both, 3 neither, 0 unscored, n = 64) is stored as `settled`
      toward B with interval [0.0554, 0.4392], counts summing to 64, `provisional: true`,
      `standing: development-result`.
- [ ] Each disposition is produced by a fixture: a crossing; a tie (0 discordances of 86); budget exhaustion;
      30% unscored with a sign-ambiguous range (`incomparable`); both arms violating a hard limit
      (`infeasible`).
- [ ] The absolute block is present for both arms in every record; a winner that violates a hard limit carries
      the warning.
- [ ] No studio verb returns data of a sealed confirmation study to a session without the admin scope (each
      verb tested; refusal 840).
- [ ] `study.compare` is reachable through `frob --json` and MCP and returns exit codes as specified (840 exit 5
      for a sealed study read without `qual:admin`; 802 with pointer `study`, exit 3, for an unknown one);
      `scripts/e2e/study_compare_record.sh` passes.
- [ ] The comparison writer cannot set `provisional: false` or `standing: qualified` (no constructor; a
      compile-fail test pins it).

verb:eval.compare seed=17 needs=cpu plants=counts-off,no-absolute`), on the cart fixture of BD-123 (`synthetic`).
Steps: (1) `frob eval compare cart-a cart-b --task cart-sill-dock --max-pairs 128 --key sc-cmp-1 --json` (then
`frob job wait` on exit 8); (2) `frob study compare <id> --json`: validate the record against `frob schema
frob.comparison/1 --json`, check that its identity is the hash of its canonical bytes, that the absolute block is
present for both arms, `provisional: true`, `standing: development-result`, the accounting identity against
`planned`, and the exit code against the disposition (0 for `settled`/`tied`, 2 otherwise); (3) repeat step 2:
byte-identical; (4) `frob study compare <id> --metric success --json`: one metric block only; (5) an identifier no
study has: `FROB-REF-802`, pointer `study`, exit 3; (6) a sealed confirmation study created in the scratch namespace by `frob eval
qualify cart-b --task cart-sill-dock --claim '<a synthetic absolute claim>' --key sc-qual --json` on a `synthetic`
sealed family (BD-259, BD-261), read by a session without `qual:admin`: exit 5, `FROB-REF-840`, and no field of
the confirmation data in the envelope. Plants: `counts-off` (a stored record whose counts do not sum to
`planned`: the writer refuses it) and `no-absolute` (a record without the absolute block: schema failure).
Logged to `target/e2e-logs/study_compare_record/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript, on NDJSON lines with the
STD-E2E fields (`ts`, `script`, `bead` = N28a-21, `step` (`compare`, `study_compare`, `repeat`, `metric_filter`,
`unknown`, `sealed_read`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence`
block): disposition per metric, counts with the identity against `planned`, interval, the divergence tick, labels
(`provisional`, `standing`, `rank_robustness`), the record's content hash, the claim product; refusals with code
and first repair; on failure the first field that differs from the expected record and the study id; a final
`summary`. The same reads run again as steps 8 and 11 of `scripts/e2e/atelier_compare.sh` (N28a-30).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the worked study result above. Negative controls: a record whose counts do not sum to `planned`
(the writer refuses to store it); a mutant that omits the absolute block (schema failure); a mutant that marks
a discovery result `qualified` (no constructor exists; the test is the compile failure); a sealed read.
Determinism: D0; byte-identical records across platforms for the same outcomes.

## Considerations

- The 10% unscored threshold for `incomparable` is a DESIGN default recorded in the comparison policy; the
  worst-case range is always printed so a reader can apply a stricter one.
- A relative win between two designs that both fail the mission is the commonest way a comparison misleads;
  the absolute block exists for that and is never optional.
- OPEN-ATEL-4 (two revalidation verbs over one engine) does not touch this record; revalidation reads the
  record's dependency support (identities and the address list) when an edit puts a comparison in question.
