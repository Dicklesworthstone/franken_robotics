## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, its measurement and its fallback; a kill gate that
fires is not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Each is decided at the milestone of its gate and
recorded as an architecture decision record.

KILL-STATIC governs the right of the studio's static analyses to refuse designs. Through `design.check` the
analyses stop a design before simulation and, as rung R0 of the evaluation ladder, exclude designs from
campaigns. That right rests on the bet that static verdicts predict simulated failure better than a trivial
heuristic and do not discard winners (BET-05). The measurement is made on the design corpus at the third
milestone (bead BD-250, gate GATE-ATEL-01). This bead applies the rule to that measurement and makes the
consequences real in the code and the registries.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, writing the ADR, switching the policy if the gate fires, stating and testing what
changes downstream. Out: the measurement (BD-250); the analyses; re-running the bet later (a new registered
measurement).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-ATEL-01 fails: on the held-out third of the design corpus, the area under the ROC curve of
the `design check` score against simulated failure is below 0.8, or its paired 95% lower bound above the
mass-and-torque heuristic is below 0.05; or a planted defective design of a covered kind is not flagged; or a
planted sound design is refused. BET-05's falsifier adds two conditions read from the same record: the audit of
discarded designs finds a discarded design in the true top-k, or a planted winner is discarded.

**Milestone.** M3.

**Measurement that decides it.** The gate row and per-design table of BD-250: AUC_check, AUC_heur, the lower
bound of their difference, the potency-control outcomes, the discard audit (m audited discards, top-k designs
among them, planted winners' outcomes).

**Decision rule.**
1. All of: AUC_check ≥ 0.8; lower bound of the difference ≥ 0.05; every covered planted defect flagged; every
   planted sound design passed; no true top-k design and no planted winner among the discards → **not fired**.
   `design check` keeps its refusals. The measured values replace the targets in the claim text.
2. The AUC conditions fail → **fired**.
3. The AUC conditions pass and a potency control fails, or a winner was discarded → the gate has failed, and
   the rule opens **one repair cycle** in the sense of N40-03 (one bounded attempt, opened by a log line naming
   the failed receipt and the hypothesis, closed by a re-measurement with the same corpus identity, thresholds
   and controls). The mechanism change in that cycle is fixed in advance: the refusing severity of the
   analysis that produced the wrong verdict is downgraded (the others keep theirs), or that analysis is
   repaired. Re-measurement passes → **not-fired**, and the ADR records the analysis downgraded during the
   cycle; it fails again → **fired** for every analysis. A second cycle is not opened (N40-03: it would be a
   `deferred` decision with its own ADR). The decision outcome is always one of N40-03's four (`not-fired`,
   `fired`, `not-reached`, `deferred`); a partial outcome is never written (DESIGN: keeps the closed set,
   while still sparing analyses that work one repair cycle).

**Fallback, named in advance.** `design check` verdicts are downgraded to advisory lints and the pruning claim
is withdrawn.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- The `check` policy table: every finding severity `Refusing(code)` that comes from an analysis of stages 2 to
  8 becomes `High` (exit 2, never exit 3). Stage 1 (form) keeps its refusals: a design with a unit error or a
  missing fill has no model to simulate, which is not a prediction.
- The loop-order rule no longer blocks simulation on a check finding; it still requires that the check ran, and
  results carry the findings as conditions.
- Rung R0 of the ladder and stage 1 of the cascade stop excluding designs on static findings; typed bounds with
  a certificate that the mission subsystem checks independently (a Farkas-certified infeasibility) keep their
  standing, because they are proofs about a stated model and not predictions.
- The ten-fold arithmetic and the claims table drop "static analysis prunes designs before simulation"; the
  claim that remains is the first shipped suite of pre-simulation analyses callable by an agent, with feedback
  in seconds and typed repairs. The phase ledger records that the pruning lever lost its claim.
- Beads that exist only on the "refusals stand" branch: none are deleted; the prune-soundness audit of the
  cascade has nothing static left to audit and reports `not-run: KILL-STATIC`.

**Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-STATIC-M3.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`;
`docs/delivery/adr/` is retired) with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as
registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number;
the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on; the beads re-read
(each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"); what changes downstream; the recording session.
It also carries the corpus identity, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds. The row of KILL-STATIC in the gate
register gets N40-03's one decision form, `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, with the
receipts of GATE-ATEL-01's measurement (and, after a repair cycle, of the re-measurement). The decision is made
by a session that did not run the measurement.

**Owner declaration of the kill gate.** As N28c-25 assigns to a deciding bead, this bead writes the studio's
`[[gate]]` block for KILL-STATIC with `role = "owner"`: `trigger` ("GATE-ATEL-01 fails (BET-05)"), `fallback`
("`design check` verdicts are downgraded to advisory lints and the pruning claim is withdrawn"), `decided =
"M3"`, `mode = "exit"`, `reads = ["GATE-ATEL-01"]`, the ledger's text byte for byte, in
`registries/gate_declarations.toml` (the place N28c-25 uses until the block is agreed with `contract.toml`);
N40-30 compares it with the ledger row of N40-26.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites AUC_check, AUC_heur, the lower bound, the control outcomes and the discard audit, with the
      corpus identity.
- [ ] The gate register carries the decision (`fired` or `not-fired`; never a value outside N40-03's four
      outcomes) with measured values, not targets; after a repair cycle the ADR names the analysis that was
      downgraded or repaired and both receipts.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-STATIC-M3.md` exists with every field N40-03 requires, and the owner declaration of
      KILL-STATIC agrees with the ledger row (N40-30's comparison passes).
- [ ] If fired: `frob design check` on the planted top-heavy design returns exit 2 with the finding, not exit 3
      (test); `frob sim run` on it proceeds without an override and carries the finding as a condition; a
      stage-1 defect still refuses with its code.
- [ ] If fired: the campaign report prints `static pruning: withdrawn (KILL-STATIC)`.
- [ ] If not fired: the claims table shows the measured AUC and bound.
- [ ] A decision recorded before the measurement's ledger sequence number is rejected by the register check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_static.rs` (rule and register) and `crates/frob-analyze/tests/policy_switch.rs`
(the switch; DESIGN paths, the convention of the studio's other kill decisions):
- `kill_static_rule_table`: (AUC 0.86, bound 0.09, controls ok, audit clean) → not-fired; (0.74, …) → fired;
  (0.86, bound 0.03) → fired; (0.86, 0.09, one sound design refused by A-08) → repair cycle with A-08
  downgraded, then not-fired if the re-measurement is clean and fired if it fails again; (0.86, 0.09, planted
  winner discarded) → repair cycle for the discarding analysis, the same two endings.
- `outcome_in_closed_set`: every decision the rule writes is one of `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached`,
  `deferred`; a planted `fired-partial` is rejected by the register load.
- `second_cycle_not_opened`: a second failure after the cycle yields `fired`, never another cycle.
- `policy_switch_downgrades_refusing`: with the switch on, every stage-2-to-8 `Refusing` is `High`; stage 1 is
  unchanged.
- `loop_order_after_kill`: a High finding does not block `sim.run`; the condition is recorded.
- `certified_bounds_keep_standing`.
- `decision_before_measurement_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_static_record.sh`: reads the gate row of GATE-ATEL-01 and the per-design table with
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example registry_report -- gates --chapter 28 --milestone M3 --json` (N28c-25;
there is no `frob conformance` or `frob gates` command), applies the rule, compares with
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-STATIC-M3.md` and the register, and (when fired) stores BD-100's top-heavy quadruped fixture
from a scratch copy with `frob design new ks-top-heavy --file <copy> --key "$(e2e_key new-top-heavy)" --json`
(N14a-21, reached through BD-250), runs `frob design check ks-top-heavy --json` (expect exit 2 with the finding
at severity High) and `frob sim run ks-top-heavy --tier referee --seconds 2 --key "$(e2e_key ks-sim-1)" --json`
(a run verb, so it carries an idempotency key; expect it to proceed with the finding as a condition) to
observe the downgraded behaviour, and `frob design check` on a stored stage-1 defect (a unit error; expect exit
3 with its code). It
sources the STD-E2E helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_static_record/<utc>.ndjson`; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` (`read`,
`rule`, `compare`, `observe`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and, for the verb calls, the
envelope's `evidence` block; measured values: the five inputs with their thresholds (AUC_check against 0.8, the
lower bound against 0.05, control and audit outcomes), the decision, the ADR path and register row, the observed
exit codes against the branch's expectation; on failure the first disagreement and the paths; a final `summary`
line naming KILL-STATIC.

### Known answers and negative controls

The rule fixtures above. Negative controls: a register row holding targets in place of measured values is
rejected; a policy switch that also downgrades stage-1 refusals is caught by `policy_switch_downgrades_refusing`.

## Considerations

- The repair cycle keeps what works: the analyses are independent, and one that mis-flags a sound design should
  not cost the others their standing. It is bounded to one cycle (N40-03) so that it cannot become a way to tune
  on the held-out split. The corpus identity, thresholds and controls stay fixed; the held-out third for the
  re-measurement is redrawn by a new key registered before the re-measurement, because the repair was chosen
  after seeing the old held-out third (DESIGN: the fixture is the corpus, which does not change).
- Reopening the question needs a new registered measurement on a corpus identity not used for tuning.
- Even fired, the analyses stay in `check` and in `next_actions`; their measured value was always feedback in
  seconds, and the ceiling of pruning was about 1.19 times on trainings.
