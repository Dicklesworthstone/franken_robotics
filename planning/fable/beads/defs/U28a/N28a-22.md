## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, its measurement and its fallback; a kill gate that
fires is not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. Each is decided at the milestone of its gate and
recorded as an architecture decision record.

KILL-STATIC governs the right of the studio's static analyses to refuse designs. Through `design.check` the
analyses stop a design before simulation and, as rung R0 of the evaluation ladder, exclude designs from
campaigns. That right rests on the bet that static verdicts predict simulated failure better than a trivial
heuristic and do not discard winners (BET-05). The measurement is made on the design corpus at the third
milestone (bead BD-250, gate GATE-ATEL-01). This bead applies the rule to that measurement and makes the
consequences real in the code and the registries.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, writing the ADR, switching the policy if the gate fires, stating and testing what
changes downstream. Out: the measurement (BD-250); the analyses; re-running the bet later (a new registered
measurement).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-ATEL-01 fails: on the held-out third of the design corpus, the area under the ROC curve of
the `design check` score against simulated failure is below 0.8, or its paired 95% lower bound above the
mass-and-torque heuristic is below 0.05; or a planted defective design of a covered kind is not flagged; or a
planted sound design is refused. BET-05's falsifier adds two conditions read from the same record: the audit of
discarded designs finds a discarded design in the true top-k, or a planted winner is discarded.

**Milestone.** M3.

**Measurement that decides it.** The gate row and per-design table of BD-250: AUC_check, AUC_heur, the lower
bound of their difference, the potency-control outcomes, the discard audit (m audited discards, top-k designs
among them, planted winners' outcomes).

**Decision rule.**
1. All of: AUC_check ≥ 0.8; lower bound of the difference ≥ 0.05; every covered planted defect flagged; every
   planted sound design passed; no true top-k design and no planted winner among the discards → **not fired**.
   `design check` keeps its refusals. The measured values replace the targets in the claim text.
2. The AUC conditions fail → **fires**.
3. The AUC conditions pass and a potency control fails, or a winner was discarded → **fires for the analysis
   that produced the wrong verdict** (its refusing severity is downgraded, the others keep theirs), and the
   whole gate is re-measured after one repair cycle; a second failure fires it for all.

**Fallback, named in advance.** `design check` verdicts are downgraded to advisory lints and the pruning claim
is withdrawn.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- The `check` policy table: every finding severity `Refusing(code)` that comes from an analysis of stages 2 to
  8 becomes `High` (exit 2, never exit 3). Stage 1 (form) keeps its refusals: a design with a unit error or a
  missing fill has no model to simulate, which is not a prediction.
- The loop-order rule no longer blocks simulation on a check finding; it still requires that the check ran, and
  results carry the findings as conditions.
- Rung R0 of the ladder and stage 1 of the cascade stop excluding designs on static findings; typed bounds with
  a certificate that the mission subsystem checks independently (a Farkas-certified infeasibility) keep their
  standing, because they are proofs about a stated model and not predictions.
- The ten-fold arithmetic and the claims table drop "static analysis prunes designs before simulation"; the
  claim that remains is the first shipped suite of pre-simulation analyses callable by an agent, with feedback
  in seconds and typed repairs. The phase ledger records that the pruning lever lost its claim.
- Beads that exist only on the "refusals stand" branch: none are deleted; the prune-soundness audit of the
  cascade has nothing static left to audit and reports `not-run: KILL-STATIC`.

**Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-STATIC.md` with the measured numbers, corpus identity, commit, hardware fingerprint,
seeds, the decision, the fallback in force and the affected beads; the row of KILL-STATIC in the gate register
with the same receipt. The decision is made by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites AUC_check, AUC_heur, the lower bound, the control outcomes and the discard audit, with the
      corpus identity.
- [ ] The gate register carries the decision (`fired`, `not-fired` or `fired-partial: <analysis>`) with
      measured values, not targets.
- [ ] If fired: `frob design check` on the planted top-heavy design returns exit 2 with the finding, not exit 3
      (test); `frob sim run` on it proceeds without an override and carries the finding as a condition; a
      stage-1 defect still refuses with its code.
- [ ] If fired: the campaign report prints `static pruning: withdrawn (KILL-STATIC)`.
- [ ] If not fired: the claims table shows the measured AUC and bound.
- [ ] A decision recorded before the measurement's ledger sequence number is rejected by the register check.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_static_rule_table`: (AUC 0.86, bound 0.09, controls ok, audit clean) → not fired; (0.74, …) → fired;
  (0.86, bound 0.03) → fired; (0.86, 0.09, one sound design refused by A-08) → fired-partial: A-08;
  (0.86, 0.09, planted winner discarded) → fired-partial for the discarding analysis.
- `policy_switch_downgrades_refusing`: with the switch on, every stage-2-to-8 `Refusing` is `High`; stage 1 is
  unchanged.
- `loop_order_after_kill`: a High finding does not block `sim.run`; the condition is recorded.
- `certified_bounds_keep_standing`.
- `decision_before_measurement_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_static_record.sh`: reads the gate row of GATE-ATEL-01 and the per-design table, applies the
rule, compares with the ADR and the register, and (when fired) runs `frob design check` and `frob sim run` on
the top-heavy fixture to observe the downgraded behaviour. Logged per the shared standard: the five inputs with
their thresholds, the decision, the ADR path, the observed exit codes.

### Known answers and negative controls

The rule fixtures above. Negative controls: a register row holding targets in place of measured values is
rejected; a policy switch that also downgrades stage-1 refusals is caught by `policy_switch_downgrades_refusing`.

## Considerations

- A partial firing keeps what works: the analyses are independent, and one that mis-flags a sound design should
  not cost the others their standing. It is bounded by one repair cycle so that it cannot become a way to tune
  on the held-out split; the re-measurement uses a fresh keyed split of the corpus.
- Reopening the question needs a new registered measurement on a corpus identity not used for tuning.
- Even fired, the analyses stay in `check` and in `next_actions`; their measured value was always feedback in
  seconds, and the ceiling of pruning was about 1.19 times on trainings.
