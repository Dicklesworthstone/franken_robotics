## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; one that fires is not a
failure, because the fallback is the plan. KILL-LADDER governs layer 2 of the evaluation ladder's allocation
rule: conformal survivor sets calibrated on an audit lane (bead BD-329). Layer 2 is Frontier. It is only worth
its complexity if it reaches the same accepted decisions as successive halving for less compute without losing
top designs. For a Frontier decision core the fallback is also its kill fallback: if the core's calibration
fails its gate after one repair cycle, the core stays on its fallback, and the registry says so.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger to the recorded measurements, the ADR, the registry state of DC-02 and DC-03, the
downstream switch. Out: the measurements (BD-310 for the pilot, BD-329 for the gate).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-ATEL-08 fails after one repair cycle. The gate: layer 2 against the fixed table on the same
population and addresses: held-out coverage at least 1 − α − 0.03 per stratum; audited top-k recall no lower
than halving's; fewer GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision at a 95% paired lower bound above 1; a planted
over-scored design caught at the next rung; synthetic drift trips the alarm. The pilot form of the falsifier
(from the logged replay): layer 2 does not reach halving's audited top-k recall at fewer GPU-seconds.

**Milestone.** M4. The pilot at M3 sets the starting posture (shadow behind the fixed table) and cannot by
itself adopt layer 2.

**Measurement that decides it.** From BD-329's gate row, first run and, if it failed, the run after one repair
cycle: per-stratum coverage; recall of both rules from the audit lane; the paired lower bound on the ratio of
GPU-seconds (halving over layer 2); the two controls.

**Decision rule.**
1. All five conditions hold on the first run or on the repair run → `not fired`: DC-02 and DC-03 move from
   `shadow` to `canary` on one stratum and then to `default` under the adoption rule; the measured ratio
   replaces any target in the claims table.
2. Any condition fails on both runs → **fires**.
3. A control fails (the over-scored design is not caught, or drift does not trip the alarm) on either run →
   **fires** without a repair cycle for the control itself: a calibration that cannot see its own failure is
   not adopted.

**Fallback, named in advance.** The fixed halving table is the allocation rule; the audit lane is kept as
measurement.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `registries/cores/DC-02.toml` and `DC-03.toml`: adoption status stays `shadow`, with `kill: KILL-LADDER` and
  the ADR id; the cascade runs layer 1 in every stratum.
- The audit lane still runs at a tenth of rung spend, because it feeds the rank-agreement ledger and the
  screening audit; its results no longer set a margin.
- The mode `selective` has no rule to apply; strata that qualify for it are treated as `discard-only` (the
  fixed table), and reports print "k survivors".
- The handover to the finalist layer is by count alone (at most 64 survivors of the last population rung).
- The ten-fold arithmetic keeps only halving's saving for the population stage.

**Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-LADDER.md` with measured values, the population identity, commit, hardware
fingerprint, seeds; the kill row in the gate register; decided by a session that did not run the measurement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites coverage per stratum, both recalls, the cost ratio with its bound and both controls, for
      each run made.
- [ ] The gate register and the two core records carry the decision and adoption status.
- [ ] If fired: `frob opt cascade` with default settings uses the fixed table in every stratum (test), still
      runs the audit lane, and prints the fallback in `report.campaign`'s list of fallbacks that fired.
- [ ] If not fired: adoption advances no further than `canary` until the core-level gate has also passed.
- [ ] A decision recorded before the gate row's ledger sequence is rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_ladder_rule_table`: (all pass) → not fired; (recall lower, then pass after repair) → not fired;
  (recall lower twice) → fired; (cost bound 0.97 twice) → fired; (drift alarm silent) → fired at once.
- `fired_forces_fixed_table`; `audit_lane_still_runs_when_fired`; `selective_treated_as_discard_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ladder_record.sh`: reads the gate rows and the registry, applies the rule, compares with the
ADR, and when fired runs a nine-design seeded cascade to observe the fixed table and the report line. Logged:
the five inputs against thresholds per run, the decision, the adoption status, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls

The rule fixtures. Negative controls: a register row with targets in place of measurements; an adoption status
of `default` with no passed gate (rejected by the registry check).

## Considerations

- "One repair cycle" is one change to the layer's implementation or defaults followed by one re-measurement on
  a fresh keyed population draw; it is not permission to tune until the gate passes.
- Reopening needs a new registered measurement; a different α or audit share is a new decision-policy version.
- KILL-RANK is separate: it concerns whether the cheap rung carries rank information at all, and is decided
  with the rank-agreement experiment.
