## Background

A kill gate is a decision named in advance with its trigger, measurement and fallback; one that fires is not a
failure, because the fallback is the plan. KILL-VOI governs the ranking of experiments by a value-of-information
model: layer 3 of the evaluation ladder's allocation rule and the ordering inside `experiment.suggest`
(decision core DC-05, bead BD-351). The published evidence does not support the ranking against a capable
agent (plausibly below 1.5 times), which is why it was built to degrade gracefully: the model never issues a
verdict, so removing its ranking costs efficiency and nothing else. The other kill gate on the same verb,
KILL-AUGUR-PROBE, concerns the probe generator and is decided elsewhere; the two do not overlap.

## Scope

In: applying the trigger, the ADR, the registry state of DC-05, the downstream switch. Out: the pilot replay
(BD-310) and the gate measurement (BD-351).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** GATE-ATEL-15 fails. The gate: on the offline replay of the ladder log (64 designs × 3 rungs × 32
addresses, four choosers, 100 replays), the knowledge-gradient chooser is at least 1.5 times better than the
agent chooser in evaluations to a true top-3 design at 95% confidence; and the value is exactly zero on a
fixture where no outcome can change the choice. The pilot form of the falsifier: the knowledge-gradient
chooser is less than 1.5 times better than the agent.

**Milestone.** M4.

**Measurement that decides it.** From BD-351's gate row: the ratio R = (agent chooser's median evaluations to a
true top-3)/(knowledge-gradient chooser's), with its interval over the 100 replays; the zero-value fixture's
result; and, for the record, the same ratio against e-race elimination and uniform allocation.

**Decision rule.**
1. R ≥ 1.5 (the interval's lower end reported beside it) and the zero-value fixture passes → `not fired`:
   DC-05 moves from `shadow` to `canary` under the adoption rule.
2. R < 1.5 → **fires**.
3. The zero-value fixture fails → **fires**, and the defect is filed as a correctness bug in the value
   function, because a non-zero value where nothing can change is wrong whatever R is.

**Fallback, named in advance.** Value of information is a stop rule only; `experiment.suggest` lists proposals
unranked by value.

**What changes downstream when it fires.**
- `experiment.suggest` returns proposals in the fixed order (check, simulate, train, referee, then
  measurements with the direct measurement listed), each still with its cost and testable prediction, and
  still ends with STOP when no proposal's value clears the price; the `ratio` field is omitted and the output
  says `ranking: withdrawn (KILL-VOI)`.
- The finalist layer of the ladder buys experiments in the fixed order until STOP or budget.
- `registries/cores/DC-05.toml`: adoption `shadow`, `kill: KILL-VOI`; its calibration monitor on registered
  predictions keeps running, because prediction intervals are still issued.
- The ten-fold arithmetic drops any factor attributed to experiment ranking; the stopping saving remains under
  the comparison and racing rules.

**Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-VOI.md` with R and its interval, the four choosers' figures, the fixture result,
the log identity, commit and seeds; the kill row in the gate register; decided by a session that did not run
the measurement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR cites R with its interval, the comparators and the zero-value result.
- [ ] The gate register and the DC-05 record carry the decision and adoption status.
- [ ] If fired: `frob experiment suggest … --json` has no `ratio` field, lists the fixed order, and still emits
      STOP (test); the cascade's report lists the fallback among those that fired.
- [ ] If not fired: adoption goes no further than `canary` before the core-level gate row exists.
- [ ] A decision recorded before the gate row's ledger sequence is rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `kill_voi_rule_table`: (R = 1.8, zero ok) → not fired; (R = 1.2) → fired; (R = 2.0, zero-value fixture
  returns 10⁻⁴) → fired.
- `fired_output_is_unranked_with_stop`; `finalist_layer_fixed_order_when_fired`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_voi_record.sh`: reads the gate row and the replay table, recomputes R, applies the rule,
compares with the ADR and the registry, and when fired calls `frob experiment suggest` on the three-finalist
fixture to observe the unranked form. Logged: R against 1.5, the zero-value result, the decision, the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls

The rule fixtures. Negative control: a register row with 1.5 in place of the measured R is rejected.

## Considerations

- The agent arm is a moving comparator: a stronger agent raises the bar. The decision is tied to the agent and
  version recorded in the replay; reopening it needs a new registered replay.
- A fired gate here says nothing about the probe generator, whose value is judged against a programmed expert
  and the direct measurement.
