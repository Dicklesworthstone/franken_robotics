## Background

Eleven decisions recur across the studio, and each one moves compute or changes what is reported as
established. Each is given one mathematical family, compiled ahead of time into a small runtime artifact (an
integer guard table, an automaton or a monitor, versioned, stored under `registries/cores/`, regenerated in
continuous integration and diffed), with a loss model, proof obligations, a deterministic fallback and a
budgeted mode. The runtime evaluates tables; no decision reads the wall clock, device load or arrival order.
The reason: replay, audit, and a rollback that is a configuration flag. They are named DC-01 to DC-11 (not
D-numbers, which already name determinism classes).

Where a core governs a mechanism that another subsystem owns, the studio fixes the decision rule (the table,
its loss model, its obligations, its fallback) and the owning subsystem fixes the mechanism. This bead records
the two cores of the failure plane whose mechanisms live in the adversarial-worlds crate and the
counterfactual-debugging crate: it writes their records, compiles their tables, and produces their rows of
the gate GATE-ATEL-13 (each core's table regenerates bit-identically; its null calibration holds; its fallback
is demonstrated by forcing each trigger; its shadow run records both actions). Nothing here is measured yet:
failure signatures are predicted, substituted numbers are illustrative unless marked TARGET, and each core
ships in shadow behind its baseline. Parameters of fallbacks, triggers and budgets are DESIGN defaults recorded
in the core's contract; changing one is a new version of the decision policy.

## Scope

In: the two records, their compiled artifacts, null-calibration tests, forced-trigger demonstrations, shadow
records, demonstration manifests. Out: the core contract and registry tooling (BD-309); the mechanisms
(`nemesis.bound`, splitting, interventions, attribution: their beads); the interference tests between cores
(BD-350).

## Technical approach

**DC-04 · Certify, refute or stop on a rare failure.** Owner `frob-nemesis`; timescale per finalist; tier
Solid for the bound, Frontier for splitting.
- *Artifacts.* The certification table n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉ (nominal episodes without failure needed to bound the
  failure rate below p* at confidence 1 − δ); a sequential binomial bound that ends `certified`, `refuted` or
  `exhausted` (a likelihood-mixture confidence sequence for a Bernoulli rate); a splitting schedule on a
  distress score, with fixed levels and child world addresses for forks; a failure-mode catalogue with a
  Good–Turing monitor of unseen mass (modes seen once divided by failures seen); a truncated-weight lower
  bound for refutation.
- *Loss model and split of budget.* False certification far outweighs false refutation. Explore first, to a
  fifth of the budget or until unseen mass is at most 0.05; refute if the lower bound exceeds p*; spend the
  rest on nominal sampling, for finalists only.
- *Obligations.* The bound holds at confidence 1 − δ for independent nominal episodes with a frozen failure
  predicate, and is scoped to its tier. Splitting supplies discovery and `Estimated` rates only. Failures are
  replayed one tier up. A bound refuses episodes selected by a battery or by a search (FROB-REF-549: a
  rare-failure bound over battery-selected or search-selected episodes; repair: draw nominal episodes).
- *Fallback and triggers.* Crude Monte Carlo alone. Triggers: under 1% of forks reach the next level (the score
  is uninformative); forks without fresh randomness.
- *Budgeted mode.* Exploration at most a fifth; on exhaustion a refusal that carries the bound reached.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* At δ = 0.05, bounds of 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁶ need 3 × 10⁴, 3 × 10⁵ and
  3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes: 75 s, 12.5 min and 2.1 h at 400 episodes per second. Splitting to 10⁻⁶ with six
  levels of probability 0.1 and 10⁴ trajectories per level uses 6 × 10⁴ fragments at a coefficient of
  variation near 7% (cv² ≈ L·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·N)). Forks find failures much sooner than waiting; published
  adaptive stress testing reports 2 to 20 times fewer episodes (FACT, source register); 10 to 100 times at
  rates of 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁵ is a TARGET. Proving absence has a fixed price that no sampler discounts without
  assuming where failures live. If throughput is a tenth of that figure, 10⁻⁶ costs 21 h: certify 10⁻⁵ and
  report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.
- *Sibling facts.* The sibling suite has Gaussian importance sampling and multilevel Monte Carlo; no splitting
  code exists there (FACT, to be re-read).
- *Rejected.* Extreme-value extrapolation of failure tails (checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ takes the same
  3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes that certify directly). Defensive-mixture importance sampling as the certified bound
  (its floor is ln(1/δ)/(β·n), never below crude sampling on the nominal share β; it survives for refutation,
  by a lower bound, and for tightening an estimate once failures are observed). Subset simulation by Markov
  chains (deferred unless forks carry no fresh randomness).

**DC-06 · Which cause and which repair rank first.** Owner `frob-autopsy`; timescale per failure cluster;
tier Solid.
- *Artifacts.* Typed interventions (`do(torque limit off)`, `do(friction := nominal)`,
  `do(estimator := privileged)`), each one declared mechanism wide; a fork ledger; a placebo flip rate ρ₀ from
  an intervention that should not matter; a probability of necessity per cause; minimal repair sets by delta
  debugging; the labels `but-for`, `joint` and `chaotic: unattributable`. The output is which repair edit ranks
  first.
- *Loss model.* A wrong first repair costs an agent turn and its compute; naming a cause in a chaotic regime
  sends the agent after noise, which costs more than saying nothing.
- *Obligations.* The empty intervention replays the failure bit for bit, so the starting point is exact. Each
  intervention changes one declared mechanism, checked by model diff. A cause is named only when its flip rate
  beats ρ₀ under a sequential test of the DC-01 kind. Design-parameter interventions re-run from the episode
  start, because winding temperature and the policy's own history differ; environment and controller-state
  interventions may fork from a snapshot under the fork classification. Attribution arithmetic runs in double
  precision.
- *Fallback and triggers.* First-anomaly ordering, labelled `Estimated`. Triggers: a replay mismatch; ρ₀ above
  0.2.
- *Budgeted mode.* 12 interventions, singles and pairs, about 700 forks. A coalition-value summary is computed
  only when no single or pair flips the outcome, and is labelled a description: such values can be negative
  and cannot establish necessity.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* Of 200 failing worlds re-run from snapshots, removing the torque limit
  prevents 164, nominal friction prevents 38 and a placebo nudge prevents 12. Net of placebo: 0.76 and 0.13.
  If the placebo rate is near a cause's rate, nothing is attributed; if only the pair prevents the failure,
  the cause is joint.
- *Rejected.* Coalition-value attribution as the primary output (values can be negative and cannot establish
  necessity; kept as a budgeted description). Copy-on-write device state for forks (wrong bottleneck: copying
  8,192 worlds at 30 kB is 0.25 GB). Observational causal adjustment (exact intervention is available).

**What this bead builds for each core.**
1. The record `registries/cores/DC-04.toml` / `DC-06.toml` with every field of the core contract: decision,
   owner crate, timescale; family and compiled artifacts with version and content hash; loss model as declared
   states, actions and cost constants; proof obligations by identifier; fallback and triggers; budgeted mode;
   adoption status (`shadow`); a replay scenario; a calibration report; a demonstration manifest (commands that
   run in under 60 s, with checksums, and the claim identifiers they support).
2. The generators in `frob-opt::cores`: for DC-04 the table of n over a grid of (δ, p*), and the integer guard
   table of the sequential bound (n, k) → `certified`, `refuted`, `continue`, `exhausted` at the declared
   mixture; for DC-06 the guard table of the placebo-adjusted flip-rate test and the label automaton
   (`but-for`, `joint`, `chaotic: unattributable`, `none`). Outputs are canonical bytes; CI regenerates and
   diffs.
3. Null calibration. DC-04: 10⁴ simulated runs at true rate p* show certification at most δ of the time under
   optional stopping; DC-06: 10⁴ runs in which the cause's flip rate equals the placebo's name the cause at
   most α of the time.
4. Forced triggers: fork success under 1% at a level; forks without fresh randomness; a replay mismatch;
   ρ₀ = 0.25. Each must put the core on its fallback and write the trigger into the evidence record.
5. Shadow records: each decision record carries the identities read, the table version, the ledger frontier,
   the action taken, and the action the baseline (crude Monte Carlo; first-anomaly ordering) took.

- [ ] Both tables regenerate bit-identically in CI on both development platforms.
- [ ] DC-04 arithmetic: at δ = 0.05, n = 29,958 for p* = 10⁻⁴ (reported as 3 × 10⁴), 299,574 for 10⁻⁵,

      regenerate; the fallback is in force meanwhile).

### Known answers and negative controls

The worked arithmetic above. Negative controls: a certification table computed with ln(1/δ)/(β·n) presented as
tighter than crude sampling; a cause named when its flip rate equals the placebo's; a table edited by hand
(hash mismatch, 871). Determinism: tables are D0 data; identical bytes across platforms.

## Considerations

- The primary risk of DC-04 is a failure predicate changed after sampling began; the mechanism's own refusal
  covers it, and the record names the frozen predicate's hash.
- DC-04 → DC-06 → DC-10 is a one-way pipeline: failures found feed attribution, attributed modes feed the
  scene-coverage incidence matrix, and nothing flows back inside a campaign.
- OPEN: a certified rate of 10⁻⁶ exceeds the default compute class; the fail-closed default is to certify
  10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.
