## Background

The studio compiles each of its eleven recurring decisions into a small versioned table with a loss model,
proof obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode, stored under `registries/cores/` and
regenerated in continuous integration (decision cores DC-01 to DC-11). The runtime evaluates tables and reads
no clock. Where a core governs a mechanism another subsystem owns, the studio fixes the decision rule and the
owner fixes the mechanism.

This bead records three such cores: whether a simulated result may carry a reality band (the calibration
subsystem's mechanism), which solver effort profile a run uses (the dynamics subsystem's), and whether a kernel
rewrite is accepted (the kernel toolchain's). For each it writes the record, compiles the table, and produces
the row of the gate GATE-ATEL-13: the table regenerates bit-identically; its null calibration holds; its
fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger; its shadow run records both actions. Parameters are DESIGN
defaults in each core's contract; substituted numbers are illustrative unless marked TARGET.

## Scope

In: three records, their compiled artifacts, calibration tests, forced-trigger demonstrations, shadow records,
demonstration manifests. Out: the core contract and tooling (BD-309); the mechanisms and their own gates; the
interference tests (BD-350).

## Technical approach

**DC-07 · Whether a result may carry a reality band.** Owner `frob-ground`; per real batch; Frontier.
- *Artifacts.* A one-sided band, real ≥ sim − q̂, per scope key (robot, firmware, floor, policy); a test
  martingale on the band's conformal p-values; the table of scopes.
- *Loss model.* Citing a simulated result as predictive outside its calibrated family, against withholding a
  usable prediction inside it.
- *Obligations.* Coverage at least 1 − α within one calibrated robot and task family, varying by about six
  points at 20 calibration episodes (FACT, source register). Nothing for designs that were never built.
  Conditions of real episodes come from a pre-registered draw; the experiment-choice core DC-05 chooses only
  how many. The alarm ends the regime and calibration restarts inside the new one.
- *Fallback and triggers.* `reality: no-claim`. Triggers: under 19 calibration points; martingale at or above
  20; any change of scope key.
- *Budgeted mode.* The number of real episodes is what DC-05 buys at the declared exchange rate. Below the
  calibration floor no band is issued; there is no smaller band for a smaller budget.
- *Rejected.* Kernel two-sample and optimal-transport drift tests (no power at twenty scalar residuals).
- *At this milestone.* The mechanism lands later; here the table is compiled and calibrated on synthetic
  residuals, and its adoption status is `shadow` with the note `mechanism: M5`.

**DC-08 · Which solver effort profile a run uses.** Owner `frob-dyn`; at ledger refresh; Frontier.
- *Artifacts.* Declared effort profiles (iteration counts, tolerances, substeps), each its own model identity;
  a guard table that holds, per stratum, the cheapest profile whose risk bound (n·R̂ + 1)/(n + 1) is at most τ
  on the fidelity battery (n battery points, R̂ the empirical risk of the monotonized loss); a rule that
  re-runs unconverged worlds one profile up, flagged. If the chosen profile misses the throughput budget, the
  run shrinks its world count or is refused; the profile does not move.
- *Loss model.* An under-converged result that passes as evidence, against wasted iterations.
- *Obligations.* A monotone loss, enforced by taking the running maximum over higher profiles; expected loss at
  most τ for worlds exchangeable with the battery; the profile never reads the wall clock and never changes
  inside a run; the table changes only at a ledger refresh.
- *Fallback and triggers.* The default profile of the dynamics subsystem. Triggers: fewer than 19 battery
  points in the stratum; a change of identity.
- *Budgeted mode.* At most one step up per world per run.
- *Rejected.* Feedback control (PID or model-predictive) on solver effort: wall-clock feedback into iteration
  counts makes results depend on load and breaks replay; a guard table with hysteresis passes. Lyapunov and
  bifurcation analysis of solver effort: the residual monitor covers it.

**DC-11 · Whether a kernel rewrite is accepted.** Owner `frob-kir` and `derive/frob-derive`; at build; Solid
for the exact class, Frontier for the real class.
- *Artifacts.* A closed rule table. Class E is bit-exact under round-to-nearest with contraction off:
  commutation, common-subexpression elimination, dead-code elimination, scaling by a power of two away from
  overflow and underflow, and scheduling. Class R is equal over the reals only: reassociation, distribution,
  fused multiply-add. A certificate is (source hash, target hash, rule trace), replayed by an independent
  checker.
- *Loss model.* A silent bit change invalidates replay and both ledgers; a skipped rewrite costs a few per cent
  of speed.
- *Obligations.* Each class-E rule is shown exact once on its domain, and digests match on a pinned corpus on
  every backend. A class-R rewrite needs a polynomial-identity certificate, an error bound, the fidelity
  battery and a new kernel version; a new version is a new identity and resets evidence.
- *Fallback and triggers.* The unrewritten derived kernel. Triggers: a digest mismatch; a whole-step gain under
  3%.
- *Budgeted mode.* 60 s of search per kernel; one class-R candidate per release.
- *Worked example.* In `f32` with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1: (a + b) + c = 1 and a + (b + c) = 0, because the
  spacing of `f32` near 10⁸ is 8. Sharing a subexpression used three times saves two evaluations and changes
  no bit. Regrouping makes a different kernel, which must be named as one.
- *Ceiling.* The Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step, so specializing them is
  worth about 1.2 times at most (FACT, source register). Class R and any wider search are worth their
  certificates only if this system's own M2 profile shows more than about a fifth of step time in derived
  arithmetic.
- *Rejected.* E-graph saturation with a solver over real-arithmetic rewrites (every accepted rewrite changes
  bits, so each needs an error bound, a battery run and a new kernel identity, while most step time is
  expected in the solve; reopen only on the profile condition above). Stochastic superoptimization and
  counterexample-guided synthesis of kernels (no sound equivalence check exists for floating-point code).

**What this bead builds for each core.** (1) The record `registries/cores/DC-07.toml`, `DC-08.toml`,
`DC-11.toml` with every contract field (decision, owner, timescale, family, artifacts with version and hash,
loss model as states, actions and cost constants, obligations by identifier, fallback and triggers, budgeted
mode, adoption status, replay scenario, calibration report, demonstration manifest of commands under 60 s with
checksums). (2) The generators in `frob-opt::cores`: DC-07's band table and martingale monitor parameters per
scope key; DC-08's guard table per stratum from a battery result file; DC-11's rule table with each rule's
class and domain predicate. (3) Calibration: DC-07 coverage on 10⁴ synthetic calibration draws; DC-08 the risk
bound on the fidelity battery with a planted under-converged profile (PO-ATEL-10: the risk bound holds for the
monotonized loss); DC-11 class-E exactness on the pinned corpus. (4) Forced triggers and shadow records as the
gate requires.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three records validate; `frob cores show` prints each; tables regenerate bit-identically in CI.
- [ ] DC-08: on a battery stratum with n = 39 points and empirical risks (0.00, 0.02, 0.10) for profiles
      (high, medium, low) and τ = 0.05, the bounds are 0.025, 0.0445 and 0.1225, and the table holds `medium`;
      with n = 18 the stratum is on the default profile; a planted profile that under-converges on 30% of
      worlds is never selected (PO-ATEL-10).
- [ ] DC-08: the selected profile and the run's digests are unchanged under a saturated queue (the profile reads
      no clock); a second step up in one run is refused.
- [ ] DC-07: below 19 calibration points no band is issued (`reality: no-claim`); a change of any scope-key
      field voids the band; synthetic drift pushes the martingale past 20 and voids it.
- [ ] DC-11: the `f32` example yields 1 and 0; the reassociation filed as class E is rejected by the trace
      checker; common-subexpression elimination on the pinned corpus changes no digest on any backend; a
      whole-step gain of 2% triggers the fallback.
- [ ] Pooling evidence across two model identities (two effort profiles) is refused (548); 1,000 worlds keep
      their digests after a class-E rewrite.
- [ ] The rows of GATE-ATEL-13 for DC-07 (table and calibration on synthetic residuals; mechanism adoption
      `not-yet-due (M5)`), DC-08 and DC-11 are recorded with measured values.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_cores.sh`: `frob cores show DC-07|DC-08|DC-11 --json`, each demonstration manifest, and
`frob conformance run --gate GATE-ATEL-13 --cores DC-07,DC-08,DC-11 --json`. Logged per core: table hash,
calibration value against its bound, triggers forced and fallbacks observed, a shadow record pair, the gate
row.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-705 (the `f32` regrouping gives 1 and 0; a reassociation admitted in class E must be rejected); the DC-08
arithmetic. Negative controls: the planted under-converged profile; a profile changed inside a run; a band
issued at 12 calibration points; a hand-edited table (871). Determinism: tables are D0; identical bytes on
every platform.

## Considerations

- Milestone mismatch, recorded: the core-level gate lists DC-07 among the cores due at M4, while reality bands
  land at M5. The table and its calibration on synthetic residuals close at M4; the shadow-run row needs real
  batches and is closed with the mechanism.
- An identity change (a new effort profile, a class-R rewrite) resets evidence and calibration in the ledgers
  of the other cores; a class-E rewrite changes no identity. That rule is tested between cores by the
  interference bead.
- DC-08's stratum definition follows the fidelity battery's; a stratum with too few battery points stays on
  the default, which costs iterations and never validity.
