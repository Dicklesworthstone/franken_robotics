## Background

A design is never scored by the most expensive method first. The studio scores designs on a ladder of six
rungs, each about an order of magnitude dearer than the one below, each allowed to decide only what its
evidence supports:

| Rung | What scores the design | Cost per design | What it may decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 Static | `design.check` and the bound analyses | Seconds of CPU; under 2 s for `check` (TARGET) | Refuse a design; exclude with a typed bound. It never ranks survivors |
| R1 Shared policy | The family policy of the design's morphology class, with no adaptation, on a fast tier; the synthesized baseline controller where the class has no family policy | Tens of GPU-seconds (HYPOTHESIS; the phase ledger records it) | Discard, under the rank-agreement modes. Select only in strata where rank agreement has been measured |
| R2 Fine-tuned | The family policy fine-tuned to the design at a declared fraction of specialist steps. Each fraction is its own rung for calibration | Minutes | Discard, and select among survivors |
| R3 Specialist | A policy trained or inherited for this design alone | One training run (the leading incumbent trains a quadruped velocity policy in about 410 s on one RTX 4090 and humanoids in under 30 minutes; FACT, source register) | Rank finalists, as `provisional` |
| R4 Referee | Paired re-evaluation on the referee at the same world addresses, by the promotion rule (decision core DC-01), with the tier-correction estimate | Referee episodes: 16 / 64 / 256 at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile per established verdict (TARGET, unmeasured) | Establish, or label `tier-sensitive` |
| R5 Rare failure | A failure-rate bound for finalists (decision core DC-04) | n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉ nominal episodes for a bound p* at confidence 1 − δ | Certify, refute or exhaust the bound, scoped to its tier |

Which designs climb is decided by an allocation rule in three layers. Layer 1, this bead, is the fixed table:
successive halving. It is deliberately dull. ADR-ATEL-03: successive halving is the fixed baseline and the
fallback of the ladder; every adaptive allocation rule ships in shadow behind it and reverts to it on a named
trigger, because an adaptive rule can fail in a way that costs validity or the best design, while the fixed
table can only cost compute. ADR-ATEL-04: a cheap rung discards; it does not select, except in the strata where
measured rank agreement says it can. Layers 2 (conformal survivor sets) and 3 (value of information among
finalists) are Frontier and live in other beads; they are measured against this one on the same population and
the same addresses, in GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision and in audited top-k recall.

## Scope

In: the rung type and one executor adapter per rung; the rung state machine with its transitions; the fixed
table and its schedule ledger; gating of what a rung may do by the stratum's mode; the wording rule for
finalists as this layer prints it; the identity-reset rule as this layer applies it; refusals 545 and 548 as
raised by this layer.

Built on three beads of the ladder unit, on which this bead depends: scorer admission per rung, the
evaluator-of-record rule and the D2-only rule for scores (N28b-12), whose admission function the state
machine calls for every score that may enter; the rank-agreement ledger rows with their identity scope and
reset rule (N28b-22); the three modes as an automaton with hysteresis, with refusal 545 and the survivors
wording rule (N28b-23). Where this bead restates one of those rules, the component governs it.

Out: the mechanisms behind each rung (their beads); the calibration experiment that fills the ledger
(BD-328); the audit lane, conformal survivors and the DC-02 and DC-03 runtime (N28b-13 to N28b-16, assembled
by the capstone BD-329, which depends on this bead); the survivor rule that plugs into this state machine as
the adaptive alternative (N28b-15, which depends on this bead); the finalist layer (BD-349, BD-351); the
cascade that drives the ladder over a population (BD-352).

## Technical approach

1. **Types (DESIGN sketch).**

   ```rust
   pub enum Rung { R0Static, R1SharedPolicy, R2FineTuned { fraction: Ratio }, R3Specialist, R4Referee, R5RareFailure }
   pub enum LadderMode { Selective, DiscardOnly, Uninformative }
   pub enum AllocationRule { FixedTable { eta: u8 }, ConformalSurvivors { alpha: Ratio, q_hat: f64, audit_n: u32 } }
   pub struct AllocationDecision {
       campaign: CampaignId, stratum: StratumId, from: Rung, to: Rung,
       rule: AllocationRule, mode: LadderMode,
       advanced: Vec<DesignId>, discarded: Vec<DesignId>,
       audit_lane: Vec<DesignId>,            // drawn by a keyed stream before any score exists; never cancelled
       rank_record: Option<RankAgreementId>, // a rung without a record may not discard
       policy: DecisionPolicyId,             // the versioned decision rule
       spend: Budget,
   }
   ```
2. **Rung executors.** One trait `RungScorer { fn score(designs, addresses, cx) -> Scores }` with adapters: R0
   calls the preflight and collects typed bounds; R1 applies the class's family policy (or the baseline
   controller, or the sampling controller as a declared scorer) and evaluates on the fast tier; R2 fine-tunes
   at the declared fraction, then evaluates; R3 trains or inherits a specialist, then evaluates; R4 is the
   promotion verb; R5 is the bound verb. Every score that enters the ladder is an evaluation on a D2 profile
   (bitwise per backend, independent of the batch); a policy may have been trained under the labelled D4
   training profile, but no number observed during training is a score.
3. **The sampling controller.** Admitted as an R1 scorer and as an out-of-distribution fallback. It never
   stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record: at about 0.33 and 1 GPU-minutes per 20 s episode
   (quadruped, humanoid) at a 20 ms planning step, and about 1.7 and 5.2 at 4 ms, a few hundred episodes cost
   more than training once (FACT, source register). Its own episodes are acceptance evidence only where it is
   the delivered controller, under a frozen planner record.
4. **Layer 1: the fixed table.** Inside each stratum, at each rung transition the top ⌈n/η⌉ designs by the
   lower rung's score advance, with η = 3 (81 → 27 → 9 → 3). Ties at the cut are broken by `DesignId`. The
   whole schedule (rungs, counts per transition, addresses per rung, budget per rung) is written to the ledger
   as a bracket ledger before the campaign starts; a transition that deviates from the written schedule is a
   refusal to proceed, not an adjustment. Its eliminations are rank-based and carry no e-process guarantee, and
   the record says so.
5. **Mode gating.** Before a transition from a rung, look up the stratum's mode for the rung pair:

   | Mode | Condition (all on the lower bootstrap bound) | What the lower rung may do |
   |---|---|---|
   | `selective` | Kendall τ on the top band ≥ 0.7, with the winner check passed; calibration valid (at least 19 audit designs, exchangeability martingale below 20); audited miss rate at most α | The conformal survivor rule of layer 2 |
   | `discard-only` | 0.5 ≤ τ < 0.7; or a fallback trigger of DC-02 or DC-03 fired | The fixed table: the top third advances, so the top quarter always does. Where a margin exists, a design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best is never eliminated. The audit lane keeps running |
   | `uninformative` | τ < 0.5; or the stratum is unmeasured; or the planted winner was lost in this stratum | Nothing. The rung is skipped in this stratum, its budget moves up one rung, and designs it eliminated earlier in the campaign are re-opened through the audit lane |

   Re-opening, as this layer does it (DESIGN): the state machine marks each design the rung eliminated earlier
   in the campaign `reopened` in the bracket ledger, with the mode change that caused it, and offers the list
   on the audit-lane interface; the screening-audit lane (N26b-01, which depends on this bead) consumes the
   list and runs it. Until that lane exists, a reopened design re-enters at the rung above at the next
   transition.

   An unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`: the default fails closed, and a rung without a rank-agreement
   record is refused (FROB-REF-537). The synthesized baseline controller as R1 has no exemption. R0 is outside
   the modes: it only refuses or excludes with typed bounds. A request that asks a rung to select finalists in
   a stratum whose mode is not `selective` is refused with **FROB-REF-545** (repairs: run the audit lane, or
   accept "k survivors").
6. **Wording.** Every allocation decision carries its mode, and finalists chosen under any mode other than
   `selective` are described as "k survivors", never as "the top k", in envelopes and reports. A string lint in
   the report templates enforces it.
7. **Identity reset (INV-ATEL-05).** Scores, calibration and rank-agreement rows are keyed by model identity,
   policy and kernel versions, effort profile and battery version. A change of any of them resets the affected
   rows; an attempt to combine rows across two identities is refused with **FROB-REF-548** (repair: restart the
   ledger rows in the new identity).
8. **Record.** Each `AllocationDecision` is an evidence record with the decision-policy identifier
   `atelier.dc02.advance` and its version, the ledger frontier it read, and, while an adaptive rule is in
   shadow, the action the adaptive rule would have taken beside the action taken.
9. **Class.** The decision is a pure function of the scores at a pinned ledger frontier, the schedule and the
   mode table (INV-ATEL-06: it reads no clock). D0 given scores.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The fixed table on 81 designs with η = 3 advances 27, 9 and 3, by score with `DesignId` tie-breaks, and
      the bracket ledger written before the campaign matches what ran.
- [ ] In an `uninformative` stratum R1 is skipped, its budget moves to R2, and nothing is discarded by R1; in a
      `discard-only` stratum the top third advances; a selection request there is refused (545).
- [ ] A rung pair with no rank-agreement record cannot discard (537); R0 exclusions are unaffected.
- [ ] Scores from a D4 run are refused as ladder inputs; a policy trained under D4 and evaluated under D2 is
      accepted.
- [ ] Changing the effort profile mid-campaign resets the stratum's rows; pooling old and new is refused (548).
- [ ] Reports of a campaign run under `discard-only` contain "survivors" and never "top" for its finalists.
- [ ] The decision record carries the policy id and version and replays to the same decision from the ledger.
- [ ] Worked cost case reproduced by the estimator: 81 designs at 10 s, 3 min and 30 min: 40.5 h if all are
      trained; 13.5 + 81 + 270 min ≈ 6.1 h under halving.

Step `fixed_table` of `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28, which reaches this bead

The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/cascade_seeded_population/<utc>.ndjson`; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block; per transition the
stratum, mode, rule, advanced and discarded counts against the schedule, reopened designs, spend in
GPU-seconds, the policy id and version and the ledger frontier; the bracket-ledger digest before and after the
run; on failure the first transition that deviates from the schedule and the paths to reproduce; a final
`summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the halving counts and the worked cost case. Negative controls: a mutant that lets an
`uninformative` rung discard (the planted-winner fixture of the cascade loses its winner; here the unit test
catches the discard itself); a mutant that advances by completion order; a mutant that silently pools rows
across identities. Determinism: decisions identical across worker counts and under random completion order.

## Considerations

- The fixed table's weakness is the one the adaptive layers address: it advances a fixed fraction whether or
  not the rung can separate the designs. Its strength is that it cannot be wrong about validity. Both are why
  it is the baseline.
- Evidence on the middle rungs (FACT, source register; all from other engines): within one morphology class a
  family policy reaches 70 to 85% of specialist return with no adaptation, and fine-tuning matches it after
  about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run; across topology-diverse designs the figures are 10 to 40% and a gain of
  two to three times; after a single-limb topology edit inheritance costs 33 to 100%. The share of a population
  that must leave R1 for R2 is therefore expected to be about the top quarter, not the top twentieth
  (HYPOTHESIS; settled by the rank-agreement gate).
- If KILL-RANK fires, R1 is `uninformative` everywhere: every candidate that survives static analysis is scored
  on the higher rung, and the ladder's benefit shrinks to stopping and elimination among trained candidates.
- OPEN-ATEL-2 (the definition of a stratum): default one stratum per morphology class.
