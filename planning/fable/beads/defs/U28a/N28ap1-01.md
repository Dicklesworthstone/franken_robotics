## Background

`frob design check` is the first stage of the studio loop and the cheapest: static analysis in seconds, before
any controller is synthesized or any world is stepped. It is also rung R0 of the evaluation ladder: it may
refuse a design or exclude it with a typed bound, and it never ranks survivors. Its purpose is feedback an agent
can act on at once: each refusal carries ranked repairs expressed as typed edits, which become the envelope's
`next_actions`.

The preflight has eight stages in a fixed order, each able to refuse the next: 1 Form (the model compile),
2 Mass, 3 Mechanism, 4 Motion, 5 Structure, 6 Statics and sizing, 7 Place, 8 Information. Stages 2 to 8 call
the studio's analyses (A-01 to A-26, each a typed finding with ranked repairs) and live in `frob-analyze`. The
stage table is one artifact, the compiled constant `STAGES` of the staging contract (N14b-10), with the second
table `CHECK_ANALYSES` for the analyses added later (N14bx-01). This bead is the machine that executes them: the
runner, the policy, aggregation, the global ranking of repairs, the budgets with `undecided`, the stage records
other verbs read, and the verb `design.check` itself with its schema and exit codes.

Why a separate bead from the gate capstone (BD-110). The runner needs only the two stage tables, the analysis
contract (N28a-04) and the compile (stage 1); it does not need any one analysis to exist, and it is tested on
the real compile plus `synthetic` analyses registered through the real contract. Building it apart lets it
proceed in parallel with the seven analysis beads and lets the verbs that need only the runner (the loop-order
rule N28a-02, the stage records `explain.design` reads, the M1 verb registration) start before every analysis
lands. BD-110 assembles the real analyses into it and measures GATE-ATEL-02 and GATE-SKEL-04. Rejected
designs: one monolithic validator (no stage attribution, no per-stage timing, no partial runs); warnings
instead of refusals (an invalid design is refused with a stable code, never repaired in silence).

## Scope

In: the stage runner; the policy file mechanism (`registries/preflight.toml`: per finding kind, refusing with
its code or reported) and its build-time agreement test; aggregation of findings; global repair ranking into
`next_actions`; the enforcement of per-stage budgets with `undecided`; the stage records written to the ledger;
the verb `design.check` with its schema, its exit codes and the caller's `--time-budget`; evidence of the
envelope; cancellation; passing the conformance suites of N14b-10 and N14bx-01.

Out, with the bead that has it: the stage tables, the status vocabulary, the semantics of `--stages`, `--all`
and `--strict-data`, the budget rules and the report notes (N14b-10); the rows, order, binding, stopping and exit
functions `plan_check`, `stop_after` and `exit_for` for A-17, A-18 and A-20 to A-24 (N14bx-01); the analyses
and their policy rows (BD-100 to BD-106, BD-108 and their owners); assembling the real stage-1-to-6 analyses
and the measurements of GATE-ATEL-02 and GATE-SKEL-04 (BD-110); stages 7 and 8 (BD-244); the memo graph
(BD-111); design lints, which are advisory and separate (BD-064); the loop-order rule that reads the stage
records (N28a-02).

## Technical approach

1. **Command.** `frob design check <design> [--task T] [--scene S] [--budget B] [--skill K] [--stages a-b]
   [--all] [--strict-data] [--time-budget ms] [--estimate] --json`. Stages run in order and stop at the first refusing finding, unless `--all` asks
   for every finding. `--stages 2-4` selects a contiguous range to run and report, with the semantics of the
   staging contract (N14b-10): every earlier stage is still required, because each stage is able to refuse the
   next; an earlier stage whose stored stage record is valid for this model is taken from the record, and one
   that has none is computed first; a refusing earlier stage ends the run before the range. (Running only stage
   1 before a range would let stage 4 answer on a design stage 2 refuses.) `--time-budget ms` is the caller's
   budget for the run; it bounds stage 8's search (default 10 s) and never raises a stage's own budget.
   `--task`, `--scene`, `--budget` and `--skill` bind the analyses registered "when bound" (A-18 with a scene,
   A-20 with a budget or a task segment with a rise, A-24 with a skill or task wrench). Registry row
   (VERBS-design): cluster `design`, kind `read`, tier S, capability `design:read`, schemas
   `frob.design.check.in/1` and `frob.design.check.out/1`, idempotent, first milestone M1, `estimable = true`
   with `cost_model = "estimated: --estimate returns the planned stages and rows with their budgets …"` (the
   exception VERBS-design makes to the `static:` rule for reads, because a bound scene or the information stage
   takes the call above one second). The verb's phase is `check`, declared in `registries/phases.toml`
   (N28a-03), not a row field.
   **Estimate (`--estimate`, the generated `estimate_only` property).** The runner calls `plan_check(req,
   landed)` and returns the plan priced and nothing else: per stage its status-to-be (`planned`, or `not-run`
   with its reason), its analyses with each row's budget, the stage budget, and the total, with stage 8 priced at
   the call's `--time-budget`; exit 0; no analysis runs, no stage record is read or written, and the memo graph
   is not touched. DESIGN: the estimate is a pure function of the request and the two tables, so an agent can
   price a scene-bound check before spending it.
   **Refusal list** (the chapter codes of VERBS-design's `design.check` block, 31 codes; the runner raises only
   a code owned by the refusing stage's row): stage 1 101, 102, 103, 105, 107, 108 (raised at binding, which is
   part of the compile: a power-port sign that disagrees with its motion witness, or an actuator on no bus or on
   two; N14b-10's row 1), 111, 114, 121, 125, 126, 130, 134; stage 2 104, 106, 168 (N13-04's solid-fill
   plausibility, through A-02, assembled by BD-110); stage 3 101, 107, 110, 123; stage 4 118; stage 5 108, 109;
   stage 6 113, 115, 116, 124, 131, 132, 133, with 551 and 448 from the bound A-20 and A-24; stage 7 119 only;
   stage 8 120. In order: 101 to 111, 113 to 116, 118 to 121, 123 to 126, 130 to 134, 168, 448, 551. A
   `FROB-REF-308` from the bound A-18's route query is not a code of stage 7's row: the stage reports it as
   status `undecided` with 308's repairs as `next_actions` (N14b-12, N14bx-01's `exit_for`), so 308 is in no
   list. The owned-codes test reads the constant, so a later amendment of a row needs no runner change.
   **The design input** is a name, a `DesignId` or a design file in either view (NS-01 runs the file form), so
   it is a path parameter of kind `artifact-input<F>` (NJL-01's field rule: a parameter that accepts a name or
   an identity as well as a path), and the generator appends the codes that rule gives, 734 (`path-outside-roots`)
   and 828 among them, to the row's chapter codes (VERBS-design counts `design.check` among its seven rows with a
   file parameter).
2. **Tables and policy.** The runner reads the stage table from `STAGES` (N14b-10,
   `crates/frob-analyze/src/preflight/stages.rs`: per stage its analyses, the refusal codes it owns, its budget,
   tier and needs) and the late analyses from `CHECK_ANALYSES` (N14bx-01). It holds no copy of either. Every
   decision about which analysis runs, in what order, whether it is bound, which later rows become
   `not-run: stopped` after a refusal, and which exit code an outcome gets is taken from N14bx-01's pure
   functions `plan_check(req, landed) -> CheckPlan`, `stop_after(plan, refused_at, all) -> CheckPlan` and
   `exit_for(outcome) -> u8`; the runner executes the plan and adds no order or binding of its own (N14bx-01's
   `runner_order_from_constants` test is compiled behind this bead's feature and must pass). DESIGN, settling
   two drafts (one kept the whole table as data in `registries/preflight.toml`; the staging contract kept it as
   a compiled constant): the contract is the constant, typed and changed only by a dated amendment, and
   `registries/preflight.toml` holds only what is meant to be switched without touching the runner, the `check`
   policy per finding kind (refusing with its code, or reported). A build-time test fails if the policy file
   names a stage or a code that the constants do not have (a refusing kind's code must be in its stage's
   `refuses`), names a finding kind of a second-table analysis that `CHECK_ANALYSES.kinds` lacks, or omits one
   it has. The first table's analyses carry no kind list in `STAGES`; their kinds are read from each registered
   analysis's `finding_kinds()` declaration (N28a-04), and the same build-time test fails on a declared kind of
   a registered stage analysis with no policy row, and on a row naming a kind no registered analysis declares.
   At run time a finding whose kind still has no policy row (an analysis compiled behind a feature the policy
   test's build did not enable) is reported at its own severity with the note `policy-missing`, never refusing
   and never dropped (DESIGN: fail-visible, since refusing needs a code the row would have named), and BD-110's
   conformance run over the real analyses fails on any `policy-missing`. An analysis
   joins a stage by its entry in the tables; the runner finds its implementation through the registration
   interface of the analysis contract (N28a-04), and an analysis registered "when bound" runs only when the
   request names the scene, budget or skill it needs (else `not-run: unbound` with the flag that binds it).
3. **Runner.** For stage s: run its planned analyses (independent ones in parallel on the job context's pool;
   results placed by index so order is deterministic), collect `AnalysisResult`s, apply policy. A refusing
   finding ends the run after the stage completes (all findings of the refusing stage are reported, not only the
   first); later rows become `not-run: stopped` by `stop_after`. Each stage returns its evidence class and the
   model identity.
4. **Budgets.** Each analysis receives the remainder of its stage budget; on exhaustion it returns `undecided`
   with what it had established. `undecided` is never a pass and never a refusal: the stage is reported
   `undecided`, later stages still run, the exit code is 2, and the stored stage record says `undecided`. Per
   stage the runner records wall time against the stage budget, and a stage over its budget is a `budget-miss`
   finding even when the total passes (the gate measurement that aggregates these is BD-110's).
5. **Aggregation.** The result carries: `summary` (total mass, closure status, symmetry groups, mobility,
   growth vector where applicable, each read from the stage that computed it); `stages[]` with status (`passed`,
   `refused`, `undecided`, `not-run`, the wire forms of N14b-10), evidence class, wall time and budget;
   `findings[]` merged across stages, each with its stage and analysis; and `not_run[]` with the reason
   (`unbound` with the binding flag, `stopped`, `excluded`, `not-landed`).
6. **Global repair ranking.** Repairs from all findings are merged: a repair that appears under several
   findings (equal canonical edit bytes) is listed once with all the findings it addresses. The merged list is
   sorted by a lexicographic key (DESIGN: a total order, so that the result bytes are identical run to run and
   across worker counts): (a) the number of refusing findings the repair clears, descending (a repair attached
   to no refusing finding clears zero and follows every one that clears at least one); (b) the earliest stage
   among the findings it addresses; (c) its best `rank_key` (N28a-04's four-part repair order) among those
   findings; (d) the edit's canonical bytes. The first five become `next_actions` as runnable `frob design
   edit …` commands, each with a one-line reason (DESIGN: an agent acts on the first; five bounds the
   envelope, and every repair stays listed under its findings). `design.edit` is a `mutate` verb, so each
   printed command carries `--expected-parent <the checked DesignId>` (the immutable identity echoed in the
   envelope's `bound`), so that a repair applies only to the version it was computed for and a moved head is the
   stale-parent conflict 804, exit 7; it carries no `--key`, which is left to the caller (a key printed here
   would make every agent that applies it replay the first answer; N14b-19's script appends `--key` only when
   the printed command has none).
7. **Refusal envelope** (`frob.envelope/1`, BD-022). On a refusal: `ok: false`, exit 3, `refusal {code,
   message, pointer (field path), observed, required, unit, affected_claims, retry: after-change, repairs}`; the
   envelope still carries the stage list and timings. With findings and no refusal: exit 2. Clean: exit 0. The
   code is always one of the refusing stage's owned codes; a refusal with no typed repair carries an explicit
   `no_repair_reason`.
8. **Stage record.** Written to the ledger (BD-090) per (`ModelId` restricted to the fields the stage read,
   analysis versions, bindings): `{stage, model_id, verdict: passed | refused | undecided, codes, finding ids,
   timings, budget}`. The loop-order rule (N28a-02) and `explain.design` read it; `--stages` reuses it. Written
   only when the stage completes; cancellation mid-stage writes nothing for that stage.
9. **Evidence.** The envelope's class is the weakest among the stages run (an exact analysis over an `assumed`
   card is `Estimated`); tier `static`; determinism `exact` when every analysis run was D0; `claims: []`.
10. **Cancellation.** The verb honours the job context's cancellation between and inside analyses (each
    analysis polls at its own safe points); a cancelled run returns the cancellation envelope with the stages
    completed so far and no partial stage record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The runner passes the conformance suite of the staging contract (N14b-10): fixed order; stop at the first
      refusal; `--all` returns findings from every stage; `--stages` ranges require every earlier stage (from
      its stage record, or computed); `undecided` on budget exhaustion with exit 2; `--strict-data` raising 113;
      every refusal with at least one typed repair; no out-of-row refusal.
- [ ] N14bx-01's `runner_order_from_constants` passes against this runner: on a request that binds everything
      the execution order equals `plan_check`'s; a planted second order in the runner fails it.
- [ ] `registries/preflight.toml` holds the policy only, and the build-time test fails on a policy row for a
      code outside its stage's row, on a finding kind the constants lack, on a missing row for a second-table
      kind, and on a missing row for a kind a registered first-table analysis declares in `finding_kinds()`.
- [ ] Repair merging and ranking: a repair clearing two refusing findings outranks one clearing one; every
      `next_actions` entry is a command that parses and applies with `frob design edit` once the caller adds a
      key, carries `--expected-parent` equal to the checked `DesignId`, and carries no `--key`.
- [ ] `frob design check <design> --estimate --json` returns `plan_check`'s plan with every row's budget and the
      total, exit 0, and runs nothing: no stage record is written and the memo graph's counters are unchanged.
- [ ] The generated refusal list of `design.check` equals VERBS-design's block (31 codes) plus the appended
      codes, and the runner never raises a code outside the refusing stage's row.
- [ ] Stage records are keyed by the fields each stage read: renaming a body leaves every record valid; a mass
      edit invalidates stages 2 and 6 and not stage 4.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json design check` with the registered schemas and exit codes 0, 2 and 3; a
      second call with unchanged inputs returns identical result bytes; 1, 4 and 16 workers give identical
      bytes; cancellation mid-run returns no partial stage record.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-analyze` documents the runner, the policy file and the stage-record schema.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-analyze/tests/preflight_runner.rs`, `cargo test -p frob-analyze --test preflight_runner`. Stage 1 is the
real compile (N14a-08); analyses of stages 2 to 6 are `synthetic` analyses registered through the real contract
(each refuses with a stated code when a stated fixture field is present, and otherwise passes), so that the
runner is tested before the real analyses land; BD-110 repeats the relevant cases with the real ones.
- `stops_at_first_refusing_stage`: a design with a stage-2 and a stage-5 defect reports only stage 2;
  `all_flag_reports_every_stage`: with `--all`, both.
- `stage_range_requires_earlier_stages`: with valid stage records for stages 1 to 3, `--stages 4-5` computes only
  4 and 5; with none, 1 to 3 are computed first; a design that stage 2 refuses is refused at stage 2 under
  `--stages 4-5`.
- `plan_from_constants`: the stages and analyses executed, and every `not-run` reason, equal `plan_check`'s on
  20 request shapes; `stop_after` and `exit_for` decide stopping and the exit code.
- `policy_file_agrees_with_stage_table`: the three planted policy defects fail the build-time test, and a
  fourth: a `synthetic` stage-2 analysis declaring `syn.extra` in `finding_kinds()` with no policy row.
- `unlisted_kind_is_policy_missing`: a `synthetic` stage-2 analysis emitting a kind with no policy row yields a
  reported finding with the note `policy-missing`, exit 2, never 3, and the finding is in `findings[]`.
- `refusing_stage_reports_all_its_findings`.
- `undecided_is_exit_2_and_recorded`: a `synthetic` analysis with a 0 ms budget.
- `owned_codes_only`: a stage that emits a code it does not own fails the test.
- `repair_merge_and_rank`: one repair clearing two refusing findings outranks one clearing one; a repair listed
  under two findings appears once with both; between two repairs that each clear one refusing finding, the one
  addressing stage 2 precedes the one addressing stage 5; a repair of a reported (non-refusing) finding follows
  both; with seven merged repairs, `next_actions` holds the first five.
- `next_actions_runnable`: each command parses and applies (`frob design edit` on the fixture, with a key
  appended); each carries `--expected-parent` equal to the checked `DesignId` and no `--key`; applied after the
  design's head has moved, it is 804, exit 7.
- `estimate_runs_nothing`: `--estimate` on a scene-bound request returns the planned stages with budgets (stage
  8 priced at `--time-budget`), exit 0; the ledger holds no new stage record and no `synthetic` analysis was
  called (each counts its calls).
- `refusal_list_equals_registry`: the generated list of `design.check` equals the 31 codes of VERBS-design's
  block plus the appended codes (734 and 828 among them, for the design-file input); 108 is owned by stages 1
  and 5, 168 by stage 2, and 308 by no stage.
- `stage_record_key_ignores_untouched_fields`.
- `evidence_min_over_stages`: an `assumed` card field makes the envelope `Estimated`.
- `deterministic_bytes_parallel`: 1, 4 and 16 workers give identical result bytes.
- `cancel_leaves_no_partial_record`.
- `envelope_shapes`: a refusal envelope has every field of item 7; exit 0, 2 and 3 on clean, findings-only and
  refusing fixtures.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_check.sh` (bead N28a-29) drives the real `frob` binary; this bead contributes its runner
steps: `frob --json design check` with `--all`, with `--stages 4-5` on a design whose stage-2 record exists and
on one that stage 2 refuses, with a 1 ms `--time-budget` on stage 8 (no effect on stages 1 to 6), with
`--estimate` (the plan and budgets, exit 0, no stage record written), and a cancelled run. The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/atelier_check/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript. Logged per call: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall time, per-stage status and time against budget, `not-run`
reasons, the refusal code and pointer, the first three repairs, the stage records read and written (with their
key digests), the evidence block; on failure the first stage whose status or order differs from `plan_check`
and the paths to reproduce; a summary with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the option matrix of N14b-10 and the planning cases of N14bx-01 (order A-23, A-22, A-21, A-20 in
stage 6; `--stages 1-5` marks the late rows `not-run: excluded`). Negative controls: a mutant runner that
continues past a refusing stage without `--all`; a mutant that reports `undecided` as passed (the loop-order
tests then let a simulation through, which they detect); a mutant that drops `next_actions`; a mutant with a
hand-written order in the runner; a mutant that writes a stage record before the stage completes (caught by the
cancellation test). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across
platforms (D0 analyses byte-identical).

## Considerations

- KILL-STATIC (decided at M3 on the design corpus): if static verdicts predict simulated failure no better than
  a mass-and-torque heuristic, `design check` verdicts are downgraded to advisory lints and the pruning claim is
  withdrawn. The policy file is where that switch lives; nothing else in the runner changes.
- The runner must not become a second home of the stage table: any order, binding or exit rule found in the
  runner's code is a contract violation, caught by the tests above.
- Warm targets after a one-parameter edit (0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 s at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile) belong to
  the memo-graph gate, not to this bead.
- Stage records are evidence that other verbs act on (the loop-order rule refuses a simulation on a refused
  design); a stale record is a safety defect, so the key is derived from the fields read, never hand-written.
