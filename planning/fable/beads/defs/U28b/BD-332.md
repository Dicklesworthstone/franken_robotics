## Background

Analysis A-04 answers: which gaits can this body have, with what phase lags? It replaces hand-tuned
phase-offset tables per robot with two pieces of algebra (construction C-10, tier Frontier).

*Gaits are subgroup pairs.* A periodic gait of a symmetric system has a group H of spatio-temporal symmetries
(elements that map the motion to itself up to a shift in time) and a normal subgroup K of purely spatial
symmetries (no shift). H/K is cyclic (Buono and Golubitsky); if it has order m, a generator shifts time by 1/m
of the stride. Enumerating the pairs (H, K) with K normal in H and H/K cyclic enumerates the *admissible gait
types* of a body. Theorem card T-25 states that the listed pairs are exactly those pairs.

*Timing is max-plus linear.* In the max-plus algebra a ⊕ b = max(a, b), a ⊗ b = a + b, and ε = −∞ is the zero.
A footfall schedule is x(k + 1) = A ⊗ x(k), with x(k) the touchdown times of stride k. For irreducible A the
eigenvalue λ of A ⊗ x = λ ⊗ x is the maximum cycle mean of A and is the stride period; an eigenvector gives
the steady phase offsets (Lopes et al.).

Where it sits: `frob-gait` (atlas, schedules) on top of `frob-algebra` (subgroup lattice, cosets). Consumers:
the legged baseline controllers, which take their gait clock from the atlas; reward shaping, which takes phase
references; the orbit solver, which seeds shooting from the phase lags and restricts it to Fix(K); the
screening audit, whose planted winner may be "a gait outside the atlas"; North-Star scenario NS-06 through the
cascade's screening stage. Kill gate that concerns the consumers: KILL-GAIT (orbits fail to rank designs).
Its fallback keeps this bead whole: the atlas keeps supplying clocks and reward references and no design is
ranked by an orbit. BET-26's fallback is the same ("the gait table and max-plus timing analysis").

Which group is the subtle point and the reason for the design. The body's own group is small: the
leg-permutation part of Γ_obs (the observation-preserving symmetry group of the design and task), intersected
with the isotropy subgroup of the commanded velocity. A quadruped commanded forward keeps only the left–right
mirror C₂, under which trot, pace and walk are one class and bound and pronk another. The six primary quadruped
gaits are separated only by the symmetry of a **declared leg-clock network** (ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ for the eight-cell
network of Golubitsky et al.), which contains permutations no isometry of the body realizes. It is a symmetry
of the controller's coupling, not of the body, and the atlas must say which group produced each pair.
Rejected alternatives: one hard-coded gait table per archetype (the thing being replaced; kept only as the M1
stopgap of the baseline controller); using the mechanical group Γ_mech (a forward lidar breaks it, and a policy
tied under it is wrong); reporting clock-network gaits as body symmetries (a reader would take them for
properties of the mechanism).

Seed done-condition: trot, pace and bound appear as (H, K) pairs for the quadruped and the tripod gait for the
hexapod; GATE-ATEL-02 complete for A-01 to A-14.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** A design (resolved to `DesignId`, `ModelId`), optionally a task (for Γ_obs) and a commanded
velocity class (`forward`, `turn-in-place`, `none`), optionally a network name. Two ambient groups are used and
both are always labelled in the output:

- `body`: G_b = (leg-permutation image of Γ_obs) ∩ Stab(command). Computed from the legs' attachment ports and
  the generators of Γ_obs. With no task bound, Γ_obs is unknown and the verb refuses to substitute Γ_mech; it
  returns the atlas for the declared network only and says the body group is `unbound`.
- `network`: a declared group acting on *cells*, with a map from cells to legs and one primary cell per leg.

**2. Network declaration (DESIGN, `registries/gait-networks.toml`).**

```toml
[[network]]
name = "quadruped-8cell"            # Golubitsky et al.: Z4 x Z2 on eight cells, two per leg
legs = ["LH", "RH", "LF", "RF"]
cells = 8
generators = { omega = "(0 2 4 6)(1 3 5 7)", kappa = "(0 1)(2 3)(4 5)(6 7)" }   # omega: hind to fore along a side; kappa: left-right
cell_leg = ["LH", "RH", "LF", "RF", "LH", "RH", "LF", "RF"]
primary_cell = { LH = 0, RH = 1, LF = 2, RF = 3 }
```

Registered at this bead: `quadruped-8cell` (ℤ₄ × ℤ₂) and `hexapod-12cell` (ℤ₆ × ℤ₂: ω of order 6 advances along
a side through two cells per leg, κ exchanges sides; legs L1, L2, L3, R1, R2, R3). A network is admitted only
if its generators are permutations of the cells, the group acts transitively on cells, and every leg has a
primary cell.

**3. Enumeration (numbered).**
1. Build the subgroup lattice of the ambient group from its generators (`frob-algebra`).
2. For each subgroup H and each subgroup K ≤ H: keep the pair when K is normal in H and H/K is cyclic. The
   test is exact (coset multiplication tables); order m = |H|/|K|.
3. For each kept pair choose the generator σ of H/K that is least in the canonical order of cosets; the
   homomorphism θ: H → ℚ/ℤ sends h to j/m when hK = σʲ. The other φ(m) − 1 generators give the time-reversed
   and re-indexed variants and are listed as `variants`, not as separate gait types.
4. Phase of a leg: with reference cell c₀ and g_ℓ any element taking c₀ to the leg's primary cell, the phase is
   θ(g_ℓ) mod 1 for legs in the H-orbit of the reference. The choice of g_ℓ does not matter exactly when the
   stabilizer of the cell lies in K; when an element that fixes a leg's cell has a non-zero shift, the leg's
   signal has a shorter period and the entry is marked `multi-beat` with that period.
5. Legs in different H-orbits have no phase relation fixed by symmetry; the entry lists the orbits and marks
   the relation `free`.
6. Name the entry from the registry of known phase vectors (step 4 below); an unnamed pair keeps its (H, K)
   label.

**4. Named gaits (the registry rows the done-condition reads).** Phases in fractions of the stride.

| Network | Gait | K | H/K | Phases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `quadruped-8cell`, legs (LH, RH, LF, RF) | pronk | ⟨ω, κ⟩ | trivial | (0, 0, 0, 0) |
| | pace | ⟨ω⟩ | ℤ₂, κ ↦ ½ | (0, ½, 0, ½) |
| | bound | ⟨ω², κ⟩ | ℤ₂, ω ↦ ½ | (0, 0, ½, ½) |
| | trot | ⟨κω⟩ | ℤ₂, ω ↦ ½, κ ↦ ½ | (0, ½, ½, 0) |
| | jump | ⟨κ⟩ | ℤ₄, ω ↦ ¼ | (0, 0, ¼, ¼) |
| | walk | ⟨κω²⟩ | ℤ₄, ω ↦ ¼, κ ↦ ½ | (0, ½, ¼, ¾) |
| `hexapod-12cell`, legs (L1, L2, L3, R1, R2, R3) | tripod | ⟨ω², κω⟩ (order 6) | ℤ₂, ω ↦ ½, κ ↦ ½ | (0, ½, 0, ½, 0, ½) |

Counts the enumeration must reproduce (DESIGN arithmetic by counting homomorphisms into ℚ/ℤ up to
automorphism of the image): ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ has 8 subgroups and 23 admissible pairs, 6 of them with H the whole group;
ℤ₆ × ℤ₂ has 8 pairs with H the whole group (quotients of order 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 6, 6, 6). With the body group C₂
alone the pairs are (C₂, C₂), (C₂, 1) and (1, 1), and the atlas reports "trot, pace, walk: one class (left
and right half a stride apart)" and "bound, pronk: one class (left and right in phase)". For a radial hexapod
with no command, the body's rotation ρ by one leg gives the tripod as (C₆, ⟨ρ²⟩) with phases alternating
(0, ½, 0, ½, 0, ½). The names come from the literature (Golubitsky, Stewart, Buono and Collins 1999) and are
FACT to be re-read from the primary source before a gate cites them; the enumeration does not depend on them.

**5. Max-plus timing.** For a gait whose legs fall into n phase groups in cyclic order, with swing (flight)
time τ_f, minimum stance time τ_g and overlap (double-stance) time τ_Δ, the touchdown times t₁ … t_n obey
t_i(k + 1) = max(t_i(k) + τ_g + τ_f, t_{i−1}(k + 1) + τ_Δ + τ_f), with t₀(k + 1) read as t_n(k). Eliminating the
same-stride terms in order gives the matrix A. The atlas computes, in exact rationals: λ, the maximum cycle
mean (Karp's algorithm locally; `fs-tropical::max_cycle_mean` through the airlock when admitted, with both
required to agree: the timing block carries `cross_check: agree | disagree | absent`, N33bx-03's rule 4;
`disagree` withholds that schedule's timing block and names both values; `absent`, when the entry has no valid
admission record at the pin, names the reason and leaves the exact value in force); an eigenvector by the critical-graph construction; the duty factor τ_g′/λ where τ_g′ is the
realized stance; and the *transient*: the number of strides after which x(k + 1) = λ ⊗ x(k) holds when started
from the steady state of another gait (iterated to a cap of 64 strides; this is the bound on transition time
between gaits). Closed form for this family, checked against the matrix result: λ = max(τ_g + τ_f,
n·(τ_f + τ_Δ)). The eigenvector is unique up to an additive constant only when the critical graph is strongly
connected; otherwise the atlas reports the interval of admissible offsets instead of one value.

**6. Output.** Schema `frob.gait.atlas.out/1`: `{ design, model, groups: { body: {generators, order, source:
"Γ_obs ∩ Stab(command)" | "unbound"}, network: {name, order} }, entries: [ { group: "body" | "network", H, K,
m, name?, phases: {leg: rational}, variants, multi_beat?, free_orbits?, schedule?: { tau_f, tau_g, tau_delta,
lambda_s, offsets_s | offset_interval_s, duty, transient_strides } } ], limits: [...] }`. The `limits` array
always carries the sentence that the enumeration lists admissible symmetry types, not realized gaits.
Evidence: claim kind Known answer for the fixtures and Design comparison never; class `Certified` for the
enumeration when its T-25 certificate is attached and accepted (step 8a; otherwise `Tested`), determinism D0,
tier `static`. The atlas is a
memo node keyed by the model identity restricted to the fields it reads (leg ports, Γ_obs generators), the
network record, the command class and the analysis version.

**7. Verb.** `gait.atlas`, kind `read`, first milestone M4, cluster `gait`. `frob gait atlas <design> [--task T]
[--command forward] [--network quadruped-8cell] [--timing tau_f=1/5,tau_g=3/10,tau_delta=1/20] --json`.
Refusals: an unknown network name is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3 (the `--network` argument is an enumeration
generated from `registries/gait-networks.toml`, so the schema rejects it; repair: `frob gait atlas --help` lists
the registered networks); a network that fails admission never reaches the verb, because the registry is
checked in continuous integration and a failing row blocks the build; a network whose legs do not match the
design's leg ports, or an ambient group outside the supported families (a constituent of quaternionic type),
is `FROB-REF-214`, exit 3, carrying the group's order and generators and, for a leg mismatch, both leg lists
(repair: the networks registered for this leg count). DESIGN: the registry row of `gait.atlas` lists 214 as its
only chapter refusal, and a declared group that does not act on this body's legs is a group outside the
supported families for it. Exit 0 with entries; 2 when the body group is `unbound`.

**8a. The certificate.** For every atlas the verb emits a `group-enumeration` proof object bound to theorem
card T-25 (CARD-T-25): the ambient group's generators as permutations (of legs for the body group, of cells
for a declared network, with the declaration recorded as an assumption of the bundle), every listed (H, K)
pair by generators, the cyclic generator of H/K and the resulting phase lags as exact rationals. The checker
of that proof kind (N12-18) closes the group, enumerates every subgroup, and accepts only when the derived set
of pairs equals the claimed set. The evidence class is `Certified` exactly when the certificate is attached
and accepted; otherwise the atlas is `Tested` (the registry's *static* label) and says why. The card's law
tests run in this bead's test suite, and the receipt produced here moves the card's registry row for the
atlas output.

**8. The gate record.** GATE-ATEL-02 (TARGET): each analysis A-01 to A-14 passes its known-answer tests, and
`design check` finishes in under 2 s at the 95th percentile on templates. Its M1 part is recorded by the
analyses that exist then. This bead records the M4 completion: one row per analysis A-01 to A-14 with the
claim identifier of its known-answer pass, A-04's own rows from this bead, and the measured 95th-percentile
`design check` time over the eighteen templates (A-04 is not part of `check`; the measurement shows it did not
join it).

**9. Registration as analysis A-04 (the row `analyze.gait`).** The same code is registered in `frob-analyze` as
the `Analysis` A-04 under the contract of N28a-04 (`crates/frob-analyze/src/analyses/a04_gait.rs`, a thin view
that calls the atlas function of `frob-gait`; no second implementation). N28a-05's generator emits the row
`analyze.gait` from this registration, and it must equal the row VERBS-analyze has on file (`read`, tier F,
first milestone M4, `analyze:read`, `frob.analyze.gait.in/1` and `frob.analyze.gait.out/1`, owner
`frob-analyze`, example `frob analyze gait quad-a --json`). The registration (DESIGN where the row leaves the
spelling to the implementing bead):

- `ID = "A-04"`, `VERSION = 1` (raised by any change to the enumeration, the named-gait table or the network
  registry's schema, so stale memo entries are invalidated); `tier()` Frontier; `screening()` true (the row's
  standing label: the orbit part is a screening signal, and the atlas lists admissible types, not realized
  gaits); `encloses()` empty and `bound` never set (A-04 excludes nothing).
- Inputs: the design; the common `--task` binding (for Γ_obs; its commanded velocity gives the command class,
  `forward` without one); `--controller <ControllerId>` (optional; read by the orbit part); `--param
  <name>=<a>..<b>` (optional; the design parameter of the continuation part). The network is the one registered
  for the design's leg count (`quadruped-8cell` for four legs, `hexapod-12cell` for six); `analyze.gait` has no
  `--network` and no `--timing`: choosing another network or the max-plus constants is `gait.atlas`'s.
- `reads()`: the leg attachment ports, the generators of Γ_obs of the bound task, and nothing else at this bead
  (BD-333 adds the fields its orbit solve reads), so the memo key is that of section 6 plus the parameters.
- `run`: `AnalysisResult.output` = `{ atlas, orbits, continuation }`, where `atlas` is the `frob.gait.atlas.out/1`
  body of section 6 computed with the inputs above (with the certificate's hash, so a reader can still verify
  the enumeration with `frob-verify`), `orbits` reads `not-run` naming BD-333 until BD-333 fills it (orbit
  existence, Floquet multipliers, residual), and `continuation` reads `not-run` naming BD-335 until BD-335 fills
  it. The schema is unchanged when they fill. Evidence: the *analysis* label of VERBS-analyze with
  `screening = true` and class `Estimated` for the result as a whole; determinism D0 for the atlas part.
- Findings: `A-04.body-group-unbound` (Info; no task bound, so only the network's pairs are listed; repair: bind
  the task with `--task`) and `A-04.no-network` (Info; no network is registered for the leg count, zero legs
  included, so only the body pairs are listed; `repairs: []` with that reason). As every analysis verb, it
  reports a finding with exit 2 and never refuses on one.
- Declared refusals: `FROB-REF-431` (a gait derivative requested at grazing or at a fold), raised only by the
  orbit and continuation parts once BD-333 and BD-335 land but declared here so that the generated row equals
  the row on file from the day this bead lands; the generator appends `FROB-REF-551`. The atlas part raises no
  code under this verb: the network is chosen to match the legs, so `FROB-REF-214` cannot arise.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gait_atlas.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and runs the commands of the acceptance list
on the shipped templates, plus the body-group form with `--task` bound and unbound, plus the timing form. It
asserts the named pairs, the phase vectors as exact rationals, the pair counts, λ with its unit and
`cross_check: agree` on the timing form (the user-side check that N33bx-03 leaves to this script), exit codes 0
and 2, the evidence block (class `Certified`, determinism `exact`), `frob-verify` accepting the atlas's
certificate and rejecting a copy with one pair removed, and the two refusals (802 and 214, exit 3). Step group
`analyze-*` runs `frob analyze gait templates/quad-mammal --task <reference task> --json` and asserts that its
`output.atlas.entries` equal the `gait.atlas` entries of the same inputs, `screening: true` and the two

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-072 (quadruped gait atlas): the pairs recover the primary gaits; H/K cyclic; enumeration exhaustive on
  the lattice (T-25). Tolerance exact; tier G1, host. Control set by the construction's specification: the
  cyclic-quotient test replaced by an abelian-quotient test. Every quotient of the abelian group ℤ₄ × ℤ₂ is
  abelian, so the mutant also admits (whole group, ⟨ω²⟩) (quotient ℤ₂ × ℤ₂), (whole group, {e}) (quotient
  ℤ₄ × ℤ₂) and (⟨ω², κ⟩, {e}) (quotient the Klein group): it reports 8 pairs with H the whole group instead of 6
  and 26 pairs in all instead of 23; the test must fail on that mutant.
- KA-073 (max-plus timing of a trot schedule): the eigenvalue equals the stride period computed by hand
  (1/2 s). Control: the eigenvalue taken as the largest entry of A gives 3/4 s and must be rejected.
- CX-034: an orbit judged stable inside Fix(K) can hide transverse instability; the atlas therefore states
  that it lists types and makes no stability statement.
- Negative control for the group choice: with a forward lidar fitted, an atlas computed under Γ_mech (four
  elements) lists pairs the closed loop does not have; the verb must use the two-element observation-preserving
  subgroup (the A-03 known answer KA-680).
- Determinism: exact arithmetic, D0; identical bytes twice, across worker counts, debug and release, and on
  both host platforms.

## Considerations

- Reading the atlas as a list of gaits the robot can perform is the main misuse; the limit sentence and the
  `group` label on every entry are the countermeasures.
- The gait rows of the M2 fidelity battery and the M1 baseline controller need gait clocks before M4; they use
  the baseline controller's built-in table until this bead lands, and the battery is then extended by a
  recorded revision.
- Reducible schedules (a leg group that never waits for another) have several eigenvalues; the atlas reports
  the components instead of one period.
- The atlas costs milliseconds; no performance gate applies beyond staying out of `design check`.
- KILL-GAIT (decided at M4 on the orbit-screening gate) removes orbit ranking and leaves this bead's output in
  use as clocks and reward references.
- `analyze.gait` and `gait.atlas` are not two spellings of one verb: the registry (VERBS-analyze) settles that
  the `analyze` verb is the studio's finding-shaped view with its own output schema, calling this computation
  (section 9), while `gait.atlas` keeps the network and timing choices and the `Certified` class of the
  enumeration. An agent that needs those reaches for `gait.atlas`; `analyze.gait` is what a design review
  lists next to the other analyses.
