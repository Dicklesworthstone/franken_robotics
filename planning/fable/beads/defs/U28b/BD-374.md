## Background

BET-10 is the bet that agents design better with structure than with reward alone: that the typed studio
surface (static checks with ranked repairs, explanations, honest comparisons, lineage, the cascade) lets an
agent reach an accepted design sooner than the same agent given only a simulator and a scalar score. It is one
of the levers of the ten-fold thesis, and the plan's pre-mortem names its failure in advance ("structure
helped agents only slightly"), which is why the gate needs an effect size and not merely a win. This bead is
the registered experiment that decides it. Tier: Solid (it is a measurement; the mechanisms it measures have
their own tiers).

GATE-ATEL-03 (TARGET; earliest M4): the pre-registered A/B of agent surfaces on the design benchmark, with an
anytime-valid verdict and an effect size: restricted mean time to an accepted answer lower by a factor of at
least 1.5 on at least ten briefs. The incumbent arm (the same agent on a composed layer of incumbent tools)
belongs to the ten-fold test and is not part of this gate. BET-10 words its aim as "at most half the
restricted mean time" and its falsifier as "one-sided 95% lower bound on the ratio above 1.5": the aim is a
ratio of 2, and the gate passes when the lower bound exceeds 1.5.

There is one A/B, not two. The cascade is reachable by hand through the studio's verbs, through `opt.cascade`,
and through the agent-facing one-call form `solve.run`. The one-verb arm of this experiment is the second arm
of GATE-TETHER-13, and KILL-SOLVE (the one-call pipeline does no better than agents driving the verbs) is
decided on it. Running the same briefs twice under two registrations would spend sealed briefs twice and give
two answers to one question.

Terms. A *brief* is one design assignment of the benchmark with its acceptance rule (N28b-10). A *campaign* is
one agent session on one brief in one arm. The *clock* T runs from the moment the agent receives the brief to
the moment the acceptance lane accepts its first correct answer; failed runs, training, compilation, waiting
and rejected submissions are inside it. The *restricted mean time* at deadline H is E[min(T, H)]: a campaign
not accepted by H costs H. Rejected alternatives for the metric: mean score reached (rewards an arm for
lowering the bar); success rate alone (ignores time); an average of per-brief ratios (dominated by the briefs
an arm finishes fastest and blind to the brief it never finishes).

Seed done-condition: GATE-ATEL-03.

## Scope

In: executing the registered protocol on the sealed briefs; the three arms' campaigns; the statistic, the
verdict and the lower bound; exclusions; the planted-defect control's count; the gate record; what is handed
to the KILL-SOLVE decision; the consequence for the surface if the bet fails.

Also run here, in the same keyed launch order as the three main arms: the ablation arms registered with them
(the full surface with one task cluster withheld per arm), whose definition, eligibility rule, statistic and
per-cluster effect table are bead N28b-43, a dependency. The per-verb usage this run records is a
description; which clusters showed an effect is read from that table.

Out: the benchmark's briefs and acceptance rule (N28b-10); the registration document, the arm definitions and
the surface-restriction mechanism (N28b-11); the ablation arms' protocol and analysis (N28b-43); `solve.run` (BD-372) and the KILL-SOLVE decision (N32a-32, which reads this bead's (F, S) record); the decision of BET-10 itself (its decision bead, which reads the gate record and the ablation table); the incumbent
arm and the ten-fold gate (the ten-fold beads); Autopsy's own agent comparison on planted defects (BD-375).

## Technical approach

**1. Arms (defined and frozen by N28b-11).** The same agent model, version and system prompt in every arm;
only the tool surface differs.

| Arm | Tool surface | Role |
|---|---|---|
| M (minimal) | `design.new`, `design.show`, `design.edit`, `sim.run` reduced to a scalar score for the brief's task, `eval.qualify` for submission, and the three always-listed base tools `session_focus`, `job_wait`, `job_cancel` | The comparator of BET-10: reward alone |
| F (full) | The full MCP surface in its task clusters, driven verb by verb; `solve.run` withheld | The treatment of GATE-ATEL-03 |
| S (one verb) | Arm F plus `solve.run` | Read by GATE-TETHER-13's second arm and KILL-SOLVE |

Equal resources per campaign in every arm: the same GPU-second and CPU-second budgets, the same agent token
budget, the same deadline H, no human intervention (any intervention is logged and voids the campaign).

**2. Design of the run.** n briefs (at least ten sealed briefs after exclusions; the registration plans twelve
or more) × r campaigns per brief per arm (DESIGN default r = 2, with campaign seeds fixed in the
registration; r is not the ratio c of section 3). A *pair* for the gate is (M, F) on the same brief with the same campaign index, accepted on the
same sealed world clusters. The brief is the unit of inference. Campaigns are launched in the registration's
keyed and balanced order (N28b-11, step 4: the (brief, index) blocks in a keyed permutation, the arms inside each
block in a keyed balanced schedule; a plain permutation of (brief, index, arm) does not guarantee balance), so
no arm systematically runs on a warmer cache; every arm starts
from the same warm state (kernels compiled, memo store empty of the brief's designs), and the state is
recorded.

**3. The statistic (anytime-valid).** For pair j let m_j = min(T_M,j, H) and f_j = min(T_F,j, H). For a ratio c
define d_j(c) = m_j − c·f_j, which lies in [−c·H, H]; that span is declared in the registration before the
first campaign, as the comparison engine requires for an e-process. The null "the restricted mean of M is at
most c times that of F" is E[d(c)] ≤ 0. A betting e-process on d_j(c)/((1 + c)·H), consumed in the registered
pair order (never in completion order), rejects when it reaches 1/α with α = 0.05. The gate's verdict is the
rejection at c = 1.5. The effect size is the pooled ratio R̂ = Σ m_j ÷ Σ f_j (a ratio of sums, not a mean of
ratios) with its one-sided 95% lower bound L, the largest c on the registered grid {1.0, 1.1, …, 3.0} such
that the e-process of every grid point up to and including c has rejected (the maximum of the rejected
prefix; a rejection, once reached, stands). This needs no monotonicity between the grid's e-processes: if the
true ratio is ρ, L ≥ ρ requires the e-process at the smallest grid point at or above ρ, a true null, to have
reached 1/α, which by Ville's inequality happens with probability at most α at any stopping time; so L is a
valid one-sided 95% bound without a correction for the 21 grid points. If no grid point has rejected, L is
reported as `< 1.0`. The run may stop early when c = 1.5 is rejected and at least ten briefs have a completed pair, or at
futility as registered (the e-process for c = 1.0 has not exceeded 1 after all planned pairs), or when the
planned pairs are exhausted.

**4. Censoring and exclusions (decided before times are compared).** A campaign not accepted by H contributes
H. A pair in which both arms are censored stays in both sums. A harness fault that is not the arm's own is
repaired by re-running the whole pair once under the next campaign index; a second fault excludes the pair for
every arm and is counted. More than 5% of pairs excluded makes the result descriptive only. No run is dropped
because its outcome is inconvenient.

**5. Correctness is a gate, not a term.** An answer counts only when the benchmark's acceptance lane accepts
it (sealed world clusters, pre-committed submission, level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] for the i-th submission). An
answer accepted and later shown wrong within its scope scores that brief as unresolved at H for its arm.

**6. Columns recorded beside the clock.** Per campaign: GPU-hours, CPU-hours, agent tokens, tool calls, serial
agent turns, submissions and rejections, the phase ledger (authoring, check, synthesis, simulation, training,
promotion, review, wait), refusals met by code, and which verbs were called. The per-verb usage in arm F is
printed beside the per-cluster effect table of the ablation arms (N28b-43); the fallback reads the table,
and reads the usage only as a description where a cluster was not tested.

**7. The planted-defect control (BET-10's control).** On the benchmark's planted-defect briefs (a starting
design from the corpus with one planted defect), the number of serial turns from brief to an accepted fix is
counted per arm. It is reported beside the main result. It is a potency check: if arm F does not fix a planted
knee-actuator defect in fewer turns than arm M, the surface's most direct advantage is absent and the main
result is read with that in view.

**8. Outcomes, written in advance.**

| Result | Record | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| c = 1.5 rejected on at least ten briefs | GATE-ATEL-03 PASS with R̂ and L | BET-10 stands for the benchmark's strata; the surface keeps its speed claim there |
| Run completes, L ≤ 1.5 | GATE-ATEL-03 FAIL with R̂ and L | BET-10's written consequence, carried out by the bet's decision bead from the per-cluster table of N28b-43: the default visible surface shrinks to the clusters labelled `effect shown`; verbs in clusters labelled `no effect shown` or `not tested` stay callable as audit tools and carry no speed claim; the per-verb usage is printed beside the table as a description only; the ten-fold record states which lever was lost |
| Fewer than ten usable briefs, or over 5% exclusions | `descriptive`, gate not decided | Re-run needs new sealed briefs |

**9. Hand-off for KILL-SOLVE.** The same statistic is computed for (F, S) on serial turns per accepted design
and on restricted mean time and written to the record that the KILL-SOLVE decision bead (N32a-32) reads; this bead does not decide it.

**10. Artifacts.** `docs/delivery/experiments/surface-ab/` holds the registration hash, the run manifest, the
per-campaign records, the analysis notebook output as canonical JSON, and the gate receipt. Transcripts are
kept; briefs stay sealed until the receipt is written. Claim kind: Design comparison; class Tested;
adjudication `self-adjudicated` (the acceptance lane is ours), which the receipt states.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test surface_ab_stats` (the analysis code, on `synthetic` records):

- `pooled_ratio_is_ratio_of_sums`: times M = (60, 60, 240) min, F = (30, 30, 240) min, H = 240 → R̂ = 360/300 =
  1.2 (the mean of per-brief ratios would be 1.67 and is not reported).
- `censoring_charges_deadline`: an arm that never finishes one brief is charged H for it.
- `eprocess_null_calibrated`: 10⁴ synthetic runs with true ratio exactly 1.5: the c = 1.5 e-process rejects in
  at most 5% under optional stopping.
- `eprocess_power_at_two`: with true ratio 2 and the registered n, the rejection frequency is reported and
  compared with the registration's stated power.
- `lower_bound_is_rejected_prefix`: with rejections at c = 1.0 … 1.6 and also at 1.8 but not at 1.7, L = 1.6
  (the isolated rejection at 1.8 is not used); with no rejection L is `< 1.0`.
- `lower_bound_coverage`: 10⁴ synthetic runs at true ratio 1.75 under optional stopping: L ≥ 1.8 (the first
  grid point above the truth) in at most 5% of runs.
- `order_invariance`: shuffling completion order leaves every e-value unchanged.
- `exclusion_rule`: a second harness fault removes the pair from every arm; 3 of 48 pairs excluded (6.25%)
  yields `descriptive`.
- `span_must_predate_data`: an analysis invoked with a span different from the registered one is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/surface_ab_dryrun.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and runs the whole pipeline on two
*development* briefs with a scripted agent (a fixed tool-call script per arm, so the script is cheap and
deterministic): open each arm's session with `frob session open --scopes … --budget … --key <campaign key>
--json` behind the agent proxy configured from the arm's profile (N28b-11), drive the calls, submit to
the acceptance lane, compute the statistic, write a dry-run receipt. It asserts that arm M's session cannot
list or call a withheld tool, that the clocks include the rejected submission, that the receipt cites the
registration hash, and that the same script re-run gives identical e-values. Log
`target/e2e-logs/surface_ab_dryrun/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
blocks, per-campaign clock and resource columns with units, the e-value per grid point, R̂ and L against 1.5,
for arm S per brief the turns, accepted designs, turns per accepted design and the (F, S) ratio, and on failure
the first campaign whose record is incomplete. Final `summary` line naming GATE-ATEL-03, GATE-TETHER-13 (second
arm) and KILL-SOLVE. The
registered run itself is launched by `scripts/exp/surface_ab_run.sh` with the same logging.

### Known answers and negative controls

- A/A control: arm F against arm F on the development briefs must not reject c = 1.0 beyond the nominal rate
  and must give R̂ near 1; a harness that favours whichever arm runs second shows up here.
- Potency control: a scripted "oracle" arm that submits the known accepted design at once must reject c = 1.5
  against arm M on the development briefs (the test can see an effect when one exists).
- Negative control: an analysis that drops censored campaigns reports a larger ratio; the harness refuses a
  record set whose pair count differs from the registration minus counted exclusions.
- Completion cap (known answer of the estimand): if arm F misses a fraction q of deadlines, R̂ ≤ 1/q; with
  12% unfinished the ratio cannot exceed 25/3 ≈ 8.33. Checked on a synthetic record.
- Determinism: the analysis is a pure function of the per-pair outcomes in registered order.

## Considerations

- Agent nondeterminism is part of the measurement; seeds are fixed where the provider allows, and r ≥ 2
  campaigns per brief average over the rest. The brief remains the unit of inference.
- A language-model update between arms would confound the result: all arms of a brief run inside one pinned
  model version, recorded per campaign.
- Tokens are held equal, so an arm that needs more turns pays in clock time, which is the intended reading.
- Compute: design campaigns are class E2 per campaign and need owner approval; the fallback sizing is fewer
  briefs with wider intervals, never fewer than ten briefs for a verdict.
- Pre-mortem 7 ("the agent surface was too large and agents got lost") would show as arm F losing to arm M on
  turns; the per-verb usage and the count of `session.focus` calls are recorded to diagnose it.
- The result is a statement about this agent on these briefs; it is not a population claim about agents.
