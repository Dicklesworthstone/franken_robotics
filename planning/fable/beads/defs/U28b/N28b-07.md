## Background

Decision ADR-ATEL-08: agents are the discrete optimizer. Agents choose topology, leg count and actuator
families; the studio supplies fast continuous optimization, population evaluation and honest comparison
underneath. This bead is the "fast continuous optimization" part: given a design whose topology is fixed and
whose continuous parameters are declared as levers (link lengths, masses, gear ratios, mount positions,
inertias), search those parameters for a task metric or a static objective. Tier: Solid.

The plan takes the search algorithms from FrankenSim instead of writing them (FACT, to be re-read at the pin:
`fs-dfo` has CMA-ES with restart schedules; `fs-bo` has Bayesian optimization with a trust-region module
`turbo` and a multi-fidelity module `mf`; `fs-ascent::riemann` has Riemannian quasi-Newton for objectives on
manifold-valued parameters). They are reached only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, whose `optimize`
feature group exposes them behind FrankenRobotics-native traits, so a sibling API change costs one crate. The
group is off by default and adds 12 crates and about 84 thousand lines to the build (FACT, from the manifests
on 2026-11-21), so `frob-opt` enables it and nothing below it does.

What this bead adds on top of the siblings is everything that makes an optimizer safe to run inside the
studio: parameters move by typed edits along their own retractions (an inertia stays on its cone); every
candidate is an immutable design version with an identity; draws come from addressed streams so a campaign
replays; the optimizer's state is a stored object; one generation is one batch.

Rejected alternatives, with the plan's reasons: Gaussian-process surrogates over typed designs of varying
topology (no validated kernel exists; Bayesian optimization is therefore used on continuous sub-problems
only); bandit allocation as the search (regret is the wrong objective for identifying the best design);
writing optimizers in-house (the siblings exist and the airlock contains their volatility).

## Scope

In: the `ParamSpace` built from a design's levers; the `Optimizer` interface used by `frob-opt`; the three
adapters; candidate realization as typed edits; restart and trust-region state; multi-fidelity mapping onto
ladder rungs; checkpointing and replay; refusals for unsupported spaces; a local fallback for the case that
admission fails.

Out: `opt.start`, `opt.status` and racing (BD-341); the quality-diversity archive and its mutations
(BD-342); family dispatch and finite-difference gradients (BD-353, N28b-09); the race ledger (N28b-08);
objective evaluation itself (comparison and episode beads).

## Technical approach

**1. The parameter space.** `ParamSpace::from_design(design, selection)` reads the design's `levers[]`, the
parameters an optimizer may move: each has an `id`, a `path`, `bounds` or `choices`, a `step`, a `manifold`
(ℝ, ℝ₊, S¹, S², SE(3) or the pseudo-inertia cone), a `prior` and a `locked` flag. The manifold fixes the chart
and the retraction by which a real coordinate moves the parameter: additive for ℝ, multiplicative for ℝ₊, the
unit-complex map for S¹, the group retraction for SE(3), the affine-invariant retraction on the cone for an
inertia. Levers with `choices` are discrete and are left to the agent. Locked levers are excluded.
The space records which coordinates are Euclidean and which are manifold-valued. A lever without bounds is
refused for the box-constrained optimizers (CMA-ES uses its own boundary handling; Bayesian optimization
requires a box).

**2. The interface (DESIGN; names fix intent).**

```rust
pub trait StudioOptimizer {
    fn ask(&mut self, n: usize, cx: &AskCx) -> Result<Vec<Candidate>, Refusal>;   // coordinates in charts
    fn tell(&mut self, results: &[Scored]) -> Result<(), Refusal>;                 // by candidate id
    fn state(&self) -> OptimizerState;                                             // canonical bytes, hashed
    fn restore(state: &OptimizerState) -> Result<Self, Refusal> where Self: Sized;
}
pub struct Candidate { id: CandidateId, coords: Vec<f64>, fidelity: Option<Rung> }
pub struct Scored { id: CandidateId, value: ScoreValue, noise: Option<f64>, status: ScoreStatus }  // Scored | Cancelled | Refused | Faulted
```

`ask` never returns a design; `realize(candidate) -> Vec<TypedEdit>` lowers the coordinates through each
lever's retraction to `set-lever` edits against the parent (a lever move invalidates as `set-param` does), and `design.edit` produces the immutable version.
A candidate whose edits are refused by the design language (a precondition fails, an inertia leaves its cone
by round-off) is told back as `Refused` with the refusal code; it is never silently clipped.

**3. Adapters.**

| Adapter | Sibling | Use | Studio-side rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CmaEs` | `fs-dfo` | Simulated objectives, rank-based | Restarts on the sibling's schedule; population size from the batch capacity; candidates of a generation evaluated together as one mixed batch; cancelled (raced-out) candidates are told as `Cancelled` with their last rank bound, never as a score |
| `TrustRegionBo` | `fs-bo::turbo` | Expensive objectives with few parameters | A box is required; the surrogate lives over the continuous coordinates of one topology only; the trust region's state is checkpointed |
| `MultiFidelityBo` | `fs-bo::mf` | Objectives with cheap and dear evaluators | Fidelities are ladder rungs (shared policy, fine-tuned policy); a fidelity may be requested only where the rung's mode permits its use for the stratum, otherwise the request is refused and the caller falls back to single fidelity |
| `RiemannQn` | `fs-ascent::riemann` | Static objectives with exact gradients (analyses differentiated by automatic differentiation) | Gradients carry a derivative-validity label; only `smooth` gradients are accepted; manifold coordinates use their retraction and vector transport |

**4. Randomness and replay.** Every random draw an adapter needs comes from a stream keyed by
`(campaign seed, optimizer id, generation, draw index)`; where the sibling takes a seed, the seed is derived
from that key. `state()` after generation g is a pure function of the campaign seed and the scores told so
far, taken in candidate-id order (never in completion order). `restore` followed by `ask` reproduces the same
candidates bit for bit on the same platform; across platforms the determinism class of the optimizer's
arithmetic is recorded with the state (the sibling's floating-point path is not assumed bit-identical across
CPUs).

**5. Scores.** An adapter is told values with their evidence standing. Scores from a rung that is not
permitted to select in the stratum are accepted for *search* (they steer the next ask) and are marked
`provisional`; the optimizer's "best so far" is reported as "best provisional candidate", never as a verdict.
A value-like quantity produced by an optimizer (a surrogate mean, an expected improvement) never appears in a
verdict field.

**6. Fallback if admission fails.** A local `CmaEs` (rank-μ update with the standard defaults, the same
ask/tell interface) is implemented in `frob-opt` against the same known answers; the two Bayesian adapters and
`RiemannQn` are then reported as not admitted (exit 5 on request) and the campaign uses CMA-ES and racing.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-opt/src/optimize/{space.rs, iface.rs, cma.rs, bo.rs, riemann.rs, realize.rs}`;
adapters compile only with `frob-fsim/optimize`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `ParamSpace::from_design` excludes locked levers, carries units and charts, and refuses an unbounded
      lever for the box-constrained adapters.
- [ ] Each adapter passes its known answers (below) through the airlock; the local CMA-ES passes the same.
- [ ] Every realized candidate is a valid immutable design version; a candidate that would leave the inertia
      cone is told back `Refused`, and no clipped design is ever evaluated.
- [ ] `state` → `restore` → `ask` reproduces candidates bit for bit on one platform, and told scores are
      consumed in candidate-id order (shuffling arrival order changes nothing).
- [ ] A multi-fidelity request for a rung whose mode in the stratum does not permit it is refused.
- [ ] A raced-out candidate is `Cancelled`, and the CMA-ES update uses its rank bound, not a fabricated score.
- [ ] No sibling type appears in `frob-opt`'s public API (the dependency lint passes).
- [ ] `frob-opt/CONTRACT.md` documents the interface, the adapters, determinism and the fallback.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test optimizers` (real adapters, analytic objectives labelled `synthetic`):

- `cma_sphere_converges`: f(x) = Σxᵢ² in 10 dimensions from x₀ = (3, …, 3), σ₀ = 1: f below 10⁻⁸ within
  10⁴ evaluations.
- `cma_restart_escapes`: Rastrigin in 5 dimensions: with restarts the global minimum (value 0 within 10⁻⁶) is
  reached within the budget in at least 8 of 10 seeded runs; with restarts disabled in fewer.
- `turbo_branin`: the Branin function on its box: best value within 10⁻² of 0.397887 in 60 evaluations.
- `mf_uses_cheap_fidelity`: a two-fidelity synthetic pair with correlation 0.9: at equal cost the best
  high-fidelity value is no worse than the single-fidelity adapter's in at least 8 of 10 seeds.
- `riemann_inertia_stays_on_cone`: minimizing a quadratic over pseudo-inertias starting near the boundary:
  every iterate is positive definite and satisfies the triangle inequalities on principal moments.
- `realize_roundtrip`: coordinates → edits → design → `ParamSpace` coordinates agree to round-off.
- `refused_candidate_told_back`; `unbounded_lever_refused_for_bo`; `locked_lever_excluded`.
- `state_restore_bit_identical`; `tell_order_invariance`.
- `provisional_best_label`: with scores from a `discard-only` rung the report says "best provisional".

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/opt_race_campaign.sh` (bead N28b-08): `frob opt start` with `--method cma-es` on a
template with four unlocked levers, `frob opt status`, a kill of the process mid-generation and a restart that
must continue from the stored optimizer state with identical candidates. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit
codes, wall time, evidence blocks, the optimizer state hash per generation, candidate identities, and on
failure the first candidate that differs after restore.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Analytic optima: sphere (0 at the origin), Rastrigin (0 at the origin), Branin (0.397887 at its three
  minimizers). Tolerances as in the unit tests.
- Negative controls: an adapter that clips an out-of-cone inertia instead of refusing (caught by
  `riemann_inertia_stays_on_cone` and by the refusal test); an adapter that consumes scores in arrival order
  (caught by `tell_order_invariance`); a mutant that reports the surrogate's mean as the candidate's score
  (caught by the rule that every told value has an evidence identifier).
- Metamorphic: rescaling a lever's unit (metres to millimetres through its chart) leaves the sequence of
  realized designs unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; the cross-platform lane records, and does not
  require, bit equality.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-3 concerns `fs-recompute`, `fs-voi` and `fs-race`; the same admission discipline applies to this
  group, and the local CMA-ES exists so that a failed admission delays nothing on the critical path.
- A population scored under one shared controller drifts toward designs that controller steers easily; the
  optimizer cannot see this bias. The campaign reports descriptor coverage beside quality, and the ladder's
  audit lane is the control.
- Paired finite-difference gradients (short horizons, smooth metrics) can feed `RiemannQn` only when their
  pilot has passed its kill gate; otherwise the stencil is a sensitivity report and search is rank-based.
- Performance: the optimizers' own cost is negligible beside evaluation; no budget is set for it.
