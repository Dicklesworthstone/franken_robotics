## Background

An agent that evaluates one design per turn spends most of its time waiting between turns. The studio can
instead evaluate a *neighbourhood* of designs in one dispatch: a sweep along one or two parameters, a
finite-difference stencil with common random numbers, or one evolutionary generation, all inside one mixed
batch on the device. That cuts sequential agent rounds, which is one of the two levers of the ten-fold thesis
("decide many designs per turn"). `opt.start --family` is Atelier's entry to it. Tier: Frontier.

The ceiling is stated before any test. Published population co-design reports compute gains of about three to
four times, not ten (FACT, source register), and parameter sweeps inside one topology are something incumbent
tools batch today, so for parametric families this lever is not specific to this engine. Paired
finite-difference design gradients are used only over short horizons and smooth metrics, because paired
worlds decouple under contact: a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degrees is reported to diverge after 50 steps (FACT,
source register).

Ownership. The mechanism (stencil layout, shared draws, the estimator, its refusals), the pilot and the kill
gate KILL-FDGRAD belong to the population subsystem. Atelier is a consumer: this bead defines how the
optimizer asks for a family, what it may do with the answer, and what is left when the kill gate fires. The
kill gate's trigger, restated because it governs this bead: on one quadruped with 12 design parameters, the
variance ratio of paired against independent central differences at horizons of 25 to 500 steps and the sign
agreement over 20 replicate batches; the gate fires on a variance ratio below 2 at every horizon of 50 steps
or more, on sign agreement below 70%, or when no end point is confirmed by a specialist. Fallback: rank-based
search (CMA-ES, racing), with the stencil kept as a sensitivity report.

Rejected alternatives: long-horizon paired gradients (pairs decouple, and the difference is then noise of
variance about σ²/(2δ²)); gradients of binary outcomes such as a fall (zero almost everywhere, variance growing
as 1/δ); treating a gradient step's predicted gain as a result (it is a search direction until confirmed).

## Scope

## Technical approach

**1. Family kinds.** `frob opt start --design <id> --family <kind> --task <id> --objective <metric:min|max>
--key <k> …` (DESIGN: the design is named by `--design`, the same input as every other `opt.start` call of
BD-341, not positionally, so one verb has one spelling for agents and the CLI; the `opt.start` row of
VERBS-opt now states the same, with `--family`, `--sweep` and `--population` among its inputs):

| Kind | Members | Draws | Returns to the optimizer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sweep` | A grid or line over one or two levers (`--sweep thigh=0.18:0.30:13`) | Common world addresses for all members | A table of paired scores by parameter value, each `provisional` under the rung that scored it |
| `stencil` | For P free levers: the centre θ and θ ⊕ δ·eᵢ, θ ⊖ δ·eᵢ along each lever's own retraction; 2P + 1 designs | Every world of a seed shares every random draw; only the design differs | A gradient estimate per lever with a confidence interval, the divergence step of each pair, and the label `Estimated` |
| `generation` | The candidates of one `ask` of a population optimizer | Common addresses; racing inside the batch | Ranks and scores with their standing; cancelled candidates as `Cancelled` |

Worked size: a 20-parameter central stencil with 64 addresses per design is 41 × 64 = 2,624 worlds and fits
one device. A family is planned against the cost and memory estimate before dispatch; a family that does not
fit is split into the fewest dispatches that do, by whole stencil arms (a pair θ ⊕ δ·eᵢ, θ ⊖ δ·eᵢ is never
separated from its centre's addresses), and the split is reported.

**2. Limits enforced on a stencil (each a refusal or a label, never a silent change).**
- Horizon: default cap 50 control intervals. A longer horizon is admitted only where the measured variance
  ratio of paired to independent differences is at least 2 at that horizon for the class.
- Divergence: each pair records the first step at which the active contact sets of its two worlds differ. The
  estimator truncates each pair at its divergence step and labels the result `truncated-horizon`, or refuses
  if more than half the pairs diverge before a quarter of the horizon.
- Metric: smooth metrics only (energy, tracking error, margins). A binary outcome is refused.
- Determinism: pairing requires a controlled determinism class; under an uncontrolled class the request is
  refused.
- These surface as `FROB-REF-540` (gradient unreliable: non-smooth metric, horizon above the admitted cap,
  pairs diverged early, or an uncontrolled determinism class; exit 3; repairs: shorten the horizon; choose a
  smooth metric; use rank-based search).
- Members scored under a family policy: a member outside that policy's training generator support is refused
  by the scoring rung with `FROB-REF-541` (§17.16), passed through with the member named; the family result
  lists it and the rest of the family is scored.

**3. What Atelier may do with a gradient.** Under a shared policy the gradient points toward designs that
policy handles, so the estimate is classed `Estimated` and a step taken along it is checked at its end point
by a specialist or a fine-tuned policy. The stepping rule (numbered):
1. Take the step with the quasi-Newton optimizer on the levers' manifolds, with a trust radius of at most the
   stencil's δ times a declared factor (DESIGN default 4).
2. Score the end point at the confirming rung (fine-tuned policy by default) on addresses disjoint from the
   stencil's.
3. Accept the step only if the confirmed paired difference against the centre has the predicted sign with its
   confidence sequence excluding zero; otherwise halve the radius (DESIGN factor ½) and report the
   disagreement.
4. Record predicted against confirmed gain per step; sign agreement over a campaign below 70% disables
   gradient stepping for that class for the rest of the campaign and says so.
A gradient or a predicted gain never appears in a verdict field, and no design is described as better on the
strength of a gradient.

**4. Round accounting.** Each `--family` dispatch records designs decided per dispatch and the serial agent
rounds of the campaign; the campaign report prints rounds per accepted design beside compute, so the lever is
measured where it is supposed to act (rounds) and not inferred from throughput.

**5. Output.** `opt.start --family` is a `run` verb: it returns a job (exit 8) and, when done, the family
result (`frob.opt.family.out/1`): members with identities, the addresses, the returned table or gradient with
labels, the dispatch split, spend, and `next_actions` (confirm a step; widen a sweep; switch to rank-based
search). Evidence: claim kind Design comparison only for confirmed end points; everything else is a search
aid, class `Estimated`.

**6. State after KILL-FDGRAD.** If the kill gate fires, `--family stencil` remains and returns its table as a
*sensitivity report* (the input the sensitivity analysis A-16 prints), the stepping rule of 3 is disabled
behind its flag, and `--family sweep` and `--family generation` are unaffected. Until the decision is
recorded, stepping is off by default and available only as an explicit shadow run whose steps are confirmed
and logged but not followed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three kinds dispatch as one mixed batch each when they fit; the 20-lever, 64-address stencil plans
      2,624 worlds; an oversized family is split by whole arms and the split is reported.
- [ ] Every world of a seed in a stencil reads identical draws (verified by digest of the draw streams).
- [ ] The four limits of step 2 produce `FROB-REF-540` or the `truncated-horizon` label as specified.
- [ ] A gradient step is accepted only after its end point is confirmed on disjoint addresses; an unconfirmed
      step is reported and not followed.
- [ ] With the kill-gate decision absent, stepping runs only in shadow; with KILL-FDGRAD fired, stepping is
      refused and the stencil returns a sensitivity report.
- [ ] No gradient, predicted gain or sweep score from a non-selective rung appears in a verdict field.
- [ ] Rounds per accepted design and designs per dispatch are recorded per campaign.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` and MCP; cancellation mid-dispatch leaves no partial family result.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test family_dispatch`:

- `stencil_world_count`: P = 20, 64 seeds → 2,624; P = 12, 64 → 1,600.
- `split_keeps_arms_whole`: capacity 1,000 worlds, P = 20 → three dispatches, each containing the centre's
  addresses for the arms it holds.
- `sweep_table_paired`: all members share addresses; the table's differences are paired.
- `binary_metric_refused_540`; `long_horizon_refused_without_ratio`; `uncontrolled_class_refused`.
- `out_of_support_member_541_passed_through`: a sweep whose last member leaves the family policy's generator
  support returns `FROB-REF-541` for that member and scores the others.
- `early_divergence_refused`: 60% of pairs diverge before a quarter of the horizon → refusal; 40% → result
  labelled `truncated-horizon`.
- `step_requires_confirmation`: a predicted gain of +0.05 with a confirmed difference of −0.01 is rejected and
  the radius halves.
- `sign_agreement_disables_stepping`: 6 of 10 confirmed signs agree (60%) → stepping disabled for the class.
- `shadow_until_decision`; `sensitivity_only_after_kill`.
- On a `synthetic` smooth objective with known gradient (a quadratic in four levers with additive paired
  noise), the stencil estimate's interval covers the true gradient in at least 90 of 100 seeded repetitions.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/opt_family_dispatch.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E; on `batch-cpu`, `--seed 9`, each
`frob opt start --design diff-drive --task threshold-delivery … --json` call with a `--key` of its own (`fam-sweep`,
`fam-stencil`, `fam-stencil-success`, `fam-gen`), so no call returns another's stored receipt: a `--family sweep
--sweep wheel_radius=0.04:0.08:9` for threshold delivery; a `--family stencil` on four levers (`--levers`) at
the default horizon (the smaller of the task's horizon and the admitted cap of 50 control intervals; the
`opt.start` row has no horizon input, so the script sets none) with `--objective` energy per metre, minimized;
the same stencil with `--objective` success (expect `FROB-REF-540`, exit 3); a `--family generation --population 27`. Asserts: one dispatch per family (from the job
record), paired addresses, labels, the refusal, the shadow status of stepping. Log
`target/e2e-logs/opt_family_dispatch/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
blocks, world counts against expected, the gradient with its interval and divergence steps, rounds and
designs per dispatch, and on failure the first pair whose draws differ. Final `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic known answer: 41 × 64 = 2,624.
- The decoupling fact as a control: on a contact-rich fixture the recorded divergence step of pairs perturbed
  by 5 × 10⁻⁴ degrees is reported; a horizon of 500 steps must be refused or truncated, never silently used.
- Negative controls: independent draws per design (the paired variance advantage disappears and the digest
  check of shared draws fails); a step followed without confirmation (caught by `step_requires_confirmation`).
- Metamorphic: permuting the order of levers permutes the gradient components and nothing else.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; the paired lane under a controlled class
  only.

## Considerations

- KILL-FDGRAD (decided at M4 by its owner): variance ratio below 2 at every horizon of 50 steps or more, or
  sign agreement below 70%, or no end point confirmed by a specialist. Fallback as in step 6.
- RISK-47 (search or gradients exploit a contact-order or model-form artifact): the confirmation rule and the
  model-form check of finalists are the countermeasures; a gradient is never the last word.
- The lever is rounds, not throughput: a family that fills the device but returns nothing the agent can act
  on is wasted, so `next_actions` is part of the output contract.
- For parametric sweeps the same batching is available to incumbents; the report does not present it as a
  differentiator.
