## Background

Three gates name "the design benchmark": the agent-surface A/B (GATE-ATEL-03: restricted mean time to an
accepted answer lower by a factor of at least 1.5 on at least ten briefs), the maps-per-dispatch gate
(campaigns reach the acceptance target in at most half the serial rounds of one-design-per-turn search), and,
through its design strata, the partial ten-fold gate. The plan lists "the design corpus with frozen labels and
the design benchmark" among the artifacts of the third milestone and defines the corpus in detail; the
benchmark is named and not specified. This bead specifies and builds it, so that every gate that cites it
means the same thing: a fixed set of design assignments (*briefs*), one rule for when an answer is *accepted*,
and one clock. Tier: Solid. Where the plan gives no number, the choices below are DESIGN and are recorded in
the benchmark's manifest.

The definitions are taken from the program's estimand so that a result here can be read beside the ten-fold
test. *Independently accepted* means a checker that is not the proposing system accepted the answer. The
*clock* T starts when the agent receives the brief and its permitted inputs and stops when the checker accepts
the first correct answer; unsuccessful runs, training, compilation, waiting, repair and rejected submissions
are inside it. The *restricted mean time* at a common deadline H is E[min(T, H)]: a brief unresolved at H
costs H. Two properties follow and shape the benchmark: missed deadlines cap any ratio (if an arm misses a
fraction q of deadlines its ratio against a rival cannot exceed 1/q), and correctness is a gate, not a term
(the time score is never improved by lowering what counts as accepted).

Rejected alternatives: scoring by best task reward within a budget (an arm can win by exploiting the reward);
letting each experiment define its own briefs (results would not be comparable and briefs could be chosen
after seeing outcomes); acceptance on the worlds the agent developed on (the agent then optimizes the test).

## Scope

In: the brief schema and manifest; the version-1 brief set (development and sealed); construction rules that
make a brief non-trivial and solvable; the acceptance rule and its use of the blinded qualification lane;
clock events; the restricted mean time and pooled ratio as library functions; sealing and consumption rules;
the planted-defect control briefs.

Out: the A/B's arms and registration (N28b-11) and its run (BD-374); the qualification lane itself (BD-259);
world splits and holdouts (BD-235); task families (their beads); the ten-fold suite and its neutral
adjudicator.

## Technical approach

**1. A brief (`frob.brief/1`, canonical JSON under `assets/benchmark/design/v1/briefs/`).**

```json
{ "schema": "frob.brief/1", "id": "db1-quad-ramp-payload", "stratum": "legged",
  "family": { "archetype": "quad-mammal", "free_levers": ["thigh", "shank", "knee_gear", "battery_mass"],
              "choices": { "knee_actuator": ["qdd-s", "qdd-m", "qdd-l"] }, "fixed": ["topology"] },
  "task": "t:…", "requirement": "carry 4 kg up a 12 degree ramp of 5 m within 20 s without a fall",
  "threshold": "9/10", "alpha": "1/20",
  "constraints": { "mass_kg_max": "22", "footprint_m_max": ["0.9", "0.5"] },
  "budget": { "gpu_s": "7200", "cpu_core_s": "28800", "agent_tokens": "2000000" },
  "deadline_s": "10800",
  "inputs": ["brief card", "template", "development worlds"],
  "acceptance": { "kind": "qualified-design", "clusters": 64, "campaign": "q:…" },
  "control": null }
```

`control` is `{ "kind": "planted-defect", "corpus_entry": "d:…" }` for a control brief: the starting design is a
corpus design with one planted defect, and the answer must be an accepted repair.

**2. The version-1 set (DESIGN; TARGET sizes).** Sixteen sealed briefs and four development briefs. Strata at
the third milestone: `legged` (quadruped, hexapod, biped) and `wheeled` (differential, car-like, mecanum,
omnidirectional); tracked and hybrid classes are added at the fourth as a new benchmark version. Tasks are
drawn from the families that exist by then: stand and recover, threshold delivery, timed docking, velocity
tracking, terrain traversal, interior navigation, payload transport, low-friction crossing, tight-space
maneuvering. Each stratum has at least six sealed briefs, so that ten usable briefs remain after exclusions.
Four of the sixteen are planted-defect control briefs (two per stratum). Development briefs are public and are
used to build harnesses, set deadlines and run pilots; they never enter a gate's statistic.

**3. Construction rules for a brief.**
1. *Not trivial:* the family's template fails the acceptance rule (verified once, on development worlds of
   the same distribution), so at least one consequential edit is needed.
2. *Solvable:* the authors hold one reference answer that passes acceptance; its existence is recorded as a
   sealed hash, and its content is never given to an arm.
3. *Bounded:* the deadline H is set per stratum before sealing (DESIGN default: twice the median time of the
   minimal-surface arm on the stratum's development briefs, at most 3 h), and budgets are equal for every arm
   that will ever run the brief.
4. *One answer kind:* a qualified design (a design in the family, a controller behind the standard
   observation and action interface, and its declared clocks).
5. *Fresh worlds:* acceptance uses sealed world clusters from the held-out split that no development world
   shares a building, a mirrored layout or an edited variant with.

**4. The acceptance rule.** A submission is a call of the blinded qualification lane on the brief's campaign.
It is committed before any world is drawn; each submission draws fresh sealed world clusters; the i-th
submission on a brief is tested at level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] with α = 0.05, so retries cost clock time and
cannot buy acceptance by repetition. The answer is accepted when, over the n drawn clusters, the exact
one-sided lower confidence bound (Clopper–Pearson) on the requirement pass rate is at least the brief's
threshold, and the design satisfies the brief's constraints and passes `design.check` with no refusal. The arm
receives a receipt holding only the decision, the submission index, the level spent, the units used and a
hash; a rejected submission returns no per-world outcome. Worked values at n = 64: 64 of 64 passes give a
lower bound of 0.944 at the first submission (level 0.025), 0.928 at the second (0.00833) and 0.918 at the
third; 62 of 64 give 0.892 at the first, which fails a threshold of 0.9.

**5. The clock.** Two events define T for a campaign: `brief-delivered` (written when the session receives
the brief card) and `accepted` (written by the lane with the accepting receipt). Both are ledger events with
the session and brief identities. Everything between them counts, including time the harness spends between a
request and its service (charged to the phase `wait`). A campaign with no `accepted` event by H is censored
at H.

**6. Library functions (`frob-arena::benchmark`).** `rmt(times, censored, H)` = mean of min(T, H);
`pooled_ratio(pairs, H)` = Σ min(T_b, H) ÷ Σ min(T_a, H) over paired campaigns (a ratio of sums, never a mean
of per-brief ratios); `completion_cap(q)` = 1/q. They are pure, exact in rational arithmetic on integer
milliseconds, and shared by every experiment that reports on the benchmark. Beside them, the clock's two ledger
functions: `deliver_brief(session, brief)` writes `brief-delivered`, and `campaign_time(session, brief, H)` reads
the two events and returns T, or censoring at H; the experiment harness calls them, and an example program
(`benchmark_clock`) exposes them to scripts.

**7. Sealing and consumption.** The benchmark manifest lists every brief's hash; the manifest hash is
committed to the ledger when the version is frozen. A sealed brief may be used by exactly one registered run
(one experiment, all its arms); the registration names the briefs it consumes and the ledger refuses a second
registration that names any of them. After a run's receipt is written its briefs become development briefs of
the next version.

**8. No new verb.** Briefs are data; the user-side path is `task.show` on the brief's task, the design verbs,
and `eval.qualify` for submission. A session's brief card is delivered by the experiment harness.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Version 1 exists with sixteen sealed and four development briefs, at least six sealed per stratum and
      four planted-defect controls, each validating against `frob.brief/1`.
- [ ] For every brief: the template fails acceptance on development worlds; a sealed reference-answer hash is
      on record; H and budgets are set and identical across arms.
- [ ] The acceptance rule reproduces the worked values (0.944, 0.928, 0.918 at 64 of 64 for submissions 1 to
      3; 0.892 at 62 of 64) and accepts or rejects accordingly at threshold 0.9.
- [ ] A submission returns only the receipt fields; no per-world outcome leaks on rejection.
- [ ] `rmt`, `pooled_ratio` and `completion_cap` pass their known answers.
- [ ] The manifest hash is in the ledger; a second registration naming a consumed sealed brief is refused.
- [ ] No sealed acceptance world shares a building, mirrored layout or edited variant with a development
      world (checked against the split record).
- [ ] The benchmark, its rules and its DESIGN defaults are documented in `crates/frob-arena/CONTRACT.md`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-arena --test design_benchmark`:

- `brief_schema_validates`; `brief_hash_stable_under_key_order`.
- `acceptance_bounds`: (n, x, i) = (64, 64, 1) → 0.9440; (64, 64, 2) → 0.9279; (64, 64, 3) → 0.9179;
  (64, 62, 1) → 0.8916; each to four places.
- `alpha_schedule_sums_below_alpha`: Σᵢ α/[i(i + 1)] over any number of submissions is at most α.
- `rmt_censors_at_deadline`: times (1,800 s, 5,400 s, never) with H = 10,800 s → (1,800 + 5,400 + 10,800)/3 =
  6,000 s.
- `pooled_ratio_is_ratio_of_sums`: (3,600, 3,600, 14,400) against (1,800, 1,800, 14,400) → 21,600/18,000 =
  6/5.
- `completion_cap`: q = 3/25 → 25/3.
- `consumed_brief_refused`; `template_must_fail` (a brief whose template passes is rejected by the loader);
  `reference_hash_required`.
- `clock_includes_rejected_submission`: delivered at t = 0, rejected at 2,000 s, accepted at 5,000 s → T =
  5,000 s.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/design_benchmark_brief.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and walks one development brief
from the user's side. The `brief-delivered` event is written by this bead's clock-event writer, exposed for the
script as `cargo run -p frob-arena --example benchmark_clock -- deliver --brief <id> --json` (an example program,
not a verb, per section 8; the experiment harness of N28b-11 calls the same function). Then: `frob task show
<task> --json`; `frob design check <template> --json`; a submission of the template with `frob eval qualify
<template> --task <task> --claim '<requirement of the brief at its threshold>' --campaign <the brief's campaign>
--key dbb-sub-1 --json` (expect a rejection receipt with index 1 and level 0.025, and no per-world outcome); a
scripted repair by `frob design edit <template> <edit> --expected-parent <DesignId> --key dbb-edit-1 --json`; a
second submission of the repaired design under key `dbb-sub-2` (expect acceptance at level 0.00833); then
`cargo run -p frob-arena --example benchmark_clock -- report --brief <id> --deadline-s 10800 --json` reads the two
ledger events, computes T and the restricted mean time over this single campaign with `rmt`, and the script checks
T against H. Log `target/e2e-logs/design_benchmark_brief/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit codes,
wall time, evidence blocks, receipts (decision, index, level, units, hash), the lower bound against the
threshold, T against H, and on failure the first receipt field that is missing or extra. Final `summary`
line.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Completion cap (KA-009): with 12% of deadlines missed the ratio cannot exceed 25/3 ≈ 8.33.
- The acceptance arithmetic above (exact binomial).
- Negative controls: an acceptance rule that reuses the same world clusters across submissions at a constant
  level (a scripted adversary that retries then gets accepted on a no-signal design; the lane's own gate shows
  false promotion above 99% for that mutant); a clock that stops at the first submission instead of the first
  acceptance; a ratio computed as a mean of per-brief ratios.
- Determinism: brief hashes, the manifest hash and the library functions are D0.

## Considerations

- The acceptance lane is ours, so results on this benchmark are `self-adjudicated`; the label is printed with
  every ratio, and the words reserved for the neutral adjudicator of the ten-fold test are not used.
- Sealed briefs are a consumable. Sixteen allow one registered run with twelve or more usable briefs; every
  further experiment needs a new version.
- Deadlines set from development runs can be wrong for sealed briefs; a stratum in which every arm is
  censored on more than half the briefs is reported as uninformative, not as a tie.
- Twelve of the eighteen archetypes have no permissively licensed reference robot; briefs use templates and
  generic actuator cards, and the share of `assumed` card fields is printed with every result.
- Owner of the brief set: Arena lead. Changing a DESIGN default here is a new benchmark version.
