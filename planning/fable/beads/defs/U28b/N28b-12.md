## Background

A design is never scored by the most expensive method first. The ladder has six rungs, each about an order of
magnitude dearer than the one below, each allowed to decide only what its evidence supports. Before any
allocation rule runs, something has to answer a plainer question for each rung: *what scores the design
there, and is that scorer admissible for this design and task?* This bead is that admission step. Tier: Solid.

| Rung | What scores the design | Cost per design | What it may decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 Static | `design.check` and the bound analyses | Seconds of CPU; under 2 s for `check` (TARGET) | Refuse a design; exclude with a typed bound. It never ranks survivors |
| R1 Shared policy | The family policy of the design's morphology class, with no adaptation, on a fast tier; the synthesized baseline controller where the class has no family policy | Tens of GPU-seconds (HYPOTHESIS; the phase ledger records it) | Discard, under the rank-agreement modes. Select only in strata where rank agreement has been measured |
| R2 Fine-tuned | The family policy fine-tuned to the design at a declared fraction of specialist steps. Each fraction is its own rung for calibration | Minutes | Discard, and select among survivors |
| R3 Specialist | A policy trained or inherited for this design alone | One training run (FACT, source register: the leading incumbent trains a quadruped velocity policy in about 410 s on one RTX 4090 and humanoids in under 30 minutes) | Rank finalists, as `provisional` |
| R4 Referee | Paired re-evaluation on the referee at the same world addresses, with the tier-correction estimate | 16 / 64 / 256 referee episodes at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile per established verdict (TARGET, unmeasured) | Establish, or label `tier-sensitive` |
| R5 Rare failure | A failure-rate bound for finalists | n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉ nominal episodes for a bound p* at confidence 1 − δ | Certify, refute or exhaust the bound, scoped to its tier |

What published work says about the middle rungs (FACT, source register; figures from other engines, re-read
before a gate cites them): within one morphology class a family policy reaches 70 to 85% of specialist return
with no adaptation, and fine-tuning matches it after about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched
return; across topology-diverse designs at affordable pretraining the zero-shot figure is 10 to 40% and the
fine-tuning gain two to three times; inheritance from a class prior costs about 2 to 25% of such a run, the
one published parent-to-child figure is about 50%, no source measures a small typed parameter edit, and after
a single-limb topology edit the cost is 33 to 100%. Cheap rungs are biased as well as noisy: early evaluation
underestimates slow learners, and a population scored under one shared controller drifts toward designs that
controller steers easily. Those facts are why admission is explicit and recorded on every score.

Design rule taken from the optimization section (co-design with control): the inner loop is a controller that
needs no per-design training, the synthesized baseline (seconds) or the class's family policy; fine-tuning is
spent only on survivors and specialist training only on finalists.

## Technical approach

**1. The scorer record (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct Scorer {
    rung: Rung,                       // R0Static | R1SharedPolicy | R2FineTuned{fraction} | R3Specialist | R4Referee | R5RareFailure
    kind: ScorerKind,                 // Check | FamilyPolicy | Baseline | Sampling | FineTune | Specialist | Referee | RareFailure
    controller: Option<ControllerId>, // or PolicyId for a learned policy; content hashes
    tier: Tier, effort: EffortProfileId,
    determinism: DetProfile,          // the profile of the *evaluation*
    evaluator_of_record: bool,
    flags: FlagSet,                   // e.g. model-privileged for a sampling controller
}
```

`ScorerId` is the BLAKE3 of the record's canonical bytes. It is part of the key of every rank-agreement row
(policy and kernel versions, effort profile), so changing a scorer starts new rows.

**2. Admission at R1 (numbered, first that applies).**
1. The class has a family policy and the design lies inside its training generator's support: `FamilyPolicy`,
   no adaptation. Outside the support the owner's refusal `FROB-REF-541` is not surfaced as a failure of the
   campaign; admission falls through to 2 and records `out-of-support` on the score.
2. The baseline controller synthesizes for the design (outcome `synthesized`): `Baseline`. The outcomes
   `synthesized-failed` and `not-synthesizable` are kept as typed results: the first is a failed trial of the
   design at R1, the second sends admission to 3.
3. `Sampling`: the sampling-based predictive controller, which needs no training. Its `ControllerId` carries
   its planning step; results are flagged `model-privileged` where the planner reads privileged state.
4. Nothing admissible: the design skips R1 and is scored at the next rung, with the reason recorded. It is
   never discarded for want of a scorer.

The baseline controller is a scorer like any other: when it serves as R1 its rank agreement with trained
policies is measured in the same ledger and it has no exemption. A request to use any R1 or R2 scorer to
select or discard in a stratum with no rank-agreement record for that `ScorerId` pair is refused by the
ledger's owner with `FROB-REF-537`.

**3. The sampling controller is a scorer, not an evaluator of record.** It is admitted as an R1 scorer and as
the out-of-distribution fallback above. It never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record:
at about 0.33 and 1 GPU-minutes per 20 s episode (quadruped, humanoid) at a 20 ms planning step, and about 1.7
and 5.2 at 4 ms, a few hundred episodes cost more than training once (FACT, source register). So:
`evaluator_of_record = false` for `Sampling` at every rung; a request to rank finalists (R3) or to enter a
promotion (R4) with a sampling-controller score is refused at admission; and the R1 use is capped at the
screening allowance of three episodes per design (DESIGN default, recorded in the policy). One exception is
admitted and typed: where the sampling controller *is the delivered controller* of the answer, its own
episodes are acceptance evidence, under a frozen planner record (planner version, sample count, horizon,
planning step, cost weights, all hashed before the acceptance run).

**4. Each fine-tune fraction is its own rung.** `R2FineTuned { fraction }` with fraction a reduced rational of
specialist steps. Scores at fraction 1/20 and at 1/5 are never pooled in calibration and have separate
rank-agreement rows. OPEN-ATEL-6 default: one R2 rung at 5%.

**5. Training profile against evaluation profile.** A policy may be trained under the labelled D4 training
profile (fast, not replayable bit for bit). Every score that enters the ladder is an *evaluation* on a D2
profile: the same backend, replayable, paired by world address. Admission checks the evaluation's profile; a
score produced under D4, or by arms in different classes, is refused entry with `FROB-REF-542` (a paired study
under an uncontrolled determinism class, or with arms in different classes; repair: rerun both arms on a D2
profile; exit 3). The training profile is recorded on the policy and does not block admission.

**6. What every score carries.** `ScorerId`, the rung, `evaluator_of_record`, flags, the standing
(`provisional` below R4), the world addresses, and the mode of the rung in the design's stratum at the time
(`selective`, `discard-only`, `uninformative`). Campaign reports print the scorer of every rung per stratum.

**7. Surface.** No new verb. `opt.cascade --estimate` and `opt.status` list the admitted scorer per rung and
class before spend. Layer: `frob-opt::ladder::scorer`. Determinism: admission is a pure function of the
design, task, registries and ledger frontier (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Admission at R1 follows the order family policy, baseline, sampling, skip, and records the reason for
      every fall-through; a design outside generator support is scored by the next admissible scorer and
      marked `out-of-support`.
- [ ] A baseline `synthesized-failed` is a failed trial at R1; `not-synthesizable` falls through.
- [ ] A sampling-controller score is refused as evaluator of record at R3 and R4, and is accepted as
      acceptance evidence only with a frozen planner record that predates the run.
- [ ] Scores at two fine-tune fractions have distinct rung keys and are never pooled.
- [ ] A D4 evaluation, or arms in different determinism classes, is refused with `FROB-REF-542`; a policy
      trained under D4 and evaluated under D2 is admitted.
- [ ] Every score in a campaign carries its `ScorerId`, standing and mode; the estimate lists scorers per
      rung before any spend.
- [ ] Changing the scorer (a new policy version) changes `ScorerId` and so starts new rank-agreement rows.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test scorer_admission`:

- `r1_prefers_family_policy_in_support`; `r1_falls_to_baseline_out_of_support` (flag recorded);
  `r1_falls_to_sampling_when_not_synthesizable`; `r1_skip_when_nothing_admissible` (design goes to R2, not
  discarded).
- `baseline_failed_is_failed_trial`: outcome `synthesized-failed` counts in the denominator as a failure.
- `sampling_never_evaluator_of_record`: requests at R3 and R4 refused.
- `sampling_as_delivered_controller_needs_frozen_record`: without the record, refused; with a record hashed
  after the first acceptance episode, refused; with one hashed before, admitted.
- `sampling_screening_cap`: a request for 10 episodes per design at R1 is reduced to the cap of 3 only by an
  explicit refusal with the cap as repair, never silently.
- `fraction_is_rung_key`: fractions 1/20 and 1/5 hash to different rung keys.
- `d4_score_refused_542`; `d4_trained_d2_evaluated_admitted`; `mixed_class_arms_refused_542`.
- `scorer_id_changes_with_policy_version`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `scorers` of `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28): `frob opt cascade --estimate
--json` on a seeded population that contains one class with a family policy, one without, and one design the
baseline cannot synthesize; asserts the admitted scorer per rung and class and the recorded reasons; then a
request that forces a D4 evaluation into the ladder (expect `FROB-REF-542`, exit 3). Logged per STD-E2E:
commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, scorer identities per rung, and on failure the first design
whose admitted scorer differs from the expectation.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Cost known answer for the rule in 3 (arithmetic on the cited figures): 300 episodes of the sampling
  controller for a quadruped at a 20 ms step cost about 300 × 0.33 = 99 GPU-minutes, against about 7 minutes
  for one specialist training run at the incumbent's figure; the rule's reason holds by an order of magnitude.
- Negative controls: a ladder that lets a sampling-controller score rank finalists (the admission test must
  refuse); a score from a D4 run entered into a paired comparison (refused); two fractions pooled into one
  calibration set (the pooled row is rejected by the rung-key check).
- Determinism: admission decisions identical twice and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-6 (owner: Cortex lead; decided by the calibration experiment). Fail-closed default: one R2 rung
  at 5%.
- The R1 cost figure ("tens of GPU-seconds") is a HYPOTHESIS until the phase ledger records it; the estimate
  prints it with that label.
- If the family-policy bet is killed, R1's first choice disappears and the baseline controller becomes the R1
  scorer for every class; nothing in this bead changes.
- A scorer that is easy to exploit biases the whole search; the audit lane and the model-form check are the
  controls, and this bead's part is only to make the scorer visible on every score.
