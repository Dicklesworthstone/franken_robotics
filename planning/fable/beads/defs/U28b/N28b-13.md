## Background

Every cheap evaluation rung of the ladder is trusted by measurement, and a measurement needs a sample that
the thing being measured did not choose. If the designs used to calibrate a rung are the ones the rung
advanced, the calibration is biased toward agreement: the rung looks accurate on exactly the designs it liked.
The plan's answer is the **audit lane**: a tenth of a rung's spend (DESIGN default) goes to designs drawn by a
keyed random stream *before any score exists*. Each is evaluated at the higher rung at a fixed n and is never
cancelled by an e-process. The audit lane is the only source of calibration; calibrating on the designs that
advanced is forbidden. Tier: Frontier as part of the calibrated layer of the ladder; the draw itself is exact
and simple.

The lane serves four consumers with one sample: the conformal margin of the survivor rule (calibration
residuals), the rank-agreement ledger (its continuous source of rows), the screening audit (the stratified
audit of discards with its exact bound), and the exchangeability alarm. It is also what survives if the
adaptive layer is killed: the kill gate's fallback keeps the fixed halving table as the allocation rule and
the audit lane as measurement.

Rejected alternatives: drawing audit designs after scoring, "at random among the discards" (the draw can then
be influenced by scores through any code path that sees them, and replay cannot prove it was not); a draw by
arrival order (depends on scheduling); re-weighting audit designs by their lower-rung score to save compute
(breaks exchangeability, which is the property the conformal guarantee rests on).

## Scope

In: the keyed draw and its size rule; stratification and the merging of starved strata; the commitment record
that proves the draw preceded the scores; the fixed-n evaluation contract; the never-cancelled rule and its
enforcement points; overlap with the race; what the lane publishes to its consumers.

Out: the residuals, margin and survivor set (N28b-14, N28b-15); the alarm (N28b-16); ledger rows (N28b-22);
the audit's bound and planted winner (N28b-29); the trust rule (BD-345).

## Technical approach

**1. When the draw happens.** A population P enters a rung pair (lo, hi) in a stratum when its members are
admitted to be scored at lo. At that moment no member has a score at lo or hi for this campaign. The draw is
made then, in the same ledger transaction that records the admission.

**2. The draw (DESIGN; a pure function of identities).**

```
h(d) = Philox( key = campaign_seed,
               counter = (lo64(DesignId), hi64(DesignId), rung_pair, generation),
               stream_id = "audit-lane" )  as u64
audit set A  = the m designs of P ∩ stratum with the smallest h(d)
```

The stream identifier is its own, so the draw is independent of every draw the physics or the optimizer
makes. The fixed-size form is used because it merges exactly across shards and agents: the m smallest hashes
of a union are the m smallest of the per-part m smallest. The set does not depend on arrival order, slot,
shard count, preemption or which agent proposed a design. Replay recomputes it.

**3. The size rule.** Let S be the rung's planned spend for the stratum in the campaign manifest and c_hi(n)
the estimated cost of one design at the higher rung at the fixed n. Then m = ⌊0.1·S / c_hi(n)⌋ (the tenth is
the DESIGN default and a field of the manifest), and m is capped at |P ∩ stratum|. The calibration consumer
needs at least 19 residuals at α = 0.05; when the tenth buys fewer than 19 audit designs in a stratum, strata
are merged upward to the morphology class before the stratum falls back to the fixed table, and the merge is
recorded. The audit lane's spend is charged to the campaign and printed in its report.

**4. The commitment.** The admission transaction writes an `audit-draw` evidence record: campaign, rung pair,
stratum (or merged strata), generation, m, the sorted list of audit `DesignId`s, its BLAKE3, and the
transaction's commit sequence number. Any score at lo or hi for a member of P in this campaign has a larger
sequence number. A checker can therefore verify "drawn before any score existed" from the ledger alone. An
`AllocationDecision` cites the record in its `audit_lane` field.

**5. The fixed-n contract.** Each audit design is evaluated at the higher rung on the first n world addresses
of the rung's address schedule (n is a manifest field, per rung pair), to completion. It is also scored at
the lower rung like every other member. The pair (y_lo, y_hi) is published only when both are complete. An
audit design whose higher-rung evaluation ends in a numerical fault or a refusal is published with that
status; it is not replaced by another design, because a replacement would be chosen after an outcome exists.

**6. Never cancelled, re-weighted or selected after a score exists (INV-ATEL-03).** Enforcement points:
- the race engine receives the audit set and may eliminate an audit design *from the race*, but the design's
  episodes up to n continue; its e-process is simply no longer updated;
- the promotion core's stopping rule does not apply to audit episodes;
- budget rebalancing and campaign cancellation: a cancelled campaign cancels audit episodes like everything
  else and marks the audit of that rung `incomplete` (which the trust rule treats as a missing audit); short
  of cancelling the campaign, no request may cancel an audit episode;
- weights: every audit design has weight 1 in every consumer; there is no importance weighting.
A request that violates any of these is refused with `FROB-REF-546` (calibration from survivors, or an audit
episode cancelled or re-weighted; repair: redraw the audit lane by key, which means a new generation's draw,
never a re-draw of the same one; exit 3).

**7. Blinding.** An audit job and a survivor's job for the same design, addresses and budget are the same job
type and byte-identical requests. Lane membership is evaluated by the campaign controller only.

**8. Overlap.** A design can be both a survivor and an audit design. Its higher-rung episodes are run once,
and both roles read them in address order; the audit role reads exactly the first n.

**9. Re-opening.** When a rung becomes `uninformative` in a stratum, designs it eliminated earlier in the
campaign are re-opened through the lane: they are scored at the higher rung at the fixed n, in order of h(d).
Re-opened designs are not calibration data for the rung that discarded them (they were selected by its
failure) and are flagged `reopened`.

**10. Layout and determinism.** `crates/frob-opt/src/ladder/audit_lane.rs`. The draw and the commitment are
D0. Published pairs inherit the determinism class of their episodes (D2 evaluations).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The audit set is a pure function of (campaign seed, design identities, rung pair, stratum, generation,
      m): identical under 100 random slot assignments, shard counts, arrival orders and agent partitions.
- [ ] The `audit-draw` record's commit sequence is lower than that of every score of the rung for the
      population, on every campaign in the test suite; a draw recorded after a score is rejected.
- [ ] m follows the size rule; a stratum with fewer than 19 audit designs is merged upward and the merge is
      recorded.
- [ ] Every audit design reaches n higher-rung episodes unless the campaign itself is cancelled; a race
      elimination does not stop them.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-546` fires for: cancelling an audit episode; a weight other than 1; a calibration set that
      includes a non-audit design; a re-draw of an existing generation's set.
- [ ] Audit and survivor job requests for the same design are byte-identical.
- [ ] Faulted audit designs are published with their status and never replaced.
- [ ] Re-opened designs are flagged and excluded from calibration.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test audit_lane`:

- `draw_is_function_of_identities`: 81 designs, m = 8: the same set under 100 permutations and 5 shard counts.
- `draw_merges_exactly`: the 8 smallest of the union equal the 8 smallest of per-shard 8 smallest.
- `draw_independent_of_scores`: changing every lower-rung score leaves the set unchanged (the function has no
  score argument; the test guards against a later refactor adding one).
- `size_rule`: S = 4,860 GPU-seconds, c_hi(n) = 180 → m = ⌊486/180⌋ = 2, below 19 → merge upward; S = 48,600 →
  m = 27.
- `commitment_precedes_scores`; `late_draw_rejected`.
- `race_elimination_keeps_audit_running`: an audit design eliminated at round 9 of 64 still has 64 episodes.
- `cancel_audit_refused_546`; `reweight_refused_546`; `survivor_in_calibration_refused_546`;
  `redraw_same_generation_refused_546`.
- `overlap_reads_first_n`: a design that is survivor and audit runs each episode once.
- `faulted_audit_not_replaced`; `reopened_flagged_and_excluded`.
- Property test for INV-ATEL-03 over 1,000 random campaign schedules with races, preemption and budget
  rebalances: no audit episode is cancelled, re-weighted or selected after a score for its design exists.
- Uniformity: over 10⁶ draws of m = 10 from 100 designs with random seeds, each design's inclusion frequency
  lies inside the exact binomial bounds for probability 1/10.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `audit_lane` of `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28): run `frob opt cascade` on the
seeded population twice with different worker counts and once with random preemption enabled; extract the
`audit-draw` records with `frob evidence trace`; assert identical audit sets and hashes across the three
runs, sequence numbers below all scores, n episodes per audit design, and zero cancelled audit episodes. Then
issue a job cancellation aimed at one audit episode (expect `FROB-REF-546`, exit 3). Logged per STD-E2E:
commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, m per stratum against the size rule, the set hashes, the
sequence numbers, and on failure the first design in one run's set and not the other's.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Counterexample of the construction: computing the margin from promoted designs only under-covers on a
  fixture where the residual correlates with the cheap score. The fixture (N28b-14) is run here with a lane
  that draws after scoring among survivors: the calibration consumer must be refused, and if the refusal is
  disabled the coverage test must fail.
- Negative control for independence of arrival: an arrival-order reservoir gives different sets under
  different schedules (asserted different, as a check that the test can see the defect).
- Interference rule (carried by the composition bead and asserted here on a small replay): no audit episode
  was stopped by an e-process.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; across the two machines' backends (the set
  is exact everywhere).

## Considerations

- The tenth is a DESIGN default. A smaller share starves calibration (fewer than 19 points per stratum means
  no adaptive rule); a larger one gives back the ladder's saving. The share is a field of the decision policy
  and changing it is a new policy version.
- The lane measures the rung on the population it actually sees; when the optimizer drifts to a region the
  audit set does not cover, the alarm is what notices.
- Cost accounting must charge the lane to the campaign: a speed claim that omits it overstates the ladder.
- Audit pairs hold design identities and scores only; no scene data.
