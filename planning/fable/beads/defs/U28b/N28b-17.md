## Background

The plan requires a ceiling to be stated before any speed test: a number that says how much a mechanism could
save if it worked perfectly, computed before anyone measures what it does save. For the ladder the ceiling is
simple and sobering. The ladder cannot cost less than one first-rung score for every design, the audit lane,
and the specialist, referee and rare-failure rungs for the finalists. Everything an adaptive allocation rule
can win lies between that floor and the fixed halving table. This bead turns that statement into a cost model
with a read-only estimate, so that every campaign prints its floor, its baseline and its plan before spending,
and every claim about the ladder's saving is checked against the floor. Tier: Solid (it is arithmetic over
declared costs; the costs themselves are HYPOTHESIS until the phase ledger records them).

It also carries, as known answers, the plan's worked case and its table of what the evidence supports, so
that the optimistic readings ("the ladder is ten times cheaper") cannot re-enter by repetition.

| Quantity | Figure | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Saving from anytime-valid stopping and elimination | Two to five times when candidates are separated; about none when they are not | FACT for the sibling's fixtures (at least 2 asserted, 11.7 measured, on a separated field; 1.03 on an inseparable one); HYPOTHESIS for robot design populations |
| Information-gain selection against a capable agent | Unsupported; plausibly below 1.5 times. A language-model tuner beat random search more often than Gaussian-process optimization at 10 and at 30 evaluations, and model-based search lost to a genetic algorithm on a robot co-design benchmark | FACT, source register. The finalist layer is therefore a stop rule and a budget allocator |
| Referee cost saved by pairing with the fast tier | Variance scales by 1 − ρ²: 1.96 times at ρ = 0.7, 5.3 at 0.9, 10 at 0.95. Published cross-fidelity correlation collapses at large dynamics gaps | Arithmetic; ρ unmeasured. Expect about two times until ρ above 0.9 is measured |
| Rank agreement of the shared-policy rung with specialists on the referee | Unmeasured anywhere. Nearest neighbours: rank correlation 0.40 overall | FACT, source register |
| Share of a population that must leave the first rung for the second | About the top quarter, not the top twentieth | HYPOTHESIS inferred from the neighbours; settled by the rank-agreement gate |
| Compute gain of published shared-policy population methods | About three to four times | FACT, source register |

## Scope

In: the cost model for the three allocation regimes (train everything, the fixed table, the conformal
survivor rule) and the floor; inputs from the scorer admission and the cost estimator; the estimate record
`frob.ladder.estimate/1` that `opt.cascade --estimate` prints (the verb's option is BD-352's, which depends on
this bead); the registered prediction of a campaign's saving; the known answers. NEG-08's itemized cost record
(`rung_costs`, `audit_lane` by count with the rung named, `total` with its formula) is the itemized form of this
model's conformal-survivor cost: it lives in the same module, `crates/frob-opt/src/ladder/cost.rs`, and its
tests in the same target, `cargo test -p frob-opt --test ladder_cost` (NEG-08 depends on this bead).

Out: measuring the saving (the race ledger N28b-08 and the gate N28b-19); the allocation rules themselves; the
program-level ceilings of the ten-fold test.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** N designs per stratum; the admitted scorer per rung with its estimated cost per design c_r
(from the cost and memory estimator, with its interval and its evidence label); k finalists wanted; the
allocation rule and, for the adaptive rule, the current margin q̂ and the lower-rung score distribution (or,
before any score exists, a prior on the surviving fraction: DESIGN default one quarter); the audit share
(default one tenth); referee episodes per verdict (TARGET 16 / 64 / 256 at the 50th / 95th / 99th
percentile); the rare-failure target (p*, δ) with n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉ nominal episodes as the planning figure.

**2. The model (per stratum; times in seconds, exact rationals).**

- *Train everything:* C_all = N · c_top, with c_top the specialist rung's cost.
- *Fixed table, η = 3:* n₁ = N, n_{r+1} = ⌈n_r/3⌉; C_table = Σ_r n_r · c_r.
- *Conformal survivors:* C_conf = N·c₁ + |V|·c₂ + k·c₃ (+ later rungs for the k finalists), with |V| the
  survivor count from the margin, or its predicted value; plus the audit lane, a_share · (C_conf's rung
  spend), charged separately.
- *The floor:* C_floor = N·c₁ + audit + k·(c₃ + c₄ + c₅). No rule that scores every design once at the first
  rung, keeps an audit lane and fully evaluates k finalists can cost less.
- *Headroom of the adaptive rule:* C_table ÷ C_floor. If that is under 1.5 the estimate says the adaptive
  layer cannot matter much for this campaign, before it runs.

**3. The worked case (illustrative, not a measurement; reproduced exactly by the model).** 81 designs with
rungs of 10 s, 3 min and 30 min. Train everything: 81 × 30 min = 40.5 h. Halving at η = 3: 81 × 10 s +
27 × 3 min + 9 × 30 min = 13.5 min + 81 min + 270 min = 364.5 min ≈ 6.1 h. With q̂ = 0.04 on a unit-scaled
score, a third-best lower-rung score of 0.74 puts the cut at 0.66, six designs survive, and with three
finalists the ladder costs 13.5 min + 18 min + 90 min = 121.5 min ≈ 2.0 h, or about 2.2 h with a tenth added
for the audit lane. With q̂ = 0.10 about twenty designs survive, more than 3k = 9, and the adaptive layer gives
back most of its advantage. The size of q̂ is exactly what the rank-agreement ledger measures, which is why
that experiment comes first.

**4. `opt.cascade --estimate` (read-only; runs no physics).** Output `frob.ladder.estimate/1`: per stratum the
admitted scorers and costs with their labels; N, k; C_all, C_table, C_conf (predicted), C_floor, each in
GPU-seconds and CPU-seconds; the headroom; the audit lane's cost; the expected referee and rare-failure
spend; the modes currently in force (a stratum whose first rung is `uninformative` is costed with every
design at the higher rung, and the estimate says so before the campaign starts); and warnings (headroom under
1.5; a rung with no cost record). Every figure carries its label (FACT from our phase ledger, HYPOTHESIS,
TARGET). The conformal cost is printed itemized, under the line names of NEG-08's record, never as one
unqualified "cost with the audit lane": `rung_costs` (per rung, count × unit cost), `audit_lane` in both
readings, each under its own name (`share`: a_share × the rung spend, 12.15 min on the worked case; `count`: the
audit lane's planned size from N28b-13's keyed draw × the time of the rung its designs are scored at, with the
rung named, 19 × 3 min = 57 min on GB-35's card inputs), and `total` for each reading with its formula echoed
(133.65 min and 178.5 min).

**5. The registered prediction.** When a campaign starts, the predicted saving against the fixed table
(C_table ÷ C_conf, an interval propagated from the rung costs' intervals, at confidence 0.8) is registered
through BD-344's primitive under the studio predictor `studio:saving` (the name N28c-14 fixes, as for the race
ledger's prediction of N28b-08), hashed and frozen before the first score, and scored at campaign close against
the measured saving on the same population and addresses. The predicted total spend of the campaign's job is
registered at admission by `studio:estimate` (N28c-14) and is not registered a second time here. Calibration
of these predictions is printed in the campaign report; they are among the studio's own registered predictions.

**6. The check on claims.** A reported ladder saving S (fixed-table cost ÷ measured cost on the same
population and addresses) is rejected by the report generator if S exceeds C_table ÷ C_floor computed from
the same campaign's measured rung costs: a saving above the ceiling means the accounting is wrong (the audit
lane was left out, or a rung's cost was not charged).

**7. Layout and determinism.** `crates/frob-opt/src/ladder/cost.rs`. The model is exact rational arithmetic
on declared inputs (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked case reproduces: 40.5 h; 364.5 min; cut 0.66 with six survivors; 121.5 min; 133.65 min with
      the audit tenth (printed as about 2.2 h).
- [ ] The variance-pairing figures reproduce: 1/(1 − ρ²) = 1.96, 5.26 and 10.26 at ρ = 0.7, 0.9 and 0.95.
- [ ] The estimate (`frob.ladder.estimate/1`, the record `opt.cascade --estimate` prints) returns the five
      costs, the headroom, modes and labels without running any episode (zero dispatches counted) and warns
      when headroom is under 1.5.
- [ ] The conformal cost is itemized: rung lines, the audit lane's share and count readings under their own
      names with the rung named, and a total per reading with its formula.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/ladder_estimate.sh` passes.
- [ ] A stratum in `uninformative` mode is costed with every design at the higher rung.
- [ ] The saving prediction is registered before the first score and scored at close.
- [ ] A reported saving above the campaign's ceiling is rejected with the two numbers.
- [ ] Every figure in the estimate carries an evidence label; none is printed bare.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test ladder_cost`:

- `train_all_81`: 81 × 1,800 s = 145,800 s.
- `halving_81`: (81, 27, 9) × (10, 180, 1,800) s = 810 + 4,860 + 16,200 = 21,870 s.
- `conformal_six_survivors`: 810 + 6 × 180 + 3 × 1,800 = 7,290 s; with audit share 1/10: 8,019 s.
- `floor_81_k3`: 810 + 3 × 1,800 = 6,210 s before audit and later rungs; headroom 21,870/6,210 = 3.52.
- `halving_rounds_up`: N = 80 → (80, 27, 9); N = 10 → (10, 4, 2).
- `uninformative_first_rung`: all 81 at the second rung: 81 × 180 + 27 × 1,800 = 63,180 s.
- `pairing_variance_factor`: the three values above to two decimals.
- `rare_failure_planning_count`: δ = 0.05, p* = 10⁻⁴ → ⌈2.99573/10⁻⁴⌉ = 29,958.
- `saving_above_ceiling_rejected`: a claimed saving of 4.0 with headroom 3.52 is refused.
- `saving_prediction_frozen_before_first_score`: the `studio:saving` record for a campaign is registered with
  the model's interval before the first score; a registration attempted after the first score is refused by
  BD-344's primitive; scored against a measured saving of 2.9 an interval [2.5, 3.5] counts as covered and
  [3.0, 3.5] as missed.
- `estimate_runs_no_physics`: dispatch counter stays at zero.
- `estimate_itemizes_audit_lane`: on the worked case with 19 audit designs at R2, the record holds
  `audit_lane.share` = 729 s and `audit_lane.count` = 19 × 180 = 3,420 s with rung R2 named, totals 8,019 s and
  10,710 s with their formulas, and no line that sums the audit lane into the rung costs.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ladder_estimate.sh`, this bead's own script, built only on its dependencies (`opt.cascade` is
BD-352's and depends on this bead, so the script cannot call it). It sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and
reaches the model through the example program `cargo run -p frob-opt --example ladder_estimate -- --population
<set> --task <id> --k <n> [--rung-costs <s,s,s> --audit-count <n> --audit-rung <R>] [--register-for <planned
campaign>] [--check-saving <S>] --json` (an example program, not a verb). Steps: `frob sim estimate <design> --task <task> --tier <tier> --json`
(BD-219) for one design per admitted scorer, whose figures the estimate must carry with their labels; the
estimate for an 81-design population (five costs, headroom, modes, labels, the itemized audit lines, zero
dispatches); the worked case with declared rung costs 10, 180, 1,800 s, 19 audit designs at R2 (40.5 h,
364.5 min, 121.5 min, 12.15 and 57 min, totals 133.65 and 178.5 min, labelled as an example); a stratum whose
first rung is `uninformative` (every design costed at the higher rung, and said); the saving prediction
for a planned campaign registered through BD-344 (`--register-for`) and read back with `frob predict report --predictor studio:saving --json`, frozen
before any score; `--check-saving 4.0` against headroom 3.52 (rejected with both numbers). Logged per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/ladder_estimate/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, the five costs
with units, the audit lines, headroom against 1.5, the prediction identifier, and on failure the first cost line
that is unlabelled or inconsistent; final `summary` line. The step `estimate` of
`scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (N28b-28) later runs the verb form on the seeded population and checks
measured spend against the registered prediction.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The worked case and the table of supported numbers above (arithmetic, exact).
- Negative controls: an estimate that omits the audit lane (the conformal case then prints 121.5 min as the
  total; the test requires the audit line and the larger figure); a report that charges memo-served episodes
  at zero in the baseline but not in the measured arm (caught by the ceiling check on a planted campaign).
- Metamorphic: scaling every rung cost by a constant leaves all ratios unchanged.
- Determinism: exact arithmetic, identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- Rung costs are the weak input: "tens of GPU-seconds" for the first rung is a HYPOTHESIS until our own phase
  ledger exists; the estimate must keep printing that label until then.
- A phase that accounts for less than nine tenths of elapsed time cannot deliver a ten-fold improvement alone
  even if its cost falls to zero; the estimate therefore prints the ladder's share of the campaign's expected
  elapsed time beside its saving.
- If the rank-agreement gate fails everywhere, the first rung is `uninformative`, the model's "conformal"
  line equals its "second-rung-for-all" line, and the benefit claim for the ladder shrinks to stopping and
  elimination among trained candidates; the estimate shows that state directly.
- The illustrative ratios (40.5 h against 2.2 h) must never be quoted as results; the output labels the
  worked case as an example wherever it prints it.
