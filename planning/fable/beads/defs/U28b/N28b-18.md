## Background

Two Frontier layers of the ladder decide where evaluation effort goes: the conformal survivor rule at the
population stage, and value of information at the finalist stage. The plan funds neither before a paper-scale
pilot with a falsifier has run, and it says in advance what the published evidence suggests: information-gain
selection against a capable agent is unsupported and plausibly worth less than 1.5 times (a language-model
tuner beat random search more often than Gaussian-process optimization at 10 and at 30 evaluations, and
model-based search lost to a genetic algorithm on a robot co-design benchmark; FACT, source register). The
finalist layer is therefore designed as a stop rule and a budget allocator, and this pilot is what would
entitle it to be more.

The pilot is cheap by construction because it simulates once and replays many times. Log once, on an
incumbent engine or on `batch-cpu`: 64 designs × 3 rungs × 32 addresses (about one GPU-day, TARGET). Replay
choosers over the log with no further simulation: an agent reading the log so far, the knowledge-gradient
chooser, e-race elimination, and uniform allocation. Metric: evaluations until a true top-3 design is
identified at 95% confidence, over 100 replays. KILL-LADDER fires if the conformal layer does not reach
halving's audited top-k recall at fewer GPU-seconds; KILL-VOI fires if the knowledge-gradient chooser is less
than 1.5 times better than the agent. Fallbacks: the fixed table alone; value of information as a stop rule
only. Gate GATE-ATEL-15 (earliest M4) is this replay: the knowledge-gradient chooser at least 1.5 times better
than the agent chooser in evaluations to a true top-3, and the value exactly zero on a fixture where no
outcome can change the choice.

Rejected alternatives: piloting on live campaigns (each chooser would then see different episodes and the
comparison would be confounded with simulation noise; the log gives every chooser the same data); expected
information gain as the chooser (it buys knowledge that cannot change the choice, and its estimator fails
under misspecification).

## Scope

In: the log format and its freeze; the replay engine; six choosers behind one interface; the common
identification rule; the metrics; the zero-value fixture; the pilot run and its record; re-running on a log
from our own engine at M4.

Out: producing the log (BD-310); the production value-of-information ranking and `experiment.suggest`
(BD-351); the compiled core tables (BD-349); the live gate of the conformal layer (N28b-19); the kill
decisions (N28b-20, N28b-21).

## Technical approach

**1. The log (`frob.replay-log/1`).** A tensor Y[d, r, a]: the score of design d (64 designs of one
morphology class, drawn by a keyed stream before any score existed) at rung r (shared policy with no
adaptation; fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps; specialist) on world address a (32 addresses, the same for
every design and rung), with the cost in GPU-seconds of each cell, the scorer identities, the engine and its
version, and the seed. The log is content-addressed and its hash is committed before any chooser is run.
*Truth* is defined from the log itself: the true top 3 are the three designs with the highest mean
specialist-rung score over all 32 addresses.

**2. The replay engine.** A replay r ∈ 1…100 fixes, per design and rung, a keyed permutation of the 32
addresses; a chooser repeatedly asks for the next evaluation "(design, rung)" and receives the next unseen
score of that cell in the replay's order, paying the cell's cost. No chooser sees a score it has not bought.
All choosers of a replay see the same permutation, so comparisons between choosers are paired by replay.

**3. Choosers (one interface: `next(state) -> (design, rung) | Stop`).**

| Chooser | Rule |
|---|---|
| `uniform` | Round-robin over designs at the top rung |
| `halving` | The fixed table with η = 3 across the three rungs, on a schedule fixed before the replay |
| `conformal` | The survivor rule with an audit lane of a tenth of rung spend, margin from the audit designs, the 3k trust check and the alarm (the real modules of this unit) |
| `e-race` | Mixture e-process elimination at the top rung, rounds as the only clock |
| `knowledge-gradient` | A Gaussian posterior on each design's top-rung mean with sampling and rung-bias variance components; each step buys the (design, rung) cell with the largest closed-form expected value of sample information per unit cost; stops when no cell clears the price |
| `agent` | A language-model agent, pinned, that reads the log so far through one tool and names the next cell; fresh context per replay; transcripts stored |

The closed form used by `knowledge-gradient`: for the difference Δ ~ N(m, s²) between the current best and a
rival, an experiment that removes variance s̃² has EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃ (φ and Φ the
standard normal density and distribution). For bounded scores the chooser first checks whether any admissible
completion of the unseen scores could change the current top-3 set; if none can, the value is *exactly zero*
and it stops.

**4. The identification rule (the same evaluator for every chooser; it is not part of any chooser).** After
each purchase the evaluator updates paired e-processes at the top rung between designs, on the scores bought
so far (the span is the score's declared range). A design d is *identified as top-3 at 95% confidence* when
the evidence that d ranks below at least three others has not accrued and all but at most two rivals are
eliminated against d at level α = 0.05 by the mixture construction. The metric of a replay is the cumulative
cost (and the count) of evaluations at the first moment any design is identified; a replay that exhausts the
log without identification is censored at the log's total cost.

**5. Metrics reported.** Per chooser over 100 replays: restricted mean cost and count to identification; the
share censored; whether the identified design is in the true top 3 (it must be, in at least 95% of
uncensored replays, or the evaluator is miscalibrated and the pilot is void); for `halving` and `conformal`
also the top-3 recall of their finalists and total GPU-seconds. Paired contrasts: agent ÷ knowledge-gradient
(ratio of restricted means, with a paired bootstrap one-sided 95% lower bound over replays); halving ÷
conformal in GPU-seconds at equal or better recall.

**6. Decisions the pilot informs (rules fixed in the pilot's registration before the log is opened).**
- GATE-ATEL-15 / KILL-VOI: the ratio agent ÷ knowledge-gradient in evaluations to identification. Threshold:
  at least 1.5 (the plan's number, applied to the ratio of restricted means); the lower bound is recorded
  beside it, and a ratio whose lower bound does not exceed 1 is reported as "no difference shown" whatever
  its point value (DESIGN rule, to be confirmed by the Atelier lead in the registration). The zero-value
  fixture must return exactly zero.
- Pilot row of the conformal layer / KILL-LADDER: `conformal` reaches `halving`'s top-3 recall at fewer
  GPU-seconds, or it does not.

**7. Controls.** Potency: a synthetic log with one design far ahead (every chooser must identify it quickly,
and the knowledge-gradient chooser must stop early); a synthetic log where rung 1 is a monotone function of
rung 3 (the conformal chooser must beat halving). Clean case: a synthetic log with 64 identical designs (no
chooser may identify a top-3 design in more than 5% of replays).

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-conformance/src/pilots/chooser_replay/`; registration and results under
`docs/delivery/experiments/chooser-replay/`. Determinism: every chooser except `agent` is a pure function of
the log and the replay key (D0); the agent's transcripts are stored and its replays are reported as run once.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The log's hash and the pilot's registration (choosers, identification rule, thresholds) are in the
      ledger before the first replay.
- [ ] Six choosers run over 100 replays with paired permutations; the non-agent choosers reproduce bit for
      bit on a second run.
- [ ] The evaluator's identified design is in the true top 3 in at least 95% of uncensored replays for every
      chooser; on the 64-identical-designs log it identifies anything in at most 5% of replays.
- [ ] The knowledge-gradient value is exactly zero on the fixture where no outcome can change the choice, and
      the chooser stops.
- [ ] The closed-form values of the worked example reproduce: 0.0013 for 200 more fast-tier pairs and 0.0055
      for 8 referee pairs (m = 0.03, s² = 0.03² + 0.04²), and about 10⁻⁵ for a second fast batch.
- [ ] The pilot record states the two contrasts with their lower bounds and the decision each implies; the
      GATE-ATEL-15 quantities are recorded with measured values.
- [ ] Both potency controls pass (the planted leader is found; conformal beats halving on the monotone log).
- [ ] The harness re-runs unchanged on a log from our own engine for the M4 gate record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test chooser_replay`:

- `evsi_fast_batch`: prior N(0.03, 0.0025) with sampling variance 0.0009 at 100 pairs of per-pair noise 0.3;
  200 more pairs remove 0.0006: EVSI = 0.02449 × [φ(1.2247) − 1.2247·Φ(−1.2247)] = 0.00131.
- `evsi_referee_batch`: 8 referee pairs of per-pair noise 0.1 (observation variance 0.00125) remove 0.001667:
  EVSI = 0.04082 × [φ(0.7348) − 0.7348·Φ(−0.7348)] = 0.00550.
- `evsi_second_fast_batch`: from 300 to 500 pairs removes 0.00012: EVSI ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁵.
- `value_per_cost_order`: 0.0013 per 1 GPU-second against 0.0055 per 27: the fast batch first, by about
  sixfold; after it the referee batch.
- `zero_value_when_choice_fixed`: scores bounded in [0, 1], leader ahead by more than the largest possible
  swing of the unseen addresses: value exactly 0 (integer arithmetic on the bound), chooser returns `Stop`.
- `replay_permutation_is_keyed`; `no_chooser_sees_unbought_score` (the interface gives no access; a
  property test audits the purchase log against what each chooser read).
- `evaluator_null_calibration`: 64 identical synthetic designs, 1,000 replays: identification in ≤ 5%.
- `evaluator_identifies_planted_leader`.
- `paired_bootstrap_lower_bound`: on a synthetic pair of cost vectors with constant ratio 2, the lower bound
  is 2 (the harness's own known answer).
- `censoring_at_log_total`: a chooser that never identifies is charged the log's total cost.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chooser_replay_pilot.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E. It builds a small `synthetic` log
(16 designs × 3 rungs × 16 addresses, seeded), runs the five non-agent choosers and a scripted stand-in for
the agent chooser (a fixed greedy rule, labelled as a script, so the script needs no model access), prints
the metrics table, and runs the two potency logs and the clean log. The registered pilot itself is launched
by `scripts/exp/chooser_replay_run.sh` on the real log with the real agent. Log
`target/e2e-logs/chooser_replay_pilot/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit codes, wall time, the log
hash, per chooser the restricted mean cost and count with units, censored share, top-3 hit rate against 0.95,
the two ratios with lower bounds against 1.5 and 1, the zero-value assertion, and on failure the first replay
in which a non-agent chooser's purchase sequence differs between two runs. Final `summary` line naming
GATE-ATEL-15.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The worked example of the value-of-information core (illustrative inputs, exact arithmetic): the three
  EVSI values above; "at m ≥ 0.15 no experiment can change the choice" is represented by the bounded
  zero-value fixture.
- Negative controls: an evaluator that lets choosers peek (the audit of purchases fails); a chooser scored by
  its own stopping claim instead of the common evaluator (rejected by the harness: choosers cannot report
  identification); a top-3 truth recomputed from the scores bought (it must come from the full log).
- Determinism: non-agent choosers are bit-reproducible; twice on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The agent chooser is the comparator that matters and the least reproducible; its model version, prompt hash
  and transcripts are part of the record, and 100 replays of it are the dominant cost of the pilot.
- One class, 64 designs and 32 addresses give modest power; the registration states the smallest ratio the
  pilot can distinguish from 1 at 100 replays, computed from the synthetic logs.
- A log from an incumbent engine tests the choosers, not our simulator; the M4 gate record repeats the replay
  on a log from our own engine, and a disagreement between the two is itself reported.
- If KILL-VOI fires (the expected outcome on published evidence), nothing is lost that the plan counted on:
  value of information remains the stop rule, and `experiment.suggest` lists proposals unranked by value.
