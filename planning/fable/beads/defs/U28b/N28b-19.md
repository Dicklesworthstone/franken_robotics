## Background

Decision ADR-ATEL-03 makes successive halving the fixed baseline and the fallback of the ladder: every
adaptive allocation rule ships in shadow behind it and reverts to it on a named trigger, because an adaptive
rule can fail in a way that costs validity or the best design, while the fixed table can only cost compute.
ADR-ATEL-09 adds the controller gate: no adaptive controller is enabled by default without a replay scenario,
evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback; adoption is shadow, then canary, then
default. GATE-ATEL-08 is the test the conformal survivor layer must pass to leave shadow. Its threshold
(TARGET), in the form every method gate takes (competing arms, a potency control the method must catch, a
clean case it must pass, a paired lower bound, a fallback named in advance):

- held-out coverage at least 1 − α − 0.03 per stratum;
- audited top-k recall no lower than halving's;
- fewer GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision, at a 95% paired lower bound above 1;
- a planted over-scored design is caught at the next rung;
- synthetic drift trips the alarm.

Its pilot row is due at M3 (the offline replay and the rank-agreement pilot); the gate itself at M4, on our
engine. Kill gate KILL-LADDER fires if this gate fails after one repair cycle; fallback: the fixed halving
table is the allocation rule and the audit lane is kept as measurement. Tier: this bead is a measurement of a
Frontier mechanism.

## Scope

In: the paired-campaign protocol; the five measurements with their estimators; the registration of the gate
run; the gate receipt; the inputs handed to the KILL-LADDER decision.

Out: the mechanisms measured (N28b-13 to N28b-16, BD-329); the decision (N28b-20); the offline pilot
(N28b-18); the full cascade gate (N28b-28).

## Technical approach

**1. Arms and pairing.** Arm T: the fixed table (η = 3) at every transition. Arm C: the conformal survivor
rule where the mode is `selective`, with the audit lane, the trust check and the alarm. Both arms keep the
same audit lane (same keyed draw), so the lane's cost is in both. A *pair* is the two arms on the same seeded
population, the same world addresses at every rung, the same scorers and the same campaign seed. Episodes are
shared through the memo graph where both arms request the same (design, rung, address); each arm is charged
the recorded cost of every episode it requested, whether or not it was served from the memo (so sharing does
not flatter either arm). R pairs with different campaign seeds and population draws: at least ten (DESIGN
default 16), fixed in the registration.

**2. "The same accepted decision".** A campaign's decision is the set of finalists it promotes on the referee
with a settled comparison (or its honest terminal disposition: tied within the margin, inconclusive at this
budget). A pair is *concordant* when both arms end with the same decision. GPU-seconds are compared on
concordant pairs; discordant pairs are counted, listed, and adjudicated against the population's stored full
evaluation (which arm, if either, found the true top k). More than 20% discordant pairs makes the
GPU-seconds comparison descriptive only (DESIGN rule), because the arms are then not buying the same thing.

**3. The five measurements.**

| Measurement | Estimator | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Held-out coverage | Per stratum: among audit designs of generation g + 1, the share whose residual is at most the margin calibrated on generations up to g; pooled over generations and pairs | At least 1 − α − 0.03 (0.92 at α = 0.05), in every stratum with a valid calibration |
| Audited top-k recall | Per arm: the share of the true top k (from the audit lane's higher-rung scores, cross-checked against the stored full evaluation) that the arm advanced, per transition | Arm C's recall no lower than arm T's, per stratum, on the paired difference |
| GPU-seconds to the same decision | Per concordant pair: ratio of arm T's to arm C's GPU-seconds; the performance harness's one-sided 95% paired lower bound over pairs, on the log ratio | Lower bound above 1 |
| Planted over-scored design | A design whose first-rung score is inflated by a fixed offset (it steers easily under the shared policy and is mediocre when trained) | In every pair it advances under arm C and is eliminated at the next rung, with its residual recorded above the margin |
| Synthetic drift | From a fixed generation the generator moves to a region where the first rung's error is three times larger | The alarm fires in the affected stratum in every pair, and later transitions there use the fixed table |

**4. Protocol (numbered).**
1. Register the run (population generator and seeds, R, strata, α, k, the estimators, the discordance rule,
   the repair-cycle rule) and hash the registration into the ledger before the first episode.
2. Run arm C in *shadow* first on two pairs: it decides nothing; both actions are recorded per transition.
   Check that the shadow records are complete (every decision has both actions).
3. Run all R pairs with arm C as default in its own campaigns.
4. Compute the five measurements from the ledger (allocation decisions, margin records, audit pairs, race
   ledgers, phase ledger).
5. Write the gate receipt with measured values. On failure, one repair cycle is allowed: a single change set
   to the mechanism, documented, followed by a full re-run on a fresh population draw with a new
   registration. A second failure is the trigger of KILL-LADDER.

**5. What is reported besides pass or fail.** The observed saving against the campaign's ceiling (the cost
model's headroom); the distribution of survivor counts against 3k; how often the trust check fell back; the
share of strata that were ever `selective`; the audit lane's cost. A pass with a saving near 1 is reported as
that: the layer is safe and buys little.

**6. Demonstrated fallback.** For each fallback trigger of the survivor rule (fewer than 19 calibration
points; martingale at or above 20; audited miss rate above α; survivors above 3k for two generations; a
change of identity or scope), one pair forces the trigger and shows the stratum reverting to the fixed table.
This is the "fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger" clause of the decision-core gate, measured
here for DC-02 and DC-03 because the campaigns already exist.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ATEL-08 is recorded with measured values for all five measurements, the number of pairs,
      concordant and discordant counts, and PASS or FAIL; the registration hash predates the first episode.
- [ ] Held-out coverage is reported per stratum with its count of held-out audit designs; a stratum with
      fewer than 19 calibration points is reported as not calibrated, not as passing.
- [ ] The paired lower bound on the GPU-second ratio is computed by the performance harness and printed with
      the point ratio and the ceiling.
- [ ] The planted over-scored design is caught in every pair; the synthetic drift trips the alarm in every
      pair.
- [ ] Each of the five fallback triggers is forced once and the reversion is recorded.
- [ ] Shadow records hold both actions for every transition of the two shadow pairs.
- [ ] Negative controls are caught (below).
- [ ] The inputs for the KILL-LADDER decision (the receipt, the repair-cycle record if any) are filed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test gate_atel_08` (estimators on `synthetic` ledgers):

- `heldout_coverage_estimator`: 100 held-out residuals of which 93 are within the margin → 0.93 ≥ 0.92 pass;
  91 → fail.
- `recall_paired_difference`: per-transition recalls (1.0, 0.67, 1.0) for arm C and (1.0, 0.67, 0.67) for arm
  T → difference non-negative, pass.
- `gpu_ratio_lower_bound`: sixteen pairs with constant ratio 1.5 → lower bound 1.5 (the harness's own known
  answer); sixteen pairs with ratio 1 and noise → lower bound at or below 1.
- `discordant_pairs_rule`: 4 of 16 discordant (25%) → GPU comparison marked descriptive.
- `memo_shared_episodes_charged_to_both`.
- `overscored_caught`: the planted design's residual exceeds the margin and it is absent from the next
  transition's advanced set.
- `forced_trigger_reverts` for each of the five triggers.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ladder_layer2_gate.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E. On the seeded population at reduced
scale (27 designs, three rungs, two pairs, `batch-cpu`) it runs `frob opt cascade --allocation fixed` and
`--allocation conformal` (BD-352's spelling) with the same seeds, extracts decisions with `frob evidence trace`, and computes the
five measurements with the gate's estimators; it then enables the planted over-scored design and the drift
and re-runs. The full gate run uses `scripts/exp/ladder_layer2_gate_run.sh` with the registered R. Log
`target/e2e-logs/ladder_layer2_gate/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence
blocks, per stratum coverage against 0.92, recall per arm, GPU-seconds per arm and pair with the ratio and its
lower bound against 1, survivor counts against 3k, alarm events, forced-trigger reversions, and on failure
the first transition at which arm C lost a true top-k design that arm T kept. Final `summary` line naming
GATE-ATEL-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Coverage arithmetic: at n = 39 the coverage of one calibration averages 0.95 and falls below 0.885 in one
  draw in twenty; the gate's 0.03 slack is on the pooled held-out estimate, not on a single calibration.
- Clean case: a population in which the first rung is a noiseless monotone function of the specialist rung:
  arm C must match arm T's decision and use fewer GPU-seconds.
- Negative controls: an arm C calibrated on survivors (coverage falls below the threshold and the input is
  refused upstream); an accounting that omits the audit lane from arm C (the saving exceeds the ceiling and
  the report generator rejects it); an arm C that advances only the top k (recall falls below arm T's on the
  population with the planted over-scored design).
- Determinism: every measurement is a pure function of the ledger; the reduced-scale script is run twice and
  must give identical decisions and estimators.

## Considerations

- The expected outcome is uncertain by the plan's own account: whether the survivor rule beats halving
  depends on the size of the margin, which the rank-agreement experiment measures. If the first rung is
  `uninformative` in every class (KILL-RANK), this gate cannot be run for the first rung and is reported as
  `not-run` for it; it can still run for the fine-tune rung against specialists.
- "The same accepted decision" is the hard part of the protocol; without it a rule could look cheaper by
  deciding less. The discordance rule and the adjudication against the full evaluation are the guard.
- The one-time full evaluation of the seeded population is charged to the gate, not to either arm.
- The repair cycle is one cycle. Tuning the mechanism against the gate population repeatedly would turn the
  gate into a training set; the re-run uses a fresh draw.
