## Background

The plan funds no Frontier mechanism without a kill gate decided in advance: a trigger with a number, a
milestone, a measurement, and a fallback that is already built. An upper-tier capability counts as
implemented only when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on record, and a capability that fell back at
its kill gate is described by the name of the fallback. The Frontier mechanism here is layer 2 of the
ladder's allocation rule: conformal survivor sets with an audit lane (decision cores DC-02, adaptive part,
and DC-03). Its baseline and fallback is layer 1, successive halving with η = 3 on a schedule written to the
ledger before the campaign starts.

The reason the fallback is acceptable is the asymmetry that governs the whole chapter: an adaptive rule can
fail in a way that costs validity or the best design; the fixed table can only cost compute. So nothing is
lost in correctness by killing the adaptive layer; what is lost is whatever compute it would have saved,
which the plan already expects to be modest unless the rank agreement between rungs turns out high.

This bead is the decision. It implements nothing; it reads a gate receipt, records a ruling and flips a
registry field.

## Scope

In: the trigger; the evidence the decision reads; who decides and when; the two outcomes and every downstream
change each implies; the record.

Out: the measurement (N28b-19) and its pilot (N28b-18); the mechanisms (N28b-13 to N28b-16, BD-329).

## Technical approach

**Trigger (from the gate register).** KILL-LADDER fires when GATE-ATEL-08 fails after one repair cycle.
GATE-ATEL-08 (TARGET): the conformal layer against the fixed table on the same population and addresses:
held-out coverage at least 1 − α − 0.03 per stratum; audited top-k recall no lower than halving's; fewer
GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision at a 95% paired lower bound above 1; a planted over-scored design
caught at the next rung; synthetic drift trips the alarm. The pilot's form of the same question, read first:
in the offline replay over the logged tensor, the conformal chooser does not reach halving's audited top-k
recall at fewer GPU-seconds.

**Milestone.** Decided at M4, at the milestone of its gate, and recorded as an architecture decision record.
A first reading is taken at M3 from the pilot: if the pilot already fails on an incumbent engine's log, the
M4 work on the adaptive layer is limited to the shadow run needed to measure the gate once on our own engine.

**The measurement that decides.** The gate receipt of N28b-19 (measured values of the five conditions, the
number of concordant pairs, the repair-cycle record if a first run failed) and the pilot record of N28b-18.
The decision is taken by the Atelier lead and reviewed by a fresh-context session that did not implement the
layer; the reviewer recomputes the five measurements from the ledger before the ruling is filed.

**Decision rule.**

| Evidence | Ruling | Registry state |
|---|---|---|
| GATE-ATEL-08 PASS (first run or after the one repair cycle) | Adopt: shadow → canary (one stratum per campaign) → default, each step needing a campaign with complete shadow records and no fallback trigger attributable to a defect | DC-02 (adaptive) and DC-03 `canary`, then `default` |
| GATE-ATEL-08 FAIL after one repair cycle | KILL-LADDER fires | DC-02 adaptive part and DC-03 stay `shadow` permanently; the registry says the core is on its fallback |
| Gate `not-run` (no stratum ever `selective`, for example after KILL-RANK) | No adoption; recorded as not decided on our engine, fallback in force | `shadow` |

**If it fires, what changes downstream.**
- The fixed halving table is the allocation rule at every rung transition. The audit lane is kept as
  measurement: it still feeds the rank-agreement ledger, the screening audit and the margins printed in
  reports.
- The mode `selective` no longer changes who advances; modes still gate what a rung may *discard* (a rung
  that is `uninformative` is still skipped).
- The survivor rule, trust check and alarm keep running in shadow and their would-be actions stay in the
  evidence records, so a later re-examination needs no new code.
- The handover of finalists to the value-of-information layer is unaffected (it is governed by KILL-VOI).
- The cascade's cost estimate uses the fixed-table line; the ladder's benefit claim shrinks to stopping and
  elimination among trained candidates plus whatever the first rung may discard; the ten-fold record states
  which factor was lost.
- Beads that exist only on the adopt branch: the canary and default rollout steps of the adaptive layer
  inside the cascade. They depend on this decision and are not started before it.

**Record.** `docs/delivery/adr/KILL-LADDER.md` and a ledger evidence record: the gate receipt's identifier,
measured values against thresholds, the ruling, the registry diff, the reviewer's session, the date. The
honest-status table is regenerated from it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the gate receipt and the pilot record by identifier, and states the
      ruling with the measured values that justify it.
- [ ] The reviewer session differs from the implementing sessions and its recomputed measurements match the
      receipt.
- [ ] `registries/cores/` shows the adoption status the ruling implies for DC-02 and DC-03, and `cores.show`
      prints it with the fallback in force.
- [ ] If fired: `opt.cascade` on the seeded population allocates by the fixed table at every transition while
      the audit lane and shadow records continue; reports describe the ladder by the fallback's name.
- [ ] If adopted: the canary step's campaign and its shadow comparison are on record before `default`.
- [ ] The ten-fold record and the cost estimate reflect the ruling.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test kill_ladder_switch`: `shadow_status_never_acts` (with status `shadow`, every
allocation decision's rule is `FixedTable` and the record still carries the conformal baseline's would-be
action); `canary_limits_to_one_stratum`; `default_acts_where_selective`; `status_change_is_policy_version`
(flipping the status changes the decision-policy version recorded on decisions).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ladder_switch.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E: it runs `frob opt cascade` on the
reduced seeded population under each of the three registry states (set in a scratch copy of the registry),
asserts the rule cited by every allocation decision and the continued presence of audit-draw and shadow
records, and runs `frob cores show DC-02 --json` to check the printed status and fallback. Log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_ladder_switch/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, the rule
per transition, counts of shadow records, and on failure the first transition whose rule contradicts the
registry state. Final `summary` line naming KILL-LADDER.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The fallback must be shown to work before it is needed: the `shadow` state is the fallback, and the script
  proves the cascade completes under it with the same accepted decision as the fixed table alone.
- Negative control: a registry state `default` with a stale table hash must not act; the core refuses with
  `FROB-REF-871` (a decision-core table that is missing, stale against its contract, or not the registry
  version; repair: regenerate; the fallback is in force meanwhile).
- Determinism: the rule in force is a function of the registry version recorded on each decision.

## Considerations

- A kill gate that is never decided is the failure mode this bead prevents: without a ruling the adaptive
  layer would stay half-adopted. The M4 exit review does not close with this decision missing.
- One repair cycle means one. A second failure fires the gate; a later reopening needs new evidence (for
  example a higher measured rank agreement) and a new registration.
- Passing the gate with a saving near 1 is a legitimate reason to decline adoption: the lead may rule "safe,
  not worth its complexity" and leave the layer in shadow; the record then says so and KILL-LADDER is logged
  as not fired, adoption declined.
