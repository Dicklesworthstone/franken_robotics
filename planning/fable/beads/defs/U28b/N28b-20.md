## Background

The plan funds no Frontier mechanism without a kill gate decided in advance: a trigger with a number, a
milestone, a measurement, and a fallback that is already built. An upper-tier capability counts as
implemented only when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on record, and a capability that fell back at
its kill gate is described by the name of the fallback. A kill gate that fires is not a failure, because the
fallback is the plan. The Frontier mechanism here is layer 2 of the ladder's allocation rule: conformal
survivor sets with an audit lane (decision cores DC-02, adaptive part, and DC-03). Its baseline and fallback
is layer 1, successive halving with η = 3 on a schedule written to the ledger before the campaign starts.
Layer 2 is only worth its complexity if it reaches the same accepted decisions as successive halving for less
compute without losing top designs.

The reason the fallback is acceptable is the asymmetry that governs the whole chapter: an adaptive rule can
fail in a way that costs validity or the best design; the fixed table can only cost compute. So nothing is
lost in correctness by killing the adaptive layer; what is lost is whatever compute it would have saved,
which the plan already expects to be modest unless the rank agreement between rungs turns out high. For a
Frontier decision core the fallback is also its kill fallback: if the core's calibration fails its gate after
one repair cycle, the core stays on its fallback, and the registry says so.

This bead is the decision. It implements nothing; it reads a gate receipt, records a ruling and flips a
registry field. It is the only bead that decides KILL-LADDER (the key N28a-25 was merged into it).

## Scope

In: the trigger; the evidence the decision reads; who decides and when; the two outcomes and every downstream
change each implies; the registry state of DC-02 and DC-03; the record.

Out: the measurement (N28b-19) and its pilot (N28b-18, on the log of BD-310); the mechanisms (N28b-13 to
N28b-16, assembled by BD-329). The mechanism being measured does not depend on this decision; this decision
depends on the measurement.

## Technical approach

**Trigger (from the gate register).** KILL-LADDER fires when GATE-ATEL-08 fails after one repair cycle.
GATE-ATEL-08 (TARGET): the conformal layer against the fixed table on the same population and addresses:
held-out coverage at least 1 − α − 0.03 per stratum; audited top-k recall no lower than halving's; fewer
GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision at a 95% paired lower bound above 1; a planted over-scored design
caught at the next rung; synthetic drift trips the alarm. The pilot's form of the same question, read first:
in the offline replay over the logged tensor, the conformal chooser does not reach halving's audited top-k
recall at fewer GPU-seconds.

**Milestone.** Decided at M4, at the milestone of its gate, and recorded as an architecture decision record.
A first reading is taken at M3 from the pilot: it sets the starting posture (shadow behind the fixed table)
and cannot by itself adopt layer 2. If the pilot already fails on an incumbent engine's log, the M4 work on
the adaptive layer is limited to the shadow run needed to measure the gate once on our own engine.

**The measurement that decides.** The gate receipt of N28b-19, for the first run and, if it failed, for the
run after one repair cycle: per-stratum coverage; top-k recall of both rules from the audit lane; the paired
lower bound on the ratio of GPU-seconds (halving over layer 2) with the number of concordant pairs; the two
controls (the planted over-scored design; the synthetic drift); the repair-cycle record. Beside it, the pilot
record of N28b-18. The decision is taken by the Atelier lead and reviewed by a fresh-context session that
did not implement the layer and did not run the measurement; the reviewer recomputes the five measurements
from the ledger before the ruling is filed.

**Decision rule.**

| Evidence | Ruling | Registry state |
|---|---|---|
| GATE-ATEL-08 PASS, all five conditions, on the first run or after the one repair cycle | Not fired. Adopt: shadow → canary (one stratum per campaign) → default, each step needing a campaign with complete shadow records and no fallback trigger attributable to a defect; adoption advances no further than `canary` until the core-level gate (the GATE-ATEL-13 rows of DC-02 and DC-03) has also passed. The measured ratio replaces any target in the claims table | DC-02 (adaptive) and DC-03 `canary`, then `default` |
| The gate fails on the first run and again on the run after the one repair cycle, on any of the three measured conditions (coverage, recall, cost bound; not necessarily the same condition on both runs), or the first run fails and no repair cycle is registered by the decision date | KILL-LADDER fires | DC-02 adaptive part and DC-03 set to `fallback-only`; the registry says the core is on its fallback |
| A control fails on either run (the over-scored design is not caught, or drift does not trip the alarm) | KILL-LADDER fires without a repair cycle for the control itself (DESIGN): a calibration that cannot see its own failure is not adopted | `fallback-only` |
| Gate `not-run` (no stratum ever `selective`, for example after KILL-RANK) | No adoption; recorded as not decided on our engine, fallback in force | `shadow` |

**Fallback, named in advance.** The fixed halving table is the allocation rule; the audit lane is kept as
measurement.

**If it fires, what changes downstream.**
- The fixed halving table is the allocation rule at every rung transition, in every stratum. The audit lane
  still runs at a tenth of rung spend and is kept as measurement: it still feeds the rank-agreement ledger,
  the screening audit and the margins printed in reports; its results no longer set a margin that acts.
- `registries/cores/dc-02.toml` and `registries/cores/dc-03.toml` (the record files of the core contract):
  `status = "fallback-only"`, with the kill gate `KILL-LADDER` and the identifier of the decision record. In
  the gate register the KILL-LADDER row reads `killed: fallback adopted`.
- The mode `selective` has no rule to apply and no longer changes who advances: strata that qualify for it
  are treated as `discard-only` (the fixed table), and reports print "k survivors". Modes still gate what a
  rung may *discard* (a rung that is `uninformative` is still skipped).
- The survivor rule, trust check and alarm stay compiled; their would-be actions continue to be written to
  the evidence records as they are in shadow and act on nothing, so a later re-examination needs no new code.
- The handover of finalists to the value-of-information layer is by count alone (at most 64 survivors of the
  last population rung); what happens after the handover is governed by KILL-VOI, not by this gate.
- The cascade's cost estimate uses the fixed-table line; the ladder's benefit claim shrinks to stopping and
  elimination among trained candidates plus whatever the first rung may discard; the ten-fold arithmetic
  keeps only halving's saving for the population stage, and the ten-fold record states which factor was lost.
- Beads that exist only on the adopt branch: the canary and default rollout steps of the adaptive layer
  inside the cascade. They depend on this decision and are not started before it.

**Record.** The decision record at the location the kill-gate record convention fixes (DESIGN default
`docs/delivery/adr/ADR-KILL-LADDER-M4.md`) and a ledger evidence record: the gate receipt's identifier, measured values against
thresholds for each run made, the population identity, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, the ruling,
the registry diff, the reviewer's session, the date. The kill row in the gate register is updated and the
honest-status table is regenerated from it. A decision recorded before the gate row's ledger sequence is
rejected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record exists, cites the gate receipt and the pilot record by identifier, and states the
      ruling with the measured values that justify it: coverage per stratum, both recalls, the cost ratio
      with its bound and both controls, for each run made.
- [ ] The reviewer session differs from the implementing sessions and from the session that ran the
      measurement, and its recomputed measurements match the receipt.
- [ ] `registries/cores/` shows the adoption status the ruling implies for DC-02 and DC-03, the gate register
      carries the decision, and `cores.show` prints the status with the fallback in force.
- [ ] If fired: `frob opt cascade` with default settings on the seeded population allocates by the fixed
      table at every transition in every stratum (test) while the audit lane and the evidence records
      continue; `report.campaign` lists the fallback among the fallbacks that fired and describes the ladder
      by the fallback's name.
- [ ] If not fired: adoption advances no further than `canary` until the core-level gate has also passed,
      and the canary step's campaign and its shadow comparison are on record before `default`.
- [ ] A decision recorded before the gate row's ledger sequence is rejected.
- [ ] The ten-fold record and the cost estimate reflect the ruling.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test kill_ladder_switch`:
- `kill_ladder_rule_table`: (all pass) → not fired; (recall lower, then pass after repair) → not fired;
  (recall lower twice) → fired; (cost bound 0.97 twice) → fired; (recall lower, then cost bound 0.97 after
  the repair) → fired; (coverage below 0.92, no repair registered) → fired; (drift alarm silent) → fired at
  once.
- `shadow_status_never_acts` (with status `shadow`, every allocation decision's rule is `FixedTable` and the
  record still carries the conformal baseline's would-be action); `fallback_only_never_acts`;
  `canary_limits_to_one_stratum`; `default_acts_where_selective`; `status_change_is_policy_version`
  (flipping the status changes the decision-policy version recorded on decisions).
- `fired_forces_fixed_table`; `audit_lane_still_runs_when_fired`; `selective_treated_as_discard_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ladder_switch.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E. Part one (the record): it reads the
gate rows and the registry, applies the rule table, and compares the result with the decision record. Part
two (the switch): it runs `frob opt cascade` on the reduced seeded population (nine designs suffice for the
fired state) under each registry state (`shadow`, `canary`, `default`, `fallback-only`, set in a scratch copy
of the registry), asserts the rule cited by every allocation decision, the continued presence of audit-draw
and evidence records and the report line of the fallback, and runs `frob cores show dc-02 --json` to check
the printed status and fallback. Log `target/e2e-logs/kill_ladder_switch/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit
codes, wall time, evidence blocks, the five inputs against thresholds per run, the decision, the adoption
status, the decision-record path, the rule per transition, counts of shadow records, and on failure the
first transition whose rule contradicts the registry state. Final `summary` line naming KILL-LADDER.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The rule fixtures of `kill_ladder_rule_table`.
- The fallback must be shown to work before it is needed: the script proves the cascade completes under
  `shadow` and under `fallback-only` with the same accepted decision as the fixed table alone.
- Negative controls: a register row with targets in place of measurements (rejected); an adoption status of
  `default` with no passed gate (rejected by the registry check); a registry state `default` with a stale
  table hash must not act: the core refuses with `FROB-REF-871` (a decision-core table that is missing,
  stale against its contract, or not the registry version; repair: regenerate; the fallback is in force
  meanwhile).
- Determinism: the rule in force is a function of the registry version recorded on each decision.

## Considerations

- A kill gate that is never decided is the failure mode this bead prevents: without a ruling the adaptive
  layer would stay half-adopted. The M4 exit review does not close with this decision missing.
- One repair cycle means one: one change to the layer's implementation or defaults followed by one
  re-measurement on a fresh keyed population draw; it is not permission to tune until the gate passes. A
  second failure fires the gate.
- Reopening needs new evidence (for example a higher measured rank agreement) and a new registered
  measurement; a different α or audit share is a new decision-policy version.
- Passing the gate with a saving near 1 is a legitimate reason to decline adoption: the lead may rule "safe,
  not worth its complexity" and leave the layer in shadow; the record then says so and KILL-LADDER is logged
  as not fired, adoption declined.
- KILL-RANK is separate: it concerns whether the cheap rung carries rank information at all, and is decided
  with the rank-agreement experiment.
