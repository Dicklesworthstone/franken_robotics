## Background

At the finalist stage of the ladder, and in the verb that suggests the next experiment, the studio can rank
information-gathering actions (more fast-tier pairs, a referee batch, a fine-tune, a bench measurement) by
their expected value of sample information per unit cost and buy them in that order until none clears the
budget price. That ranking is Frontier (decision core DC-05), and the plan is frank that the published
evidence does not support it against a capable agent: a language-model tuner beat random search more often
than Gaussian-process optimization at 10 and at 30 evaluations, and model-based search lost to a genetic
algorithm on a robot co-design benchmark. The mechanism is therefore designed so that being wrong is cheap.
Decision ADR-ATEL-07: parametric models rank experiments; distribution-free tests issue verdicts. The
value-of-information model never issues a verdict, so a wrong model costs efficiency and never validity.

KILL-VOI decides whether the ranking earns its place. Two kill gates bear on experiment suggestion and do not
overlap: KILL-AUGUR-PROBE governs the probe generator; KILL-VOI governs the ranking. This bead is the
KILL-VOI decision. It implements nothing.

## Scope

In: the trigger; the evidence; who decides and when; the outcomes and what each changes downstream; the
record; the re-measurement of GATE-ATEL-15 on our own engine's log at M4.

Out: the replay harness (N28b-18); the ranking and the verb (BD-351); the DC-05 table (BD-349).

## Technical approach

**Trigger.** KILL-VOI fires when GATE-ATEL-15 fails. GATE-ATEL-15 (TARGET; earliest M4): the offline replay
over the logged tensor of 64 designs × 3 rungs × 32 addresses: the knowledge-gradient chooser is at least 1.5
times better than the agent chooser in evaluations to a true top-3 design identified at 95% confidence, over
100 replays; and the value is exactly zero on a fixture where no outcome can change the choice. In the
pilot's words: the gate fires if the knowledge-gradient chooser is less than 1.5 times better than the agent.

**Milestone.** Decided at M4 and recorded as an architecture decision record. The pilot gives a first reading
at M3 on a log from an incumbent engine or `batch-cpu`; the gate value is taken at M4 from the same harness
re-run on a log produced by our own engine with our own scorers (family policy with no adaptation, fine-tune
at 5%, specialist).

**The measurement that decides.** The record of N28b-18: the ratio of restricted mean evaluations (agent ÷
knowledge gradient) with its paired one-sided 95% lower bound; the evaluator's calibration (top-3 hit rate at
least 95%, null identification at most 5%); the zero-value fixture; the agent's model version and transcripts.
Decided by the Atelier lead; reviewed by a fresh-context session that recomputes the ratio from the purchase
logs.

**Decision rule.**

| Evidence | Ruling | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Ratio at least 1.5 with a lower bound above 1, evaluator calibrated, zero-value fixture exact | Not fired | DC-05's ranking proceeds through shadow, canary and default; `experiment.suggest` ranks by value per cost |
| Ratio below 1.5, or lower bound at or below 1 | KILL-VOI fires | Fallback below |
| Evaluator miscalibrated or zero-value fixture not exact | Pilot void; repair the harness and re-run once before ruling | — |

**Fallback, and what changes downstream if it fires.**
- Value of information is a *stop rule only*: the finalist layer still stops buying when no admissible
  experiment can change the choice or none clears the price, and still refuses to spend past that point.
- `experiment.suggest` lists proposals *unranked by value*: each proposal keeps its testable prediction, its
  cost, its model class and the direct-measurement comparator; the list is ordered by the fixed experiment
  order (check, simulate, train, referee), which is DC-05's deterministic fallback.
- The handover of finalists stays one-way; after it, work is ordered by the fixed order instead of by value.
- Nothing about verdicts changes: they come from the promotion core and the comparison rules either way.
- The registered-prediction machinery is unaffected; the calibration trigger of DC-05 (coverage of 80%
  intervals outside 65 to 92% over 30 experiments) remains a second, independent route to the same fallback.
- The cost estimate drops any saving attributed to value-ordered experiments; the ten-fold record notes it.
- Beads on the "not fired" branch only: the canary and default rollout of DC-05's ranking. They depend on
  this decision.

**Invariant that holds on both branches.** A value-of-information quantity never appears in a verdict field
(INV-ATEL-12); citing one as a verdict is refused with `FROB-REF-872` (repair: run the comparison; exit 3).

**Record.** `docs/delivery/adr/KILL-VOI.md` and a ledger evidence record: the pilot and gate records'
identifiers, the measured ratio and bound against 1.5 and 1, the ruling, the registry diff for DC-05, the
reviewer's session.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-ATEL-15 is recorded at M4 with measured values from a log produced by our own engine: the ratio,
      its lower bound, the number of replays, the censored shares, the zero-value result.
- [ ] The decision record cites those values and the M3 pilot's, states the ruling, and is reviewed by a
      session that recomputed the ratio.
- [ ] The adoption status of DC-05 in `registries/cores/` matches the ruling; `cores.show` prints it.
- [ ] If fired: `experiment.suggest` returns proposals without a value ranking, in the fixed order, each with
      its prediction and direct-measurement comparator; the finalist layer orders work by the fixed order and
      still stops when no outcome can change the choice.
- [ ] On both branches: no value-of-information quantity in any verdict field of the seeded campaign's
      results; `FROB-REF-872` fires on a planted citation.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test kill_voi_switch`: `fallback_lists_unranked_in_fixed_order` (four proposals
come back as check, simulate, train, referee regardless of their computed values);
`stop_rule_survives_fallback` (a fixture where no outcome can change the choice returns STOP under both
states); `ranked_when_not_fired` (value per cost descending); `voi_never_in_verdict_872`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_voi_switch.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E: under each registry state for DC-05 it
runs `frob experiment suggest <finalists> --budget … --json` on the reduced seeded population and
`frob opt cascade` through the finalist stage; asserts the ordering rule in force, the STOP on the fixed
fixture, the one-way handover, and the absence of value quantities in verdict fields. Log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_voi_switch/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, the
proposal order with values and costs, and on failure the first proposal out of order for the state in force.
Final `summary` line naming KILL-VOI and GATE-ATEL-15.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The worked example of DC-05 (illustrative): with m = 0.03 and s² = 0.03² + 0.04², 200 more fast-tier pairs
  are worth 0.0013 for 1 GPU-second and 8 referee pairs 0.0055 for 27; the fast tier wins sixfold per unit
  cost; a second fast batch is worth about 10⁻⁵, so the referee goes next; at m ≥ 0.15 no experiment can
  change the choice. Under the "not fired" state the suggestion order must follow these values; under the
  fallback it must not depend on them.
- Negative control: a suggestion list that hides the direct-measurement comparator (the list must always
  carry it, on either branch; the counterexample is a probe that was right in 675 of 675 in-class cases and
  reversed all 225 held-out decisions, while direct measurement was right in 900 of 900).
- Determinism: the order in force is a function of the registry version recorded on each decision.

## Considerations

- The plan expects this gate to be close or to fire. That is acceptable by design: the fallback is free, and
  the honest statement "agents order experiments about as well as the closed form does" is itself a finding
  about where the studio's value lies.
- The comparison depends on which agent is used; the model version is part of the record, and a re-run with a
  different agent is a new registration, not a correction of this ruling.
- OPEN-ATEL-1 (the exchange rate between GPU-seconds and real experiment cost) affects only rankings that
  mix simulation with bench work; the replay uses simulation cells only, so the gate does not wait on it.
