## Background

The ladder is only as good as the agreement between adjacent rungs, and that agreement has not been published
for the case that matters. What published neighbours report (FACT, source register): the value predictions of
a shared policy correlate with rollouts of the same policy at a rank correlation of 0.40 overall and 0.50,
0.60 and 0.83 on three robots, over 17 designs, which is an easier comparison than a shared policy against
specialists; in architecture search with shared weights, sparse Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three
benchmarks; a universal controller reaches up to 80% of individually trained controllers; and in the
17-design study the top design was the same under both scores. Agreement of 0.7 or better near the top is
therefore not supported, top-1 agreement at modest correlation is plausible, and the default, until a stratum
is measured, is that the first rung neither selects nor discards (counterexample CX-318: rank agreement near
the top can be assumed).

The rank-agreement ledger measures agreement and gives the measurement consequences. Its rows come from two
sources: one calibration experiment per morphology class, and the audit lane of every campaign, continuously.
The calibration experiment is the decisive early experiment of the program: forty designs in one class are
each scored by the shared policy on the fast tier, by a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps, and by a
specialist promoted on the referee; it reports Kendall τ on the top band, top-10 recall and bootstrap
intervals, and ρ_ep on 256 paired episodes per design; it costs about 45 GPU-hours, or about 5 if the
specialists of the family-policy experiment are reused (TARGET). It is built, with the verb
`eval.rank-agreement`, by the learning unit. This
bead is the second source and the row discipline both share: how a row is keyed, versioned, reset and traced.
Tier: Frontier (the ledger gates a Frontier mechanism); the row handling itself is plain bookkeeping that
must be exactly right, because a row pooled across two policies would license a rung on evidence about a
different rung.

Rejected alternatives: one global rank-agreement number per rung pair (the cheap rung's bias is not uniform
over design space, so a global number hides the strata where it fails); updating a row in place (a decision
made last week must be traceable to the row as it was then); recomputing agreement from survivors' scores
(selected by the lower rung, hence biased toward agreement).

## Scope

In: the row's key and measures as stored; append-only row versions pinned to ledger frontiers; the update
from published audit pairs; identity scope and the reset rule; regimes after an alarm; strata; provenance;

Out: the statistics' definitions and the calibration experiment (BD-328, tier pairs BD-265); modes and their

## Technical approach

**1. The row (the plan's layout; stored as `frob.rank-agreement/1` in the ledger table `rank_agreement`).**

```
key        morphology class · task family · rung pair (lo, hi) · stratum
           scorer identities (policy and kernel versions) · effort profile · battery version · regime
measures   n_designs
           Kendall τ on the top band, with a bootstrap interval     (top band = top m of either rung, m = max(10, 3k))
           top-k recall of lo against hi
           Spearman ρ over the stratum
           mean signed residual per region                           (the bias of the cheap rung)
           conformal margin q̂ at α · exchangeability martingale value
           per-episode cross-tier correlation ρ_ep on paired addresses (for the specialist → referee pair)
           containment_rank(m) · regret of the applied cut
provenance source (calibration-experiment | audit-lane) · audit-draw records · claim_id · recording session
version    sequence number within the key · ledger frontier at which it was computed
```

`regime` is a counter that the exchangeability alarm increments (DESIGN addition, so that rows before and
after a voided calibration are distinct keys). Rows are immutable; an update appends a new version.

**2. Update from the audit lane (numbered; runs at each generation boundary of a campaign).**
1. Collect the audit pairs published since the row's last version for the key (same stratum, rung pair,
   identity scope, regime), with their `audit-draw` references.
2. Form the cumulative set: all audit pairs of the key to date, across campaigns. Pairs are never dropped
   except by a reset (step 4) or a regime change.
3. Recompute every measure with the shared statistics library (the same code the calibration experiment
   uses): τ on the top band with its bootstrap interval (designs resampled with replacement, 2,000 resamples
   from an addressed stream; the lower 95% bound is the 5th percentile), top-k recall, Spearman ρ, signed
   residual per region, q̂ and the martingale value from the calibration bead, ρ_ep from the tier-correction
   ledger where the pair is specialist → referee, the containment rank and regret.
4. Append the new version with `source: audit-lane`, the frontier, and the list of draws.
A measure whose sample is too small is stored as `insufficient` with its count: τ on the top band needs at
least m designs with both scores; the margin needs at least 19 calibration residuals; ρ_ep needs at least 200
paired addresses. `insufficient` is never rendered as a number.

**3. Identity scope and resets.** Evidence and calibration are never pooled across model identities. A change
of model, kernel version, effort profile or battery version resets the affected ledger rows (INV-ATEL-05). In
this ledger "model" is the scorer on either side of the pair (a new family-policy checkpoint is a new
scorer). Because the scope is part of the key, a change simply starts a new key with n_designs = 0; the old
rows remain, readable and cited by old decisions, and are no longer extended. A request to combine rows of
two scopes (to reach the sample floor sooner, say) is refused with `FROB-REF-548` (pooling evidence or
calibration across two model identities; repair: restart the ledger rows in the new identity; exit 3).

**4. Strata.** OPEN-ATEL-2 is open; the default is one stratum per morphology class. The stratum is a field
of the key, so the later choice (blocks of archive cells; class by task family) changes keys, not code. When
the audit lane merged starved strata upward to reach 19 points, the row is written for the merged stratum and
names its members; the member strata have no row of their own and are therefore unmeasured.

**5. Unmeasured means uninformative.** A (class, task family, rung pair, stratum, scope) with no row, or with
`insufficient` τ, is unmeasured. The mode logic reads that as `uninformative`, and a rung asked to select or
discard there is refused with `FROB-REF-537` (a rung used to select or discard without a rank-agreement
record that meets its threshold; repairs: run the rank-agreement experiment; use the rung as advisory). The
synthesized baseline controller is a scorer like any other and has no exemption.

**6. Reads.** The allocation site reads the latest version of a key *at a pinned ledger frontier* and cites
(key, version) in the `rank_record` field of its allocation decision. A decision therefore names exactly the
evidence that licensed it, and a replay at the same frontier reads the same row.

**7. Provenance and claims.** Every row version is an evidence item with a `claim_id` (claim kind Design
comparison, class Tested: "at this frontier, in this scope, τ on the top band is at least L") and lists the
audit draws or the calibration experiment it rests on. The pre-registered thresholds (0.7 and 0.5) are not
stored in the row; they live in the registration and the mode policy, so a row cannot be made to pass by
editing a threshold.

**8. Layout and determinism.** `crates/frob-opt/src/ladder/rank_ledger.rs`; table in `frob-chronicle`.
Updates are pure functions of the cumulative audit pairs and the addressed bootstrap stream (D0 given the
scores).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A campaign's audit lane appends one row version per key per generation, with `source: audit-lane`, the
      frontier and the draws; earlier versions are unchanged.
- [ ] Measures equal those of `eval.rank-agreement` run on the same pairs (one statistics library; a
      differential test compares the two entry points).
- [ ] A new policy checkpoint, kernel version, effort profile or battery version starts a new key with
      n_designs = 0; the old key receives no further versions.
- [ ] Pooling two scopes is refused with `FROB-REF-548`.
- [ ] An exchangeability alarm increments `regime` for the key; rows of the old regime are not extended.
- [ ] `insufficient` is stored and printed for under-sized measures (fewer than m designs; fewer than 19
      residuals; fewer than 200 paired addresses).
- [ ] An allocation decision's `rank_record` resolves to the exact row version read, and a replay at the same
      frontier reads the same version.
- [ ] A merged stratum's row names its members, and each member reads as unmeasured.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test rank_ledger`:

- `append_only_versions`: three generations → versions 1, 2, 3; version 1's bytes unchanged.
- `cumulative_across_campaigns`: two campaigns in one scope contribute 8 and 12 audit pairs → n_designs = 20.
- `scope_change_starts_new_key`: a new `PolicyId` on the lower rung → new key, n = 0.
- `pooling_refused_548`.
- `regime_increment_on_alarm`.
- `top_band_definition`: k = 3 → m = max(10, 9) = 10; k = 5 → m = 15; the band is the union of the top m of
  each rung.
- `insufficient_measures`: 8 designs with k = 3 → τ `insufficient (8 < 10)`; 18 residuals → margin
  `insufficient`; 150 paired addresses → ρ_ep `insufficient`.
- `kendall_small_case`: lower-rung order (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) against higher-rung order (1, 3, 2, 4, 5): one
  discordant pair of ten, τ = (9 − 1)/10 = 0.8.
- `winner_check_fixture`: forty designs, the cheap rung exact except that the true best is ranked third: τ on
  the true top ten = 41/45; containment rank 3 for m = 1.
- `pinned_read_is_stable`: appending versions after the pin does not change what a pinned read returns.
- `merged_stratum_members_unmeasured`.
- `bootstrap_stream_is_addressed`: the interval is identical across runs and worker counts.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `rank_ledger` of `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28): after two campaigns on the
seeded population, the row versions are read back through `frob evidence trace` on the `rank_record` of
each allocation decision and through the campaign report's ledger section; the script asserts their count, sources, n_designs, the equality of measures with a recomputation from the published
audit pairs, and that every allocation decision's `rank_record` resolves. It then swaps the family-policy
checkpoint and asserts that the next campaign reads the stratum as unmeasured (and that a selecting request
is refused with 537). Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, row keys and
versions, τ with its interval, recall, q̂, and on failure the first row whose recomputed measure differs.

### Known answers and negative controls

- CX-318 (rank agreement near the top can be assumed): the published neighbours' figures above; the rule it
  protects is that an unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`.
- The forty-design winner-check fixture: τ = 41/45 ≈ 0.911 and containment rank 3.
- Negative controls: a row extended with survivors' pairs (the update refuses any pair without an
  `audit-draw` reference); a row updated in place (the version-1 byte check fails); a threshold stored in the
  row and edited (there is no such field; the schema test asserts its absence).
- The Gauntlet's own check of this ledger (planted populations with known top-weighted rank correlation on
  each side of each threshold): the measured top-band τ covers the planted value in its bootstrap interval.
- Determinism: identical rows twice, across worker counts, and with campaigns interleaved in either order.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-2 (owner: Atelier lead; blocks GATE-ATEL-07). Fail-closed default: one stratum per morphology
  class; an unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`.
- The audit lane samples the populations campaigns actually visit, which drift toward regions the optimizer
  likes. The row therefore describes the rung on visited designs; the alarm and the regime counter keep an
  old description from being applied to a new region.
- Cumulative rows grow slowly (tens of designs per campaign); the cost of the bootstrap is negligible.
- If KILL-RANK fires, rows keep accumulating: the measurement continues even when the first rung is
  `uninformative` everywhere, which is what would justify reopening the question later.
