## Background

The six-stage co-design cascade is the ladder applied to a population, with a control built into every
stage. `opt.cascade` runs it; each stage is also callable alone.

| Stage | Content | Rungs | Output | Control built into the stage | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Deduplicate and check | One canonical representative per orbit under the observation-preserving subgroup; the mission-observable digest; `design.check` with the bound analyses | R0 | Representatives; typed exclusions | Two designs that must merge and two that must split | Population subsystem, `frob-analyze` |
| 2 Reuse and synthesize | Memo hits for analyses, controllers, step plans and episodes; module contracts reused where the edit is local; bounded exact linkage synthesis when the brief asks for a mechanism | R0 | Reuse receipts; candidate set | A mirror swap must reuse; a shared-bus edit must invalidate | `frob-opt`, design and mission subsystems |
| 3 Screen | Gait-orbit screening (A-04), family policy with no adaptation, task-wrench attainability (A-24) | R1 | Survivor set with the mode of each stratum | The blinded stratified audit of discards and the planted winner | `frob-opt`, control and learning subsystems |
| 4 Race | A mixed-morphology race on paired addresses; fine-tuning for survivors, specialists for finalists | R1 to R3 | Finalists, `provisional` | A null race keeps the best with probability at least 1 − α | `frob-opt`, population subsystem |
| 5 Rank robustness | A-19: the top-k and the runners-up re-evaluated under model-form variants, with the created-energy audit | R3, R4 | Each finalist typed `model-robust` or `model-conditional` | Planted cone and energy-pumping exploits must be caught | `frob-analyze`, fidelity and contact subsystems |
| 6 Qualify | Sealed confirmation on fresh world clusters with pre-committed requests; promotion on the referee; the rare-failure bound | R4, R5 | A qualified design comparison with receipts | False-promotion rate at most α under adaptive retries on no-signal worlds | Arena, adversarial subsystem |

The controls are the point. The plan's rule is that a gate nothing can fail validates nothing, and the
cascade applies it at run time: each stage carries a planted case it must catch and a clean case it must
pass, so that a campaign's own record shows its machinery was working on that population. Two further rules
come with it. **Every stage may end the campaign honestly:** "every admitted alternative fails", "tied within
the margin" and "inconclusive at this budget" are results. And a stage that cannot run its control (no
planted winner available, no second contact law admitted) reports the control as `not-run`, and the result
carries that. Tier: Solid for the controls (the cascade as a whole is Frontier).

The loop's order is itself a rule (decision ADR-ATEL-01: check before you simulate, simulate before you
train, train before you claim; each stage is cheaper than the next by roughly an order of magnitude, a
HYPOTHESIS settled by the phase ledger, and each can refuse the next). Invariant INV-ATEL-01 makes it
checkable: no stage of the loop runs on a design the previous stage refused, unless the request carries an
override that is recorded on every downstream result.

Rejected alternatives: controls run once in a test suite and assumed thereafter (a rung's behaviour depends
on the population; a control inside the campaign tests the rung where it is used); a campaign that always
returns a ranked list (it would rank noise when nothing passed); silently skipping a control that cannot run.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**1. Interfaces (DESIGN; names fix intent).**

```rust
pub trait Stage {
    fn id(&self) -> StageId;                                    // 1..=6
    fn run(&mut self, input: StageInput, cx: &CampaignCx) -> Result<StageOutput, Refusal>;
    fn control(&self) -> &dyn StageControl;
}
pub trait StageControl {
    fn plan(&self, manifest: &CampaignManifest) -> ControlPlan;     // Planned(items) | NotRunnable(reason)
    fn evaluate(&self, evidence: &StageEvidence) -> ControlOutcome;  // Passed | Failed(findings) | NotRun(reason)
}
pub enum Disposition { Ranked, EveryAdmittedAlternativeFails, TiedWithinMargin, InconclusiveAtThisBudget }
```

A control *plan* is made before the stage scores anything and is part of the campaign manifest hash (the
planted items, their sealed identities, the expected outcome of each). A control is *evaluated* when the
stage closes, from the ledger.

**2. The control record (`frob.stage-control/1`).** `{ campaign, stage, control: name, planned: [item
hashes], outcome: "passed" | "failed" | "not-run", reason?, findings: [...], evidence: [EvidenceId],
action_taken }`. It is an evidence item. `action_taken` names what the failure caused (for stage 3: the rung
set to `uninformative` and the label `screen-biased`; for stage 1: the quotient disabled and deduplication by
canonical form only; and so on, each defined by the stage's own bead).

**3. `not-run`.** A control is `not-run` only for a reason of the registered list
(`registries/cascade/not-run-reasons.toml`, DESIGN; a reason is added by registering it there with the bead
whose control uses it): `no-planted-winner`, `no-second-contact-law`, `no-planted-exploit`,
`trivial-symmetry` and `no-mirror-pair` (the halves of the stage 1 and 2 controls, N28b-26), `no-exclusions`
(the prune audit of stage 1, N28b-30, after KILL-STATIC), `stage-skipped`, `budget`. The campaign result's `controls`
array lists every stage's outcome; a `not-run` control adds the condition `control-not-run: <stage>` to the
result's claim product, where it joins by union and cannot be dropped by a consumer. A `not-run` control is
never rendered as `passed`.

**4. Dispositions and labels.** A campaign ends with exactly one disposition:

| Disposition | When | What the result holds |
|---|---|---|
| `Ranked` | Finalists exist with settled or provisional comparisons | A ranked, typed result set; each entry with its standing, modes of the rungs that selected it, rank-robustness type, qualification receipt if any |
| `EveryAdmittedAlternativeFails` | No design passes stage 1, or none meets the requirement at the last rung reached | The typed exclusions and failures, with the cheapest repair per design in `next_actions` |
| `TiedWithinMargin` | The best designs are tied within the declared margin at the budget | The tie set and the margin |
| `InconclusiveAtThisBudget` | The budget ended before a comparison settled | The evidence so far and the estimated cost to settle |

Labels are independent of the disposition and accumulate: `screen-biased`, `budget-truncated`,
`model-conditional`, `tier-sensitive`, `control-not-run`. All four dispositions are legitimate results with
exit code 0 or 2 (2 when any label or finding is present); none is an error.

**5. INV-ATEL-01.** The framework passes a design to stage n + 1 only with stage n's admission record for it.
A design refused by a stage (for example excluded by `design.check` with a typed bound) reaches a later stage
only if the request carries `--override <stage>:<refusal code>`; the override is written to the manifest and
adds `override: <stage>` to the conditions of every downstream result for that design. An attempt to run a
later stage on a refused design without the override is rejected inside the framework (it is a programming
error surfaced as an integrity fault, exit 9, not a user refusal, because no verb exposes it).

**6. Phase and cost.** Every stage writes its phase (authoring, check, synthesis, simulation, training,
promotion, review) and cost to the phase ledger at close, with the control's cost as a separate line, so the
campaign report can state what the controls cost and so the ten-fold test's ledger is complete.

**7. Callable alone.** Each stage's mechanism is reachable through its own verbs (`phylum.quotient` and
`design.check`; `result.revalidate` and the memo store; `nemesis.audit`; `opt.start`; `fidelity.modelform`;
`eval.qualify` and `nemesis.bound`), and `opt.cascade` accepts a stage range. A stage run alone still plans
and evaluates its control; a control cannot be disabled, only reported `not-run` for a registered reason.

**8. Layout and determinism.** `crates/frob-opt/src/cascade/{stage.rs, control.rs, disposition.rs}`. Control
plans and outcomes are pure functions of the manifest and the ledger (D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All six stages implement `Stage` and expose a `StageControl`; a cascade run writes six control records.
- [ ] Control plans are in the manifest hash before the first score; a plan added later is rejected.
- [ ] `not-run` appears only with a registered reason, sets the condition on the result's claim product, and
      is never printed as `passed`.
- [ ] Each of the four dispositions is reachable and returned on its fixture; none is reported as an error.
- [ ] INV-ATEL-01: a property test over random campaigns finds no stage run on a design the previous stage
      refused without a recorded override; an override appears on every downstream result of that design.
- [ ] Every stage writes a phase-ledger line and a separate control-cost line.
- [ ] A stage run alone evaluates its control.
- [ ] `frob-opt/CONTRACT.md` lists the stages, controls, dispositions, labels and the override.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test cascade_framework` (stages wired to `synthetic` scorers; the framework is the
real code):

- `six_control_records`; `control_plan_in_manifest_hash`; `late_plan_rejected`.
- `not_run_reason_registered`: an unregistered reason string is rejected; every reason used by the stage
  controls of N28b-26, N28b-27, N28b-30, BD-345 and BD-346 is in the registry (a scan of their control
  implementations).
- `not_run_sets_condition`: a result with a `not-run` stage-5 control carries `control-not-run: 5`; combining
  it with another result keeps the condition.
- `disposition_every_alternative_fails`: a population in which every design violates the mass budget.
- `disposition_tied`: two finalists whose paired difference stays inside the margin through the budget.
- `disposition_inconclusive`: budget ends with the confidence sequence straddling the margin.
- `disposition_ranked`.
- `refused_design_not_passed_on` (property, 1,000 random populations with random stage-1 refusals).
- `override_recorded_downstream`: an override of one design's stage-1 refusal (given with that refusal's
  code) → its stage-3, -4 and -6 results carry `override: 1`.
- `control_cannot_be_disabled`: there is no configuration that omits a control; the only path to a missing
  outcome is `not-run`.
- `phase_ledger_lines`: twelve lines for a full run (six stages, six controls).

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28), step `framework`: runs `frob opt
cascade` on four small seeded populations built to end in the four dispositions, and once with stage 5's
second contact law withheld; asserts the dispositions, exit codes (0 or 2, never an error code), the control
array, the `not-run` condition in the envelope's evidence block and in `frob report campaign`. Logged per
STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, per stage the control name, planned item count,
outcome and action, the disposition, labels, and on failure the first stage whose control record is missing.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The stage table above is the specification; the test suite asserts each stage's control name against it.
- Negative controls: a framework that drops `not-run` controls from the result (the claim-product assertion
  fails); a cascade that returns `Ranked` with an empty finalist set (rejected by the result type); a stage
  that reads a refused design (the property test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting the order of designs in the population leaves control outcomes, disposition and
  labels unchanged.
- Determinism: identical records twice, across worker counts and under random preemption.

## Considerations

- Controls cost compute (the audit lane's tenth, the stage-5 tenth, the planted items). The report states
  the total; a campaign that cannot afford its controls says `not-run: budget` and is labelled.
- A control can fail for reasons unrelated to the candidate designs (a broken plant). The record holds the
  planned items' hashes so a failed control can be replayed alone.
- The cascade as a whole is Frontier: each of its adaptive parts has its own kill gate and falls back stage
  by stage; this framework is what makes the fallbacks visible in one result.
- ADR-ATEL-10 (two planes): the framework reads results and writes requests; it cannot change what a world
  computes. No kernel, step plan or physics path depends on anything here.
