## Background

Stage 4 of the cascade is a race: a mixed-morphology population on paired world addresses, with fine-tuning
for survivors and specialists for finalists, across the shared-policy, fine-tuned and specialist rungs. Its
output is a set of finalists, `provisional`. Its built-in control: a null race keeps the best with
probability at least 1 − α. Stage 6 is qualification: sealed confirmation on fresh world clusters with
pre-committed requests, promotion on the referee, and the rare-failure bound. Its output is a qualified
design comparison with receipts. Its built-in control: a false-promotion rate of at most α under adaptive
retries on no-signal worlds. Tier: Solid for both controls.

Each control guards one way a campaign can manufacture a winner from noise. In a race over N candidates,
eliminating on any single pairwise crossing multiplies the chance of losing the true best by about N − 1; the
mixture construction avoids it (a candidate's elimination evidence is the average of its pairwise e-processes
against all original opponents, and elimination needs that mixture to reach 1/α), and an implementation that
eliminates on single crossings must run each comparison at α/(N − 1) instead. At qualification, one
comparison is valid under optional stopping but does nothing about an agent that submits fifty candidates to
the same held-out worlds until one passes; the blinded lane handles that with fresh sealed world clusters and
an error budget α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] for the i-th submission.

The gate-level proofs of those two properties live with their mechanisms (a null race of 81 over 512 replays;
2,000 no-signal campaigns with 500 adaptive retries). This bead builds the *per-campaign* form: a cheap
control that runs inside every cascade and shows, on that campaign's own data, that the property held.

## Scope

In: the stage-4 control (`null-race`) and the stage-6 control (`no-signal-candidate` with the alpha-spend
check); their planted items; evaluation; actions on failure; the boundary that keeps the cascade from reading
confirmation worlds.

Out: the race and its error proof (BD-341, BD-262); the lane, its sealing and the 500-retry gate (BD-259,
BD-260); promotion (BD-263); the rare-failure bound (BD-276); stage 4's training content; the framework
(N28b-25).

## Technical approach

**1. Stage 4 control: `null-race` (no new simulation).** After the race of a generation ends, its recorded
per-address paired outcomes are replayed under a null built from the same data:

1. Take the race's candidates and the paired differences between each candidate and every opponent at each
   address consumed.
2. For replay j = 1 … 512, draw from an addressed stream (campaign seed, generation, j, "null-race") a
   permutation of the candidate labels *within each address* (every address's outcomes are reassigned to
   candidates uniformly at random). Under this relabelling no candidate is better than another in
   distribution, and the noise distribution is the campaign's own.
3. Designate candidate index 0 as "the best" (any fixed index: under the null all are equal) and run the
   same race rule (same α, same round cap, same mixture) on the relabelled outcomes.
4. Count the replays in which the designated candidate is eliminated.

The control passes when that count is within the exact binomial acceptance bound for probability α over 512
replays: at α = 0.05 at most 38 eliminations (the 0.99 quantile of Binomial(512, 0.05); the expected count is
25.6). It fails above that. On failure: the race's eliminations in this generation are voided, the
eliminated candidates are re-admitted, the stage falls back to the fixed halving schedule for the rest of the
campaign, and the result is labelled `race-uncalibrated`. The control's cost is arithmetic on stored
outcomes.

**2. Stage 4 planted winner (shared with the screening audit).** Where the campaign has a planted winner, a
race with a planted winner returns it among the finalists; that outcome is read from the screening audit's
record and is listed in this stage's control record for convenience. It is not a second mechanism.

**3. Stage 6 control: `no-signal-candidate`.**
- *Planted item.* A candidate that is known to be no better than the reference: the reference design itself
  with an inert edit (a rename or a parameter move verified beforehand, on development worlds at fixed n, to
  leave the requirement pass rate within a quarter of the comparison margin). Its plan is sealed in the
  campaign manifest.
- *Procedure.* The planted candidate is submitted to the lane against the reference by a scripted adversary
  that retries after every rejection, up to the campaign's retry cap (DESIGN default: the same number of
  submissions the real finalists used, at least 5), each submission pre-committed and drawing fresh sealed
  clusters at level α_i.
- *Evaluation.* The planted candidate is never promoted in this campaign; and the alpha-spend ledger of the
  campaign shows every request committed before its worlds were drawn and a total spend of at most α per
  claim.
- *Action on failure.* A promotion of the planted candidate voids the campaign's qualification: every
  qualified result of the campaign reverts to `provisional`, the label `qualification-uncalibrated` is set,
  and the event is filed with the lane's owner. One such event in a campaign is expected with probability at
  most α by construction; the report says so and the campaign is re-qualified with fresh clusters.

**4. The boundary (INV-ATEL-11).** No verb of this chapter reads a confirmation world of the blinded
qualification lane. Stage 6 passes designs, controllers and committed requests to the lane and receives
receipts (decision, submission index, level spent, units used, a hash). It never receives a per-world
outcome of a rejected submission, and the cascade's own decisions after qualification (the final ranking,
the report) are functions of receipts only. The capability scopes enforce it; this bead adds a static check
that no module under `frob-opt` depends on the lane's sealed-world reader.

**5. Rare-failure episodes.** The stage's bound for finalists uses nominal draws only. A bound over
battery-selected or search-selected episodes is refused with `FROB-REF-549` (repair: draw nominal episodes;
exit 3); the stage passes the bound's owner only episodes drawn by the task's nominal randomization and
records the proposal law of each.

**6. Records.** Two control records per campaign (`frob.stage-control/1`): for `null-race` the number of
replays, eliminations of the designated candidate, the bound and the outcome per generation; for
`no-signal-candidate` the planted item's hash, the submissions with their levels, the decisions and the
alpha-spend total. Determinism: both are pure functions of stored outcomes, receipts and addressed streams.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-opt/src/cascade/{stage4_control.rs, stage6_control.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a campaign with the correct race rule the `null-race` control passes in every generation (at most
      38 eliminations of the designated candidate in 512 replays at α = 0.05).
- [ ] With the race rule replaced by single-crossing elimination at level α (no mixture, no α/(N − 1)
      correction), the control fails on a race of 27 or more candidates, the eliminations are voided and the
      stage falls back to the fixed schedule with the label.
- [ ] The control consumes no new episodes (dispatch count zero).
- [ ] The planted no-signal candidate is not promoted on the seeded campaign; every request's commit
      precedes its world draw; total alpha spend per claim is at most α.
- [ ] With the lane replaced by one that reuses world clusters at a constant level, the scripted adversary
      gets the planted candidate promoted, the control fails and the campaign's qualified results revert to
      `provisional` with the label.
- [ ] No module of `frob-opt` links the sealed-world reader; a request from the cascade for a per-world
      outcome of a rejected submission is refused by the lane.
- [ ] A rare-failure bound offered battery-selected episodes returns `FROB-REF-549`.
- [ ] Both control records exist for every cascade run and appear in the campaign report.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test cascade_stage46_controls`:

- `binomial_bound_512`: the 0.99 quantile of Binomial(512, 1/20) is 38 (exact integer arithmetic).
- `null_relabelling_is_keyed`: the same campaign seed gives the same 512 permutations.
- `null_race_passes_mixture_rule`: recorded outcomes of a `synthetic` 81-candidate race; eliminations of the
  designated candidate ≤ 38.
- `null_race_fails_single_crossing_rule`: the same data under the uncorrected rule; eliminations far above
  the bound (asserted above 38).
- `corrected_single_crossing_passes`: single crossings at α/(N − 1) pass.
- `failure_voids_eliminations_and_falls_back`.
- `no_signal_candidate_not_promoted` (`synthetic` lane outcomes with zero true effect, 1,000 seeded
  campaigns): promoted in at most the binomial acceptance bound for α.
- `alpha_spend_sum`: submissions 1 to 5 spend α·(1/2 + 1/6 + 1/12 + 1/20 + 1/30) = α·5/6 ≤ α.
- `commit_precedes_draw`.
- `reused_clusters_mutant_fails_control`.
- `cascade_cannot_read_sealed_worlds` (dependency lint plus a runtime refusal test).
- `battery_selected_episodes_refused_549`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `stage4` and `stage6` of `scripts/e2e/cascade_seeded_population.sh` (bead N28b-28): after `frob opt
cascade` on the seeded population, read the two control records with `frob evidence trace`; assert the
replay count, the elimination count against 38, the planted candidate's receipts (all rejections), the
alpha-spend total against α, and that `frob report campaign` prints both controls. A second run with each
mutant enabled asserts the failure actions and labels. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time,
evidence blocks, per generation the elimination count and bound, per submission the index, level and
decision, the spend total, and on failure the first replay or submission that breaks the expectation.

### Known answers and negative controls

- PO-ATEL-04 (in a race the true best is eliminated with probability at most α; discharged by the mixture
  derivation and a null race of 81 over 512 replays within the exact binomial acceptance bound): the same
  bound, 38 of 512, is used here per campaign.
- The lane's own gate, for reference: over 2,000 no-signal campaigns with 500 adaptive retries, false
  promotion in at most 123 campaigns; a planted true gain promoted within 10 requests in at least 80%;
  reused clusters at a constant level give a false promotion in more than 99%.
- Negative controls: the two mutants above (uncorrected single-crossing race; reused clusters).
- INV-ATEL-02 (a study's statistic is a pure function of per-address outcomes in address order): the null
  replay permutes labels within addresses and consumes addresses in order, so it inherits the property; a
  test replays under shuffled completion order and requires identical counts.
- Determinism: identical counts twice and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- A per-campaign control at level α will fail by chance in a small share of clean campaigns (about 1% for
  the null race at the 0.99 bound; at most α for the no-signal candidate). The actions are conservative
  (fall back, re-qualify), so a chance failure costs compute and never validity, and the report states the
  expected chance rate beside the outcome.
- The null race tests the *rule's* calibration on the campaign's noise; it cannot detect a scorer that is
  biased toward one design, which is the audit lane's and the model-form check's job.
- A design comparison that is to be reported as a claim goes through the lane; comparisons on discovery
  worlds, including everything stage 4 produces, are development results and stay `provisional`.
- Sealed clusters are a consumable; the planted candidate's submissions use clusters too, and the campaign's
  estimate includes them.
