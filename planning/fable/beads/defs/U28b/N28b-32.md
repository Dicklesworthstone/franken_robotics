## Background

An edit is an event that puts evidence in question. The studio does not ask "is the cache still warm"; it
asks which verdicts survive, which are revoked, and what the cheapest set of reruns is. `frob result
revalidate <verdict or claim> --after-edit <edit>` answers with the surviving obligations and the licence for
each, the invalidated regions, and the required reruns with their cost. The mission cluster's revalidation
verb is the same engine applied to a mission answer. Decision ADR-ATEL-05 states the principle: a hash
identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck. A content hash says that an input or a result is
the same object and says nothing about whether a verdict survives a different input; reuse of a verdict needs
a dependency argument or an implication argument, recorded and independently checkable.

This bead is the verb's surface: its registry entry, schemas, envelope and refusals, and the rules of what an
answer must contain so that a reused verdict can never look like a recomputed one. The engines behind it
arrive in steps (mission cells at the first milestone; every verdict kind with the rule registry at the
third; implication rules for non-mission verdicts at the fourth), and the surface is fixed first so that
none of those steps changes what an agent sees. Tier: Solid for the surface and for grades 1 and 2; grade 3
is Frontier and sits behind a flag.

Every stored verdict has this shape (`frob.verdict/1`):

```
verdict record
  claim        claim_id · claim type · statement · quantifier prefix
  kind         inclusion (a witness exists) | exclusion (no witness can exist) | comparison | optimum | estimate
  assumptions  disturbance set · world set · hypothesis support · actuator limits · requirement ·
               policy class · information contract (channels and their instants) · catalog and prices ·
               model identity · tier · kernel versions · effort profile
  obligations  feasibility obligations   (the witness is valid)
               optimality obligations    (every cheaper rival is excluded; the cost bound; catalog membership)
               each with: evidence_id · dependency support (the exact fields it read) · checker
  provenance   evidence closure · decision-policy id · trace ids
```

and reuse comes in four grades:

| Grade | Licence to skip the recheck | What is recorded | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Identity | The obligation's full key is unchanged | The memo hit | Solid |
| 2 Dependency | Every changed field lies outside the obligation's recorded dependency support | A mask over the obligations, checkable against the supports without rerunning anything | Solid |
| 3 Implication | A changed field lies inside the support, and a polarity rule of the assumption lattice preserves the obligation | The rule's name, the old and new assumption sets, the order relation between them and its check | Frontier |
| 4 None | — | The rerun and its cost estimate | — |

## Scope

In: the registry entry; input and output schemas; how targets and changes are named; the per-obligation
answer; the split verdict; the mask as an evidence item; exit codes; refusals at the surface; what the
envelope's evidence block says about a revalidated verdict; the staged availability by milestone.

Out: the engine, edit classification and rule registry (BD-292; mission cells at M1 in the Augur unit);
grades 3 and 4 for non-mission verdicts (BD-347); the rerun planner's cost model (N28b-33); the polarity
table's fixtures (N28b-34); the edit-time invalidation set (N28b-01).

## Technical approach

**1. Registry entry.** The row of record is VERBS-result's (a dependency of this bead), and this bead enters
no second one; the handler is built against it:

```toml
[[verb]]
name = "result.revalidate"
cluster = "result"
kind = "run"
tier = "S"
capabilities = ["result:run"]          # implies result:read
input_schema = "frob.result.revalidate.in/1"
output_schema = "frob.result.revalidate.out/1"
refusals = ["FROB-REF-835", "FROB-REF-854", "FROB-REF-869", "FROB-REF-870", "FROB-REF-872"]
idempotent = false                     # a run verb, idempotent by its key
first_milestone = "M4"
task_clusters = ["mission", "repair", "transfer"]
examples = ["frob result revalidate quad-tray --since d:7a3e --key rev-1 --json"]
wait = { cli = "to-completion", mcp_ms = 5000, mcp_max_ms = 30000 }
```

(An earlier draft of this bead carried its own entry, with the scopes `result:read` and `result:revalidate`,
the schema name `frob.revalidate.out/1`, the task clusters `revalidate` and `missions`, `idempotent = true` and
`first_milestone = "M1"`; it is withdrawn in favour of the row above.) The verb table of the plan lists the
verb at the fourth milestone, while the gate register gives GATE-ATEL-11 a first row at the first milestone
("dependency grades on mission cells"). The row keeps the appendix's `first_milestone = "M4"` (the field is the
appendix's value; the handler decides what a build serves), and this bead's handler answers from M1 in reduced
form, listed by `frob capabilities` as implemented with its restriction named (`targets: mission cells; grades 1
and 2`); later engines extend it without a schema change (OPEN-ATEL-4 default: two verbs, `result.revalidate` and
`mission.revalidate`, over one engine).

**2. Input (`frob.result.revalidate.in/1`).** `target`: a `VerdictId`, a `ClaimId`, a mission answer, or a
design (by name or version; every stored verdict whose evidence closure cites a version of that design, the
form the plan's walkthroughs use: `frob result revalidate carrier-b --since d:a3f1… --json`, and over MCP
`result_revalidate { "design": "quad-tray", "since": "d:7a3e…" }`); `change`: one of `after_edit` (an edit or
patch identifier in a lineage), `since` (an earlier version in the target's lineage: the edits from it to the
current head, equivalent to `between <since> <head>` with the head pinned in the answer), `between` (two
versions of any identified object: design, card, scene, task, clock, mission), or `amendment` (a set of field
changes). On the command line the change is exactly one of `--after-edit <edit>`, `--since <identity>`,
`--between <a> <b>` (the spellings of the row of record) or `--amendment field=value,…` (the generated form of
the object property); two of them together, or none,
is `FROB-REF-802` with `pointer: /change`; `grades`: the highest grade the caller allows (default 2; 3 only with the flag
`frontier-revalidate-implication` enabled for the session); `estimate_only`; `run` (submit the planned reruns
as jobs; the planner behind it is N28b-33, and until it lands `run` is accepted and answered with the reruns
listed and none submitted, reason `no-planner`); a budget and an idempotency key. All of these fields exist
from the first milestone, so no later engine changes the schema.

**3. Output (`frob.result.revalidate.out/1`).**

```json
{ "target": "v:7d21…", "frontier": "184467",
  "change": [ { "component": "free space of the scene", "direction": "enlarged", "fields": ["scene.doorway_w"] } ],
  "obligations": [
    { "id": 0, "role": "feasibility", "grade": 2, "status": "preserved",
      "licence": { "kind": "dependency", "mask": "ev:19c4…" } },
    { "id": 3, "role": "optimality", "grade": 4, "status": "revoked",
      "licence": { "kind": "none" }, "touched_by": "scene.doorway_w" } ],
  "verdict_after": { "feasible": "preserved", "optimal": "revoked, recheck" },
  "invalidated_regions": [],
  "reruns": [ { "what": "exclude every cheaper rival", "cost": { "cpu_core_s": [2, 9] },
                "command": "frob mission synthesize … --key …" } ],
  "counts": { "preserved": 3, "revoked": 1, "by_grade": { "1": 0, "2": 3, "3": 0, "4": 1 } } }
```

For a design target the answer is a list of per-verdict answers of this shape under `verdicts`, plus a
`summary` with the walkthrough's view: `inherited` (per verdict or per kind, a count and the reason in
words, for example "reads half-width, knee ratio and ratings; the foot is outside its read set"),
`invalidated` (count, kind and the touching field) and `reruns` (count and the interval estimate). A verdict
is inherited only when every one of its obligations is preserved; one revoked obligation puts it in
`invalidated`, with the split still shown in its own entry.

Rules on the answer:
- every obligation of the target appears exactly once, with a grade and a licence (INV-ATEL-07: every reused
  verdict carries a grade and a licence; a grade-3 licence names a rule in the registry);
- feasibility and optimality are reported separately, never merged into one word. Standing example: a cost-9
  cart may be the certified minimum while a cost-5 relay is excluded by a doorway; widen the doorway and the
  cart and its trace remain valid, so the verdict splits into `feasible: preserved` and `optimal: revoked,
  recheck`;
- a `preserved` status is never produced by default: with no licence the status is `revoked` (grade 4) and
  the rerun is listed;
- for a mission answer, `invalidated_regions` lists the cells whose verdicts are withdrawn, and for a missed
  dependency found later the withdrawal names the two worlds or the new mode that invalidates the verdict.

**4. The mask.** A grade-2 answer stores a mask as an evidence item: per obligation, the changed fields, the
recorded support and the statement "disjoint". It is checkable against the supports without running any
solver or simulation, by the offline auditor as well as by the engine. A grade-3 licence stores the rule's
name, the old and new assumption sets, the order relation and its check.

**5. Refusals at the surface** (the chapter list of the row: 835, 854, 869, 870, 872; the kind's appended codes
come on top).
- `FROB-REF-835` (owner: the lemma chapter, D-11): a verdict reused after a change on the strength of a content
  hash alone. DESIGN split from 870, following ADR-ATEL-05 ("a hash identifies; only an implication licenses"):
  835 when the request offers as licence the equality of a result's or an input's hash (`licence.kind = "hash"`),
  which is never an implication; 870 when no licence is offered or the one offered names an unknown rule.
  Repairs: record the implication that licenses the reuse; re-derive the verdict; mark it unresolved. Exit 3.
- `FROB-REF-870`: reuse of a verdict with no licence, or under a rule the registry does not know; repair:
  recheck; exit 3. Raised when a caller (or an importing tool) presents a verdict as still valid after a
  change with no accompanying licence, and when a licence names an unknown rule. The checker rejects an
  unknown rule name or a changed quantifier prefix even when every hash is consistent.
- `FROB-REF-854` (owner: the mission chapter): reuse outside a screen's or classifier's eligibility; repair:
  revalidate.
- `FROB-REF-869`: the target's evidence closure is dangling or tombstoned; repair: re-run the evidence.
- `FROB-REF-872` (the advisory type wall, N28c-12, M3): an identifier of result kind `advisory` (a
  value-of-information output) passed as the target, which the row marks `accepts = "verdict"`; repair: run the
  comparison; exit 3. It is raised once N28c-12 lands; before that an advisory target finds no verdict record
  and is `FROB-REF-869`.

**6. Exit codes and evidence.** 0: every obligation preserved. 2: at least one revoked or to be rechecked
(a completed answer with findings). 3: refusal. 8: accepted and still running (a grade-3 check or an
`estimate` that needs the cost model may take longer than the wait). The envelope's evidence block for a
revalidated verdict keeps the original claim's class only for preserved obligations and adds
`revalidated: { grade_max, licence_ids }`; a consumer can therefore always tell a carried-forward verdict from
a fresh one. Determinism: D0 (a pure function of the two states and the stored records at a pinned
frontier). Unsafe reuse is a false accept, even if a later change restores feasibility; the surface never
"heals" a past unsafe reuse by silence.

**7. Layout.** Verb executor in `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/result_revalidate.rs`, calling the engine trait
`Revalidate` (implemented first for mission cells). The engines plug into
this surface and depend on it: mission cells at M1 (N25a-13), every verdict kind at M3 (BD-292), grades 3
and 4 for non-mission verdicts at M4 (BD-347). Schema files under `schemas/` (`schemas/frob.result.revalidate.in.1.json`
and `.out.1.json`, the naming rule of N32a-02). Until an engine is registered for a target kind the verb
answers every obligation `revoked` (grade 4, the default) with the reason `no-engine`; the surface is tested
at this bead's close against a test-only `FixtureEngine` (feature `fixture-engine`, labelled `synthetic`)
that returns scripted licences, so that every rule of section 3 is exercised before any engine exists.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The served entry is VERBS-result's row (kind `run`, scope `result:run`, `frob.result.revalidate.out/1`,
      `idempotent = false`, `first_milestone = "M4"`); `frob result revalidate --help` and the MCP tool
      `result_revalidate` are generated from it; the schemas validate the examples; exactly one change form is
      accepted per call.
- [ ] With the fixture engine: a change outside every support returns all obligations `preserved` at grade 2
      with a checkable mask; a change inside a support returns grade 4 with the rerun listed; with no engine
      registered for the target kind every obligation is `revoked` with reason `no-engine`. (The M1 row of
      GATE-ATEL-11 on real mission cells is measured through this surface by N25a-13, which plugs in the
      mission-cell engine and depends on this bead.)
- [ ] Every obligation appears once with grade and licence; an answer missing either is rejected by the
      output schema.
- [ ] A design target with `since` returns one entry per verdict citing the design, a summary whose counts
      equal the entries (inherited only when every obligation is preserved), and the same per-verdict
      answers as revalidating each verdict alone with `between <since> <head>`.
- [ ] The doorway fixture's verdict record, answered by the fixture engine with the licences of KA-700
      (feasibility obligations grade 2, the rival-exclusion obligation grade 4), renders `feasible: preserved`
      and `optimal: revoked, recheck`; the same answer from the real engine is BD-292's acceptance at M3.
- [ ] A verdict presented as valid after a change with no licence, and a licence naming an unknown rule, both
      return `FROB-REF-870`; a licence that is a hash equality returns `FROB-REF-835`; a changed quantifier
      prefix is rejected even with consistent hashes.
- [ ] Grade 3 is unavailable without the flag; with the flag off, an obligation that only a rule could
      preserve is grade 4.
- [ ] The mask re-checks offline (auditor) with no solver or simulator present.
- [ ] Exit codes 0, 2, 3 and 8 are produced on their fixtures; replay with the same idempotency key returns
      the stored answer.
- [ ] CX-316's plant `optimum-kept-on-feasibility` builds as its `frob-verbs/nc-<nnnn>` branch and is caught:
      `split_verdict_doorway` fails on that build and passes on the normal build.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs --test result_revalidate_surface`:

- `served_entry_is_row_of_record`: the entry the handler is bound to equals VERBS-result's fields above;
  `schema_rejects_missing_licence`; `schema_rejects_missing_grade`.
- `one_change_form`: `--since` with `--between` → `FROB-REF-802`, `pointer: /change`; none of the four → the
  same.
- `every_obligation_listed_once` (property over random verdict records).
- `default_is_revoked`: an engine that returns no licence for an obligation yields `revoked`, grade 4; with no
  engine registered for the target kind every obligation is `revoked` with reason `no-engine`.
- `split_verdict_doorway`: cost-9 cart, cost-5 relay, doorway widened.
- `design_target_since`: a fixture design cited by five verdicts (three outside the edit's supports, two
  touched) → `summary.inherited` count 3, `invalidated` count 2, each entry equal to the single-verdict answer
  with `between`; a verdict with one revoked obligation of four is `invalidated`, not inherited.
- `run_without_planner`: `run` accepted, reruns listed, none submitted, reason `no-planner`.
- `no_licence_refused_870`; `unknown_rule_refused_870`; `hash_licence_refused_835`; `changed_prefix_rejected`.
- `grade3_needs_flag`.
- `mask_checkable_without_solver`: the mask verifier links no solver crate (dependency lint) and accepts the
  fixture's mask; a mask whose support omits a changed field is rejected.
- `evidence_block_marks_revalidated`.
- `exit_codes`: (all preserved, 0), (one revoked, 2), (refusal, 3), (long check, 8).
- `tombstoned_target_869`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/result_revalidate.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E. Its `surface` section, written by
this bead and run at its close on a build with the `fixture-engine` feature: `frob result revalidate --help`
and the MCP tool listing (both generated from the entry); `frob result revalidate <fixture verdict> --between
<v1> <v2> --key <case key> --json` for the scripted cases (every call carries its own idempotency key, since the
verb is of kind `run`) (all preserved, exit 0; one revoked with its rerun, exit 2; the split
doorway verdict; a fixture design with `--since`, its summary counts checked against the per-verdict entries; a long check returning a job, exit 8, then `frob job wait`); refusal 870 on a hand-edited
answer that claims survival without a licence and on a licence naming an unknown rule; 835 on a licence that is
a hash equality; 802 on two change forms in one call; 869 on a tombstoned target; the same request again with its idempotency key (the stored answer); the mask's offline check with
`frob-verify`; and, on the release build, a target kind with no engine (every obligation `revoked`,
`no-engine`). The real engines are exercised end to end by their own scripts, which call this verb: mission cells at M1
by N25a-13 through `scripts/e2e/mission_stance.sh` (N25a-21: the passive-spring amendment), the frozen
campaign at M3 through `scripts/e2e/mission_revalidate_campaign.sh` (N25a-26). Later steps of this script
come with their beads: `plan` (N28b-33, M3: design edits through `--after-edit` and the doorway fixture on
the real engine) and `polarity` (N28b-34, M4: grade 3); none changes the surface steps. Log `target/e2e-logs/result_revalidate/<utc>.ndjson` with transcript:
commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, per obligation the grade, licence kind and status, counts
by grade, solver calls (expected zero for grades 1 and 2), and on failure the first obligation whose status
differs from a fresh recomputation. Final `summary` line naming GATE-ATEL-11.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-4 (owner: Tether lead; blocks the verb registry freeze at M3): whether `result.revalidate` and
  the mission cluster's verb stay two verbs over one engine or become one. Default: two verbs, one engine;
  this surface is written so that merging them is a registry change only.
- The ceiling, stated before any speed test: rollouts and training are at least nine tenths of per-design
  compute and depend on every mass and geometry parameter, so exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is
  at most about a tenth of compute. Revalidation is an evidence rule and a hygiene measure. It is not a
  source of the ten-fold, and the verb's output never presents carried-forward evidence as time saved without
  the column name.
- Training avoided by a warm start is not reuse in this sense; it is reported in its own column of the reuse
  ledger and credited to inheritance once.
- RISK-38 (an incomplete key or support serves a stale verdict; high): the surface's contribution is that
  nothing is preserved without a recorded, checkable licence.
