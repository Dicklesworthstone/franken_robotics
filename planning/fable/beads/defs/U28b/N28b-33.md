## Background

`result.revalidate` answers three questions after a change: which verdicts survive, which are revoked, and
what the cheapest set of reruns is. The first two are decided by the grades of reuse. This bead is the third:
given the obligations that no licence preserves (grade 4), produce the smallest amount of recomputation that
rebuilds their evidence, with its cost, before anything is spent. Tier: Solid.

It matters for two reasons. An agent choosing between two edits should see what each would cost in evidence,
not only in design merit. And the plan's ceiling for reuse is modest (rollouts and training are at least
nine tenths of per-design compute and depend on every mass and geometry parameter, so exact reuse after a
dynamics-affecting edit is at most about a tenth of compute), which means the honest answer after most edits
is "nearly everything dynamic reruns"; the planner's job is to say that plainly, with a number, and to find
the cases where it is not true (a controller-only, reward-only or scene-only edit reuses every model
analysis).

Rejected alternatives: rerunning the whole evidence closure of a touched verdict (wasteful when one
obligation is revoked and ten are preserved); a planner that guesses costs from averages (the memo graph
already records what each node cost to compute); folding the plan into the edit response (the edit must stay
interactive; planning reads more of the ledger).

## Scope

In: the plan's inputs; the node set to recompute; the choice among alternative checkers; costing; ordering;
the plan record; launching the plan as jobs; the dirty fraction and the unnecessary-invalidation count.

Out: grading and licences (BD-292, BD-347); the verb's surface (N28b-32); the memo store and its keys
(N28b-37, N28b-38); the cost estimator (BD-219, BD-297).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** The revalidation answer (per obligation: grade, status, the fields that touched it); the
target's evidence closure in the artifact graph (which evidence items each obligation rests on, and which
memo-graph nodes produced them); the memo graph's node records (key, kind, determinism class, recorded
recompute cost); the cost estimator for work that has no recorded cost under the new inputs.

**2. Which nodes must be recomputed.** Let D be the set of memo nodes whose keys change under the edit (the
*dirty* set: every node whose declared inputs include a changed field, closed under the graph's dependency
edges) and N(o) the set of nodes that the evidence of a revoked obligation o rests on. The rerun set for o is
D ∩ N(o) evaluated under the new inputs; nodes in N(o) \ D are served from the store. For the whole answer
the rerun set is the union over revoked obligations, each node counted once. Because nodes are deterministic
functions of their keys there is nothing to choose inside one evidence route; the choice is between routes.

**3. Alternative routes.** An obligation may name more than one checker (for example an exclusion can be
re-established by a typed bound from a static analysis or by a full search; a comparison by its stored
study type only). For each revoked obligation the planner lists the admissible routes with their rerun sets
and costs. It picks routes greedily by marginal cost given the nodes already selected (shared nodes are
free the second time), cheapest first, and reports the alternative it did not pick. The result is a plan,
not a proof of minimality over all route combinations; the plan record says "greedy over routes" and gives
the cost of the all-default plan beside it. With one route per obligation, which is the common case, the plan
is exactly minimal.

**4. Costing.** A node with a recorded cost under an identical shape class uses that record (GPU-seconds,
CPU-seconds, wall time), scaled by the estimator's calibration for the device. A node with no record uses
the estimator with its interval. Costs are printed cold and warm separately where a kernel compile or a
scene compile is in the set (a cold figure flatters a cache: a kernel compile of 95.91 s beside 240 s of
training is 28.6% of a cold run, and the cached 0.89 s is 0.37% of a warm one; FACT recorded in the plan's
source register). Totals are sums of intervals, never point values.

**5. Ordering.** Reruns are ordered so that the cheapest *decisive* work comes first: feasibility obligations
before optimality obligations (a revoked feasibility makes the optimality recheck moot); within a role,
ascending cost; ties by evidence identifier. The order is deterministic.

**6. The plan record (`frob.rerun-plan/1`, the `reruns` field of the verb's output).**
`{ target, obligations_revoked, nodes: [ { key, kind, cost: { gpu_s: [lo, hi], cpu_core_s: [lo, hi] },
cold_extra?, serves: [obligation ids] } ], routes: [ { obligation, chosen, alternatives } ],
total: { warm, cold }, dirty_fraction, commands: [ … ], method: "greedy over routes" }`.
`dirty_fraction` is the recomputed share of the target's evidence closure by recorded cost. `commands` are
the exact verb invocations with idempotency keys that would execute the plan.

**7. Running the plan.** `frob result revalidate … --run` submits the plan's commands as jobs under the
session's budget. If the budget is below the plan's lower cost bound the verb refuses at admission with the
budget failure class (exit 4) and still returns the plan; nothing partial is published as a revalidated
verdict. The code is the kind's `FROB-REF-701` (the estimate's upper end exceeds the remaining budget: a
budget below the lower bound is below the upper end too), with the plan, its interval and the binding unit
attached as that row requires; no new code is introduced. A budget between the two bounds passes this check,
and each submitted job then meets its own admission. When the jobs finish, the revoked obligations are re-graded from the new evidence (they become new
evidence items; the old verdict record is superseded by a new one that cites it).

**8. Unnecessary invalidations.** When a reference recomputation of the target exists (in the frozen
amendment campaign, always), the planner's set is compared with the set of obligations whose evidence
actually changed; obligations rerun whose result is bit-identical to the old one are counted as unnecessary
invalidations and reported with their share of unaffected verdicts. The gate tolerates at most 20%.

**9. Layout and determinism.** `crates/frob-verbs/src/revalidate/plan.rs`. The plan is a pure function of
the ledger at a pinned frontier (D0); only measured costs vary between machines, and they are recorded with
the device they came from.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For a controller-only edit of a design with stored analyses, episodes and a comparison: the plan
      contains the episodes and the comparison and no model analysis; for a mass edit it contains every
      rollout of the design; for a display rename it is empty.
- [ ] Shared nodes are counted once; the total equals the sum over distinct nodes.
- [ ] Feasibility reruns precede optimality reruns; the order is identical across runs.
- [ ] With two routes for an exclusion, the cheaper is chosen and the other is listed.
- [ ] Costs are intervals; cold and warm totals are separate when a compile is in the set.
- [ ] `--run` with a budget below the lower bound exits 4 and returns the plan; with enough budget the jobs
      run and the target is re-graded from the new evidence.
- [ ] On the doorway fixture the plan contains only the optimality recheck ("exclude every cheaper rival").
- [ ] The unnecessary-invalidation count and share are reported when a reference exists.
- [ ] The plan performs ledger reads only (zero dispatches before `--run`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs --test rerun_plan` (a `synthetic` memo graph with recorded costs):

- `dirty_intersect_needed`: a graph of 12 nodes, 5 dirty, 4 needed by the revoked obligation, 2 in both →
  the plan has those 2.
- `shared_node_counted_once`: two obligations sharing an episode set of cost 300 GPU-seconds → total 300, not
  600.
- `greedy_route_choice`: routes of cost 40 and 900 for one exclusion → 40 chosen, 900 listed.
- `feasibility_before_optimality`; `order_is_deterministic`.
- `cold_warm_split`: a kernel compile of 95.91 s and a 240 s run → warm 240 s, cold 335.91 s; the compile's
  share of the cold total is 28.6%.
- `controller_only_edit_plan`; `mass_edit_plan`; `rename_empty_plan`.
- `budget_below_lower_bound_exit_4`.
- `dirty_fraction`: closure cost 1,000, plan cost 90 → 0.09.
- `unnecessary_invalidation_count`: 10 unaffected verdicts, 2 rerun with identical results → 20%.
- `intervals_add`: [2, 9] + [10, 14] = [12, 23].

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the step `plan` to `scripts/e2e/result_revalidate.sh` (written by N28b-32; the step runs from
M3 on, on the real engine of BD-292): for a template with a stored A-11
analysis, a 64-world run and a comparison, apply a controller-gain edit, a payload-mass edit and a rename;
call `frob result revalidate <design> --after-edit <edit> --estimate --key rv-plan-<edit> --json` for each
(`--estimate` is the command line's one spelling of the common property `estimate_only`; `--estimate-only` is an
unknown parameter, 802), then the first again with `--run` in place of `--estimate` under its own key
`rv-run-1` (a run verb: a different request under the estimate's key would be `FROB-REF-803`); then, on the
doorway fixture, the widening edit with `--after-edit` (the plan holds only "exclude every cheaper rival",
and the answer shows `feasible: preserved`, `optimal: revoked, recheck` from the real engine); asserts the
plans above, the totals as intervals, zero dispatches before `--run`, and that after the run the comparison
is a new evidence item citing the superseded one. Logged per STD-E2E: commands, exit codes, wall time,
evidence blocks, the node list with kinds and costs, totals (warm and cold) with units, the dirty fraction,
measured cost of the executed plan against its interval, and on failure the first node in the plan that a
fresh recomputation shows unchanged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The reuse arithmetic the plan states: with rollouts and training at nine tenths of per-design compute,
  exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is at most about one tenth; the `mass_edit_plan` test asserts a
  dirty fraction of at least 0.9 on the fixture whose closure is built with that split.
- KA-700: after widening the doorway only the optimality obligation is revoked, so only its recheck is
  planned.
- Negative controls: a planner that reruns the whole closure of a touched verdict (the controller-only test
  fails: model analyses appear in the plan); a planner that prices a shared node twice.
- Determinism: identical plans twice and across worker counts at one frontier.

## Considerations

- The plan is an estimate; the campaign's registered predictions include it, and its calibration (measured
  cost inside the interval) is tracked like any other studio prediction.
- "Cheapest" is cheapest among registered routes. A cleverer route that nobody registered is invisible to
  the planner, by design: a route is only admissible if its checker is known to the verdict record.
- Training avoided by a warm start is not part of this plan's saving; it is counted once, in the
  "reacquisition avoided" column of the reuse ledger.
- KILL-REUSE does not affect this bead: with grades 1 and 2 only, more obligations are grade 4 and the plans
  are larger, which is exactly what its cost trigger measures.
