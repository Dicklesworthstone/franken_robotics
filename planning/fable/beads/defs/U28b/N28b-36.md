## Background

Three of the four grades of verdict reuse are uncontroversial: identity (nothing in the key changed),
dependency (nothing the obligation read changed) and none (rerun). The third grade, implication, keeps a
verdict although something it read did change, on the strength of a polarity rule of the assumption lattice.
It is the only grade that can be wrong while every hash is consistent, and the plan treats it accordingly:
Frontier, behind a flag, with a pilot that has a hard stop, and with a negative result recorded in advance
(on small cases a correctly keyed dependency graph did identical work, so grade 3 has no demonstrated
advantage). An upper-tier capability is implemented only when its pilot and its kill-gate decision are on
record, and one that fell back at its kill gate is described by the name of the fallback. A kill gate that
fires is not a failure, because the fallback is the plan. This bead is that decision for grade 3. It
implements nothing. It is the only bead that decides KILL-REUSE (the key N28a-24 was merged into it).

Grades 1 (identity) and 2 (dependency) are Solid and stay whatever this gate decides. Grade 3 carries the
worst failure the studio can commit, a false accept, for a benefit that the plan-time evidence could not
show: on the four-state camera fixture a correctly keyed dependency graph did exactly the same work as a
semantic receipt.

The asymmetry that makes the fallback cheap: revalidation is an evidence rule and a hygiene measure, not a
source of speed (exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is at most about a tenth of compute). Killing
grade 3 costs some rechecks of analyses and certificates and nothing else.

## Scope

In: the trigger; the evidence read; who decides; the outcomes and their downstream effects; the registry
state of the rules and the flag's default; the list of revoked licences; the record.

Out: the pilot (N28a-23); the gate run (N28b-35); the rules and their tests (N28b-34); the engine (BD-292,
BD-347); the verdict of the bet this gate serves (BET-33, which depends on this bead, reads this record and
applies no trigger of its own). The mechanism being measured does not depend on this decision; this decision depends on the
measurement.

## Technical approach

**Trigger (from the gate register).** KILL-REUSE fires on any unsafe reuse in GATE-ATEL-11, or if grade 3
saves less than a fifth of the recheck cost that grade 2 leaves (TARGET). With C₂ the pooled recheck cost of
the arm using grades 1 and 2 and C₃ that of the arm using grades 1 to 3 on the frozen 30-amendment campaign,
from the same cache state and dirty fraction: the cost clause fires when the saving s = (C₂ − C₃)/C₂ < 1/5.
With U the number of incorrectly inherited verdicts in the arm using grades 1 to 3, the safety clause fires
when U ≥ 1; an unsafe reuse counts even if a later amendment restores feasibility.

**Milestone.** Decided at M4, at the milestone of its gate, and recorded as an architecture decision record.
The mission chapter's standing rule applies from the third milestone and is not superseded: one incorrectly
inherited verdict from an implication rule removes that rule at once, whenever it happens. The pilot
(N28a-23) can end the question earlier: if either of its falsifiers held, the production path was not funded
and this decision records "fired on the pilot".

**The measurement that decides.** The GATE-ATEL-11 receipt of N28b-35: unsafe-reuse counts per arm and per
rule; decision equality with full recomputation; unnecessary invalidations; C₂, C₃ and the saving with its
interval, pooled and per category; the incumbent dependency graph's cost for comparison; the PO-ATEL-06
record of N28b-34 (every rule's derivation, fixtures and the exhaustive finite-game check). Decided by the
Atelier lead; reviewed by a fresh-context session that recomputes the unsafe-reuse count and the saving from
the ledger.

**Decision rule.**

| Evidence | Ruling (decision outcome, N40-03) | Registry state |
|---|---|---|
| The pilot's falsifier held | KILL-REUSE fires on the pilot (`fired`, citing the pilot result's receipt under `receipts/pilots/`), with no gate measurement | Every rule `disabled`; the production path of grade 3 was not funded |
| Any unsafe reuse by any rule in the grade-3 arm (U ≥ 1) | KILL-REUSE fires (`fired`), and the rule that licensed each unsafe reuse is listed | Every rule `disabled (KILL-REUSE)`; the flag `frontier-revalidate-implication` removed from the release configuration |
| No unsafe reuse, saving below 1/5 | KILL-REUSE fires on the cost clause (`fired`) | As above |
| No unsafe reuse, saving at least 1/5, and the incumbent dependency graph does not match that saving | Not fired (`not-fired`, citing the GATE-ATEL-11 receipt) | Rules enabled one at a time, each only for the verdict kinds and components on which it preserved obligations in the campaign; grade 3 stays off by default and on by flag, per campaign, and does not become the default before a second campaign of amendments, frozen separately, repeats the result |
| No unsafe reuse, saving at least 1/5, but the incumbent graph matches it | Not fired on the letter (`not-fired`); adoption declined (DESIGN: the saving is then a property of good keys, not of implication) | `disabled`, recorded as "declined" |

**Fallback, named in advance.** Grades 1 and 2 only.

**If it fires, what changes downstream.**
- Grades 1 and 2 only. `result.revalidate` and the mission verb return grade 4 for every obligation whose
  support was touched; their output schema is unchanged (grade 3 simply never appears). A request that allows
  grade 3 (`--grades 3`) is answered under the release configuration with no grade-3 licence and a warning
  that names the fallback; it is not refused, which is the behaviour the verb's surface fixes.
- Licence records of grade 3 already issued (in flagged or canary campaigns) are revoked and their verdicts
  scheduled for recheck. The decision produces that list and the record carries it; afterwards a ledger query
  returns no stored verdict with a grade-3 licence, and every revoked verdict has a recheck job or a
  `not-rechecked` mark.
- The mission subsystem's implication rules fall under the same decision for `result.revalidate`; its own
  standing gate continues to remove single rules.
- Reports print `reuse: dependency-keyed only (KILL-REUSE)`. No ten-fold arithmetic changes, since
  revalidation was never counted as a source of the ten-fold.
- The sixteen polarity rules stay in the registry as `disabled` and stay tested: an auditor still uses them
  to *reject* an unlicensed claim that a verdict survived, and refusal `FROB-REF-870` (reuse of a verdict
  with no licence, or under a rule the registry does not know) is unaffected.
- The rerun planner's plans grow by the obligations grade 3 would have preserved; the reuse ledger's
  "evidence carried forward" column shrinks accordingly and says why.
- The bet that revalidation never returns a stale verdict (falsified by one false reused verdict on the
  sealed amendment suite) is unaffected on the fallback, since grades 1 and 2 passed the same gate.
- Nothing in the cascade depends on grade 3; stage 2's control (a mirror swap must reuse; a shared-bus edit
  must invalidate) is satisfied by grades 1 and 2.
- Beads on the adopt branch only: per-rule enablement and its canary campaign. They depend on this decision.

**Record.** The decision record is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-REUSE-M4.md` (N40-03's one form, convention 10), with
N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied;
each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (U against zero, C₂, C₃ and s against one
fifth, or the pilot's falsifier); the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name revalidation carries from
now on; the beads re-read; what changes downstream; the recording session, which implemented no part of grade
3. Beside them: the campaign identity, commit, hardware fingerprint and seeds, the registry diff (per rule),
the list of revoked licences, the reviewer's session, the date, and a ledger evidence record. The KILL-REUSE
row of the gate register gets its decision entry `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` with
that path and the receipt hashes; the decision is taken by a session that did not run the measurement. A decision recorded before the
measurement's ledger sequence is rejected.

- [ ] The honest-status table describes revalidation by the grades actually in force.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs --test kill_reuse_switch`:
- `kill_reuse_rule_table`: (pilot falsified) → fired; (U = 1, s = 0.6) → fired; (U = 0, s = 0.12) → fired;
  (U = 0, s = 0.31) → not fired.
- `saving_formula`: C₂ = 400 core-s, C₃ = 300 core-s ⇒ s = 0.25.
- `fired_revokes_grade3_licences`; `fired_request_for_grade3_gets_grade4_and_warning`.
- `all_disabled_means_no_grade3`;
`enabled_rule_scoped_to_kind` (a rule enabled for mission inclusions does not preserve a design-comparison
obligation); `planted_unsafe_reuse_disables_rule` (the registry row flips to `disabled` and the next call
returns grade 4); `disabled_rules_still_reject_unlicensed_claims` (refusal 870 unaffected).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_reuse_switch.sh` sources the shared helper of STD-E2E. Part one (the record): it reads the
gate row and the arm table, recomputes U and s, applies the rule table and compares the result with the
decision record and the register; when fired it calls `frob result revalidate … --grades 3 --key kr-fired
--json` to observe the fallback answer and counts the revoked licences. Part two (the switch): under the
scratch copy of the registries that STD-E2E's `e2e_registries_scratch` makes, in each state (all disabled; one
rule enabled; that rule then disabled by a planted unsafe reuse) it runs `frob result revalidate … --grades 3
--key kr-<state>-<fixture> --json` on the interval-reach and doorway fixtures (a run verb: one key per state
and fixture, so no call returns another state's stored answer) and asserts the grades and licences returned, and that a hand-made claim of survival without a licence is refused with 870 in every state. Log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_reuse_switch/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, per
obligation the grade and licence, the registry state hash, U against 0, s against 1/5, the decision, the
revoked licence count, the decision-record path, and on failure the first obligation whose grade contradicts
the registry state. Final `summary` line naming KILL-REUSE.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The cost clause's arithmetic: C₂ = 400 s and C₃ = 340 s give a saving of 15%, below one fifth: fires;
  C₃ = 300 s gives 25%: does not fire on cost.
- The kept negative result: on the four-state camera fixture a semantic receipt and a dependency graph both
  evaluated 2, 0, 0, 1 scene pairs and recomputed the verdict 1, 0, 0, 1 times; the decision record restates
  it as the prior against which the campaign's saving is read.
- Negative controls: a ruling "not fired" filed while the receipt shows an unsafe reuse is rejected by the
  record's own check (the ruling is validated against the receipt's fields); an unsafe reuse that a later
  amendment "repairs" must still count in U; a register row holding the target 1/5 in place of the measured
  s is rejected.
- Determinism: the grades returned are a function of the registry version recorded on each answer.

## Considerations

- The expected outcome, by the plan's own evidence, is that the gate fires on the cost clause. That is a
  cheap, clean result, and the system's safety argument does not change.
- The one-fifth threshold is a TARGET fixed before data; it is not to be revisited after the receipt is
  read. A reopening needs new evidence (a richer plant on which a non-dependence certificate is cheap) and a
  newly sealed campaign.
- Per-rule enablement keeps a single bad rule from costing the others, and keeps the blast radius of a
  future defect to one registry row.
- The asymmetry is deliberate: one unsafe reuse outweighs any saving. The saving threshold exists so that a
  safe but pointless mechanism does not stay in the product as a liability.
- The ceiling was stated first: reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is at most about a tenth of compute, so
  even a kept grade 3 is an evidence-hygiene feature.
