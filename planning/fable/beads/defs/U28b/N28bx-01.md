## Background

The scene subsystem's retrofit search (`scene.retrofit`) makes the building a design variable with prices,
so that "add a ramp" and "fit larger wheels" are compared in one budget. Its procedure has five steps, and
the fourth is not the scene subsystem's work: *the cheapest design edit that flips the same verdict is
requested from the design search, priced from its part cards, with its controller obtained by the route the
original used. A redesign changes the model, so its arm is not frozen-policy and its report says so.* The
facility query of the mission subsystem leans on the same sentence: editing the floor and editing the robot
enter the same budget.

No bead delivers that request. The optimization verbs (`opt.start`, `opt.status`) move a design's declared
levers to improve a *task metric*; mission refutation returns the smallest change of the *mission* that
relaxes a refuted query; the dual mission query returns the smallest priced *world* edit. The missing piece
is the mirror image of the kit search on the robot side: given a failing verdict and its evidence, search
the priced edits of the design in order of price and return the first one that flips it. Without it the
retrofit report can only say `unresolved` in its redesign column, and the comparison that is the point of
the catalogue (bet BET-37, North-Star scenario NS-18: a twelve-dollar ramp against a chassis redesign) is
never made. Tier: Solid: it is orchestration over typed edits, part cards and existing re-decision, and adds
no numerical hypothesis.

Terms. A *typed edit* is one entry of the design language's edit catalogue (`set-lever`, `set-param`,
`replace`, …), each with a precondition, an inverse and a declared invalidation set. A *lever* is a
parameter the design declares free, with bounds; optimizers may move only levers, and a locked path may not
be written. A *part card* is the data card of a purchased part or a material, with a price field carrying
provenance (`measured`, `datasheet`, `assumed`). A *verdict record* (`frob.verdict/1`) names the requirement
decided, the world addresses and seeds, the identities read, and the outcome.

Design decisions (DESIGN, each with its reason).
1. **Price order, full re-decision.** Candidates are tried cheapest first and each is decided by the full
   procedure that produced the failing verdict. Reason: a static figure is a screen, not a verdict. For a
   20 mm edge the friction a wheel needs is tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h), yet in an executed planar sill
   study 62 of 156 cases that a sampled static test rejected succeeded dynamically (FACT, source register;
   re-read before a gate cites it). A search that prunes on the screen returns a dearer wheel than needed.
2. **The minimality claim is typed.** `minimum` only when the candidate set is finite and every cheaper
   candidate was decided and did not flip; otherwise `feasible-candidate` with the gap to the cheapest
   undecided candidate. Reason: a search that stopped is not a proof that nothing cheaper works.
3. **No new verb.** The entry point is a campaign objective of `opt.start`. Reason: the verb registry has no
   row for a separate search verb, and the campaign record, budget, cancellation and status already exist.

Rejected: ranking redesigns by simulated reward (the criterion is the hard requirement that failed);
letting the search move any parameter (it would find numbers nobody meant to free); reusing the original
policy on the redesigned body (a different model under an old policy is an untested pair, not a redesign).

## Scope

In: the candidate generator over priced edits; price deltas with provenance; the controller route; the
search loop, its claim and its record; the oracle interface through which a verdict's owner re-decides; the
built-in oracle for task verdicts; `opt.start --objective flip-verdict`; refusals; the wheel-against-ramp
known answer on the design side.

Out: the kit search, the frozen-policy arm, the report and the re-decision rules for clearance cuts,
counterexample worlds and alias witnesses (N19b-39, N19b-40, which supply their oracle to this bead); the
catalogue of building amendments (BD-369); continuous optimizers (N28b-07); price sources (the owner
decision on card prices; until it is taken prices are `assumed` and flagged).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-opt/src/flip/{candidates.rs, price.rs, route.rs, search.rs}`; objective
registered in `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/opt_start.rs`.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub trait VerdictOracle {            // implemented by the owner of the failing verdict
    fn addresses(&self) -> &AddressSet;                      // world addresses and seeds of the failure
    fn redecide(&self, model: ModelId, controller: ControllerRef) -> Redecision; // Flipped | NotFlipped | Unresolved{band}
}
pub struct FlipCandidate { edits: Vec<TypedEdit>, price_delta: Money, provenance: Provenance, screen: Option<ScreenNote> }
pub struct FlipResult { claim: FlipClaim, edit: Option<FlipCandidate>, design: DesignId, model: ModelId,
                        controller_route: Route, policy: Option<PolicyId>, verdict: VerdictId,
                        tried: Vec<Tried>, unresolved: Vec<Open>, frozen_policy: bool /* always false */ }
pub enum FlipClaim { Minimum, FeasibleCandidate { gap: Money }, NoneFound, NothingToFlip }
pub enum Route { BaselineSynthesis, FamilyPolicy { class }, TrainingRecipe { recipe: RecipeId } }
```

1. **Candidates.** From the design, within its locks: (a) every value of every `finite` lever; (b) every
   `replace` of a module or card by a catalogue alternative with the same port signature (wheel sizes,
   actuator cards, soles); (c) each continuous lever sampled on a declared ladder of at most 9 points across
   its bounds (DESIGN default); (d) pairs of the above when `max_edits = 2` (default 1; cap 2). A candidate
   whose edit fails its precondition or a required analysis is dropped with the refusal code as its reason.
2. **Price.** `price_delta` = cost roll-up of the edited design − cost roll-up of the original, both from
   part-card prices by the design's own budget rule; provenance is the weakest of the cards involved. A
   candidate with an unpriced card is dropped with reason `unpriced`. A negative delta (a cheaper part that
   flips) is allowed and sorts first.
3. **Order.** Ascending `price_delta`; ties by the number of edits, then by the edit's canonical bytes. The
   static screens of the analyses (friction demand at an edge, reach, envelope) annotate a candidate
   (`screen`) and never remove it.
4. **Controller by the original's route.** Read how the failing design's controller was obtained from its
   evidence: `baseline-synthesis` → synthesize again for the new model; `family-policy` → apply the class
   policy if the edited design is inside the class, otherwise drop the candidate with FROB-REF-534 (a family
   policy applied outside its class) as the reason; `training-recipe` → re-run the declared recipe with its
   recorded seeds and budget, charged to the campaign. The route and any new policy identity are recorded.
5. **Search.** For each candidate in order, within the compute budget: apply the edits (new `DesignId`),
   compile, obtain the controller, call `oracle.redecide`. Stop at the first `Flipped`. `Unresolved` is
   recorded and the search continues; it is never counted as a flip.
6. **Claim.** `Minimum` if every cheaper candidate is `NotFlipped`; `FeasibleCandidate { gap }` if a cheaper
   candidate is `Unresolved`, was dropped for budget, or lies between ladder points of a continuous lever
   (gap = price of the found edit − price of the cheapest such candidate); `NoneFound` otherwise.
7. **Built-in oracle.** For a task verdict record: re-run the same requirement on the same addresses and
   seeds at the tier that produced it; `Flipped` when the requirement is satisfied by the record's own
   decision rule. Other evidence kinds are decided by the oracle their owner passes in.
8. **Surface.** `frob opt start --objective flip-verdict --verdict <id> [--max-edits 1] [--max-price …]
   [--budget …] --key <k> --json` (the design is the verdict's; a run verb, so `--key` is required and two
   searches a test compares carry distinct keys); progress and the result through `opt.status`. Input schema
   `frob.opt.start.in/1` gains the objective kind and the inputs `verdict`, `max_edits`, `max_price`; result
   schema `frob.opt.flip/1`. Exit 0 when an edit is found, 2 when none is (finding: candidates tried and why
   each failed), 8 while running. The `opt.start` row of VERBS-opt lists the objective, its inputs `--verdict`,
   `--max-edits` (above 2 is 802) and `--max-price`, and FROB-REF-869 under `--objective flip-verdict`, with
   this bead as their owner.
9. **Evidence.** Claim kind Design comparison, class `Tested`; a fast-tier flip is `provisional` until
   promoted by the trust ladder; `data_flags: ["prices: assumed"]` whenever a price is assumed; Reality
   `no-claim`. Determinism: candidate order and prices D0; the verdicts carry the class of their tier.

Refusals and findings (existing codes): a verdict whose citation does not resolve (its evidence closure is
dangling or tombstoned): FROB-REF-869; a verdict that is not a failure (its outcome already satisfies the
requirement): no candidate is generated and the answer is exit 2 with claim `NothingToFlip` (a finding, not
a refusal; FROB-REF-869 is about dangling citations and does not fit it); a design with no lever and no catalogue alternative: exit 2
with `NoneFound` and an empty `tried` (a finding, not a refusal); an edit to a locked path is never
generated (FROB-REF-111 would be the edit engine's answer and is asserted not to occur).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Wheel against ramp, design side: on the planted 20 mm threshold failure with a wheel catalogue of radii
      50, 60, 75, 100 and 125 mm at rising prices, the search returns `replace wheel → 100 mm` as `Minimum`
      on the quasi-static fixture (creep approach), with its price delta and provenance, the new `ModelId`,
      `controller_route`, `frozen_policy: false`, and a passing verdict on the same world addresses.
- [ ] On the dynamic variant (approach at 0.63 m/s) where the 75 mm wheel succeeds although its static
      screen (0.927 > μ = 0.8) fails, the search returns 75 mm; a build that prunes on the screen is caught.
- [ ] With the 75 mm run forced `Unresolved`, the claim is `FeasibleCandidate` with the gap equal to the
      price difference between the 100 mm and 75 mm wheels.
- [ ] No candidate writes a locked path; an unpriced card and an out-of-class family policy each drop their
      candidate with the stated reason.
- [ ] The entry point accepts an owner-supplied `VerdictOracle` and calls it only on the verdict's recorded
      addresses (tested with a `synthetic` oracle that flips at a chosen candidate). `scene.retrofit
      --compare-redesign` (N19b-40, which depends on this bead) fills its `redesign` and `redesign_verdict`
      fields through this hook with its own oracle; that use is asserted by N19b-40's retrofit gate script, not
      here.
- [ ] `opt.start --objective flip-verdict` is reachable through `frob --json` and MCP, is cancellable
      between candidates, and charges every re-decision and every controller route to the campaign budget.
- [ ] Planted defects are each caught: `prune-on-static-screen`; `stop-at-first-by-generation-order`
      (not price order); `reuse-old-policy`; `claim-minimum-with-unresolved-cheaper`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test flip`:

- `friction_demand_screen_values`: tan α for h = 20 mm at r = 50, 60, 75, 100, 125 mm is 4/3, 1.1180,
  0.9271, 0.75, 0.6459 (annotation only).
- `order_is_price_then_size_then_bytes`; `negative_delta_sorts_first`.
- `locked_path_never_generated`; `unpriced_dropped`; `family_policy_out_of_class_dropped_534`.
- `minimum_requires_all_cheaper_decided`; `gap_is_to_cheapest_undecided`.
- `route_follows_original`: three fixtures, one per route; the training-recipe fixture uses a `synthetic`
  recipe of 10³ steps and asserts the same seeds are replayed.
- `unresolved_is_not_a_flip`; `budget_stop_reports_tried`.
- `oracle_is_called_on_recorded_addresses`: the address set passed to the oracle equals the verdict's.
- `pairs_only_with_max_edits_2`.
- `passing_verdict_nothing_to_flip`: a verdict that already passes → claim `NothingToFlip`, exit 2, zero
  candidates tried; `dangling_verdict_refused_869`: a verdict whose evidence closure is tombstoned → 869.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/opt_flip_verdict.sh --seed <s>`: (1) create the threshold fixture and run the task to obtain
the failing verdict; (2) `frob opt start --objective flip-verdict --verdict <id> --key flip-1 --json`, poll
`frob opt status`; (3) assert the edit, claim, price delta, identities, route and the passing verdict on the
same addresses; (4) the dynamic variant (`flip-2`); (5) the forced-unresolved variant (`flip-3`); (6)
cancellation with `frob job cancel` after the first candidate (`flip-4`); every variant has its own key, so
none is answered from another's stored receipt. The retrofit gate script (`scripts/e2e/scene_retrofit_gate.sh`, owned by the retrofit beads)
exercises the same entry point through `scene.retrofit`. NDJSON per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/opt_flip_verdict/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities including the policy identity, seed), per candidate its edits, price delta, provenance, screen
note and outcome, the campaign spend, and on failure the first differing field with the campaign and run
identifiers. The `summary` line lists `gates: ["GATE-HAB-11"]` as touched; the gate is recorded by the
retrofit gate bead.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Closed form: a driven wheel of radius r meeting an edge of height h quasi-statically needs μ ≥
  √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h): 4/3 for r = 50 mm, h = 20 mm (impossible on any floor card); 0.75 for r = 100 mm.
  The retrofit search must return the ramp (a wedge with run twelve times its rise needs 1/12), this search
  must return the wheel, and the report shows both.
- A body arriving at √(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the energy to rise 20 mm before losses: the dynamic variant.
- Negative controls: the four planted defects.
- Metamorphic relation: raising every price by one factor leaves the returned edit unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine (same candidate order, same result); debug and release.

## Considerations

- Prices and their legality are `assumed` until the owner decides the price sources; every result says so,
  and a comparison of a kit price with a redesign price inherits the weaker provenance.
- A continuous lever's ladder cannot prove a minimum; the claim says `feasible-candidate` there. A mission
  query with a declared cost and a matching lower bound is the route to a proved minimum.
- The training-recipe route can dominate the cost of the search; the budget is checked before a candidate
  starts, and a candidate that cannot be afforded is recorded as undecided, never skipped silently.
- If the retrofit kill gate fires, the search verb of the scene side is withdrawn; this objective remains
  usable on its own.
