## Background

The design studio (Atelier) compiles each of its eleven recurring decisions into a decision core: one mathematical
family, compiled ahead of time into a small runtime artifact, with a loss model, proof obligations, a
deterministic fallback, a budgeted mode and an adoption status, stored as a record under `registries/cores/`
(ADR-ATEL-06: the runtime evaluates tables; no decision reads the wall clock, device load or arrival order;
reason: replay, audit, and a rollback that is a configuration flag). Three cores are Solid [S] and due at
milestone M3, because the mechanisms they govern land at M3:

- DC-01: promote a fast-tier result, reject it as tier-sensitive, or buy another pair (governs `eval.promote`).
- DC-09: which job runs when on a device (governs the daemon `frobd`).
- DC-10: which scenes form a battery (governs `nemesis.cover`).

The mechanisms belong to other subsystems (the trust ladder, the daemon, the adversarial-world subsystem). This
bead fixes the decision rule of each as a registry record: the table, its loss model, its obligations, its
fallback. It then demonstrates the M3 rows of gate GATE-ATEL-13: each core's table regenerates bit-identically,
its null calibration holds, its fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger, and its shadow run records both
actions. Each core ships in shadow behind its fallback (ADR-ATEL-09): it reports both rules and acts on the old
one until the comparison has been read. None of the numbers below is a measurement; worked values are
illustrative arithmetic and serve as known answers for the tables.

Alternatives rejected for these three cores, with the reason on record so the question is not reopened without
new evidence: a learned correction of the fast tier, or a learned step surrogate as a screen (DC-01: it hides
exactly the artifacts the trust ladder exists to expose); adaptive-fidelity stopping as a decision procedure
(DC-01: stopping a pair early on cheap labels does not compute the sealed fixed-n decision; kept only as a
scheduling heuristic that orders work); bandit allocation on dispatch (DC-09: a second controller on shared
telemetry, and regret is the wrong objective); mechanism design for the queue (DC-09: estimates come from the
system, not from agents); replicated consensus, gossip membership and escrow counters (DC-09: one daemon per host,
nothing is replicated).

## Scope

In: the three records `registries/cores/dc-01.toml`, `dc-09.toml`, `dc-10.toml` with every contract field; their
committed default tables; replay scenarios, calibration reports and demonstration manifests; the M3 measurement of
GATE-ATEL-13 for the three; registration so that `frob cores show` displays them.
Out: the contract and loader (N28c-01); the compiler (N28c-02); decision records and adoption (N28c-03); the verb
(N28c-04); the mechanisms and their own gates (BD-263 with GATE-FID-02; BD-296 with GATE-TETHER-10 and
KILL-PREEMPT; BD-278 with GATE-NEMESIS-08); the other eight cores (BD-349); interference between cores (BD-350).

## Technical approach

Each record has the fields: decision, owner crate, timescale, family, artifacts (`frob.core-table/1` with version
and hash), loss (states, actions, cost constants), obligations, fallback and triggers, budgeted mode, adoption
status, replay scenario, calibration report, demonstration manifest (commands under 60 s, checksums, claim
identifiers supported).

**DC-01 · Promote, reject or keep sampling.** Policy `atelier.dc01.promote`; owner `frob-opt`; timescale per
episode batch; family sequential evidence.
- Artifacts. One bit per paired episode: did the fast tier and the referee disagree at this world address beyond
  the fidelity ledger's frozen envelope for the quantity? Two likelihood-ratio e-processes on the exceedance rate
  with rates p₀ < p₁ fixed in advance: E⁺ tests the null "rate ≥ p₁" and promotes on crossing 1/α⁺; E⁻ tests the
  null "rate ≤ p₀" and labels the result `tier-sensitive` on crossing 1/α⁻. After n pairs with k exceedances,
  ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁) and E⁻ = (p₁/p₀)ᵏ·((1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀))ⁿ⁻ᵏ. Both compile to one
  integer guard table (n, k) → promote, reject, continue, per ledger stratum, with the envelope hash and the
  address schedule hash in its header.
- Loss model. States: faithful, sensitive. Actions: promote, reject, buy a pair at cost c. Thresholds
  α⁺ = c/(π·C_fp) and α⁻ = c/((1 − π)·C_fn), with π the ledger's share of sensitive results and C_fp (cost of a
  wrong promotion) and C_fn (cost of a wrong rejection) declared in the record. The constants are OPEN-ATEL-1;
  until the owner decides them (N28c-26) the record carries the illustrative set c = 2, π = 0.1, C_fp = 2,000,
  giving α⁺ = 2/(0.1 × 2,000) = 0.01, and α⁻ = 0.05, with p₀ = 0.05, p₁ = 0.30, each labelled `open`.
- Obligations. PO-ATEL-01: wrong promotion at most α⁺ and wrong rejection at most α⁻ under optional stopping, by
  Ville's inequality (a nonnegative supermartingale that starts at 1 exceeds 1/α with probability at most α at any
  stopping time), provided episodes are chosen by address before outcomes exist and the envelope is frozen first.
  Independence between episodes is not needed: under any conditional exceedance probability p ≥ p₁ each factor of
  E⁺ has expectation (1 − p)(1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁) + p·p₀/p₁ ≤ 1.
- Fallback and triggers. Fixed 30 pairs, promote on at most 2 exceedances (at p₁ = 0.30 this promotes wrongly with
  probability 0.0021, and at p₀ = 0.05 it promotes with probability 0.81; binomial arithmetic). Triggers:
  `missing-ledger-stratum`; `identity-mismatch`; `null-self-test-failed`.
- Budgeted mode. 32 pairs per result; on exhaustion the result stays `provisional`.
- Worked values (known answers). A clean referee pair multiplies the evidence by 0.95/0.70 = 1.36 and an
  exceedance divides it by 6. Promotion takes 16 clean pairs, 22 with one exceedance, 29 with two; at α⁻ = 0.05 two
  exceedances within the first three pairs reject. If C_fp is ten times smaller (α⁺ = 0.1), or p₁ = 0.5, eight
  clean pairs suffice.
- Primary risk: an envelope tuned after outcomes. Countermeasure: the envelope is hashed into the ledger before
  sampling. Negative control: the planted tier-sensitive policy of the fidelity gate must be caught.

**DC-09 · Which job runs when.** Policy `tether.dc09.dispatch` (DESIGN name); owner `frobd`; timescale per quantum;
family queueing with size classes and preempt-resume.
- Artifacts. Size classes from the system's own `--estimate`, never from the agent (`interactive`: a point estimate
  of at most 30 s of device time; `batch`; `campaign`); preempt-resume at snapshot boundaries with a quantum of at
  most 5 s; deficit round-robin per agent, weighted by remaining budget, shortest estimated remaining work first
  within an agent; an admission guard; a capacity table of wait by class against utilization; a monitor of
  estimate calibration with a conformal interval on step time per device family. The guard table with hysteresis
  maps integer ledger state to an action in five states: under-filled (free slots beyond the reserve while
  episodes of this shape class wait → co-batch at the next reset boundary); saturated (no free slot and an
  interactive job waiting → use the reserve; if full, preempt the batch job with the most remaining work at its
  next snapshot boundary); mixed shapes (open a second environment if the memory preflight passes and the job's
  estimated work exceeds the creation cost, otherwise queue); one agent dominant (serve the others first);
  estimates unreliable (fallback).
- Loss model. Agent idle time on interactive rungs, against preemption overhead and unfairness. In seconds:
  L = Σ_j c(j)·wait_j + (idle slot-seconds ÷ B) + Σ_a max(0, deficit_a) + Σ preemption cost, with c = 1 for
  interactive and 0.1 for batch jobs, B the batch capacity in slots, and the preemption cost its measured snapshot
  and restore time (DESIGN defaults, refitted from the M2 queue trace).
- Obligations. Utilization below 0.8 per device (admission guard); a resumed job keeps its digests; an ageing bound
  prevents starvation (a waiting job's effective work decays linearly to zero over 600 s). Arrivals are assumed
  near-Poisson; swarms burst, so the capacity table multiplies waits by Kingman's correction
  (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²), with c_a² and c_s² the squared coefficients of variation of inter-arrival and service
  times measured from the daemon's own trace.
- Fallback and triggers. Round-robin per agent, run to completion. Triggers: `estimates-unreliable` (of the last 50
  jobs, a fifth exceeded twice their estimate); `resume-digest-differs` (then also per-job dispatch and an alarm).
- Budgeted mode. One preemption per batch job per 30 s; the batch class keeps at least 30% of device time.
- Worked values (known answers, M/G/1 arithmetic, a model and not a measurement). One device at utilization
  ρ = 0.7 with 90% of jobs taking 10 s and 10% taking 30 min: E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s², λ = 1/270 s⁻¹,
  short-class load ρ₁ = 0.0333. First come, first served: W = λ·E[S²]/(2(1 − ρ)) = 2,000.6 s. Non-preemptive
  priority for the short class: λ·E[S²]/(2(1 − ρ₁)) = 620.9 s. Preempt-resume: λ₁·E[S₁²]/(2(1 − ρ₁)) = 0.17 s
  (printed as about 0.2 s) plus at most one quantum; long jobs slow by 1/(1 − ρ₁) − 1 = 3.4%. Capacity-table rows
  at ρ = 0.5, 0.8, 0.9: (857 s, 439 s, 0.12 s), (3,430 s, 713 s, 0.20 s), (7,716 s, 806 s, 0.22 s).
- Flip condition recorded in the record: if a device is reserved for interactive work, or a resume costs several
  seconds, running to completion is right.
- Scope rule. The core decides when a job's episodes run and never which episodes exist or in what order a study
  consumes them.

**DC-10 · Which scenes form a battery.** Policy `arena.dc10.battery` (DESIGN name); owner `frob-arena`; timescale
per battery revision; family submodular coverage.
- Artifacts. A scene × failure-mode incidence matrix (a scene exposes a mode when at least one reference design
  fails in that mode in that scene), each mode weighted by frequency × severity × designs affected, in integer
  weights; a lazy-greedy trace of marginal gains; the certificate OPT_k ≤ f(S) + (sum of the k largest remaining
  marginal gains), where f(S) is the total weight of modes exposed by at least one scene of S; one slot in five
  reserved for random held-out scenes.
- Loss model. Uncovered failure-mode weight, against battery runtime.
- Obligations. Weighted coverage is monotone and submodular, so greedy selection reaches (1 − 1/e)·OPT at a fixed
  number of scenes; with unequal scene costs the cost-benefit variant (rank by gain per second) is used and its
  weaker ratio is stated; the matrix records the identities it was built under (model class, tier, kernel
  versions, taxonomy). PO-ATEL-05: the greedy certificate is correct, discharged by independent recomputation of
  marginal gains in integer weights.
- Fallback and trigger. The standard battery of the verification program. Trigger: `identity-changed` (any change
  of model class, tier, kernel versions or taxonomy since the matrix was built).
- Budgeted mode. Any greedy prefix is a valid battery with its own certificate.
- Failure mode. A battery that overfits past failures; the held-out slots, and the rule that a rare-failure bound
  (DC-04) refuses battery-selected episodes, contain it.
- Worked values (known answers). 40 modes of total weight 100; greedy gains of 31, 22, 14, 9, 6 and 4 cover 86; the
  six largest remaining gains sum to 12, so OPT₆ ≤ 98 and the battery is within 86/98 = 88% of the best six-scene
  battery, against a worst case of 63%.

**Steps.**
1. Write the three records against the contract; register each generator with the table compiler; commit the
   default tables and their hashes.
2. Write a replay scenario per core (a recorded decision sequence that must be reproduced from the table and the
   pinned ledger rows) and a calibration report (DC-01: wrong-promotion and wrong-rejection frequencies over 10⁴
   null runs at p₁ and at p₀ with optional stopping; DC-09: the capacity-table rows recomputed, and the estimate
   calibration monitor's coverage on the queue trace; DC-10: lazy greedy equals plain greedy, and the brute-force
   optimum lies under the certificate on every instance of 20 scenes or fewer).
3. Write `demos/dc-01/demo.yaml`, `demos/dc-09/demo.yaml`, `demos/dc-10/demo.yaml`: commands under 60 s on the CPU
   tier with checksums and the claim identifiers they support.
4. Force every trigger (through the forcing interface of the decision-record module) and record the fallback
   action for each: three for DC-01, two for DC-09, one for DC-10.
5. Run each core in shadow on its mechanism's M3 fixtures and record both actions per decision.
6. Record the GATE-ATEL-13 rows for the three cores in the gate registry with the measured values.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/cores/dc-01.toml`, `dc-09.toml` and `dc-10.toml` load, pass `xtask check-contracts`, and
      `frob cores show dc-01|dc-09|dc-10 --json` displays table, loss model, obligations, fallback and status.
- [ ] Seed done-condition, first half: each of the three tables regenerates bit-identically under
      `xtask check-generated` (hashes equal on the Mac and the Linux host, debug and release).
- [ ] Seed done-condition, second half: each fallback is demonstrated by forcing each of its triggers (six
      triggers in all); every forced decision's record shows `rule = Fallback { trigger }` and the fallback action.
- [ ] Null calibration, DC-01 (PO-ATEL-01): over 10⁴ seeded null runs at exceedance rate p₁ with adversarial
      optional stopping the wrong-promotion frequency is within the exact binomial acceptance bound for α⁺ = 0.01
      (at most 132 of 10⁴ at a 0.001 test level), and at rate p₀ the wrong-rejection frequency is within the bound
      for α⁻ = 0.05.
- [ ] DC-01 table known answers: promote at (n, k) = (16, 0), (22, 1), (29, 2); reject at (3, 2); continue at
      (15, 0), (21, 1).
- [ ] DC-09 capacity-table known answers at ρ = 0.7: 2,000.6 s, 620.9 s and 0.17 s (each within 0.5%); the guard
      table is part of the daemon's configuration digest.
- [ ] DC-10 certificate known answer: gains (31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4) cover 86; bound 98; ratio at least 0.877; the
      certificate recomputed independently in integer weights agrees (PO-ATEL-05).
- [ ] Each core's shadow run on its M3 fixtures records both actions for 100% of decisions; the applied action is
      the baseline's.
- [ ] The negative controls below are caught; GATE-ATEL-13 rows for DC-01, DC-09 and DC-10 are recorded with
      measured values, by a session that did not implement the work.
- [ ] Each demonstration manifest runs in under 60 s and its checksums match.

  fields present; obligations resolve; governed verbs exist.

- `dc09_capacity_rows`: the four utilization rows from E[S] = 189, E[S²] = 324,090 in exact rationals, compared to

- `dc09_guard_states`: five synthetic integer states, one per row of the guard table, give the five actions;
  hysteresis: a state oscillating across the saturation boundary changes action at most once per two quanta.
- `dc09_ageing_bound`: a job waiting 600 s has effective work 0 and is selected before any newer job.
- `dc10_greedy_certificate`: a 12-scene integer instance built to give gains (31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4) returns covered
  86 and bound 98; `dc10_lazy_equals_plain`; `dc10_bruteforce_under_certificate` on 200 random instances of at most
  20 scenes; `dc10_holdout_slots`: one slot in five is filled from the held-out pool by a keyed draw.
- `forced_triggers_all_six`: each trigger yields the fallback action and a record.
- `shadow_records_both`: for each core on a scripted fixture.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m3.sh` (N28c-05) runs the regeneration check, `frob cores show` for the three cores,
the three demonstration manifests, the forced-trigger demonstrations and a shadow run through the real governed
verbs (`frob eval promote`, a queued pair of jobs under `frobd`, `frob nemesis cover`). It logs table hashes,
null-calibration counts against their bounds, each forced trigger with the observed action, shadow agreement
counts, and a `summary` line naming GATE-ATEL-13.

## Considerations

- OPEN-ATEL-1 (the loss constants of DC-01) blocks the DC-01 row of GATE-ATEL-13, which is why this bead depends
  on the owner-decision bead for it. The record can be written, regenerated and demonstrated with the
  illustrative constants, but the thresholds α⁺ and α⁻ derive from the constants, so until the owner fixes them
  the DC-01 row's status is `blocked: OPEN-ATEL-1`, DC-01 stays in shadow and its fixed 30-pair fallback acts.
  The DC-09 and DC-10 rows are not blocked and are recorded as soon as they are measured.
- The tables for DC-01 are per ledger stratum; only the default table is committed. A stratum created at run time
  gets its table from the same generator, recorded with its hash before first use.
- DC-09's obligation that scheduling does not change evidence (PO-ATEL-09: random preemption, reordering and
  co-tenancy over 1,000 episodes in 100 schedules leave digests and verdicts identical) is measured with the
  composition tests at M4 (BD-350); at M3 the daemon's own gate covers resume digests.
- The plan prints the preempt-resume wait both as 0.2 s and as 0.17 s; the exact value is 0.1724 s and tests use
  0.17 with a 0.5% tolerance on the exact rational.
- If KILL-PREEMPT fires for the daemon, DC-09's record is set to `fallback-only` (round-robin, run to completion)
  and the registry says so; the same convention applies to any core whose mechanism is killed.
