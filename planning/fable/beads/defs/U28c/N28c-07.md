## Background

The design studio makes eleven recurring decisions through decision cores DC-01 to DC-11 (compiled decision rules,
each from one mathematical family with a table, a loss model, obligations and a fallback). For each core, more
elaborate methods were considered and are deliberately not built. The plan records each rejection with its reason
"so the question is not reopened without new evidence". Without a durable record, a later contributor will propose
the same method again, spend the pilot again, and may ship it without the reason it was rejected ever being
checked. This bead turns the rejection table into a registry file that the core contract check and the verb
`cores.show --rejected` read, and states for each entry what evidence would reopen it.

This is an architecture-decision record in data form. It is cheap, and it protects several expensive rules: for
example that solver effort never reacts to the wall clock (which would make results depend on load and break
replay), and that no learned surrogate stands in front of the trust ladder (which would hide exactly the
artifacts the ladder exists to expose). Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the registry file with every rejected method below; the loader; the contract-check rule; the reopening
procedure; the entry shape the `--rejected` view prints.
Out: building any of these methods; the cores themselves; the `--rejected` option of `cores.show` (N28c-04, which
depends on this bead and reads the file through the loader).

## Technical approach

1. **File `registries/cores/rejected.toml`** (DESIGN): one `[[rejected]]` entry per row with `method`,
   `method_key` (a stable kebab-case key, for example `bandit-allocation`), `patterns` (lower-case substrings
   whose presence in an artifact's `generator` name marks an implementation of the method, for example
   `["bandit", "ucb", "thompson"]`), `cores`, `why_not`, `kept_as` (what survives of the idea, if anything),
   `reopen_condition`, `status = "rejected"`.

   | Method | Core | Why not | What is kept; when to reopen |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | E-graph saturation with a solver over real-arithmetic rewrites | DC-11 | Every accepted rewrite changes bits, so each needs an error bound, a battery run and a new kernel identity, while most step time is expected in the solve | Reopen only if the project's own M2 profile shows more than about a fifth of step time in derived arithmetic |
   | Stochastic superoptimization and counterexample-guided synthesis of kernels | DC-11 | No sound equivalence check exists for floating-point code | Reopen on a sound floating-point equivalence checker |
   | Extreme-value extrapolation of failure tails | DC-04 | Checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ takes the same 3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes that certify directly | Not reopened unless a tail check cheaper than direct certification is shown |
   | Defensive-mixture importance sampling as the certified bound | DC-04 | Its floor is ln(1/δ)/(β·n) for nominal share β, never below crude sampling on the nominal share | Kept for refutation, by a lower bound, and for tightening an estimate once failures are observed |
   | Gaussian-process surrogates over designs | DC-02, DC-05 | No validated kernel exists on typed designs of varying topology; continuous sub-problems already have a Bayesian optimizer from the simulation sibling | Reopen on a validated kernel over typed designs |
   | A learned correction of the fast tier, or a learned step surrogate as a screen | DC-01, DC-02 | It hides exactly the artifacts the trust ladder exists to expose | Kept: paired tier correction on referee episodes that were actually run |
   | Feedback control (PID or model-predictive) on solver effort | DC-08 | Wall-clock feedback into iteration counts makes results depend on load and breaks replay | Kept: a guard table with hysteresis, which passes |
   | Bandit allocation (upper confidence bounds, posterior sampling) | DC-02, DC-09 | Regret is the wrong objective for identifying the best design, and a bandit on dispatch is a second controller on shared telemetry | Kept: the fixed halving table, and the conformal ladder where rank agreement licenses it |
   | Expected information gain | DC-05 | It buys knowledge that cannot change the choice; the estimator is doubly intractable and fails under misspecification | Kept: value of information, which is zero when no outcome can change the choice |
   | Kernel two-sample and optimal-transport drift tests | DC-07 | No power at twenty scalar residuals | Reopen if calibration sets reach hundreds of multivariate residuals |
   | Mechanism design for the queue | DC-09 | Estimates come from the system, not from agents | Kept: size classes with preemption and resume |
   | Coalition-value attribution as the primary output | DC-06 | Values can be negative and cannot establish necessity | Kept as a budgeted description, computed only when no single or pair flips the outcome |
   | Adaptive-fidelity stopping as a decision procedure | DC-01, DC-02 | Stopping a pair early on cheap labels does not compute the sealed fixed-n decision, and coarse concordance has no sound relation to strict outcomes | Kept as a scheduling heuristic that orders work without touching validity |
   | Reuse of a verdict by sensitivity margin | Revalidation (not a core) | Withdrawn for want of a certificate; a matched dependency graph duplicated every saving | Reopen on a cheap, checkable non-dependence certificate |
   | Replicated consensus, gossip membership, escrow counters for the daemon | DC-09 | One daemon per host; nothing is replicated | Kept: a single-writer job ledger |
   | Copy-on-write device state for forks | DC-06 | Wrong bottleneck: copying 8,192 worlds at 30 kB is 0.25 GB | Reopen if per-world state grows by two orders of magnitude |

   Not scored, each for a one-line reason, recorded with `status = "not-scored"`: convex dual prices (one scalar,
   already the DC-05 threshold); Lyapunov and bifurcation analysis of solver effort (the residual monitor covers
   DC-08); observational causal adjustment (exact intervention is available); subset simulation by Markov chains
   (deferred unless forks carry no fresh randomness).
2. **Loader** in `frob-opt::cores::rejected`, returning entries by core.
3. **Contract-check rule** (inside `xtask check-contracts`, no new check): a core record any of whose artifact
   `generator` names contains one of the `patterns` of a rejected entry that lists that core fails the check,
   naming the `method_key`, unless `registries/cores/reopened.toml` holds an entry naming the method, the new
   evidence, the pilot record and the ADR that reopened it. The match is on generator names and not on `family`,
   because the contract (N28c-01) already refuses any family outside its eight; a rejected method that would need
   a new family is stopped there, and reopening it is a change to the contract as well as an entry here.
4. **Enforcement that follows from three entries and is tested elsewhere**, cited here so the link is not lost:
   solver effort reads no clock (the purity guard of decision records and interference test IT-4); the bound of
   DC-04 never uses a fitted tail or an unweighted mixture as certification (the refusal of non-nominal episodes);
   the revalidation rule registry does not contain a sensitivity-margin rule (an unknown rule name is refused
   with `FROB-REF-870`).
5. **Reopening procedure** (documented in the file header): new evidence is recorded; a paper-scale pilot with a
   falsifier is run and registered; an ADR is written; only then is a `reopened.toml` entry added. The rejected
   entry is never deleted; it gains `status = "reopened"` with the ADR.
6. **View.** `frob cores show dc-NN --rejected --json` (N28c-04) lists the entries for the core, each as
   `{ method, why_not, kept_as, reopen_condition, status }`.

Determinism D0; no claim is produced.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/cores/rejected.toml` holds the sixteen rejected methods and the four not-scored ones, each with
      core, reason, what is kept and a reopen condition.
- [ ] `xtask check-contracts` fails, naming `bandit-allocation`, on a synthetic DC-02 record with the admitted
      family `fixed-table` and an artifact generator `frob_opt::ladder::ucb_allocation`, and passes once a
      matching `reopened.toml` entry with an ADR reference is present.
- [ ] The loader returns, for `dc-09`, bandit allocation, mechanism design and replicated consensus with their
      reasons (the `--rejected` view of `cores.show`, N28c-04, prints exactly these).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/core_contract.sh --phase rejected` passes and its planted records fail as stated.
- [ ] An attempt to delete a rejected entry fails the generated-artifact check (the count and keys are pinned by a
      test).
- [ ] The arithmetic in two reasons is checked by tests: 8,192 × 30 kB = 245.76 MB ≈ 0.25 GB; at δ = 0.05 a bound
      of 10⁻⁶ needs ⌈ln(0.05)/ln(1 − 10⁻⁶)⌉ = 2,995,731 nominal episodes, about 3 × 10⁶.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/core_rejected.rs`: `sixteen_plus_four_entries`; `every_entry_has_reason_and_reopen`;
`rejected_family_fails_contract_check`; `reopened_entry_passes_with_adr`; `entries_by_core` (DC-11 has two, DC-04
two, DC-09 three, DC-06 two); `copy_on_write_arithmetic` (245.76 MB); `tail_check_arithmetic` (2,995,731);
`defensive_mixture_floor` (with β = 0.1 and n = 29,958 = ⌈ln(20)/10⁻⁴⌉ clean episodes, the mixture's certified
bound is ln(20)/(0.1 × 29,958) = 0.99998 × 10⁻³, ten times worse than the ln(20)/29,958 = 0.99998 × 10⁻⁴ that the
same episodes certify nominally); `pattern_match_on_generator` (generator `frob_opt::ladder::ucb_allocation` on a
DC-02 record matches `bandit-allocation`; the same generator on a DC-07 record, which the entry does not list,
does not).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the phase `rejected` to `scripts/e2e/core_contract.sh` (written by N28c-01; `--phase rejected`
runs alone and passes at this bead's close). On scratch copies of the registry under
`target/e2e-scratch/core_contract/`: `cargo xtask check-contracts` passes on the clean tree; fails, naming
`bandit-allocation`, with the synthetic DC-02 record whose generator is `frob_opt::ladder::ucb_allocation`; still
fails with a `reopened.toml` entry that has no ADR reference; passes with the ADR reference; and
`cargo xtask check-generated` fails when one rejected entry is deleted. Logged through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/core_contract/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` (`bead` `N28c-07` on this phase's lines): command lines,
exit codes, wall times, the `evidence` block (class Tested, determinism D0), `metric` lines with entry counts by
core against 16 rejected and 4 not scored, `assert` lines with the failing `method_key`, on failure the scratch
path, and the `summary` line. The user-facing view (`frob cores show dc-09 --rejected --json`) is asserted by
`scripts/e2e/cores_show.sh` (N28c-04).

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the three arithmetic checks above (245.76 MB; 2,995,731; 0.99998 × 10⁻³ against 0.99998 × 10⁻⁴).
- Negative control: the synthetic bandit record must fail the contract check; a `reopened.toml` entry without an
  ADR reference must not make it pass; a deleted entry must fail the generated-artifact check.

## Considerations

- The register is not a ban on research. It is a statement of the evidence that would change the decision, and
  the reopen conditions are deliberately concrete (a profile share, a sample size, a checker).
- The profile condition for DC-11 is read at M2 with the measurement of the derivation toolchain; if more than
  about a fifth of step time is in derived arithmetic, the e-graph entry's reopen condition is met and the owner
  decides. FACT to re-read before citing: the Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's
  step, which caps specialization near 1.2 times.
- Keep entries short; the reasoning lives in the bead of the core concerned.
