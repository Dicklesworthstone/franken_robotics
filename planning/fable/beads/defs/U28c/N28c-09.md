## Background

Two decision cores of the design studio decide what evidence is worth buying and what a simulated number may be
cited for. DC-05 decides which experiment to buy next, or to stop (family: Bayesian value of information; owner
`frob-opt`, governing `experiment.suggest`; timescale per experiment). DC-07 decides whether a simulated result
may carry a reality band, and when the band is void (family: a conformal band with an exchangeability alarm; owner
`frob-ground`; timescale per real batch). A decision core is a compiled decision rule with a table, a loss model,
obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode, registered under `registries/cores/`. Gate
GATE-ATEL-13 requires for each core that its table regenerates bit-identically, its null calibration holds, its
fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger, and its shadow run records both actions; this bead measures the
M4 rows for DC-05 and DC-07.

Both cores are Frontier [F] and both are bounded by one rule (ADR-ATEL-07): parametric models rank experiments;
distribution-free tests issue verdicts. The value-of-information model of DC-05 never issues a verdict, so a wrong
model costs efficiency and never validity. DC-07 gives nothing for designs that were never built. For a Frontier
core the fallback is also its kill fallback: if calibration fails this gate after one repair cycle the core stays
on its fallback (for DC-05 that is also the fallback of kill gate KILL-VOI).

Rejected for these cores: expected information gain (DC-05: it buys knowledge that cannot change the choice; the
estimator is doubly intractable and fails under misspecification); Gaussian-process surrogates over designs (no
validated kernel on typed designs of varying topology); kernel two-sample and optimal-transport drift tests
(DC-07: no power at twenty scalar residuals).

## Scope

In: conformance tests and their GATE-ATEL-13 tier entries for DC-05 and DC-07, with the rule text they test.

## Technical approach

**DC-05 record, as tested.**
- Artifacts: an experiment table (simulation at a rung, a bench measurement, a real batch), each row with a cost in
  GPU-second equivalents at a declared exchange rate, a closed-form expected value of sample information (EVSI)
  and their ratio; a Gaussian posterior on the finalists' score differences with sampling, tier-bias and
  reality-gap variance components read from the ledgers; the stop rule: no ratio clears the budget price.
- Formula. For two finalists with posterior difference Δ ~ N(m, s²) and an experiment that removes variance s̃²
  from Δ: EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)], z = |m|/s̃, with φ and Φ the standard normal density and distribution
  function, in the units of the objective. A measurement with noise variance v on a quantity of prior variance w
  removes w²/(w + v). With more finalists the same quantity is the expected reduction in opportunity loss of the
  chosen design.
- Loss model: opportunity loss of the accepted design plus experiment cost.
- Obligations: the value is zero when no outcome can change the choice; its intervals are scored against
  registered predictions; it never issues a verdict (INV-ATEL-12: a value-of-information quantity never appears in
  a verdict field; `FROB-REF-872` when one is cited as a verdict).
- Fallback: the fixed order check, simulate, train, referee. Trigger: `interval-coverage-out-of-band` (80%
  intervals cover outside 65 to 92% over 30 experiments).
- Budgeted mode: 64 candidates, closed form only.
- Exchange rate and constants: OPEN-ATEL-1 (owner Atelier lead); until decided, the fixed experiment order acts
  and the illustrative rate of 30 core-seconds per GPU-second is recorded as `open`.

**DC-05 known answers** (illustrative arithmetic; the table must reproduce it). m = 0.03; sampling variance 0.03²
and tier-bias variance 0.04², so s = 0.05; per-pair noise 0.3 on the fast tier and 0.1 on the referee.

| Experiment | Variance removed | s̃ | EVSI | Cost (GPU-s equivalents) | Value per cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 more fast-tier pairs (acts on the sampling component: w = 0.0009, v = 0.09/200) | 0.00060 | 0.0245 | 0.00131 | 1 | 0.00131 |
| 8 referee pairs (measure Δ itself: w = 0.0025, v = 0.01/8) | 0.001667 | 0.0408 | 0.00550 | 27 | 0.000204 |
| A second batch of 200 fast-tier pairs after the first | 0.00012 | 0.0110 | 1.02 × 10⁻⁵ | 1 | 1.02 × 10⁻⁵ |
| Perfect information on all simulation (s̃ = s) | 0.0025 | 0.05 | 0.00843 | — | — |
| 20 real pairs at noise 0.3, reality-gap term 0.10 (w = 0.0125) | 0.00919 | 0.096 | 0.0251 | declared | — |
| 20 real pairs, reality-gap term 0.02 (w = 0.0029) | 0.00114 | 0.0337 | 0.00345 | declared | — |

The fast tier wins by a factor of 6.4 per unit cost on the first purchase; after it, the referee goes next: cheap
episodes are bought until only tier bias is left, which only the referee can shrink. At m = 0.15 even perfect
information is worth 1.9 × 10⁻⁵: stop and decide.

**DC-07 record, as tested.**
- Artifacts: a one-sided band, real ≥ sim − q̂, per scope key (robot, firmware, floor, policy), with q̂ the
  ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest calibration residual; a test martingale on the band's conformal p-values; the table
  of scopes.
- Loss model: citing a simulated result as predictive outside its calibrated family, against withholding a usable
  prediction inside it.
- Obligations: coverage at least 1 − α within one calibrated robot and task family, varying by about six points
  at 20 calibration episodes; nothing for designs that were never built; conditions of real episodes come from a
  pre-registered draw, and DC-05 chooses only how many; the alarm ends the regime and calibration restarts inside
  the new one.
- Fallback: `reality: no-claim`. Triggers: `under-19-calibration-points`; `martingale-at-or-above-20`;
  `scope-key-changed`.
- Budgeted mode: the number of real episodes is what DC-05 buys at the declared exchange rate. Below the
  calibration floor no band is issued; there is no smaller band for a smaller budget.

**The four clauses, as tests.**
1. *Regeneration.* DC-05: the committed experiment-table schema and the standard-normal lookup used for φ and Φ
   (a fixed rational table with interpolation rule, so that the ranking is bit-stable across hosts; DESIGN) and
   the stop-rule guard. DC-07: the conformal rank table, the scope-key schema and the band automaton (states
   `no-claim`, `calibrating`, `band`, `void`). All regenerate under `xtask check-generated`.
2. *Null calibration.* DC-05 (its "null" is the zero-value obligation): on fixtures where no outcome can change the
   choice (s̃ = 0; or a single finalist; or |m|/s ≥ 8), the value is exactly zero (the table rounds values below
   10⁻¹² of the objective span to zero by rule, and returns STOP); value is non-decreasing in s̃ and non-increasing
   in |m| on a 50 × 50 grid. DC-07: over 10⁴ calibration draws at n = 20, α = 0.10 with exchangeable synthetic
   residuals, coverage has mean 0.905 ± 0.003, standard deviation 0.063 ± 0.004, and falls below 0.80 with
   frequency 0.069 ± 0.008 (Beta(19, 2)); under exchangeability the martingale reaches 20 in at most 5% of 10⁴
   sequences (Ville's inequality).
3. *Forced fallbacks.* DC-05: a prediction ledger fixture in which 17 of 30 stated 80% intervals covered (57%)
   and one in which 29 of 30 covered (97%) each fire the trigger; the next suggestion follows the fixed order and
   the record names the trigger; 24 of 30 does not fire. DC-07: 18 calibration points; a planted shift in
   residuals (mean shift of two residual standard deviations from the 21st episode) that drives the martingale
   past 20; a firmware change in the scope key. Each yields `reality: no-claim` and a fallback record.
4. *Shadow records.* DC-05 in shadow: each suggestion records the value-ranked list and the fixed-order choice,
   and acts on the fixed order. DC-07 in shadow: each result records the band it would carry and prints
   `reality: no-claim`.

Data for DC-07 in this bead is `synthetic` and labelled so.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Artifacts of both cores regenerate bit-identically on both hosts.
- [ ] DC-05 table reproduces the six rows above: EVSI within 1% (0.00131, 0.00550, 1.02 × 10⁻⁵, 0.00843, 0.0251,
      0.00345), ratio of the first two per unit cost 6.4 ± 0.1, and the purchase order fast batch, then referee.
- [ ] Zero value: exactly 0 and STOP on the three no-change fixtures; at m = 0.15 the best ratio is below any
      budget price above 2 × 10⁻⁵ per GPU-second and the core stops.
- [ ] Monotonicity of the value in s̃ and in |m| on the grid.
- [ ] No DC-05 quantity appears in any verdict field of any record produced by the tests; a request to cite one
      as a verdict is refused with `FROB-REF-872`.
- [ ] DC-07 coverage statistics at n = 20, α = 0.10 within the stated tolerances; the martingale's false-alarm
      frequency at most 0.05 (within the binomial acceptance bound over 10⁴ sequences).
- [ ] Forced fallbacks: two coverage fixtures for DC-05 and three triggers for DC-07 give the fallback action and
      a record; the in-band fixture does not.
- [ ] Budgeted mode: with 65 candidates DC-05 evaluates the 64 with the largest prior variance and says so; with a
      real-episode budget that buys 18 calibration points DC-07 issues no band.
- [ ] Shadow records present for 100% of decisions; applied actions are the fallbacks'.
- [ ] The four clauses for DC-05 and DC-07 are registered as entries under GATE-ATEL-13 in the tier target
      `g1_known_answers` (the gate record's `tier`), with the negative controls below behind
      `gauntlet-mutations`; `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers
      --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-ATEL-13` passes with their measured values in the receipt and every
      control `caught` (DC-07's shadow clause entered `not-yet-due (M5)`). This bead writes no gate row: the M4
      part has one recorder, N28cp3-01. On calibration failure after one repair cycle the records are set to
      `fallback-only`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/cores_dc05.rs`: `evsi_closed_form_points` (the six rows); `variance_removed_formula`
(w²/(w + v) on (0.0009, 0.00045) → 0.0006); `zero_when_no_change`; `monotone_in_removed_variance`;
`monotone_in_mean_gap`; `stop_when_no_ratio_clears_price`; `budget_64_candidates`;
`coverage_trigger_bounds` (hits ≤ 19 or ≥ 28 of 30 fire; 20 to 27 do not); `fallback_fixed_order`
(check, simulate, train, referee); `phi_table_bit_stable` (lookup equal on both hosts).
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/cores_dc07.rs`: `band_rank_table` (n = 20, α = 0.10 → rank 19; n = 18, α = 0.05 →
none); `coverage_beta_19_2`; `martingale_false_alarm_rate`; `planted_shift_trips_alarm` (alarm within 15 episodes
of the shift in at least 95 of 100 seeded sequences); `scope_change_voids_band`; `no_band_below_floor`;
`one_sided_band_form` (the band reads real ≥ sim − q̂ and nothing about an upper side).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m4.sh`, phases `dc-05` and `dc-07` (the script is shared by the four M4 row beads;
each phase runs alone with `--phase dc-NN`; whichever row bead lands first creates it from the STD-E2E template):
`cargo xtask check-generated`; `frob cores show dc-05 --rows --json` and `frob cores show dc-07 --rows --json`;
`frob experiment suggest <two-finalist fixture> --budget gpu_s=600 --json` in shadow (the budget keys of
`experiment.suggest`'s row are `wall_s`, `gpu_s`, `cpu_core_s`), asserting the ranked list
(fast batch, then referee), the STOP entry on the m = 0.15 and no-change fixtures, and the fixed-order action
applied; `frob report campaign <fixture> --cite <proposal-id> --json` refused with `FROB-REF-872`; the coverage and
martingale harnesses (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- cores.dc07.coverage --draws
10000 --seed $FROB_E2E_SEED --json`, data labelled `synthetic`; the conformance crate has no binary,
ADR-GAUNT-01 of BD-025, and the studio's parameterized harnesses are subcommands of its example target
`atelier_harness`, which the first bead to need it creates and to which this bead adds `cores.dc07.coverage`);
the five forced-trigger fixtures. Log: `target/e2e-logs/decision_cores_m4/<utc>.ndjson` with
a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N28c-09`), `step` (`dc-05.*`, `dc-07.*`), `event`, the exact
command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim Known answer, class Tested,
determinism D0, seed); `metric` lines carry each EVSI against its expected value and 1% tolerance, the ratio
against 6.4 ± 0.1, the coverage mean, standard deviation and share below 0.80 against their tolerances, and the
false-alarm frequency against 0.05; `refusal` lines carry `FROB-REF-872` with pointer and repairs; `digest` fields
carry the artifact hashes and the suggestion list's digest; on failure the first differing row or decision with
the fixture path and seed; a `summary` line naming GATE-ATEL-13 (DC-05, DC-07). The phases write no gate row;
N28cp3-01's phase `gate` runs them as stages beside the tier run and records the M4 part.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The EVSI table above; Beta(19, 2) statistics (mean 0.905, standard deviation 0.063, 0.069 below 0.80).
- Negative controls that must fail: a chooser that ranks by expected information gain (it assigns positive value
  to the m = 0.15 fixture, where no outcome can change the choice); a band issued at 18 calibration points; a band
  kept after a scope-key change; a value-of-information figure written into a verdict's `statistic` field; a
  chooser that selects the real-episode conditions instead of only their number.
- Metamorphic relations: scaling the objective by a constant scales every EVSI by the same constant and leaves
  the ranking unchanged; permuting candidate order leaves the chosen experiment unchanged (ties broken by
  experiment identifier).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; both hosts; debug and release (D0 for tables; the suggestion list is
  byte-identical at a pinned ledger frontier).

## Considerations

- Whether value-of-information ranking beats a capable agent is unsupported in the published record (plausibly
  below 1.5 times), which is why DC-05 is a stop rule and a budget allocator and why KILL-VOI exists; this bead
  tests the rule's arithmetic and safety, not its benefit.
- DC-07's coverage moves by several points at small calibration sets; the tolerance bands above are for the
  rule's distribution, and a report must not present a band's nominal level as a guarantee for one draw.
- The standard-normal lookup is a DESIGN choice to keep rankings bit-stable; its error bound (below 10⁻⁷ in φ
  and Φ) is part of the table's contract.
