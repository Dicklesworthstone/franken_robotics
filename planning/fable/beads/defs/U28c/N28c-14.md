## Background

Registered predictions are the studio's cheapest honesty mechanism. Before a run, an agent may register a
prediction (`frob predict register --metric cot-elec --interval 0.42 0.50 --confidence 0.8 --for <planned-run>`).
It is hashed and frozen before the run starts and scored afterwards with a proper scoring rule, and
`frob predict report` shows each agent's calibration. The mechanism costs almost nothing and answers a question
nothing else does: whether an agent understands the robot it is designing or is searching blindly. The verbs and
the ledger are the seed bead for registered predictions (BD-344).

This bead makes the studio hold itself to the same rule. The plan requires:

- The studio registers its own predictions under the same rule: the intervals of `sim.estimate`, the outcome
  intervals of `experiment.suggest`, and the saving a race or a ladder expects.
- Every suggestion registers its prediction: the predicted outcome interval of the chosen experiment is registered
  before it runs. The calibration of those intervals is the fallback trigger of decision core DC-05 (the
  value-of-information rule that decides which experiment to buy next).
- A refit after scoring opens a new prediction version; the old one stays scored.

Why it matters: the value-of-information model is parametric (a Gaussian posterior with variance components read
from ledgers). It never issues a verdict, so a wrong model cannot make a false claim, but it can waste budget
indefinitely unless its predictions are scored. Scoring them is what turns "the model may be wrong" into a
measured trigger. The same applies to cost estimates and to the saving a race is expected to deliver (the plan
expects two to five times fewer evaluations when candidates are separated and nothing otherwise, a HYPOTHESIS
settled per campaign by the saving the race ledger records).

Rejected alternatives: scoring the studio's predictions in a separate internal log (two rules, and the studio
could be held to a weaker one); overwriting a prediction when the model is refitted (erases the evidence of
miscalibration). Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the studio predictor identities; the three automatic registration hooks; freezing before dispatch; prediction
versions and refits; accounting of unscored predictions; what `predict.report` shows for the studio.
Out: the verbs, the ledger table, the hash-and-freeze primitive and the scoring rule (BD-344); the monitor that
turns calibration into a hint or a trigger (N28c-15); the predictions' content models (the estimate model, the
value-of-information table, the race).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-opt/src/predict/studio.rs`.

1. **Predictor identity.** Each prediction names its predictor. Agents are identified by their session's agent
   identity. The studio's predictors are `studio:estimate`, `studio:suggest` and `studio:saving`, each with a
   `PredictionVersion { model: DottedName, version: u32, fitted_at_frontier: LedgerFrontier }`.
2. **Prediction record fields the studio fills** (the ledger schema belongs to BD-344): predictor; version; metric
   (a registered metric name with unit); interval [lo, hi]; confidence c ∈ (0, 1); target (the planned run, job,
   experiment or campaign identifier, bound to immutable identifiers); for a suggestion, one interval per
   mechanism block where mechanisms predict different outcomes; the hash of the canonical record; the ledger
   sequence number of registration.
3. **Hook 1: estimates.** When a verb is submitted as a job, the admission path already computes the
   `sim.estimate` interval for CPU core-seconds and GPU-seconds. In the same transaction that admits the job, and
   before dispatch, `studio:estimate` registers one prediction per resource with the estimate's interval and
   level (0.90 where the stratum is calibrated; the fixed factor-of-two interval is registered at confidence
   `uncalibrated` and is reported separately, never pooled into coverage).
4. **Hook 2: suggested experiments.** When `experiment.suggest` returns a ranked list, each proposal carries a
   predicted outcome interval at confidence 0.8 (DESIGN default, the level of the DC-05 trigger). The prediction
   is registered when the agent or the cascade *accepts* a proposal by submitting the experiment with
   `--from-proposal <id>`; the submission is refused with a conflict (exit 7) if the proposal's inputs have
   changed since it was issued (stale parent), and the prediction is frozen in the admission transaction.
   Proposals that are never bought are not scored and are counted as `not-bought`.
5. **Hook 3: expected savings.** At the start of a race or of a ladder campaign, `studio:saving` registers the
   expected saving: the ratio of evaluations (or GPU-seconds) the fixed baseline would spend to what the adaptive
   rule expects to spend to the same accepted decision, as an interval at confidence 0.8. The outcome is the
   ratio the race ledger or the campaign's phase ledger records at close, measured against the baseline replay on
   the same population and addresses.
6. **Freeze-before-outcome.** All three hooks register through the same primitive agents use; a registration whose
   target already has an outcome row is refused by that primitive (the seed bead's done-condition). The hooks
   register inside the admission transaction, so the ordering "prediction sequence number < first outcome
   sequence number" holds by construction and is asserted.
7. **Versions and refits.** When a model is refitted (a new estimate calibration stratum; a refit of the
   variance components of the value-of-information posterior; a change of the race's separation prior), the
   predictor's version increments. Predictions registered under the old version stay in the ledger and stay
   scored; calibration is reported per version; coverage windows used by monitors read the current version only
   and say how many scored predictions it has.
8. **Accounting.** For each predictor and version: registered = scored + pending + not-run + voided, each an
   integer. `not-run` covers a cancelled or refused target; `voided` covers a target whose identity changed (for
   example a job re-planned under a new kernel version). Nothing is silently dropped; a failed or losing
   experiment is scored like any other.
9. **Report.** `frob predict report --predictor studio:suggest --json` (and the default report's `studio`
   section) shows per version: counts by state, coverage of stated intervals with an exact binomial interval,
   the mean score under the ledger's proper scoring rule, and sharpness (median interval width relative to the
   metric's declared span).

Determinism: registration is part of the admission transaction (class D0 given the inputs); scoring is a pure
function of the prediction and the outcome row.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Submitting any job registers `studio:estimate` predictions for its resources before the first chunk of
      outcome is published (asserted by ledger sequence order on 100 jobs).
- [ ] Buying a proposal with `--from-proposal` registers the outcome prediction in the admission transaction; a
      proposal whose inputs changed is refused with exit 7 and nothing is registered.
- [ ] Starting a race and a ladder campaign each registers one `studio:saving` prediction; at close each is
      scored against the recorded ratio.
- [ ] After a refit, the predictor's version is incremented, old predictions remain and remain scored, and the
      report shows both versions separately.
- [ ] The accounting identity holds for each predictor and version on a fixture with cancelled, refused and
      re-planned targets.
- [ ] A studio prediction registered after its outcome exists is refused by the same primitive as an agent's
      (planted: the hook invoked after dispatch).
- [ ] `frob predict report --predictor studio:suggest --json` returns counts, coverage with its interval, mean
      score and sharpness; uncalibrated estimate intervals are reported apart and excluded from coverage.
- [ ] The studio's predictions are stored with result kind `advisory` and cannot be cited as verdicts.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/predict_studio.rs`:
- `estimate_registered_before_dispatch`;
- `proposal_prediction_frozen_at_admission`; `stale_proposal_conflict_exit_7`;
- `saving_prediction_scored_at_close`: fixture race with 9 candidates where the baseline replay spends 900
  evaluations and the race 300; predicted interval [2, 5]; outcome 3.0; hit;
- `inseparable_field_scores_a_miss`: fixture with identical candidates; outcome ratio 1.03; predicted [2, 5];
  miss, scored and kept;
- `refit_opens_new_version`: 12 predictions under version 1, refit, 5 under version 2; report shows 12 and 5;
- `accounting_identity`: 40 registered = 31 scored + 4 pending + 3 not-run + 2 voided;
- `uncalibrated_estimates_not_pooled`;
- `per_mechanism_intervals`: a probe proposal with two mechanism blocks registers two intervals; the outcome is
  scored against the union for coverage and against each block for the mechanism record;
- `late_registration_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/predict_calibration.sh` (N28c-18) and `scripts/e2e/experiment_suggest.sh` (N28c-16) exercise the
hooks through real verbs: submit a job and read its estimate prediction back with `frob predict report`; buy a
proposal; run a nine-candidate race. Logged for this bead: ledger sequence numbers of registration and first
outcome (assert the order), the prediction hashes as `digest` fields, counts by state, and coverage figures with
their thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer for the saving prediction's reference values (FACT about the simulation sibling's fixtures, to be
  re-read at the pinned commit): its racing battery asserts a saving of at least 2 on a separated field and below
  1.5 on an inseparable one, with 11.7 and 1.03 measured; the two fixtures above are modelled on those cases.
- Negative controls that must fail: a hook that registers after the first outcome; a refit that overwrites the
  old version's rows; a report that drops not-run predictions from the denominator; pooling uncalibrated
  intervals into coverage.
- Determinism lanes: registration hashes equal twice on one machine and across worker counts (records contain no
  wall-clock time in the hashed payload).

## Considerations

- Registering inside the admission transaction adds a ledger write to every job; it is one row and shares the
  transaction, so the latency cost is expected to be negligible, and is measured against the interactive budget.
- Confidence 0.8 for suggestion intervals is a DESIGN default tied to the DC-05 trigger; changing it is a new
  version of that decision policy.
- Privacy: predictions name metrics and identifiers, never private-scene payloads.
- If the estimate model moves to another owner's crate, the hook stays in the admission path and only the
  predictor's model name changes.
