## Background

The studio keeps a ledger of registered predictions: an interval and a confidence for a named metric, frozen
before the run it concerns and scored afterwards. Agents register predictions about their designs; the studio
registers its own (cost estimates, the outcome intervals of suggested experiments, the saving a race expects). A
calibration figure that nobody acts on is decoration. The plan puts calibration to work in exactly two places:

1. An agent whose 80% intervals cover far from 80% of the time is told so in `next_actions`, with the suggestion to
   buy evidence before the next edit.
2. The studio's own miscalibration is a fallback trigger of decision core DC-05 (the value-of-information rule
   that decides which experiment to buy next, or to stop): when coverage of its 80% intervals falls outside 65 to
   92% over 30 experiments, the core falls back to the fixed order check, simulate, train, referee.

Both rest on one small computation, done here once. The band is not arbitrary: with 30 scored predictions at a
true coverage of 0.8 the number of hits is Binomial(30, 0.8); "outside 65 to 92%" means at most 19 hits or at
least 28, which leaves at most 5% in each tail (2.6% below, 4.4% above). A perfectly calibrated predictor is
therefore flagged in about 7.0% of windows; that false-flag rate is stated to the reader and is the reason the
consequence of the flag is mild (a hint, or a fixed and safe fallback).

Why a trigger and not a correction: a parametric value model (a Gaussian posterior with variance components from
ledgers) beside distribution-free tests is safe only because it never issues a verdict (ADR-ATEL-07). Widening
its intervals automatically would hide a wrong variance model; falling back to a fixed order and refitting under
a new prediction version exposes it. Tier: Solid [S] for the monitor; DC-05 itself is Frontier.

## Scope

In: the coverage monitor; the band rule for any confidence level; the hint and where it appears; the DC-05 trigger
evaluation at a pinned ledger frontier; behaviour after a refit; the report fields.
Out: the prediction ledger, verbs and scoring rule (BD-344); the studio's registration hooks (N28c-14); the DC-05
table (BD-349) and its conformance row (N28c-09); the suggestion ranking (BD-351).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-opt/src/predict/monitor.rs`.

1. **Coverage window.** For a predictor (an agent, or a studio predictor at its current prediction version) and a
   confidence level c, the window is the most recent n = 30 scored predictions at that level, ordered by the
   ledger sequence number of scoring. `hits` is the count whose outcome fell inside the stated interval. Windows
   are evaluated at a pinned ledger frontier, so the result is a pure function of ledger state.
2. **Band rule (DESIGN, reproducing the plan's numbers at c = 0.8).** For level c and n = 30, the lower cut L is
   the largest h with P(Binomial(30, c) ≤ h) ≤ 0.05, and the upper cut U is the smallest h with
   P(Binomial(30, c) ≥ h) ≤ 0.05; the predictor is `out-of-band` when hits ≤ L or hits ≥ U. At c = 0.8: L = 19 and
   U = 28, which is the plan's "coverage outside 65 to 92% over 30". The cuts are computed in exact rational
   arithmetic and stored in a small committed table for c ∈ {0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95}; other levels are computed on
   demand by the same function. When no h satisfies a cut's condition the cut is absent and that side cannot
   flag: at c = 0.95 and n = 30, P(Binomial(30, 0.95) ≥ 30) = 0.215, so there is no upper cut (L = 25) and a
   predictor at that level is only ever `over-confident` or in band; the table records the absent cut as `none`. With fewer than 30 scored predictions at a level the status is `insufficient`,
   never `out-of-band`.
3. **Direction.** `over-confident` when hits ≤ L (intervals too narrow); `under-confident` when hits ≥ U
   (intervals too wide to be useful).
4. **The agent hint.** Every verb whose envelope has `next_actions` consults the monitor for the calling agent at
   level 0.8 (and at any other level the agent has at least 30 scored predictions for). When the status is
   `out-of-band`, one `next_actions` entry is added, ranked first among non-blocking suggestions:
   `{ "command": "frob experiment suggest <current design or comparison> --budget …",
      "why": "Your last 30 registered 80% intervals covered 17 (57%); the calibrated range is 20 to 27 of 30. Buy
      evidence before the next edit." }` The text states the hit count, the band, the direction, and that a
   calibrated predictor is flagged in about 7% of windows. The hint never blocks a verb and is not a refusal. It
   is suppressed for 10 subsequent calls after being shown, so it does not crowd out other actions (DESIGN).
5. **The DC-05 trigger.** `interval-coverage-out-of-band` is evaluated on the window of predictor
   `studio:suggest` at its current version, level 0.8, at the decision's pinned frontier. When out of band, DC-05's
   decision uses its fallback (the fixed order: check, simulate, train, referee), the decision record names the
   trigger with the hit count, and `experiment.suggest` output carries `ranking: "fixed-order (fallback:
   interval-coverage-out-of-band, 17 of 30)"`.
6. **After a refit.** A refit of the value model opens a new prediction version. Its window starts empty; while
   it holds fewer than 30 scored predictions, a core that was on the fallback because of this trigger stays on
   the fallback and keeps registering shadow predictions from the refitted model; when the new version's window
   is full and in band, the trigger clears. A core that has never fired acts on its table while its first window
   fills, in whatever adoption state its record gives.
7. **Report fields.** `frob predict report` gains, per predictor and level: `window { n, hits, band: [L + 1,
   U − 1], status, direction }`, plus the exact binomial interval for coverage.

Determinism class D0 (exact integer arithmetic on ledger rows at a frontier).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Band table: at c = 0.8, n = 30 the cuts are L = 19 and U = 28; tail probabilities 0.0256 and 0.0442 (to three
      significant figures); a predictor with 20 to 27 hits is in band.
- [ ] A fixture agent with 17 hits of 30 gets the hint in `next_actions` of its next verb call, with the counts,
      the band and the direction `over-confident`; with 29 of 30 the direction is `under-confident`; with 24 of 30
      no hint appears; with 29 scored predictions the status is `insufficient` and no hint appears.
- [ ] The hint never changes an exit code and never appears in a refusal.
- [ ] DC-05 trigger: with `studio:suggest` at 17 of 30 the next `experiment.suggest` follows the fixed order and
      the decision record names the trigger; at 24 of 30 it ranks by value.
- [ ] After a refit the window restarts; the core stays on fallback until 30 predictions of the new version are
      scored and in band (tested with 29, then 30).
- [ ] The monitor's result is identical for the same ledger frontier regardless of the order in which predictions
      were scored in wall-clock time (replay under 100 schedules).
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/predict_calibration.sh --phase monitor` passes at this bead's close, with its log.
- [ ] False-flag rate: over 10⁴ simulated calibrated predictors (true coverage 0.8) the flagged fraction is 0.070
      ± 0.008; detection: true coverage 0.5 is flagged in at least 93% of windows and 0.97 in at least 91%.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the phase `monitor` to `scripts/e2e/predict_calibration.sh` (owned by N28c-18, which depends on
this bead; the phase runs alone with `--phase monitor`), so the hint and the trigger are checked through the real
binary at this bead's close. The named snapshots (`agent-narrow-30`, `agent-ok-30`, `agent-29`, `agent-wide-30`,
`studio-suggest-17-of-30`, `studio-suggest-24-of-30`, labelled `synthetic`) are ledger snapshots under
`conformance/predict/`; a step that names one empties the run's state root (`FROB_STATE`, which `e2e_init`
exports) and restores the snapshot into it before `frob` is called (`frob` has no `--root` option and reads no
other state, STD-E2E's isolation rule; no verb takes a snapshot or fixture option; the registries of record are
read in place):
1. Registers and scores 30 predictions for a fixture agent whose intervals are deliberately too narrow (real
   `frob predict register` calls against `synthetic` outcome rows), then `frob design check quad-a --json` →
   the hint is first among non-blocking `next_actions`, with the hit count, the band "20 to 27 of 30", the
   direction and the 7% false-flag statement; the exit code equals that of the same command on the
   `agent-ok-30` snapshot (24 hits, no hint); `agent-29` (29 scored) → `insufficient`, no hint; `agent-wide-30`
   (29 hits) → `under-confident`; the next ten calls carry no hint, the eleventh does.
2. A refused verb on the narrow snapshot → the refusal envelope carries no hint.
3. `studio-suggest-17-of-30` (which holds the two finalists of N28c-16's `two-finalists` fixture) → `frob
   experiment suggest <d1> <d2> --budget gpu_s=600 --json` shows `ranking:
   "fixed-order (fallback: interval-coverage-out-of-band, 17 of 30)"` and the DC-05 decision record (read with
   `frob evidence trace`) names the trigger and the frontier; `studio-suggest-24-of-30` → ranked by value, or the
   shadow form that `frob cores show dc-05 --json` reports.
4. Refit: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- predict.refit --json` (N28c-14's
   subcommand of the example target of N28c-06), then 29 and then 30 scored in-band predictions of the new
   version → fallback held at 29, cleared at 30.
5. `frob predict report --json` → `window { n, hits, band: [20, 27], status, direction }` and the exact binomial
   interval for coverage, per predictor and level.
6. The same frontier replayed under 100 shuffled scoring schedules (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
   atelier_harness -- predict.window-replay --schedules 100 --json`, this bead's subcommand) → identical window
   and status.
Lines go to `target/e2e-logs/predict_calibration/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with `bead` `N28c-15`, `step`
`monitor.*`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric`
lines carry hits, band and status for each snapshot against the expected values, and the cuts L and U per
committed level; the `next_actions` entry verbatim; the DC-05 decision record identifier, its rule and the
trigger's hit count; `assert` lines comparing exit codes with and without the hint; on failure the snapshot path
and the first differing field; a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: L = 19, U = 28; false-flag rate 0.0698; detection 0.951 at true coverage 0.5, 0.709 at 0.6, 0.812
  at 0.95, 0.940 at 0.97, 0.997 at 0.99 (exact binomial sums; tests allow ± 0.01 for simulation).
- Negative controls that must fail: a monitor that uses all predictions instead of the last 30 (an agent that was
  bad long ago and is now calibrated stays flagged); a monitor that reads predictions of an old version after a
  refit; a trigger evaluated at the ledger tip instead of the pinned frontier (replay mismatch); a hint that
  blocks the verb.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts 1 and 36; both hosts.

## Considerations

- The band at 30 predictions has modest power against mild miscalibration (71% at a true coverage of 0.6); that
  is acceptable because the consequences are mild and because the fallback is safe. A larger window is a new
  version of the decision policy.
- The hint must stay advisory. An agent optimizing to silence it by registering very wide intervals is caught by
  the upper cut and by the sharpness figure in the report.
- Coverage is the trigger, the proper scoring rule is the report; the two can disagree (good coverage with
  useless width), and the report shows both.
- OPEN-ATEL-1 leaves DC-05 on its fixed order by default until the exchange rate is decided; the monitor still
  runs, in shadow, so that a calibration record exists when the core is considered for adoption.
