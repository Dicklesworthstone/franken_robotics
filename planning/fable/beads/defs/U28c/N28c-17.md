## Background

The design studio has a rule for choosing the next experiment by its value: decision core DC-05 values each
candidate experiment (more fast-tier pairs, a referee batch, a fine-tune, a bench measurement) by the expected
reduction in opportunity loss of the current choice per unit cost (for two finalists with posterior difference
Δ ~ N(m, s²), an experiment removing variance s̃² is worth EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)], z = |m|/s̃), buys in order
of value per cost, and stops when nothing clears the budget price. `experiment.suggest` exposes the ranking, and
the finalist stage of the evaluation ladder (layer 3) uses it. The mechanism is Frontier, and the published record
does not support a large benefit against a capable agent: information-gain selection is plausibly below 1.5 times;
a language-model tuner beat random search more often than Gaussian-process optimization at 10 and at 30
evaluations, and model-based search lost to a genetic algorithm on a robot co-design benchmark (FACT from the
source register, to be re-read before a gate cites it). The plan therefore treats the rule as a stop rule and a
budget allocator, designs it so that being wrong is cheap, and names a kill gate in advance. Decision ADR-ATEL-07:
parametric models rank experiments; distribution-free tests issue verdicts. The value-of-information model never
issues a verdict, so a wrong model costs efficiency and never validity, and removing its ranking costs efficiency
and nothing else.

KILL-VOI. Trigger: GATE-ATEL-15 fails. GATE-ATEL-15 (claim kind: Design comparison): in the offline replay, the
knowledge-gradient chooser is at least 1.5 times better than the agent chooser in evaluations to a true top-3; and
the value is exactly zero on a fixture where no outcome can change the choice. Fallback, named in advance: value
of information is a stop rule only; `experiment.suggest` lists proposals unranked by value. Decided at M4 and
recorded as an ADR. A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; a
trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs. A kill
gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan. This bead is that decision. It
implements nothing. It does not overlap with KILL-AUGUR-PROBE, which governs the probe generator (judged against
a programmed expert and the direct measurement) and is decided by the mission-query work; a fired gate here says
nothing about the probe generator.

This is the only bead that decides KILL-VOI (the keys N28a-26 and N28b-21 were merged into it).

## Scope

In: the decision protocol; how the measurement is read; who decides; the ADR; the registry state of DC-05; the
downstream changes on each branch; the fail-closed posture until the decision.
Out: building the logs (BD-310 at M3 on an incumbent engine or `batch-cpu`; at M4 the log of our own engine's
ladder with our own scorers, produced with BD-351 and the fine-tuning rungs of BD-321); the replay harness, its
choosers and its runs at M3 and M4 (N28b-18); the ranking and the verb (BD-351); the DC-05 table (BD-349); the
zero-value clause through the verb (N28c-16); the ladder's own kill gate KILL-LADDER (N28b-20), which is decided
on the same log by a different comparison (layer 2 against halving's audited top-k recall at fewer GPU-seconds).
The mechanism being measured does not depend on this decision; this decision depends on the measurement.

## Technical approach

1. **The experiment being read** (protocol frozen before the log is collected, registered in the pilot register).
   Log once: 64 designs × 3 rungs × 32 world addresses (about one GPU-day, TARGET). Replay four choosers over the
   log with no further simulation: (a) an agent reading the log so far through the studio's read verbs; (b) the
   knowledge-gradient chooser of DC-05; (c) e-race elimination; (d) uniform allocation. Metric: evaluations until
   a true top-3 design is identified at 95% confidence, over 100 replays. "True top-3" is defined from the full
   log at the highest rung, before any replay.
   - *Two readings.* The pilot gives a first reading at M3 on a log from an incumbent engine or `batch-cpu`. The
     gate value is taken at M4 from the same harness re-run on a log produced by our own engine with our own
     scorers (family policy with no adaptation, fine-tune at 5%, specialist). Only the M4 reading decides.
   - *Context read beside them.* The paper-scale pilot of the value-of-information construction (thirty
     registered experiments on the mission slice, scored for interval coverage, with the probe comparator) is on
     record at M3; its record is cited in the ADR as evidence about the value model's calibration and does not
     enter the trigger.
2. **Form of the comparison** (the method-experiment form every such gate has: competing arms, a potency control,
   a paired lower bound, a pre-declared fallback, a ceiling).
   - Pairing: replay i of every chooser uses the same permutation seed of addresses; the quantity is the paired
     ratio rᵢ = evaluations(agent)ᵢ / evaluations(knowledge-gradient)ᵢ.
   - Decision statistic: the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on the pooled ratio of means over the 100 paired
     replays (computed by the performance harness's paired lower-bound routine; ten pairs of constant ratio r
     must return the bound r). Pass when the bound is at least 1.5. A replay in which a chooser never identifies
     a top-3 design within the log is right-censored at the log size and counted in a restricted mean.
   - Settled reading (DESIGN). The plan says "at least 1.5 times better" without naming the statistic. The rule of
     method experiments is that a kill rule reads a bound and not a point estimate, so the bound is compared
     with 1.5. The point ratio of restricted means, the ratio of medians with its interval, and the same ratios
     against e-race elimination and uniform allocation are recorded beside it and decide nothing. A point ratio
     at or above 1.5 whose bound is below 1.5 does not pass.
   - Evaluator calibration: the identified design must be in the true top 3 in at least 95% of uncensored
     replays, and identification on a null log (no design separated) must occur in at most 5% of replays. If
     either fails the reading is void: the harness is repaired and the replay re-run once before any ruling.
   - Potency control: a planted log in which one rung's noise is 10 times smaller than recorded for half the
     designs; the knowledge-gradient chooser must beat uniform allocation by at least 2 times at the lower bound
     (the harness can see an effect). A/A control: agent arm against itself with different session seeds must not
     clear 1.02.
   - Ceiling, written before the run: the best possible chooser (an oracle that knows the true top-3) needs at
     least 3 × (evaluations needed to confirm one design at 95%); the ratio agent/oracle bounds what any chooser
     can gain. If that ceiling is below 1.5, the gate cannot pass and the fallback is adopted without the run.
   - Agent arm disclosure: model and version, prompt, tool list, token and turn budget, transcripts, and the
     number of sessions that failed to complete, all recorded; the agent arm is given the same information the
     chooser has.
3. **Zero-value clause.** Read from GATE-ATEL-15's record, which N28c-16 (the gate's one recorder, after both
   clauses) enters from the receipt of the gate's tier run: value exactly zero on the fixtures where no outcome
   can change the choice. If this clause fails, the gate fails
   regardless of the ratio (the rule would buy experiments that cannot matter), and the defect is filed as a
   correctness bug in the value function: a non-zero value where nothing can change is wrong whatever the ratio.
4. **Decision.** Taken by the Atelier lead; reviewed by a fresh-context session that did not implement DC-05 and
   did not run the measurement, and that recomputes the bound from the purchase logs before the ruling is filed.

   | Evidence | Ruling | Consequence |
   |---|---|---|
   | Bound at least 1.5, evaluator calibrated, zero-value fixture exact | Not fired | DC-05 may move from `shadow` to `canary` and then `default` under the adoption rules, no further than `canary` before the core-level gate row (GATE-ATEL-13, DC-05) exists; `experiment.suggest` ranks by value per cost; the gate row records the bound |
   | Bound below 1.5, or the zero-value fixture not exact | One repair cycle is allowed only for an implementation defect found by the controls (not for retuning the value model on the replay log); then, if still failing, KILL-VOI fires: the KILL-VOI row's `decision.outcome = "fired"`, and GATE-ATEL-15 takes status `killed` with `detail = "KILL-VOI: value of information is a stop rule only"` (N40-03's propagation) | Fallback below |
   | Evaluator miscalibrated | Reading void; repair the harness and re-run once before ruling | — |
5. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `registries/cores/dc-05.toml`: `status = "fallback-only"` for ordering, with the kill gate `KILL-VOI` and the
     decision record's identifier; the stop rule (stop when no admissible experiment can change the choice or
     none clears the price, and spend nothing past that point) stays in force, because the fallback keeps value
     of information as a stop rule.
   - `experiment.suggest`: proposals are listed unranked by value (ordered by the fixed order check, simulate,
     train, referee, then measurements, then by cost), which is DC-05's deterministic fallback; each still
     carries its cost, testable prediction, model class and, for measurements, the direct-measurement comparator,
     and the list still ends with STOP when nothing clears the price. The `ratio` field is omitted and the
     envelope says `ranking: "unranked (KILL-VOI)"`.
   - The ladder's layer 3 (finalists, at most 64): the handover stays one-way; after it, allocation follows the
     fixed order of experiments until STOP or budget; every verdict still comes from the promotion rule and the
     comparison rules, as before.
   - The plan's benefit claim for experiment choice is withdrawn from the ten-fold accounting (any factor
     attributed to experiment ranking is dropped; the stopping saving remains under the comparison and racing
     rules); the cost estimate drops any saving attributed to value-ordered experiments; the phase ledger records
     that the lever was not available.
   - Beads that exist only on the surviving branch (adoption of DC-05 beyond shadow, that is the canary and
     default rollout of the ranking; any later refinement of the value model) depend on this decision and are
     closed as not built.
   - Unaffected: registered predictions and their calibration monitor, which keeps running because prediction
     intervals are still issued (the calibration trigger of DC-05, coverage of 80% intervals outside 65 to 92%
     over 30 experiments, remains a second, independent route to the same fallback); measurement proposals with
     comparators; the advisory type wall.
6. **Invariant that holds on both branches.** A value-of-information quantity never appears in a verdict field
   (INV-ATEL-12); citing one as a verdict is refused with `FROB-REF-872` (repair: run the comparison; exit 3).
7. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** DC-05 is in `shadow`: it records its ranking and the fixed order acts.
   No report states a saving from value-of-information ranking.
8. **Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-VOI-M4.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`;
   the required fields are N40-03's: the kill gate, the milestone, the trigger as registered and the amendments
   applied, each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, the outcome, the fallback in
   force, the beads re-read, what changes downstream, and the recording session) with the protocol hash, the log
   identity, commit and seeds, the M3 pilot reading and the M4 gate reading, the bound, the point ratio and the
   four choosers' figures, both controls, the ceiling, the zero-value result, the decision, the registry diff for
   DC-05 and the downstream list; a ledger evidence record of the same; the decision entry of the KILL-VOI row of
   `registries/gates.toml`, `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` (N40-03), citing the receipt
   hashes of GATE-ATEL-15's record; written by a session that did not implement DC-05. This bead writes nothing
   in GATE-ATEL-15's record (its one recorder is N28c-16; a fired outcome makes its status `killed` through
   N40-03's propagation). A decision recorded before the gate row's ledger sequence is rejected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol (arms, pairing, statistic, controls, ceiling, censoring rule, agent-arm disclosure) is
      registered with its hash before the replay runs; the registration's ledger sequence number precedes the
      first replay's.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-15's record at M4 (entered by N28c-16, its one recorder) holds measured values from a log
      produced by our own engine, and this bead reads them: the lower bound on the ratio, the point ratio, the
      number of replays, the censored shares, the potency-control result, the A/A result and the zero-value
      result.
- [ ] The ADR exists at M4, cites those values and the M3 pilot's, has the trigger evaluated against its number
      (bound below 1.5, or a non-zero value on a no-change fixture, fires), and the KILL-VOI row records the
      decision: `decision.outcome = "not-fired"` (with GATE-ATEL-15 at `pass`), or `"fired"` (with GATE-ATEL-15
      `killed`, detail naming the fallback). The ADR is at `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-VOI-M4.md` with N40-03's required
      fields. The reviewing session recomputed the bound.
- [ ] The owner declaration of KILL-VOI (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`, carrying the ledger's
      text), which N28c-25 leaves to the deciding bead, is written by this bead and agrees with the ledger row
      (N40-30's comparison passes).
- [ ] The gate register and the DC-05 record carry the decision and adoption status; `cores.show` prints it.
- [ ] If fired: DC-05's record is `fallback-only`; `frob experiment suggest … --json` has no `ratio` field,
      lists the fixed order with each proposal's prediction and direct-measurement comparator, and still emits
      STOP (test); the finalist layer orders work by the fixed order and still stops when no outcome can change
      the choice; the dependent beads on the losing branch are closed with a typed reason. (The cascade's report
      naming KILL-VOI among the fallbacks that fired is checked by the cascade bead BD-352, which reads the DC-05
      record; it is not a prerequisite of this decision.)
- [ ] If not fired: the adoption record for DC-05 cites this decision, and adoption goes no further than `canary`
      before the core-level gate row exists.
- [ ] On both branches: no value-of-information quantity in any verdict field of the seeded campaign's results;
      `FROB-REF-872` fires on a planted citation.
- [ ] Measurement sanity: the paired-bound routine returns r on ten pairs of constant ratio r; the potency
      control is detected; the A/A arm stays below 1.02.
- [ ] No amendment to the trigger, threshold or fallback is made after the log exists; a decision recorded
      before the gate row's ledger sequence is rejected.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_voi.rs`: `paired_bound_constant_ratio` (ten pairs at r = 1.7 → bound 1.7);
`censoring_restricted_mean` (a replay that never identifies is counted at the log size, 6,144 evaluations);
`decision_rule_table` (bound 1.49 fires; 1.50 does not; a point ratio of 1.8 with bound 1.2 fires; a zero-value
fixture returning 10⁻⁴ fires at any bound); `ceiling_below_threshold_adopts_fallback`;
`protocol_hash_precedes_replay` (ordering check on a fixture ledger).

`cargo test -p frob-opt --test kill_voi_switch`: `fallback_lists_unranked_in_fixed_order` (four proposals come
back as check, simulate, train, referee regardless of their computed values, with no `ratio` field);
`stop_rule_survives_fallback` (a fixture where no outcome can change the choice returns STOP under both states);
`finalist_layer_fixed_order_when_fired`; `ranked_when_not_fired` (value per cost descending);
`voi_never_in_verdict_872`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_voi.sh` sources the STD-E2E helper. Part one (the record): it reads the replay records and the
gate row from the ledger, recomputes the paired lower bound, the point ratio and the controls with
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- atelier.voi-replay --json` (this bead's subcommand of
the example target of N28c-06, a thin parser over N28b-18's replay library), checks the zero-value clause in
GATE-ATEL-15's record (entered by N28c-16 from its receipt), applies the rule table and compares the result with the ADR and the registry. Part two (the switch): under each
registry state for DC-05 (set in a scratch copy of the registry) it runs
`frob experiment suggest <finalists> --budget gpu_s=600 --json` on the three-finalist fixture of the reduced
seeded population, then the finalist loop of BD-351 from the user's side (run the first proposal through its
`command` with a `--key` of its own, re-suggest, until STOP); it asserts the ordering rule in force, the STOP on
the fixed fixture, that each round orders work and never writes a verdict, and the absence of value quantities
in verdict fields. Log
`target/e2e-logs/kill_voi/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time, evidence blocks, per chooser
the restricted mean evaluations, the paired ratio and its lower bound against 1.5, the control results, the
ceiling, the protocol and log digests, the proposal order with values and costs per registry state, the decision
and the ADR path, and on failure the first proposal out of order for the state in force. A final `summary` with
the trigger evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-VOI", "GATE-ATEL-15"]`. It exits zero when the measurement is complete
and the switch behaves, whatever the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer for the harness: constant-ratio pairs return the ratio; the log size is 64 × 3 × 32 = 6,144
  evaluations.
- The worked example of DC-05 (illustrative): with m = 0.03 and s² = 0.03² + 0.04², 200 more fast-tier pairs are
  worth 0.0013 for 1 GPU-second and 8 referee pairs 0.0055 for 27; the fast tier wins sixfold per unit cost; a
  second fast batch is worth about 10⁻⁵, so the referee goes next; at m ≥ 0.15 no experiment can change the
  choice. Under the "not fired" state the suggestion order must follow these values; under the fallback it must
  not depend on them.
- Negative controls: the potency log (must be detected); the A/A arm (must not clear 1.02); a replay in which the
  knowledge-gradient chooser is given the true ranking (a leak) must be rejected by the check that the chooser's
  inputs are a prefix of the log; a register row with 1.5 in place of the measured bound is rejected; a
  suggestion list that hides the direct-measurement comparator (the list must always carry it, on either
  branch; the counterexample is a probe that was right in 675 of 675 in-class cases and reversed all 225
  held-out decisions, while direct measurement was right in 900 of 900).
- Determinism: chooser arms (b) to (d) are pure functions of the log and the permutation seed (identical across
  runs); the agent arm is recorded, not regenerated, when the decision is re-audited; the order in force is a
  function of the registry version recorded on each decision.

## Considerations

- The plan expects this gate to be close or to fire. That is acceptable by design: the fallback is free, and the
  honest statement "agents order experiments about as well as the closed form does" is itself a finding about
  where the studio's value lies.
- The agent arm's strength matters: a weak agent would let the gate pass for the wrong reason, and a stronger
  agent raises the bar. The arm uses the same model family and tool surface as the studio's benchmark agents,
  with the configuration recorded; the decision is tied to the agent and version recorded in the replay, and a
  re-run with a different agent is a new registration, not a correction of this ruling.
- The measurement is on one logged population of 64 designs; the decision is scoped to that evidence, and the ADR
  says so. New evidence reopens the question only through the rejected-methods procedure (a new pilot and an
  ADR).
- OPEN-ATEL-1 (the exchange rate between GPU-seconds and real experiment cost) affects only rankings that mix
  simulation with bench work; the replay uses simulation cells only, so the gate does not wait on it.
- Related risk: structure helping agents only slightly (the surface A/B) is a different question with its own
  gate; this decision is about the chooser, not the surface.
- Cost: the replay needs no simulation; the agent arm costs 100 sessions.
