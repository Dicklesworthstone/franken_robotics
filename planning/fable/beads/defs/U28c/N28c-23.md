## Background

A proof obligation is a statement that a mechanism's guarantee rests on, together with the way it is discharged:
a written derivation, an exact enumeration, a simulation with a stated acceptance bound, or a combination. The
design studio (Atelier) states ten, PO-ATEL-01 to PO-ATEL-10. They are what stands between the studio's decision
rules and the claim that those rules control error: a promotion table that keeps wrong promotion below its level
under optional stopping; a fixed-n rule with at least 95% simultaneous coverage; conformal coverage with its Beta
spread; a race that rarely eliminates the true best; a greedy certificate; polarity rules for reuse; memoized
evaluation equal to full recomputation; a bisect with bounded error; scheduling that does not change evidence; a
risk bound for solver effort.

Each obligation is discharged by the bead that owns its mechanism. This bead keeps the ledger: it writes the
derivations that are owed as documents, fixes the acceptance bound of each simulation, records for each obligation
the run that discharged it, and fails if any is undischarged at the milestone where its gate is due. It is the
bead that carries the proof-obligation table of the chapter. Gate GATE-ATEL-13 names six of them explicitly: each
decision core's "null calibration holds (PO-ATEL-01 to -05, -10)". Tier: Solid [S] for the ledger; the obligations
of Frontier mechanisms are discharged or the mechanism stays on its fallback.

Design reason for written derivations beside simulations: a simulation shows a bound holds on the cases run; the
derivation says why it must hold and under which assumptions, and the assumptions are exactly what a later change
can silently break (an envelope tuned after outcomes; an audit set that is not exchangeable; a predicate that is
not monotone).

## Scope

In: `docs/proofs/atelier/PO-ATEL-NN.md` for the six obligations that need a written derivation; the acceptance

**Derivations to write (each a short document with statement, assumptions, proof, and what breaks it).**

1. *PO-ATEL-01.* After n audited pairs with k exceedances, E⁺ₙ = ((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁))ⁿ⁻ᵏ·(p₀/p₁)ᵏ. Given the past, if
   the next pair exceeds with probability p, the factor has expectation
   g(p) = (1 − p)(1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁) + p·p₀/p₁, which is linear in p, equals 1 at p = p₁, and has slope
   p₀/p₁ − (1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁) < 0 because p₀ < p₁. So g(p) ≤ 1 for every p ≥ p₁: E⁺ is a nonnegative supermartingale
   under the null "rate at least p₁", read conditionally, and by Ville's inequality the probability that it ever
   reaches 1/α⁺ is at most α⁺. The mirror argument for E⁻ under "rate at most p₀". Assumptions: episodes chosen by
   address before outcomes exist; the envelope frozen and hashed first. Independence is not used. What breaks it:
   an envelope chosen after outcomes; addresses chosen after seeing fast-tier results in a way that depends on
   referee outcomes.
2. *PO-ATEL-02.* The rule: n pairs; b = pairs where the candidate succeeds and the baseline fails; a = the
   reverse; effect (b − a)/n; exact Clopper–Pearson intervals [L_b, U_b] and [L_a, U_a] on the two discordant
   probabilities at 97.5% two-sided each. Each interval misses its parameter with probability at most 2.5%, so
   both hold with probability at least 95% (Bonferroni), and then the true effect lies in [L_b − U_a, U_b − L_a].
   Verdicts: promote if the lower endpoint exceeds the margin and hard feasibility holds; practical tie if the
   interval lies inside ±margin; inferior if the upper endpoint is below −margin; otherwise inconclusive.
   Reachability is a finite enumeration over all (a, b) with a + b ≤ n: C(n + 2, 2) pairs (C(66, 2) = 2,145 at
   n = 64; C(88, 2) = 3,828 at n = 86). With perfect concordance the interval is ±(1 − 0.0125^(1/n)): 0.0662 at
   n = 64, so no tie at a five-point margin; 0.04968 at n = 86, the first n below 0.05. Promotion with a = 0 needs
   b ≥ 15 at n = 64. Exact curtailment: a fixed-n study may stop early only when every admissible completion of
   the unresolved labels yields the same verdict.
3. *PO-ATEL-04.* For candidate i, the elimination evidence is Mᵢ = the average over original opponents j of the
   pairwise e-process testing "j is no better than i". If i is truly best, each pairwise process is a nonnegative
   supermartingale started at 1, so is their average (rounds are the only clock and every survivor consumes one
   observation per round in index order, so all are adapted to one filtration), and by Ville's inequality Mᵢ
   reaches 1/α with probability at most α. An implementation that eliminates on any single pairwise crossing must
   run each comparison at α/(N − 1) by the union bound.
4. *PO-ATEL-05.* Let f be monotone and submodular, S the greedy set and T any set of k scenes. Then
   f(T) ≤ f(S ∪ T) ≤ f(S) + Σ over t in T∖S of [f(S ∪ {t}) − f(S)] ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k largest marginal gains
   with respect to S. Hence OPT_k ≤ f(S) + that sum. Assumptions: weights declared in advance, nonnegative,
   integer; gains recomputed against the final S (a stale gain from an earlier iteration is larger and gives a
   looser but still valid bound only if it was computed against a subset of S; a gain from a different matrix is
   invalid, which is the negative control).
5. *PO-ATEL-06.* One paragraph per polarity rule in the form "if component X changes in direction D and nothing
   else in the obligation's dependency support changes, then a result of kind K is preserved because …". Example:
   disturbance set narrowed, inclusion result: the witness strategy succeeds against every disturbance of the
   old set, hence against every disturbance of the subset; preserved with the same witness. Example: catalog part
   added, exclusion or optimum: a new part can create a cheaper feasible rival; recheck. Each rule's document
   lists its side conditions (for example "actuator limits relaxed: inclusion preserved if the old command stays
   realizable and the plant still realizes the old dynamics") and the pure-change requirement: a rule applies
   only to a pure change in its direction, and joint edits need disjoint dependency supports.
6. *PO-ATEL-08.* A path of L edits is searched with at most ⌈log₂ L⌉ probes, each an e-process at level
   α/⌈log₂ L⌉; by the union bound the probability that any probe decides wrongly is at most α. The result is a
   pair of adjacent versions with different decided outcomes (`a-change`), which is a true statement whenever no
   probe erred; `first-change` is issued only after every earlier version has a decided outcome or the caller has
   declared the predicate monotone (recorded as an assumption), so it cannot be issued wrongly by the search
   itself.

**Ledger.** `registries/obligations.toml` gains one row per obligation: `id`, `statement`, `mechanism`,
`derivation` (path or `none`), `discharge` (test names and acceptance bound), `due` (milestones), `status`
(`open`, `discharged`, `failed`), `claim_ids` (of the discharging runs), `recorded_by`. The conformance crate's
check `obligations_atelier` fails when: an obligation due at the current milestone is not `discharged`; a
`discharged` row has no resolvable claim identifier; a derivation path is missing; a core record names an
obligation not in the ledger.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Six derivation documents exist (PO-ATEL-01, -02, -04, -05, -06, -08), each with statement, assumptions,
      proof and "what breaks it"; PO-ATEL-06 has one section per polarity rule.
- [ ] `registries/obligations.toml` has the ten rows with acceptance bounds as tabulated.
- [ ] Each obligation's discharge is recorded with claim identifiers from the run of its owner's test, at or
      before its due milestone: PO-ATEL-02 and the analysis part of -07 at M1; the episode part of -07 at M2;
      -01 and -05 at M3; the rest at M4.
- [ ] The exact numbers recompute: C(66, 2) = 2,145; C(88, 2) = 3,828; 1 − 0.0125^(1/64) = 0.0662;
      1 − 0.0125^(1/86) = 0.0497 and 1 − 0.0125^(1/85) = 0.0503; the binomial bounds 132 of 10⁴ and 42 of 512; the
      Beta(38, 2) 5th percentile 0.8840.
- [ ] The slope argument of PO-ATEL-01 is checked numerically on a grid: g(p) ≤ 1 for p in [p₁, 1] and g(p₁) = 1,
      for the default parameters and for 100 random (p₀, p₁) pairs.
- [ ] The ledger check fails on each planted defect: a due obligation left `open`; a `discharged` row with a
      dangling claim identifier; a core record citing `PO-ATEL-11`.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-13's calibration clause is recorded as satisfied only when PO-ATEL-01 to -05 and -10 are
      `discharged`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_obligations.sh`: runs `frob-conformance report obligations --chapter 28 --json`, asserts all
rows due at the current milestone are `discharged`, traces each row's claim identifiers with `frob evidence
trace`, and re-runs the two cheapest discharges live (the PO-ATEL-02 enumeration at n = 64 and 86 through
`frob eval preanalyze --n 64 --margin 0.05 --json`, and the PO-ATEL-05 recomputation through `frob nemesis cover`
on the worked instance). Structured log via the STD-E2E helper under `target/e2e-logs/atelier_obligations/`:
per obligation a `metric` line with status, bound and measured value; `assert` lines for resolved claims; on
failure the obligation identifier and the missing artifact; a `summary` naming GATE-ATEL-13.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-693 (fixed-n rule at n = 64: 2,145 pairs; 521, 521, 1,103, 0; first tie at n = 86); KA-694 (zero
  discordances in 64 pairs: one-sided 95% bound 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.04573); KA-695; KA-696; KA-704.
- Negative controls: an envelope changed after the first outcome (PO-ATEL-01's assumption; must void the bound
  in the null runs); a certificate from stale gains of another matrix (PO-ATEL-05); a study that declares "tie"
  reachable at n = 64 (PO-ATEL-02); a `first-change` at the last edit of pass, fail, pass, pass, pass, fail
  (PO-ATEL-08); a memo key without delivery time (PO-ATEL-07).
- Determinism lanes: enumerations in exact arithmetic are identical on both hosts; simulations are seed-explicit
  and repeated twice.

## Considerations

- Simulated null runs test an error rate at a test level; a bound of "at most 132 of 10⁴" will be exceeded by a
  correct implementation about once in a thousand runs. A failure is re-run once with a new seed before it is
  treated as a defect, and both runs are recorded.
- PO-ATEL-02's coverage is conservative (Bonferroni over two exact intervals); the derivation says so, and no
  tighter claim is made.
- PO-ATEL-06 is the obligation most exposed to scope creep: a new polarity rule is a new row with its own
  derivation and fixtures before it can license any reuse, and a rule that licenses one false reuse is disabled
  until repaired.
- If a theorem card later proves PO-ATEL-01 or -05 in a proof checker, the ledger row gains the certificate
  reference and keeps the simulation as a regression.
