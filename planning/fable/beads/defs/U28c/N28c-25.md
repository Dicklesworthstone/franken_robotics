## Background

A gate is a recorded measurement against a threshold. The program's rules are strict: a gate passes only with a
record in `registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and that
resolves in the artifact graph; a gate with no record is `not run`, which is not a pass; the plan's statuses are
`not run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩`, to which the register
format (N40-01) adds `no-claim` and `superseded`; every gate names exactly one of seven
claim kinds; every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches; where a chapter and the
register differ, the register governs on identity, claim, earliest milestone and every number it states. A kill
gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance, is never quietly re-scoped, and its decision is recorded as an
ADR at the milestone named; a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

The design studio (Atelier) defines nineteen convergence gates, GATE-ATEL-01 to GATE-ATEL-19, and appears in
eight kill gates. Each gate is measured by the bead that owns its mechanism. The record of each is created by
the register bead that owns it (Scope names each). This bead supplies the studio's side of those records before
any of them is measured: the full threshold of each gate, its controls and its owners, and the chapter's
statement of its parts by milestone. It adds the check that the studio's method gates have the form the chapter
requires: competing arms, a potency control that the method must catch and a clean case it must pass, a paired
lower bound, and a fallback named in advance. It measures nothing itself (its `gates` field is empty on
purpose); it is what lets the milestone exit reviews ask "is every part due here recorded, with a caught
control?" mechanically. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

This bead is the Atelier chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the nineteen gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as
  the defining statement); control attachments, in the `controls` field of the records that N40-02 and N40-10
  created, and the `tier` field; the chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its per-milestone parts and
  its measuring beads; the owner's claim reading of GATE-ATEL-19.
- For the eight kill gates: the map from each to its owner and deciding bead, and the check that each has its
  ledger record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The method-gate form check and the milestone schedule check.
- The `gates` subcommand of the example target `registry_report` (the report the end-to-end script reads).

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-ATEL-01 to -16 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-ATEL-17 to -19: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode, owner, consumers and what each trigger reads:
  the fallback ledger. N40-26 has KILL-STATIC, KILL-RANK, KILL-LADDER, KILL-VOI, KILL-REUSE and KILL-ATLAS;
  N40-29 has KILL-BUDGETCURVE; N40-24 has KILL-FDGRAD, which the family-batch subsystem owns.
- Measuring any gate (the beads named in the tables); deciding any kill gate (their decision beads); the
  register's generated tables (the conformance crate's generator).

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-ATEL-01 to -16 are
created by N40-02 and those of GATE-ATEL-17 to -19 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`,
`kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The kill records are created by the fallback ledger (N40-26,
N40-29, N40-24) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, owner, consumers and `reads`; their amendments,
dated and each made before the measurement it governs, and their decision ADRs are records of N40-03. This bead
creates no record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers of the negative controls registered in
   `frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`, and the tiers the gate is measured on.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` (a list of `{ milestone, scope }`) and
   `threshold_numbers`, in the `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection
   (`registries/gate_declarations.toml` until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The
   declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint
   does not compare: `criterion`, the full threshold of the table above, of which the record's `threshold` is a
   brief; `measured_by`, the bead keys; `recorded_by` per part, the one bead whose session enters that part with
   `record_gate` (the program's conventions, item 9: one recorder per gate part, after every clause's
   measurement; scripts produce receipts and never write rows); and `kill`, the kill gate decided on the gate,
   if any. The recorder of each part is the bead that states in its own text that it records that part; where
   the table above names one it is that bead, and for the parts recorded outside this unit (GATE-ATEL-01 to -12)
   the declaration takes the recorder each owner names (for example BD-110 and BD-332 for the two parts of -02,
   BD-262 and N28b-03 for the two parts of -04, N28a-29 and BD-348 for the two parts of -05, N28a-18 for -06,
   N28b-19 for -08 at M4, N28b-35 for -11 at M4, N28b-40 and N28b-42 for the two parts of -12). A bead that
   measures a clause of a part and is not its recorder (BD-108 and BD-124 at M1, for instance) writes no row.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its owner and deciding bead as data
   of its checks. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is
   written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30. For
   KILL-FDGRAD the studio's declaration has `role = "consumer"`.

The Claim, Parts by milestone, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns above are the chapter's statement of fields
the register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10 and the ledger when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree. Where a trigger or fallback above spells out what the ledger names by identifier (the first policy
rung R1; the pilot of the regime atlas; the ten-fold accounting), the ledger's text is the one a declaration
carries. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of
N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`,
`pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`); the record of a part is its `commit`, `command`,
`receipt`, `claim_id`, `measured`, `evidence` and `recorded` fields. A kill decision's outcome is `not-fired`,
`fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03).

**The method-gate form check.** GATE-ATEL-01, -03, -07, -08, -15 and -18 are method gates (claim kind Design
comparison). For each, before its first run, the registry must hold: (1) the competing arms, including the
strongest conventional alternative at equal resources and the mechanism's own fallback; (2) a potency control (a
planted effect the experiment must detect) and a clean case or A/A arm it must pass; (3) the paired lower bound
as the decision statistic, never a ratio of means; (4) the pre-declared fallback with its kill gate; (5) the
ceiling, computed before the run; (6) for a fixed-n study the reachability pre-analysis, and for an anytime study
the budget at which the threshold can be crossed under the declared alternative. The conformance check
`gates_atelier_form` fails a method gate whose record lacks any of the six, or whose first measurement precedes
their registration in ledger order.

**Milestone schedule check.** `gates_atelier_schedule` asserts, for the current milestone, that every part due
there has a record that is a `pass` or a kill decision with its fallback adopted, and that each record's controls
are marked caught: M1: GATE-ATEL-02 (existing analyses), -05 (A-20 to A-23), -06, -11 (mission cells), -12
(analysis part). M2: -12 (episode part). M3: -01, -04 (comparison), -07 and -08 (pilots), -11 (dependency
grades), -13 (DC-01, DC-09, DC-10), -17, -19; KILL-STATIC decided. M4: -02 and -05 (the rest), -03, -04 (bisect),
-07 to -10, -11 and -13 (the rest), -14, -15, -16, -18; KILL-RANK, KILL-LADDER, KILL-VOI, KILL-REUSE, KILL-ATLAS,
KILL-BUDGETCURVE decided, each recorded as an ADR.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds the nineteen gate records and the eight kill records once each (created by
      N40-02, N40-10 and the ledger; none by this bead), and the generated-artifact check regenerates the
      studio's section of the gate tables from them. A planted second record for a studio identifier fails the
      load with `duplicate-id`.
- [ ] Every gate record has at least one control identifier, and every gate's owner declaration has the full
      threshold, at least one measuring bead and exactly one `recorded_by` bead per part, with claim and milestone
      parts equal to the record's; every
      kill record has a trigger, a fallback, a decision milestone and an owner, and every kill gate a deciding
      bead.
- [ ] `gates_atelier_form` fails on a planted method gate that lacks a potency control, and on one whose first
      measurement precedes the registration of its arms.
- [ ] `gates_atelier_schedule` fails at M3 on a fixture registry where GATE-ATEL-13's DC-09 part is `not run`, and
      passes when that part is a `pass` with a caught control.
- [ ] A declaration whose `criterion` is edited after a measurement exists, without a dated amendment, fails the
      check.
- [ ] The row for GATE-ATEL-12 carries the claim Performance and the row for GATE-ATEL-19 the claim Known answer,
      as the register rules require one claim per gate.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_gate_ledger.sh`: runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example registry_report -- gates
--chapter 28 --milestone <Mn> --json` and asserts the schedule for the milestone given. ADR-GAUNT-01 (BD-025)
gives `frob-conformance` no binary, so the report is the `gates` subcommand of the example target
`registry_report`, which this bead adds (N28-CX adds its `controls` subcommand; whichever lands first creates
`crates/frob-conformance/examples/registry_report.rs`): it loads `registries/gates.toml` and the owner
declarations and prints, per gate part due at the milestone, the status, the measured values, the controls and
whether each is caught. For each recorded part the script resolves the claim identifiers with
`frob evidence trace`. It then calls `e2e_registries_scratch` (the one way a script changes a registry: it copies
`registries/`, which holds both `gates.toml` and `gate_declarations.toml`, and exports `FROB_REGISTRIES_ROOT`,
which the conformance runners resolve through N32ax-03's resolver; this script records no gate, so the
`override` marking costs nothing) and runs the two checks on that copy, restored from the registries of record
before each planted defect and holding one at a time (a method gate without a potency control;
a method gate whose first measurement precedes its arms' registration; a part recorded `pass` with an uncaught
control; GATE-ATEL-13's DC-09 part `not run` at M3; a criterion edited after a measurement without a dated
amendment; a second record for a studio identifier), and asserts that each fails with the gate and field named,
while the clean copy passes. At this bead's own close (M1) the schedule is asserted for M1 on the parts that
exist, the later parts reported `not run`. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/atelier_gate_ledger/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N28c-25`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence`
block; per gate part a `metric` line with status, measured values against thresholds and controls caught;
`assert` lines for resolved claims, for the method-gate form and for each planted defect; on failure the gate,
the part, the missing field and the scratch path; a `summary` with parts due, recorded, passed, killed and not
run. The script is run by the milestone exit reviews.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the counts (nineteen gates; eight kill gates, of which one is owned elsewhere and consumed
  here, KILL-FDGRAD by the family-batch subsystem; KILL-RANK is owned here, and the dynamics subsystem, whose
  tier-pair record BD-265 supplies, is its consumer).
- Negative controls: the planted incomplete method gate; a part recorded `pass` with an uncaught control; a kill
  gate re-scoped after its measurement.

## Considerations

- The chapter gives GATE-ATEL-12 two claims and GATE-ATEL-19 two; the register allows one. The row follows the
  register for -12 (Performance) and takes Known answer as the governing part for -19, because its pass
  condition is an accounting identity and two structural facts. This is the owner's reading: N40-10 enters
  GATE-ATEL-19 with claim `known-answer` on it, the timing columns being reported under the Performance
  disclosure rules, and the row is not held `blocked: claim reading`.
- KILL-BUDGETCURVE has no decision milestone in the register's main tables; its gate GATE-ATEL-18 is at M4 and
  its pilot at M3. The ledger (N40-29) decides it at M4, the milestone of the gate beside it. The pilot reading
  at M3 is an input to that decision and not a second decision point.
- This bead must stay data and two checks. It does not add a seventh workspace check; both checks are tests of
  the conformance crate.
- RISK-14 (the design corpus is tuned to the analyses) is mitigated inside GATE-ATEL-01's row by the potency
  controls and a held-out third of the corpus; the row records those as controls.
