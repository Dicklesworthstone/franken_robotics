## Background

A gate is a recorded measurement against a threshold. The program's rules are strict: a gate passes only with a
record in `registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and that
resolves in the artifact graph; a gate with no record is `not run`, which is not a pass; statuses are `not run`,
`pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩`; every gate names exactly one of seven
claim kinds; every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches; where a chapter and the
register differ, the register governs on identity, claim, earliest milestone and every number it states. A kill
gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance, is never quietly re-scoped, and its decision is recorded as an
ADR at the milestone named; a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

The design studio (Atelier) defines nineteen convergence gates, GATE-ATEL-01 to GATE-ATEL-19, and appears in eight
kill gates. Each gate is measured by the bead that owns its mechanism. This bead enters the studio's rows in the
gate registry before any of them is measured, with their parts by milestone, their controls and their owners, and
adds the check that the studio's method gates have the form the chapter requires: competing arms, a potency
control that the method must catch and a clean case it must pass, a paired lower bound, and a fallback named in
advance. It measures nothing itself (its `gates` field is empty on purpose); it is what lets the milestone exit
reviews ask "is every part due here recorded, with a caught control?" mechanically. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the registry rows; the per-milestone parts; control attachments; the method-gate form check; the kill-gate
rows with trigger, fallback, decision milestone and owner.
Out: measuring any gate (the beads named in the tables); deciding any kill gate (their decision beads); the
register's generated tables (the conformance crate's generator).

**Registry mechanics (DESIGN).** Each gate row: `id`, `claim`, `threshold` (text), `parts` (list of
`{ milestone, scope }`), `controls` (identifiers in the negative-control registry), `measured_by` (bead keys),
`kill` (the kill gate decided on it, if any), `status`, `records` (per part: commit, command, output digest,
receipt hash, claim identifiers, measured values, recording session). Each kill-gate row: `id`, `trigger`,
`fallback`, `decided_at`, `owner`, `consumers`, `adr`, `status`, `amendments` (dated, each made before the
measurement it governs).

**The method-gate form check.** GATE-ATEL-01, -03, -07, -08, -15 and -18 are method gates (claim kind Design
comparison). For each, before its first run, the registry must hold: (1) the competing arms, including the
strongest conventional alternative at equal resources and the mechanism's own fallback; (2) a potency control (a
planted effect the experiment must detect) and a clean case or A/A arm it must pass; (3) the paired lower bound
as the decision statistic, never a ratio of means; (4) the pre-declared fallback with its kill gate; (5) the
ceiling, computed before the run; (6) for a fixed-n study the reachability pre-analysis, and for an anytime study
the budget at which the threshold can be crossed under the declared alternative. The conformance check
`gates_atelier_form` fails a method gate whose record lacks any of the six, or whose first measurement precedes
their registration in ledger order.

**Milestone schedule check.** `gates_atelier_schedule` asserts, for the current milestone, that every part due
there has a record that is a `pass` or a kill decision with its fallback adopted, and that each record's controls
are marked caught: M1: GATE-ATEL-02 (existing analyses), -05 (A-20 to A-23), -06, -11 (mission cells), -12
(analysis part). M2: -12 (episode part). M3: -01, -04 (comparison), -07 and -08 (pilots), -11 (dependency
grades), -13 (DC-01, DC-09, DC-10), -17, -19; KILL-STATIC decided. M4: -02 and -05 (the rest), -03, -04 (bisect),
-07 to -10, -11 and -13 (the rest), -14, -15, -16, -18; KILL-RANK, KILL-LADDER, KILL-VOI, KILL-REUSE, KILL-ATLAS,
KILL-BUDGETCURVE decided, each recorded as an ADR.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds the nineteen gate rows and the eight kill-gate rows with the fields above, and
      the generated-artifact check regenerates the studio's section of the gate tables from them.
- [ ] Every gate row has at least one control identifier and at least one measuring bead; every kill-gate row has
      a trigger, a fallback, a decision milestone and an owner.
- [ ] `gates_atelier_form` fails on a planted method gate that lacks a potency control, and on one whose first
      measurement precedes the registration of its arms.
- [ ] `gates_atelier_schedule` fails at M3 on a fixture registry where GATE-ATEL-13's DC-09 part is `not run`, and
      passes when that part is a `pass` with a caught control.
- [ ] A row whose threshold text is edited after a measurement exists, without a dated amendment, fails the
      check.
- [ ] The row for GATE-ATEL-12 carries the claim Performance and the row for GATE-ATEL-19 the claim Known answer,
      as the register rules require one claim per gate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gates_atelier.rs`: `nineteen_gates_eight_kills`; `every_gate_has_control_and_owner`;
`parts_match_schedule` (the per-milestone lists above); `method_gate_needs_six_parts`;
`measurement_before_registration_rejected`; `threshold_edit_needs_amendment`; `one_claim_per_gate`;
`kill_gate_has_trigger_fallback_milestone`; `not_run_is_not_pass`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_gate_ledger.sh`: runs `frob-conformance report gates --chapter 28 --milestone <Mn> --json`
and asserts the schedule for the milestone given; for each recorded part it resolves the claim identifiers with
`frob evidence trace`. It sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/atelier_gate_ledger/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: per gate part a `metric` line with status, measured
values against thresholds, controls caught; `assert` lines for resolved claims and for the method-gate form; on
failure the gate, the part and the missing field; a `summary` with parts due, recorded, passed, killed and not
run. The script is run by the milestone exit reviews.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the counts (nineteen gates; eight kill gates, of which two are owned elsewhere and consumed
  here: KILL-FDGRAD by the family-batch subsystem, and KILL-RANK shared with the dynamics subsystem's tier-pair
  record).
- Negative controls: the planted incomplete method gate; a part recorded `pass` with an uncaught control; a kill
  gate re-scoped after its measurement.

## Considerations

- The chapter gives GATE-ATEL-12 two claims and GATE-ATEL-19 two; the register allows one. The row follows the
  register for -12 (Performance) and takes Known answer as the governing part for -19, because its pass
  condition is an accounting identity and two structural facts; this is noted in the coverage file as a point
  for the register owner.
- KILL-BUDGETCURVE has no decision milestone in the register's main tables; its gate GATE-ATEL-18 is at M4 and
  its pilot at M3, so the row uses M4 with a pilot reading at M3.
- This bead must stay data and two checks. It does not add a seventh workspace check; both checks are tests of
  the conformance crate.
- RISK-14 (the design corpus is tuned to the analyses) is mitigated inside GATE-ATEL-01's row by the potency
  controls and a held-out third of the corpus; the row records those as controls.
