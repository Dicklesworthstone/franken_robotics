## Background

Two decision cores of the design studio turn declared costs into thresholds. DC-01 (promote a fast-tier result,
reject it as tier-sensitive, or buy another referee pair) uses the thresholds α⁺ = c/(π·C_fp) and
α⁻ = c/((1 − π)·C_fn), where c is the cost of one more audited pair, π is the fidelity ledger's share of
tier-sensitive results, C_fp is the cost of a wrong promotion and C_fn the cost of a wrong rejection. DC-05 (which
experiment to buy next, or stop) compares experiments by expected value per cost, with cost in GPU-second
equivalents at a declared exchange rate between simulation compute, CPU compute and real experiments (a bench
measurement, a batch on a real robot).

The plan leaves these numbers open as OPEN-ATEL-1: "the loss constants of DC-01 (C_fp, C_fn, the pair cost c) and
the exchange rate between GPU-seconds and real experiment cost in DC-05. Owner: Atelier lead. Blocks GATE-ATEL-13.
Default: the fixed 30-pair rule and the fixed experiment order." They are open because they are value judgements
about what a wrong promotion costs the user, not facts the system can measure, and because c and π need the M2
profile and the first fidelity ledger. This bead is the decision: it collects the measured inputs, presents the
consequences of the candidate constants, and records the owner's choice as a new version of each decision policy.

Why it matters: with the illustrative constants (c = 2, π = 0.1, C_fp = 2,000) the promotion level is
α⁺ = 2/(0.1 × 2,000) = 0.01 and promotion takes 16 clean referee pairs; with C_fp ten times smaller it is 0.1 and
takes 8. The constants move referee spend by a factor of two and the wrong-promotion rate by a factor of ten.

## Scope

In: the decision protocol, the inputs, the option table, the record, the regeneration it triggers, the fail-closed
default until then.
Out: the tables' implementation; the gate measurement (BD-309 for DC-01; the M4 conformance bead for DC-05).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs to assemble (measured, with their receipts).**
   - c: the mean and 95th-percentile cost of one audited pair (one fast-tier episode already paid plus one
     referee episode) per robot class, in GPU-second equivalents, from promotion wall-clock records.
   - π: the share of audited results per ledger stratum that ended `tier-sensitive` on the standard battery.
   - The CPU-to-GPU exchange rate actually observed (core-seconds of the referee per GPU-second of the batched
     tier at equal simulated time); the plan's illustration assumes 30 core-seconds per GPU-second.
   - Where a real-data lane exists: the declared cost of one bench measurement and of one real episode (operator
     time, wear, set-up), each as a number the owner signs, since no ledger measures them.
2. **Option table to present (computed, not chosen, by this bead).** For each candidate (C_fp, C_fn) and the
   measured c and π: α⁺, α⁻; clean pairs to promote; pairs to promote with one and with two exceedances; whether
   promotion is reachable inside the 32-pair budget; expected referee pairs per promotion at exceedance rates p₀
   and p₁; wrong-promotion and wrong-rejection bounds. Three reference rows are always included:
   - the illustrative set: c = 2, π = 0.1, C_fp = 2,000 → α⁺ = 0.01; with α⁻ = 0.05 (equivalent to C_fn ≈ 44) →
     16 clean pairs, 22 with one exceedance, 29 with two;
   - the tier-correction contract's illustrative set (wrong establishment 100, a referee episode 1, a wrong
     refusal 10) with π = 0.1 → α⁺ = 0.1, α⁻ = 0.111 → 8 clean pairs;
   - the fixed fallback (30 pairs, promote on at most 2 exceedances): wrong promotion 0.0021 at p₁ = 0.30,
     promotion probability 0.81 at p₀ = 0.05.
   For DC-05: for each candidate exchange rate, the ranking of the worked experiment menu (200 fast-tier pairs
   worth 0.0013 at cost 1; 8 referee pairs worth 0.0055 at cost 27) and the rate at which the order flips (the
   referee batch wins per unit cost when its cost falls below 27/6.4 ≈ 4.2 GPU-second equivalents).
3. **The decision record.** `registries/cores/constants.toml` (DESIGN): `[dc-01] c, C_fp, C_fn, pi_source, p0,
   p1`, `[dc-05] exchange { core_s_per_gpu_s, bench_measurement, real_episode }`, each with `decided_by`,
   `date`, `rationale`, `receipts` (the measured inputs' evidence identifiers). An ADR `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-1.md`
   holds the option table and the choice. The open-question register row is tombstoned with the ADR, not reused.
4. **What the decision triggers.** New `policy.version` for DC-01 and DC-05 (changing a recorded parameter is a
   new version of the decision policy); regeneration of the DC-01 tables for every ledger stratum with the new
   α⁺ and α⁻; re-run of the DC-01 null calibration; the DC-01 row of GATE-ATEL-13 moves from
   `blocked: OPEN-ATEL-1` to measured; DC-05's record leaves `open` on its exchange rate.
5. **Fail-closed default until decided.** DC-01 acts on the fixed 30-pair rule; DC-05 acts on the fixed order
   check, simulate, train, referee. Both run in shadow under the illustrative constants so that the option table
   has real shadow data when the owner decides. `frob cores show dc-01 --json` labels each constant
   `open: OPEN-ATEL-1`.
6. **Guard against deciding from outcomes.** The constants are chosen from costs and ledger shares, never from
   which results would then promote. The ADR records that the option table was produced before any result's
   promotion outcome under the candidate constants was displayed to the decider (the table shows rates and
   counts, not result identities).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The measured inputs (c per class, π per stratum, the observed exchange rate) are recorded with evidence
      identifiers that resolve.
- [ ] The option table exists with the three reference rows and at least the owner's candidate; its arithmetic is
      checked by tests (α⁺ = 0.01 → 16, 22, 29; α⁺ = 0.1 → 8; fallback 0.0021 and 0.81; flip cost 4.2).
- [ ] `registries/cores/constants.toml` and the ADR exist with the owner's decision, or the register row remains
      open with the default in force and the DC-01 gate row `blocked: OPEN-ATEL-1`.
- [ ] After a decision: DC-01 and DC-05 policy versions are incremented, tables regenerate, and `frob cores show`
      no longer labels the constants `open`.
- [ ] Before a decision: promotions follow the fixed 30-pair rule and suggestions follow the fixed order (asserted
      on fixtures), and shadow records under the illustrative constants exist.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/open_atel_1.rs`: `thresholds_from_constants` (exact rationals: 2/(1/10 × 2,000) = 1/100;
1/(1/10 × 100) = 1/10; 1/(9/10 × 10) = 1/9); `pairs_to_promote_by_alpha` (16 at 1/100; 8 at 1/10);
`budget_reachability` (at α⁺ = 1/100 promotion with three exceedances needs 36 pairs, outside the 32-pair
budget); `exchange_rate_flip` (cost below 4.2 flips the order of the worked menu); `default_in_force_when_open`
(with no constants file the core reports `FallbackInForce`); `version_bump_on_decision`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/open_atel_1.sh`: prints the option table from the measured ledgers with
`frob-conformance run atelier.dc01-options --json`, asserts the default behaviour through `frob eval promote` and
`frob experiment suggest` on fixtures, and, once a constants file exists, asserts regeneration and the labels in
`frob cores show dc-01 --json`. Logged through the STD-E2E helper under `target/e2e-logs/open_atel_1/`: the
measured inputs with units, each option row, the rule in force, and a `summary` stating `decided` or `open`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-695 (16 clean pairs, 22 with one exceedance at α⁺ = 0.01). Fallback binomials 0.002113 and 0.8122.
- Negative control: a constants file edited without a policy version bump fails the contract check; a table
  regenerated under constants that differ from the file fails the staleness check (`FROB-REF-871`).

## Considerations

- The constants may differ by robot class or by claim use (a result headed for a build packet costs more to
  promote wrongly than a development result); the record allows per-stratum overrides, each a separate policy
  version, but the first decision should be one global set.
- If the owner declines to decide, the default is a complete product posture: fixed 30 pairs and a fixed
  experiment order, both safe. The cost is efficiency, about a factor of two in referee pairs on clear cases.
- The exchange rate for real experiments has no meaning until a real-data lane exists; that half of the decision
  can be deferred without blocking DC-01.
