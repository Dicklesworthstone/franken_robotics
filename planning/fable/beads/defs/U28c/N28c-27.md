## Background

The evaluation ladder of the design studio lets a cheap rung (for example a shared family policy on a fast tier)
discard or select designs only where its agreement with the dearer rung has been measured. The measurement is kept
per stratum: the rank-agreement ledger stores, for each morphology class, task family, rung pair and stratum,
Kendall τ on the top band with a bootstrap interval, top-k recall, Spearman ρ, the mean signed residual per region
(the bias of the cheap rung), the conformal margin q̂ and the exchangeability martingale's value. Each rung pair in
each stratum is then in one of three modes: `selective` (τ at least 0.7 at the lower bootstrap bound),
`discard-only` (0.5 to 0.7), or `uninformative` (below 0.5, or unmeasured). The thresholds 0.7 and 0.5 are fixed
before data and are no longer open.

What a stratum is remains open as OPEN-ATEL-2: "the definition of a stratum (archive cell blocks, or morphology
class by task family). Owner: Atelier lead. Blocks GATE-ATEL-07. Default: one stratum per morphology class; an
unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`." The choice matters in two opposite ways. Strata that are too coarse hide
regional bias: a cheap rung systematically under-scores bodies that are hard to steer with a shared controller,
and one margin for a whole class is then too narrow in that region and too wide elsewhere. Strata that are too
fine starve calibration: a conformal margin at α = 0.05 needs at least 19 audit designs in the stratum, and the
audit lane is only a tenth of a rung's spend.

The decision is due at M1, earlier than its gate, because the rank-agreement experiment is pre-registered at M1
with its design set and strata fixed before data, and its pilot on an incumbent engine runs at M3. This bead is
the decision.

## Scope

In: the options, the arithmetic that constrains them, the decision record, the default, what changes downstream.
Out: the ledger and the experiment (their own beads); the mode thresholds (settled).

## Technical approach

1. **Options.**
   - A. One stratum per morphology class (the default).
   - B. Morphology class × task family.
   - C. Blocks of archive cells inside a class (the cells of the quality-diversity archive over behaviour
     descriptors such as mass, speed, energy per metre and footprint, grouped into blocks).
   - D. A two-level scheme: calibrate per block where a block has at least 19 audit designs, otherwise merge
     upward to the class (the budgeted mode the survivor-margin core already has), with per-bucket margins and a
     refusal below the calibration floor instead of extrapolation.
2. **Arithmetic the decision must respect.**
   - Floor: at α = 0.05 the margin index ⌈(n + 1)·0.95⌉ exceeds n for n < 19, so a stratum with fewer than 19
     audit designs has no margin.
   - The calibration experiment scores 40 designs per morphology class. Under A each class has 40 calibration
     designs (margin index 39 of 40). Under two strata per class, 20 each (index 20 of 20: the largest residual).
     Under three, 13 or 14 each: below the floor; those strata are `uninformative` until campaigns add audit
     draws.
   - A campaign of 81 designs spends a tenth of each rung on the audit lane: about 8 audit designs per
     generation for the whole population; a stratum that receives a third of them needs seven generations to
     reach 19.
   - Coverage at small n moves by several points: with n = 39 coverage averages 0.95 and falls below 0.884 in one
     calibration draw in twenty; at n = 19 the spread is wider (Beta(19, 1): mean 0.95, 5th percentile 0.854).
3. **Decision protocol.** The owner chooses among A to D from: the size of the pre-registered design set per
   class; the expected number of campaign generations; and evidence of regional bias from the M3 pilot if it is
   available by then (the mean signed residual per archive block; a block whose bias exceeds the class margin is
   the case for C or D). If the decision is taken at M1 before any pilot data, the options are compared on the
   arithmetic alone.
4. **Record.** `registries/strata.toml` (DESIGN): `scheme`, `key` (the fields that form a stratum key),
   `merge_rule` (when to merge upward), `min_audit` (19 at α = 0.05), `decided_by`, `date`, `rationale`. An ADR
   `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-2.md`. The stratum key is part of the rank-agreement row key, the conformal margin table
   key and the ledger identity; changing the scheme later resets the affected ledger rows (evidence is never
   pooled across a changed key).
5. **Fail-closed default until decided.** Scheme A. An unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`: the rung neither
   selects nor discards there, its budget moves up one rung, and results are labelled as unranked "k survivors".
6. **Downstream.** The pre-registration of the rank-agreement experiment records the scheme in force; the ladder
   and the survivor-margin core read `registries/strata.toml`; campaign reports print the scheme with the modes
   table.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The option table with the arithmetic above is recorded in the ADR; each number is checked by a test.
- [ ] `registries/strata.toml` exists with the chosen scheme, or holds scheme A marked `default (OPEN-ATEL-2
      open)`.
- [ ] The pre-registration record of the rank-agreement experiment cites the scheme and its date, and the date
      precedes the first data.
- [ ] With the default in force, a stratum with no rank-agreement record yields mode `uninformative` and the
      rung is skipped there (asserted on a fixture campaign).
- [ ] A change of scheme after ledger rows exist resets those rows (a fixture ledger shows zero rows pooled
      across the old and new keys).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/strata.rs`: `floor_is_19_at_alpha_0_05`; `forty_designs_by_scheme` (A: 40; two strata: 20
and 20; three: 13, 13, 14 → below the floor); `margin_index` (n = 39 → 38; n = 40 → 39; n = 20 → 20; n = 19 → 19);
`beta_spread_at_19` (5th percentile of Beta(19, 1) is 0.05^(1/19) = 0.854); `default_scheme_is_class`;
`unmeasured_is_uninformative`; `scheme_change_resets_rows`; `merge_upward_rule` (blocks with 7, 8 and 9 audit
designs merge to a class-level set of 24).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/open_atel_2.sh`: reads `registries/strata.toml`, runs `frob eval rank-agreement --show-strata
--json` on the fixture population and asserts the strata formed and their audit counts, then runs the fixture
campaign and asserts the unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`. Logs through the STD-E2E helper under
`target/e2e-logs/open_atel_2/`: scheme, strata with counts against the floor of 19, modes, and a `summary`
stating `decided` or `default`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-696 (the conformal margin at n = 39, α = 0.05 is the 38th ordered residual); the floor n ≥ 19.
- Negative control: a scheme that silently extrapolates a margin to a stratum with 12 audit designs must be
  refused by the calibration floor; a ledger that pools rows across a changed stratum key must be refused
  (`FROB-REF-548`).

## Considerations

- Published neighbours do not support agreement of 0.7 or better near the top (rank correlations of 0.40 overall
  and 0.50, 0.60, 0.83 on three robots over 17 designs; sparse Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three
  benchmarks), so many strata may be `discard-only` or `uninformative` whatever the scheme; a finer scheme then
  costs calibration and buys nothing. This argues for starting with A and refining by evidence.
- The compute for the rank-agreement experiment needs the owner's approval separately; without it every cheap
  rung is `uninformative` and this decision has no effect yet.
- RISK-27 (rank agreement too low for cheap rungs to eliminate designs) is the risk this decision interacts with
  most; the mode table and the audit lane are its mitigations, not the stratum scheme.
