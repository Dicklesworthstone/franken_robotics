## Background

A screen can throw away the winner, and nothing downstream will notice: an e-value between the eighth and ninth
survivors says nothing about a design discarded at the first rung. The design studio therefore audits its
screening stages, and one of the audits is the planted winner. Before a campaign, a design known from an earlier
specialist-and-referee result to belong in the top k is inserted in a form the screen represents poorly (an
unusual gait, or a morphology class with few examples in the family policy's training set). Its identity is
sealed in the campaign manifest hash and revealed at close. It must reach the finalists. If it does not, the
results are labelled `screen-biased`, the offending rung becomes `uninformative` in that stratum and the campaign
re-opens. A stage whose audit is missing is treated the same way: the studio does not trust a rung without a
passed audit (gate GATE-ATEL-09).

The control presupposes an earlier specialist result. For a new morphology class there is none. That is
OPEN-ATEL-5: "the planted winner for morphology classes that have no earlier specialist result. Owner: Arena lead.
Blocks GATE-ATEL-09 for those classes. Default: the control is reported `not-run` and finalists are 'k
survivors'." This bead is the decision of what, if anything, replaces the default for such classes, and it makes
sure the default itself is implemented and visible.

## Scope

In: the options, their costs and failure modes, the decision record, the default's observable behaviour, what
changes downstream.
Out: the screening audit mechanism and its gate (BD-345, with its fail-closed rule for a class with no eligible
winner); the sealed planted winner of `nemesis.audit` (N26b-05); the cascade and its report (BD-352); the design
corpus (BD-248). All four are dependencies: this bead adds the per-class policy record those mechanisms read, the
option-B seed procedure, and the checks that the default is visible end to end.

## Technical approach

1. **Options.**
   - A. Default: for a class with no earlier specialist result the control is `not-run`; every result of the
     campaign carries that condition; finalists are described as "k survivors", never "the top k"; the rung is
     not trusted to select in that class (it may still discard under the fixed table where the rank-agreement
     mode allows).
   - B. Bootstrap a winner: before the first campaign in a new class, train specialists for a small seed set
     (for example 5 designs drawn by key from the class generator), evaluate them on the referee, and seal the
     best as the planted winner for subsequent campaigns. Cost: 5 specialist trainings per class (about 7 minutes
     each for a quadruped-sized body on one high-end card by published figures; up to 30 minutes for a
     humanoid-sized one), once. Failure mode: the seed set's best may not be in the true top k of a later, larger
     population; the control then tests "a good design survives", which is weaker than "a top-k design survives"
     and must be labelled so (`planted: seed-best`).
   - C. Transfer a winner: take a planted winner from the nearest class with a specialist result and apply a
     typed edit that moves it into the new class (for example add the class's distinguishing limb). Failure
     mode: the edited design's standing in the new class is unknown; without a specialist run in the new class
     it is not a planted winner at all. C is admissible only with the specialist-and-referee run of the edited
     design, which makes it a special case of B with a seed set of one.
   - D. A synthetic potency control in place of a planted design: inflate a known design's true score by a
     declared margin at the referee rung in a sealed side-campaign and require the screen to pass it. Rejected in
     advance as a replacement: it tests the audit plumbing, not whether the screen represents an unusual body;
     it may be kept as an extra control but never satisfies the planted-winner requirement.
2. **Decision protocol.** The Arena lead chooses between A and B per class, from: the number of campaigns
   expected in the class (B's one-time cost is amortized over them); the specialist training cost in that class;
   and whether the compute envelope allows the seed trainings. The choice is recorded per class.
3. **Record.** `registries/planted_winners.toml` (DESIGN): per morphology class `source` (`corpus`, `seed-best`
   or `none`), the sealed design's hash (not its identity), the specialist-and-referee evidence identifiers,
   `label` to print, `decided_by`, `date`. ADR `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-5.md`.
4. **Default behaviour that must exist regardless (fail-closed).** With `source = "none"`: stage 3's control
   prints `not-run` with the reason "no specialist result in class"; the campaign's claim product carries the
   condition; the report uses "k survivors"; a request to select "the top k" from that class's first policy rung
   is refused (`FROB-REF-545`); GATE-ATEL-09's row for that class is `blocked: OPEN-ATEL-5`, not `pass`.
5. **Downstream.** Under B, the seed trainings become a prerequisite of the first campaign in a new class and
   their cost appears in the phase ledger under `train`; the planted winner's label `seed-best` is printed in
   the screening-audit section of campaign reports.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The option table with costs and failure modes is in the ADR; a per-class decision is recorded, or the
      default stands and is marked as such.
- [ ] Default path: on a fixture class with no specialist result, the campaign report shows stage 3 `not-run`,
      finalists as "k survivors", the condition on the claim product, and `FROB-REF-545` on a request to select.
- [ ] Under option B on a fixture class: five seed designs are drawn by key before any score exists, their
      specialist-and-referee results are recorded, the best is sealed by hash in the registry, and the next
      campaign's manifest includes the sealed hash.
- [ ] A planted winner's identity does not appear in any campaign artifact before close (the manifest holds only
      the hash); at close it is revealed and the outcome recorded.
- [ ] The record of GATE-ATEL-09, entered by its recorder BD-345, lists per class `pass`, `fail` or `blocked:
      OPEN-ATEL-5`, and the class policy this bead records is what that status reads; this bead writes no gate
      row.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/planted_winner_policy.rs`: `none_source_reports_not_run`; `none_source_refuses_selection`
(`FROB-REF-545`); `seed_draw_is_keyed_and_precedes_scores`; `seed_best_is_sealed_by_hash`;
`identity_hidden_until_close` (a search of all artifacts written before close finds no occurrence of the sealed
design identifier); `seed_best_label_printed`; `synthetic_potency_control_does_not_satisfy_requirement`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/planted_winner_policy.sh`: runs the 27-design fixture campaign in a class marked `none` and asserts
the default behaviour through `frob opt cascade` and `frob report campaign` (stage 3 `not-run` with the reason,
"k survivors", the condition on the claim product) and `frob opt cascade … --finalists-from R1` refused with
`FROB-REF-545` (every `opt.cascade` call, a `run` verb, carries a key of its own, `--key
pw-<step>-$FROB_E2E_SEED`, so that 805 cannot answer first and no call is served another's receipt); then, with a
seed-best registered in the run's registries copy (`e2e_registries_scratch`), re-runs and asserts the
manifest carries only the sealed hash, the planted winner reaches the finalists and is revealed at close with the
label `seed-best`; finally, a grep of every artifact written before close finds no occurrence of the sealed
identifier, and a scratch registry holding the identifier in clear is refused at load. It sources the STD-E2E
helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/planted_winner_policy/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N28c-30`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the
envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines carry the control status per stage, the audited discards m with the
bound 1 − δ^(1/m), and the artifact count searched before close; `digest` fields carry the manifest hash and the
sealed hash; `refusal` lines carry 545 with pointer and repairs; on failure the stage, the class and the artifact
path; a `summary` naming GATE-ATEL-09's per-class status.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Screening-audit arithmetic that accompanies the control: with no true top-k design among m audited discards the
  rate of discarded winners is at most 1 − δ^(1/m) at confidence 1 − δ (9.5% at m = 30 and δ = 0.05).
- Negative controls: a campaign that reports stage 3 `passed` with no planted winner; a registry that stores the
  winner's identifier in clear; a screen that drops the planted winner without the `screen-biased` label.

## Considerations

- RISK-37 (a screen discards the winner) is the risk this control exists for; the default trades the control
  away for new classes and says so on every result, which is the honest minimum.
- Option B spends training on designs that may be poor; it is still cheaper than one undetected lost winner, and
  the seed specialists also serve the rank-agreement ledger as calibration points.
- If family policies are killed for a class (no shared-policy rung), the first rung there is the baseline
  controller; the planted-winner control applies to it in the same way.
