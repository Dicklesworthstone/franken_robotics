## Background

The evaluation ladder of the design studio has six rungs. R2 is the family policy fine-tuned to the design at a
declared fraction of specialist training steps; each fraction is its own rung for calibration, costs minutes, and
may discard and select among survivors. The decisive early experiment of the program scores forty designs in one
morphology class by the shared policy on the fast tier (R1), by a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps (R2), and by
a specialist promoted on the referee (R3 and R4), and reports rank agreement between the rungs.

OPEN-ATEL-6 asks whether a fine-tune fraction of 5% is the right R2 rung, or whether two R2 rungs are needed.
Owner: Cortex lead. Decided by the calibration experiment. Default: one R2 rung at 5%. Published evidence on
other engines (FACT, to be re-read before a gate cites it) says that within one morphology class fine-tuning
matches a specialist after about 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return, so 5% sits inside a
wide range; across topology-diverse designs the picture is worse. The question does not block a gate by itself,
but it fixes two things in the studio: the rung table that the ladder and the rank-agreement ledger key on, and
the default budget ladder of the controller-budget curves for learned policies, {0, 0.05, 0.2} of specialist
steps, whose two lower points are exactly R1 and R2. This bead is the decision.

## Scope

In: the reading of the calibration experiment for this question, the decision rule, the record, the default, and
what changes downstream.
Out: running the experiment (its own beads); the rank-agreement thresholds (settled at 0.7 and 0.5).

## Technical approach

1. **Data to read.** From the calibration experiment per morphology class: for fine-tune fractions f in the
   measured set (at least 0.05; and 0.2 where the budget-curve ladder has run), Kendall τ on the top band of R2(f)
   against the specialist-on-referee ranking with its bootstrap interval; top-10 recall; the conformal margin q̂
   of R2(f); the cost of R2(f) per design; and τ of R1 against R2(f).
2. **Decision rule (DESIGN, fixed before the data are read).**
   - Keep one R2 rung at 5% if, in the classes tested, R2(0.05) is `selective` (τ ≥ 0.7 at the lower bootstrap
     bound) or `discard-only` (≥ 0.5), and the number of survivors it passes at margin 2·q̂ is at most 3k in the
     median stratum.
   - Add a second R2 rung at a higher fraction (20% by default, matching the budget ladder) if R2(0.05) is
     `uninformative` in a class while R2(0.2) is at least `discard-only`, or if R2(0.05) passes more than 3k
     survivors and R2(0.2) cuts them to at most 3k at a total cost below sending them all to specialists.
     Arithmetic check for the second condition with illustrative costs (a specialist costing 1 unit, R2(f)
     costing f): sending s survivors through R2(0.2) and then 3k to specialists costs 0.2·s + 3k, against s for
     specialists directly; the extra rung pays when 0.8·s > 3k, that is s > 3.75·k (12 or more survivors at
     k = 3).
   - Replace 5% by another single fraction if a measured smaller fraction achieves the same mode at lower cost.
   - If no fraction reaches `discard-only`, R2 is `uninformative` for the class: the rung is skipped there and
     its budget moves to R3; this is recorded and feeds the kill decision on rank agreement.
3. **Record.** `registries/rungs.toml` (DESIGN): per morphology class the list of R2 fractions in force, each a
   separate rung for calibration (the rung type carries the fraction), with the evidence identifiers of the
   calibration rows; ADR `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-6.md`. Changing the list resets the affected rank-agreement rows
   (a different rung pair is a different ledger key).
4. **Fail-closed default until decided.** One R2 rung at 5%; like every rung, `uninformative` in any stratum where
   its rank agreement is unmeasured.
5. **Downstream.** The ladder's rung table and the fixed halving schedule read the list; the budget ladder for
   learned policies in the controller-budget curves stays {0, 0.05, 0.2} unless this decision changes the
   fractions, in which case its middle and top points follow the decided R2 fractions; campaign reports print the
   fractions in the modes table.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision rule is recorded with a date that precedes the reading of the calibration data for this
      question.
- [ ] The ADR tabulates, per class and fraction, τ with its interval, top-10 recall, q̂, survivors at 2·q̂ and
      cost, and states the outcome of the rule.
- [ ] `registries/rungs.toml` lists the R2 fractions per class, or the default 5% marked `default (OPEN-ATEL-6
      open)`.
- [ ] With two R2 fractions registered for a fixture class, the ladder treats them as two rungs with separate
      rank-agreement rows and margins, and the halving schedule has one more transition.
- [ ] Changing the fraction list resets the affected ledger rows (no pooling across rung definitions).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-opt/tests/rung_fractions.rs`: `default_single_rung_at_5_percent`;
`two_fractions_are_two_rungs` (distinct ledger keys for fractions 1/20 and 1/5);
`extra_rung_pays_threshold` (cost 0.2·s + 3k < s exactly when s > 3.75·k; at k = 3: false for s = 11, true for
s = 12); `decision_rule_table` (synthetic τ inputs: (0.72, —) → keep; (0.45, 0.61) → add 20%; (0.40, 0.42) →
uninformative); `fraction_change_resets_rows`; `budget_ladder_follows_rungs`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/open_atel_6.sh`: reads the calibration rows with `frob eval rank-agreement --show --json`, evaluates
the pre-registered rule with `frob-conformance run atelier.r2-rule --json`, and, on the fixture class with two
fractions registered, runs `frob opt cascade --replay` and asserts two R2 transitions in the bracket ledger. Logs
through the STD-E2E helper under `target/e2e-logs/open_atel_6/`: per class and fraction the measured values with
thresholds 0.7 and 0.5, the rule's outcome, the rung list in force, and a `summary` stating `decided` or
`default`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the break-even survivor count 3.75·k for an extra rung at 20%.
- Negative controls: a rule evaluated after being edited post-data (the date check fails); a ladder that pools
  rank-agreement rows of the 5% and 20% rungs (`FROB-REF-548`).

## Considerations

- The decision is per morphology class; a class of topology-diverse designs may need the extra rung while a
  parametric class does not.
- A second R2 rung adds an audit lane (a tenth of that rung's spend) and a calibration set; with few designs per
  stratum it can starve calibration, which interacts with the stratum definition (OPEN-ATEL-2).
- If family policies are killed for a class, R1 and R2 do not exist there as policy rungs and this decision is
  moot for that class.
