## Background

Every cheap rung of the design studio's evaluation ladder scores a robot body together with a controller of fixed
competence: the sampling-based predictive controller at one sample count, a family policy with no adaptation, a
fine-tune at one fraction of specialist steps. The score therefore ranks body × controller, and a design that
needs more controller effort to show what it can do is under-scored. The proposed mechanism (Frontier) measures
this per design before the design is discarded: score each design at a ladder of controller budgets
b₀ < b₁ < b₂, fit a saturating law J_d(b) = J_d∞ − a_d·(c(b)/c(b₀))^(−γ), and escalate designs whose estimated
ceiling J_d∞ could still reach the top of their stratum, flagging them `controller-limited` instead of calling
them inferior.

Every Frontier mechanism has a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that runs before its work package is funded; a
work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded and its fallback ships. This bead is that pilot. The
ceiling is stated before any test: the rule cannot rank better than running every design at the top budget; its
benefit is the compute between that and the escalation rule, at most c(b₂) divided by the mean cost (16/3.8 = 4.2
in the plan's illustration with an effort ratio of 4, 30% of designs escalated to b₁ and 10% to b₂), and it is
nothing when no two curves cross. The pilot's question is exactly that: do rankings depend on controller budget
at all? Protocol from the plan: on `batch-cpu`, 20 variants from two templates at sample counts
N_s ∈ {32, 128, 512} on shared addresses. Falsifiers: if no two curves cross, rankings do not depend on budget,
the mechanism is not built, and the screening rung is the better for it; if more than a fifth of the curves are
refused by the fit, the law is wrong for this controller.

The first trigger of kill gate KILL-BUDGETCURVE ("the pilot shows no crossing") is read here. Fallback named in
advance: fixed-budget scores, with the saturation index printed as a description and no escalation rule.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the pilot run, the crossing analysis, the refusal-rate analysis, the pilot record, the
first reading of the kill gate.
Out: the mechanism (the budget coordinate, rows, fit, interval, escalation and verbs, each its own bead, all of
which depend on this pilot); the oracle experiment and the final kill decision at M4.

## Technical approach

1. **Protocol, frozen and registered before execution.**
   - Designs: 20 variants, 10 from each of two templates (the reference quadruped template and one wheeled or
     hybrid template; DESIGN choice recorded), drawn by a keyed stream over the templates' declared parameter
     levers (link lengths, masses, gear ratios) within ±15%.
   - Controller: kind `mpc-sample` (at each control tick it samples N_s perturbed command sequences over a
     horizon, rolls each out on the design's own compiled model, and applies the first command of a weighted
     average), with a fixed number of annealing iterations N_it = 2 and horizon of H = 16 planning steps at a
     20 ms planning step, control at 50 Hz. Effort per tick c = N_s·N_it·H model steps.
   - Budgets: N_s ∈ {32, 128, 512}, a constant effort ratio of 4.
   - Task and addresses: the flat velocity task; 8 world addresses shared by all designs and all budgets (DESIGN:
     more than the default screening allowance of three episodes, so that paired noise can be estimated), 20 s
     episodes, class D2 on `batch-cpu`.
   - Score: the task's unit-scaled score in [0, 1] per episode; J_d(b) is the mean over the 8 addresses.
   - Cost estimate (arithmetic): steps per tick 1,024, 4,096 and 16,384; per 20 s episode (1,000 ticks) 1.0 × 10⁶,
     4.1 × 10⁶ and 1.6 × 10⁷ planning steps; 20 designs × 8 addresses × the three budgets is about 3.4 × 10⁹
     planning steps: under one day of the CPU of the large workstation (TARGET).
2. **Run.** `frob control mpc` per (design, address, budget) through the job queue, results keyed by
   (design, world address, budget); every budget runs on the same addresses, so differences across budgets and
   across designs are paired.
3. **Crossing analysis.** For each of the C(20, 2) = 190 design pairs (i, j) compute the paired differences over
   addresses D₀ = J_i(b₀) − J_j(b₀) and D₂ = J_i(b₂) − J_j(b₂) with their paired standard errors. A pair *crosses*
   when D₀ and D₂ have opposite signs and each is significant one-sided at level 0.05/190 (a correction for the
   190 pairs; DESIGN). Report the number of crossing pairs, the largest crossing (the worked illustration has a
   swing from +0.06 to −0.04), and the Kendall τ between the rankings at b₀ and at b₂.
4. **Curve analysis.** Apply the three-point form to each design: Δ₁ = J(b₁) − J(b₀), Δ₂ = J(b₂) − J(b₁),
   ρ = Δ₂/Δ₁, J∞ = J(b₀) + Δ₁/(1 − ρ), saturation index s = (J(b₂) − J(b₀))/(J∞ − J(b₀)). Count the fits that
   would be refused: scores decreasing by more than paired noise; Δ₂ ≥ Δ₁ (still accelerating); Δ₁ within noise
   of zero.
5. **Falsifier evaluation.**
   - F1: zero crossing pairs → the mechanism is not built; KILL-BUDGETCURVE's first trigger is met; the fallback
     ships (fixed-budget scores; the saturation index may still be printed as a description).
   - F2: more than a fifth of the curves refused (5 or more of 20) → the saturating law is wrong for this
     controller; the fit and escalation are not built for `mpc-sample`; the pilot may be repeated once for the
     learned-policy ladder ({0, 0.05, 0.2} of specialist steps) when fine-tuning exists, under a new pilot
     record.
   - Otherwise the pilot passes and the mechanism beads are funded.
6. **Potency and A/A controls of the pilot itself.** Potency: two synthetic designs are added whose scores are
   generated from the law with crossing curves (0.61, 0.70, 0.72) and (0.55, 0.68, 0.76) plus the measured paired
   noise; the analysis must report them as a crossing pair. A/A: the same design registered twice under two
   identifiers must not form a crossing pair with itself.
7. **Record.** A row in the pilot register: mechanism and tier (controller-budget curves, Frontier); the protocol
   hash; the falsifiers F1 and F2; the ceiling (4.2 in the illustration, recomputed from the measured escalation
   shares the pilot implies); the fallback and kill gate; cost; tool and pin (`batch-cpu` and the controller at
   the commit used); then the result. The ledger sequence number of the protocol precedes the first episode.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol is registered with its hash before the first episode is dispatched.
- [ ] All 480 (design, address, budget) results exist, keyed by (design, world address, budget), on shared
      addresses, at class D2.
- [ ] The crossing analysis reports the number of crossing pairs with the correction stated, the rank correlation
      between budgets, and the largest swing; the curve analysis reports J∞, s and the refused share.
- [ ] The potency pair is detected as crossing and the A/A pair is not.
- [ ] The falsifiers are evaluated by the rule above and the result is written to the pilot register and to the
      KILL-BUDGETCURVE row (first trigger: met or not met).
- [ ] If F1 holds, the mechanism beads are closed as not built with this pilot cited; if neither holds, they are
      released.
- [ ] The pilot is recorded by a session that did not write the mechanism.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_budget_curve.rs` (the analysis code, run on fixtures):
`crossing_detected_on_worked_pair` (A: 0.61, 0.70, 0.72; B: 0.55, 0.68, 0.76 with zero noise → one crossing;
D₀ = +0.06, D₂ = −0.04); `no_crossing_on_parallel_curves`; `bonferroni_level_is_alpha_over_190`;
`three_point_values` (J∞ 0.726 and 0.888; s 0.95 and 0.62); `refusal_counts` (a decreasing curve, an accelerating
curve, a flat curve each counted); `threshold_curve_refused` (0.20, 0.20, 0.80: Δ₁ = 0); `falsifier_rule`
(0 crossings → F1; 5 of 20 refused → F2; 4 of 20 → not F2); `a_a_pair_never_crosses`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_budget_curve.sh`: submits the 480 jobs through `frob control mpc … --json` with explicit seed
and addresses, waits, runs `frob-conformance run pilots.budget-curve --json` for the analysis, and writes the
pilot record. It sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_budget_curve/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript: the protocol hash; per job the command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence`
block; `metric` lines for crossing pairs (falsifier threshold: 0), refused share (threshold: one fifth), rank
correlation, measured paired noise, total planning steps and wall time; `assert` lines for the potency and A/A
controls; a `summary` with the falsifier evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-BUDGETCURVE"]`. It exits zero when the
measurement is complete, whatever the outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Worked case (illustrative): design A scores 0.61, 0.70, 0.72 and design B 0.55, 0.68, 0.76; ρ_A = 0.222,
  J_A∞ = 0.726, s_A = 0.95; ρ_B = 0.615, J_B∞ = 0.888, s_B = 0.62; A leads by 0.06 at b₀ and B by 0.04 at b₂.
- Negative controls: the A/A pair; a planted analysis defect that compares unpaired means (it reports crossings
  among identical designs under address noise and must be caught by the A/A control).
- Determinism lanes: the 480 results twice on one machine and across worker counts (class D2: identical digests);
  the analysis is exact given the results.

## Considerations

- The sampling controller holds the model it controls, so its scores say what a body can do under a clairvoyant
  model; results carry the flag `model-privileged`. The pilot asks only whether rankings move with budget, which
  that flag does not invalidate.
- Two templates and 20 variants are a small population; "no crossing" on it is evidence against building, not
  proof that curves never cross elsewhere. The kill decision is scoped to that evidence and the rejected-methods
  procedure governs reopening.
- A zero-delay planner is unrealistic; the pilot uses the default modelled delay of one control period and
  records it in the controller identity.
- The published cost row for this controller (about 0.33 GPU-minutes per 20 s episode at 2,048 samples on one
  high-end card) is a FACT about another engine; this pilot's cost is measured, not inferred from it.
