## Background

A cheap evaluation rung scores a robot body together with a controller of fixed competence, so the score ranks
body × controller and a design that needs more controller effort is under-scored. Controller-budget curves
(Frontier; built only if the pilot shows curves that cross) measure each design at a ladder of controller budgets
b₀ < b₁ < b₂ on the same world addresses and fit, per design d in a morphology class, the saturating law

    J_d(b) = J_d∞ − a_d·(c(b)/c(b₀))^(−γ),     a_d ≥ 0,  γ > 0 shared by the class,  J_d non-decreasing in b,

where J_d(b) is the design's mean score at budget b, c(b) the scalar controller effort at that budget (model
steps per tick for the sampling controller), J_d∞ the score the design would reach with unlimited controller
effort, a_d the gap at the base budget, and γ the rate at which effort closes the gap. The *saturation index*
s_d = (J(b_max) − J(b₀))/(J_d∞ − J(b₀)) is the share of the predicted gain already realized at the highest budget
reached.

What the estimate may do is tightly limited: J_d∞ is `Estimated`; it steers allocation and never enters a verdict
field (the same invariant that binds value-of-information quantities); the measured contrast at the highest
budget reached goes through the ordinary comparison and promotion path unchanged and is the only quantity that
can become `established`. This bead is the fit: point estimates, the index, and the refusals. A saturating law
does not describe every design, and the plan names the case that breaks it: a body that needs a threshold budget
to find any gait scores 0.20, 0.20, 0.80; the two lower budgets extrapolate to J∞ = 0.20, wrong by at least 0.60,
and the three-point form divides by zero. Such a curve is refused, the design is labelled `curve-unfit`, and it
is escalated and never discarded on its curve.

Rejected alternatives: a richer parametric family per design (three points cannot identify more than the
three-point form does); a learned predictor of the specialist score from the cheap score (it hides the artifacts
the ladder exists to expose, and has no refusal for threshold behaviour).

## Scope

In: the two estimators, the saturation index, the refusal tests and labels, the monotonicity handling, the output
type, the numerical details needed for bit-stable results.
Out: the rows and paired-noise queries (N28c-34); the interval for J∞ (N28c-36); the escalation rule and the
`controller-limited` flag (N28c-37); verbs (N28c-38); the oracle experiment.

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-opt/src/budget_curve/fit.rs`, feature `frontier-budget-curve`. Inputs come from the paired
queries: per design the level means J(b₀), J(b₁), J(b₂) over the common worlds, the mean increments
Δ₁ = J(b₁) − J(b₀) and Δ₂ = J(b₂) − J(b₁), their paired standard errors se₁, se₂ with degrees of freedom, and the
efforts c(bᵢ).

1. **Estimator E1: the three-point closed form** (ladders with a constant effort ratio r, three levels). Under the
   law with a per-design rate, Δ₁ = a·(1 − r^(−γ)) and Δ₂ = a·r^(−γ)·(1 − r^(−γ)), so with ρ = Δ₂/Δ₁:
   - ρ = r^(−γ), hence γ_d = −ln ρ / ln r;
   - a_d = Δ₁/(1 − ρ);
   - J_d∞ = J(b₀) + Δ₁/(1 − ρ);
   - s_d = (J(b₂) − J(b₀))/(J_d∞ − J(b₀)) = (1 + ρ)(1 − ρ) = 1 − ρ².
   E1 interpolates the three points exactly (zero residual). It is the default on constant-ratio ladders and is
   the estimator the known-answer gate's worked case is stated for.
2. **Estimator E2: least squares with a class-shared γ** (any ladder with strictly positive efforts; at least two
   designs with admissible curves in the class; also used when a ladder has more than three levels). For a fixed
   γ let xᵢ = (c(bᵢ)/c(b₀))^(−γ) and yᵢ = J(bᵢ). The law is linear in (J∞, a): y = J∞ − a·x. Per design:
   a(γ) = −S_xy/S_xx and J∞(γ) = ȳ + a(γ)·x̄, with S_xy = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ), S_xx = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²; the residual sum
   of squares RSS_d(γ) has one degree of freedom at three budgets. The class rate is γ̂ = argmin over γ of
   Σ_d RSS_d(γ), found by a deterministic search: a fixed grid of 200 log-spaced points on [0.05, 4.0], then
   golden-section refinement of the best bracket to a relative width of 10⁻⁶ (the grid and tolerances are part
   of the estimator's version). Designs whose curves are refused are excluded from the pooled residual.
3. **Which estimate is reported.** On a constant-ratio three-level ladder both are computed; E1 is reported as
   `j_inf`, E2 as `j_inf_pooled`, with their difference as a diagnostic. On other ladders E2 is reported. The
   estimator name and version are part of the output and of the predictor's identity for calibration.
4. **Refusals (`curve-unfit`), evaluated before any estimate is formed; the first that holds is the reason
   reported.** Order: `effort-undefined` and `noise-undefined` first (without efforts and paired noise the other
   three tests cannot be formed), then `decreasing`, `flat-start`, `accelerating` (the threshold case meets both
   `flat-start` and `accelerating` and reports `flat-start`). With the paired standard errors:
   - `decreasing`: Δ₁ < −2·se₁ or Δ₂ < −2·se₂ (scores decrease by more than paired noise);
   - `flat-start`: |Δ₁| ≤ 2·se₁ (Δ₁ is within noise of zero; the three-point form would divide by a noise-sized
     number);
   - `accelerating`: Δ₂ ≥ Δ₁, that is ρ ≥ 1 (the curve is still accelerating; no finite ceiling);
   - `noise-undefined`: fewer than two common worlds;
   - `effort-undefined`: the ladder's base effort is zero or the efforts are not strictly increasing.
   The factor 2 is a DESIGN default recorded with the estimator. A refused design has no J∞ and no index; its
   status is `Unfit(reason)`.
5. **Small negative second increment.** If −2·se₂ ≤ Δ₂ < 0 (a decrease within noise), the curve is treated as
   saturated at b₁: ρ := 0, J∞ := max(J(b₁), J(b₂)), s := 1, status `Fit` with note `saturated-within-noise`.
   This enforces "J_d non-decreasing in b" without refusing a design for noise.
6. **Bounds.** a_d ≥ 0 by construction after the refusals. If J∞ exceeds the metric's declared upper bound (for
   example above 1 on a unit-scaled score), it is clipped to the bound with note `clipped-to-span`.
7. **Output (DESIGN).**
   ```rust
   pub struct CurveFit {
       design: DesignId, family: ControllerFamilyId, ladder: BudgetLadderId,
       scores: Vec<(u8, f64)>, worlds_used: u32,
       status: FitStatus,                     // Fit { notes } | Unfit(Reason)
       estimator: EstimatorId,                // ("three-point", 1) or ("pooled-gamma", 1)
       j_inf: Option<Advisory<f64>>, j_inf_pooled: Option<Advisory<f64>>,
       a: Option<f64>, gamma: Option<f64>, rho: Option<f64>,
       saturation: Option<Advisory<f64>>, residual: Option<f64>,
   }
   ```
   `j_inf` and `saturation` are advisory quantities (class `Estimated`, model `BudgetCurveCeiling` and
   `SaturationIndex`): they cannot be converted into a verdict statistic and are serialized only in advisory
   blocks.
8. **Numerics for bit stability.** Sums in world-address order; the division Δ₁/(1 − ρ) computed as
   Δ₁²/(Δ₁ − Δ₂) to avoid forming ρ first; logarithms only for the reported γ (not on the path to J∞ in E1); the
   golden-section search uses a fixed iteration count. Class D2 on one backend for the scores; the fit itself is
   a deterministic function of its inputs and is required to give identical results on both hosts for inputs
   that are exact decimals with at most six digits (tested on the fixtures).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Worked case, estimator E1: scores (0.61, 0.70, 0.72) give ρ = 0.222, J∞ = 0.726, s = 0.95; scores
      (0.55, 0.68, 0.76) give ρ = 0.615, J∞ = 0.888, s = 0.62 (each to three decimals; s to two).
- [ ] Identity: on any admissible three-point input, s = 1 − ρ² to within 10⁻¹².
- [ ] Threshold case (0.20, 0.20, 0.80) is refused (`flat-start`), with no J∞ returned; (0.50, 0.55, 0.65) is
      refused (`accelerating`); (0.70, 0.60, 0.61) with se₁ = 0.01 is refused (`decreasing`).
- [ ] A second increment of −0.004 with se₂ = 0.005 gives status `Fit` with `saturated-within-noise`, J∞ = J(b₁)
      and s = 1.
- [ ] E2 recovers planted parameters on noiseless synthetic data: for 50 designs drawn from the law with γ = 0.8
      and random (J∞, a), the pooled γ̂ is within 10⁻⁴ of 0.8 and each J∞ within 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] On a class where all designs share one γ and the ladder has a constant ratio, E1 and E2 agree to within
      10⁻⁶ on noiseless data; on the worked pair (which have different rates) their difference is reported as a
      diagnostic and E1 is the reported estimate.
- [ ] `j_inf` and `saturation` are advisory types; a compile-fail test shows they cannot be passed to the verdict
      writer.
- [ ] A design with a refused fit is returned with `Unfit` and is never assigned a rank from its curve.
- [ ] Identical outputs on the Mac and the Linux host for the fixture inputs.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The worked case and the threshold counterexample above (the values of the known-answer gate for this
  mechanism).
- Clip check: Δ₁ = 0.12, Δ₂ = 0.06, ρ = 0.5, J∞ = 0.80 + 0.12/0.5 = 1.04.
- Negative controls that must fail: a fit that returns J∞ = 0.20 for the threshold case; a fit that averages
  unpaired scores; a fit that reports a standard error of the two-parameter fit as the interval of J∞ (the
  interval belongs to the calibrated margin, not to this fit); a build in which `j_inf` is a bare `f64`. The
  first is the control the counterexample registry seeds for its late entry CX-28L-1 against GATE-ATEL-17, and
  this bead is its planting bead (plant `N28c-35:curve-fit-threshold`): the fit without its `curve-unfit`
  refusals is the `cfg(feature = "curve-fit-threshold")` branch of `frob-opt::budget_curve::fit`, declared as
  implying the crate's `conformance` feature and built only with `--features
  gauntlet-mutations,frob-opt/curve-fit-threshold` (here `cargo test -p frob-opt --features
  frontier-budget-curve,curve-fit-threshold --test budget_curve_fit -- refuse_threshold_case`, which must fail on
  that build: the fit returns 0.20 and the design would be discarded). Under any other feature name the seeded
  control is `stale` and GATE-ATEL-17 cannot be recorded.
- Metamorphic relations: adding a constant to all three scores shifts J∞ by that constant and leaves ρ and s
  unchanged; scaling scores by k > 0 scales J∞ and a by k and leaves ρ, γ and s unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; both hosts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- With three budgets the three-point form has no residual to test the law with; the pooled-γ fit has one degree
  of freedom per design, and the calibration of the interval on audit designs is the real test of the predictor.
  The plan's wording gives both a class-shared γ and a per-design closed form; this bead implements both and
  reports which was used, and the coverage notes flag the ambiguity for the owner.
- More than a fifth of curves refused in a class means the law is wrong for that controller (the pilot's second
  falsifier); the fit reports the refused share per class so that the condition is visible after the pilot too.
- The escalation rule treats `Unfit` designs as unsaturated, so a refusal costs compute, never a lost design.
