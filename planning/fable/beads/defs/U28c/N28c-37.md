## Background

Controller-budget curves let the design studio tell a weak body from a body that merely needs more controller
effort. Each design is scored at a ladder of controller budgets b₀ < b₁ < b₂ on shared world addresses; a
saturating law gives an estimate J_d∞ of the score at unlimited controller effort, with a conformal interval
calibrated on audit-lane designs that were scored by specialists. This bead is what the estimate is for: an
escalation rule that spends higher budgets only on designs whose ceiling could still matter, and a flag that
stops a controller-starved design from being reported as inferior. The plan's rule:

- Every candidate runs at b₀. A design moves to the next budget when the upper end of its interval for J_d∞
  reaches the k-th best measured score of its stratum. A design whose fit is refused is treated as unsaturated
  and escalates.
- A design is flagged `controller-limited` when J_d∞ − J_d(b_max) exceeds the decision margin of the study;
  campaign reports print the flag beside the rank, and a `controller-limited` design is never described as
  inferior.
- `opt.cascade` applies the escalation rule at stage 3 (the screening stage of the six-stage co-design cascade).

Cost, illustrative: with an effort ratio of 4 and escalation of 30% of designs to b₁ and 10% to b₂, the mean cost
per design is 1 + 0.3 × 4 + 0.1 × 16 = 3.8 units of b₀, against 16 for running every design at b₂; the ceiling of
the benefit is 16/3.8 = 4.2, and it is nothing when no two curves cross. The mechanism is Frontier [F]: it is
built only if its pilot shows crossing curves, it ships in shadow behind the fixed table (ADR-ATEL-09: no adaptive
controller is enabled by default without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a
demonstrated fallback), and kill gate KILL-BUDGETCURVE names its fallback in advance: fixed-budget scores, with
the saturation index printed as a description and no escalation rule.

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-opt/src/budget_curve/escalate.rs`, feature `frontier-budget-curve`.

1. **Quantities.** For a stratum at a decision point (a pinned ledger frontier at a generation boundary):
   `S_k`, the k-th best *measured* score among the stratum's designs, each taken at the highest budget it has
   reached (k = finalists wanted); for each design the *upper end* U_d of its ceiling interval given the levels
   it has run.
2. **Ceiling interval by levels run (DESIGN; the plan states the rule for a fitted curve and is silent on what
   the interval is before three budgets exist).**
   - After b₀ only: no curve exists. U_d = J_d(b₀) + q̂₀, where q̂₀ is the conformal margin of the gap
     (specialist score minus the b₀ score) on the stratum's audit designs, with the same floor of 19; without
     calibration U_d is the top of the metric's declared span.
   - After b₀ and b₁: the two-point estimate with the class rate γ̂ (the pooled least-squares rate from designs of
     the class that ran all three budgets, which the audit lane always does): J_d∞ = J(b₀) + Δ₁/(1 − r^(−γ̂)) with
     r the effort ratio; refused when Δ₁ is within paired noise of zero or negative beyond noise; U_d = J_d∞ + q̂₁
     with q̂₁ calibrated for this predictor.
   - After all three: the three-point estimate with its margin q̂₂; U_d = J_d∞ + q̂₂.
   Each predictor has its own calibration record; each falls back to the span when uncalibrated.
3. **Escalation decision.** A design at level i < max moves to level i + 1 when U_d ≥ S_k. A design whose fit is
   refused at its level is treated as unsaturated and moves. Audit-lane designs always run every level (they are
   never cancelled or skipped). Decisions are taken for the whole stratum at once from the pinned frontier, so
   they do not depend on completion order.
4. **Uncalibrated behaviour: the fixed table.** When the stratum has no calibration for the predictor in use, the
   rule gives no information (every U_d is the span top). The decision then follows the ladder's fixed table:
   the top ⌈n/3⌉ designs by measured score move to the next budget (halving at η = 3), and the envelope says the
   rule reduced to the fixed table and why. With an effort ratio of 4 the fixed table costs
   1 + (1/3)·4 + (1/9)·16 = 4.1 units of b₀ per design.
5. **Flags.**
   - `controller-limited`: J_d∞ − J_d(b_max) > m, with m the decision margin of the study and J_d∞ the
     three-point estimate at the highest level reached. Text printed with it: "controller-limited: the estimated
     controller ceiling exceeds the measured score by more than the decision margin". Such a design is offered to
     the next rung of the ladder (a fine-tune or a specialist) by the ordinary advancement rule and is never
     removed from the candidate set by this rule.
   - `curve-unfit` (with its reason): the design is escalated and never discarded on its curve.
   - `saturated`: s_d ≥ 0.9 and J_d∞ − J_d(b_max) ≤ m (DESIGN threshold for the descriptive label only).
6. **What the rule may not do.** It never discards a design: discarding belongs to the ladder's advancement rule
   under its rank-agreement mode. It never writes a verdict: J∞, intervals and flags are advisory; the measured
   contrast at the highest budget reached goes through comparison and promotion unchanged. It never requests
   work for a design that the finalist layer already owns (one-way handover).
7. **Registration as a decision-core artifact.** The rule is an artifact of the advancement core DC-02
   (`kind = "guard-table"`, name `budget-escalation`): its table maps (level, calibrated?, fit status, sign of
   U_d − S_k) to an action; fallback: the fixed table; triggers: `under-19-calibration-points`,
   `martingale-at-or-above-20`, `refused-share-above-one-fifth` (more than a fifth of the stratum's curves
   refused: the law is wrong for this controller), `identity-or-scope-changed`; budgeted mode: at most three
   levels, and the default screening allowance of three episodes per design per level. It ships in `shadow`:
   both the rule's set and the fixed table's set are recorded per decision and the fixed table acts, until the
   oracle experiment has been read.
8. **Cost accounting.** Per stratum and campaign: shares f₁, f₂ of designs that reached b₁ and b₂; mean cost
   1 + f₁·r + f₂·r² in units of b₀; the ceiling c(b₂)/mean cost; GPU-seconds spent per level. On the published
   quadruped row (0.33 GPU-minutes per 20 s episode at 2,048 samples; cost proportional to the sample count) a
   design escalated through all three default budgets on three addresses costs
   3 × (64 + 256 + 1,024)/2,048 × 0.33 = 0.65 GPU-minutes.
9. **Integration.** `opt.cascade` stage 3 calls `escalate::decide(stratum, frontier)` after each budget level's
   episodes are complete; flags travel on the design's stage result into the survivor set, the finalists table
   and `report.campaign`; `explain.compare` prints both curves.

Determinism: decisions are pure functions of table version, ledger rows at the pinned frontier and addresses
(the purity invariant of decision cores).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Worked stratum (k = 1, margin m = 0.05, calibrated with q̂₂ = 0.03): design A (0.61, 0.70, 0.72; J∞ = 0.726)
      is `saturated`; design B (0.55, 0.68, 0.76; J∞ = 0.888; 0.888 − 0.76 = 0.128 > 0.05) is
      `controller-limited`; B is never described as inferior although it trails A at b₀ by 0.06.
- [ ] Threshold design (0.20, 0.20, 0.80): refused at b₁ (Δ₁ = 0), escalated to b₂, labelled `curve-unfit`, and
      present in the survivor set by its measured 0.80.
- [ ] Uncalibrated stratum: the decision equals the fixed table (top ⌈n/3⌉ move) and the envelope states the
      reduction.
- [ ] Calibrated stratum: exactly the designs with U_d ≥ S_k move; audit designs run all levels regardless.
- [ ] Shadow mode: both sets are recorded for 100% of decisions and the applied set is the fixed table's.
- [ ] Each of the four triggers, forced, yields the fixed-table action and a record naming it.
- [ ] No design is removed from the candidate set by this rule (property test over random strata); no advisory
      value appears in a verdict record.
- [ ] Cost accounting reproduces 3.8 units for (f₁, f₂) = (0.3, 0.1) at r = 4, the ceiling 4.2, 4.1 units for the
      fixed table, and 0.65 GPU-minutes for the three-address escalation.
- [ ] Decisions are identical under 100 random completion orders of the level episodes.

`opt.cascade` and fail the schema) with the rule in shadow and asserts the
applied sets equal the fixed table's, the rule's sets (read from the shadow records) equal the expected escalated

through `frob cores show dc-02 --json`; then replays the same fixture with its core table setting `budget-escalation`
to `default` through `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- cores.replay --fixture
conformance/campaigns/budget-27-acting/ --schedule <file> --cores dc-02 --json` (the replay driver of N28c-06, a
dependency: a subcommand of the conformance crate's example target, the crate having no binary under ADR-GAUNT-01
of BD-025; the product verb has no replay or state option) and asserts the applied sets now equal
the rule's; and forces each of the four triggers with `--force-trigger dc-02:<trigger>`, asserting the fixed-table
action and a record naming the trigger. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/budget_curve_cascade/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N28c-37`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; per
stratum S_k, per design the levels run, U_d, the action and the flag; `metric` lines for f₁, f₂, mean cost against the
fixed table's and the ceiling; `assert` lines for "applied equals fixed table in shadow"; `digest` fields for the
decision records and the fixture manifest; on failure the stratum, the design and the store path; a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Worked values: J∞ 0.726 and 0.888; 1 + 0.3 × 4 + 0.1 × 16 = 3.8; 16/3.8 = 4.2; 0.65 GPU-minutes.
- Negative controls that must fail: a rule that discards a design whose upper end is below S_k (the planted
  threshold design is lost); a rule that describes a `controller-limited` design as inferior in its stage output;
  a rule that cancels an audit design's higher levels; a rule that acts while in shadow.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts 1 and 36; both hosts.

## Considerations

- The interval used before three budgets exist is a design completion: the plan defines escalation in terms of
  the interval for J∞ and gives a cost illustration in which most designs stop at b₀, which requires an interval
  at b₀. The prefix predictors above are the minimal consistent reading, each calibrated like every other
  margin; the coverage notes flag this for the owner.
- The label `controller-limited` is also produced by mission queries from utility brackets (a sound upper bound
  against achieved performance). The two are different evidence for the same message; where both exist the
  report prints both sources, and neither is described as inferior.
- KILL-BUDGETCURVE: if at the oracle experiment regret at equal compute falls by less than 30%, or J∞ is further
  from the oracle than the top-budget score for more than half the designs, the rule's record is set to
  `fallback-only`: fixed-budget scores, the saturation index as a description, no escalation.
- The sampling controller is a screening scorer only; its episodes are never acceptance evidence, whatever the
  budget level.
