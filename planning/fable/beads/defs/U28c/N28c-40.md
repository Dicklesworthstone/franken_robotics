## Background

Controller-budget curves (Frontier) let the design studio rank robot designs by an estimate of what each could do
with more controller effort instead of by its score under one cheap controller. The mechanism scores every
candidate at a base controller budget, escalates to higher budgets only the designs whose estimated ceiling J∞
(with its calibrated interval) could still reach the top of their stratum, and flags designs whose ceiling
exceeds their measured score by more than the decision margin as `controller-limited`. The known-answer gate at
M3 shows the arithmetic is right. This bead is the gate that asks whether the mechanism is worth having.

Gate GATE-ATEL-18 (claim kind: Design comparison; first milestone M4): on 30 designs in three classes with an
oracle (a specialist per design over five seeds, and the sampling controller at 8,192 samples): top-3 regret at
equal total compute at most half that of ranking at the base budget; J∞ closer to the oracle than J(b_max) for at
least half the designs. The ceiling was stated before any test: the rule cannot rank better than running every
design at the top budget; its benefit is the compute between that and the escalation rule (at most 4.2 times in
the plan's illustration), and it is nothing when no two curves cross. Kill gate KILL-BUDGETCURVE reads this
experiment: it fires if regret at equal compute falls by less than 30%, or if J∞ is further from the oracle than
J(b_max) for more than half the designs; the fallback is fixed-budget scores with the saturation index printed as
a description and no escalation rule.

As a method gate it must have the form every such gate has: competing arms, a potency control the method must
catch and a clean case it must pass, a paired lower bound, a ceiling computed first, and a fallback named in
advance. Reported improvements from mechanisms like this one are easy to manufacture against a weak baseline;
the base-budget arm is therefore given the same total compute.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol, the oracle, the arms, the statistics, the controls, the run, the gate record.
Out: the mechanism (its beads); the kill decision (N28c-41); the pilot (N28c-32).

## Technical approach

1. **Registered before the first run** (protocol hash in the ledger precedes any episode): the six parts below,
   the design list, the addresses, the seeds, the decision statistics.
2. **Designs.** 30 designs in three morphology classes, 10 per class, drawn by a keyed stream from the design
   corpus's classes (a parametric quadruped class; a wheeled or hybrid class; a third class chosen for expected
   difficulty, since classes expected to be hard are sampled on purpose); the draw precedes any score.
3. **Oracle.** For each design d: (a) a specialist policy trained for that design alone, five seeds, scored on
   the experiment's 32 world addresses at class D2; (b) the sampling controller at N_s = 8,192 on the same
   addresses. The oracle score J*_d is the larger of the five-seed mean of (a) and the score of (b); both are
   recorded and the choice per design is reported. The oracle is computed and sealed (hash in the ledger) before
   any arm's ranking is read. The oracle's episodes (32 per design at N_s = 8,192) exceed the sampling
   controller's screening allowance of three episodes per design. They run as `control.mpc` requests that name the
   registered experiment, `frob control mpc <design> --task flat-velocity --planner <N_s = 8,192 setting>
   --protocol <experiment id> --key oracle-<design>-$FROB_E2E_SEED --json` (N22a-24 rule 1: the product verb is
   not replaced; it admits the count the protocol declares, records the exception on each result and refuses the
   episode past it with `mpc-episode-cap`). The experiment's registration in the method-experiment register
   (N38a-07) declares the sampling controller's episode count per design for every use it makes of the
   controller: the oracle's 32, the three-level sweep on the 32 addresses of step 4 (counted by address, N28c-33),
   and arm B's further b₀ addresses. The episodes keep the labels `screening: sampling-control` and
   `model-privileged`, and are never acceptance evidence.
4. **Arms**, each producing a ranked top-3 per class (M, F and T on the 32 addresses; B on those and further
   addresses, below). Every design is run at all three levels on the 32 addresses (clause 2 needs every design's
   full curve, and arm T's b₂ scores are among them); arms M and F are then replayed over that store through
   the replay driver of N28c-06 (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- cores.replay`: a
   subcommand of the conformance crate's example target, the crate having no binary under ADR-GAUNT-01 of
   BD-025) with the core table's `budget-escalation` artifact acting (M) or the fixed table (F), each arm's compute being counted from the levels it requested. Decisions are pure functions of the
   rows at the pinned frontier (N28c-37), so the replay selects the same levels as a live run would, and the rule
   can act here although it ships in shadow:
   - M (the mechanism): the escalation rule with its calibrated intervals, on the default ladder
     N_s ∈ {64, 256, 1,024}; total compute C_M measured in model steps.
   - B (base-budget ranking at equal total compute): every design at b₀ only, with the compute C_M spent on more
     b₀ episodes (more addresses drawn from the same address family); ranking by mean b₀ score.
   - F (the fallback): fixed halving across budget levels (top third moves up), at its own compute, reported.
   - T (the ceiling arm): every design at b₂ on the 32 addresses; ranking by b₂ score; its compute is 16 units of
     b₀ per design and address. Not a competitor; it bounds what M could achieve.
5. **Statistic 1: top-3 regret.** For an arm X and class g, regret R_X,g = (mean J* of the oracle's top 3 in g) −
   (mean J* of the 3 designs X ranks first in g). The experiment is replicated over R = 20 keyed resamples of the
   address set (each arm re-ranks on each resample; the oracle stays fixed), giving paired regrets
   (R_M, R_B) per class and resample. Decision quantity: the pooled ratio of mean regrets R_M/R_B with a
   one-sided 95% upper confidence bound from the paired resamples (the paired-bound routine of the performance
   harness, applied to the ratio; ten pairs of constant ratio r must return r). Gate clause 1 passes when the
   upper bound is at most 0.5. If R_B is zero in every resample (the base budget already finds the oracle's top
   3), the clause is recorded as "no room": the mechanism has nothing to gain, which counts toward the kill
   trigger "regret falls by less than 30%".
6. **Statistic 2: closeness to the oracle.** For each design with a fitted curve (the three-point estimate from
   its full curve on the 32 addresses, so b_max = b₂ for every design), compare |J_d∞ − J*_d| with
   |J_d(b₂) − J*_d|. Gate clause 2 passes when J∞ is strictly closer for at least half of the designs (at least
   15 of 30; designs with refused fits count as "not closer").
7. **Controls.**
   - Potency: three synthetic designs are added to one class with planted curves that cross real ones (drawn
     from the law with a large gap a and a low base score); M must place at least one of them in its top 3 where
     the planted oracle says it belongs, and B must not. If M misses them, the experiment cannot see the effect
     it claims.
   - A/A: arm M against arm M with a different resample seed must give a regret ratio whose interval contains 1
     and does not clear 1.02 in M's favour.
   - Leak check: the escalation rule's inputs are verified to contain no oracle score (the ledger frontier the
     rule reads precedes the oracle rows, or the oracle rows are in a namespace it cannot read).
8. **Ceiling, computed before the run from arm T's design:** regret of T against the oracle; M cannot be expected
   to beat it. If T's own regret is already at least half of B's, clause 1 is unreachable and the gate is
   recorded as failed on ceiling without interpreting M.
9. **Compute accounting.** Model steps and GPU-seconds per arm; shares f₁ and f₂ of designs escalated; mean cost
   per design in units of b₀ against 16; the realized ratio against the ceiling 16/(1 + 4f₁ + 16f₂).
10. **Gate run and record (this bead is GATE-ATEL-18's one recorder).** The statistics of steps 5, 6, 8 and 9 and
    the controls of step 7 are the gate's entry in the tier its record's `tier` field names, a function of the
    sealed experiment store (the same library `--phase statistics` calls), with the potency, A/A and leak
    controls behind `gauntlet-mutations`. The script runs it with `e2e_tier <tier> GATE-ATEL-18 --mutations`,
    asserts from the receipt the ratio and its bound, the clause-2 count, every control `caught` and the ceiling,
    and writes no row. A session that did not implement the mechanism enters the gate from that receipt with
    `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-ATEL-18 --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19),
    with the regret ratio and its bound, the count for clause 2, both controls, the ceiling, compute per arm, the
    disclosure of training settings for specialists and the claim identifiers. KILL-BUDGETCURVE's decision
    (N28c-41) reads this record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, design list, addresses, seeds and statistics are registered before the first episode; the
      oracle is sealed before any arm's ranking is read.
- [ ] Arms M, B, F and T are run on the stated addresses with compute recorded; arm B's compute equals arm M's
      within 2%.
- [ ] Clause 1 measured: the pooled regret ratio R_M/R_B with its one-sided 95% upper bound, against 0.5.
- [ ] Clause 2 measured: the number of designs (of 30) for which J∞ is closer to the oracle than J(b₂), against
      15.
- [ ] The potency control is detected by M and not by B; the A/A arm does not clear 1.02; the leak check passes.
- [ ] The ceiling from arm T is recorded before M's result is read.
- [ ] GATE-ATEL-18 is entered with `record_gate` from the receipt of the gate's tier run, as `pass` or `fail`
      with measured values, not targets, by a session that did not implement the mechanism; a `killed` status
      after the kill decision is derived by N40-03's propagation, never written here.
- [ ] Every `control.mpc` request of the oracle names the experiment with `--protocol` and carries a distinct key;
      a 33rd oracle episode for one design is refused with `mpc-episode-cap`.
- [ ] If the compute envelope was not approved, the reduced design count and the wider interval are stated in
      the row.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gate_atel_18.rs` (the analysis code on fixtures): `top3_regret_definition`
(oracle scores (0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5); an arm selecting the designs scored 0.9, 0.7 and 0.5 has regret
0.8 − 0.7 = 0.1); `paired_ratio_bound_constant` (ten pairs at ratio 0.4 → bound 0.4); `no_room_case` (R_B = 0 in
all resamples → recorded "no room"); `closeness_count` (refused fits counted as not closer);
`equal_compute_check` (2% tolerance); `ceiling_unreachable_detected`; `oracle_is_max_of_two` and its per-design
report; `leak_check_rejects_oracle_in_inputs`; `decision_table` (ratio bound 0.50 passes clause 1, 0.51 fails;
14 of 30 fails clause 2, 15 passes).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_atel_18.sh`: registers the protocol (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness --
atelier.budget-curve-oracle --phase register --json`; this bead adds the subcommand `atelier.budget-curve-oracle`
with its phases `register`, `oracle`, `arm-b` and `statistics` to the example target), trains the specialists (`frob train start <design> --task flat-velocity --algo ppo --key …
--json` per design and seed, reusing matching specialists), runs the 8,192-sample oracle as the `control.mpc`
requests of step 3 (`--protocol <experiment id>`, a key per design) and seals it (`--phase oracle`), runs every
design at the three levels on the 32 addresses (`frob control mpc <design> --task flat-velocity --ladder
mpc-sample.ns-x4 --levels all --episodes 32 --protocol <experiment id> --key sweep-<design>-$FROB_E2E_SEED
--json`, N28c-33's sweep, memoized) and arm B's further b₀ addresses (`--phase arm-b`, the same verb at `--level 0`
under the protocol), replays arms M and F through `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- cores.replay --fixture
<experiment store> --schedule <file> --cores dc-02 --json` with the acting and fixed tables, calls `--phase
statistics` for the ratios, counts, controls and the leak check, and ends with the gate's tier run of step 10. It
sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/gate_atel_18/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N28c-40`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block;
`metric` lines for regrets per class and resample, the pooled ratio and its bound against 0.5, the closeness count
against 15, compute per arm with arm B's against arm M's (2%), escalation shares, the ceiling; `assert` lines for
the controls and the leak check; `digest` fields for the oracle seal, the protocol and the replay records; on
failure the first violated registration order or the arm whose compute is out of tolerance; the `cmd` line of
the tier run with its `receipt` hash; a `summary` with `gates: ["GATE-ATEL-18"]` and the receipt hash. The script
exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the result.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Harness known answers: constant-ratio pairs return the ratio; the regret fixture above.
- Negative controls: the potency designs (must be found by M); the A/A arm; a planted leak (the rule given
  oracle scores) must be rejected by the leak check; an arm B given less compute than M must fail the
  equal-compute assertion.
- Determinism lanes: every arm is class D2 on one backend and reproduces twice with identical rankings; resamples
  are keyed; specialist training runs under the labelled training profile and only its evaluations (at D2) enter
  any statistic.

## Considerations

- The gate threshold (regret at most half) and the kill trigger (regret falls by less than 30%) leave a band: an
  improvement between 30% and 50% fails the gate without firing the kill gate. In that band one repair cycle is
  allowed for implementation defects found by the controls, the result is recorded as `fail`, and the rule stays
  in shadow; the kill decision bead states this explicitly.
- Specialists are imperfect oracles (five seeds, a finite training budget); the 8,192-sample planner covers
  bodies for which training is hard, at the price of being model-privileged. Both scores are reported so a reader
  can see which one set each oracle value.
- The sampling controller's episodes are screening evidence only; nothing in this experiment is acceptance
  evidence for any design.
- Cost is dominated by the 150 specialist trainings; reuse of specialists from the family-policy and
  rank-agreement experiments is allowed when the designs coincide and their identities match.
