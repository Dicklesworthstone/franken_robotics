## Background

Controller-budget curves are a Frontier mechanism of the design studio. Each design is scored at three controller
budgets on shared world addresses; a saturating law extrapolates the score at unlimited controller effort (J∞);
an escalation rule spends higher budgets only on designs whose ceiling could still reach the top of their
stratum; designs whose ceiling exceeds their measured score by more than the decision margin are flagged
`controller-limited`. The aim is to avoid discarding a good body because the cheap controller could not drive it.
The ceiling of the benefit was written down first: the rule cannot rank better than running every design at the
top budget, and it is nothing when no two curves cross.

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate named in advance. KILL-BUDGETCURVE. Trigger: the pilot shows no
crossing; or at gate GATE-ATEL-18 regret at equal compute falls by less than 30%; or J∞ is further from the
oracle than J(b_max) for more than half the designs. Fallback: fixed-budget scores, with the saturation index
printed as a description and no escalation rule. A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is
never quietly re-scoped; a change to trigger, threshold or fallback is a dated amendment made before the
measurement it governs; its decision is recorded as an ADR; a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan. This bead is the decision, in two readings: the pilot's at M3 (which decides
whether the mechanism is built at all) and the oracle experiment's at M4 (which decides whether it stays). The
first reading is made at M3 by the pilot bead (N28c-32: one `[[pilot.result]]` appended to the pilot record
`PILOT-BUDGETCURVE-01`, which is the funding decision the mechanism beads wait on, and its dated note
`docs/adr/ADR-PILOT-BUDGETCURVE-01.md`). The row is `pilot-before-funding` and decided at M4 (N40-29), and the
pilot reading is an input to that decision, not a second decision point (N28c-25): this bead recomputes the first
reading from the pilot record as a cross-check, makes the second reading, and enters the row's one decision
entry.

## Scope

In: the recomputation of the first reading, the second reading, the decision rule, the M4 ADR, what changes
downstream on each branch, the fail-closed posture until decided.
Out: running the pilot and appending the first reading with its note (N28c-32); the oracle experiment (N28c-40);
the mechanism beads. If the first reading met the trigger, the oracle experiment is closed as not built, this bead
becomes ready with it, and it enters the decision `fired` on the first trigger with the pilot run's receipt, the
second reading stated in the ADR as `not run: pilot falsifier held at M3`, citing the pilot note.

## Technical approach

1. **First reading, M3: the pilot (appended to the pilot record by N28c-32; recomputed here by the same rule).** Input: the pilot record (20 variants from two templates at sample counts
   32, 128 and 512 on shared addresses). Trigger clause 1 is met when the crossing analysis finds zero crossing
   pairs among the 190 design pairs (a pair crosses when the paired contrast at the base budget and at the top
   budget have opposite signs, each significant at the level corrected for 190 pairs), provided the pilot's own
   potency control was detected (otherwise the pilot is void and is repeated once, not read as "no crossing").
   - Met → the pilot's verdict is `falsifier-holds` and no mechanism bead is fundable from M3 (N38a-09). The
     mechanism beads (budget coordinate, rows, fit, interval, escalation, verbs, their end-to-end script and the
     oracle experiment) are closed as not built, citing the pilot. The fixed-budget screening rung stands as it
     is. This bead's M4 entry is `fired` on this clause.
   - Not met → the verdict is `falsifier-rejected` and the mechanism is funded; the number of crossing pairs is
     carried into the M4 ADR as "first reading: not met".
   A side condition from the pilot: if more than a fifth of the curves were refused, the saturating law is wrong
   for the sampling controller; the mechanism is then funded only for controller kinds whose refused share is at
   most a fifth, which may be none.
2. **Second reading, M4: the oracle experiment** (30 designs in three classes; oracle: a specialist per design
   over five seeds and the sampling controller at 8,192 samples). Let R_M and R_B be the top-3 regrets of the
   mechanism and of base-budget ranking at equal total compute, pooled over classes and resamples, and let
   N_closer be the number of designs for which J∞ is closer to the oracle than the top-budget score.
   - Trigger clause 2: the regret reduction 1 − R_M/R_B is less than 0.30 (judged on the one-sided 95% lower
     confidence bound of the reduction being below 0.30, that is, the mechanism has not been shown to reduce
     regret by 30%; a "no room" result, R_B = 0, counts as less than 30%).
   - Trigger clause 3: N_closer < 15 of 30 is not enough to fire; the clause fires when J∞ is *further* from the
     oracle for more than half the designs, that is when at least 16 of 30 have |J∞ − J*| > |J(b₂) − J*|
     (designs with refused fits are counted in neither group for this clause and are reported).
   - Either clause met → outcome `fired`: the fallback is in force, and the `killed: fallback adopted` status of
     the rows it replaces is derived by N40-03's propagation, never written.
   - Neither met and GATE-ATEL-18 passed (regret at most half; J∞ closer for at least half) → outcome
     `not-fired`; the mechanism may move from `shadow` to `canary` under the adoption rules.
   - Neither met but GATE-ATEL-18 failed (a regret reduction between 30% and 50%, or closeness between the two
     thresholds) → outcome `not-fired` (the trigger was evaluated and is not met), with the band stated in the
     ADR: the rule stays in `shadow`, one repair cycle for implementation defects is allowed, GATE-ATEL-18's record
     (entered by N28c-40) is `fail`, and it is not silently promoted: leaving `shadow` needs that gate re-measured
     and re-entered `pass` by its recorder, and a re-measurement that meets a trigger clause re-opens this
     decision (N40-03 rule 6, `reading = "reopened"`).
3. **What changes downstream if it fires at M4.**
   - The escalation artifact's record is set to `fallback-only`: cheap rungs run at one fixed budget; advancement
     follows the ladder's own rule.
   - `eval.compare --budget-curve` remains as a descriptive view: scores by budget and the saturation index
     printed as a description; no J∞-driven allocation; the flag `controller-limited` is no longer used by the
     cascade (the label can still arise from mission-query utility brackets, which are independent evidence).
   - Reports stop printing budget-curve flags beside ranks; the rejected-methods register gains an entry with
     the measured numbers and the reopen condition (new evidence of crossing curves at a larger effect, with a
     new pilot).
   - The budget coordinate on controller identities and the budget rows stay (they are inert data structures and
     serve the descriptive view); the calibration records for J∞ are no longer maintained.
4. **Until decided (fail-closed).** Before the pilot: nothing of the mechanism is built. Between M3 and M4: the
   rule runs in `shadow` only; the fixed table acts; no report states a saving from escalation.
5. **Record.** The row's one decision entry, in N40-03's form `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session,
   receipts }`: outcome `fired` or `not-fired`; `receipts` the pilot run's receipt and the receipt of
   GATE-ATEL-18's record (only the pilot's when the first trigger was met); `adr` the path
   `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BUDGETCURVE-M4.md` (N40-03 rule 6; `docs/delivery/adr/` is retired), the ADR carrying N40-03's
   required fields and: the pilot protocol hash and result, citing the pilot note; the oracle experiment's
   protocol hash, R_M, R_B, the reduction with its bound, N_closer and the "further" count, the controls, the
   ceiling; the reading of each clause; the downstream list applied. Entered by a session that did not implement
   the mechanism; nothing else in the row or in any gate record is written by hand. This bead also writes the
   owner declaration of KILL-BUDGETCURVE (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` `pilot-before-funding`,
   `reads`), which N28c-25 leaves to the deciding bead, carrying the ledger's text.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The first reading, as appended to the pilot record at M3 by N28c-32, is recomputed from that record and
      matches (crossing pairs, potency control, refused share, verdict); a mismatch stops the decision and is
      reported.
- [ ] Second reading recorded at M4 with the regret reduction and its bound against 0.30, the "further from the
      oracle" count against 16 of 30, and the state of GATE-ATEL-18.
- [ ] The decision entry loads (no `decision-adr-path`, no `decision-entry-duplicate`) with outcome `not-fired`
      (mechanism adopted to `canary` or kept in `shadow` with the reason) or `fired` (the fallback in force, the
      `killed` statuses derived); its ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BUDGETCURVE-M4.md` holds both readings.
- [ ] If fired: the escalation record is `fallback-only`, the cascade no longer consumes the flag, the verb
      prints the descriptive form, and the rejected-methods register has the entry.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, thresholds or fallback is dated after the measurement it governs.
- [ ] The owner declaration of KILL-BUDGETCURVE agrees with the ledger row (N40-30's comparison passes).
- [ ] The decision table below is implemented as a test and matches the ADR.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_budgetcurve.rs`: `first_reading_table` (0 crossings with potency detected →
`falsifier-holds`, M4 outcome `fired`; 0 crossings with potency missed → void, repeat; 3 crossings → not fired; 5 of 20 refused → law wrong for
this controller); `second_reading_table` (reduction lower bound 0.29 → fired; 0.30 → not fired by clause 2;
16 further → fired; 15 further → not fired; reduction 0.40 with the gate failed → stays in shadow, gate `fail`);
`no_room_counts_as_below_30`; `refused_fits_in_neither_group`; `amendment_date_check`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_budgetcurve.sh`: reads the pilot record and its note and, at M4, the oracle experiment's
records from the ledger, recomputes both readings with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness --
atelier.kill-budgetcurve --json` (asserting the first equals the recorded one; the conformance crate has no binary,
ADR-GAUNT-01 of BD-025, and the studio's parameterized harnesses are subcommands of its example target
`atelier_harness`, to which this bead adds `atelier.kill-budgetcurve`), and prints the decision; when the decision is "fired" it asserts
the downstream state through real verbs (`frob cores show dc-02 --json` shows the escalation artifact
`fallback-only`; `frob eval compare d:A d:B --task flat-velocity --budget-curve --key kbc-desc-$FROB_E2E_SEED
--json`, with the worked store restored into the run's state root `FROB_STATE`, prints the descriptive form)
and replays the 27-design fixture through `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example atelier_harness -- cores.replay
--fixture conformance/campaigns/budget-27/ --schedule <file> --cores dc-02 --json` (the replay driver of N28c-06;
the product verb has no replay option),
asserting no escalation; it also runs the decision on planted records: a pilot with the potency pair missed (must
read `void`), point estimates without the bound (rejected), an ADR dated before the oracle seal (ordering check
fails). It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/kill_budgetcurve/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N28c-41`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code,
wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `metric` lines carry crossing pairs, refused share, regrets, the
reduction and its bound against 0.30, the "further" count against 16 of 30; the decision per reading; `digest`
fields carry the protocol and ADR digests; on failure the record path and the first mismatching quantity; a
`summary` with `gates: ["KILL-BUDGETCURVE"]`. It exits zero when the readings are complete, whatever the
decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Decision-table known answers as in the unit tests; the illustrative ceiling 16/3.8 = 4.2.
- Negative controls: a pilot whose potency pair was not detected must not be read as "no crossing"; a decision
  taken from point estimates without the bound must be rejected by the check that the recorded quantity is a
  bound; an ADR dated before the oracle was sealed must fail the ordering check.
- Determinism: both readings are pure functions of recorded ledger rows.

## Considerations

- The first reading can save the whole mechanism's cost, which is why the mechanism beads depend on the pilot:
  if rankings do not depend on controller budget on the pilot population, nothing is built, and the plan says
  the fixed-budget screening rung is the better for it.
- The pilot uses the sampling controller on two templates; a "not fired" first reading does not show that
  learned-policy budgets behave the same way, and the learned-policy ladder still lacks a usable effort scale;
  the ADR records which controller kinds the decision covers.
- Evidence scope: both readings are about the populations tested. The reopen path after a kill is the
  rejected-methods procedure (new evidence, a new pilot, an ADR), not a quiet retry.
