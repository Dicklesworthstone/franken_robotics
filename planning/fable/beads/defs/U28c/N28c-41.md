## Background

Controller-budget curves are a Frontier mechanism of the design studio. Each design is scored at three controller
budgets on shared world addresses; a saturating law extrapolates the score at unlimited controller effort (J∞);
an escalation rule spends higher budgets only on designs whose ceiling could still reach the top of their
stratum; designs whose ceiling exceeds their measured score by more than the decision margin are flagged
`controller-limited`. The aim is to avoid discarding a good body because the cheap controller could not drive it.
The ceiling of the benefit was written down first: the rule cannot rank better than running every design at the
top budget, and it is nothing when no two curves cross.

Every Frontier mechanism has a kill gate named in advance. KILL-BUDGETCURVE. Trigger: the pilot shows no
crossing; or at gate GATE-ATEL-18 regret at equal compute falls by less than 30%; or J∞ is further from the
oracle than J(b_max) for more than half the designs. Fallback: fixed-budget scores, with the saturation index
printed as a description and no escalation rule. A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is
never quietly re-scoped; a change to trigger, threshold or fallback is a dated amendment made before the
measurement it governs; its decision is recorded as an ADR; a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan. This bead is the decision, in two readings: the pilot's at M3 (which decides
whether the mechanism is built at all) and the oracle experiment's at M4 (which decides whether it stays).

## Scope

In: the two readings, the decision rule, the ADR, what changes downstream on each branch, the fail-closed
posture until decided.
Out: running the pilot (N28c-32) and the oracle experiment (N28c-40); the mechanism beads.

## Technical approach

1. **First reading, M3: the pilot.** Input: the pilot record (20 variants from two templates at sample counts
   32, 128 and 512 on shared addresses). Trigger clause 1 is met when the crossing analysis finds zero crossing
   pairs among the 190 design pairs (a pair crosses when the paired contrast at the base budget and at the top
   budget have opposite signs, each significant at the level corrected for 190 pairs), provided the pilot's own
   potency control was detected (otherwise the pilot is void and is repeated once, not read as "no crossing").
   - Met → status `killed: fallback adopted` at M3. The mechanism beads (budget coordinate, rows, fit, interval,
     escalation, verbs, their end-to-end script and the oracle experiment) are closed as not built, citing the
     pilot. The fixed-budget screening rung stands as it is.
   - Not met → the mechanism is funded; record "first reading: not fired" with the number of crossing pairs.
   A side condition from the pilot: if more than a fifth of the curves were refused, the saturating law is wrong
   for the sampling controller; the mechanism is then funded only for controller kinds whose refused share is at
   most a fifth, which may be none.
2. **Second reading, M4: the oracle experiment** (30 designs in three classes; oracle: a specialist per design
   over five seeds and the sampling controller at 8,192 samples). Let R_M and R_B be the top-3 regrets of the
   mechanism and of base-budget ranking at equal total compute, pooled over classes and resamples, and let
   N_closer be the number of designs for which J∞ is closer to the oracle than the top-budget score.
   - Trigger clause 2: the regret reduction 1 − R_M/R_B is less than 0.30 (judged on the one-sided 95% lower
     confidence bound of the reduction being below 0.30, that is, the mechanism has not been shown to reduce
     regret by 30%; a "no room" result, R_B = 0, counts as less than 30%).
   - Trigger clause 3: N_closer < 15 of 30 is not enough to fire; the clause fires when J∞ is *further* from the
     oracle for more than half the designs, that is when at least 16 of 30 have |J∞ − J*| > |J(b₂) − J*|
     (designs with refused fits are counted in neither group for this clause and are reported).
   - Either clause met → status `killed: fallback adopted`.
   - Neither met and GATE-ATEL-18 passed (regret at most half; J∞ closer for at least half) → the mechanism may
     move from `shadow` to `canary` under the adoption rules.
   - Neither met but GATE-ATEL-18 failed (a regret reduction between 30% and 50%, or closeness between the two
     thresholds): the rule stays in `shadow`, one repair cycle for implementation defects is allowed, the gate
     row is `fail`, and this state is re-read at the next milestone exit; it is not silently promoted.
3. **What changes downstream if it fires at M4.**
   - The escalation artifact's record is set to `fallback-only`: cheap rungs run at one fixed budget; advancement
     follows the ladder's own rule.
   - `eval.compare --budget-curve` remains as a descriptive view: scores by budget and the saturation index
     printed as a description; no J∞-driven allocation; the flag `controller-limited` is no longer used by the
     cascade (the label can still arise from mission-query utility brackets, which are independent evidence).
   - Reports stop printing budget-curve flags beside ranks; the rejected-methods register gains an entry with
     the measured numbers and the reopen condition (new evidence of crossing curves at a larger effect, with a
     new pilot).
   - The budget coordinate on controller identities and the budget rows stay (they are inert data structures and
     serve the descriptive view); the calibration records for J∞ are no longer maintained.
4. **Until decided (fail-closed).** Before the pilot: nothing of the mechanism is built. Between M3 and M4: the
   rule runs in `shadow` only; the fixed table acts; no report states a saving from escalation.
5. **Record.** ADR `docs/adr/KILL-BUDGETCURVE.md` with: the pilot protocol hash and result; the oracle
   experiment's protocol hash, R_M, R_B, the reduction with its bound, N_closer and the "further" count, the
   controls, the ceiling; the decision at each reading; the downstream list applied. Gate registry row for
   KILL-BUDGETCURVE updated at each reading; recorded by a session that did not implement the mechanism.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] First reading recorded at M3 with the number of crossing pairs, the potency control's result and the
      refused share; the mechanism beads are released or closed accordingly, each closure citing the pilot.
- [ ] Second reading recorded at M4 with the regret reduction and its bound against 0.30, the "further from the
      oracle" count against 16 of 30, and the state of GATE-ATEL-18.
- [ ] The ADR exists with both readings; the gate row's status is one of: not fired (mechanism adopted to
      `canary` or kept in `shadow` with the reason), or `killed: fallback adopted`.
- [ ] If fired: the escalation record is `fallback-only`, the cascade no longer consumes the flag, the verb
      prints the descriptive form, and the rejected-methods register has the entry.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, thresholds or fallback is dated after the measurement it governs.
- [ ] The decision table below is implemented as a test and matches the ADR.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_budgetcurve.rs`: `first_reading_table` (0 crossings with potency detected →
killed; 0 crossings with potency missed → void, repeat; 3 crossings → not fired; 5 of 20 refused → law wrong for
this controller); `second_reading_table` (reduction lower bound 0.29 → fired; 0.30 → not fired by clause 2;
16 further → fired; 15 further → not fired; reduction 0.40 with the gate failed → stays in shadow, gate `fail`);
`no_room_counts_as_below_30`; `refused_fits_in_neither_group`; `amendment_date_check`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_budgetcurve.sh`: reads the pilot record and, at M4, the oracle experiment's records from the
ledger, recomputes both readings with `frob-conformance run atelier.kill-budgetcurve --json`, and prints the
decision; when the decision is "fired" it asserts the downstream state through real verbs
(`frob cores show dc-02 --json` shows the escalation artifact `fallback-only`; `frob eval compare
--budget-curve --json` prints the descriptive form; `frob opt cascade --replay` on the 27-design fixture applies
no escalation). Sources the STD-E2E helper; logs to `target/e2e-logs/kill_budgetcurve/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`:
crossing pairs, refused share, regrets, the reduction and its bound, the counts, the decision per reading, the
protocol digests, and a `summary` with `gates: ["KILL-BUDGETCURVE"]`. It exits zero when the readings are
complete, whatever the decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Decision-table known answers as in the unit tests; the illustrative ceiling 16/3.8 = 4.2.
- Negative controls: a pilot whose potency pair was not detected must not be read as "no crossing"; a decision
  taken from point estimates without the bound must be rejected by the check that the recorded quantity is a
  bound; an ADR dated before the oracle was sealed must fail the ordering check.
- Determinism: both readings are pure functions of recorded ledger rows.

## Considerations

- The first reading can save the whole mechanism's cost, which is why the mechanism beads depend on the pilot:
  if rankings do not depend on controller budget on the pilot population, nothing is built, and the plan says
  the fixed-budget screening rung is the better for it.
- The pilot uses the sampling controller on two templates; a "not fired" first reading does not show that
  learned-policy budgets behave the same way, and the learned-policy ladder still lacks a usable effort scale;
  the ADR records which controller kinds the decision covers.
- Evidence scope: both readings are about the populations tested. The reopen path after a kill is the
  rejected-methods procedure (new evidence, a new pilot, an ADR), not a quiet retry.
