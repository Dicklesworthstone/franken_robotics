## Background

The design studio's chapter closes its mechanisms with a table of known answers and negative controls: for each
subject (an analysis, the fixed-n rule, a decision core, deduplication, revalidation, bisect) one case whose truth
is known by derivation or exact enumeration, and one negative control that must fail. A known-answer test shows
that the implementation recovers an analytic or synthetic truth; the negative control shows that the test can
fail (a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing). The mechanism beads test their own rows. The battery
carries the table as a whole: every row is a named fixture with a pinned expected value and tolerance, its
negative control is attached to the negative-control registry, all rows run in one suite, and one report shows,
per row, the value, the tolerance and whether the control was caught. That report is how a milestone exit review
sees at a glance that no row of the table was dropped.

Rows fall due at three milestones: the static analyses and the fixed-n rule at M1; decision cores DC-01, DC-09 and
DC-10, the dependency grades of revalidation, A-09, A-17, A-18 and deduplication at M3; the rest at M4. The
battery is therefore built in three stages, each landing with the rows due at its milestone, so that the M1 and
M3 exits each have the report rather than waiting for M4. This bead is the first stage: the suite, the staging
mechanism, the report command, the end-to-end script, and the nine rows due at M1. N28cp1-02 adds the M3 rows and
N28c-24 the M4 rows and the whole-table check. The three gates read here at M1 are GATE-ATEL-02 (A-01 to A-14
known answers), GATE-ATEL-05 (A-15 to A-26 known answers and negative controls; its M1 part is A-20 to A-23) and
GATE-ATEL-06 (the fixed-n planner's enumeration at n = 64 and n = 86). The battery measures none of these gates by
itself; their rows are recorded by the owning beads, and the battery shows that every row of the chapter's table
is present, passing and controlled. Tier: Solid [S].

## Technical approach

1. **Row record.** Each row is a struct `{ ka, subject, case, expected, tolerance, tier, control, gate, due,
   owner }` loaded from `conformance/ka/atelier/KA-NNN.toml`; expected values are exact rationals or integers
   where the tolerance is exact, and decimal strings with a stated number of digits where the tolerance is "to
   the printed digits". The suite calls the real component through its public surface; no value is computed by
   the battery from the component under test.
2. **Rows of this stage.**

   | KA | Subject | Known answer (expected) | Tolerance | Negative control that must fail | Owner |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | KA-680 | A-03 symmetry: a quadruped with one forward lidar | Four-element mechanical group; two-element observation-preserving subgroup | exact (group) | A policy tied under the mechanical group is refused | BD-102 |
   | KA-681 (A-07) | A-07: a mecanum base with misaligned rollers | A rank drop in the growth vector | exact (rank) | A curvature-only check passes the defective base | BD-106 |
   | KA-682 | A-08: the stance enumeration | 11,520 of 11,520 classifications agree with the exact classifier; equal-spring forces (175, 115, 85, 25) N | exact | The polygon-and-total-capacity screen accepts 1,596 rejected bodies | BD-100 |
   | KA-684 | A-20: an 8 kg body on a 12 mm rise against a 0.90 J budget | 8 × 9.81 × 0.012 = 0.94176 J excludes the passive crossing; 0.05 J of approach kinetic energy removes the exclusion | exact to the printed digits | An exclusion issued with approach energy unbound | BD-108 |
   | KA-685 | A-21: a packet at tick 1 serving decisions that commit at ticks 2 and 4; delays to ticks 2 and 3 | The answer changes only at the second delay and only for the first decision | exact | A check that compares content and ignores arrival | BD-108 |
   | KA-686 | A-22: 75 mm wheel, total reflected inertia 0.00107890625 kg·m², current target held for 10 ms | One-tick spectral radii 1.4820 with target-current feedforward and 0.9207 with measured-current feedforward | to the printed digits | The instantaneous-current model gives 2.6917 for both and misses the stable case | BD-108 |
   | KA-687 | A-23: two fingers on one bus | Zero schedules at a one-tick deadline; exactly two at two ticks | exact (count) | The additive-peak rule rejects the feasible two-tick design | BD-108 |
   | KA-693 | Fixed-n rule at n = 64, five-point margin | 2,145 count pairs: 521 promote, 521 inferior, 1,103 inconclusive, 0 ties; the first tie at n = 86 | exact | A study that declares "tie" reachable at n = 64 | BD-124 |
   | KA-694 | Zero discordances in 64 pairs | One-sided 95% bound 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.04573 | to the printed digits | Defined here (the registry states none): the exponent taken with n + 1, 1 − 0.05^(1/65) = 0.04504, a smaller bound than the truth, which the test must reject | BD-124 |

   The KA-694 control is on the unsafe side on purpose. The row is a bound on an error rate, so its record
   declares `safe = "higher"` (N38b-57), and the loader rejects as `cannot-fail` a control whose value lies on the
   safe side (N38b-28, rule 3): a two-sided bound, 1 − 0.025^(1/64) = 0.0560, is larger than the truth and could
   never be registered. The defect chosen is the one N38b-33 proposes for this row, so that bead's record links
   to this one and the row has one control.
3. **Mechanics.** Each row's test function is `ka_NNN_<subject>` and asserts the expected value within the
   tolerance; rows "to the printed digits" compare after rounding to the digits shown. Each row with a control has
   a companion `ka_NNN_control`. The control is the record seeded by the counterexample registry or by the row's
   control cell (N38b-05), and for a seeded row the battery plants nothing of its own: the planted variants of
   this stage's analysis rows are built by N28a-11, a dependency, under the plant names of N28-CX
   (`a08-polygon-screen` for KA-682, `a20-no-approach-energy` for KA-684, `a21-content-only` for KA-685,
   `a22-instantaneous-current` for KA-686, `a23-additive-peak` for KA-687). A control that is a wrong formula or
   implementation choice is a mutation control in the control runner's form (N38b-04): a
   `cfg(feature = "<feature>")` branch in the owning crate, the feature exactly as the record writes it (the plant
   name for a seeded control; `nc-<nnnn>` for one this bead registers directly, which is KA-694's), built one per
   build with `--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>`; its companion is compiled only under that
   feature and asserts that the row's test fails on that build. Any other control is a planted input that the
   companion submits, asserting the stated refusal (KA-680's tie request under the mechanical group, CX-305's
   planted input `a03-tie-under-mechanical-group`, refused with `FROB-REF-161` at M1 by the tie consumer of the
   symmetry interface, N10a-08, a dependency for that reason; KA-693's study that declares a tie reachable at
   n = 64). No planted defect sits behind a crate's `conformance` feature:
   that feature exposes test-support options, the control runner would report such a control `stale`, and the
   mutation guard would not keep it out of product builds. One control of this stage is not due at M1: KA-681's
   is CX-304's curvature-only mutation, which BD-243 builds at M3; at `FROB_MILESTONE=M1` the control runner
   reports it `not-yet-due`, the A-07 half of the row is checked alone, and stage M3 (N28cp1-02) catches it.
   Rows adopted from planning pilots (KA-682, KA-686) carry
   `to-be-rerun: true` until the pilot has been re-executed in this repository and its value re-pinned.
4. **Staging.** `FROB_MILESTONE` (M1, M3 or M4) selects the rows that are required. A required row that cannot run
   is a failure; a row not yet due prints `not run`, which is never a pass. The battery carries one fixture file
   per registry row from this stage on (with `due` set), so the rows of later stages exist as `not run` entries
   from M1 and a dropped row is visible at once.
5. **Report.** `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example registry_report -- ka --chapter 28 --json` lists, per
   row: measured value, expected, tolerance, pass, fail or `not run`, control caught or not, the gate, the owning
   bead and the claim identifier of the run. The conformance crate is a library with test targets and has no
   binary, verb or `xtask` subcommand (ADR-GAUNT-01, BD-025), so there is no `frob-conformance report …`
   command; what the crate allows beside its test targets are example targets. The chapter's registry reports
   are subcommands of one example, `crates/frob-conformance/examples/registry_report.rs`, which N28-CX (a
   dependency) creates with its `controls` subcommand; this bead adds `ka`, which reads the fixtures and the
   receipts of the last suite run and prints one JSON object. The generated-artifact check fails when a row of the known-answer registry (N28-KA) has no fixture,
   when a fixture's expected value differs from the registry's, or when a row due at the current milestone has no
   control (the program's no-control-no-pass rule).
6. **Independent arithmetic.** The arithmetic of the rows of this stage is recomputed inside the suite in exact
   arithmetic, independent of the component under test: 0.94176; C(66, 2) = 2,145 and C(88, 2) = 3,828; 0.04573,
   the control's 0.04504 and, as a check that the two-sided form is a different and larger number, 0.0560.

Determinism: exact rows are class D0; KA-686 is compared after rounding to the printed digits.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The nine rows of this stage exist as fixtures with pinned expected values and tolerances and agree with the
      known-answer registry number for number; every other registry row exists as a `not run` fixture with its
      `due` milestone.
- [ ] With `FROB_MILESTONE=M1` the nine rows pass and every one of their controls that is due at M1 is caught,
      each on its own build (`--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>`) or as its planted input,
      including the KA-694 control defined here, which the loader accepts as lying on the unsafe side; KA-681's
      control is reported `not-yet-due`, never `caught` and never `stale`.
- [ ] A required row that cannot run fails the suite; a row not yet due prints `not run`.
- [ ] `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example registry_report -- ka --chapter 28 --json` lists every registry
      row with its status, gate and owner; a missing fixture or a changed expected value fails the
      generated-artifact check.
- [ ] The independent arithmetic recomputes 0.94176, 2,145, 3,828, 0.04573, 0.04504 and 0.0560.
- [ ] Every control of this stage that is due at M1 is `caught` in the battery's own run (the `assert` lines of
      phase `m1` and the report's control column). The registry's `last_seen` entry with session and date is
      written by the recorders of the gates that own the rows, from their receipts (N38b-19), never by this
      bead, which records no gate.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/atelier_known_answers.sh --phase m1` passes, with its log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The battery is the test suite. Self-tests of the battery in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/ka_atelier_meta.rs`:
`fixture_per_registry_row` (a scratch registry with one extra row fails); `expected_values_match_registry` (a
fixture edited to 2,146 fails); `not_run_is_not_pass` (the report never counts a `not run` row as passed);
`required_row_missing_component_fails`; `printed_digits_rounding` (0.92069 rounds to 0.9207 and passes, 0.9210
fails); independent checks `zero_discordance_bound` (0.045730), `control_bound_is_unsafe_side` (1 − 0.05^(1/65) =
0.045042, below the truth, so the loader's direction check accepts it; the same check rejects 0.0560 as
`cannot-fail`), `two_sided_bound_is_different` (0.0560),
`enumeration_counts` (2,145 and 3,828), `step_energy` (0.94176 J).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/atelier_known_answers.sh` (created by this bead; N28cp1-02 adds the phase `m3` and N28c-24 the phase
`m4`; each phase runs alone with `--phase <name>`). Phase `m1`: `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test ka_atelier`
with `FROB_MILESTONE=M1`; `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example registry_report -- ka --chapter 28 --json`;
and from the user's side
`frob analyze symmetry` on the one-lidar quadruped (KA-680), `frob analyze bus-schedule` on the two-finger design
at deadlines of one and two ticks (KA-687), `frob analyze energy-budget` on the 12 mm rise with and without the
0.05 J approach bound (KA-684) and `frob eval preanalyze --rule paired-clopper-pearson/1 --n 64 --margin 0.05
--level 0.05 --json` and at n = 86 (KA-693). It then runs the generated-artifact check against a fixture set
with one row removed (must fail, naming the row). Nothing can point the check at a loose copy of
`conformance/ka/atelier/` (the fixtures lie outside `registries/`, so `e2e_registries_scratch` does not reach
them), so the planted set is built by the self-test, which the script runs by name: `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test ka_atelier_meta -- fixture_per_registry_row`. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/atelier_known_answers/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N28cp1-01`), `step` (`m1.*`), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and, for verb calls,
the envelope's `evidence` block; per row a `metric` line with measured, expected, tolerance and unit; an `assert`
line per control (`caught: true`); on failure the row, both values and the fixture path; a `summary` with rows
passed, failed and `not run`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The rows above are the content. The battery's own negative controls: a scratch fixture set with a row removed;
a fixture whose expected value was regenerated from the implementation (detected by the registry comparison). The
KA-694 control is defined here because a row without a control validates nothing. Determinism lanes: exact rows
identical on both hosts and in debug and release.

## Considerations

- A control must be a defect whose value lies clearly outside the row's tolerance and, on a row with a declared
  safe direction, on the unsafe side; 0.04504 differs from 0.04573 in the third printed digit and is the smaller
  bound.
- Several subjects' fixtures belong to other subsystems (the stance enumeration of A-08, the motor model of A-22);
  the battery calls their components and does not copy their fixtures.
- A row whose expected value changes is a change to a known answer and needs the derivation on file updated first
  (N28-KA); the battery's fixtures are never regenerated from the implementation.
- Runtime: the M1 rows are analyses and enumerations on the CPU; under two minutes (TARGET).
