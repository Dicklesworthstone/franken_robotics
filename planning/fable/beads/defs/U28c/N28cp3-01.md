## Background

Gate GATE-ATEL-13 (claim kind: Known answer) says of each of the eleven decision cores of the design studio
(compiled decision rules with a table, a loss model, proof obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted
mode, recorded under `registries/cores/`): its table regenerates bit-identically; its null calibration holds
(PO-ATEL-01 to -05, -10); its fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger; its shadow run records both
actions. The gate has two parts. The M3 part covers DC-01, DC-09 and DC-10 and is recorded by N28c-05. The M4
part covers the other eight: DC-02 and DC-03 (ladder advancement and survivor margins), DC-04 and DC-06
(rare-failure bound, cause ranking), DC-05 and DC-07 (value of information, reality band), DC-08 and DC-11
(solver effort profile, kernel rewrites). Their tables come from BD-349 (with the records of N28a-27 and N28a-28),
and their four clauses are measured by four row beads that proceed in parallel: N28c-08 (DC-02, DC-03), N28c-09
(DC-05, DC-07), N28c-10 (DC-04, DC-06) and N28c-11 (DC-08, DC-11).

A gate part is one record in `registries/gates.toml`, written once, by one recorder, from the receipt of one tier
run in which every control of the part was caught (N38b-19's same-run rule and INV-GAUNT-05: a pass is recorded
by a session that did not implement the capability). None of the four row beads can be that recorder: each would
have to wait for the other three, and BD-349, which the row beads depend on, cannot wait for them either (no bead
waits on what waits on it). This bead is the one place after all four: it runs the eight cores' phases together,
obtains the tier receipt of the whole part, checks it against the phases' measurements, and records the part.
It measures nothing new. Tier: Solid [S] (the cores under test are a mix of Solid and Frontier; a Frontier core
on its fallback is recorded on its fallback).

## Scope

In: the phase `gate` of `scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m4.sh`, which runs the eight row phases as stages and then
the tier run; the cross-check of the receipt against the phases' `metric` lines; the check that each core's
adoption status agrees with its outcome; the recording of the M4 part of GATE-ATEL-13 with `record_gate`.
Out: each core's tests, controls and phase (`dc-02` to `dc-11`: N28c-08 to N28c-11, which register their checks
under the GATE-ATEL-13 entry of the tier target and write no row); the tables and records (BD-349, N28a-27,
N28a-28); the M3 part (N28c-05); interference between cores (BD-350, GATE-ATEL-14); the register's record of the
gate (N40-02) and the chapter's owner declaration (N28c-25); the kill decisions that read the outcome (KILL-LADDER,
N28b-20; KILL-VOI, N28c-17).

## Technical approach

1. **Preconditions.** The four row beads are closed, so the entries for DC-02 to DC-08 and DC-11 exist under
   GATE-ATEL-13 in the tier target named by the gate record's `tier` field (`g1_known_answers`), each with its
   negative controls behind `--features gauntlet-mutations`, and the script has the phases `dc-02`, `dc-03`,
   `dc-04`, `dc-05`, `dc-06`, `dc-07`, `dc-08` and `dc-11` (the script is created by whichever row bead lands
   first, from the STD-E2E template; this bead adds the phase `gate`). The proof-obligation ledger N28c-23 is
   closed, so its check `obligations_atelier` is the calibration clause's entry under GATE-ATEL-13 and fails the
   clause while any of PO-ATEL-01 to -05 and -10 is not `discharged`.
2. **Stages.** `scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m4.sh --phase gate` runs the eight row phases in one log, under one
   seed (`FROB_E2E_SEED`, default 20281104), each with its own keys as its bead states; a failing phase fails the
   stage and the script continues to the tier run, so that the receipt and the log both show the failure.
3. **Tier run.** `e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-ATEL-13 --mutations` (STD-E2E: `FROB_TEST_SEED=$FROB_E2E_SEED cargo
   test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-ATEL-13`). The receipt
   `frob.gauntlet.receipt/1` (N38a-02) carries the gate line with, per core and per clause, the measured values
   and the outcome of every registered control. At M4 the filter also runs the M3 entries (DC-01, DC-09, DC-10):
   they must still pass, which is a regression check on the M3 part and records nothing here.
4. **Cross-check (`gate.crosscheck`).** From the receipt (`e2e_get --file "$E2E_LAST_RECEIPT"`) and the phases'
   `metric` lines, assert for each of the eight cores (the comparison is the script check `cores-crosscheck`,
   called as `e2e_check cores-crosscheck --gate GATE-ATEL-13 -- "$E2E_LAST_RECEIPT" <log>`, since a shell script
   cannot pair a receipt's values with logged metrics; Unit tests):
   - regeneration: the artifact hashes in the receipt equal those `frob cores show dc-NN --json` prints and those
     the phase logged;
   - null calibration: each value the row bead states lies within its tolerance in both places, and the two
     agree (the same seed gives the same synthetic draws): DC-03 coverage mean 0.950 ± 0.002 and 5th percentile
     0.884 ± 0.003 at n = 39; DC-05 value exactly 0 with STOP on the three no-change fixtures; DC-07 coverage mean
     0.905 ± 0.003 at n = 20, α = 0.10, and martingale false alarms at most 0.05; DC-08 mean loss of the chosen
     profile at most 0.05 over 10⁴ draws (PO-ATEL-10); DC-04 and DC-06 within the exact binomial acceptance
     bounds their bead states; DC-11 class-E rules bit-exact over 10⁴ random programs; and the ledger entry of
     N28c-23 `pass`, with PO-ATEL-01 to -05 and -10 `discharged` in the report `cargo run -p frob-conformance
     --example registry_report -- obligations --chapter 28 --json` prints;
   - forced fallbacks: one fallback record per trigger the core's record lists, each naming the trigger (five
     each for DC-02 and DC-03, three each for DC-04 and DC-06, one for DC-05 counted by its two firing coverage
     fixtures, three for DC-07, three for DC-08, two for DC-11);
   - shadow records: both actions for 100% of decisions, except the DC-07 shadow clause, which is entered
     `not-yet-due (M5)` (the band on admitted data is BD-393 at M5); no other clause may be `not-yet-due`;
   - controls: every registered control of the part is `caught` in this run.
5. **Status agreement.** For a Frontier core (DC-02 and DC-03 adaptive layers, DC-05, DC-07, DC-08, DC-11 real
   class) whose clause failed after one repair cycle, `registries/cores/dc-NN.toml` must have `status =
   "fallback-only"` and name the gate and the decision record; a core with `status = "default"` must have every
   clause `pass` in this receipt (the script check `cores-status`, on the receipt and the eight envelopes of
   `frob cores show dc-NN --json`). A disagreement fails the stage, naming the core.
6. **Recording (this bead is the M4 part's one recorder).** The script writes no gate row and never runs
   `record_gate` (STD-E2E's linter refuses a log that does). A session that implemented none of the eight cores'
   tables, rows or tests enters the part from the receipt of step 3: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
   record_gate -- --gate GATE-ATEL-13 --part M4 --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19), which refuses with
   `control-missing-from-receipt` or `control-not-caught` unless every control of the part was caught in that run,
   and with `self-certified` when the session is among the implementers. A failing clause is recorded as `fail`
   with its measured value: the record is made whichever way the measurement falls, and the kill decisions that
   read it (KILL-LADDER for DC-02 and DC-03, KILL-VOI for DC-05) are taken by their decision beads; a `killed`
   status is derived by N40-03, never written here.

Determinism: tables and decisions class D0 on the fixtures; the receipt's measured values are reproduced exactly
by a second run with the same seed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m4.sh --phase gate` exits 0 on a correct tree: the eight row phases pass, the
      tier run's receipt has the GATE-ATEL-13 line `pass` for DC-02 to DC-08 and DC-11 (DC-07's shadow clause
      `not-yet-due (M5)`), every control `caught`, and the cross-check of step 4 finds no difference.
- [ ] The status agreement of step 5 holds for all eight records.
- [ ] The M4 part of GATE-ATEL-13 is entered from that receipt with `record_gate` by a session that did not
      implement the cores, with the measured values per core and clause; the script itself writes no row.
- [ ] With any one planted defect of the row beads compiled in (for example calibration from survivors for DC-03,
      a band kept after a scope-key change for DC-07, an un-monotonized guard table for DC-08), the stage exits
      non-zero, the receipt's gate line is `fail` for that core, and the log's first failing line names the core,
      the clause and both values.
- [ ] A receipt produced without `--mutations` is rejected by the cross-check (`controls` empty) and would be
      refused by the recorder.
- [ ] Two runs with the same seed give identical `metric` values and identical receipt gate lines.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The phase `gate`'s two checks that a shell script cannot make are script checks (STD-E2E: each a function in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/e2e/checks/<name>.rs`, registered in `checks/mod.rs`, standard library and the
tool's JSON reader only): `cores-crosscheck` (step 4: the receipt's values per core and clause against the
phases' `metric` lines, the controls and the `not-yet-due` rule) and `cores-status` (step 5: each core record's
`status` against its clauses' outcomes; it reads the records as `frob cores show dc-NN --json` prints them).
Their tests, in the target `e2e_scripts` (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test e2e_scripts`), on recorded
receipts, logs and envelopes labelled `synthetic`: `cores_crosscheck_needs_eight_cores` (a receipt without the
DC-06 lines fails, naming DC-06); `cores_crosscheck_values_match_metrics` (a receipt whose DC-03 coverage mean is
0.951 while the phase logged 0.950 fails with both values); `cores_crosscheck_uncaught_control_fails` (one
control `missed`: fails, naming the control); `cores_crosscheck_not_yet_due_only_dc07_shadow` (a `not-yet-due`
DC-07 shadow clause passes; the same on DC-05 fails); `cores_crosscheck_rejects_receipt_without_mutations`;
`cores_status_agreement` (a record with `status = "default"` beside a failed clause fails; `fallback-only` beside
it passes).

### End-to-end script and logging

The phase `gate` of `scripts/e2e/decision_cores_m4.sh`, as above. It sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/decision_cores_m4/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a human-readable transcript. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N28cp3-01` on the stage's own lines; the row phases' lines keep their beads), `step`
(`gate.stages`, `gate.tier`, `gate.crosscheck`, `gate.status`), `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`,
`end`), the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block where a verb was called;
the `cmd` line of the tier run carries the field `receipt` (its hash); `metric` lines carry, per core and clause,
the receipt's value against the phase's value and the threshold; `assert` lines one per core and clause and one
per control (`caught: true`); `digest` fields carry the eight cores' table hashes; on failure the core, the
clause, both values, the seed and the receipt path. The final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed,
`controls_caught`, the receipt hash and `gates: ["GATE-ATEL-13"]` with the part `M4`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the values the row beads state (KA-696 conformal index 38 at n = 39; KA-705 the `f32` regrouping
  example; the DC-05 EVSI table; the Beta(19, 2) statistics), read back from the receipt.
- Negative controls: each row bead's registered controls, run in the tier run under `--mutations`; this bead's
  own: a receipt from a run without mutations; a receipt edited after the run (its BLAKE3 name no longer matches
  its bytes: the recorder refuses `receipt-hash-mismatch`); a phase value that disagrees with the receipt.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; both hosts (D0 for tables and decisions).

## Considerations

- Runtime: the sum of the eight phases and one tier run with mutations; the null-calibration simulations
  dominate (TARGET under 60 minutes on the CPU tier; the measured wall time is logged).
- A Frontier core that fails its clause after one repair cycle does not block the record: the part is recorded
  `fail` with the measured value, the core goes `fallback-only`, and the kill gate's decision bead reads the
  record. The M4 exit review (BD-378) reads this part's record and does not re-measure it.
- DC-07's band acts only on admitted reality data at M5 (BD-393); until then its shadow clause is `not-yet-due
  (M5)` and nothing else of the part may use that status.
- The receipt is the evidence; the phases' logs are the explanation. A disagreement between them is a failure,
  never a warning.
