## Background

Every refusal in the system has a stable public code (`FROB-REF-nnn`), one message, ranked repairs and an
exit code; the machine-readable registry is `registries/refusals.toml`, an unknown code is a load-time error,
and codes are never reused. An agent repairs by code, so a refusal raised under a private name is one the
agent surface cannot document, list per verb or test.

The studio (Atelier) lets an agent, and the studio itself, register a prediction before a run:
`frob predict register --metric cot-elec --interval 0.42 0.50 --confidence 0.8 --for <planned-run>`. The
prediction is hashed and frozen before the run starts and scored afterwards with a proper scoring rule; a
refit after scoring opens a new prediction version and the old one stays scored. The mechanism is only
worth anything if two things cannot happen: a prediction registered once its outcome is known, and a frozen
prediction quietly changed. The bead that builds the mechanism refuses both. The studio chapter's refusal
table has fifteen codes and none for either; its registry bead therefore has no rows for them, and the
mechanism raises them under the provisional names `prediction-after-outcome` and
`prediction-changed-after-freeze`. The delivery registers list both names as "awaiting codes from their
owners". Nothing files the request.

How codes are obtained (the rule of the single refusal registry, restated because this bead applies it):
requests go into an append-only allocation ledger (`registries/refusal_allocations.toml`, one row per
request with slug, requesting bead, defining subsection, condition, family, optional proposal, interim form,
status). The allocation function is a pure function of the ledger and the registry. Its rules, in order:
deduplicate first (a request whose condition an existing code already states gets no number: status
`merged`, and the bead is added to that row's `raised_by`); family by what is refused, never by the chapter
that asks; process in order of defining subsection; a proposal is only a preference; a number is never
reused; until a number is assigned the raising bead uses its declared interim behind one named constant.
The evidence-and-protocol family `8xx` is over capacity, and its remaining requests are `held` on their
interim form until the owner rules on how the family continues.

This bead files the two requests, states what the rule gives for each, and writes everything about the two
refusals that does not depend on the number: trigger, payload, repairs, exit, raising sites, tests. Tier:
Solid.

Rejected alternatives: inventing two numbers here (bypasses the allocation rule and collides with a full
family); keeping the private names for good (an agent cannot repair by a name that is in no registry);
folding both into "arguments fail the schema" (FROB-REF-802: the arguments are well formed; what is refused
is their timing).

## Scope

In: the two ledger requests with family, condition, interim form and expected outcome under the rule; the
registry row content for each; the relation to the calibration subsystem's code 883; message and field
templates; the per-verb refusal lists; a trigger fixture per refusal; the hand-off to the raising bead.

Out: the prediction mechanism and its freeze (BD-344); the allocation function and ledger format (N35-33);
the owner decision on how the full `8xx` family continues (a decision bead of the numerics unit); the calibration subsystem's own frozen predictions and
sealed reads (the Ground registry bead and `ground.score`).

## Technical approach

**1. The two requests** (rows appended to the allocation ledger).

| Slug | Defined in | Condition (one sentence) | Family | Proposal | Interim form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `prediction-changed-after-freeze` | §28.17 | An attempt to alter a prediction after it was frozen | `8xx` (evidence and protocol) | none | 883 with `detail = "studio-prediction"` |
| `prediction-after-outcome` | §28.17 | A prediction registered when an outcome for its target already exists in the ledger, or the target's job has committed its first chunk | `8xx` | none | 883 with `detail = "registration-after-outcome"` |

Family reason (DESIGN): what is refused is a breach of evidence order in the ledger, the same kind of thing
as the neighbouring codes for sealed reads, spent holdouts and late leases; it is not a learning-protocol
rule of the `5xx` block although the studio chapter's other codes sit there.

**2. What the allocation rule gives.**
- `prediction-changed-after-freeze`: **merged into FROB-REF-883.** That code's printed meaning is "a read of
  sealed data with no frozen prediction, or a prediction changed after freezing", and its second clause is
  this condition word for word. Under the deduplication rule the request gets no number; `raised_by` of 883
  gains BD-344 and the verb `predict.register`; the two raising sites are told apart by `pointer`
  (`prediction` for the studio's record, `ground.prediction` for the calibration subsystem's). The
  provisional name disappears from the code base when the constant is switched.
- `prediction-after-outcome`: **a new condition.** 883's first clause refuses a *read* of sealed data that
  no frozen prediction precedes; this request refuses a *registration* that an outcome precedes. The two
  protect the same order from opposite ends and need different repairs, so the request stands, in `8xx`.
  That family has no free number outside the hold, so its status is `held` (and, before N35x-01's hold is in
  the ledger, `requested` with reason `owner-decision`, N35-33 rule 8) and it is raised on its interim form
  until the owner's ruling releases a number. If the registry owner judges the condition identical to
  883's first clause, the row is `merged` instead and the same `detail` value stays as the discriminator;
  either outcome is a one-line change in the raising bead.

**3. Row content** (number-independent; entered in the registry when the status is `assigned` or `merged`).

| | `prediction-after-outcome` | `prediction-changed-after-freeze` (883, studio site) |
|---|---|---|
| Message | "A prediction was registered after its outcome existed" | "A frozen prediction cannot be changed" |
| Trigger | `predict.register` finds in the ledger a result carrying the metric for the target identity, or a committed chunk of the target's job, with a sequence number below the registration's | A registration by the same predictor for the same target whose content differs from a record in the target's freeze set |
| Order source | Ledger append order; never a clock | The freeze set embedded in the dispatched job record |
| `pointer` | `prediction.target` | `prediction` |
| `observed` | The sequence number and kind of the earliest outcome record | The frozen `PredictionId` and the differing field |
| `required` | "registration before the target's first outcome record" | "no change after freeze" |
| Ranked repairs | 1. Predict a run that has not started. 2. Register the next version, which applies to later targets | 1. Register a new version; it applies to targets not yet dispatched. (None for this target: the record stands and will be scored.) |
| `retry` | `after-change` | `never` for this target |
| Exit | 3 | 3 |
| Raised by; verbs | The prediction ledger; `predict.register`, and `experiment.suggest` when it registers its own outcome interval | The prediction ledger; `predict.register` |
| First due | M4 (with the mechanism); the rows are entered now | M4 |

**4. Per-verb lists and documents.** A row's list holds codes only (VERBS-predict, `refusal_lists`; N35-33
rule 7: a bare slug is not a refusal). The list of `predict.register` is 883, which covers the studio site of
the merged request and is the code the held request is raised under; the list of `experiment.suggest`, which
raises the held request's interim form when it registers its own outcome interval, carries 883 the same way.
The slug `prediction-after-outcome` is on no row's list until it has a number: it stands in the row's
"Pending, uncoded" line and in the registry's interim list (N35-33 rule 7), which `frob schema --refusals
--json` serves with the bead that owns each interim use (BD-344). When the number is assigned, the rows gain it
in the same change that switches the constant. The generated agent guide prints the interim form and the repair
for each.

**5. One named constant each.** `REFUSAL_PREDICTION_AFTER_OUTCOME` and
`REFUSAL_PREDICTION_CHANGED_AFTER_FREEZE` in the refusal module of `frob-core`, each resolving to (code,
detail). Switching from interim to assigned is an edit of the constant and a schema-snapshot update; no
call site changes.

**6. Trigger fixtures** (`tests/fixtures/refusals/prediction/`): `late.json` (a ledger with a result row at
sequence 41 for target t and a registration attempt at sequence 57) and `changed.json` (a frozen record
with interval [0.42, 0.50] and an attempt to register [0.40, 0.52] for the same predictor and target).

Determinism D0. Nothing here is a claim; the rows are registry data.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-core --test refusal_prediction_rows` (the real registry loader and allocation function):

- `ledger_has_both_requests`; `dedup_merges_changed_after_freeze_into_883`: the allocation function returns
  `merged` with target 883 because the condition sentence matches 883's second clause in the condition
  table.
- `after_outcome_is_held_while_family_is_full`: with the hold on 836 to 839 in force the status is `held`
  and no number is printed; with the hold lifted in a test ledger the request is numbered by the rule and
  the constant resolves to that number with no `detail`.
- `payload_late`: from `late.json` → `observed` = sequence 41, kind `result`; `required` as stated; first
  repair "predict a run that has not started".
- `payload_changed`: from `changed.json` → `observed` names the frozen `PredictionId` and the field
  `interval`; `retry: never`. Both payload tests print the envelope they built as one JSON line tagged
  `refusal-fixture`, which the end-to-end phase below reads.
- `order_is_ledger_not_clock`: the late fixture with wall-clock times reversed still refuses.
- `two_sites_one_code_883`: the studio site and the calibration site produce the same code and exit with
  different `pointer` values.
- `slug_on_a_row_list_fails_load`: a verb entry whose list names a slug, pending or unknown, is a load-time
  error (a row's list holds codes only); the pending slug loads in the interim list while its ledger row is open.

### End-to-end script and logging

Phase `predict-refusals` of the studio chapter's refusal battery `scripts/e2e/atelier_refusals.sh`, whose
whole run belongs to N28c-21 at M4. The script is split into phases, each runnable alone with `--phase <name>`;
this bead lands first (M1) and therefore creates the script from the STD-E2E template with this phase in it.
Before the mechanism lands at M4 the phase runs the payload builder on the two fixtures through this bead's own
test target, in the cargo form of BD-025 (ADR-GAUNT-01: `frob-conformance` is a library with test targets and
no binary, and there is no `frob conformance` command): `FROB_TEST_SEED=1 cargo test -p frob-core --test
refusal_prediction_rows -- payload_late payload_changed --nocapture`, whose two tests print each refusal
envelope they build from `late.json` and `changed.json` as one JSON line tagged `refusal-fixture` (DESIGN). The
phase extracts those two lines, validates each against `frob schema frob.refusal/1 --json`, asserts code,
detail, pointer, observed, required, first repair and exit 3,
checks that `frob schema --verbs --json` lists 883 under `predict.register` with no slug on its list and that
the interim list of `frob schema --refusals --json` holds `prediction-after-outcome` with 883, its `detail` and
BD-344, and
logs each step `mechanism-pending`. Once BD-344 has landed the same phase, unchanged in its assertions, drives the
verb: (1) register a prediction for a target that already has a result → exit 3, the interim code with
`detail = "registration-after-outcome"`, the earliest outcome's sequence number; (2) register, dispatch, then try
to change the interval → exit 3, 883 with `pointer = "prediction"`; (3) apply the first repair of each (a new
target; a new version) and assert success. It writes `target/e2e-logs/atelier_refusals/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N28cx-01`), `step` (`predict-refusals-*`), `event`
(`refusal` for the refusal lines), the exact command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the
refusal block (code, detail, pointer, observed, required, repairs, retry), the ledger sequence numbers involved,
and on failure the first differing field and the fixture path. A phase `summary` line gives assertions passed
and failed and whether the run was `mechanism-pending`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers are the two fixtures: sequence 41 before 57; [0.42, 0.50] against [0.40, 0.52].
- Negative controls: a registry row entered with an invented number (the loader's rule "a row outside the
  printed set must have an assigned ledger row" fails the load); the changed-after-freeze request given a
  number of its own (the cross-chapter check reports two codes for one condition); order taken from
  timestamps (the reversed-clock test fails).
- Determinism: registry load and allocation output identical on two runs and two platforms.

## Considerations

- The merge into 883 makes one code serve two subsystems. That is what the deduplication rule intends; the
  `pointer` keeps the sites apart, and the pair is entered in the registry's neighbour table so that a third
  code for the same condition is never requested.
- While the after-outcome request is `held`, agents see 883 with a `detail`. The repairs printed are this
  bead's, not the calibration site's, because repairs are selected by (code, detail) for interim forms.
- The studio registers its own predictions (cost estimates, outcome intervals of suggested experiments,
  expected savings of a race); they are subject to the same two refusals and are not exempt.
