## Background

One of the project's goals is to account for energy: a per-actuator and per-contact balance in which every joule
that enters a rollout is attributed to where it went, closing to a stated residual. The energy ledger is that
balance. It is useful in three ways: an agent sees *where* a design wastes energy ("38 of 100 J: copper loss in
the knees during stance"); cost of transport decomposes along the same lines; and energy that appears from
nowhere becomes measurable, which is how a policy that exploits the simulator is caught.

The ledger is possible because a robot is modelled as a composition over typed ports: each component exposes
ports (a shaft port carries angular velocity and torque, an electrical port voltage and current, a thermal port
temperature and heat flow), interconnection is power-conserving, and the port powers of a composition sum to
zero. The energy ledger is the port structure read at run time: every term is a port quantity, the power
crossing one typed port, integrated over the step.

**ADR-CHRON-08: the numerical residual is its own line, and created energy is audited.** The integrator's and
the solver's energy error is never folded into a physical term, and energy that appears from nowhere is a
finding about the simulator, reported against the result that benefited. Rejected: distributing the residual
over friction and damping, which looks tidier and hides exactly the defect the ledger exists to show.

This bead builds the ledger on the referee (the double-precision reference tier) at M1. The batched tiers and
the audit monitors follow at M2. Seed done-condition: GATE-CHRON-03 on the referee; a damped pendulum's ledger
matches the hand calculation. Tier Solid.

## Technical approach

**1. The identities, for each world and episode.**

```
battery energy out        = Σ actuators (copper loss + friction loss + gear loss + Δ stored) + mechanical work at joints
mechanical work at joints = Δ kinetic + Δ potential + contact dissipation (normal damping + sliding friction)
                            + joint damping + work on fixtures and payloads + numerical residual
```

**2. Lines** (joules; one accumulator each per world, and per port where a breakdown is declared):

| Line | Definition per step of length h |
|---|---|
| `battery_out` | Decrease of the pack's chemical energy |
| `bus_positive`, `bus_negative` | h·max(0, P) and h·min(0, P) with P = Σⱼ vⱼ·iⱼ over the drives: regeneration is visible and never netted against draw without saying so |
| `copper`, `friction`, `gear` (per actuator) | Loss lines of the actuator account: i²·R, bearing and seal friction, mesh loss |
| `stored` (per actuator, by store) | Increase of magnetic, rotor-kinetic, elastic, polarization and thermal stored energy |
| `heat_to_ambient` | Heat leaving thermal nodes |
| `motoring`, `regeneration_to_battery`, `shunt_dissipation`, `clipped_braking` | Braking modes as separate lines |
| `joint_work` (per joint) | τ·Δθ with the step's own discrete velocity: the mechanical port of the actuator |
| `d_kinetic`, `d_potential` | State functions evaluated at the committed states before and after the step |
| `contact_normal_damping`, `contact_sliding_friction` (per contact site) | ∫ c·ḋ² dt and ∫ f_t·v_slip dt at the solved state |
| `joint_damping` (per joint) | ∫ b·θ̇² dt, and limit and stiction rows by their own laws |
| `fixture_payload_work` | Work done on fixtures and payloads, and by moving surfaces on the robot, with its sign |
| `algorithmic_exchange` | Reserved; zero until the split of the impulse-level correction is delivered |
| `numerical_residual` | "Integrator and solver": the identity's own term, equal to −r per step (r below), so that `joint_work` = `d_kinetic` + `d_potential` + dissipation + `fixture_payload_work` + `algorithmic_exchange` + `numerical_residual` holds exactly |

**Sign convention (DESIGN; the plan states r for the audit and the residual as a term of the identity, and the
two have opposite signs).** The per-step *audit residual* is r = `d_kinetic` + `d_potential` − (`joint_work` −
`fixture_payload_work` − D), with D the sum of the step's dissipation lines (contact normal damping, contact
sliding friction, joint damping) and the external work on the robot equal to −`fixture_payload_work`. r > 0
means energy appeared that no port supplied; r is what the created-energy monitors read (N29b-02, N29a-22). The
ledger line `numerical_residual` is −r. On the damped pendulum (no joint work) the line is positive:
`joint_damping` + `numerical_residual` = −(`d_kinetic` + `d_potential`) = 4.905 J. The verb `telemetry.energy`
(N24a-37) prints the line with this sign.

**3. Stored heat is stored energy.** Copper, friction and gear losses flow into thermal nodes. They are listed
for attribution and are not added again as losses: the loss lines satisfy `copper + friction + gear =
Δ thermal stored + heat_to_ambient` (the thermal sub-balance), and the first identity is checked with thermal
storage counted once. Counting stored heat again as a loss produces a false defect; that mutant is a registered
negative control of the actuator account. The electrical identity is therefore checked in the form of the
actuator account (per element and per step): `battery_out` = `joint_work` + Σ `stored` (every store, thermal
included) + `heat_to_ambient` + the actuator account's own residual (BD-076, N15b-05), which is reported beside
the ledger and never merged into `numerical_residual`.

**4. Accumulation on the referee.** After each committed step the ledger reads the step's port quantities
through the referee adapter and adds them into double-precision accumulators with compensated (Neumaier)
summation, one pair (sum, compensation) per line and per port. `d_kinetic` and `d_potential` are differences of
state functions, so they telescope exactly over an episode. The per-step residual r is stored at telemetry
level T2 on the Energetics stream; totals are T0.

**5. Closure.** Two figures are computed and both are recorded:
- *Closure defect* ε = |K_end − K_0 + U_end − U_0 − Σ(joint and external work) + Σ(dissipation) − Σ r| divided
  by W_abs, where W_abs = Σ over steps of (|joint work| + |external work| + |dissipation|) is the absolute work
  exchanged. It measures the bookkeeping: independent accumulations of the two sides of the identity.
- *Residual share* ρ = |Σ r| / W_abs: the size of the "integrator and solver" line itself.
INV-CHRON-09: the lines of the energy ledger, including the residual line, sum to zero; no physical term absorbs
the residual. The zero sum is taken over the signed lines of the mechanical identity: `joint_work` −
`d_kinetic` − `d_potential` − D − `fixture_payload_work` − `algorithmic_exchange` − `numerical_residual` = 0.
The stored totals are quantized once (quantum 1 nJ, integers) and the stored `numerical_residual` total is the
integer that makes this sum zero, so the stored ledger sums to zero exactly and the residual line carries every
rounding.

**6. Breakdowns.** By joint, by limb (the joints of one kinematic branch below the base) and by stance or
swing (a limb is in stance during a step when one of its contact sites carries normal load at the committed
state). Every breakdown row sums to its parent line in the same integers. Cost of transport is reported twice
and never mixed (Appendix D.4; the form N24a-18 registers as the metrics `cot-mech` and `cot-elec`): CoT_mech =
W_joint/(m·g·d) with W_joint the `joint_work` line as it stands (negative work included), and CoT_elec =
E_batt/(m·g·d) with E_batt the net `battery_out`; m is the total mass (robot and carried payload), g the gravity
magnitude and d the base's path length. Each decomposes along the same lines and breakdowns; the distance floor
and the aggregation over episodes are N24a-18's rules.

**7. Energy per task, two ways.** The mean over completed episodes, and the total over all attempts divided by
completions. A design that succeeds once for 10 J and fails once for 20 J reports 10 J and 30 J; a rival with
two 12 J successes reports 12 J and 12 J; the first figure alone would rank them wrongly. Both are always
printed, with their counts.

**8. Every energy figure names its residual.** `EnergyFigure { value, unit, residual_abs, residual_share,
frontier }` is the only type by which an energy number leaves the crate.

**9. First offending event.** Each step, each port's local balance is checked against the tier's tolerance
(TARGET, the same as the actuator account N15b-05: on the referee |local residual| / scale above 10⁻⁹ per step,
scale being the absolute work exchanged at that element in the step, floored at one quantum of 1 nJ).
When a step's residual exceeds it, the ledger records the earliest step and the element whose local balance
failed (an actuator element from the actuator account, a contact site, a joint), as a T1 event
`energy-balance-exceeded`, not only the episode total.

**10. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/energy/{lines.rs, accumulate.rs, breakdown.rs, per_task.rs,
offender.rs}`; unit-test fixtures under `tests/fixtures/energy/`.

**11. Registered fixtures.** The runs the script makes through `sim.run --fixture` use fixtures this bead writes
and registers in N24b-03's registry: one manifest of schema `frob.fixture/1` each, at
`assets/fixtures/<name>/fixture.toml`, `kind = "synthetic"`, constants as exact decimal strings hashed into
`ConstantsHash` before the first run, every input declared (so `--scene`, `--task` and `--controller` beside
`--fixture` are 802), and one analytic row per known answer whose `derivation` names N29-KA's page for that
known answer by path and hash (one page per known answer: a copy under N24b-03's per-fixture directory would
duplicate its constants). The loader compares a derivation's front matter with the manifest's `ka` column; the
two pages' front matter already lists their KA identifiers (N29-KA), and this bead adds to it the test that
checks each row, as N24-KA's rule asks and as a carrying bead may extend N29-KA's page. `--fixture` takes no
positional design and runs every design of its manifest, so each design that must be read on its own is a
fixture of its own.
- `pendulum-damped` (KA-707, the gate's referee fixture; also BD-208's on the batched tiers): one design,
  `assets/fixtures/pendulum-damped/pendulum.fbot`: a base fixed to the world, one passive revolute joint about a
  horizontal axis with viscous damping 0.5 N·m·s/rad and no actuator, and one link whose 1 kg mass is a point
  1 m from the axis (massless rod: zero inertia about the point); scene: gravity 9.81 m/s², no contact geometry
  (the run is contact-free); task: released at rest at 60° from the downward vertical, horizon 60 s, outcome
  `completed` at the horizon; controller: none. Row: KA-707 with N29-KA's derivation
  `docs/derivations/chronicle-pendulum-ledger.md`.
- `energy-per-task-a` and `energy-per-task-b` (KA-708, the two designs of the per-task comparison): one design
  each, a declared task with a completion criterion, and an input table of two rows, one per world, whose
  constants (DESIGN, stated in the derivation `docs/derivations/chronicle-energy-per-task.md` of N29-KA, which
  the rows cite) make the recorded
  energy of the episodes, within the referee ledger's bound of 10⁻⁹ relative: design A, 10 J completed and 20 J
  failed; design B, 12 J and 12 J, both completed. Each fixture is run under its own key and read with
  `telemetry.energy --per-task` on its own run: one read over both runs would pool the two designs' episodes
  into one pair of figures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pendulum_ledger_hand_calculation` (KA-707): a point mass of 1 kg on a massless 1 m rod, g = 9.81 m/s²,
  viscous joint damping 0.5 N·m·s/rad, released at rest from 60°, run 60 s on the referee. Expected:
  `d_potential` = −1 × 9.81 × 1 × (1 − cos 60°) = −4.905 J; `d_kinetic` within 10⁻¹² J of 0 (the energy
  envelope decays as e^(−0.5·t), so 4.905·e^(−30) ≈ 5·10⁻¹³ J remains); `joint_damping` + `numerical_residual`
  = 4.905 J; `joint_work`, contact lines and battery lines 0; ε < 10⁻⁹.
- `pendulum_step_refinement`: halving h reduces |Σ r| by the integrator's order (a factor between 1.8 and 4.2),
  and leaves ε below 10⁻⁹.
- `sign_error_localized` (control of KA-707): the damping port's sign is flipped in a test build; the first
  offending event names that joint at step 1.
- `energy_per_task_two_ways` (KA-708): (10, fail 20) → 10 and 30; (12, 12) → 12 and 12.
- `motor_lifts_mass`: a geared motor raises 2 kg by 0.5 m at constant speed: `d_potential` = 9.81 J;
  `joint_work` = 9.81 J + `joint_damping`; `battery_out` = `joint_work` + copper + friction + gear + Δ stored.
- `regeneration_visible`: lowering the same mass with a regenerating drive gives `bus_negative` < 0.
- `stored_heat_not_double_counted`: with a thermal node of 50 J/K, losses of 100 J raise `stored.thermal` and
  `heat_to_ambient` to a total of 100 J; a mutant that also adds the 100 J as a loss fails the first identity.
- `breakdown_sums_to_parent` (property, 10³ ledgers).
- `closure_exact_in_integers`; `residual_not_absorbed`.
- `figure_requires_residual` (compile-fail).
- `residual_sign_convention`: a planted step that adds 1 mJ of kinetic energy with no port supplying it gives
  r = +1 mJ and a `numerical_residual` line of −1 mJ; the pendulum gives a positive line.
- `d4_energy_balance_oracle` (oracle library N12-44, Appendix D.4 row "energy balance"): the oracle's
  motor–thermal fixture, run for one step through this ledger, matches supplied = Δ stored + dissipated +
  exported with heat held in the thermal node counted as stored; the oracle's negative control (heat booked as
  both stored and lost) shows here a deficit equal to that heat. Oracle values are read from the library and
  never regenerated from this crate.
- `cot_two_forms` (N12-44 row "cost of transport"; the hand case of N24a-18): m = 8.000 kg, g = 9.81 m/s²,
  d = 1.00 m, net battery energy 15.696 J and joint work 11.772 J give CoT_elec = 0.2000 and CoT_mech = 0.1500.
- `fixture_manifests_load`: `pendulum-damped`, `energy-per-task-a` and `energy-per-task-b` load through N24b-03's
  loader; each declares its scene, task and controller and exactly one design; the rows cite KA-707 and KA-708
  with N29-KA's derivation files; changing one constant changes `ConstantsHash`.

## Considerations

- **Which residual does the gate bound?** The gate text says "the energy ledger closes: residual below 10⁻⁹
  relative on the referee". Read as bookkeeping closure (ε) it is reachable in double precision. Read as the
  size of the integrator-and-solver line (ρ), it depends on the referee's integration scheme and step and may
  not be reachable at the default step. This bead records both and, fail-closed, requires both for PASS; the
  ambiguity is listed in the unit's coverage notes for the owner. Nothing may be re-labelled to make ρ pass:
  the residual line is never absorbed.
- Witness migration in the transactional step and fixture 1 both depend on this ledger closing to its stated
  residual; they read `EnergyFigure`.
- The quantum of 1 nJ bounds the rounding carried by the stored residual at one quantum per stored value.
- On the referee the created-energy bound is 10⁻⁶ relative; the monitors that apply it arrive with the audit
  bead and read the per-step r stored here.
