## Background

A simulation studio produces far more data than it can keep. Traces of sampled worlds, snapshots, cached
results and failure captures accumulate at gigabytes per campaign, and something must be deleted. The danger is
specific: retention deletes the interval that explains a decision, and afterwards a verdict stands with nothing
under it. The record subsystem (Chronicle) therefore deletes under one decision:

**ADR-CHRON-09: retention never removes evidence silently.** Deletion leaves a tombstone; a metric whose
samples are gone is refused, not interpolated; chunks in the evidence closure of a live claim are pinned.

What is kept, and for how long:

| Class | Contents | Default policy |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-pinned | Chunks, snapshots, ledger rows, certificates and receipts in the closure of a live claim; qualification receipts; registered predictions | Kept until the claim is withdrawn; then tombstoned |
| Failure captures | T2 and T3 rings of failing worlds | Kept while the run is the head of a lineage or is cited by an open comparison, bisect or autopsy; then 30 days |
| Sampled traces | T2 and T3 of sampled worlds that did not fail | Evicted under the store budget, oldest unpinned first |
| T0 and T1 | Metrics, histograms, digests, events | Kept for the life of the run record |
| Memo artifacts | Cached results | 64 GB by default; evict, never block |

`frob store status` reports what each class holds and what is pinned and why; `frob store collect --dry-run`
lists what a collection would remove. Seed done-condition: GATE-CHRON-12. Tier Solid.

Rejected alternatives: time-based deletion of everything older than N days (deletes the evidence of a verdict
that is still cited); reference counting (a crash between a reference and its count leaks or frees wrongly;
mark-and-sweep from declared roots is recomputable); deleting without a record (a later citation then fails
with "not found", which cannot be told apart from "never existed").

## Scope

In: the class of every stored object; pins and their reasons; the rule that a verdict's closure is pinned in
the verdict's transaction (`issue_verdict`, over a verdict record `frob.verdict/1` of N24a-11); roots; mark; sweep by class under the budget; tombstones; the
behaviour of a later citation; the function `withdraw(claim, reason)` and the release of its pins; the rule
that private objects, their tombstones and their totals are hidden without the scope; the two verbs; the gate
with its random cycles; the end-to-end script.
Out: the artifact graph and closure (BD-095); the memo store's own policy and eviction (N28b-38 for analyses,
BD-220 for episodes), which this bead reports; `scene.purge` (BD-305), which calls the sweep with a lineage
closure; pinned cursors (N29a-04), which this bead reads as roots; the retention windows and budgets as decided
numbers, the eviction order and `store.toml` (the decision N29b-12, which measures with `store.status`, depends
on this bead and re-runs the gate under the decided policy; this bead is built on 30 days and 64 GB); the edge
cases (the re-check of pins inside the sweep's transaction, the coverage audit that every live claim's closure
is pinned and the rule for every claim writer, withdrawal as a surface with its consequences for dependent
claims and gate rows, life cycles and orphan detection of non-claim holders: N29b-13, which depends on this
bead and adds step groups to its script); the privacy class on every stored Chronicle object through
placement, reads, exports, views and purge against pins (N29b-14, at the third milestone).

## Technical approach

**1. Classification.** Every object in the store (a column file, a snapshot, a certificate bundle, a receipt, a
report, a still, a cached result) has one class, computed and not declared: `evidence-pinned` if a pin names
it; else `t0-t1` if it is a T0 or T1 chunk; else `failure-capture` if its capture record says so; else
`memo` if the memo graph owns it; else `sampled-trace`.

**2. Pins.** Table `pin`: `(object hash, reason kind, reason id, taken_seq)`. Reason kinds: `claim` (a live
claim's closure), `qualification-receipt`, `prediction`, `open-job`, `lineage-head`, `open-study` (a
comparison, bisect or autopsy that cites the run), `cursor` (an open pagination pin), `hold` (an operator's
explicit hold with a text reason). An object may have several pins; it is unpinned when it has none.

**3. Pins are taken when a verdict is issued.** The helper `issue_verdict(txn, verdict)` computes the evidence
closure of the verdict's claim and inserts a `claim` pin for every stored object in it, in the same ledger
transaction that writes the verdict row and the claim. Either the verdict exists with its closure pinned or
neither exists. So the interval that explains a decision cannot be collected while the decision stands.

**4. Withdrawal.** `withdraw(claim, reason)` marks the claim withdrawn, removes its `claim` pins in one
transaction and records the reason. Objects that lose their last pin fall back to their unpinned class. A
withdrawn claim prints as withdrawn and cannot be cited.

**5. A collection cycle** (`collect(budget, now, dry_run)`), as numbered steps:
1. *Roots.* All rows of `pin`; every chunk at or below the frontier of an open job; every object reachable from
   a lineage head; every registered prediction; every chunk visible at an open cursor's pin.
2. *Mark.* From the roots follow the artifact graph's derived-from and supported-by edges and the snapshot
   diff-to-base links (a sparse difference keeps its base alive). The marked set is never touched.
3. *Sweep by class,* over unmarked objects only:
   - `t0-t1`: never removed while the run record exists;
   - `failure-capture`: removed when its run is no longer a lineage head or cited by an open study and its age
     exceeds the window (default 30 days);
   - `sampled-trace`: removed oldest first while the store exceeds its budget (default 64 GB);
   - `memo`: reported only; the memo store evicts itself and never blocks.
4. *Tombstone.* For each object removed, in the same transaction as the removal of its row: insert
   `tombstone(content hash, class, time, reason)` with reason one of `budget`, `window-expired`,
   `claim-withdrawn`, `purge`, `operator`; then delete the file. A crash between the transaction and the file
   deletion leaves an unattached file that the next cycle removes; a crash before the transaction changes
   nothing.
5. *Gaps.* A removed chunk leaves its `chunk` row in place with a `collected` mark, so the frontier stays
   contiguous and queries see a gap with reason `collected`, never a hole. This bead adds `collected` to the
   registered gap reasons of the format contract (BD-090's list) and to the offline auditor's copy (N29a-03) in
   the same change, so a bundle exported after a collection still verifies.
A dry run performs steps 1 to 3 and returns the list with bytes per class and removes nothing.

**6. After collection.** A citation of a collected object fails with the tombstone's reason: any claim whose
closure reaches it prints `evidence: unavailable` and is refused with FROB-REF-869. A metric that needs a
collected chunk is refused with FROB-REF-888 (repairs: re-run with the channel retained; accept
`unavailable`). An attempt to delete a pinned object (through `--force`, a purge that meets a pin, or an
internal defect) is refused with FROB-REF-813 (a deletion that would remove a pinned object; repair: withdraw
the claim first; exit 3) and names the pins.

**7. Privacy.** Telemetry derived from a private scene inherits the scene's privacy class through retention and
export: tombstones of private objects are themselves private rows, and `store.status` shows private totals
only to a caller with the scope.

**8. Verbs.**
Both rows are VERBS-store's (owner `frob-chronicle`, evidence *operational*: they report the store, not a
measurement); this bead implements them and does not redefine them.
- `store.status` (read, M2, capability `store:read`; schemas `frob.store.status.in/1` and
  `frob.store.status.out/1`; no chapter refusal of its own): per class the object count
  and bytes; the budget and the bytes over or under it; pinned bytes by reason kind with the top pin holders
  (claim identifiers, job identifiers); the oldest unpinned sampled trace; tombstone counts by reason; the
  memo store's size against its 64 GB default; the retention window in force.
- `store.collect` (admin, M2, capability `store:admin`; schemas `frob.store.collect.in/1` and
  `frob.store.collect.out/1`; refusal FROB-REF-813, exit 3): `--dry-run` (the default when no key is given),
  `--budget <size>` (bytes, or with an SI suffix `kB`, `MB`, `GB`: `1MB` is 10⁶ bytes), `--now <instant>` for
  tests, `--class <c>` to restrict, `--force` (remove the objects of the named class that the window and
  budget rules would keep; a pinned object is never removed, and meeting one refuses the whole call with
  FROB-REF-813 naming the pins).
  Returns the removed (or would-be removed) objects by class with bytes, the tombstones written, and what was
  kept and why for the ten largest kept objects. Idempotency key required for a real collection.

**9. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/retain/{class.rs, pin.rs, mark.rs, sweep.rs, tombstone.rs}`;
`crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/{store_status.rs, store_collect.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, GATE-CHRON-12 (TARGET): 10⁴ random collection cycles remove no pinned object; every
      removal has a tombstone; a citation of a collected object is refused with its reason. The measured
      counts are recorded.
- [ ] INV-CHRON-12: collection never removes a pinned object, and every removal leaves a tombstone (property
      test over random stores, pins, budgets and crash points).
- [ ] A verdict and the pins of its closure commit together: a crash injected at every statement of
      `issue_verdict` leaves either both or neither.
- [ ] After a claim is withdrawn its formerly pinned objects become collectable and their tombstones say
      `claim-withdrawn`; before withdrawal a forced deletion returns FROB-REF-813 naming the claim.
- [ ] A sparse snapshot difference keeps its base alive; collecting a base that a kept difference needs is
      impossible (planted).
- [ ] A removed chunk leaves a `collected` gap; the frontier of its sequence is unchanged; a metric over it is
      refused with FROB-REF-888.
- [ ] `store.collect --dry-run` removes nothing and its list equals what a real collection then removes.
- [ ] `store.status` totals equal the sums over objects; pinned bytes by reason sum to the pinned total (an
      object with two pins is counted once in the total and listed under each reason).
- [ ] T0 and T1 chunks are never removed while their run record exists.
- [ ] Both verbs are reachable through `frob --json` and MCP; `store.collect` without the admin scope is
      refused with exit 5.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `classification_table`: one object of each kind lands in its class; a pin moves it to `evidence-pinned`.
- `verdict_pins_closure_atomically` (fault at each statement, 100 seeds).
- `mark_follows_derived_from_and_diff_base`.
- `sweep_sampled_oldest_first`: budget 10 GB, 14 GB of sampled traces of ages 1 to 14 days at 1 GB each: the
  four oldest are removed.
- `failure_capture_window`: a capture of age 31 days on a run that is no longer a lineage head is removed; at
  29 days it is kept; while cited by an open autopsy it is kept at any age.
- `t0_t1_never_swept`.
- `tombstone_fields`: content hash, class, time, reason.
- `crash_between_txn_and_unlink`: the file is unattached and removed by the next cycle; the tombstone exists
  once.
- `cite_collected_869_with_reason`; `metric_over_collected_888`; `delete_pinned_813`.
- `withdraw_releases_pins`.
- `open_cursor_is_root`; `open_job_frontier_is_root`.
- `dry_run_equals_real`.
- `private_tombstones_hidden_without_scope`.
- Property `inv_chron_12`: 10⁴ random cycles on `synthetic` stores of 50 to 500 objects with random pins,
  withdrawals, budgets and injected crashes: the set of removed objects is disjoint from the pinned set at the
  time of removal, and equals the set of tombstones.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/store_retention.sh`: (1) a `batch-cpu` campaign of three small runs with sampled traces and one
planted failure cluster (`frob sim run <reference quadruped> --tier batch-cpu --worlds 256 --seconds 4
--telemetry T2 --trace-rate 1/16 --seed <s> --key sr-<i> --json` for i = 1, 2, 3, through N16b-25's batched
handler and N29a-12's sampling; the second run carries the planted failure cluster, whose failing worlds leave
failure captures through N29a-11's ring flush); (2) a verdict on the first run issued through `issue_verdict` (the conformance harness's entry, over the
`frob.verdict/1` record of `frob task verdict`; the verbs that issue comparison verdicts arrive at M3 and their
scripts re-run this step group); (3) `frob store status
--json` (classes, pinned bytes, reasons); (4) `frob store collect --dry-run --budget 1MB --json` and then the
real collection with a key; (5) assertions: no object of the verdict's closure is gone (`frob evidence trace
<claim> --verify` exits 0); sampled traces of the third run are gone and `frob telemetry query` on them exits
3 with FROB-REF-888 and reason `collected`; `frob artifact read <digest>` of a removed object is refused with
its tombstone; (6) `frob store collect --class evidence-pinned --force --key <seed>-force --json` (a real
collection, so it carries a key; without one the call is a dry run) is refused with FROB-REF-813, exit 3,
naming the pins, and the pinned bytes of `store.status` are unchanged afterwards; (7) the 10⁴-cycle battery
through the gate's tier run, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers
--features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-CHRON-12` (the entry fixes the 10⁴ cycles and plants the four controls
below in the same run); the gate row is entered from its receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
record_gate -- --gate GATE-CHRON-12 --receipt <hash>` by a session that did not implement the work. Log to
`target/e2e-logs/store_retention/<utc>.ndjson`: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command,
exit code, wall time, evidence block; `metric` lines with bytes per class before and after, (pinned objects
removed, threshold 0), (removals without a tombstone, threshold 0), (citations of collected objects refused,
of attempted); on failure the object hash, its pins and the cycle's seed. The `summary` line names
GATE-CHRON-12.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-720's control: a claim printed after its evidence was collected (must be refused).
- Negative controls: a sweep that ignores pins of kind `cursor`; a tombstone written after the file deletion
  outside the transaction (a crash between them leaves a removal with no tombstone; the crash test catches
  it); pins taken in a second transaction after the verdict (the fault test finds a verdict with an unpinned
  closure); age measured from last access instead of creation (non-deterministic sets).
- Metamorphic: two dry runs with no intervening change return identical lists; collection is idempotent.
- Determinism: the set removed is a pure function of (store state, budget, `now`); checked on repeat.

## Considerations

- RISK-39 (retention deletes the interval that explains a decision): early signals are a citation of a
  collected object being refused and an `evidence.trace` closure that does not resolve; mitigations are the
  pins taken with verdicts, tombstones and this gate.
- OPEN-CHRON-4 (owner: the owner of the machines): the failure-capture window and the default store budget per
  machine. Fail-closed default: 30 days and 64 GB; both are configuration read at each cycle and recorded in
  its receipt.
- Collection is bounded per cycle (a byte and a time limit) so that it never starves publishing; it runs in the
  daemon's single writer.
- Everything derivable by replay (a sampled trace of a replayable world) is cheap to lose; what cannot be
  regenerated (a real log, a qualification receipt) is in the pinned class by construction.
