## Background

Long work in this system is a *job*: a simulation, a training run, a campaign. An agent submits it, may lose
its connection, may cancel it; the daemon may restart; a device may fail in the middle of a command buffer. The
job protocol is the set of rules under which, whatever happens, the ledger stays consistent, every admitted job
remains discoverable, nothing partial is published as a result, and no device resource leaks. Its publishing
half already exists on the CPU: the chunk-publishing transaction with its lease fence and committed frontier.
This bead puts device workers behind it and completes the job side at milestone M2.

The pieces, from the surface chapter's protocol:
- **States.** Submission ends in `refused` (admission) or `admitted`. An admitted job becomes `running`,
  `failed_to_start` or `cancelled`. A running job may be `preempted` and resumed, and ends `complete`,
  `failed` or `cancelled`. Each terminal receipt counts completed, failed, refused and unstarted trials, and
  the four counts sum to the planned number. Checkpoint and resume events are recorded. Every transition is
  identified by (job, logical world, episode, tick).
- **Chunks.** Each (world, episode) has a fixed logical chunk partition that does not depend on how the device
  was sharded. One transaction enforces the lease generation, the expected predecessor and a contiguous commit
  frontier. A duplicate identical chunk reuses its original receipt. Different bytes for a committed extent
  are a replay error and the job fails as indeterminate. On restart the daemon ignores unattached chunks and
  rebuilds from the last committed checkpoint.
- **Cancellation.** At the kernel-batch boundary: request, drain in-flight device work, discard unpublished
  state, acknowledge with the last committed tick per world. The receipt reports outstanding device work at
  the moment of the request and the observed cancellation latency. A dropped handle is not a cancellation
  receipt.
- **The ledger.** A FrankenSQLite database under the daemon's state root, with the daemon as its only writer.
  Job logs persist independently of any client connection.

Seed done-condition: device rows of GATE-CHRON-04 and GATE-CHRON-11; GATE-TETHER-08. Tier Solid.

Rejected alternatives: letting each device worker write the ledger directly (the engine's concurrent-writer
mode is reported to have a snapshot-drift defect; one writer avoids it and makes the commit order total);
resuming an episode on a different device family after a loss (bits would differ from the committed chunks
unless the bitwise class holds across the families).

## Scope

This bead extends the job ledger of N32a-08 to devices; it does not define it. In: two columns added to the
job row (`device`, `shape_class`); the device causes of the existing transitions; device workers and how they
publish; the two chunk scales; the lease manager that calls the ledger's lease row for device events (a failed
device chunk that moves worlds, a shard split, a stall, a device loss, a restart); device-fault handling;
cancel on devices (draining device work, releasing device resources) and its acknowledgement; what a daemon
with device workers adds to restart recovery; resource accounting on stop; the device lanes of the fault
battery.
Out, with the bead that has it: the job ledger itself, built for the CPU at M1 and used here unchanged: the
tables `job`, `job_event`, `job_log` and `run_lease`, the eight states and twelve legal transitions, the rule
that each transition is one transaction with its event row and that a terminal state writes its receipt in the
same transaction, the lease generation as a ledger row, and the five steps of restart recovery (N32a-08, on
which this bead depends). This bead first carried its own job row, transition list and event row; they are
withdrawn in favour of that bead's, and sections 1, 2 and 8 below say only what devices add. The receipt and
acknowledgement types and the rule that maps trial outcomes to the four counts (N29a-05).
Also out: the publishing transaction (BD-091) and stop semantics (N29a-05), reused unchanged; the job verbs
(N29a-23); the gate measurement and script (N29a-24); the idempotency transaction, cursors and big integers at
the surface (BD-211); the daemon process `frobd` that hosts the engine (N32a-14); slot dispatch, size classes
and preemption policy (M3 beads); the fence itself (BD-175 with N21b-07); the decisions on chunk length and
lease granularity (N29b-18, whose decided L this bead takes as given, and N29b-19, which measures this protocol
on two devices: this bead is built on its default of one lease generation per run, and the two-device gate row
of N29a-24 waits for it).

## Technical approach

**1. The job row** is the table `job` of N32a-08, written only by the job engine in `frob-verbs`: job
identifier, principal and namespace, verb, canonical request digest, run identifier, state, planned trials and
the four counts, the unit, the size class, the budget reservation, the admission sequence and the terminal
receipt. This bead adds, by an additive migration, the columns `device` and `shape_class` (the device and the
shape class the job was assigned). What this bead first listed as columns lives elsewhere in that ledger: the
lease generation in `run_lease`, the last transition as the newest `job_event` row, and the idempotency key
with its unique index on (principal, verb, key) in the binding table of the admission transaction (N32a-09 at
M1, completed by BD-211).

**2. State machine.** The table of N32a-08, unchanged: `admitted → running | failed_to_start | cancelled`;
`running → preempted | complete | failed | cancelled`; `preempted → running | cancelled | failed`. Each
transition is one ledger transaction that appends a `job_event` row, whose payload for a change of state
carries from, to, cause, commit_seq, the trace identifier and the wall time (the last two outside the event's
digest); an illegal transition is a typed internal error and is never written; terminal states write the
terminal receipt in the same transaction. All of that is specified and tested there. What this bead adds is
the device causes: `failed_to_start` with a device that could not be opened (the device refusal recorded);
`failed` after the last retry of a device chunk (FROB-REF-607), after a chunk conflict (9 below) and after a
budget stop; `cancelled` after the device drain of 7; the `recovery` event after a restart with device
workers.

**3. Workers.** One device worker per device (one host thread per CUDA context; one Metal worker per process).
A worker never touches the ledger: it sends publish requests over an in-process channel to the daemon's single
writer and receives receipts. It holds a `Lease { run, gen, holder }`.

**4. Two chunk scales.** A *device chunk* is the unit of device commit: the control intervals between two host
synchronizations (the shorter of one rollout segment and the number of intervals that fit the cancellation
latency target of 250 ms at the 99th percentile). A *logical chunk* is the record's fixed extent of L control
ticks per (world, episode, stream). After each device chunk's `Completed` receipt the worker drains the
statistics ring; when a world's logical extent is complete (or its episode ends) the worker writes the column
file and sends the publish request with the world's digest at the extent's last tick (d and x from the ring's
digest section, N29ap1-01). Telemetry of an
incomplete extent stays in the worker's host buffers and is *unpublished state*.

**5. Lease manager.** `reassign(run)` increments the run's generation whenever the assignment of worlds to
workers changes: a retry after a failed device chunk that moves worlds, a shard split, a device loss, a
restart. Each worker calls `renew` at every logical chunk boundary before publishing; a publish under an older
generation is refused (FROB-REF-875: re-acquire and replay from the last committed tick).

**6. Device faults.**
- *A failed device chunk* (rule of the commit fence): the committed slab was never written, so the worker
  restores from it and resubmits, at most four attempts with growing waits; host buffers filled from the
  failed attempt are discarded first. Each retry is counted in the receipt. After the last attempt:
  FROB-REF-607.
- *A stalled device:* the watchdog marks it `Stalled` (FROB-REF-615) and the supervisor restarts the worker
  from the last committed epoch; the lease generation is incremented.
- *Device loss* (FROB-REF-601): the generation is incremented; the lost worker's unpublished state is gone.
  Worlds with an episode in flight are resumed on a device of the *same family and fingerprint* from the
  snapshot at their last committed logical boundary (stored by N29ap1-02, restored with `restore` of N29a-18),
  or, for a world with no stored snapshot there, by re-running it from its address (tick zero from the address
  and the settled-pose cache) under the same step plan; a resumed world re-produces committed chunks byte for
  byte, which publish as duplicates and return the original receipts. This needs the step and the publishing
  transaction only, not the replay engine of BD-203 (which compares against a record and depends on this bead
  for its own jobs and cancellation on devices).
  If no such device is available the affected trials end `refused` with the device refusal; they are not moved
  to another family mid-episode (DESIGN: bits would differ from the committed chunks, a conflict by
  construction). Whole episodes not yet started may run anywhere the job's request allows.

**7. Cancel.** On a cancel request the engine sets the run's stop word; each worker observes it at its next
kernel-batch boundary, lets the device chunk in flight complete or abandons it at the fence, publishes any
extent that became complete, discards the rest, releases its device resources (arena lease, stream-ordered
pool allocations, snapshot staging) and acknowledges with the last committed tick per world, the outstanding
device work at the request and the observed latency. The engine writes `cancelled` with the terminal receipt.
A budget stop follows the same drain with cause `budget-stop` and refusal FROB-REF-702 and ends in `failed`,
as the state table of N32a-08 has it (`cancelled` is reserved for a requested cancel).

**8. Restart.** The recovery steps are those of N32a-08 (open the ledger and take the writer lock; enqueue
every `admitted` job with no start record; for every job in `running` or `preempted` ignore unattached chunks,
increment the lease generation and rebuild from the last committed checkpoint; complete the bookkeeping of a
cancel that was requested and not acknowledged; record a `recovery` event per job touched). When the daemon
starts with device workers this bead adds, at those steps: (a) on opening, the ledger's integrity check is
run before any worker is created; (b) unattached column files (written by a worker, never committed) are
ignored and queued for removal; (c) each interrupted job is rebuilt per world: the frontier gives the last
committed tick, and a device worker resumes from the snapshot at that boundary or by re-running from the
address, on a device of the same family and fingerprint (6 above); (d) the resumption is recorded in the `recovery` event with the
device and the new lease generation. Terminal counts after a restart still sum to planned.

**9. Conflict.** Different bytes for a committed extent: the publishing transaction marks the run
`determinism-violated` and refuses (store cause FROB-REF-878); the engine fails the job as indeterminate and
the surface reports the chunk conflict as FROB-REF-810 with exit 9 (two codes, one per layer, as N32a-08
states; the receipt carries both). Serving memoized results of the node kinds involved stops.

**10. Resource accounting.** Every worker exit path (complete, cancel, fault, restart) ends in a release
routine that returns the free-slot count, the arena's lease table and the pool's outstanding bytes to their
pre-job values; the engine records these three numbers before and after each job.

**11. Layout.** `crates/frob-verbs/src/jobs/{row.rs, states.rs, engine.rs, restart.rs}` exist from N32a-08;
this bead adds the migration in `row.rs`, the device paths in `engine.rs` and `restart.rs`, and the new files
`crates/frob-verbs/src/jobs/lease_manager.rs` and `crates/frob-chronicle/src/device/worker_publish.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, with N29a-24: the device rows of GATE-CHRON-04 (the chunk protocol under seeded
      fault schedules with INV-CHRON-01 to -04 holding and each planted violation refused 100 of 100, now with
      real device workers), the device rows of GATE-CHRON-11 (full await-point coverage of the publish and
      submit paths; every failure minimized to a regression that replays in under one second; dual-run
      agreement on 100 small jobs), and GATE-TETHER-08 (TARGET): under seeded faults (late lease, overlapping
      ranges, a reset reusing tick zero, a kill between chunk write and publish, a daemon restart) the frontier
      stays contiguous, a duplicate chunk reuses its receipt, a conflicting chunk is flagged, terminal counts
      sum to planned, a cancel names the last committed tick, and no device resource leaks.
- [ ] Every device path ends in a transition of the table of N32a-08 written as one transaction with its
      event row (that property is that bead's; here it is re-run with device workers), and the migration that
      adds `device` and `shape_class` leaves every M1 job row readable.
- [ ] A device chunk failed by the fault-injection seam is retried from the committed slab and the published
      chunks equal those of the unfaulted run; after four failures the job reports FROB-REF-607.
- [ ] After a device loss mid-episode, resumed worlds publish duplicates for committed extents and new chunks
      whose chain continues; with no same-family device the trials are `refused` and counted.
- [ ] A daemon killed at a random instant and restarted leaves every admitted job discoverable by key, no hole
      in any frontier, and no unattached file referenced by any row.
- [ ] A cancel is acknowledged with the last committed tick per world equal to the store's frontier, and the
      three resource numbers return to their pre-job values in 10³ cancel cycles.
- [ ] The ledger has exactly one writer thread (asserted in the engine).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `device_causes_use_legal_transitions`: each device cause of section 2 (device not opened, retries exhausted,
  chunk conflict, budget stop, drained cancel, restart) produces exactly its transition of the table of
  N32a-08; the exhaustive table test and `transition_writes_event_atomically` are that bead's and are re-run
  here with a device worker attached.
- `job_row_migration_additive`: a ledger written by the M1 engine opens after the migration with `device` and
  `shape_class` null on old rows.
- `worker_never_writes_ledger` (the worker type has no ledger handle; compile-fail).
- `logical_extent_spans_device_chunks`: L = 256 with device chunks of 12 intervals: an extent completes after
  the 22nd device chunk and is published once.
- `failed_device_chunk_discards_host_buffer`: ticks from a failed attempt never reach a column file.
- `retry_then_607`: the fault-injection seam fails one device chunk three times and lets the fourth attempt
  through: the published chunks equal the unfaulted run's and the receipt counts 3 retries; with four failures
  the job ends `failed` with FROB-REF-607 and its four counts still sum to planned.
- `device_loss_resume_same_family`: duplicates return original receipts; chain continues.
- `device_loss_no_family_refuses_trials`: a loss with only a device of another family present: the trials in
  flight are counted in `refused` with FROB-REF-601, none is resumed on the other family, and whole episodes not
  yet started run there when the request allows it.
- `late_worker_fenced_875`: a worker paused across a `reassign` publishes and is refused.
- `restart_recovery_steps`: a ledger with one running job, two unattached files and one admitted job: after
  `restart` the first is resumed, the files are queued for removal, the third is enqueued.
- `cancel_ack_from_store` (the worker's in-memory tick counter is corrupted in the test; the acknowledged last
  committed tick per world still equals the frontier rows); `budget_stop_702` (state `failed`, cause `budget-stop`, receipt counts the cut-short trials in `unstarted` and `interrupted`).
- `conflict_fails_job_indeterminate`: a worker that publishes different bytes for a committed extent gets
  FROB-REF-878 from the store; the job ends `failed`, the envelope reports FROB-REF-810 with exit 9, the receipt
  carries both codes and both hashes, and the run is marked `determinism-violated`.
- `resources_return_to_baseline`: 10³ jobs ended in rotation by completion, cancel, injected fault and daemon
  restart: after each, the free-slot count, the arena's lease table and the pool's outstanding bytes equal their
  pre-job values.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/job_protocol_devices.sh` (owned by N29a-24) starts `frobd`, submits jobs with `frob sim run …
--sync-wait-ms 0`, injects faults (a kill of the daemon, a kill of a worker between column write and publish,
a command-buffer failure through the gateway's seam, a device-loss simulation) and reads results with the job
verbs. This bead's step groups are `states-*`, `publish-*`, `restart-*` and `cancel-*`; their lines carry the
job identifier, state transitions with (world, episode, tick), lease generations, frontiers before and after,
receipts reused, retry counts and the three resource numbers.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-711 on devices: two identical publishes yield one row and one receipt; a crash after commit returns the
  original receipt.
- Negative controls (mutants that the battery must catch): a worker that publishes its partial extent on
  cancel; a restart that trusts unattached files; a resume on another device family (conflict, FROB-REF-878);
  a retry that keeps host buffers from the failed attempt (a duplicate tick in a chunk, caught by the content
  hash against the replayed chunk); a cancel that returns before the device chunk has completed (leaked pool
  bytes).
- The invariants that are the oracles: INV-CHRON-01 to -04; with the surface: one job and one budget charge
  per (principal, verb, idempotency key) under any interleaving and any crash point; killing `frob`,
  `frob-mcp` or `frob-tui` at any instant leaves the daemon's ledger consistent and every admitted job
  discoverable by its key; a cancelled wait is not a cancelled job unless the call asked for that; a cancelled
  job drains.
- Determinism: the published chunks of a job are independent of where faults occurred (compared by chain heads
  against an unfaulted run).

## Considerations

- OPEN-CHRON-5 (owner: Tether lead): with one generation per run, a fault on one device fences the healthy
  device's worker too, which must renew at its next boundary; per-shard generations with a versioned
  world-to-shard map avoid that pause. Default: one per run; the two-device rows of the gate are recorded under
  the default and re-run if the decision changes.
- On Metal the wait on a command buffer cannot be interrupted; a cancel during a long device chunk is bounded
  by the device chunk length, which is why that length is tied to the 250 ms target.
- The surface chapter says the surface owns leases and the publishing transaction and gives the conflict its
  own code (FROB-REF-810); here the transaction lives in the store (FROB-REF-878) and the engine calls it, and
  both codes are reported. The split of ownership is listed in the unit's coverage notes.
- KILL-SLOT and preempt-resume are M3 matters; `preempted` exists in the state machine now so that the row
  format does not change later.
