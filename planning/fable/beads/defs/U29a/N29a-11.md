## Background

Failures are the episodes an agent most needs to see in full, and they are not known in advance. Recording
control-rate traces and full-state snapshots for every world would cost far more than the gate on telemetry
cost allows; recording them only for a sampled 1% would miss most failures. The record subsystem (Chronicle)
resolves this with a ring: a per-world ring buffer keeps the last seconds at T2 and T3 on the device and is
flushed when a world fails, so failures are always recorded in full without paying to record everything. The
ring is also frozen around a mode change or a requested tick.

T2 is the telemetry level of control-rate traces (joint states, torques, contact wrenches, estimator state);
T3 is full-state snapshots sufficient for exact restart. A ring entry therefore has two parts: one T2 row per
control tick, and one snapshot every snapshot period.

Memory decides who gets a ring. At a per-world state of tens of kilobytes, twenty snapshots covering two
seconds for each of 8,192 worlds would take several gigabytes (20 × 35 kB × 8,192 = 5.7 GB for the reference
quadruped), so full-state rings are kept for a reservoir of worlds and every other failing world is
reconstructed by replay, at the cost of one episode prefix on the CPU. The ring is an optimization, not a
requirement: any tick of any replayable episode can be recovered by replaying the world from its address.
Tier Solid.

Rejected alternatives: flushing on a host-side trigger only (the host synchronizes once per device chunk, so
up to a chunk of history would be lost without a device-side ring); copy-on-write snapshots (state is
write-dense: every step rewrites configuration, velocity, contacts and warm starts, so a fork has copied
everything after one step and has paid for indirection in every read).

## Technical approach

**1. Layout (DESIGN).** Two ring families in the device arena, both written only by `stat`-class kernels:
- *T2 ring:* per ring-holding world, D_t control ticks of the declared T2 channels (default depth 2 s: 100
  ticks at 50 Hz), as structure-of-arrays columns with a per-world write cursor (tick mod D_t).
- *T3 ring:* per ring-holding world, D_s snapshot slots of the canonical state slabs (default one snapshot
  every 0.1 s: 20 slots over 2 s), each with its tick, its state digest and its digest-chain value.
Bytes per ring-holding quadruped world: about 20 × 35 kB = 0.7 MB for T3 plus T2 columns (about 100 × 0.5 kB).

**2. Who holds a ring.** (a) Every world of the address-keyed trace sample holds both rings (and is in any case
traced continuously at T2). (b) The *ring reservoir*: a further set of worlds chosen by address, using the same
hash under the stream identifier `ring-reservoir`, sized so that total ring bytes fit the budget the estimator
gives (default: at most 10% of the arena, DESIGN). (c) All other worlds hold a *light ring*: the T2 ring only,
with no snapshots, when the budget allows, else nothing. The assignment is a pure function of the manifest and
the addresses, so replay recomputes it.

**3. Write path.** After stage 10 of each control tick the T2 kernel writes the tick's row at the cursor and
advances it. Every snapshot period the snapshot kernel (`K-snapshot`, the indexed row copy of N29ap1-02) copies
the world's state rows into the next T3 slot. Both are masked by the ring-holder bit and by a per-world `frozen` bit: a
frozen ring is not written.

**4. Triggers.** Each sets `frozen` for the world and records the trigger tick t*:
- *Failure:* the failing bit, raised by a failure monitor at its first anomaly. The ring keeps running through
  the terminal event and a post-window of 0.2 s after it (so that stills at −0.5 s, 0 s and +0.2 s around the
  terminal event exist), then freezes; at the default depth the capture is therefore the 2 s window that ends
  0.2 s after the terminal event. The post-window exists only where the environment's reset mode leaves a
  world running after its terminal event (explicit reset, the default); under masked automatic reset the
  capture ends at the terminal tick and the +0.2 s still is reported unavailable.
- *Mode change:* a declared mode switch (a controller layer change, a fallback taken, a shield engaging, an
  actuator entering thermal protection). The ring freezes half a depth after the switch, so the capture is
  centred on it.
- *Requested tick:* a tick named in the run manifest (`capture.at`) or the first firing of a device-side
  predicate named there (`capture.when`). Same centring.
A world may be frozen once per episode by default; a second trigger while frozen is recorded as a T1 event
with the reason `ring-busy` and no capture. The window of a capture is computed by one function,
`ring::window(manifest, trigger)`, which the replay capture of N29ap1-03 also calls, so the two paths cannot
disagree on which ticks a capture covers.

**5. Flush.** At the next device commit the host sees the `frozen` bit in the statistics ring, takes a read
lease on the ring slabs, and copies the world's ring: T2 rows are unrolled in tick order and appended to the
world's T2 streams as ordinary chunks (with a leading gap record covering the ticks before the ring's oldest
entry, reason `level-not-enabled`); each T3 slot becomes a stored snapshot (container of N29a-18) with its
BLAKE3 content hash in the `snapshot` table, after the host recomputes the state digest from the bytes and
compares it with the device's digest for that tick (the host side of N29ap1-02; a mismatch is a fault recorded
in the divergence ledger). The capture is recorded with class `failure-capture`, its trigger and `source =
ring`. The host then clears `frozen` at a between-chunks write lease if the episode continues. The recording
steps are one routine, `flush::record_capture`, fed either from ring slabs (here) or from replay buffers
(N29ap1-03).

**6. Worlds without a full ring.** For a failing world with no T3 ring the flush records what exists (T2 rows
or nothing) and writes a `reconstruct` marker: the episode's address, the trigger and the tick range wanted, as
a ledger row that stays pending until a capture clears it. `sim.replay --capture failure` (N29ap1-03) honours
the marker by replaying the world from its address (or its nearest stored snapshot) on its originating tier and
capturing the range; many such worlds are replayed in one batch.

**7. Admission.** Ring bytes are part of the buffer layout and of the memory estimate; a manifest whose ring
depth and reservoir do not fit is refused at admission with the device memory refusal (the estimate exceeds the
device budget), never trimmed silently. The ring depth and snapshot period are manifest fields.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/device/{ring_t2.rs, ring_t3.rs, trigger.rs, flush.rs}`;
`crates/frob-kernels/src/stat/k_ring.rs` with its dialect twin.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every failing world of a device run has, after the run, either a full capture (T2 for the 2 s window
      that ends 0.2 s after the terminal event, and the T3 snapshots of that window) or a pending `reconstruct`
      marker with its address, trigger and tick range (that replay turns the marker into the same capture,
      byte for byte, is N29ap1-03's criterion).
- [ ] A mode change at tick t yields a capture covering [t − 1 s, t + 1 s] at the default depth; a requested
      tick likewise; a predicate's first firing likewise.
- [ ] Physics digests are identical with rings on and off, and with any reservoir size.
- [ ] The set of ring-holding worlds is identical under slot permutation and shard split.
- [ ] Each flushed snapshot's host-recomputed digest equals the device digest for its tick.
- [ ] A ring configuration that exceeds the budget is refused at admission with the bytes requested and
      available.
- [ ] A second trigger on a frozen world is recorded as an event and does not corrupt the first capture.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ring_unroll_order`: depth 100, cursor at 37: rows come out as ticks t − 99 … t.
- `failure_capture_window`: terminal event at tick 500 (50 Hz, depth 100 ticks, post-window 10 ticks): T2
  covers ticks 411 to 510 and the 20 snapshots at ticks 415, 420, …, 510 (the 0.1 s period) exist; the oldest
  snapshot is 85 ticks (1.7 s) before the terminal event, and tick 400 (the 2 s lead) is not in the ring.
  With the depth set to 115 ticks (2.3 s) the ring holds 23 snapshots, 400 to 510; with 110 ticks (2.2 s)
  it holds 22, the oldest at 405.
- `mode_change_centred`; `requested_tick_centred`; `predicate_first_fire_capture`.
- `frozen_ring_not_written`: after freeze, further ticks do not move the cursor.
- `second_trigger_ring_busy`.
- `reservoir_is_address_function` (property): the set of ring-holding worlds is equal under 100 slot
  permutations and under shard counts, and two runs on the same addresses keep rings for the same worlds.
- `ring_bytes_formula`: computed from the design byte model, so that a change to the model changes the stated
  default knowingly: 20 × 8,192 × 35,000 bytes = 5,734,400,000 bytes, about 5.7 GB of T3 ring for 8,192
  quadruped worlds with full rings, refused on a 24 GB card whose arena budget for rings is 10%; a reservoir
  of 512 worlds needs about 0.36 GB and fits.
- `flush_digest_tripwire`: one flipped bit in a T3 slot after the device digest was taken is caught.
- `reconstruct_marker_fields`: a failing world outside the ring reservoir gets one pending marker with its
  address, trigger, and the tick range `ring::window` gives (the byte comparison with a ring capture,
  `reconstruct_marker_equals_ring`, is N29ap1-03's).
- `leading_gap_recorded`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step group `ring-*` of `scripts/e2e/sim_replay.sh` (N29a-19, which depends on this bead and on N16b-25): a
4,096-world device run of a task with a planted failure cluster, `frob sim run <reference quadruped> --task

BD-090/BD-207 and N29a-12's default trace rate; an absent device is `Skipped`, never passed); `frob telemetry
failures <run> --json` lists the failing
worlds; for three of them with a ring, `frob telemetry query <run> --stream kinematics --world <w> --json` must
return the last 2 s with the leading gap record, and for three without, the run's pending markers must name
them with their windows (the step group `reconstruct-*` of N29ap1-03 then turns the markers into captures and
compares one ring capture with its replay byte for byte). Lines carry the world address, trigger, window in
ticks, snapshot count, digests, the markers written and the ring bytes admitted.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic known answer: 20 × 35 kB × 8,192 = 5.7 GB.
- Negative controls: a ring written by arrival order of events (captures differ under slot permutation); a
  reservoir chosen by arrival order instead of by address (the address-function property fails); a
  flush that reads the ring before the commit receipt (the lease check refuses it); a ring that keeps writing
  after the terminal event (the window test fails); a capture taken from a world's ring after the slot was
  reused by a new episode (the address check on the ring header catches it).
- Metamorphic relation: splitting a rollout at a ring snapshot and resuming equals running it whole.
- Determinism lanes: captures twice on one device; `batch-cpu` across worker counts; ring against replay.

## Considerations

- OPEN-CHRON-2 (owner: Chronicle lead): ring depth, default 2 s. OPEN-AUTOPSY-4 (owner: Chronicle lead): the
  reservoir size per device; default sized by the budget estimator, with every other fork point reconstructed
  by replay.
- The fork ladder used by counterfactual debugging starts at 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 s before a failure (N27a-03).
  What the default ring serves, by arithmetic on the defaults above (DESIGN arithmetic, stated identically in
  N27a-03; an earlier draft of this bead said the ring serves all four leads without replay, which its own
  window contradicts): a failure capture is the 2 s window that ends 0.2 s after the terminal event, with a
  snapshot every 0.1 s: 20 snapshots, the newest 0.2 s after the failure and the oldest 1.7 s before it (at
  50 Hz control and a terminal event at tick 500, the window covers ticks 411 to 510 and the snapshots are
  at 415, 420, …, 510; the T2 rows reach back to tick 411, the snapshots to 415). Leads 0.5 s and 1 s land
  on a ring snapshot (ticks 475 and 450). Lead 0.25 s lies between two snapshots and is reached from the
  snapshot 0.3 s before the failure (tick 485) by at most 0.05 s of replay. Lead 2 s (tick 400) lies 0.3 s
  before the oldest ring snapshot and needs replay from an earlier stored snapshot or from the episode
  start. Serving the 2 s lead from the ring takes a 2.3 s ring (23 snapshots, ticks 400 to 510); a 2.2 s
  ring's oldest snapshot is at tick 405, still 0.1 s short. That depth is an input to the open question on
  ring depth (OPEN-CHRON-2, decided in N29b-18), not a change made here.
- Flushed failure captures are retained while the run is the head of a lineage or is cited by an open
  comparison, bisect or autopsy, and then for 30 days by default; sampled traces of non-failing worlds are
  evicted first under the store budget.
- A ring holds privileged state; captures inherit the run's privacy class.
