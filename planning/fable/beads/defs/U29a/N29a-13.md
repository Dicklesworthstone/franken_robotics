## Background

Distributional summaries (the 95th percentile of knee torque, the distribution of friction utilization) are
what an agent most often wants from a batch of thousands of worlds. The usual tools for them, mergeable
quantile sketches of the compactor and digest families, are excluded from the evidence path here: their output
depends on insertion and merge order, so the same worlds replayed under a different shard split give different
bits.

Counterexample on the smallest scale: a compactor of capacity two that keeps the lower of each sorted pair
summarizes the stream 1, 2, 3 as {1 with weight 2, 3} and the stream 3, 2, 1 as {2 with weight 2, 1}, so the
medians are 1 and 2; integer bin counts are 1, 1, 1 in both orders. A second case from the kernel chapter: a
two-centroid sketch that merges its closest pair, fed {0, 3, 5, 9}, ends as (8/3 with weight 3; 9 with weight
1) in the order 0, 3, 5, 9 and as (1.5 with weight 2; 7 with weight 2) in the order 0, 9, 5, 3.

The replacement is a histogram of integer counts over integer bins. By the decision that evidence aggregates
are integers (ADR-CHRON-03), its merge is integer addition, which is commutative and associative, so the result
has the same bits under any scheduling. A quantile is then reported honestly: as the bounds of the bin that
contains it. This bead is the host-side definition (the mathematics, the layouts, the query); the device kernel
and the verb are separate beads. Tier Solid.

## Scope

The program's text describes integer log-bin histograms twice: from a channel value quantized by a declared
quantum (the telemetry chapter, this bead) and from the `f32` bit pattern (the numerics rule, N35-21). Both
are built, each in one bead, on one bin function, and they never compete for the same data (DESIGN, the same
statement in N35-21): **a declared telemetry channel is always binned in the quantized form of this bead**
(everything `K-x-hist` accumulates, the wide-count drain totals and `telemetry.histogram` returns), because a
channel's quantum gives exact bins for small values, a declared range with an overflow bin on each side, and
bounds that print as exact decimals in the channel's unit, and because one channel must never have two
histograms with different bin edges. The pattern form (`Hist14` of N35-21) is only for `f32` values that have
no declared quantum and is never stored or returned for a declared channel.

In: quantization by the channel's quantum; the (m, s) layout over the one bin function (magnitude range, sign
sides, overflow bins) and the bounds of its bins; the two default layouts; counts, saturation and overflow
bins; merge; the quantile query in exact integer arithmetic; canonical bytes; the exhaustive and property
tests of the proof obligation.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the octave-splitting index and its inverse as code: `frob_core::hist::int_bin` and `int_bin_bounds`, written
  once by N35-21, a dependency. This bead calls them and holds no second copy; the formula is restated below
  because the layout and the proof obligation are stated in its terms.
- the `f32`-pattern histogram `Hist14`, its serialized form and the schema definition `QuantileInterval` that
  admits this bead's quantile object as one of its two shapes: N35-21.
- the device kernel `K-x-hist` (N29a-14); the host drain handler (BD-207) and the drain of device bins into
  ledger totals (N21c-40); the verb (N29a-15); choosing which channels get histograms (channel declarations,
  BD-207).

## Technical approach

**1. Quantization.** A channel declares a quantum u. A value x becomes q = round-half-even(x/u), saturated to
the channel's integer range, with one overflow bin on each side. The result depends only on the bits of x.

**2. Bins.** The index of a magnitude below 2ᵐ and the bounds of a bin are `frob_core::hist::int_bin(a, s)` and
`int_bin_bounds(b, s)` (N35-21); this bead adds the magnitude limit m, the two sign sides and the overflow
bins around them. The rule those functions implement, in this bead's terms:
Parameters: m magnitude bits and s sub-bucket bits (s < m). For a magnitude a = |q|:
- a < 2ˢ: one bin per integer, exact: `bin(a) = a`;
- 2ˢ ≤ a < 2ᵐ: the octave e = ⌊log₂ a⌋ is split into 2ˢ equal sub-buckets with index
  `(a >> (e − s)) & (2ˢ − 1)`, and `bin(a) = ((e − s + 1) << s) + ((a >> (e − s)) & (2ˢ − 1))`;
- a ≥ 2ᵐ: the overflow bin of that sign.
There are (m − s + 1)·2ˢ bins per sign, and every bin's width is at most 2⁻ˢ of its lower edge. Bounds of bin
b: for b < 2ˢ, [b, b]; otherwise with o = (b >> s) − 1 and j = b & (2ˢ − 1): lower = (2ˢ + j) << o, upper =
lower + 2ᵒ − 1. The value q = 0 goes to bin 0 of the non-negative side; the negative side starts at magnitude 1.

**3. Defaults (DESIGN).** Run-level histograms: m = 24, s = 5, 640 bins per sign, relative resolution 2⁻⁵ =
3.125% (printed as 3.1% in the telemetry chapter and bounded as "at most 3.2%" in the numerics rule; one
number), 64-bit totals. Per-world T0 histograms: m = 16, s = 3, 112 bins per sign with 16-bit saturating counts,
about 450 bytes per channel per world ((2 × 112 + 2 overflow) × 2 bytes = 452).

**4. Arithmetic.** Count-leading-zeros, shifts, masks and integer add: exact on every backend. e = (bit width
− 1) − clz(a).

**5. Counts and merge.** A histogram is `Histogram { m, s, quantum, neg: [C; B], pos: [C; B], under: C, over:
C }` with B = (m − s + 1)·2ˢ. Merging two histograms with equal (m, s, quantum) is element-wise integer
addition; with unequal parameters it is a typed error. For the 16-bit per-world form the addition saturates at
65,535; saturating addition of non-negative counts is still commutative and associative (the result is
min(Σ, 65,535) in any grouping), and a count of 65,535 is read as "at least 65,535" and flagged.

**6. Quantile query.** Input: a probability as a rational c/d with 0 < c/d ≤ 1. Let N be the total count (all
bins, both overflow bins). The target rank is r = ⌈c·N / d⌉ computed in 128-bit integers. Walk the bins in
ascending value order (negative overflow; negative bins from the largest magnitude down; non-negative bins up;
positive overflow), accumulating counts, and stop at the first bin where the cumulative count reaches r. Return
`{ rank: r, of: N, bin: [q_lo, q_hi], interval: [(q_lo − ½)·u, (q_hi + ½)·u], count_in_bin, exact: q_lo ==
q_hi, saturated: bool, overflow: none | under | over }`. The half-quantum widening makes the interval a true
enclosure of the raw values that quantize into the bin. The query never returns a point it did not measure:
there is no interpolation inside a bin, and a quantile in an overflow bin is reported as a one-sided interval
with `overflow` set. N = 0 is refused with FROB-REF-888 (a metric computed over missing samples). The result
type has no accessor that returns a single number. In an envelope it is one of the two shapes of the schema
definition `QuantileInterval` (N35-21; required by the wording rules wherever a quantile comes from a
histogram): as the verb prints it (N29a-15), `{ "p", "rank", "interval": [lo, hi], "bin": [q_lo, q_hi],
"count_in_bin", "exact", "overflow" }`, with the interval's bounds as exact decimal strings in the channel's
unit, counts as decimal strings, and `of` and `saturated` carried once in the enclosing histogram object as
`count` and `saturated`. Non-finite samples never reach a bin count as such: a NaN faults its world and
enters no bin, and an infinity saturates into the overflow bin of its sign (the kernel's rule, N29a-14); the
`non_finite` count of the pattern form has no counterpart here.

**7. Canonical bytes.** Header (m, s, quantum as a rational, count width) followed by non-zero bins as (side,
bin, count) in ascending order, little-endian. Equal histograms have equal bytes.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/hist/{bins.rs, histogram.rs, quantile.rs, encode.rs}`; `bins.rs` is
the (m, s) layout (sides, range, overflow) over `frob_core::hist::int_bin` and contains no index arithmetic of
its own.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] PO-CHRON-04, width: an exhaustive check over all 16-bit magnitudes shows that every value falls in exactly
      one bin, that bin bounds contain it, and that each bin's width is at most 2⁻ˢ of its lower edge, for
      (m, s) = (16, 3) and for (16, 5).
- [ ] PO-CHRON-04, merge: merge is associative and commutative with the empty histogram as identity (property
      test), for both count widths.
- [ ] INV-CHRON-06 for histograms: counts are identical under 100 permutations and 100 shard splits of 10⁴
      samples.
- [ ] Bin counts are (m − s + 1)·2ˢ: 640 for (24, 5) and 112 for (16, 3).
- [ ] The compactor example: streams 1, 2, 3 and 3, 2, 1 at u = 1 both give counts 1, 1, 1 and the median query
      (1/2) returns the bin [2, 2] in both.
- [ ] A quantile is always an interval that contains the true sample quantile of the raw data (property test
      against a sorted copy), and its relative width is at most 2⁻ˢ plus one quantum.
- [ ] A quantile falling in an overflow bin is reported as one-sided; an empty histogram is refused.
- [ ] Merging histograms with different parameters is a typed error.
- [ ] One bin function: `frob-chronicle` contains no octave-splitting index of its own; the layout calls
      `frob_core::hist::int_bin` (test `layout_index_is_core_int_bin` over the (24, 5) and (16, 3) layouts; a
      source check finds no second implementation).
- [ ] The quantile object validates against the schema definition `QuantileInterval` in its channel-histogram
      shape; a scalar in its place fails.

## Considerations

- **Two descriptions of the bins.** This bead bins a quantized integer; the numerics chapter describes the bin
  as the sign, the exponent and the top five mantissa bits of the `f32` pattern. They are the same octave and
  sub-bucket rule applied to different integers, and for an integer value from 32 up to 2²⁴ at s = 5 they name
  the same interval. This bead's form governs for every declared channel (DESIGN, stated in Scope and in
  N35-21) because the channel's quantum gives exact small values and a declared range; the bit-pattern form
  (`Hist14`, N35-21) serves values without a quantum, and the kernel is written so that it bins whichever
  integer it is handed.
- Relative resolution 2⁻⁵ = 3.125% at s = 5 is a design default; a channel that needs more declares a larger s and pays
  bins: 1,216 per sign at (24, 6).
- A saturating 16-bit count reaches 65,535 after 21.8 minutes of one bin at 50 Hz; per-world T0 histograms are
  for per-episode use, run-level histograms for totals.
- A histogram of a channel with gaps reports the gap count beside N; a quantile over a channel with missing
  samples is refused rather than computed from what remains.
