## Background

The project claims that any world of a batch can be taken out and re-run by itself: from its address, with its
identities, giving the same bits on the same backend whatever batch it originally ran in, and agreeing with the
CPU reference inside the determinism class the device attains. Incumbent simulators document bitwise replay of
a whole batch on the same GPU; none documents a bitwise re-run of one world out of its batch, and one holds a
world equal to its isolated run only to 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴. The property is therefore worth claiming only if it is
exact, and only after it has been measured. This bead is the measurement:

> GATE-CHRON-01 (Known answer; M2; TARGET): 1,000 random worlds from a GPU run replay with identical digests on
> the same backend, and inside the class on `batch-cpu`; the same worlds replayed alone, in a batch of another
> size and among other co-tenants give the same digests.

Determinism classes used here. D1: the same bits on `batch-cpu` (single precision), CUDA and Metal; a
hypothesis per device family until its own gate passes. D2: the same bits run to run on one device family and
across batch size, slot, co-tenants, bucket capacity and shard count. D3: agreement inside a recorded envelope
together with agreement of task decisions. "Inside the class on `batch-cpu`" means: bit equality if the family
has attained D1, otherwise the D3 comparison (state error in physically scaled units inside the envelope until
the first divergence, and the decision-level test).

This is a test bead. It also owns the end-to-end script for the replay verb, because the gate *is* the verb
used at scale. Tier Solid; verification tier G5, all tiers.

## Scope

In: the source run; the draw of 1,000 worlds; the four replay compositions; the CPU lane with its class rule;
the planted mismatches; the script `scripts/e2e/sim_replay.sh`; the gate rows per device family.
Out: the mechanisms (BD-203, BD-204, N29a-18, N29a-11); the general batch-composition gate over the whole step
(BD-181); the decision-level equivalence test itself (BD-177), which this bead calls.

## Technical approach

**1. Source run, per device family.** The reference quadruped on rough ground under its baseline controller,
8,192 worlds, one episode of 10 s (500 control ticks, two logical chunks at L = 256), seed 20261122,
`trace.rate = 1/100`, rings at their defaults, profile `device-replay`. The run records, for every world, the
state digest and digest-chain value at each chunk end, and per-tick digests for traced and ring-holding worlds.

**2. The draw.** 1,000 world addresses drawn without replacement by the Philox stream `gate-replay-draw` keyed
by the run seed, so that the draw is recomputable. The draw is stratified to include every failing world if
there are fewer than 100 and at least 100 otherwise, because failures are the worlds replay exists for.

**3. Lane A, same backend, four compositions.** Each drawn world is replayed from tick zero by address and,
where one exists, from its latest stored snapshot:
- A1 *alone*: a batch of one in the smallest shape class that admits the model;
- A2 *another batch size*: the 1,000 worlds together in a batch of 1,024 (another capacity than 8,192), in a
  slot order drawn by seed;
- A3 *other co-tenants*: each world in a batch of 256 with 255 foreign worlds of another seed and task;
- A4 *another shape class*: the 1,000 worlds in a bucket with larger capacities (for example contact capacity
  64 and row capacity 320 instead of 32 and 160), where the family's batch-composition gate covers capacity.
For every replay, every recorded comparison point must be equal: chunk-end d and x for all, per-tick values
where recorded. Measured value per composition: (worlds with all points equal, 1,000).

**4. Lane B, `batch-cpu`.** Each drawn world is replayed on `batch-cpu` in single precision under the same
step plan. If the family holds D1 (its own gate is current), the requirement is bit equality at every
comparison point: (worlds equal, 1,000) with threshold equality. If the family is D2 for itself and D3 against
others, the requirement is the D3 comparison: the one-sided 95% upper bound (exact binomial) on the rate of
discordant decisions over the 1,000 worlds is at most 1%; the bound on the net rate is at most 0.5%; continuous
outcomes are equivalent inside the fidelity ledger's margins; and for every world the scaled state error stays
inside the envelope up to its first recorded divergence, which is filed in the divergence ledger. The row
states which rule applied. With zero discordant worlds out of 1,000 the upper bound is about 0.3%.

**5. Planted mismatches (negative controls).** Run on `batch-cpu` and on one device:
- a stored chunk digest with one flipped bit: the replay reports the chunk and tick, exit 2;
- a build whose digest folds padding lanes: lane A4 fails;
- a build with a batch-wide observation normalizer (two worlds observing 1 and 3 and normalized by their batch
  mean see −1 and +1; the first world alone sees 0): lane A1 fails;
- a build whose reduction tree is shaped by capacity: lane A2 or A4 fails;
- a snapshot with its packet queue stripped: the from-snapshot replay differs from the from-zero replay.

**6. Gate record.** Per device family: the five counts (A1 to A4 and B) of 1,000, the class rule applied in B
with its measured bounds, mismatches filed, controls caught of 5, the commit, the device fingerprint digest,
the seed. A family that is absent or not admitted is `not run` with the reason; under the no-GPU variant the
gate is recorded with `batch-cpu` as the source backend (lanes A1 to A4 in both precisions) and lane B is the
double-precision `strict` profile compared at D3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On each admitted device family: 1,000 of 1,000 worlds equal in each of A1, A2, A3 and A4.
- [ ] Lane B: 1,000 of 1,000 bit-equal where D1 holds; otherwise the D3 rule passes with its measured bounds
      recorded; the rule applied is printed in the row and in every replay envelope.
- [ ] From-snapshot and from-zero replays agree for every drawn world that has a snapshot.
- [ ] The five planted controls are caught.
- [ ] Every mismatch found has a divergence-ledger row with step, kernel and quantity (from the teleport
      localizer) and none is dropped from the count.
- [ ] The script exits non-zero below any threshold, naming the first failing world address, composition and
      comparison point with both digests.
- [ ] The gate rows cite claim identifiers that resolve; the bead log carries the measured counts (for example
      `worlds_replayed: 1000`, `digest_mismatches: 0`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/g5_replay.rs`:
- `draw_is_recomputable`: the same run seed gives the same 1,000 addresses; failing worlds are included by the
  stated rule.
- `composition_builders`: A1 to A4 produce batches with the stated sizes and with the drawn world at a slot
  that differs from its original slot.
- `comparator_counts_points`: a world with 2 chunk-end points and 500 per-tick points compares 502 points.
- `binomial_upper_bound`: 0 discordant of 1,000 gives 0.2991% at one-sided 95% (1 − 0.05^(1/1000)); 3 of 1,000
  gives 0.7735%.
- `each_control_fails_its_lane` (five cases on `batch-cpu`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sim_replay.sh` (this bead owns it; step groups `snapshot-*`, `ring-*`, `digest-*`, `replay-*` and
`teleport-*` come from N29a-18, N29a-11, BD-203 and BD-204):
1. `frob sim run --tier gpu-cuda|gpu-metal --workload Q --worlds 8192 --seconds 10 --telemetry T2 --trace-rate
   1/100 --determinism device-replay --seed 20261122 --key src --json`.
2. `frob sim replay --run <r> --world <w> --alone --key a1-<w> --json` for ten drawn worlds from the command
   line (the user's path), asserting exit 0 and `result: Bitwise`; the remaining worlds of A1 and all of A2 to
   A4 through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g5 -- GATE-CHRON-01 --backend <family>`, which drives the
   same verb implementation in batches and writes a receipt.
3. `frob sim replay --run <r> --world <w> --tier batch-cpu --key b-<w> --json` for the same ten worlds, then
   lane B through the harness.
4. The planted controls.
5. NS-02 as a scripted scene: one world made to produce a non-finite value on the device by a planted fault is
   replayed with `--tier batch-cpu`; the report must stop at the first differing or refused step and name the
   stage and the contact.
It sources the shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/sim_replay/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line
has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time and the evidence block (class,
tier, determinism class applied, identities, seed, placement receipt). `metric` lines carry the five counts
with threshold 1,000, the discordance bounds with their thresholds (1% and 0.5%), and replay wall time per
world. `assert` lines carry both digests. On failure: the world address, composition, tick, both digests, the
divergence-ledger row and the paths of the source run and the replay. The `summary` line names GATE-CHRON-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The known answer is the record itself: the digests the source run committed.
- Arithmetic known answers for the bound: 1 − 0.05^(1/1000) = 0.2991%.
- Negative controls as listed; the padding counterexample ((−0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0, so padding with +0.0 changes
  a digest over bits) and the normalizer counterexample are the two smallest cases behind controls two and
  three.
- Metamorphic relation MR-01 (batch-composition invariance: a world's digests are identical alone, in any
  batch, slot, co-tenancy, bucket capacity and shard count of one device family; mutations: a reduction tree
  shaped by capacity; a batch-wide normalizer).
- Determinism lanes: this gate is the across-composition lane for replay; it is run twice on one machine with
  equal outcome vectors.

## Considerations

- Compute: 5,000 single-world-equivalent replays of 10 s per family; batched lanes take seconds on a device,
  and lane A1 (1,000 batches of one) is the slow one. It may be run as 1,000 sequential admissions into one
  resident environment; the disclosure says which.
- KILL-D1 decides which rule lane B uses for a family; the gate does not fail because D1 failed, it records the
  rule. It fails if the applicable rule is not met.
- If lane A fails on a family, bitwise replay, forks, attribution and memoized episodes are all unsupported on
  it; the milestone's stop decision then falls back to the no-GPU variant for those features.
- A world whose replay is refused (a missing identity, an evicted kernel cache entry that cannot be rebuilt
  offline) counts as not equal and is listed with its refusal.
