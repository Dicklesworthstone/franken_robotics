## Background

A fast contact model can hand a policy energy that no actuator supplied. A learning agent finds such energy
quickly: a hopping policy that gains height from a penetration correction, a gait that pushes along the
diagonal of a pyramidal friction cone where the model grants 41% more traction than the physics. The result is
a design or policy that wins a comparison because of the simulator. The energy ledger makes this measurable,
and three monitors watch for it in every world.

| Monitor | Quantity | Flag | Threshold (TARGET) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residual sign | r = Δ kinetic + Δ potential − (actuator work + external work − all dissipation), per step; created energy is Σ max(0, r) over a window | `energy-created` | Created energy above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier; above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee |
| Contact passivity | The net work a contact does on the bodies over one contact episode, from touch to release | `contact-active` | Net positive work above the tier's residual bound: the contact returned more than it absorbed |
| Cone excess | Friction utilization ‖f_t‖ / (μ·f_n) per contact | `cone-excess` | Utilization above 1.05 for more than 1% of stance time; a pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal |

`energy-created`, with this window and this denominator, is the one flag of the created-energy audit; the
dynamics chapter applies it and defines no other label or threshold. A flagged episode keeps its result and
gains the flag. A design or policy whose advantage in a comparison coincides with flagged episodes is typed
`model-conditional`, and the exploitation detectors read the same flags.

This bead builds the three monitors over the ledger kernel's outputs, the verb `fidelity.energy` (created-
energy totals per world and contact mode for a run, with the worlds that crossed the bound; read; M2), and
measures the energy half of GATE-CHRON-08 ("the created-energy audit flags every planted trampolining and
cone-excess exploit and no honest fixture"). Tier Solid.

Rejected alternatives: discarding flagged episodes (it would move failures of the simulator out of the
denominator and hide them); a single global energy tolerance per run (a short burst of created energy is
diluted by a long quiet episode, which is why the rule is windowed).

## Technical approach

**1. `energy-created`.** Inputs per world, per control tick, from the ledger kernel's window buffer: the tick's
created energy c_t = Σ over the tick's physics steps of max(0, r) and the tick's absolute work exchanged w_t =
Σ (|joint work| + |external work| + |dissipation|). For every window of 0.5 s (25 control ticks at 50 Hz;
windows are half-open (a, b] and slide by one tick) compute C = Σ c_t and W = Σ w_t.
- Fast tiers: flag when C > 0.01·W.
- Referee: flag when C > 10⁻⁶·W.
A window with W below the ledger's quantum times the tick count is compared in absolute terms against the
tier's residual bound (so that a body at rest with W = 0 is not flagged by rounding and is not exempt from
real creation). The first flagged window's end tick is recorded as a T1 event with C, W and the contact mode
that contributed most. The comparison is made in the ledger's integers (C·100 > W), so the flag is identical
under any world order.

**2. `contact-active`.** A *contact episode* is the maximal run of ticks in which one feature pair is active,
from touch to release (from the Contact stream's make and break events). Its net work on the bodies is N =
Σ (normal and tangential contact power)·h over the episode, counted positive when the contact adds mechanical
energy to the bodies. Energy stored in compliance during the episode and returned before release nets to zero
by construction. Flag when N exceeds the tier's residual bound for the episode: bound = (10⁻³ on
single-precision tiers, 10⁻⁹ on the referee) × the absolute work exchanged by that contact over the episode,
with the same absolute floor as above. An episode still open at the end of an episode of the world is closed
at the terminal tick.

**3. `cone-excess`.** Per contact and tick, utilization u = ‖f_t‖ / (μ·f_n) for f_n above the contact law's
noise floor. Per world: stance time T_s = number of (contact, tick) pairs with load; excess time T_x = pairs
with u > 1.05. Flag when T_x > 0.01·T_s. The maximum utilization and its feature pair are recorded. A
pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal; the circular cone admits 1 in every direction.

**4. Flags and storage.** Flags are bits on the `episode` row with their first tick and magnitudes; a flagged
episode keeps its result. Run-level counts per flag and per contact mode are integer aggregates. Monitors run
on the host from T0 window data for every world (no trace needed for `energy-created`), from the Contact
stream for traced and failing worlds, and in-kernel as integer counters for `cone-excess` and for episode net
work on all worlds (two counters and one double-float accumulator per contact slot).

**5. The verb `fidelity.energy`** (cluster `fidelity`, kind `read`, M2, capability `telemetry:read`, schema
`frob.fidelity.energy/1`). `frob fidelity energy <run> [--world <w>] [--by mode|world] [--limit n] --json`.
Output: per contact mode (none, sticking, sliding, impact, limit) the created-energy total with its unit and
the absolute work exchanged; the worlds that crossed the bound, each with its worst window (ticks, C, W,
ratio), paged; the counts of episodes flagged `energy-created`, `contact-active` and `cone-excess` over the
planned denominator; the thresholds in force with their provenance (TARGET, or the re-baselined value and its
date); the frontier. Evidence: class `Tested`, the run's tier and determinism class. Exit 0 with no flagged
world; exit 2 with any (a finding). Refusals: FROB-REF-840 (sealed run), FROB-REF-888 (the window buffer or
Contact stream is missing for the scope asked), FROB-REF-802.

**6. Fixtures (in `tests/fixtures/energy_audit/`).**
- *Planted trampolining (DESIGN numbers):* a 1 kg body dropped from 0.2 m onto a stiff penalty contact (k = 10⁶ N/m, no
  damping) at h = 2 ms, near the explicit stability limit h·√(k/m) = 2: it gains height bounce after bounce.
  Expected: `energy-created` and `contact-active`.
- *Planted position correction:* a position-level penetration correction that removes d = 1 mm in one step at
  h = 2 ms gives the body 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event from nothing. Expected: `energy-created`.
- *Planted cone excess:* a block pushed along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone with μ = 0.5: utilization
  reaches √2 ≈ 1.414 for the whole push. Expected: `cone-excess`.
- *Honest fixtures:* the same drop under the convex compliant law at the default step (raises neither energy
  flag); the same push under the circular cone (utilization 1.0, not flagged); the reference quadruped
  standing and trotting under its baseline controller; the damped pendulum.

**7. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/energy/audit/{created.rs, passivity.rs, cone.rs, flags.rs}`;
`crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/fidelity_energy.rs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-CHRON-08, energy half (TARGET): every planted trampolining and cone-excess exploit is flagged, and
      no honest fixture is flagged, on `batch-cpu` and on each admitted device; the measured counts (planted
      flagged of planted, honest flagged of honest) are recorded.
- [ ] The stiff penalty bounce raises `energy-created` and `contact-active`; the same drop under the convex law
      at the default step raises neither; a block pushed along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone raises
      `cone-excess` with utilization √2 within 10⁻³; the circular-cone push is not flagged.
- [ ] With the audit switched off (a conformance build), a comparison between the bouncing policy and an honest
      one is won by the bouncing policy; with it on, the comparison's advantage coincides with flagged
      episodes and is reported as such (the label itself is another unit's).
- [ ] The window rule is exact: a burst of created energy of 2% of the work exchanged within one 0.5 s window
      of a 20 s episode is flagged although it is 0.05% of the episode's work.
- [ ] A flagged episode keeps its result and appears in every denominator.
- [ ] Flags and totals are byte-identical under permutation of worlds and shard split.
- [ ] `frob fidelity energy <run> --json` lists the worlds that crossed the bound with their worst window, the
      totals per contact mode and the thresholds in force; reachable over MCP as `fidelity_energy`.
- [ ] On the referee the threshold 10⁻⁶ is applied; on fast tiers 1%.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `window_rule_boundaries`: C/W = 0.0100 is not flagged, 0.0101 is; windows are (a, b]; a burst straddling two
  windows is caught by the sliding window.
- `windowed_not_global`: the 2%-in-one-window case above.
- `rest_is_not_flagged`: W = 0, C = 0 for 10 s.
- `absolute_floor`: W = 0 with C above the residual bound is flagged.
- `contact_episode_net_work`: compress 1 mm into k = 10⁵ N/m (0.05 J stored) and release: N = 0 within the
  bound; a contact that returns 0.06 J: flagged.
- `open_episode_closed_at_terminal_tick`.
- `cone_utilization_pyramid_diagonal`: f_t = (μ·f_n, μ·f_n) gives u = 1.41421.
- `cone_excess_time_fraction`: 2% of stance pairs above 1.05 is flagged; 0.5% is not.
- `flags_are_integer_decisions`: C·100 > W evaluated in integers; identical under 100 permutations.
- `position_correction_energy`: ½ × 1 kg × (0.5 m/s)² = 0.125 J per event.
- `referee_threshold`: created energy of 2·10⁻⁶ relative is flagged on the referee and not on a fast tier.
- `verb_schema_and_exit_codes`; `missing_window_buffer_888`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/energy_audit.sh`: for `batch-cpu`, each present device, and the `strict` profile: run the four
planted fixtures and the four honest fixtures with `frob sim run … --json`; for each run `frob fidelity energy
<run> --json` and `frob telemetry energy <run> --json`; assert the expected flag set per fixture, utilization
√2 on the pyramid, and exit codes (2 for planted, 0 for honest). Then the audit-off control. Log to
`target/e2e-logs/energy_audit/<utc>.ndjson`: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command,
exit code, wall time, evidence block; `metric` lines with C, W and their ratio against 0.01 (or 10⁻⁶), the net
work of the worst contact episode against its bound, the excess-time fraction against 0.01 and the maximum
utilization; (planted flagged, planted) and (honest flagged, honest) with thresholds (equal, 0); on failure
the fixture, the world, the window and the path of the run. The `summary` line names GATE-CHRON-08 (energy
half).

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-709: the stiff penalty bounce raises `energy-created` and `contact-active`; the convex law at the default
  step raises neither (exact flags; G1 · referee, lockstep). Negative control: the audit switched off, the
  bouncing policy wins the comparison.
- KA-710: a push along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone reaches utilization √2. Negative control: a
  circular-cone run must not be flagged.
- KA-354 and CX-147 (a position-level correction removing 1 mm in 2 ms gives 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event);
  CX-148 (a pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more than the cone).
- Further mutants: a global (whole-episode) ratio; compliance energy counted as creation; utilization computed
  per axis instead of by norm (the pyramid's diagonal then reads 1.0); flagged episodes filtered from the
  denominator.
- Determinism lanes: flags twice on one machine, across worker counts, across backends (integer decisions on
  equal inputs; on a device without the bitwise class the fixture margins are wide enough that the flags
  agree, and the script checks that they do).

## Considerations

- Thresholds are TARGETs and are re-baselined once at M2 against the frozen fidelity envelopes; the value in
  force and its date are printed by the verb. This bead defines no second label and no second threshold.
- RISK: an honest fixture near a threshold flaps. The fixtures above are chosen far from the thresholds; the
  standard battery's created energy is recorded against its frozen bound by the dynamics unit's gate.
- The failure-taxonomy half of GATE-CHRON-08 is recorded separately; the gate row is complete only when both
  halves have a record.
- A policy can also exploit the simulator without creating energy (for example through an estimator leak);
  those cases belong to the exploitation detectors, which read these flags among other signals.
