## Background

A long verb does not block its caller. It is admitted as a job under an idempotency key and, if it does not
finish inside a bounded wait, the caller receives a *job handle* with exit code 8 ("accepted and still
running", which is not a failure). From then on the caller needs five operations: list my jobs, read one job's
state, wait on it for a bounded time, find a job again after the connection died, and cancel it. These are the
`job` cluster of the verb registry, first milestone M2. They are how an agent lives with work that outlasts a
turn.

Two decisions of the surface shape them.
- **Wire cancellation of a waiting call ends the wait, not the job; `job.cancel` cancels the job.** A client
  timeout is not a decision to discard admitted, charged work; the job is recoverable by its key.
- **Never resubmit blindly after a transport failure.** When the connection, the client or the daemon died, or
  a deadline passed before a receipt arrived, the job may be running. The caller reconnects and calls
  `job.find` with the idempotency key; one job and one charge exist or none do.

The job state machine, the device workers and the cancel path are the job-protocol bead; this bead is the
caller-facing surface over them. Tier Solid.

## Scope

In: the five verbs with registry entries, schemas, the `job` envelope block, exit codes and refusals; the
bounded wait; lookup by key; listing with pinned pagination; cancel and its acknowledgement as seen by the
caller.
Out: the state machine and workers (BD-210); admission, budgets and the idempotency transaction (BD-211);
MCP-specific progress notifications, resources and the optional Tasks adapter (the MCP beads); scheduling
(M3).

## Technical approach

**The `job` block of the envelope** (present on exit 8 and on every job verb): `id`, `state`, `verb`,
`progress { done, total, unit }`, `eta_s` with its interval (or `uncalibrated`), `poll_after_ms`,
`last_committed` (the commit sequence number and, per world on request, the last committed tick),
`submitted_at`, `receipt` (the terminal receipt when the job has ended). Progress always has a denominator.

| Verb | Kind | Capability | Input | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `job.list` | read | `job:read` | optional `state`, `verb`, `since`; `limit`, `cursor` | Jobs in the caller's namespace with state and progress, ordered by (submission instant, job identifier), paged against the committed frontier |
| `job.status` | read | `job:read` | `job`; `--worlds` for per-world last committed ticks; `--events n` for the last n transitions | The job block; the transition tail; retry counts; the device and shape class |
| `job.wait` | read | `job:read` | `job`, `sync_wait_ms` | The job's final envelope if it ends inside the wait, else the job block with exit 8 |
| `job.find` | read | `job:read` | `verb`, `idempotency_key` | The job bound to (principal, verb, key), or `found: false` |
| `job.cancel` | admin | `job:cancel` | `job`, `reason` | The cancellation acknowledgement and the terminal receipt |

**`job.wait`.** Waits on the job's completion against a timer of `sync_wait_ms` (default 5,000 and maximum
30,000 on MCP; on the CLI the default is to wait to completion with progress events, and `--sync-wait-ms`
bounds the wait; 0 returns at once; out of range is FROB-REF-802). If the job ends first, the result is the
job's own stored final envelope, byte for byte what the submitting call would have returned (exit 0 or 2, or
its refusal), with `idempotent_replay: false` and the job block added. If the timer wins: exit 8, the job
block, and `next_actions` naming `job.wait` again. Cancelling the waiting call ends the wait and leaves the
job running.

**`job.find`.** Looks up the unique index (principal, verb, key). Found: the job block and, if the job has
ended, its final envelope with `idempotent_replay: true`. Not found: `found: false`, exit 0, and the statement
that no job and no charge exist for that key, so resubmission with the same key is safe. A key bound to a
different resolved request is not this verb's concern; the resubmission would be refused with FROB-REF-803.

**`job.cancel`.** Requires the scope (exit 5 without it). Steps: (1) if the job is terminal, return its
receipt with `already: <state>` and change nothing; (2) set the stop word; (3) wait for the workers'
acknowledgement, bounded by the device-chunk length plus a margin; (4) return `{ state: "cancelled",
last_committed: [per world: world, episode, tick], outstanding_at_request, latency_ms, discarded_ticks,
receipt }`. The receipt's four counts sum to the planned trials. If the acknowledgement does not arrive in
time (a stalled device), the verb returns exit 8 with state `cancelling`; the watchdog path completes the
cancel and `job.status` shows it. `job.cancel` is idempotent: repeating it returns the same acknowledgement.

**`job.status`.** Reads only the ledger; works while the daemon is busy and after it restarts. With
`--events n` it returns the last n transitions, each identified by (job, logical world, episode, tick).

**`job.list`.** Namespace-scoped. Paged with the store cursor; a cursor used with changed filters is refused
(the store's cursor mismatch refusal, FROB-REF-896, surfaced with the surface's cursor code). The listing
states its `frontier`.

**Failure classes.** Domain failures (a refusal, a missing scope) return an envelope with exit 3 or 5.
Protocol failures (unknown verb, schema failure) return exit 3 with FROB-REF-801 or -802. A transport failure
has no envelope; exit 9 if the CLI survives the daemon. An unknown job identifier is a domain refusal
(FROB-REF-802 with pointer `job`), not an empty answer.

**Registry.** Each verb has its `registries/verbs.toml` entry with schemas `frob.job.<name>.in/1` and
`frob.job.out/1`, `first_milestone = "M2"`, `idempotent = true`, `task_clusters` containing every cluster
(job tools are base tools an agent always sees), and examples such as `frob job wait j:77c0… --json`.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/{job_list.rs, job_status.rs, job_wait.rs, job_find.rs,
job_cancel.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A job submitted with `--sync-wait-ms 0` returns exit 8 with a handle; `job.wait` with a bounded wait
      returns exit 8 until the job ends and then the job's final envelope, identical in its `result` and
      `evidence` to what a waiting submission returns.
- [ ] Every `job.wait` returns within `sync_wait_ms` plus 500 ms with a result or a handle.
- [ ] After the client process is killed during a wait, `job.find` with the key returns the job in 100 of 100
      trials, and the job's result is retrievable; with an unused key it returns `found: false`.
- [ ] A cancelled wait (the waiting process receives an interrupt) leaves the job running; `job.cancel` ends
      it.
- [ ] `job.cancel` names the last committed tick per world, equal to the store's frontier, reports outstanding
      work and latency, and its receipt's four counts sum to planned; repeating it returns the same answer.
- [ ] `job.cancel` without the scope is refused with exit 5; on a terminal job it changes nothing.
- [ ] `job.list` shows only the caller's namespace, each job once under concurrent submissions, with progress
      as "k of n".
- [ ] `job.status` answers from the ledger with the daemon stopped (CLI in-process read) and after a restart.
- [ ] All five are reachable through `frob --json` and as MCP tools (`job_list`, `job_status`, `job_wait`,
      `job_find`, `job_cancel`), with identical envelopes on both surfaces.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `job_block_fields`: every field present; `progress` has `done`, `total`, `unit`; integers above 2⁵³ as
  strings.
- `wait_returns_final_envelope`: the envelope stored at job end is returned unchanged.
- `wait_timer_wins_exit_8`; `wait_zero_is_immediate`; `wait_out_of_range_802` (30,001 ms on MCP).
- `wait_cancelled_job_keeps_running`.
- `find_by_key_found_and_not_found`; `find_is_scoped_to_principal_and_verb`: the same key under another verb
  or another principal is not found.
- `cancel_ack_fields`; `cancel_idempotent`; `cancel_terminal_job_no_change`; `cancel_scope_required`.
- `cancel_stalled_device_returns_cancelling`.
- `list_namespace_only`; `list_pinned_pagination`; `list_cursor_mismatch_refused`.
- `status_events_tail`: transitions carry (world, episode, tick).
- `unknown_job_is_refusal`.
- `surface_parity`: the CLI and MCP envelopes of each verb are equal apart from the per-surface wait default.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step groups `verbs-*` of `scripts/e2e/job_protocol_devices.sh` (N29a-24): start `frobd`; `frob sim run --tier
<device> --worlds 4096 --seconds 60 --key job-1 --sync-wait-ms 0 --json` (exit 8); `frob job list --json`;
`frob job status <id> --events 5 --json`; `frob job wait <id> --sync-wait-ms 2000 --json` (exit 8) repeated;
kill the waiting process and run `frob job find --verb sim.run --key job-1 --json`; `frob job cancel <id>
--reason test --json`; `frob job status <id> --worlds --json` compared with `frob telemetry query`. Lines
carry the command, exit code, wall time against `sync_wait_ms` + 500 ms, the job block, and for the cancel the
acknowledged ticks against the store's frontier and the three resource numbers.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: exit code 8 for an admitted, running job; the waits of the worked session of the surface
  (a job held by repeated bounded waits until it completes); a killed connection during a wait leaves the job
  recoverable by key in 100 of 100 trials.
- Negative controls: a `job.wait` whose cancellation cancels the job (the kept-running test fails); a
  `job.find` that searches by key alone (finds another principal's job); a `job.cancel` that returns before
  the acknowledgement with a made-up tick (differs from the store); a `job.list` paged by offset (repeats a
  job under concurrent submission).
- Invariants exercised: a cancelled wait is not a cancelled job unless the call asked for that, and a
  cancelled job drains; killing a client at any instant leaves every admitted job discoverable by its key;
  the four terminal counts sum to the planned trials.
- Determinism: job verbs are reads of the ledger at a pin; their answers are equal on repeat at the same
  frontier.

## Considerations

- OPEN-TETHER-3 (owner: Tether lead): the default and maximum of the bounded wait against measured agent turn
  time and verb latency. Fail-closed defaults: 5,000 and 30,000 ms.
- The dashboard may issue `job.cancel` only under a token that holds the scope; it is an ordinary client.
- `eta_s` is an interval; until per-stratum calibration exists it is marked `uncalibrated` and widened by a
  fixed factor of 2.
- A job's stored final envelope is evidence-pinned as long as a claim cites the run; otherwise it follows the
  retention of the run record.
