## Background

A long verb does not block its caller. It is admitted as a job under an idempotency key and, if it does not
finish inside a bounded wait, the caller receives a *job handle* with exit code 8 ("accepted and still
running", which is not a failure). From then on the caller needs five operations: list my jobs, read one job's
state, wait on it for a bounded time, find a job again after the connection died, and cancel it. These are the
`job` cluster of the verb registry, first milestone M2. They are how an agent lives with work that outlasts a
turn.

Two decisions of the surface shape them.
- **Wire cancellation of a waiting call ends the wait, not the job; `job.cancel` cancels the job.** A client
  timeout is not a decision to discard admitted, charged work; the job is recoverable by its key.
- **Never resubmit blindly after a transport failure.** When the connection, the client or the daemon died, or
  a deadline passed before a receipt arrived, the job may be running. The caller reconnects and calls
  `job.find` with the idempotency key; one job and one charge exist or none do.

The job state machine, the device workers and the cancel path are the job-protocol bead; this bead is the
caller-facing surface over them. Tier Solid.

## Scope

This bead is the five verbs as code a caller reaches: their bodies in `frob-verbs`, the content of their input
and output schemas, the three fields the job verbs add to the `job` block, their exit codes and refusals,
listing with pinned pagination, the listing's operator projection (`all_agents`), event stream (`follow`) and
`dispatcher` block, and the cancel acknowledgement as the caller sees it. Each verb is thin: it
validates, calls one mechanism owned elsewhere, and shapes the envelope.

Out, with the bead that has it (each a dependency unless marked):
- the bounded wait itself: the common property `sync_wait_ms` with its range, defaults and refusal, the wait
  loop and its deadline clock, the deferred envelope with `eta_s` and `poll_after_ms`, the 500 ms timing budget
  and the server's request timeout: N32a-16. `job.wait` is that bead's attach-and-wait function behind a
  registered verb; nothing about the timer is specified here.
- the lookup by idempotency key (`find_by_key` over the unique index of the binding table) and the admission
  transaction it reads: N32a-09. `job.find` is that function behind a registered verb. The same path on the
  daemon under faults, and its campaign: BD-211 (related).
- what cancelling a waiting call does on each surface (wire cancellation, the CLI's interrupts, `on_cancel`),
  progress frames and their mapping to MCP progress and CLI progress events, and the rule that the daemon's
  drain, never the adapter's, protects device state: N32a-17, which depends on this bead and tests those
  behaviours through these verbs.
- the `job` block's six base fields: N32a-08 (through BD-210). The state machine, the workers, the stop word
  and the cancel acknowledgement inside the daemon: BD-210.
- the five registry rows (kinds, capabilities, schema names, task clusters, MCP tool names, examples, evidence
  labels) and the cluster's conformance script: VERBS-job (a dependency; this bead implements the verbs behind
  those rows).
- MCP resources and the optional Tasks adapter (the MCP beads); scheduling (M3).

## Technical approach

**The `job` block of the envelope** (present on exit 8 and on every job verb). Its six base fields are those of
the job ledger bead (N32a-08), the same as in the deferred envelope of N32a-16: `id`, `state`,
`progress { done, total, unit }`, `eta_s` with its interval (or `uncalibrated`), `poll_after_ms`,
`last_committed` (the commit sequence number and, per world on request, the last committed tick). Progress
always has a denominator. `eta_s` and `poll_after_ms` are computed by the rule of N32a-16 and are not
recomputed here. The job verbs add three fields (DESIGN; optional in the schema, always present in the answers
of the five job verbs): `verb` (the verb that created the job), `submitted_at`, and `receipt` (the terminal
receipt when the job has ended). Reason: a caller that reaches a job through `job.find` or `job.list` did not
receive it from a submitting call and cannot otherwise tell what it is, when it was admitted or how it ended.

| Verb | Kind | Capability | Input | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `job.list` | read | `job:read` (option `all_agents`: also `session:read`) | optional `state`, `verb`, `since`, `all_agents`; `limit`, `cursor`, `follow` | Jobs in the caller's namespace with state and progress, ordered by (admission sequence, job identifier), paged against the committed frontier, each page with the `dispatcher` block; with `follow`, then a stream of job events |
| `job.status` | read | `job:read` | `job`; `--worlds` for per-world last committed ticks; `--events n` for the last n transitions | The job block; the transition tail; retry counts; the device and shape class |
| `job.wait` | read | `job:read` | `job`, `sync_wait_ms` | The job's final envelope if it ends inside the wait, else the job block with exit 8 |
| `job.find` | read | `job:read` | `verb`, `idempotency_key` | The job bound to (principal, verb, key), or `found: false` |
| `job.cancel` | admin | `job:cancel` | `job`, `reason` | The cancellation acknowledgement and the terminal receipt |

**`job.wait`.** The verb validates `job` and `sync_wait_ms` and calls the attach-and-wait function of N32a-16
on the existing job (steps 4 and 5 of that bead's loop). The timer's rules are that bead's and are restated
only so this contract can be read alone: default 5,000 and maximum 30,000 ms on MCP; on the CLI the default is
to wait to completion with progress events, and `--sync-wait-ms` bounds the wait; 0 returns at once; out of
range is FROB-REF-802. What this verb adds: if the job ends first, the result is the job's own stored final
envelope, whose `verb` is the verb that created the job and whose `result` and `evidence` are byte for byte
what the submitting call would have returned (exit 0 or 2, or its refusal), with `idempotent_replay: false`
and the job block added. If the timer wins: exit 8, the job block, and `next_actions` naming `job.wait` again.
A terminal job answers at once on any later wait. The verb has no side effect on the job: releasing the waiter
for any reason changes no job state (what a surface does when its waiting call is cancelled, and `on_cancel`,
are N32a-17's).

**`job.find`.** Inputs `verb` and `idempotency_key`, both required, because a key is scoped to (principal,
verb); on the CLI `frob job find --verb <verb> --key <key>`. The verb calls `find_by_key(principal, verb, key)`
of the admission bead (N32a-09), a read of the unique index (principal, verb, key) that never creates
anything. Found: the job block and, if the job has ended, its final envelope with `idempotent_replay: true`.
Not found: `found: false`, exit 0, and the statement that no job and no charge exist for that key, so
resubmission with the same key is safe. A key bound to a different resolved request is not this verb's
concern; the resubmission would be refused with FROB-REF-803.

**`job.cancel`.** Requires the scope `job:cancel` (`FROB-REF-720`, exit 5, without it). Input `job` and an
optional `reason` (DESIGN: free text recorded in the cancel transition, so that a later reader of the ledger
sees why work was discarded; it is an optional property of `frob.job.cancel.in/1`, which this bead writes, and
the row's inputs in VERBS-job name it). The drain
and the acknowledgement are the daemon's (BD-210); the verb's steps as the caller sees them: (1) if the job is
terminal, return its receipt with `already: <state>` and change nothing; (2) set the stop word; (3) wait for the workers'
acknowledgement, bounded by the device-chunk length plus a margin; (4) return `{ state: "cancelled",
last_committed: [per world: world, episode, tick], outstanding_at_request, latency_ms, discarded_ticks,
receipt }`. The receipt's four counts sum to the planned trials. If the acknowledgement does not arrive in
time (a stalled device), the verb returns exit 8 with state `cancelling`; the watchdog path completes the
cancel and `job.status` shows it. `job.cancel` is idempotent: repeating it returns the same acknowledgement.
The row's chapter refusal FROB-REF-546 (a cancel aimed at one audit-lane episode of a running campaign, from
M3) is raised by N28b-13's extension of this verb, which reads lane membership from the campaign controller;
this bead raises no chapter refusal of its own.

**`job.status`.** Reads only the ledger; works while the daemon is busy and after it restarts. With
`--events n` it returns the last n transitions, each identified by (job, logical world, episode, tick).

**`job.list`.** Namespace-scoped. Paged with the cursor of N32a-10 (`frob.cursor/1`); a cursor used with
changed filters is refused with FROB-REF-806 (exit 3), whose cause is the store's cursor-mismatch refusal
FROB-REF-896 (the division recorded in N29-REF). The order key is N32a-10's declared key for this source,
(admission sequence, job identifier): the admission sequence is the counter the admission transaction assigns
(N32a-09), so the order is submission order and does not depend on wall clocks (a clock jump is in the fault
menu); membership is pinned to the first page's frontier and a job's mutable fields are read at page time with
`as_of`. The filters `state`, `verb` and `since` (submission instant) are optional properties of
`frob.job.list.in/1`, named by the row. The listing states its `frontier`.

Every page also carries the row's `dispatcher` block, a required property of `frob.job.list.out/1` with exactly
five fields: `slot_reserve` (the fraction of slots held for the interactive class), `job_cap_per_environment`,
`quantum_s`, `ageing_period_s` (OPEN-TETHER-4; defaults 0.05, 64, 5 s and 600 s until N32a-22 decides them and
updates the daemon's default configuration) and `config_digest` (the configuration digest N32a-14 computes over
the scheduler's parameters, recorded in every job receipt and reported by `frob doctor --json`). The handler
reads all five from the daemon's configuration as it is in force and has no source of its own (no constant in
`frob-verbs`); in process, without a daemon, it reads the configuration the daemon would start with, the one
`frob doctor --json` reports on that path. This is the one place the dashboard's Queue and Devices panes and
KILL-TUI's fallback read the reserve and the other constants.

Two options serve the dashboard's event stream (N32b-06) and the operator view (N32b-03, N32b-08), both M3
consumers of this verb (DESIGN, requested by them; ADR-TETHER-10: the dashboard keeps no private interface):
- `all_agents: true`, whose `option_capabilities` entry in the row is `session:read` (the operator scope that
  `session.show --all` also needs). With it the listing carries other principals' jobs with exactly the fields
  job identifier, namespace, verb, size class, slot count and state, and never a field of a result, a design or
  a scene: the operator row type has no such field, so the projection cannot leak one. The scope refusal
  (FROB-REF-720, exit 5, without `session:read`) is the dispatch check of option scopes (N32b-01); the
  visibility rule for callers without the option (other principals' jobs only as counts by size class and
  state) is N32b-03's, applied in dispatch after token verification, so these verbs carry no per-principal
  logic of their own. Until tokens and namespaces exist there is one local principal and the option changes
  nothing visible.
- `follow: true` (the paginated option every paged read has; N32a-10's segment rule) in its streaming form on
  the daemon: after the last page the `frob.rpc/1` request stays open and the daemon sends each later segment as
  it commits, as one progress frame per job event visible to the caller (admitted, refused, started, chunk
  committed with progress `done`/`total` and unit, estimate updated, preempted, resumed, cancelled, completed,
  failed; each frame with the job, the world, episode and tick where they apply, the daemon's instant and the new
  `frontier`), until the client closes. Late events are never spliced into delivered pages. On the CLI the frames
  are printed as line-delimited JSON (`frob job list --follow --json`), which is also KILL-TUI's fallback; on MCP
  they are progress notifications by N32a-17's mapping (N32a-17 depends on this bead and tests that surface).
  Without the daemon (in-process CLI) `follow` returns the next segment per call, as N32a-10 specifies. Frames
  are notifications: a displayed total is always read from an envelope.

**Evidence.** Every answer of the five verbs about a job is *operational* (the rows' label): no claim,
`evidence.claims` empty. The one exception is by construction: when `job.wait` or `job.find` returns the job's
stored final envelope, that envelope keeps the evidence block the submitting verb produced (for a `sim.run`, its
run evidence), because it is that verb's answer replayed, not a new answer about the job; the job block beside
it is operational.

**Failure classes.** Domain failures (a refusal, a missing scope) return an envelope with exit 3 or 5.
Protocol failures (unknown verb, schema failure) return exit 3 with FROB-REF-801 or -802. A transport failure
has no envelope; exit 9 if the CLI survives the daemon. An unknown job identifier is a domain refusal
(FROB-REF-802 with pointer `job`), not an empty answer.

**Registry.** The five rows of `registries/verbs.toml` are entered by the registry bead of the cluster
(VERBS-job) and this bead implements what they name; the registry row governs every value of a row, and the
values are: input schema `frob.job.<name>.in/1`; output schema `frob.job.<name>.out/1`, one
per verb (`frob.job.list.out/1`, `.status.out/1`, `.wait.out/1`, `.find.out/1`, `.cancel.out/1`), each
embedding the one `job` block definition; `first_milestone = "M2"`; `idempotent = true`; capabilities
`job:read` for the four reads and `job:cancel` for `job.cancel`; task clusters: `job.wait` and `job.cancel`
are base tools that every MCP tool list carries (not counted against a cluster's seven tools), `job.find` and
`job.status` are in `operate`, `job.list` in `operate` and `fleet`; MCP tool names `job_list`, `job_status`,
`job_wait`, `job_find`, `job_cancel`; examples such as `frob job wait j:77c0… --sync-wait-ms 30000 --json` and
`frob job find --verb nemesis.search --key nem-1 --json`. This bead writes the content of the ten schemas.

**Layout.** `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/{job_list.rs, job_status.rs, job_wait.rs, job_find.rs,
job_cancel.rs}`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A job submitted with `--sync-wait-ms 0` returns exit 8 with a handle; `job.wait` with a bounded wait
      returns exit 8 until the job ends and then the job's final envelope, identical in its `result` and
      `evidence` to what a waiting submission returns.
- [ ] Every `job.wait` returns within `sync_wait_ms` plus 500 ms with a result or a handle (the timing budget
      is N32a-16's; this line checks that the verb adds nothing to it).
- [ ] After the client process is killed during a wait, `job.find` with the verb and the key returns the job
      in 100 of 100 trials, and the job's result is retrievable; with an unused key it returns `found: false`;
      the same key under another verb or another principal is not found.
- [ ] Releasing a `job.wait` (the waiter is dropped at the dispatch interface) changes no job state and the
      job runs on; `job.cancel` ends it. (That a wire-cancelled or interrupted waiting call does exactly this
      on each surface, and the `on_cancel` variant, are measured in N32a-17.)
- [ ] `job.cancel` names the last committed tick per world, equal to the store's frontier, reports outstanding
      work and latency, and its receipt's four counts sum to planned; repeating it returns the same answer.
- [ ] `job.cancel` without the scope is refused with `FROB-REF-720`, exit 5; on a terminal job it changes
      nothing.
- [ ] The five rows served by `frob capabilities --json` equal the registry rows of VERBS-job (schema names,
      capabilities, task clusters, MCP tool names); no value of a row is defined a second time in this bead's
      code.
- [ ] `job.list` shows only the caller's namespace, each job once under concurrent submissions, in admission
      order, with progress as "k of n".
- [ ] Every page of `job.list` carries `dispatcher` with exactly `slot_reserve`, `job_cap_per_environment`,
      `quantum_s`, `ageing_period_s` and `config_digest`; under the default configuration the values are 0.05,
      64, 5 and 600, and `config_digest` equals the configuration digest `frob doctor --json` reports; after a
      changed constant and a daemon restart both the value and the digest change.
- [ ] `job.list --all-agents` rows carry exactly identifier, namespace, verb, size class, slot count and state
      (a schema walk of the operator row finds no result, design or scene field); the row's
      `option_capabilities` is `{ all_agents = ["session:read"] }`.
- [ ] `frob job list --follow --json` against `frobd` prints the pages and then one frame per job event of a
      job submitted afterwards (admitted, started, chunk committed, completed), each with a frontier strictly
      greater than the previous, until the client closes; nothing already delivered reappears.
- [ ] `job.status` answers from the ledger with the daemon stopped (CLI in-process read) and after a restart.
- [ ] All five are reachable through `frob --json` and as MCP tools (`job_list`, `job_status`, `job_wait`,
      `job_find`, `job_cancel`), with identical envelopes on both surfaces.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `job_block_fields`: every field present; `progress` has `done`, `total`, `unit`; integers above 2⁵³ as
  strings.
- `wait_returns_final_envelope`: the envelope stored at job end is returned unchanged.
- `wait_timer_wins_exit_8`; `wait_zero_is_immediate`; `wait_out_of_range_802` (30,001 ms on MCP): the verb's
  answers, produced by the wait function of N32a-16, whose own tests cover the clock, the race of completion
  and timer, and the chain of waits.
- `released_wait_changes_no_job_state`: the waiter of a `job.wait` is dropped at each frame boundary of a
  20-frame job; the job's transition log shows no transition caused by the drop and the job completes.
- `job_block_base_fields_are_ledger_block`: the six base fields equal the block N32a-08 produces for the same
  job; `verb`, `submitted_at` and `receipt` are the only additions.
- `find_by_key_found_and_not_found`; `find_is_scoped_to_principal_and_verb`: the same key under another verb
  or another principal is not found.
- `killed_client_recoverable_100` (integration, `batch-cpu` behind an in-test daemon): for seeds 0 to 99 a
  child `frob sim run --key kc-<s> --json` (16 worlds, 2 s) is killed at a seeded instant during its wait; a
  fresh `frob job find --verb sim.run --key kc-<s> --json` returns the job every time, and its final envelope
  once it ends; one job and one charge exist per key.
- `evidence_labels`: answers about a job carry operational evidence; a final envelope returned by `job.wait`
  or `job.find` carries the submitting verb's evidence byte for byte.
- `cancel_ack_fields`; `cancel_idempotent`; `cancel_terminal_job_no_change`; `cancel_scope_required`.
- `cancel_stalled_device_returns_cancelling`.
- `list_namespace_only`; `list_pinned_pagination`; `list_cursor_mismatch_refused` (806 with cause 896).
- `list_dispatcher_block` (daemon in-test): each page's `dispatcher` has exactly the five fields, equal to the
  daemon's configuration (0.05, 64, 5, 600 under the defaults) and to the digest its `doctor` answer reports;
  a configuration with `slot_reserve = 0.02` gives 0.02 and a different digest; a planted handler constant
  (a copy of the defaults in `frob-verbs`) fails this second case.
- `list_order_is_admission_order`: three jobs admitted with a clock jumped back between the second and the
  third list in admission order, not by `submitted_at`.
- `list_all_agents_projection`: the operator row type serializes exactly the six fields; a planted extra
  `result` field fails the schema walk.
- `list_follow_streams_events` (daemon in-test): after the last page, a job submitted and run to completion
  produces the frames admitted, started, one chunk-committed per committed chunk with `done`/`total`, completed,
  in commit order, each frame's `frontier` strictly increasing; closing the client ends the stream and leaves the
  job running; in process, `follow` returns the next segment per call.
- `status_events_tail`: transitions carry (world, episode, tick).
- `unknown_job_is_refusal`.
- `surface_parity`: the CLI and MCP envelopes of each verb are equal apart from the per-surface wait default.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step groups `verbs-*` of `scripts/e2e/job_protocol_devices.sh` (N29a-24): start `frobd`; `frob sim run quad-a
--tier <device> --worlds 4096 --seconds 60 --seed 20261122 --key job-1 --sync-wait-ms 0 --json` (exit 8; the
design positional and the batched tier are N16b-25's, a dependency through N32a-16); `frob job list --json`,
asserting that its `dispatcher.config_digest` equals the configuration digest of `frob doctor --json`;
`frob job status <id> --events 5 --json`; `frob job wait <id> --sync-wait-ms 2000 --json` (exit 8) repeated;
kill the waiting process and run `frob job find --verb sim.run --key job-1 --json`; `frob job cancel <id>
--reason test --json`; `frob job status <id> --worlds --json` compared with `frob telemetry query`. Lines
carry the command, exit code, wall time against `sync_wait_ms` + 500 ms, the job block, and for the cancel the
acknowledged ticks against the store's frontier and the three resource numbers.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: exit code 8 for an admitted, running job; the waits of the worked session of the surface
  (a job held by repeated bounded waits until it completes); a killed connection during a wait leaves the job
  recoverable by key in 100 of 100 trials.
- Negative controls: a `job.wait` whose release cancels the job (`released_wait_changes_no_job_state` fails); a
  `job.find` that searches by key alone (finds another principal's job); a `job.cancel` that returns before
  the acknowledgement with a made-up tick (differs from the store); a `job.list` paged by offset (repeats a
  job under concurrent submission).
- Invariants exercised: a cancelled wait is not a cancelled job unless the call asked for that, and a
  cancelled job drains; killing a client at any instant leaves every admitted job discoverable by its key;
  the four terminal counts sum to the planned trials.
- Determinism: job verbs are reads of the ledger at a pin; their answers are equal on repeat at the same
  frontier.

## Considerations

- OPEN-TETHER-3 (owner: Tether lead): the default and maximum of the bounded wait against measured agent turn
  time and verb latency. Fail-closed defaults: 5,000 and 30,000 ms.
- The dashboard may issue `job.cancel` only under a token that holds the scope; it is an ordinary client.
- `eta_s` is an interval, computed in one place (N32a-16): the cost estimate's interval scaled by the fraction
  of work remaining. Until per-stratum calibration exists it is marked `uncalibrated`, and the interval is
  the point estimate divided and multiplied by the fixed factor of 2 that admission uses for an uncalibrated
  stratum (DESIGN, stated in N32a-16 as well, so that the printed interval and the admission check agree).
- A job's stored final envelope is evidence-pinned as long as a claim cites the run; otherwise it follows the
  retention of the run record.
