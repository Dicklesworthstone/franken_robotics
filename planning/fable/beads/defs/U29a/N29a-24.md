## Background

The job protocol promises that the record stays consistent whatever fails. On the CPU that promise was
measured with a simulated device. At milestone M2 the workers are real: a CUDA context per card, a Metal
worker, device chunks committed at a fence, leases that fence late writers, a daemon that can be killed. Three
gate rows close here:

> GATE-TETHER-08 (Known answer; M2; TARGET): job protocol under seeded faults (late lease, overlapping ranges,
> a reset reusing tick zero, a kill between chunk write and publish, a daemon restart): the frontier stays
> contiguous, a duplicate chunk reuses its receipt, a conflicting chunk is flagged, terminal counts sum to
> planned, a cancel names the last committed tick, and no device resource leaks.

> GATE-CHRON-04, device row (TARGET): the chunk protocol under 10⁵ seeded fault schedules: INV-CHRON-01 to -04
> hold in every one; each planted violation (late lease, overlap, conflict, tick-zero reuse) is refused, 100 of
> 100.

> GATE-CHRON-11, device row (TARGET): simulation testing: full await-point coverage of the publish and submit
> paths; every failure minimized to a regression that replays in under one second; dual-run agreement on 100
> small jobs.

The simulation-testing design keeps a fake device honest by *dual runs*: the same small job is executed against
the fake device and against a real backend and both must leave the same ledger state, so the fake cannot drift
into testing a protocol the real workers do not follow. On the CPU the real backend was `batch-cpu`; the
device rows repeat the dual runs against CUDA and Metal workers and add faults only real devices have.

This is a test bead with the end-to-end script for the job protocol on devices. Tier Solid; verification tier
G4 (chaos), sites `daemon`, `cuda`, `metal`.

## Technical approach

**1. Two layers of testing, both required.**
- *Simulated layer* (fast, exhaustive in schedules): the lab-runtime harness with the fake device runs the
  M2 code of the publish and submit paths, including the worker-to-writer channel, the lease manager, restart
  recovery and the cancel path. 10⁵ seeded schedules (TARGET); lineage-driven selection first. Oracles: the
  four invariants below, one job and one charge per key, and the end state computed from the seed.
- *Real layer* (slow, real hardware): seeded faults injected into real jobs on each device family.

The oracles, in full: INV-CHRON-01 (a committed chunk key maps to one content hash for the life of the run);
INV-CHRON-02 (for every (run, world, episode, stream) the committed chunks are exactly the indices 0 to the
frontier); INV-CHRON-03 (one idempotency key yields one job, one budget charge and one receipt, under any
crash or retry); INV-CHRON-04 (a paginated query returns every row visible at its pin exactly once).

**2. Await-point coverage.** The harness instruments every await point on the publish and submit paths (the
list is generated from the source by the harness's instrumentation pass and committed as
`crates/frob-conformance/chaos/await_points.toml`). A crash fault must hit each at least once across the
schedules. Measured value: (points hit, points listed); the threshold is equality. A new await point without a
schedule that hits it fails the gate.

**3. Real-device fault battery.** For each device family, 200 seeded trials of a small job (512 worlds, 2
episodes of 6 s, T2 on a 1/16 sample), each with one fault drawn by seed:

| Fault | How it is injected | What must hold afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| Late lease | A worker thread is suspended across a forced `reassign`, then resumed | Its publish is refused (FROB-REF-875); nothing written; the frontier is contiguous |
| Overlapping ranges | A forced shard split mid-extent makes two workers hold parts of one extent | Non-extent publishes refused (FROB-REF-885); the extent is published once after resume |
| Reset reusing tick zero | An episode ends and the slot's next episode starts at tick zero | Both episodes present under different keys |
| Kill between chunk write and publish | The worker is killed after the column file is written and before the transaction | The file is unattached and ignored at restart; the chunk is re-produced and committed once |
| Daemon restart | `frobd` receives a kill signal at a seeded instant and is started again | Every admitted job is found by key; interrupted jobs resume from the last committed checkpoint |
| Command-buffer failure | The gateway's fault seam (Metal) or an injected launch error (CUDA) | Retry from the committed slab; published chunks equal the unfaulted run's |
| Device loss | The worker's device handle is invalidated | Resume on a same-family device, or trials `refused` and counted |
| Duplicate delivery | A publish request is delivered twice | One row; the original receipt both times |
| Conflict | One byte of a re-produced chunk is flipped before publish | FROB-REF-878, run marked `determinism-violated`, job indeterminate (exit 9) |
| Cancel at a seeded instant | `frob job cancel` from a second process | The acknowledgement names the store's last committed ticks; resources return to baseline |

After every trial the script compares the run's chain heads per (world, episode, stream) with those of an
unfaulted reference run of the same seed (except for the conflict and refused-trial cases, whose expected
outcomes are stated), checks that the four terminal counts sum to planned, and reads the three resource
numbers (free slots, arena lease table, pool outstanding bytes).

**4. Dual runs.** 100 small jobs (seeds 0 to 99; 16 worlds, 1 episode of 2 s) are each executed against the
fake device under the lab runtime and against the real worker of each device family. Because the fake's chunk
bytes are a pure function of the address while the real device produces physics, the comparison is of
*protocol state*: the sets of committed keys, frontiers, commit-sequence order per (world, episode, stream),
job transitions and terminal counts must be equal; content hashes are compared only fake-to-fake and
real-to-real. Measured value: (jobs agreeing, 100).

**5. Planted violations on devices.** The four mutants of the CPU battery (lease check removed; alignment
relaxed; conflict replaced by overwrite; key without the episode index) are run on one device family, 100
trials each: the real protocol refuses 100 of 100 and each mutant is caught by an oracle.

**6. Leak check.** 10³ cycles of submit, run for a seeded fraction of the job, cancel: after each cycle the
three resource numbers equal their baseline, and after all cycles the device's allocated bytes (as the
backend reports them) equal the value before the first.

**7. Gate records.** Three rows, each with measured values, commit, device fingerprint digests and base seed:
GATE-TETHER-08 (trials passed of 200 per family, per fault kind; leak cycles clean of 10³); GATE-CHRON-04
device row (schedules passed of 10⁵; violations refused per kind of 100; mutants caught); GATE-CHRON-11 device
row (await points hit of listed; regressions stored and their slowest replay time; dual-run agreement of 100
per family). Under the no-GPU variant of the milestone the rows are recorded for `batch-cpu` workers behind
the same daemon and say so.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TETHER-08: on each admitted device family every one of the 200 seeded fault trials ends with a
      contiguous frontier, receipts reused for duplicates, conflicts flagged, terminal counts summing to
      planned, cancels naming the last committed tick, and resource numbers at baseline.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-04, device row: 10⁵ of 10⁵ simulated schedules satisfy the oracles with the M2 code paths; the
      four planted violations are refused 100 of 100 on a real device; the four mutants are caught.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-11, device row: every listed await point is hit by a crash fault; every failure found is
      stored as a minimized regression that replays in under one second; 100 of 100 dual runs agree per family.
- [ ] 10³ cancel cycles leak nothing.
- [ ] A daemon killed at 100 seeded instants leaves every admitted job discoverable by key and every frontier
      contiguous.
- [ ] The script records `not run` with the reason for an absent device family and fails only on a measured
      value below its threshold.
- [ ] Each gate row cites claim identifiers that resolve.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/g4_job_protocol_devices.rs`:
- `fault_draw_is_seeded`: seed → (fault kind, instant) is reproducible and covers all ten kinds over 200 seeds.
- `await_point_list_matches_source`: the committed list equals a fresh instrumentation pass; a planted new
  await point makes the coverage check fail.
- `protocol_state_comparator`: equal for a fake and a real run of the same job; unequal when one commit is
  reordered within a sequence.
- `chain_head_comparator_fires`: one changed chunk in a faulted run is detected.
- `leak_detector_fires`: a planted unreleased pool allocation is reported after one cycle.
- `regression_replay_under_one_second` for every stored regression.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/job_protocol_devices.sh` (this bead owns it; step groups `states-*`, `publish-*`, `restart-*`,
`cancel-*` come from BD-210 and `verbs-*` from N29a-23):
1. Start `frobd` with a fresh state root; `frob doctor --json`; `frob spark fingerprint --json`.
2. The reference job and the user-side verb sequence (submit with exit 8, list, status, bounded waits, find
   after a killed client, cancel).
3. Ten hand-picked fault trials driven from the shell, one per fault kind (for example: `kill -9` of `frobd`
   at a seeded delay, restart, `frob job find`, `frob job wait`, then chain-head comparison with `frob run
   export` and `frob-verify` on both runs).
4. The full batteries through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g4 -- GATE-TETHER-08 --backend <family>
   --trials 200`, `… -- GATE-CHRON-04 --device-row --seeds 100000`, `… -- GATE-CHRON-11 --device-row`, reading
   their receipts.
5. The leak cycles.
It sources the shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/job_protocol_devices/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript.
Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time and the evidence
block with the placement receipt and fingerprint digest. `metric` lines carry trials passed per fault kind
with threshold, schedules passed of 10⁵, await points hit of listed, dual runs agreeing of 100, leak cycles
clean of 10³, cancel latency at the 50th and 99th percentile against the 250 ms target (reported, not gated
here). `assert` lines that compare state carry chain heads or protocol-state digests of both runs. On
failure: the fault kind, seed and instant, the first differing key, the job identifier, the minimized
regression's path and the daemon's state root. The `summary` line names the three gates.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-711 on devices (two identical publishes, one row and one receipt; a crash after commit returns the
  original receipt).
- Negative controls: the four protocol mutants; a worker that publishes a partial extent on cancel; a restart
  that adopts unattached files; a retry that keeps host buffers of the failed attempt; a cancel that returns
  early (leak detector); a fake device that reorders commits within a sequence (dual-run comparator).
- PO-CHRON-01 (the publishing transaction preserves INV-CHRON-01 to -03 under every fault of the menu) is
  re-discharged with the device-era code.
- Metamorphic relation MR-36 (a repeated idempotency key returns the original receipt; a page sequence pinned
  to a frontier shows each row once under concurrent appends) and MR-37 where the worker-process fallback is
  in use (a job run through the worker returns the digests of the same job run in process, also after the
  worker is killed and the job resubmitted).
- Determinism: for a given seed the published chain heads are independent of the fault's instant.

## Considerations

- Real-device trials need both machines; under the default compute envelope they run one device at a time for
  at most one hour, which fits 200 trials of a six-second job per family.
- A command-buffer fault seam exists only in builds with debug assertions on the Metal gateway; the trial uses
  such a build of the worker and says so in the disclosure.
- OPEN-CHRON-5: the two-device row is recorded under one lease generation per run; if per-shard generations
  are adopted the battery is re-run.
- If the lab runtime cannot be admitted, the simulated layer falls back to chaos tests at kernel-batch
  boundaries with a hand-written virtual clock; the row then records the reduced method, and await-point
  coverage is reported as not measured.
