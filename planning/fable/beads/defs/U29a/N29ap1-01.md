## Background

Replay, teleport, forks, the first-divergence search, the background referee and memoized episodes all start
from one question: are these two world states the same bits? On the batched tiers (`batch-cpu`, CUDA, Metal)
the answer must be cheap enough to ask every control tick for every world and exact on every backend. The record
subsystem (Chronicle) answers it with a per-world **state digest** computed in the kernel, and a per-world
**digest chain** that covers the whole prefix of the trajectory.

**ADR-CHRON-05: digests are integer mixes computed in-kernel; BLAKE3 is used on the host.** Reason: integer
arithmetic is exact on every backend. The device digest is a 128-bit integer mix of the raw bit patterns of a
world's canonical state slabs. It is a tripwire, not an identity (a 128-bit mix is not a cryptographic hash);
identities in the record are BLAKE3 hashes computed by the host over committed snapshots.

This bead wires the digest into the batched step: where and when it is computed, the chain, what is recorded at
each telemetry level, the emission at the commit fence, and the step-plan variant that digests every stage of a
tick for localization. It is the component of replay (BD-203) that most other work needs on its own: device
telemetry (BD-207), the job protocol on devices (BD-210), the device ring (N29a-11), the determinism lanes and
batch-independence checks read digests without needing replay. It was split from BD-203 in polishing round 1 for
that reason. Tier Solid. Crate `frob-kernels` (layer 3), with the record fields in `frob-chronicle`.

Terms. A *control tick* is one step of the controller clock; a tick runs one or more physics substeps, each of
the ten stages of the batched step (1 kinematics, 2 collision geometry, 3 broad phase, 4 narrow phase, 5 contact
compaction, 6 dynamics terms, 7 constraint rows, 8 solve, 9 integration on the group and the energy ledger, 10
sensors, observations, rewards and resets). The *canonical state slabs* are the
step plan's ordered list of slabs a snapshot must restore (configuration, velocity, anchors, warm starts,
actuator and battery state, packet queues and the rest). A *world address* is `(run_seed, world, episode)`,
four `u32` words. A *device chunk* is the unit of device work between two commit fences (bounded by the 250 ms
cancellation latency); a *logical chunk* is the record's extent of L control ticks (default 256). The *commit
fence* is `K-commit`, which writes the checkpointable state to the other of two committed slabs; the host reads
a chunk's results only after its `Completed` receipt.

Rejected alternatives: digests computed on the host from read-back state (a host round trip per tick, and it
would not exist for worlds that are not read back); a floating-point checksum (order-dependent and not exact); a
chain that folds the previous chain value into the digest itself (two runs whose states re-converge could then
never show equal digests again, which the divergence search needs).

## Scope

In: the per-tick digest d_t of every world after stage 10 of the last substep of each control tick; the digest

(BD-204); the divergence search (BD-206); the ring (N29a-11); the gate measurement (N29a-19).

## Technical approach

**1. Per-tick digest and chain.** After stage 10 of the last physics substep of every control tick, for every
active world, the exact-class kernel computes d_t = `K-x-digest`(address, canonical state slabs) as four `u32`
words (a `u128` with the first word least significant), and updates the world's digest chain
x_t = D(address, x_{t−1} ‖ d_t): the same mix applied to the eight words of the previous chain value and the new
digest, with x_{−1} = 0. Because each x_t covers the whole prefix, x_t(A) = x_t(B) exactly when the digests agree
at every tick up to t, up to the collision budget of a 128-bit mix; "the prefixes agree up to tick t" is
monotone in t, which is what makes binary search over chunks valid in the divergence search. The digest does not
fold x, so d_t stays a function of the world's state alone: two runs whose states re-converge show equal d_t
again while their chains stay different. Padding lanes are never folded (the active-lane rule of N21a-23), so a
world's digest is the same at any bucket capacity.

**2. The chain slab.** x lives in a per-world slab of four `u32` words that is on the canonical slab list's
companion list of *carried* slabs: `K-commit` copies it with the committed state and every snapshot carries it
in its header, so a continuation resumes the chain; it is not folded into d (it is not physical state).

**3. What is recorded.**
- Every world, every logical chunk (T0): d and x at the chunk's last tick. d fills the `state_digest` field of
  the chunk record (BD-091); d and x fill the digest fields of the chunk's T0 row (N29a-07).
- Ring-holding and traced worlds: d_t and x_t for every control tick, in the ring (N29a-11) and in the T2
  stream (Provenance, channels `state_digest` and `digest_chain`, kind `state`, exact, strings on the wire).
- The run digest R (the word-wise wrapping sum over worlds of D, N21a-23) once per device chunk, as a run-level
  tripwire that is independent of slot order.
- Per-stage digests are never recorded in a normal run.

**4. The `stage-digest` step-plan variant.** A variant of the step plan inserts, after each of the ten stages of
each physics substep, the stage-tagged digest (N21a-23's per-stage form, with the stage tag folded first) over
that stage's output slabs, for an index vector of selected worlds. Its outputs are records
(substep, stage, kernel name from the plan's stage-to-kernel map, four words). It is used only when one control
tick is re-run to localize a difference (BD-204, BD-206). The variant reads and never writes physics slabs: the
d_t of a tick run with and without it are equal (checked).

**5. Emission at the fence.** This bead extends `K-commit` so that, for each world, d and x at the device chunk's
last tick are written into the statistics ring slot of the same epoch. The host reads them only after the
`Completed` receipt, under the read lease of that slot. A retried device chunk recomputes the same values,
because a replay from the committed slab is bit-identical; a value read before `Completed` is a defect the fence
gate catches.

**6. Host reference.** `frob_chronicle::digest::reference(address, slab bytes)` evaluates the same mix over
host bytes in the canonical order. It is used by the snapshot host side (N29ap1-02) to check each stored
snapshot against the device's d for its tick, and by the tests below.

**7. Determinism and evidence.** The digest kernel is class `exact`, math mode `ExactInteger`, determinism D0:
the same words on every backend for the same state bits. Equality of the state bits themselves is the step's
class (D2 per backend, D1 across backends where attained). A digest comparison is evidence class `Tested` and is
printed as "digests equal", never as "states proved equal".

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-kernels/src/exact/{digest_chain.rs, stage_digest.rs}` with dialect twins for CUDA
and Metal; the `K-commit` extension in the fence module of N21b-07; `crates/frob-chronicle/src/digest.rs` (host
reference, record fields).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] PO-CHRON-06: the in-kernel digest distinguishes states that differ in any recorded bit, to the collision
      budget of a 128-bit mix: flipping each bit of each canonical slab of a pinned 4 kB corpus changes d; over
      10⁶ random single-bit flips each output bit changes with frequency in [0.49, 0.51]; the output is
      identical on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal for the corpus.
- [ ] INV-CHRON-08, digest part: a world's d_t and x_t do not depend on batch size, slot, co-tenants or bucket
      capacity (property test on `batch-cpu` in both precisions over 200 worlds; the device lanes are measured by
      N29a-19).
- [ ] The chain covers the prefix: two traces equal except at one tick have equal x before it and different x at
      and after it, while d is equal again after the states re-converge.
- [ ] The `stage-digest` variant leaves every physics digest unchanged over 200 ticks, and its records name
      substep, stage and kernel.
- [ ] The kernel side of the record: at every device chunk's last tick the statistics slot holds d and x for every
      world, and for an index vector of selected worlds the per-tick output holds d_t and x_t for every control
      tick. These are the values that BD-207, BD-210 and N29a-11 (all of which depend on this bead) publish into
      the T0 row, the chunk record, the T2 stream and the ring; the published fields are checked by those beads
      and by the stage `digest-*` below.
- [ ] Emission at the fence: 10⁵ fence cycles with retries planted give values equal to those of an unretried run,
      and no value is read before `Completed`.
- [ ] The run digest per device chunk is unchanged under 100 slot permutations of the same worlds.
- [ ] Cost: the digest reads each canonical slab of a world once per control tick (asserted from the kernel
      manifest's read footprint and by `digest_single_pass`). Its share of step time is measured by the telemetry
      cost gate GATE-CHRON-13 (N29a-17, which depends on this bead), not here.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels digest_chain` and `cargo test -p frob-chronicle digest`:
- `chain_covers_prefix`: two 300-tick traces of a `synthetic` state equal except at tick 40: x differs at every
  tick ≥ 40; d is equal at ticks after the states are made equal again.
- `chain_starts_at_zero`: x₀ = D(address, 0⁴ ‖ d₀).
- `digest_ignores_padding`: capacities 32, 64 and 128 give equal d and x for the same world.
- `digest_bit_sensitivity` (PO-CHRON-06): every single-bit flip over a 4 kB pinned state changes d; over 10⁶
  random flips each output bit changes with frequency in [0.49, 0.51].
- `digest_independent_of_slot_and_batch`: 200 worlds run at batch sizes 200 and 37 + 163 and with slots
  permuted; every (address, tick) has equal d and x.
- `stage_digest_variant_is_pure`: d_t equal with and without the variant, 200 ticks; records name substep,
  stage and kernel.
- `emission_after_completed_only`: the host reader refuses a statistics slot whose epoch has no `Completed`
  receipt.
- `retry_recomputes_same_values`: a device chunk failed and retried by the fence gives the same d and x.
- `host_reference_equals_kernel`: the host reference over read-back bytes equals the kernel's d for 1,000
  worlds on `batch-cpu`.
- `run_digest_slot_invariant`: 100 slot permutations, equal R.
- `digest_single_pass`: the kernel manifest lists every canonical slab once in its read footprint and none
  twice; a `synthetic` state of 4 kB is read as 1,024 words exactly.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/state_digest_chain.sh` has two stages. Stage `kernel` runs when this bead closes and uses only what
the bead and its dependencies deliver: `cargo test -p frob-kernels digest_chain` and `cargo test -p
frob-chronicle digest` on `batch-cpu` and on each device backend present (the tests drive the real batched step
through the backend harnesses), with every test's result logged. Stage `digest-*` is the user's path. It needs
`sim.run` on the batched tiers (N16b-25, a dependency through BD-172) and the publication of d and x into the
record (BD-207, BD-210), which depend on this bead, so it is written here and first run by
`scripts/e2e/sim_replay.sh` (N29a-19): `frob
sim run quad-a --tier batch-cpu --worlds 200 --seconds 2 --seed 7 --telemetry T2 --trace-rate 1/1 --key dc-1
--json` and the same with `--worlds 37 --key dc-2` (the same seed gives the same addresses for worlds 0 to 36,
in a batch of another size; every world is traced, so the Provenance stream holds d_t and x_t for every tick),
then `frob telemetry query <run> --stream provenance --channels state_digest,digest_chain --world 0-36 --json`
for both runs, asserting equality by address and tick; repeated on `gpu-cuda:0` and `gpu-metal` where present
(same-backend comparisons only). It writes
`target/e2e-logs/state_digest_chain/<utc>.ndjson` through the STD-E2E helper: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block (determinism class, tier, seed,
identities), the number of addresses compared, the run digest per chunk as hex, and on failure the first
address and tick whose d or x differs with both values and the two run identifiers; a final `summary` line with
assertions passed and failed. The same step group (`digest-*`) is called by `scripts/e2e/sim_replay.sh`
(N29a-19).

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer: the pinned 4 kB corpus has a checked-in d per backend, all equal.
- Negative controls (registered): a digest that folds padding lanes (changes with capacity); a chain that omits
  x_{t−1} (the prefix test fails); a digest that folds x into d (re-convergence test fails); an address that
  includes the slot (slot test fails); a host reader that reads before `Completed` (fence test fails).
- Metamorphic: permuting world indices permutes results and changes nothing else; adding inactive worlds or
  unused capacity changes no active world's digest (MR-01).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release on `batch-cpu`; each backend
  against its own record (class D2 for the state, D0 for the digest of given bits).

## Considerations

- Cost: about 1% of the memory traffic of the step at the reference sizes (one integer read of the state per
  tick); measured inside GATE-CHRON-13 (N29a-17). If it exceeds its share, the fallback is to digest only at
  logical-chunk ends for worlds that hold no ring and are not traced, and to obtain per-tick digests by
  same-backend replay with capture (BD-203), which the divergence search already supports.
- KILL-D1 (decided at M2): if a device family cannot hold bit equality with `batch-cpu`, that family is class D2
  for itself and D3 against others; the digest is unaffected (it is D0 on given bits), only what equal digests
  across backends may be expected to show.
- The collision budget is about 2⁻¹²⁸ per comparison for independent states; it is stated with every equality
  as "digests equal".
- Adding a slab to the canonical list changes the digest version; the step plan records the version, so old
  records are compared only with the version they were made with.
