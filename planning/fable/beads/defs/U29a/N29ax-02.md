## Background

Telemetry has four levels. T0 (per-episode metrics, histograms and per-chunk minimum-maximum envelopes) and
T1 (events: contacts made and broken, limit and saturation episodes, faults, refusals) are recorded for
every world, always. T2 (traces at control rate: joint states, torques, contact wrenches) is recorded only
for an address-keyed sample of worlds and for failing worlds, because it cannot be afforded everywhere.

The manufacturing subsystem (Foundry) has a screening check that needs load data from *every* rollout: after
the first rollouts of a design, each link whose fill is a shell or a lattice is sized again "under the peak
port wrenches of T1 telemetry, taken time-coherently"; a link that is under-built under measured loads
labels the design `structure-unchecked` and blocks its promotion (GATE-FOUNDRY-15, first milestone M3). The
full load-extraction machinery reads T2 traces and arrives at M5 for qualified finalists; it cannot serve a
check that runs on every world of a screening campaign at M3.

T1 as drafted holds events, not samples, and T0 envelopes hold per-channel extrema. Neither can supply what
the check needs, and the obvious substitute is unsafe. The rule (architecture decision ADR-FOUNDRY-03):
**time-coherent cases only.** A simply supported beam of span L carries P at L/3 at one instant and P at
2L/3 at another; the mid-span moment is P·L/6 each time. Assembling both peaks into one case gives P·L/3, a
twofold over-design; if the second load has the opposite sign the assembled case gives zero while each real
case gives ±P·L/6, which is unsafe. So per-channel maxima from T0 envelopes may not be combined into a load
case. What is needed is one *real instant*: every port wrench of the link and its body twist taken from the
same tick, the tick at which a chosen scalar was largest.

This bead adds that capability to the record subsystem (Chronicle) as a general mechanism: a *peak capture*
keeps, per world, per entity and per episode, the sample at the running maximum of a registered scalar, on
the device, at fixed memory, and emits it as one T1 event when the episode ends. Tier: Solid.

Terms. An *entity* is a link (later: a joint or a contact site). A *registered scalar* is a named,
non-negative function of committed step state, evaluated per entity per tick. A *payload* is the fixed list
of state values copied when the scalar reaches a new maximum. A `stat` kernel is a telemetry kernel whose
outputs never feed physics state.

Rejected alternatives: T2 on every world (several times the 3% cost budget of always-on telemetry, and far
more data than one sample per link); host-side search of the ring buffer (the ring holds seconds, an episode
may last minutes); the T0 envelope maxima (not time-coherent, shown unsafe above); sampling at control rate
only (an impact peak lies inside a control tick).

## Scope

In: the registry of scalars; the capture declaration; the device fold and its state; emission at episode end
as a T1 event; the CPU reference path; drain, chunk publication and query; refusal when a consumer asks for
a capture that was not recorded; the first registered scalar and payload (link bending moment at the
proximal joint; port wrenches and body twist); cost measurement.

Out: the sizing check, the label `structure-unchecked` and the promotion refusal (N31b-40); load campaigns,
envelope cases in many directions and spectra from T2 (the M5 load-extraction beads); stills and ring
flushing on failure (the device telemetry bead).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `registries/peak-scalars.toml`; `crates/frob-kernels/src/stat/k_stat_peak.rs` with its
dialect twins; `crates/frob-chronicle/src/peaks.rs`; the two scripted fixtures of the acceptance criteria,
registered with N24b-03's fixture registry so that `sim.run --fixture <name>` runs them (each supplies the
design, the scene and the input table of its scripted loads): `assets/fixtures/cantilever-tip-force/fixture.toml`
and `assets/fixtures/beam-two-instant/fixture.toml` (DESIGN names).

1. **Registry row.** `id = "link-bending-proximal"`, `entity = "link"`, `unit = "N·m"`, `quantum = 0.001`
   (the scalar is compared as q = round-half-even(s/quantum) saturated to i32, so the comparison is exact on
   every backend), `definition` (the norm of the two bending components of the proximal joint's constraint
   wrench, in the link frame), `payload = ["port-wrenches", "body-twist"]`, payload width P = 6·ports + 6.
2. **Declaration.** The run manifest (or the task) lists captures: `capture.peaks = [{ scalar =
   "link-bending-proximal", entities = "links-with-shell-or-lattice-fill" | [ids] }]`. The declaration is in
   the manifest core, so two runs with and without it have different run identities and the same physics
   digests. On the CLI a run declares one with `frob sim run … --peak-capture <scalar>[=<entity ids>]`
   (repeatable; the default entity set is the scalar row's). The option is spelled `--peak-capture`, not
   `--capture`, because `sim.replay --capture` already means something else; it is on the `sim.run` row of
   VERBS-sim (a dependency), with this bead as its extender. A task may carry the same declaration.
3. **Per-world state** (fixed capacity from the shape class): per captured entity `best_q: i32` (initial
   −1), `best_step: u32`, and P payload words (`f32` on single-precision tiers, `f64` on the referee and
   `strict`). For a quadruped with 12 two-port links: 12 × (8 + 18 × 4) = 960 bytes per world; 3.9 MB for
   4,096 worlds.
4. **The fold** (kernel `K-stat-peak`, run after the step's commit on every committed physics step, not
   only on control ticks). For each captured entity: compute s from committed state, q = quantize(s);
   `take = q > best_q` (strict: the first occurrence of a maximum wins); `best_q = select(take, q, best_q)`,
   `best_step = select(take, step, best_step)`, and each payload word is `select(take, current, stored)`.
   Selects only, no data-dependent branch, no cross-world reduction, no atomics. Every payload word is read
   in the same kernel invocation from the same committed state: that is the time coherence.
5. **Emission.** At the episode's terminal step, and before any reset touches state, one event per captured
   entity is written to a dedicated section of the statistics ring (`peaks`, capacity = entities × captures;
   it cannot overflow and does not compete with contact events): `peak-sample { capture, entity, step, tick,
   scalar_q, payload[P] }`. The state is then re-initialized. An episode cut by a budget stop emits nothing
   partial; the chunk records a gap with reason `episode-incomplete`. This bead adds that reason to the
   registered, extensible gap-reason list of the chunk contract (BD-090; requested there) and to the equal copy
   kept by the auditor (N29a-03), with a test that the two lists match.
6. **Host side.** The drain appends the section at the fence with the other T1 data (idempotent per epoch).
   Events are published on the Kinematics stream as channel kind `event` (DESIGN; an event is never
   decimated). Each declared capture is one channel named `peak.<scalar id>` (for example
   `peak.link-bending-proximal`) of kind `event`, so `telemetry.query` reads it with its existing selectors and
   no new option: `frob telemetry query <run> --stream kinematics --channels peak.link-bending-proximal --json`
   returns one row per (world, episode, entity), paged on the committed frontier, and the kind table keeps every
   event under any downsampling.
7. **CPU reference path.** The same fold as a host loop on the referee and on `batch-cpu`; the device result
   equals it bit for bit on integer fields, and on payload words wherever the state itself is bit-equal
   (within the determinism class otherwise; when two backends' states differ inside tolerance the selected
   step may differ, and the comparison is then made on `scalar_q` within one quantum).
8. **Refusal.** A consumer that asks for a capture a run did not record, or whose events fall in a gap, is
   refused: FROB-REF-888 (a metric computed over missing samples; repair: re-run with the capture declared)
   from `telemetry.query`, and the load metric's own FROB-REF-169 (telemetry without the traces or samples a
   load metric needs) from the sizing check. Nothing is reconstructed from envelopes.
9. **Cost.** TARGET: at most 0.5% of step time for 12 entities on the reference quadruped workload, counted
   inside the always-on budget of GATE-CHRON-13 (T0 and T1 on every world at most 3% of step time). It is
   measured by that gate's protocol (N29a-17: paired, interleaved blocks of 500 environment steps, 30 blocks,
   one-sided 95% upper bound, the disclosure block), in a second set of 30 blocks balanced over the three
   configurations B, T01 and T01+peaks: bound((t_peaks − t_01)/t_B) ≤ 0.005 and bound(t_peaks/t_B − 1) ≤ 0.03. This bead adds the step group
   `peaks-*` and the configuration T01+peaks to `scripts/e2e/telemetry_cost.sh` (N29a-17, a dependency) and an added row to
   the gate's record; it writes no second cost script.

Determinism: the fold is a `stat` kernel; physics digests are identical with and without it. Evidence: an
event is a recorded sample, class `Tested` at the tier of the run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the scripted cantilever fixture (one link held fixed at its root, gravity off, so the root bending
      moment is the tip force times 0.3 m alone; tip force 600·sin(π·t/1 s) N at 0.3 m) every episode yields one
      event for the link with `scalar_q` = 180,000 quanta (180 N·m) at the step nearest t = 0.5 s, and a
      payload whose root force and moment are those of that step, bit for bit against the T2 trace of a
      traced world.
- [ ] Time coherence: on the two-instant beam fixture (P at L/3, later −P at 2L/3) the captured sample
      passes the free-body check (port wrenches and inertial load sum to zero within tolerance), and a build
      that keeps per-channel maxima fails it.
- [ ] Ties: a scalar sequence 3, 7, 7, 5 captures step 1, not step 2.
- [ ] Physics digests of a run are identical with the capture on and off, on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal.
- [ ] `batch-cpu` and each device give identical events for the same job where the determinism class
      promises equal bits.
- [ ] One event per captured entity per completed episode on every world: counts equal worlds × episodes ×
      entities; an episode cut by a budget stop has a gap and no event.
- [ ] A query for an undeclared capture is refused with FROB-REF-888; nothing is synthesized from T0.
- [ ] A budget-stopped episode's gap carries the reason `episode-incomplete`, which the auditor (N29a-03)
      accepts as a registered reason.
- [ ] The cost row is recorded with GATE-CHRON-13 and is inside the always-on budget.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob --json` (`sim.run` with the capture declared, `telemetry.query`) and MCP;
      `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-chronicle` lists the mechanism and its limits.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels --test k_stat_peak` and `cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test peaks`:

- `first_maximum_wins`: scalars 3, 7, 7, 5 → `best_step` 1; payload equals the state of step 1.
- `quantization_exact`: s = 179.9996 and 180.0004 N·m both give q = 180,000; s = 180.0006 gives 180,001.
- `payload_from_one_step`: a fixture where port A peaks at step 10 and port B at step 20 while the scalar
  peaks at step 14: the payload holds A and B as they were at step 14.
- `assembled_peaks_fail_free_body` (the planted defect): per-channel maxima give a non-zero free-body
  residual on the beam fixture; the real capture gives zero within 10⁻⁹ relative.
- `reset_between_episodes`: the second episode's event does not depend on the first's maximum.
- `emits_before_reset`: the terminal step's own state is eligible.
- `budget_stop_emits_gap`.
- `stat_kernel_does_not_touch_state`: footprint check: the kernel's write set is the peak slab only.
- `cpu_reference_equals_device_fold` on 1,000 seeded worlds.
- `undeclared_capture_refused_888`.
- `memory_per_world`: 960 bytes for the 12-link fixture.
- `gap_reason_registered`: `episode-incomplete` is in the contract's gap-reason list and in the auditor's copy,
  and the two lists are equal.
- `channel_name_per_capture`: a run declaring the capture has the event channel `peak.link-bending-proximal`;
  a run without it does not, and the query is refused with 888.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/telemetry_peak_capture.sh --seed <s>`: (1) `frob sim run --fixture cantilever-tip-force --tier
batch-cpu --peak-capture link-bending-proximal --worlds 64 --seconds 1 --seed <s> --key pk-1 --json`; (2) `frob
telemetry query <run> --stream kinematics --channels peak.link-bending-proximal --json` (N24a-36's spelling) and
assert 64 events with 180 N·m at the expected step; (3) the same on each available device (`--tier <device>
--key pk-<device>`) and compare events with step 2; (4) run with the capture off (`--key pk-off`), compare
physics digests with step 1, and assert the refusal on query (exit 3, FROB-REF-888); (5) `--fixture
beam-two-instant` with the capture and the free-body assertion (`--key pk-beam`); (6) a run stopped by its
budget mid-episode: a budget below the estimate's upper end is refused at admission (FROB-REF-701), so the run
is N32a-15's slow fixture, whose actual cost exceeds its estimate, with the capture declared on one of its links
(`--peak-capture link-bending-proximal=<link id>`) and `--budget` set between the estimate's upper end and the
actual cost (`--key pk-budget`): it is admitted, stopped with FROB-REF-702, and leaves a gap with reason
`episode-incomplete` and no event for that episode; (7) the cost row through `scripts/e2e/telemetry_cost.sh` with its `peaks-*`
group (4,096 worlds is too small for the gate's workload, which runs at 8,192). NDJSON per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/telemetry_peak_capture/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block, event counts against
worlds × episodes × entities, `scalar_q` and step against expected, the free-body residual against its
tolerance, digests compared, the cost share against 0.5% and 3%, and on failure the first world address and
entity that differ. The `summary` line lists `gates: ["GATE-FOUNDRY-15", "GATE-CHRON-13"]` as touched;
neither gate is recorded by this script.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The tube case the consumer checks with these samples: 600 N across the tip of a 0.3 m cantilever gives a
  root moment of 180 N·m.
- The beam counterexample: mid-span moment P·L/6 at each real instant; P·L/3 or zero when assembled.
- Negative controls: per-channel maxima (above); a fold evaluated only on control ticks (misses the peak of
  a fixture whose maximum lies between control ticks); `take = q ≥ best_q` (captures the last maximum; the
  tie test fails); emission after reset (the terminal state is lost).
- Metamorphic relation: permuting worlds within a batch, or changing the batch size, leaves each world's
  events unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; across backends within
  their class.

## Considerations

- Peak contact forces depend on contact stiffness; a captured peak is a statement about the simulated model.
  The model-form treatment of impact loads belongs to the finalist load extraction; the screening check that
  reads these samples inherits the tier and conditions of the rollouts.
- One scalar gives one sample per link per episode. A link that is governed by axial force and not by
  bending needs a second registered scalar; adding a registry row costs P + 2 words per entity per world.
- The capture is evaluated at physics rate. If its measured cost breaks the always-on budget, the fallback
  is evaluation at control rate with the event flagged `peak-at-control-rate`, and the consumer treats the
  flag as "no measured loads".
- Private scenes: events inherit the privacy class of the scene like all telemetry.
