## Background

The created-energy audit of the record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) has three monitors that run in every
world. The first watches the whole-body energy balance. The other two look at one contact at a time and catch
exploits that the whole-body balance can miss or cannot localize:

| Monitor | Quantity | Flag | Threshold (TARGET) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact passivity | The net work a contact does on the bodies over one contact episode, from touch to release | `contact-active` | Net positive work above the tier's residual bound: the contact returned more than it absorbed |
| Cone excess | Friction utilization ‖f_t‖ / (μ·f_n) per contact | `cone-excess` | Utilization above 1.05 for more than 1% of stance time; a pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal |

Why each exists. A real contact is passive: over an episode from touch to release it can store energy and give
back at most what it stored, and friction only dissipates. A contact that does net positive work is a trampoline
supplied by the solver, and naming the contact tells the reader where. A four-sided friction pyramid with the same
coefficient μ admits the tangential force (μ·f_n, μ·f_n), of norm √2·μ·f_n: 41% more traction along the diagonals
than physics allows (a block of weight 100 N with μ = 0.5 holds until 70.7 N under the pyramid and slides at 50 N
under the cone).

The monitors as they run on batched tiers (in-kernel integer counters, flags on the episode row, the verb
`fidelity.energy`, the planted and honest fixtures, the gate's energy half) are bead N29a-22 of unit U29a. That
bead states the rules. This bead is the sub-task underneath it: three things that the rules need before they can
be implemented once and tested honestly.

1. *What "the work a contact does" means when one of the two bodies is a moving surface.* A conveyor legitimately
   carries a robot and does positive work on it. A passivity monitor written with absolute velocities flags every
   belt, lift and moving walkway as a trampoline.
2. *A host oracle for each monitor*, a pure function of the contact telemetry stream, which is the CPU reference
   path the in-kernel counters must agree with.
3. *The differential check* between kernel and oracle on every traced world, and the hand-checkable cases.

Other readers of these flags: the task audit of the arena subsystem (intent invariants that a task must enforce,
checked with foil bodies before any design runs on it) lists "no net positive work by a contact" with the signal
`contact-active`; the rank-robustness analysis A-19 types a finalist `model-conditional` when its advantage
coincides with created energy or cone excess; the exploitation detectors of the learning subsystem read both.

## Scope

In: the work of a contact in relative velocity; contact episodes; the passivity inequality as the oracle
evaluates it; friction utilization, its exclusion floor and the two counts; the per-contact share reported
beside the pooled rule; host oracles; the kernel-against-oracle differential; unit tests and small fixtures.

Out: in-kernel accumulation, flag storage, `fidelity.energy`, the gate's energy half and its script (bead
N29a-22; seed BD-208 for the kernel). The kernel there must accumulate per contact slot both N_f and G_f (two
double-float accumulators, besides the integer counters T_s, T_x and the integer maximum of u) in relative
velocity as item 1 defines them, because the passivity bound of item 3 needs G_f; the conveyor fixture of this
bead's differential is the check, and a kernel that departs is a defect of N29a-22, not a change made here; the whole-body monitor (N29b-02); the pyramid mutation and planted
rankings (bead N16b-15); the model-form rank check and A-19 (BD-266, BD-346); carrying flags into comparisons
(N29b-04).

## Technical approach

**1. Work of a contact on the bodies, per interval.** For each row i of the contact (normal, two tangential, and
torsional or rolling rows where the law has them), with row impulse λᵢ and *relative* row velocity uᵢ, the
velocity of body A minus body B along the row with the prescribed velocity of a moving surface included:

```
w_k = Σ_rows λᵢ · ½·(uᵢ⁻ + uᵢ⁺)          uᵢ⁻, uᵢ⁺: row velocity at the start and end of the interval
N_f = Σ_{k ∈ episode} w_k                net work of contact f over its episode; positive when the contact added
                                         mechanical energy to the bodies in the relative frame
G_f = Σ_{k ∈ episode} Σ_rows abs(λᵢ · ½·(uᵢ⁻ + uᵢ⁺))     absolute work exchanged through the contact
```

w_k is the whole-interval discrete work of the row, the same quantity the ledger's closure uses, so the sum of
N_f over contacts equals the ledger's contact work. **The moving-surface rule:** work is taken in relative
velocity. The boundary work a prescribed surface does on the robot (λᵢ times the surface's velocity along the
row) is credited to the ledger's external-work line; it is not part of N_f. Consequence: a block carried without
slip by a belt at 1 m/s has N_f = 0 and positive external work; a policy that surfs a mis-modelled belt shows up
in the whole-body balance, not as a false `contact-active`.

**2. Contact episode.** For a feature pair f (stable across witness migration): opens at the first interval with
positive normal impulse after one without (touch); closes at the first interval without (release). An episode
still open when the world's episode ends is closed at the terminal tick. Energy stored in compliance during the
episode and returned before release nets to zero by construction; energy still stored at a forced close only
makes N_f more negative.

**3. Passivity, as the oracle evaluates it.**

```
flag contact-active (contact f)  iff  N_f > ε_tier · G_f + E_floor
ε_tier = 10⁻⁹ on the referee and f64 transactional profiles; 10⁻³ on single-precision tiers
E_floor = the tier's absolute residual bound (10⁻⁹ J in f64, 10⁻⁶ J in f32), the same floor as the whole-body monitor
```

The world's flag is the or over its contacts. The record names the worst contact: feature pair, touch and
release ticks, N_f, G_f.

**4. Friction utilization and the 1% rule.** Per contact and control tick, with the solved tangential force f_t,
normal force f_n and the coefficient μ the solve used for that contact in that tick (from the interface card,
including any direction or film dependence):

```
u = ‖f_t‖ / (μ · f_n)        defined when f_n is above the contact law's noise floor
                             (DESIGN default where a law declares none: 10⁻³ of the robot's weight)
T_s = number of (contact, tick) pairs with load          T_x = number of those pairs with u > 1.05
flag cone-excess  iff  100 · T_x > T_s                   (integers; more than 1% of stance time)
```

The rule is pooled over contacts, as bead N29a-22 states it. Recorded beside it, because the plan's phrase
"more than 1% of stance time" also admits a per-contact reading: `worst_contact_share = max over contacts of
T_x,c / T_s,c`, with that contact's feature pair, and `u_max` (quantized to 10⁻⁴, an integer maximum). Smallest
case in which the two readings differ: four feet with equal stance time, one foot in excess for 3% of its own
stance: pooled share 0.75% (not flagged), worst contact 3%. The record makes the case visible; changing the rule
is a decision at the re-baseline, not something this bead does. Pairs below the noise floor are excluded from
both counts and counted in `excluded_light_pairs`.

**5. Host oracles (the CPU reference path).** `audit::oracle::passivity(contact_stream) → Vec<EpisodeRecord>` and
`audit::oracle::cone(contact_stream) → ConeRecord` are pure functions of the level-T2 contact stream (feature
pair, normal, gap, impulse, stick or slip, row velocities). They share no code with the kernel beyond the
definitions above.

**6. Differential check.** For every traced world of a run, after the run: the kernel's `T_s`, `T_x` and
`u_max` equal the oracle's exactly; the kernel's per-contact N_f and G_f equal the oracle's within the
double-float tolerance (4 ulp of the accumulated value in the tier's precision); the flags are equal. A mismatch
is recorded with the world address, the contact and the first tick at which a running value differs, and fails
the run's audit self-check (`audit_state = inconsistent`, which consumers treat like `unavailable`).

**7. Flags do not alter results.** A flagged episode keeps its result and gains the flag.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/energy/audit/oracle/{passivity.rs, cone.rs}` and
`…/audit/differential.rs`; tests `crates/frob-chronicle/tests/{contact_passivity_oracle.rs,
cone_excess_oracle.rs}`. Determinism: oracles are deterministic functions of committed telemetry; counts are
exact integers. Evidence: fixture results are Known answers of class Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A block riding a conveyor at constant speed has N_f = 0 and raises no `contact-active`; the mutant that uses
      absolute velocity computes positive work and flags it (caught).
- [ ] The stiff penalty bounce raises `contact-active` on its ground contact in the oracle; the same drop under the
      convex law at the default step does not (KA-709, second flag).
- [ ] The diagonal push on the pyramid cone gives u = 1.4142 ± 0.0005 and the flag; the same push on the circular
      cone gives u ≤ 1.05 and no flag (KA-710 and its negative control: a circular-cone run flagged).
- [ ] A compliant contact that returns its stored energy during release (restitution below 1) raises nothing; the
      per-step-passivity mutant flags it and is caught.
- [ ] Kernel counters and flags equal the oracles on every traced world of the standard battery on `batch-cpu` and
      on each admitted device back end; a planted off-by-one in the kernel's stance count is caught.
- [ ] The four-feet case reports pooled share 0.75% and worst-contact share 3%.
- [ ] Oracle outputs are identical under permutation of worlds and across platforms.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `passive_spring_damper_episode`: stored and returned 0.05 J, dissipated 0.01 J → N_f = −0.01 J; not flagged.
- `active_contact_flagged`: a 1 kg body approaching at 0.5 m/s and leaving at 1.5 m/s (three times its approach
  speed, the one-step gain of the stiff-penalty fixture): N_f = ½·(1.5² − 0.5²) = +1.0 J; flagged; the record names
  the pair and both ticks.
- `open_episode_at_end_not_flagged` (a standing contact with 0.05 J stored).
- `conveyor_relative_velocity`: belt at 1 m/s, no slip → N_f = 0, external work positive; absolute-velocity mutant
  positive N_f.
- `utilization_pyramid_diagonal`: f_n = 100 N, μ = 0.5, f_t = (50, 50) N → u = 70.71/50 = 1.4142.
- `utilization_boundary`: ‖f_t‖ = 50 N → 1.00; 52 N → 1.04, not counted; 53 N → 1.06, counted.
- `one_percent_rule_integer`: T_s = 1,000: T_x = 10 → not flagged (1,000 is not greater than 1,000); T_x = 11 →
  flagged.
- `pooled_against_per_contact`: four contacts with 1,000 loaded ticks each, one with 30 in excess → T_x = 30,
  T_s = 4,000, share 0.75%, worst-contact share 3%.
- `light_pairs_excluded_and_counted`.
- `differential_detects_off_by_one`.
- Property: oracle equals a brute-force evaluation on 10³ seeded contact streams.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step group `oracle-*` added to `scripts/e2e/energy_audit.sh` (the script of bead N29a-22 for the gate's energy
half): after each fixture run on `batch-cpu` with every world traced, `frob telemetry query <run> --stream
contact --json` feeds the oracles (through the conformance binary `audit-oracle`), and the result is compared
with `frob fidelity energy <run> --json`. Asserts equality of flags and counts, N_f within tolerance, the
conveyor case and the four-feet case. NDJSON lines per the shared logging helper: command, exit code, wall time,
the envelope's evidence block with its conditions, N_f and G_f in joules against the bound, T_x and T_s against
the 1% threshold, `u_max`, `worst_contact_share`, mismatches (expected 0); on failure the first world, contact
and tick that differ and the chunk range to replay.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-709 (flags, exact) and KA-710 (utilization √2, to 5·10⁻⁴; control: a circular-cone run must not be flagged).
- The diagonal-push forces 50 N and 70.7 N; the pooled and per-contact shares 0.75% and 3%.
- Negative controls: absolute-velocity work; per-step passivity; a threshold of 1.0 (flags the honest regularized
  law); the 1% test in floating point (differs across platforms at the boundary); a kernel count that includes
  light pairs.
- Metamorphic: rotating the push by 45° on the pyramid changes u from √2 to 1; on the cone it changes nothing;
  adding a constant velocity to both bodies of a contact changes no N_f.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; `batch-cpu` against each
  device back end (counts exact).

## Considerations

- This bead overlaps bead N29a-22 by design: that bead states the two rules and owns the kernel, flags, verb and
  gate; this one holds the work definition, the oracles and the differential. If the two are merged, items 1 to 6
  go into that bead's technical approach.
- The pooled 1% rule dilutes with the number of contacts, so a morphology with more feet is less likely to be
  flagged for one slipping foot; the per-contact share is recorded so that the re-baseline at the second
  milestone can decide with data. Both thresholds are TARGETs fixed before measurement.
- An anisotropic or film-dependent μ must be the value the solve used; computing u from a nominal μ would flag
  honest sliding on a greasy patch.
- The oracles need level-T2 contact data, which exists for traced and failing worlds only; on untraced worlds the
  kernel is the only source, which is why the differential on traced worlds matters.
