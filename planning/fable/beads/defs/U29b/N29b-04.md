## Background

The created-energy audit of the record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) produces three flags per episode:
`energy-created` (the whole-body balance gained energy that nothing supplied), `contact-active` (one contact did
net positive work from touch to release) and `cone-excess` (friction utilization above 1.05 for more than 1% of
stance time). The monitors are bead N29a-22 of unit U29a, with the reference evaluators N29b-02 and N29b-03, and their in-kernel accumulation on batched tiers
is seed BD-208. A flag is only useful if it travels with the result it qualifies. The plan's rule has three
parts, and this bead is the plumbing for all three:

1. *A flagged episode keeps its result and gains the flag.* Nothing is deleted or rescored; the flag is a fact
   about the simulator, reported against the result that benefited (decision ADR-CHRON-08).
2. *A design or policy whose advantage in a comparison coincides with flagged episodes is typed
   `model-conditional`* by the rank-robustness analysis (A-19). To decide "coincides", that analysis needs, for
   each world address of a paired comparison, the flags of both arms beside both outcomes.
3. *The exploitation detectors read the same flags.* There is one source of truth for a flag, the episode record,
   and no consumer recomputes it.

Why a dedicated bead: the failure this guards against is a correct monitor whose output is dropped between the
ledger and the verdict. The plan's negative control is exactly that case: with the audit switched off, a policy
that bounces on a stiff penalty contact wins its comparison. With the audit on and the flags joined, the same
comparison carries the condition and cannot be reported as an established design comparison.

Rejected alternatives: discarding flagged episodes (it removes failures from a denominator and hides the
exploit's benefit); rescoring flagged episodes as failures (the audit is evidence about the simulator, not a
verdict about the robot); letting each consumer recompute flags from traces (traces exist only for sampled worlds).

## Scope

In: the flag bitset and detail rows on the episode record; level-T0 and level-T1 placement; run-level counts with
denominators; propagation into the claim product's conditions; the paired flag join at a pinned frontier; the
conformance-only "audit off" build; tests.

Out: computing flags (bead N29a-22; N29b-02, N29b-03; BD-208); the coincidence rule and the typing `model-conditional` (seed
BD-346 and BD-266); the detectors (seed BD-267); printing (the energy view, bead N24a-37 of unit U24a; `frob
fidelity energy`, BD-208); the end-to-end gate record (N29b-06).

## Technical approach

**1. Episode record.** The monitors write their flags as bits on the `episode` row with first tick and magnitudes
(bead N29a-22 of unit U29a); this bead fixes the bit positions every reader uses and adds the state field. The
ledger table `episode` (one row per run, world, episode) holds, at schema version 1:
`audit_flags: u32` with bit 0 `energy-created`, bit 1 `contact-active`, bit 2 `cone-excess` (bits 3 to 31
reserved, must be zero); and `audit_state: complete | unavailable`. `unavailable` is written when a monitor's
inputs were dropped (a gap in the energetics or contact stream): the flags are then unknown, not clear, and a
consumer that needs them is refused (`FROB-REF-888`, a metric computed over missing samples; repair: re-run with
the channel retained, or accept `unavailable`).

**2. Detail rows.** One level-T1 event per raised flag, written at its first crossing: `{ flag, first_tick,
worst: {…} }` with the monitor's own record (the worst window of N29b-02; the worst contact, its feature pair and
touch and release ticks, or the utilization counts, of N29b-03). Events are in every world's record, not only
traced worlds, because level T1 is recorded for every world.

**3. Run-level counts.** For a run at a pinned frontier: `flag_counts { energy_created, contact_active,
cone_excess, any, unavailable, of }` where `of` is the number of committed episodes. Counts are integer sums and
`any` is derived from the per-episode bitsets, so the figures are exact under any world order (INV-CHRON-06).
Every count is printed as "k of n" (INV-CHRON-11: every summary states its denominator).

**4. Into the claim product.** When a result is built from episodes, the union of their raised flags is added to
the claim product's `conditions` set (conditions combine by union; class, tier and determinism take the weakest).
A result whose conditions contain `energy-created` cannot be labelled `established`, and a ranking that contains
one is `model-conditional`; the label algebra enforces this and citing such a result as established is refused
(`FROB-REF-820`). `contact-active` and `cone-excess` are carried as conditions for the consumers named below; they
do not by themselves block a label (DESIGN reading: the plan gives the label consequence to `energy-created`
alone, and gives the other two to A-19 and the detectors).

**5. The flag join.** `flag_join(study, frontier) → rows` returns, for each world address of a paired study,

```
{ address, arm_a: { outcome, metric, audit_flags, audit_state },
           arm_b: { outcome, metric, audit_flags, audit_state },
  d }                                   // the paired difference the study's statistic uses
```

in address order, at the study's pinned frontier (the value of the run's commit counter the verdict cites). It is
a pure function of committed rows: it does not depend on completion order, preemption, shard count or memo hits
(the same purity the study statistic itself has, INV-ATEL-02). Consumers: A-19 recomputes the comparison on the
addresses where neither arm is flagged and types the advantage as coinciding when it does not survive; the
detectors read per-arm flag rates. The join is exposed inside the envelope of the comparison verbs as
`audit_join_ref` (an evidence identifier of kind `analysis result`), not as a new verb.

**6. Unpaired use.** For a single-arm result (an evaluation, a scorecard), `flag_rates { flag: k of n }` per
stratum is attached and each flagged address is listed up to the token budget, with a handle for the rest.

**7. The conformance build.** A feature `conformance` exposes one planted defect, "audit switched off": monitors
return clear flags unconditionally. It changes the step-plan identity, prints its label in every envelope, and is
refused by every verb outside the conformance scope. It exists so that the gate can show the audit is live.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/energy/audit/{record.rs, join.rs}`; schema `frob.audit-join/1`.
Determinism: exact integers and committed rows only. Evidence: the join is an analysis result of class Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A flagged episode's row holds its original outcome and metrics and the flag bits; the count of episodes in
      the run is unchanged by flagging.
- [ ] The bouncing-policy comparison (a hop-height task; arm A the policy that bounces on the stiff penalty
      contact, arm B an honest policy under the convex law): with the audit live, the comparison's envelope carries
      the condition `energy-created`, its label is not `established`, and the join shows arm A flagged on the
      addresses where it wins.
- [ ] With the conformance build "audit switched off", the same comparison reports arm A as the clean winner, and
      the test that asserts the condition fails: the negative control is caught.
- [ ] Run-level counts sum correctly (`any` ≤ sum of the three; `any + clean + unavailable = of`) on 100 random
      runs, and are identical under permutation and shard split.
- [ ] A consumer asking for the join over a run with a dropped energetics interval receives `FROB-REF-888`.
- [ ] The join is byte-identical when the study's episodes complete in a different order and after a daemon
      restart.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `flag_bits_roundtrip`: bitset ↔ names; reserved bits non-zero is a load error.
- `flagging_keeps_result`: an episode with success and metric 0.82 m before and after flags are written.
- `counts_identity`: 10 episodes with flags {3 energy-created of which 1 also contact-active, 2 cone-excess, 1
  unavailable} → energy_created 3, contact_active 1, cone_excess 2, any 5, unavailable 1, clean 4, of 10.
- `conditions_union`: results with conditions {energy-created} and {cone-excess} combine to both.
- `established_blocked_by_energy_created`: labelling such a result `established` is refused with FROB-REF-820.
- `join_pairs_by_address`: arms completed in different orders → rows in address order, each with both arms.
- `join_refuses_missing_audit`: one arm's `audit_state = unavailable` on an address → FROB-REF-888 naming it.
- `coincidence_input_example`: 64 addresses, arm A wins 40 to 24, 20 of A's wins on flagged addresses → the join's
  unflagged subset is 44 addresses with 20 wins to 24 (the numbers the coincidence rule of A-19 consumes).
- Property: join invariant under any completion order (10³ seeded schedules).

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `audit-join-*` of `scripts/e2e/gate_chron_08.sh` (owned by N29b-06): submit the bouncing-policy comparison
as a fixed-n paired study on `batch-cpu`; read its envelope; request the join by its evidence identifier with
`frob evidence trace`. Asserts the condition, the label, the per-arm flag counts with denominators, and (in the
conformance build) the opposite outcome. NDJSON lines: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block including
`conditions`, the study identifier and frontier, flag counts as "k of n", the join's digest; on failure the first
address whose joined flags differ from the episode rows.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-709's negative control: the audit switched off, in which the bouncing policy wins the comparison. Expected
  with the audit on: condition present, not established.
- The count identity above (exact integers).
- Negative controls: a build that drops flagged episodes before scoring (the episode count changes and the
  denominator check fails); a build that rescored flagged episodes as failures (`flagging_keeps_result` fails); a
  join keyed by arrival order (the pairing test fails).
- Metamorphic: swapping the arms negates d and swaps the flag columns; adding an unflagged honest address changes
  no existing row.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; after restart; debug and release.

## Considerations

- A flag is evidence about the model, so it must survive every summarization: token-budgeted summaries print
  flag counts before any metric of a flagged run.
- Privacy: flag detail rows name contact feature pairs and ticks, which are scene-derived; they inherit the
  scene's privacy class (N29b-14).
- Failure story 5 of the risk chapter's premortem (policies exploited contact compliance and promotions kept
  failing): the share of finalists typed `model-conditional` is one of its earliest signals, and the counts of this
  bead are its first input. RISK-05 (the convex contact model's creep misleads wheel and stair results) lists
  `model-conditional` finalists as its early warning for the same reason.
- Open design point recorded in item 4: whether `contact-active` alone should block `established`. Default (fail
  closed toward the plan's text): only `energy-created` blocks; the other two are conditions that A-19 and the
  detectors act on.
