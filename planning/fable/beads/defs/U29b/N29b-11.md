## Background

The job state machine, the idempotency binding, the chunk publisher, pagination, the lease manager, the memo
store and the dispatcher are concurrent code whose failures are rare interleavings. They are tested by
deterministic simulation, not by waiting. The harness is the lab runtime of the sibling async library: virtual
time, seeded scheduling, fault injection, lineage-driven fault selection, recorded replay and minimization of
failing schedules (FACT: `asupersync/src/lab/` contains `chaos.rs`, `injection.rs`, `ldfi.rs`, `replay.rs`,
`replay_minimization.rs`, `schedule_minimizer.rs`, `dual_run.rs` and `explorer.rs`). The device is replaced by a
fake whose episode outcomes are a pure function of the world address, so a whole campaign simulates in
milliseconds.

The first wave is seed bead BD-096 (first milestone): the harness, the fake device, the fault menu, the oracles,
lineage-driven selection, minimization, the planted lost-chunk control, dual runs against the CPU executor, and
full await-point coverage of the publish and submit paths. It registers every oracle the plan lists, and reports
some as `not exercised` because their subjects do not exist yet at the first milestone. This bead is the second
wave: it brings the subjects that land at the second milestone under the harness, and it sets up the lane that
runs 10⁵ seeded schedules per release, which is how proof obligation PO-CHRON-01 is discharged ("the publishing
transaction preserves INV-CHRON-01 to -03 under every fault of the simulation fault menu: deterministic
simulation over 10⁵ schedules; every failing schedule minimized and kept").

The plan's list, restated so this bead stands alone:

- **Fault menu.** A crash at every await point of the publish and submit paths; a torn ledger write; duplicate
  and reordered delivery; delay; lease expiry and takeover by a second writer; device loss; a full disk; a clock
  jump; cancellation at every boundary; a client that disconnects with a job running and reconnects by
  submission key.
- **Oracles.** INV-CHRON-01 (a committed chunk key maps to one content hash for the life of the run); -02 (for
  every run, world, episode and stream the committed chunks are exactly the indices 0 to the frontier); -03 (one
  idempotency key yields one job, one budget charge and one receipt, under any crash or retry); -04 (a paginated
  query returns every row visible at its pin exactly once); -12 (collection never removes a pinned object, and
  every removal leaves a tombstone); INV-ATEL-02 (a study's statistic is a pure function of the per-address
  outcomes taken in address order); INV-ATEL-06 (a decision core's action is a pure function of its table
  version, the ledger state at a pinned frontier and addresses; it reads no clock); and the one-job-one-charge
  rule of the surface (one job and one budget charge per principal, verb and idempotency key, under any
  interleaving and any crash point).
- **Targets.** Every await point on the publish and submit paths is hit by a crash fault at least once, and 10⁵
  seeded schedules run per release (TARGET).

## Scope

## Technical approach

**1. Scenario family P, pagination (oracle INV-CHRON-04).** The first-milestone gate bead of the chunk protocol
(N29a-06 of unit U29a) already runs this oracle against the CPU store; the subject here is the second-milestone
surface cursor (authenticated, with expiry and collection of pinned frontiers), under the same oracle. Readers
open paginated queries that pin, on the first
page, the run, the commit counter at that moment, the filters and the total order (tick, world, episode, stream,
sample identifier). Writers commit chunks between pages. Faults: crash between pages; a cursor replayed after
restart; a cursor presented with a changed filter or order. Oracle: the concatenation of pages equals exactly the
set of rows visible at the pin, each once; a cursor with a different pin, filter or order is refused
(FROB-REF-896); a cursor whose frontier was collected is refused as expired. Size: 10⁴ rows, pages of 100,
appends of 1 to 50 rows between any two pages (the plan's known answer KA-712).

**2. Scenario family R, retention (oracle INV-CHRON-12).** A collector runs mark-and-sweep cycles while verdict
writers pin closures, jobs open and close, lineage heads move and readers hold cursors. Faults: crash between
mark and sweep; crash between deleting an object's bytes and writing its tombstone; a verdict committing during
the sweep; a full disk during tombstone write. Oracle: no object that is pinned at any commit point is ever
absent; every absent object has a tombstone with content hash, class, time and reason; a deletion request for a
pinned object is refused (FROB-REF-813). This is the simulation form of the gate's "10⁴ random collection cycles
remove no pinned object".

**3. Scenario family M, memo store (oracles INV-ATEL-02, INV-ATEL-06).** Concurrent requests for the same memo
key; a crash after a result is computed and before the memo row commits; a shadow recompute racing a hit;
eviction under the store budget (memo artifacts evict and never block). Oracle: a study statistic computed with
memo hits equals the one computed without them, bit for bit; a decision core's action is identical when replayed
at the same pinned frontier with a different schedule and clock.

**4. Scenario family L, shards and leases (oracles INV-CHRON-01 to -03).** Two and four shards publish for one
run; faults: device loss on one shard, shard split after a retry (ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) offered for the
same ticks), lease expiry with takeover while the old writer is still alive and publishing, a reset that reuses
tick zero. Oracle as in the first wave, plus: every publish by a writer whose lease generation is stale is
refused (FROB-REF-875) and writes nothing. The family runs under both lease designs of OPEN-CHRON-5 (one
generation per run; one per shard with a versioned world-to-shard map) so that the decision bead N29b-19 can
compare stall time without a correctness difference.

**5. Coverage.** The lab runtime reports await points; the coverage set is re-enumerated on the second-milestone
code for the publish path, the submit path and the three new paths (page fetch, collect, memo commit). Gate
clause: every await point of publish and submit is hit by a crash fault at least once (required); the three new
paths are reported with the same measure and a target of 1.

**6. Dual runs.** 100 small jobs (seeds 0 to 99) executed against the fake device and against `batch-cpu`; the
two ledger states must be equal (same chunk keys, content hashes, chain digests and frontier). This keeps the
fake from drifting into testing a protocol the real workers do not follow. The device comparison is BD-210's.

**7. Lanes and budget.**

| Lane | Schedules | When | Envelope |
|---|---|---|---|
| per commit | 10³ across the families, plus every stored regression | every change to the listed paths | minutes on 8 cores |
| nightly | 10⁴, lineage-driven first | daily | under one hour |
| release | 10⁵ seeded schedules (TARGET) | each release candidate | within 8 cores, 16 GiB and one hour, or split into runs that each are |

Seeds are derived from (release tag, lane, index), so a release's 10⁵ schedules are reproducible and disjoint
from the nightly ones.

**8. Regressions.** Every failing schedule is minimized to a seed, a fault list and a schedule of minimal length
and stored under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/sim/<id>/`; it must replay to the same violated oracle in
under one second. A stored regression is never deleted; one made obsolete by a design change is marked with the
decision record that retired it.

**9. Discharge record.** `docs/proofs/chronicle/po-chron-01.md` states the obligation, the fault menu, the number
of schedules of the last release lane, the coverage figure, the count of violations (expected 0) and the list of
regression fixtures with the defect each one found. It is regenerated by the release lane.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Families P, R, M and L each run under the harness with their oracles active (no oracle reports
      `not exercised` for a subject that exists at the second milestone).
- [ ] A planted defect per family is found within 10⁴ explored schedules from a fixed seed, minimized, and replays
      in under one second: P, a query ordered by timestamp alone (repeats or skips equal-time samples); R, a
      sweep that reads pins before a concurrent verdict commits and deletes afterwards; M, a memo key that omits
      a field; L, a lease check outside the publishing transaction.
- [ ] Await-point coverage of the publish and submit paths is 1 on the second-milestone code; the three new
      paths' coverage is reported.
- [ ] Dual-run agreement on 100 of 100 small jobs against `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] The release lane completes 10⁵ schedules with zero violations, inside the machine envelope, and writes the
      discharge record of PO-CHRON-01.
- [ ] The simulation rows of GATE-CHRON-11 for the second-milestone subjects are recorded with measured coverage
      and counts (the device rows are recorded by BD-210).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `pagination_exactly_once`: 10⁴ rows, pages of 100, seeded appends → pages concatenate to the pinned set.
- `cursor_mismatch_refused_896`; `expired_cursor_refused`.
- `collect_never_removes_pinned`: 10⁴ cycles on a random pin and unpin workload (seeded).
- `tombstone_for_every_removal`: after any crash point, absent objects ⊆ tombstoned objects.
- `memo_hit_equals_recompute`; `memo_eviction_never_blocks` (a full store returns a recomputed answer, not a
  wait).
- `stale_lease_writes_nothing`: a late writer's publish is refused with 875 and the frontier is unchanged.
- `shard_split_duplicate_or_conflict`: ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) on a partition of length 50 → the second
  writer's chunk at index 1 is a duplicate (original receipt) when its bytes match and a conflict when they differ.
- `tick_zero_reuse_no_collision`: episode 1 chunk 0 and episode 0 chunk 0 are different keys.
- `regressions_replay_under_one_second` (runs every stored fixture).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chronicle_simtest.sh` runs the per-commit lane through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
sim_wave2` and one real-binary step: `frobd` under the simulation feature executes a 16-world job with a fault
plan (crash after commit, before receipt) and the client recovers by `frob job find --key`. NDJSON lines per the
shared helper: command, exit code, wall time, family, schedules run, violations, await points hit of total,
dual-run agreements of 100, regression fixtures replayed and their slowest replay time against the 1 s threshold;
on failure the seed, the fault list and the path of the minimized fixture. A `summary` line names GATE-CHRON-11.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-711 (two identical publishes yield one row and one receipt; a crash after commit returns the original
  receipt; controls: a late lease that writes, overlapping ranges that commit, a reset at tick zero that
  collides) and KA-712 (10⁴ rows read in pages during concurrent appends, each row visible at the pin exactly
  once; control: a query ordered by timestamp alone) are exercised by families L and P.
- CX-323 (ordering paginated telemetry by timestamp) is family P's planted defect.
- The four planted defects above are registered controls of GATE-CHRON-11 for these subjects.
- Determinism: a scenario is a pure function of (seed, scenario, binary); the same seed gives a byte-identical
  trace twice and on a second machine.

## Considerations

- The plan's text makes Tether the owner of leases and the publishing transaction and Chronicle the owner of the
  chunk format and store; scenarios live in `frob-conformance` and test both crates through their real code.
- OPEN-CHRON-5 (lease granularity; default one generation per run) is decided with family L's measurements
  (N29b-19).
- The fake device is only as honest as the dual runs; if the fake and `batch-cpu` ever disagree, the fake is
  fixed first and every scenario re-run.
- The dispatcher (third milestone) adds a fifth family; INV-ATEL-06 has its strongest test there.
- Release-lane time is the main cost: at about 30 ms per schedule 10⁵ schedules take under an hour on one core
  and minutes on 8; if schedules grow, the lane is split by family, never shortened.
