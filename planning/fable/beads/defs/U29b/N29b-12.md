## Background

The record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) keeps five retention classes:

| Class | Contents | Default policy |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-pinned | Chunks, snapshots, ledger rows, certificates and receipts in the closure of a live claim; qualification receipts; registered predictions | Kept until the claim is withdrawn; then tombstoned |
| Failure captures | Control-rate traces and snapshots (the device rings) of failing worlds | Kept while the run is the head of a lineage or is cited by an open comparison, bisect or autopsy; then 30 days |
| Sampled traces | Traces and snapshots of sampled worlds that did not fail | Evicted under the store budget, oldest unpinned first |
| Per-episode metrics and events | Metrics, histograms, digests, events | Kept for the life of the run record |
| Memo artifacts | Cached results | 64 GB by default; evict, never block |

Two numbers in that table are placeholders: the **30-day window** for failure captures after their run stops
being cited, and the **store budget** (64 GB) on each machine. They are open question OPEN-CHRON-4: "retention
windows for failure captures and the default store budget on each machine. Owner: owner of the machines. Blocks
GATE-CHRON-12. Default: 30 days and 64 GB." The milestone plan lists it under "owner input wanted" at the
second-milestone exit.

Why it is the owner's decision and not an engineering default: disk on the two machines is the owner's, a
campaign can produce failure captures faster than any fixed budget absorbs, and the cost of deleting too early
falls on later debugging (risk RISK-39: retention deletes the interval that explains a decision; mitigations:
decision-relevant chunks are pinned, tombstones, GATE-CHRON-12, and this question).

**Fail-closed default until this bead is closed: 30 days and 64 GB**, with three rules that hold whatever the
numbers are: retention never removes evidence silently (decision ADR-CHRON-09: deletion leaves a tombstone; a
metric whose samples are gone is refused, not interpolated; chunks in the evidence closure of a live claim are
pinned); pinned objects are never deleted to meet a budget; and going over budget degrades *new* sampled tracing
with marked gaps, never old evidence.

## Scope

In: the measurements the owner needs; the proposal with its arithmetic; the over-budget behaviour; the
configuration file and its per-machine defaults; the decision record; the retention gate's dependence on it.

Out: the collector and the verbs `store.status` and `store.collect` (seed BD-209); pinning at verdict time
(N29b-13); column compression, which changes the bytes per capture (N29b-17); the size of the device ring, which
changes what a capture contains (N29b-18).

## Technical approach

**1. Measurements (from second-milestone runs; no new mechanism).** On each machine, for the reference quadruped
and the reference humanoid workloads at the default chunk length and ring depth (256 control ticks; 2 s):
- bytes per failure capture: the flushed ring of one failing world, control-rate traces plus snapshots, as stored
  (uncompressed quantized columns by default);
- failure captures per GPU-hour for three typical jobs: a training run, a design sweep, an adversarial search;
- bytes per world-hour of per-episode metrics and events (kept for the life of the run record and therefore the
  floor of the store's growth);
- bytes per sampled world-hour at a 1% trace rate;
- the memo store's size after a standard design-check session.

**2. Arithmetic the proposal must show (a planning estimate to be replaced by the measurements; every figure
below is an assumption, labelled HYPOTHESIS).** If a quadruped capture is about 100 control ticks × 800 bytes of
trace plus 4 snapshots × 2 kB ≈ 90 kB, a sweep that fails 20% of 4,096 worlds per episode batch writes about
820 captures ≈ 74 MB per batch; at one batch a minute that is 4.4 GB per hour and 106 GB per day, which exceeds
64 GB in 15 hours with nothing else stored. The estimate shows that a budget in gigabytes and a window in days
cannot both be the binding rule, and that the order of eviction matters more than either number.

**3. What is to be decided (each with the default that holds meanwhile).**

| Parameter | Default | Options the proposal prices |
|---|---|---|
| Window for failure captures after their run is no longer a lineage head or cited | 30 days | 7, 30, 90 days; per job class |
| Store budget per machine (all classes except memo) | 64 GB | A figure per machine from its free disk, as an absolute and as a share |
| Memo budget | 64 GB; evict, never block | Unchanged unless measurements say otherwise |
| Eviction order inside the budget | Sampled traces, oldest unpinned first | Whether failure captures past their window go before sampled traces; whether captures of the same failure class beyond the first k per run are thinned |
| Behaviour over budget with nothing evictable | New control-rate tracing of sampled worlds is suspended and each untraced interval is recorded as a gap with reason `store-budget`; per-episode metrics, events and failure captures continue; `store.status` reports `over-budget` | Whether a job should be refused at admission when its estimated captures cannot fit |
| Private scenes | Same windows; data stays under the private root and inherits the scene's privacy class | Whether private captures get a shorter window |

**4. Configuration.** `store.toml` under the daemon's state root: `[budget] store_bytes`, `memo_bytes`;
`[window] failure_capture_days`; `[evict] order`; per-machine overrides keyed by hardware fingerprint. The values
in force are printed by `frob store status` and recorded in every collection receipt, so a tombstone can always
be traced to the policy that caused it. A change of policy is a new policy version (a decision-policy identifier
`chronicle.retention`, version n) and never applies retroactively to pinned objects.

**5. Procedure.** (a) Collect the measurements at the second milestone with the telemetry-cost harness and
`frob store status`. (b) Write the proposal with the table of item 3 filled per machine. (c) The owner decides.
(d) Record `docs/adr/ADR-CHRON-11.md` (proposed identifier) with the numbers, the measurements they rest on and
the conditions that reopen it (a new machine; compression adopted; a campaign class whose captures exceed the
budget in under a day). (e) Tombstone OPEN-CHRON-4 in the open-question register with the record's identifier.
(f) GATE-CHRON-12 is then run under the decided policy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The five measurements exist for both machines with their disclosure blocks (hardware, workload, world
      count, trace rate, chunk length, ring depth).
- [ ] The proposal prices every row of the table in item 3 and shows days-to-full for each job class.
- [ ] The owner's decision is recorded in `docs/adr/ADR-CHRON-11.md`, and `store.toml` defaults per machine match
      it.
- [ ] Until the decision exists, the defaults 30 days and 64 GB are in force and printed by `frob store status`
      as `policy: default (OPEN-CHRON-4 undecided)`.
- [ ] The over-budget behaviour is demonstrated: with a budget set below current use and nothing evictable, a
      sampled world's trace is replaced by a gap with reason `store-budget`, no pinned object and no failure
      capture inside its window is removed, and a metric over the gap is refused (FROB-REF-888).
- [ ] OPEN-CHRON-4 is tombstoned in the register; GATE-CHRON-12's record names the policy version it ran under.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `policy_parse_and_defaults`: an empty `store.toml` yields 64 GB, 64 GB, 30 days, the default order.
- `per_machine_override_by_fingerprint`.
- `window_arithmetic`: a capture whose run stopped being a lineage head at day 0 is collectible at day 30 and not
  at day 29, in virtual time.
- `citation_holds_capture`: a capture cited by an open comparison is not collectible at day 400.
- `over_budget_suspends_sampling_only`: budget below use with only pinned objects and in-window captures → no
  deletion; the next sampled trace is a gap `store-budget`.
- `policy_version_recorded_in_tombstone`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step group `policy-*` added to `scripts/e2e/store_retention.sh` (the script of seed BD-209 for the retention gate): `frob store status --json` before
and after writing a scratch `store.toml`; a run under a deliberately tiny budget; `frob telemetry query` across
the gap. Asserts the printed policy, the gap record and the refusal. NDJSON lines per the shared helper: command,
exit code, wall time, policy version, bytes per class with units, the budget and window in force as thresholds,
the gap's interval and reason; a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The window boundary (day 29 against day 30) and the citation hold are exact.
- Negative controls: a collector that deletes in-window captures to meet the budget (caught by
  `over_budget_suspends_sampling_only`); a policy change applied retroactively to pinned objects; an untraced
  interval with no gap record (a metric over it would then be computed from missing samples).
- The arithmetic of item 2 is recomputed from the measured inputs by a small script in the proposal, so a wrong
  estimate is replaced rather than argued.

## Considerations

- This decision blocks the retention gate, so it is one step more urgent than its milestone. The retention
  mechanism does not wait: it is built against the configuration file with the defaults.
- The plan's default "30 days and 64 GB" reads as a single store-wide budget; the register's companion row also
  states "failure captures 30 days in 64 GB". The proposal should say plainly whether the 64 GB memo budget and
  the 64 GB store budget are one pool or two; the default here is two pools, because memo artifacts evict freely
  and evidence does not.
- A short window makes the counterfactual-debugging subsystem's work harder: an autopsy opened on day 31 finds a
  tombstone. The citation hold is the remedy, and reports should say when a run's captures are close to expiry.
- Revisit when column compression is decided (N29b-17) and whenever a machine is added.
