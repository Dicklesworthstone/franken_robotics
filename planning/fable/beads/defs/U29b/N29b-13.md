## Background

The record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) cannot keep every trace forever, so it collects: sampled traces
are evicted under a store budget, failure captures expire, memo artifacts are evicted freely. Decision
ADR-CHRON-09 bounds what collection may do: **retention never removes evidence silently.** Deletion leaves a
tombstone; a metric whose samples are gone is refused, not interpolated; chunks in the evidence closure of a live
claim are pinned. Pins are taken when a verdict is issued: the verdict's evidence closure is pinned in the same
transaction that writes the verdict, so the interval that explains a decision cannot be collected while the
decision stands (risk RISK-39: retention deletes the interval that explains a decision).

Seed bead BD-209 builds retention: object classes, the `pin` table with its reason kinds (`claim`,
`qualification-receipt`, `prediction`, `open-job`, `lineage-head`, `open-study`, `cursor`, `hold`), the helper
`issue_verdict` that writes a verdict and its closure's pins in one transaction, `withdraw(claim, reason)`, the
collection cycle (roots, mark, sweep by class, tombstones, gaps), the refusal FROB-REF-813 for a deletion that
would remove a pinned object, the verbs `store.status` and `store.collect`, and the gate of 10⁴ random cycles.

This bead is the sub-task after the core mechanism: the edge cases in which a correct-looking implementation of
those rules still loses evidence or leaks pins. There are four.

1. **A verdict that commits during a cycle.** Mark runs first and sweep later. A verdict issued between them pins
   objects that the mark never saw. Unless the sweep re-checks, it deletes freshly pinned evidence. This is the
   same race as "write the verdict, then pin", moved from the writer into the collector.
2. **A writer that does not pin.** The rule protects only claims issued through the pinning transaction. A new
   subsystem that inserts a claim row directly has unpinned evidence and nobody notices until a citation fails.
3. **Withdrawal has no surface.** The repair for FROB-REF-813 is "withdraw the claim first", and the verb
   registry has no verb that does it; a function nobody can call safely becomes a database edit.
4. **Pins that outlive their holder.** A job that dies without a terminal receipt, a cursor never closed, a
   comparison abandoned: each leaves pins that grow the store for ever.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**1. Sweep re-checks pins (closing the mark-to-sweep race).** A cycle fixes its mark frontier F, the store's
commit counter when the mark begins. For each sweep candidate X, the removal is one ledger transaction that:
(a) reads `pin` for X and aborts the removal if any row exists; (b) aborts if X is in the closure of any claim
whose row has a commit counter greater than F (covers a claim whose pins are being written in a concurrent
transaction that has committed since the mark); (c) inserts the tombstone and removes the object's row. Because
`issue_verdict` commits verdict, claim and pins together, any verdict visible to (a) or (b) protects its closure,
and a verdict that commits after the removal transaction cannot have X in a resolved closure: its own closure
computation finds the tombstone and the verdict is refused (FROB-REF-869). Either order is safe; no order loses
evidence silently. The dry run performs the same checks without (c), so its list equals what a real collection
then removes at the same frontier.

**2. Every claim writer pins (the writer rule and its audit).** Rule: the only way to insert a row into `claim`
is through the pinning transaction (`issue_verdict` and its siblings for qualification receipts, registered
predictions, gate rows and accepted certificate bundles). Enforcement at two levels:
- *By construction.* The typed writer handle for the `claim` table is private to the retention module; other
  crates receive a function that takes the claim, its support and its dependencies and performs closure, pins and
  insert together. A compile-fail test pins that no other crate can name the raw handle.
- *By audit.* `audit::pins_cover_live_claims(frontier)` returns every (claim, object) pair where a live claim's
  closure contains a stored object without a `claim` pin held by that claim. It must be empty. It runs inside
  `store.collect` before the mark (a non-empty result aborts the cycle with the list: collection never proceeds
  on a store whose pins it cannot trust) and is reported by `store.status` as `unpinned_closure_objects: 0`.

**3. Withdrawal as a surface (DESIGN proposal).** `frob store collect --withdraw-claim <claim_id> --reason
<text>` (admin scope, idempotency key required), which calls BD-209's `withdraw` and adds:
- the withdrawal is refused if the claim is the support of a `qualification-receipt` unless the caller holds the
  owner scope (the blinded lane's error budget was spent on it);
- every claim that reaches the withdrawn one through `depends-on` edges becomes unavailable at the next read
  (the citation guard of N29b-10 reports `depends on withdrawn claim <id>`), and the response lists them;
- every gate row and closed bead whose claim is affected is listed, and the next re-affirmation pass (N29b-10)
  demotes those rows to `not-run`;
- the objects are not deleted by withdrawal; they lose their `claim` pins, fall back to their unpinned class and
  are tombstoned with reason `claim-withdrawn` by a later cycle under that class's policy;
- a withdrawn claim is never silently revived: re-running the evidence yields the same `claim_id` only if the
  content is identical, and the withdrawal row stays in the record with its reason and session.
If the registry's owner prefers a separate verb (for example in the `evidence` cluster), the transaction is
unchanged; the proposal is flagged in this unit's coverage notes.

**4. Holders other than claims: life cycles and orphans.**

| Reason kind | Taken | Released | Orphan test |
|---|---|---|---|
| `open-job` | As the job commits chunks | In the transaction of its terminal receipt | A job with no receipt and no live worker for longer than the daemon's restart grace; the daemon's restart logic writes a `failed` receipt, which releases the pins |
| `lineage-head` | When a run becomes a head | When the head moves; the failure-capture window starts then | A pin whose run is not a head in the lineage table |
| `open-study` | When a comparison, bisect or autopsy cites a run | When the citing object closes or is abandoned by its owner | A pin whose citing object is closed |
| `cursor` | On the first page of a paginated query | When the cursor is closed or its idle time expires; a later page is then refused as expired | A pin whose cursor is past expiry |
| `prediction` | At registration | When the prediction is scored (its claim takes over) or withdrawn | A pin whose prediction row is scored |
| `hold` | By an operator, with a text reason | By the operator | None; listed in `store.status` with age |

`audit::orphan_pins()` evaluates the orphan tests; `store.collect` releases orphans before the mark, each release
recorded with the reason `holder-gone`, and `store.status` prints the count. Releasing an orphan never deletes
anything by itself.

**5. Layout.** Additions to BD-209's `crates/frob-chronicle/src/retain/` (`sweep.rs` for item 1) plus
`retain/{audit.rs, withdraw_surface.rs, holders.rs}`. Determinism: exact. Evidence: audits return ledger rows.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Race: with a verdict committing between mark and sweep on 10⁴ seeded schedules, no object of any committed
      verdict's closure is removed; the planted collector without the re-check loses an object within 10⁴
      schedules and is caught.
- [ ] A verdict whose closure meets an object removed earlier in the same cycle is refused with FROB-REF-869 and
      writes nothing.
- [ ] `pins_cover_live_claims` is empty after every operation of a 10⁴-step random workload; a planted writer that
      inserts a claim row directly cannot compile, and (with the handle exposed in a test build) is found by the
      audit, which aborts the next collection.
- [ ] `store.collect --withdraw-claim` releases exactly that claim's pins, lists dependents and affected gate
      rows, leaves an object pinned by a second claim pinned, and is refused without the admin scope (exit 5) and
      for a qualification receipt without the owner scope.
- [ ] After withdrawal and a cycle, the objects carry tombstones with reason `claim-withdrawn`, the claim prints
      `withdrawn`, its dependents print `unavailable`, and re-affirmation demotes the affected gate row.
- [ ] Orphan pins of each kind are detected and released with reason `holder-gone`; a pin with a live holder is
      never released by the orphan pass.
- [ ] The dry run's list equals the real collection's removals at the same frontier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`crates/frob-chronicle/tests/retention_edges.rs`)

- `sweep_rechecks_after_mark`: mark at F; a verdict commits at F + 1 pinning object X; the removal of X aborts.
- `verdict_after_removal_refused_869`.
- `dry_run_equals_real`.
- `audit_empty_after_issue`; `audit_finds_direct_claim_insert` (test build).
- `collect_aborts_on_unpinned_closure`.
- `withdraw_releases_only_own_pins`: two claims pin one object; withdraw one → still pinned; withdraw the other →
  unpinned.
- `withdraw_lists_dependents`: A depends on B; withdrawing B lists A.
- `withdraw_qualification_needs_owner_scope`.
- `orphan_open_job_released_after_failed_receipt`; `orphan_cursor_after_expiry`; `live_holder_not_released`.
- `lineage_head_change_starts_window`: head moves at day 0 → the capture is collectible at day 30 and not at day
  29 (virtual time, default policy).
- Property: for 10⁴ seeded sequences of issue, withdraw, collect, crash and holder events, every live claim's
  closure objects are present (INV-CHRON-12: collection never removes a pinned object, and every removal leaves a
  tombstone).

### End-to-end script and logging

Step groups `race-*`, `withdraw-*` and `orphans-*` added to `scripts/e2e/store_retention.sh` (the script of seed
BD-209 for the retention gate): a fixed-n study is run to a verdict while a collection cycle is held between
mark and sweep by the conformance pause hook; `frob store status --json` shows the closure pinned and
`unpinned_closure_objects: 0`; `frob store collect --withdraw-claim` followed by a cycle; `frob evidence trace`
on the claim and on a dependent. NDJSON lines per the shared logging helper: command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's evidence block, pin counts by reason kind, the audit's count (threshold 0), orphan pins released,
tombstone fields, the refusal blocks; on failure the first object whose pin state differs from expectation.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer of the retention gate carried here: a citation of a collected object is refused with its reason.
- Negative controls: a sweep that trusts the mark (the race test must find a lost object); the two-step writer
  "write verdict, then pin" (a lost object) and "pin, then write verdict" (leaked pins after a crash); a force
  flag that bypasses FROB-REF-813; an orphan pass that releases a live cursor's pin.
- Determinism: the pin table after a seeded workload is identical twice and across restarts.

## Considerations

- This bead overlaps BD-209 by design: that bead owns pins, withdrawal and the cycle; this one owns four cases its
  statements leave open. If the two are merged, items 1 to 4 go into BD-209's technical approach.
- Pins make the store grow with the number of live claims. That is the intended trade: evidence for a standing
  decision is not disposable. `store.status` prints pinned bytes by reason kind so growth is visible.
- Gate rows and demonstration claims are live claims; their closures are pinned like any verdict's.
- The abort of a collection on a non-empty audit is fail closed: a store with a writer defect keeps everything
  until the defect is fixed, at the cost of disk.
- `scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything derived from it; it meets pins
  the same way an operator's delete does. N29b-14 carries that resolution.
