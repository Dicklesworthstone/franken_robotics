## Background

A scene pack built from a scan of a real home or workplace is marked `private`. It is stored under a configured
private root outside the repository; stills, replays, reports and fixtures derived from it inherit the mark and
are not exported without an explicit flag. Inheritance is computed, not declared: the mark of an artifact is the
join of the marks of everything in its lineage (decision ADR-SAFE-03), so a still of a replay of a policy
evaluated in a private scene is private without anyone marking it.

The record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) stores more scene-derived data than any other part of the system:
contact traces carry feature pairs and positions in the scene, snapshots carry the state of movable objects,
kinematic traces draw the robot's path through someone's kitchen. The retention chapter states the rule in one
sentence: **telemetry derived from a private scene inherits the scene's privacy class through retention and
export.** This bead makes that sentence true for every object Chronicle writes and every way such an object
leaves the store, including the ways that are easy to forget: a tombstone, a status listing, a log line, a
summary.

Risk RISK-12 (a scan of a private place leaks through a still, a fixture or policy weights; early warning: a
derived artifact without the private mark; owner: Chronicle lead) is the reason this is a bead and not a
sentence in a contract.

Rejected alternatives: marking runs by hand (the first forgotten flag is a leak); encrypting private chunks in
place (key management for no gain on a single-user machine; the private root and scopes already separate them);
dropping telemetry for private scenes (it would make private places undebuggable, which defeats ingesting them).

## Scope

In: what the private mark means for the objects Chronicle writes, beyond the store-wide mechanisms: that every
chunk, snapshot, column file, event, evidence node and tombstone gets the mark through the right parent edge; the
layout of Chronicle's files beneath the private root; the rules that are finer than an object-level check (a
private node inside an evidence closure, a detail row inside a result, the line between an aggregate and a
detail); which of Chronicle's export formats carry the mark and where; what a tombstone and the retention status
show; the refusal of an explicit private field in a log; tests with a planted leak path.

Out, with the bead that has it:

- the mark, its two values and its join over lineage, computed by the store at every write (N37-17). This bead
  defines no class type and no join of its own;
- the two store roots and the routing of a private object beneath the private root; the one listing filter
  `visible(session, object)` on every listing verb, catalog and shared view; the dispatcher's read check with the
  refusal `FROB-REF-731`; the one export door with its three conditions and the export log; the redaction of a
  private envelope's body in logs; the sweep over derived artifact kinds and the record of GATE-SAFE-04 (BD-305).
  This bead relies on those mechanisms for Chronicle's objects and re-specifies none of them;
- the verb `scene.purge`, its closure, its two phases, and the rule for a purge that meets pinned evidence
  (N37-19); the scopes and the path jail (BD-258, N37-11);
- weights trained in a private scene (the learning subsystem; the open question on whether weights inherit is the
  safety chapter's); the replay packet writer (bead N24a-41).

## Technical approach

**1. Class on every object.** Each chunk, snapshot, column file, ledger row that embeds scene-derived content,
evidence node and tombstone carries the mark of the mark lattice (N37-17): privacy `Public` or `Private`, stored
in the ledger's `mark` table and computed by the store's `put_derived` from the object's parents inside the
transaction that writes it. DESIGN, correcting an earlier draft of this bead that defined its own class
`private(scene_pack_id…)` with its own join in `privacy.rs`: one lattice and one join exist, N37-17's; a second
would be a second answer to "is this private". What this bead guarantees is that Chronicle's publishers pass the
right parents: a chunk, snapshot or event names its episode; an episode and a run name the scene pack, design,
task, policy and cards; a summary names the chunks it draws from (the parent table of N37-17). Two additions:

- *The cross-check at publish.* The mark of a run is fixed when the run is admitted and written into the run
  manifest. The publishing transaction compares the mark the store computed for a chunk with the run manifest's,
  and refuses the publish as an integrity fault when the chunk's is weaker (which can happen only if a parent
  edge is missing). No publisher supplies a mark. DESIGN, no new refusal code: a missing parent edge is a
  programming error, not an input a caller can repair, so the publish returns an error that maps to no code,
  nothing is written, and the job ends with the surface's `internal-fault` refusal (exit 9, retry `never`),
  whose diagnostic names the chunk key and the two marks.
- *Which packs.* For private objects Chronicle keeps an index column `private_roots`: the identifiers of the
  private scene packs among the object's ancestors (the union over its parents, written in the same
  transaction). It answers "private because of which place" for a caller with `private:read` and for the purge's
  bookkeeping, and survives in a tombstone after the lineage rows are gone. It is never consulted for a
  decision: every check reads the mark.

**2. Placement.** The store routes any object whose mark is `Private` beneath the private root, and moves an
object there when a late lineage edge raises its mark (BD-305). Chronicle's layout beneath that root is
`<private root>/chronicle/<namespace>/…`, mirroring its layout in the project store; no column file of a private
object is ever written under the project store. The ledger row of a private chunk lives in the same ledger (rows
are metadata) but its payload path resolves beneath the private root through the session's retained directory
handle, the path jail of the safety chapter.

**3. Reads.** Every verb argument that resolves to a stored object passes the dispatcher's read check (BD-305):
a `Private` object without `private:read` in the token is absent from listings and a direct request is refused
with `FROB-REF-731` ("a private object, or anything derived from one, without the private scope and flag";
exit 5). An earlier draft of this bead answered such a read with `FROB-REF-720`; the plan gives the private
refusal for it, and 720 remains the answer only where a verb's own capability is missing. For Chronicle this
covers `telemetry.query`, `telemetry.energy`, `telemetry.failures`, `sim.replay`, `artifact.read` and payload
fetches of `evidence.trace`. Two cases are finer than an object-level check and are specified here:

- *A private node inside a closure.* In `evidence.trace`, a private node appears in the closure as `{ id, kind,
  private: true }` with no content; the claim is reported unavailable to that caller with `why:
  private-no-scope` (N29b-10), so resolution never leaks content and the trace of a public claim is not refused
  as a whole.
- *A private row inside a result.* Detail rows that name contact features (in `telemetry.energy`), drill-down
  rows of `telemetry.failures` and any row carrying a position are withheld from a caller without the scope
  under the rule of step 5.

**4. Exports.** `run.export` (the `.frun` format), `artifact.export` and `telemetry.export-html` leave the store
through the one export door of BD-305: when any exported object is private they need the scope `private:export`,
the flag `--allow-private-export` and the owner-held key `private.export_allowed`; otherwise they are refused
with `FROB-REF-731` and the matching `observed`, and an admitted export is a row of the export log. What this
bead specifies is where each of Chronicle's formats carries the mark so that it survives outside the store: the
`.frun` manifest's `marks.privacy` field; the replay packet's header (writer: N24a-41); a visible banner and a
`meta` element in the exported HTML. Destination paths resolve under the export root (`FROB-REF-734`
otherwise).

**5. Aggregates and views (DESIGN rule).** A number that is a function of many worlds and reveals no scene
geometry (a success rate, an energy total, a flag count) keeps the class of its inputs for *export*, and may be
shown to the owning session without `private:read`, because the session that ran the job already holds its
results. A view that embeds scene-derived detail (a contact feature pair, a position, a still, a path) needs
`private:read`. Token-budgeted summaries list private drill-down handles only for callers with the scope.

**6. Retention.** Private objects obey the same classes, pins, windows and budgets as public ones and are
counted against the same budget. In `frob store status` they are a separate line (objects, bytes, pins) that is
printed only for a session with `private:read`; for any other session the status shows the non-private holdings
and the configured budget and nothing about private objects, not even a byte total. DESIGN, correcting an earlier
draft of this bead that showed unscoped callers the byte total: the seed's rule for views (BD-305) is that for a
session without the scope the whole surface is identical whether or not private objects exist, apart from a
direct read by identifier, and a byte total reveals that a private place was scanned and how much was recorded
there. The price, stated in the status output's help text, is that the listed holdings of an unscoped session
may sum to less than the store's use. A tombstone of a private object keeps: content hash, class, time, reason,
`privacy: private` and `private_roots`; it is itself a private row, and it does not show the scene pack's name
or `private_roots` to a caller without the scope. Catalogs, the shared archive, the leaderboard and MCP resource
lists omit private objects (the filter is BD-305's).

**7. Purge against pins (two rules that meet; the rule is N37-19's and is stated here because it acts on
Chronicle's pins).** `scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything derived
from it and writes a receipt listing the removed digests. The pin rule forbids deleting a pinned object
(`FROB-REF-813`; repair: withdraw the claim first). DESIGN, fail closed toward both, the same in N37-19: the
purge's dry run lists the live claims whose closures it would break; a real purge with `--withdraw-claims`
(argument `withdraw_claims`; scopes `scene:admin` and `private:read`) withdraws them in its first transaction,
each withdrawal and each tombstone recorded with reason `private-purge`, and then deletes; without the flag,
when any member is pinned by a live claim, it is refused with 813 and the list. A purge never leaves a claim
that still prints as resolved. Nothing the purge calls is built here: the withdrawal of a claim with a reason
(`withdraw(claim, reason)`), the release of its pins, the tombstone writer and the pin rule with 813 are the
store's beads and N29b-13's, which exist before the purge; and the query "live claims whose evidence closure
intersects this member set" is N37-19's, answered from the pin table (every claim writer pins its whole closure
in the transaction that writes the claim, N29b-13, so the pins on the members name exactly those claims). What
this bead adds is the test of that rule on Chronicle's private telemetry: chunks, snapshots and events of a
private run pinned by a claim are found by the purge's dry run, block a real purge without the flag, and with it
leave tombstones that keep `private_roots` and print nothing to an unscoped caller. A gate part or a bead's closing line that rested on a withdrawn claim is then demoted by the
re-affirmation pass of N29bp1-01, which runs after every purge; the purge receipt lists those claims so that the
demotions can be traced to it. (An earlier draft of this bead recorded the reason as `purge`; the one spelling is
`private-purge`.)

**8. Logs.** End-to-end logs, bead logs and gate rows never contain private-scene data: they carry identifiers,
counts and metrics only. Two rules of the shared logging helper do this and they are complementary: for an
envelope marked private the helper records its digest and its refusal or summary counts and never the result
body (BD-305); and when a script passes it an explicit field whose source object is private, the helper refuses
the write with an error naming the field (this bead). A script that runs on a private scene logs handles, not
values.

**9. Layout.** `crates/frob-chronicle/src/privacy.rs` (the publish cross-check, the `private_roots` index, the
row-level withholding of step 3, the aggregate rule of step 5, tombstone and status presentation; no class type
and no join, which are `frob_core::mark` and `frob_chronicle::marks` of N37-17) with calls from the publisher,
the readers and the exporters. Determinism: exact. Evidence: none created.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every object written by a run on a private scene carries the mark `Private` (N37-17) and the scene pack's
      identifier in `private_roots`, and its payload file is under the private root; a chunk whose computed mark
      is weaker than its run manifest's is refused at publish.
- [ ] Without `private:read`: private chunks, snapshots and detail rows are absent from every listing; direct
      reads are refused with FROB-REF-731; `evidence.trace` shows private nodes without content.
- [ ] `run.export`, `artifact.export` and `telemetry.export-html` on a private run are refused with FROB-REF-731
      unless scope, flag and owner key are all present, and with them write an artifact that carries the mark
      (`.frun` manifest, replay packet header, HTML banner and `meta` element).
- [ ] `frob store status` for a session without `private:read` is byte-identical before and after the private
      run; with the scope it shows the private line.
- [ ] Derived objects inherit: a replay of a private run, a still of that replay, an analysis result over its
      telemetry and a fork of it are all private without any explicit marking (the join test).
- [ ] A tombstone of a private object shown to a caller without the scope reveals no scene name or path.
- [ ] `scene.purge` (N37-19) on a scene with a live claim is refused with 813 listing the claims; with
      `--withdraw-claims` it removes the closure, leaves tombstones of reason `private-purge`, and the claims
      print `evidence: unavailable`.
- [ ] A planted leak path (a summary that prints a contact position from a private run to an unscoped caller) is
      caught by the audit test.
- [ ] No private value appears in any end-to-end log of the private fixture run (a scan of the log for known
      private strings and coordinates finds none).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `private_roots_union`: an object with a public parent and a parent rooted in pack a has roots {a}; with parents
  rooted in a and b, {a, b}; the union is commutative, associative and idempotent (property test). The laws of
  the mark's join are N37-17's tests.
- `chunk_mark_comes_from_episode`: no publisher API accepts a mark; `publish_refuses_weaker_than_run_manifest`
  (a chunk published with its episode edge omitted).
- `listing_omits_private_without_scope`; `direct_read_refused_731`.
- `export_refused_731_without_flag`; `export_writes_mark` (three formats).
- `store_status_identical_without_scope`; `store_status_private_line_with_scope`.
- `trace_closure_hides_private_payloads`.
- `aggregate_visible_detail_hidden`: success rate shown; contact feature rows refused.
- `tombstone_redaction`; `tombstone_is_private_row`.
- `purge_meets_pins_on_private_telemetry`: a claim whose evidence closure holds one chunk and one snapshot of a
  private run (and nothing else of the scene) is listed by `scene.purge`'s dry run; the real purge without
  `--withdraw-claims` is 813 and leaves ledger and both roots byte-identical; with the flag the chunk and the
  snapshot become tombstones of reason `private-purge` that keep `private_roots`, and the claim prints
  `evidence: unavailable`. The query itself and the purge's own tests
  (`purge_refused_813_without_withdraw_claims`, `purge_withdraws_claims_and_releases_pins`) are N37-19's.
- `log_helper_refuses_private_field`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chronicle_privacy.sh`: on a *synthetic* private scene pack (a generated room marked private, labelled
`synthetic`, containing a recognizable marker string and marker coordinates), run a referee job; exercise
listings, reads, exports, trace, status and purge with and without scopes. Asserts the criteria above. NDJSON
lines per the shared helper: command, exit code, wall time, scopes of the session, refusal blocks, counts of
objects by class as seen by each caller; on failure the first verb and object whose visibility differs from
the expectation (by identifier, never by content) and the scratch paths needed to reproduce. The script ends with
a scan of its own log for the marker string and coordinates and fails if any is found. A `summary` line with
assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The join laws (exact). The redaction scan (zero hits).
- Negative controls: an export path that forgets the check (planted in the replay-packet path); a listing that
  filters by name instead of by class (a renamed private chunk appears); a purge that deletes pinned evidence
  silently; a log line with a private coordinate.
- Relation to the safety chapter's gate on private places (GATE-SAFE-04): this bead supplies Chronicle's rows of
  that gate; the gate is measured by the safety unit's bead.

## Considerations

- The line between an aggregate and a detail (item 5) is a DESIGN judgement; the fail-closed reading is that
  anything with a position, a feature identifier or a pixel is a detail.
- A caller who holds a private chunk's content hash learns nothing from it; hashes are not secrets, payloads are.
- Seeds, recipes, design and module differences, receipts and policy weights are what crosses between machines;
  a private scan stays on the machine that holds it, and a job that needs it runs there. Chronicle's stores on the
  two machines are therefore never merged across the private boundary.
- Open question of the safety chapter (whether policy weights trained in a private scene inherit the mark;
  default: they inherit) does not change this bead; telemetry always inherits.
- Revisit when real-place ingestion lands (fifth milestone): the synthetic private fixture is then joined by a
  real one that never leaves its machine, and the redaction scan uses that machine's own marker list.
