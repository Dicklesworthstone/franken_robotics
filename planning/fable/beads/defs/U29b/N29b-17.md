## Background

The record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) stores bulk telemetry in content-addressed column files: a header
(schema identifier, channels with kind, unit, frame and quantization, tick range) and fixed-width little-endian
columns of quantized integers or `f32`. The files are readable with no device and no daemon, and the offline
auditor checks their hashes and chains with the standard library alone.

Open question OPEN-CHRON-1: **column compression without a third-party codec.** Owner: Chronicle lead. It blocks
nothing before the second milestone. Default: **uncompressed quantized columns.** The question exists because
telemetry volume decides how long evidence can be kept under a fixed store budget (the retention decision
N29b-12), and because the obvious answer, linking a general-purpose compression library, is closed: the project
admits dependencies sparingly, the auditor crate has an empty dependency table by rule, and a codec inside the
evidence path is supply-chain surface in exactly the place where bytes must be reproducible.

So the question is narrower than "should we compress": can a small, in-tree, deterministic transform that
exploits what the columns are (quantized, slowly varying, often constant) earn enough to be worth a format
version, without any external codec?

**Fail-closed default until this bead is closed: uncompressed columns, schema `frob.run/1` as shipped.**

## Scope

In: three candidate in-tree encodings; the measurement on recorded second-milestone telemetry; the costs that
count against an encoding; the decision rule fixed in advance; the decision record; if adopted, the follow-up
bead's outline.

Out: implementing an adopted encoding in the production writer (a follow-up bead created by the decision);
retention policy (N29b-12); the replay packet's own size rules (bead N24a-41 of unit U24a).

## Technical approach

**1. Candidates (each is a per-column, per-chunk transform; all integer arithmetic; byte-exact on every
platform).**

| Encoding | Transform | Fits | Decoder size (target) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constant and run-length | A column chunk that is one value, or runs of equal values, stored as (value, count) pairs | Validity masks, mode and contact-state channels, saturation bits, idle joints | under 60 lines |
| Delta with zigzag and fixed-width bit packing | Store the first value; then differences mapped by zigzag to unsigned; pack each block of 64 differences with the smallest bit width that fits the block | Quantized joint angles and velocities, clock phases, counters | under 150 lines |
| Frame-of-reference bit packing | Per block: minimum, then offsets in the smallest sufficient width | Quantized torques and forces with bounded range, histogram bins | under 100 lines |

`f32` columns are not transformed (the experiment may test the same schemes on their raw bit patterns and is
expected to find little). No entropy coder, no dictionary coder, no floating-point transform.

**2. What is measured.** On the recorded telemetry of the second milestone's reference workloads (quadruped and
humanoid; sampled traces, failure captures, per-episode data; both machines), for each stream and channel kind:
- compression ratio = raw bytes / encoded bytes, per stream and overall, with the share of each stream in the
  store;
- encode cost on the publish path, in nanoseconds per sample and as a share of step time (it must fit inside the
  telemetry budget of GATE-CHRON-13: levels T0 and T1 on every world at most 3% of step time, and T2 on a 1%
  sample at most 2% more);
- decode cost for `telemetry.query` and replay;
- lines of decoder that the offline auditor or any second reader would need;
- effect on random access: a query for a tick range inside a chunk must decode at most one 64-sample block more
  than it returns.

**3. Constraints that are not negotiable.** The content hash of a chunk is over the *stored* bytes, so an
encoding must be canonical: one input, one byte string (smallest width per block, no encoder choices). The
decoded column must be bit-identical to the raw column (lossless). The format is a new schema version
(`frob.run/2`) with round-trip and refusal tests; version 1 files stay readable forever. Chunks already
published are never re-encoded (their hashes are evidence).

**4. Decision rule, fixed before the data.** Adopt in-tree encoding only if all hold:
1. overall ratio on the store's actual byte mix is at least 2.0 (below that, a doubling of the budget in
   N29b-12 is cheaper than a format version);
2. encode cost keeps GATE-CHRON-13 inside its thresholds with a measured margin;
3. the auditor needs no decoder to do its job (it hashes stored bytes), or needs fewer than 300 added lines with
   no dependency;
4. canonical form and losslessness are demonstrated by property tests on 10⁶ random columns per encoding.
Otherwise the default stands and the record says why. A ratio between 1.5 and 2.0 is recorded as "not adopted;
revisit if the store budget binds".

**5. Hand-checkable ratios, to anchor expectations.** A joint-angle column quantized to 16 bits whose successive
differences fit in 5 bits packs 64 samples into 16 + 8 + 63·5 = 339 bits against 1,024: ratio 3.0. A contact-state
byte column that changes twice in a 256-tick chunk run-length encodes to 3 pairs of (1 byte, 2 bytes) = 9 bytes
against 256: ratio 28. A torque column with full-range noise (differences need 16 bits) does not compress at
all, and the encoder must fall back to raw for that block at a cost of one header byte.

**6. Output.** `docs/adr/ADR-CHRON-12.md` (proposed identifier): the measurements, the rule's four tests, the
decision; OPEN-CHRON-1 tombstoned in the open-question register with the record's identifier. If adopted, a
follow-up bead "column encoding in `frob.run/2`" with: the canonical-form specification, writer and reader,
auditor impact, the format-registry row and tests, and a migration note (none: old files keep their version).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Prototype encoders and decoders for the three candidates exist in a bench-only module (not in the
      production writer) with property tests for losslessness and canonical form.
- [ ] The measurement table exists for both workloads and both machines with disclosure blocks: ratio per stream
      and overall, encode and decode cost, decoder lines.
- [ ] The decision record applies the four-part rule literally to the measured numbers.
- [ ] If not adopted: the default is unchanged and the record states the measured overall ratio. If adopted: the
      follow-up bead exists and nothing in the store was re-encoded.
- [ ] The estimate used by the retention proposal (N29b-12) is updated with the measured bytes per capture under
      the decided encoding.
- [ ] OPEN-CHRON-1 is tombstoned in the register.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (bench module `crates/frob-chronicle/benches/column_codecs/`)

- `rle_roundtrip`, `delta_bitpack_roundtrip`, `for_bitpack_roundtrip`: 10⁶ seeded random columns each, including
  constant, monotone, alternating-sign and full-range cases; decoded equals raw bit for bit.
- `canonical_form`: encoding the decoded output reproduces the same bytes; two encoders written independently
  (reference and optimized) agree byte for byte.
- `hand_cases`: the three anchors above give 339 bits, 9 bytes and raw fallback.
- `random_access_bound`: a range query touches at most one extra block.
- `width_selection_minimal`: a block whose differences fit in 5 bits is never packed at 6.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chronicle_column_codecs.sh`: records a run in its own state root, since a script starts from an
empty store (`frob sim run g1 --tier referee --seconds 4 --telemetry T2 --seed <seed> --key cc-run-<seed>
--json`), exports it with `frob run export <run> --out <scratch dir under the export root> --key
cc-export-<seed> --json` (N29a-02's verb; a run verb: it carries a key), runs the bench binary over the bundle's
column files, and prints the table. The decision's table comes from the same export and bench steps applied to
the recorded reference workloads of item 2 on each machine. NDJSON lines per the shared helper: command, exit code, wall time,
per-stream raw and encoded bytes with the ratio against the 2.0 threshold, encode nanoseconds per sample against
the telemetry budget, decoder line counts; on failure the stream, chunk and block whose round trip or width
check failed, with the seed and the command that replays it; a `summary` line with the rule's four outcomes. The
script changes nothing in the store (digest before and after logged).

### Known answers and negative controls

- The three hand cases. Negative controls: an encoder that picks a non-minimal width (canonical-form test must
  fail); a lossy delta on `f32` bit patterns that rounds (round-trip must fail); a measurement run on synthetic
  constant data only (the script refuses to conclude from a store with no sampled traces).
- Determinism: encoded bytes identical across platforms and across debug and release for the pinned columns.

## Considerations

- Supply chain and licence: no third-party codec is admitted by this question's own wording; an in-tree encoder
  is code the project must maintain and fuzz with the other format readers.
- Quantization already removes most of what a general codec would find; the remaining gain is in slowly varying
  and constant channels, which is why the candidates are these three.
- Compression interacts with the device ring: rings are flushed raw from device memory; encoding happens on the
  host at publish time and must not delay the publishing transaction's lease window.
- Priority is the milestone's default (1). The question blocks no mechanism, and an earlier draft set it one
  step lower for that reason; but the second-milestone exit review (BD-224) waits on this bead's decision
  record, and a blocker may not sit below what it blocks. The experiment itself stays small: if the retention
  decision does not find the budget binding, "not adopted, with the measured ratio" is a complete answer.
