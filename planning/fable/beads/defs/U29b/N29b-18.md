## Background

A world's record is published in fixed logical chunks. Chunk i of episode e of world w on stream s covers
control ticks [i·L, (i + 1)·L), cut short only by the episode's terminal tick; **L is fixed for the run at
admission**. The chunk key (run, world, episode, stream, index) does not mention a device, a shard, a batch slot
or a retry, which is what makes a duplicate detectable and a conflict provable (decision ADR-CHRON-02: the
logical chunk partition is fixed per run and independent of device sharding). Separately, a per-world **ring
buffer** on the device keeps the last seconds of control-rate traces and full-state snapshots and is flushed
when a world fails, so failures are always recorded in full without paying to record everything.

Open question OPEN-CHRON-2: **the chunk length L and the depth of the device ring.** Owner: Chronicle lead. It
blocks GATE-CHRON-04 (the chunk protocol's gate), because L appears in every chunk key, every fixture and every
recorded digest chain; changing it after the gate's fixtures exist invalidates them. Default: **256 control
ticks per chunk and 2 s of ring.**

L is a trade among six costs, each of which the decision must show:

| Cost | Grows with | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ledger rows and transactions | 1/L | One publishing transaction per chunk boundary; the ledger is a single-writer database |
| Work lost on a crash, and the granularity of a restart | L | Unpublished state is discarded; a restart resumes from the last committed chunk |
| Cancellation and lease renewal latency | L | A healthy writer renews its lease at its next chunk boundary; a budget stop publishes nothing partial |
| First-divergence search inside a chunk | L | Binary search runs over chunks; inside the first unequal chunk per-step digests are compared in order |
| Pagination and retention granularity | L | Rows and eviction are per chunk |
| Host memory per publishing worker | L | A worker holds one chunk per world and stream before commit |

**Fail-closed default until this bead is closed: L = 256, ring = 2 s**, both recorded in the run manifest of
every run, so that any run made before the decision replays under the L it was made with.

## Scope

In: the cost model with its arithmetic; the measurements at the first milestone (ledger and CPU) and the list of
figures to re-read at the second (devices); the decision rule; the decision record; how L and the ring depth are
carried in manifests.

Out: the chunk protocol (BD-091, BD-210); the ring's implementation (BD-207); lease granularity (N29b-19);
retention numbers (N29b-12).

## Technical approach

**1. Arithmetic at the default (planning figures, labelled HYPOTHESIS until measured).** Control rate 50 Hz, so
L = 256 is 5.12 s of simulated time.
- *Rows.* A run of W worlds with S always-on streams publishes W·S rows per 5.12 simulated seconds. With
  W = 4,096 and S = 3 (per-episode metrics, events, provenance): 12,288 rows per chunk boundary. The binding
  figure is rows per wall second = (world-steps per second ÷ steps per control tick ÷ L) × S. At 2·10⁶
  world-steps/s and 10 steps per tick: 2·10⁵ world-ticks/s ÷ 256 × 3 ≈ 2,340 rows/s; each of the 4,096 worlds
  advances about 49 ticks per wall second, so a boundary arrives every 5.2 wall seconds. Publishing must
  therefore batch all worlds of a shard into one transaction per boundary (one transaction of 12,288 rows every
  5.2 wall seconds at that rate), never one transaction per row.
- *Lost work.* A crash loses at most L ticks per world: 5.12 s simulated, about 5 wall seconds of a full batch.
- *Divergence scan.* At most L per-step digest comparisons after ⌈log₂(chunks)⌉ chain comparisons: 256 + 10 for
  a 1,000-chunk episode.
- *Worker memory.* One chunk per world and stream: for a control-rate trace of about 200 floats per tick
  (joint states, torques, contact wrenches), 256 × 800 B ≈ 205 kB per traced world.
- *Ring.* 2 s at 50 Hz is 100 ticks: about 100 × 800 B = 80 kB of trace per world, plus snapshots (a humanoid's
  integration state is about 135 floats, FACT from the dynamics chapter, so a snapshot is under 1 kB before
  contact memory). For 4,096 worlds: about 330 MB of device memory for the trace ring. This is the figure that
  makes ring depth a real question on a 16 GiB default envelope.

**2. Candidates.** L ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024} control ticks; ring ∈ {1, 2, 4} s, with the option of a ring
that holds control-rate traces for 2 s and snapshots only at chunk boundaries.

**3. Measurements at the first milestone (CPU, the real ledger).** For each L: publishing transactions per
second sustained by the ledger at 12,288 rows per transaction, with fsync; time to recover after a forced kill
(the admission test of the ledger: open, write, forced kill, recover); observed cancellation latency
(request to acknowledgement) on the referee and on the CPU job engine; wall time of a first-divergence query on
a 1,000-chunk fixture. Measurements re-read at the second milestone (devices): ring bytes per world as stored;
flush time of a failing world; whether "the last 2 s" contains the first anomaly for the planted failure
fixtures (the share of failures whose first-cause tick lies inside the ring).

**4. Decision rule, fixed before the data.** Choose the largest L that satisfies all of: (a) lost work per crash
at most 10 s of simulated time at 50 Hz (L ≤ 512); (b) cancellation latency at most 2 s wall at the reference
throughput; (c) the ledger sustains the row rate with at least a factor of 4 head-room; (d) a first-divergence
query on a 1,000-chunk episode returns in under 1 s on the host. If two values pass, prefer the default (no
change is a virtue: fixtures and digests stay). Choose the smallest ring depth for which at least 95% of planted
failures have their first anomaly inside the ring; if none of {1, 2, 4} s reaches 95%, keep 2 s and record that
first cause comes from the first-anomaly latches, not from the ring.

**5. Carrying the values.** `chunk_len` and `ring_seconds` are fields of the run manifest and of the `.frun`
manifest ("the chunk length" is listed among its contents); they enter the run identity. Readers never assume a
value. A request to replay or fork uses the recorded L. Two runs with different L can still be compared by
`telemetry.divergence`, which aligns on ticks, not on chunk indices; its chain comparison then starts at the
coarser partition's boundaries.

**6. Output.** `docs/adr/ADR-CHRON-13.md` (proposed identifier) with the table of costs per candidate, the
measurements, the rule's outcome and the two numbers; OPEN-CHRON-2 tombstoned in the open-question register. The
ring half is marked `provisional until the second-milestone device figures` and re-affirmed or changed then, in
the same record.

- [ ] OPEN-CHRON-2 is tombstoned in the register with the record's identifier before GATE-CHRON-04's CPU rows are
      recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `chunk_extent_arithmetic`: for L ∈ {64, 256, 1,024}, chunk i covers [i·L, (i + 1)·L); an episode ending at tick
  700 with L = 256 has chunks of 256, 256 and 188 ticks.
- `manifest_carries_chunk_len`; `replay_uses_recorded_len` (a run recorded at L = 128 replays with the default
  set to 256).
- `rows_per_second_formula`: 2·10⁶ world-steps/s, 10 steps per tick, L = 256, S = 3 → 2,343.75 rows/s.
- `divergence_across_different_len`: two recordings of one deterministic episode at L = 128 and L = 256 report
  no divergence; with a planted difference at tick 300 both report tick 300.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chronicle_chunk_len.sh`: for each candidate L runs `frob sim run --tier referee --chunk-len <L>`
on a 64-world fixture, a forced kill and recovery, a cancellation, and `frob telemetry divergence` on a planted
pair. NDJSON lines per the shared helper: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, L, rows and transactions
per second with units, recovery time, cancellation latency against 2 s, divergence query time against 1 s,
lost ticks after the kill against L; a `summary` line with the candidate that the rule selects.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The extent arithmetic (256, 256, 188) and the row-rate formula are exact.
- Known answer of the chunk protocol carried as context (KA-765 of the surface chapter): with a fixed partition
  [k·C, (k + 1)·C) per (world, episode), ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) offered after a shard split resolve to a
  duplicate or a conflict, never two commits.
- Negative controls: a reader that assumes L = 256 (fails the recorded-length test); a publisher with one
  transaction per row (fails the transaction-rate assertion).
- Determinism: digests of a run do not depend on L (chunking is a property of the record, not of the physics):
  the state digest at tick t is equal for all candidates.

## Considerations

- This decision blocks the chunk protocol's gate, so it is one step more urgent than its milestone; the protocol
  bead proceeds on the default.
- A larger L lowers ledger load and raises every latency; the default sits where a crash costs about five seconds
  of work. The plan's throughput targets are TARGETs until the second-milestone profile; the rule's condition (c)
  is re-checked then.
- The ring depth interacts with retention (bytes per failure capture, N29b-12) and with first-cause reporting
  (the latches of N29b-05 make first cause independent of the ring).
- If the device ring cannot fit the envelope at 2 s for humanoids, the fallback named in advance is a ring that
  keeps control-rate traces for 1 s and snapshots at chunk boundaries only.
