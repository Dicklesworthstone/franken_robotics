## Background

Publishing a chunk of a world's record is one ledger transaction that does five things or nothing: checks the
writer's lease, checks alignment to the fixed chunk partition, checks the predecessor, detects a duplicate or a
conflict, and commits. The lease is a **fencing token**: the writer's `lease_gen` must equal the run's current
lease generation. Any reassignment of worlds to workers (by retry, shard split or device loss) increments the
generation, so every older writer is fenced out; a healthy writer renews at its next chunk boundary. A publish
under a late lease is refused and nothing is written (`FROB-REF-875`; ranked repair: re-acquire; replay from the
last committed tick).

Open question OPEN-CHRON-5: **lease granularity: one generation per run, or one per shard with a versioned
world-to-shard map.** Owner: Tether lead (the surface and daemon subsystem owns leases and the publishing
transaction; the record subsystem owns the chunk format and store). It blocks GATE-CHRON-04 on two devices.
Default: **one per run.**

The trade is simple to state. With one generation per run, *any* reassignment fences *every* writer of the run:
when one of two devices is lost, the healthy device's next publish is refused with 875, it re-acquires and
continues. That is safe and has a cost: a stall of every shard at every reassignment, and a refused publish on a
writer that did nothing wrong. With one generation per shard, only the affected shard is fenced, but safety now
depends on a second structure, the map from worlds to shards, being versioned and checked in the same
transaction; otherwise two shards can each hold a valid lease and both believe they own world 17.

**Fail-closed default until this bead is closed: one generation per run.** It cannot produce a double writer;
it can only waste time.

## Technical approach

**1. Design A (default): one generation per run.** State: `lease_gen: u64` in the run's row of the ledger table
`run_lease` (BD-091; the `run` row does not carry it). Reassignment of any world: `lease_gen += 1` in a ledger
transaction (BD-091's `reassign`). Publish precondition: `writer.lease_gen == run_lease.lease_gen`. Safety
argument: at most one assignment of worlds to writers is current per generation, and a writer holding an older
generation can commit nothing; so for every chunk key at most one writer can commit, and INV-CHRON-01 (a
committed chunk key maps to one content hash) and INV-CHRON-02 (committed chunks are exactly indices 0 to the
frontier) follow from the transaction's other checks.

**2. Design B: one generation per shard, with a versioned map.** State: `map_version: u64`; `shard_of(world)`
under that version; per shard `lease_gen[shard]`. Reassignment of worlds from shard X to shard Y: one
transaction that increments `map_version`, rewrites `shard_of` for the moved worlds, and increments
`lease_gen[X]` and `lease_gen[Y]` (both shards' membership changed); shards whose membership did not change keep
their generation. Publish precondition, checked in the publishing transaction: the writer presents
(shard, lease_gen, map_version); it must hold that `lease_gen == lease_gen[shard]`, and for every world in the
chunk batch `shard_of(world) == shard` under the *current* map. Safety argument to be written and tested before B
can be chosen: for every world, at every commit point, exactly one (shard, generation) pair passes the
precondition; a writer of an untouched shard keeps passing because neither its generation nor its worlds'
mapping changed.

**3. What is measured.**
- *Stall under A.* Time from a reassignment to the first successful publish of each unaffected shard:
  the refused publish, the re-acquire, the retry. In the simulation harness (virtual time, family L of N29b-11)
  as counts of refused publishes and re-acquire round trips per reassignment; on two real devices as wall time
  and as the share of throughput lost at realistic reassignment rates (a retry or shard split per 10³ batches; a
  device loss per day).
- *Replayed work under A.* A fenced writer discards unpublished state and replays from the last committed tick;
  the measurement is ticks replayed per reassignment (at most one chunk length per world).
- *Complexity of B.* Lines added to the publishing transaction and its tests; the number of new await points the
  simulation must cover; whether every oracle still passes over 10⁵ schedules with B.

**4. Arithmetic at the default (planning estimate, HYPOTHESIS).** With chunks of 256 control ticks at 50 Hz and a
batch that reaches a boundary about every 5 wall seconds, a fenced healthy shard loses at most the chunk in
progress: about 5 s of one device's work per reassignment. At one reassignment per hour that is 0.14% of
throughput; at one per minute, 8%. So A is acceptable unless reassignment becomes frequent, which is the case for
preempt-and-resume scheduling (third milestone) and for a fleet that migrates work between the two machines.

**5. Decision rule, fixed before the data.** Keep A if the measured throughput loss at the second milestone's
observed reassignment rate is under 1% and no third-milestone mechanism on the roadmap raises the rate above one
per minute. Choose B only if (a) the loss under A exceeds 1% or the dispatcher's preemption makes it exceed 1% by
its own estimate, (b) the safety argument of item 2 is written down and reviewed, and (c) every oracle passes
over 10⁵ seeded schedules with B, including the scenario "takeover while the old writer is alive and
publishing". If (a) holds and (b) or (c) does not, A stays and the dispatcher must batch its reassignments.

**6. If B is chosen.** The chunk record gains `map_version` beside `lease_gen` (provenance stream: identities,
versions, lease generation, frontier); the receipt is unchanged; FROB-REF-875 gains a second trigger in its
message ("the world is no longer mapped to this shard"); the format version of the ledger schema increases; runs
recorded under A remain readable.

**7. Output.** `docs/adr/ADR-CHRON-14.md` (proposed identifier): the two designs, the measurements, the rule's
outcome, and the conditions that reopen the question (a new device count; a scheduler that reassigns more often).
OPEN-CHRON-5 tombstoned in the open-question register. The decision is recorded by, or with the written decision
of, the Tether lead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both designs run under the simulation harness's family L with every oracle active; A passes 10⁵ schedules;
      B either passes 10⁵ schedules or the record states the violating schedule.
- [ ] The stall and replay measurements exist for A in simulation and on two devices (or, if only one device is
      available on the host, on two shards of one device, labelled as such).
- [ ] The decision record applies the rule to the measured loss and names A or B.
- [ ] Under the decided design, the planted "late lease that writes" is refused 100 of 100 on two devices, and a
      healthy unaffected shard never commits a chunk for a world it does not own.
- [ ] If B: the safety argument is on file and reviewed; the schema version is bumped; an A-era run replays.
- [ ] OPEN-CHRON-5 is tombstoned in the register before the two-device rows of GATE-CHRON-04 are recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gen_per_run_fences_all`: two writers at generation 3; a reassignment makes it 4; both publishes at 3 are
  refused with 875; both re-acquire at 4 and succeed.
- `gen_per_shard_fences_only_moved` (design B prototype): shards X, Y, Z; move world 17 from X to Y; writers of X
  and Y at their old generations are refused; Z's writer succeeds without re-acquiring.
- `stale_map_refused` (B): a writer of Y that presents the old map version for world 17 is refused.
- `no_double_owner` (B, property): for 10⁴ seeded sequences of reassignments, every world has exactly one
  (shard, generation) pair that passes the precondition at every commit point.
- `throughput_loss_formula`: 5 s lost per reassignment: one per hour → 5/3,600 = 0.14%; one per minute → 8.3%.

### End-to-end script and logging

Steps `lease-*` of `scripts/e2e/chronicle_simtest.sh` (owned by N29b-11) for the simulated measurements, and
`scripts/e2e/chronicle_lease_two_device.sh` for the device ones: a two-shard job on two devices of one machine
(or two shards of one back end, labelled as such; `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal` sit on two machines, and no job
spans them before the fleet scheduler, which is out of scope), a forced reassignment (a killed worker), and the
wall time to the first successful publish of the healthy shard; the script is run on each machine in turn. NDJSON lines per the shared helper: command, exit code, wall time,
evidence block, lease generations before and after, refused publishes with their refusal blocks, stall seconds
and ticks replayed with units, the loss share against the 1% threshold; on failure the shard, generation and
world whose publish differs from the expectation, with the seed; a `summary` line with the rule's outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-711's controls: a late lease that writes; overlapping ranges that commit; a reset at tick zero that
  collides: each must be refused under the decided design.
- Negative controls: design B without the map check in the transaction (the property test must find a double
  owner); a lease check performed before the transaction begins (a takeover between check and commit lets a late
  writer in; found by the simulation's crash-between-await-points exploration).
- Determinism: the simulated measurements are a pure function of seed and scenario.

## Considerations

- This decision blocks the two-device rows of a gate, so it is one step more urgent than its milestone. Device
  work proceeds on the default.
- The ledger has a single writer process (the daemon); leases fence *workers*, not database connections. Both
  designs keep that.
- B's benefit grows with the number of shards; with two devices it is small. That is the main argument for the
  default, and for revisiting only when the fleet grows or preemption arrives.
- Whatever is chosen, the fencing check stays inside the publishing transaction; that property, not the
  granularity, is what the chunk protocol's safety rests on.
