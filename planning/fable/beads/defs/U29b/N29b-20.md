## Background

**The defect this prevents.** A 1 kg block rests on a frictionless stationary support under g = 8 m/s². A step
that applies forces, then contact, then drift gives the block a free velocity g·h and removes it with the support
impulse. The kinetic energy removed per step is ½·m·(g·h)², which is ½·m·g²·h per second of simulated time: 1 J
at 32 Hz and 0.5 J at 64 Hz. The block has zero displacement and the support produces no heat. The quantity is
first order in h and vanishes under refinement. Booked as contact dissipation, it warms the tyres of a parked
robot at a rate set by the step size.

Impulse-based contact steps are of exactly this kind (FACT: the referee's contact step from the sibling
simulation library is first-order kick, contact, drift), and "energy removed by the contact solve" is the number
such a step hands you for free. The rule of the plan (tier Solid): the energy that an impulse-level contact step
exchanges with the velocity predictor is a **separate ledger line**, the *algorithmic exchange*, and only
**constitutive dissipation**, computed from a law with a dissipation function, feeds a thermal or a wear state.

Ownership: the contact subsystem (crate `frob-contact`) and the dynamics subsystem (crate `frob-dyn`) own the two
quantities; the record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) owns the ledger line and the invariant. This bead is
the contact rows. The joint rows and the predictor-side bookkeeping are N29b-21; the ledger line and the rule
about thermal states are N29b-22.

The plan states the concept, the laws and three known answers; it does not give a per-row formula. The formulas
below are this bead's DESIGN. They were chosen because they reproduce the plan's three numbers exactly, and
because the two sides of the exchange then cancel identically in every step (shown in N29b-21), which is what
"the identity is checked with both shown" requires. Rejected: defining constitutive dissipation as "whatever the
solve removed minus an estimate of the artifact" (it makes heat depend on the estimate); reporting only the net
exchange (it is identically zero and hides the 1 J/s that the reader needs to see is not heat).

## Scope

In: the row classification; the three per-row energies S, W and X for impulse-level contact rows; constitutive
dissipation D per law component; stored energy change for compliant rows; the per-row discretization remainder;
the `RowEnergy` record handed to the ledger; row-level tests of the three known answers.

Out: the three row velocities and the predictor side (N29b-21); joint-limit, stiction and backlash rows
(N29b-21); the ledger line, telemetry and binding rule (N29b-22); batched kernels (N29b-24); the hard-contact
batch law itself (a fifth-milestone seed).

## Technical approach

**1. Notation for one integration interval of length h** (a physics step, or a sub-interval of the transactional
step). v⁻: generalized velocity at the start. v*: the *unconstrained predictor*, the velocity after every force
that is a function of state has acted over the interval (gravity, bias forces, actuators, springs, dampers, and
compliant rows, see item 2). v⁺: the velocity after the constraint solve, v⁺ = v* + M⁻¹·Jᵀ·λ, with M the mass
matrix, J the stacked row Jacobians and λ the row impulses. For row i the row velocity is uᵢ = Jᵢ·v plus any
prescribed surface velocity, evaluated at the three points: uᵢ⁻, uᵢ*, uᵢ⁺. N29b-21 supplies the three values.

**2. Row classification.**
- *Impulse-level rows*: the row's impulse is an unknown fixed by a constraint (non-penetration, the Coulomb cone,
  restitution at an event). These are the contact rows of the referee's impulse step and of the hard-contact
  batch law.
- *State-function rows*: the row's force is given by a law of the state (a compliant normal force k·d + c·ḋ, a
  regularized friction force), even when it is evaluated implicitly inside a solve. These rows count as forces
  of the predictor. They have stored energy and dissipation and **no algorithmic exchange**: it is zero by
  definition, which is the plan's statement that on the compliant tiers the algorithmic exchange of a resting
  contact is zero because the contact force is a function of state.

**3. Three energies of an impulse-level row.**

```
Sᵢ = λᵢ · ½·(uᵢ* + uᵢ⁺)      stage energy: kinetic energy the row's impulse returns to (+) or removes from (−)
                              the predictor;  Σᵢ Sᵢ = T(v⁺) − T(v*)  exactly, with T(v) = ½·vᵀ·M·v
Wᵢ = λᵢ · ½·(uᵢ⁻ + uᵢ⁺)      whole-interval discrete work of the row on the bodies (what the ledger's closure uses)
Xᵢ = Sᵢ − Wᵢ = λᵢ · ½·(uᵢ* − uᵢ⁻)      the algorithmic exchange of the row
```

Proof of the sum: T(v⁺) − T(v*) = ½·(v⁺ − v*)ᵀ·M·(v⁺ + v*) = ½·(Jᵀλ)ᵀ·(v⁺ + v*) = Σᵢ λᵢ·½·(uᵢ* + uᵢ⁺). For a row
against a prescribed moving surface the same algebra leaves the term λᵢ·w (w the surface velocity along the
row), which is the boundary work credited to `W_external`.

At a located event (an impact solved on the pre-event state with no force stage before it) u* = u⁻, so Xᵢ = 0:
everything an event impulse removes is physical.

**4. Constitutive dissipation, by law component (each ≥ 0, each from the solved state, never from S).**

| Component | Formula per interval | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Sliding friction | D_f = ‖λ_t‖ · ‖u_t⁺‖, the integral of f_t·v_slip at the solved slip velocity (f_t = λ_t/h acting over h) | Every sliding contact, both row classes |
| Normal damping of the compliant law | D_n = c · (u_n⁺)² · h, the integral of c·ḋ² with ḋ the solved normal rate and c the law's damping coefficient | State-function normal rows |
| Restitution loss | D_e = ½ · m_eff · (1 − e²) · v_n², with v_n the pre-impact normal approach speed, e the restitution coefficient and m_eff = 1/(J_n·M⁻¹·J_nᵀ); for coupled rows of one island the general form D_e = −Σ_rows λᵢ·½·(uᵢ⁻ + uᵢ⁺) | A located impact, booked once at the event |
| Rolling and torsional friction, where a law has them | Impulse magnitude times solved relative rate | As the law declares |

A sticking contact (u_t⁺ = 0) has D_f = 0 exactly. A resting hard contact has no active dissipation component,
so its constitutive dissipation is exactly 0.

**5. Stored energy of a state-function row.** ΔUᵢ = U(d⁺) − U(d⁻) with U the law's potential (½·k·d² for the
linear compliant normal; the regularized friction law's tangential spring where it has one). It goes to the
ledger's potential line; storage is not dissipation and not creation.

**6. Remainder.** For every row, ρᵢ = Wᵢ + Dᵢ + ΔUᵢ is the part of the row's whole-interval work that its law
does not account for (a discretization remainder: for a decelerating slide D_f uses u_t⁺ where W uses the mean).
ρᵢ is reported to the ledger's `numerical residual` line with the row's identity. It is never added to D. In the
three gate fixtures ρᵢ = 0.

**7. Record (DESIGN sketch; the type is defined in `frob_core::energy` by N29b-21, which comes first, with these
fields; this bead fills them for contact rows).**

```rust
pub struct RowEnergy {
    feature: FeaturePairId, row: RowKind,            // Normal | Tangent1 | Tangent2 | Torsion | Rolling
    class: RowClass,                                  // ImpulseLevel | StateFunction
    stage: f64, work: f64, algorithmic: f64,          // S, W, X  (algorithmic = 0 for StateFunction)
    dissipation: Dissipation,                         // { sliding, normal_damping, restitution, other }
    stored_delta: f64, remainder: f64,
}
```

One record per active row per interval, produced by `frob-contact::energy::row_energies(step_result)`, consumed
by the ledger accumulators. X is computed by the step for every impulse-level row (N29b-21); this bead's value
must equal it, and `row_x_matches_step` asserts that on every row of the fixtures. On the referee the values are `f64`; accumulation over intervals is double-float in
the ledger.

**8. Layout.** `crates/frob-contact/src/energy/{row.rs, laws.rs}`; tests
`crates/frob-contact/tests/row_energy.rs`. Determinism: a pure function of the step's committed quantities
(class D2 with them). The record is part of the transaction's candidate state and is discarded on a retry.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Resting block (m = 1 kg, g = 8 m/s², frictionless support, impulse step): per interval the normal row has
      X = −½·m·(g·h)², that is −1/32 J at 32 Hz and −1/128 J at 64 Hz (−1 J/s and −0.5 J/s), W = 0, and every
      dissipation component exactly 0.0.
- [ ] Driven sliding block (the same block pushed by 4 N on μ = 0.5, sliding at a steady 1 m/s): D_f = 4·h J per
      interval at 32, 64 and 2,000 Hz (4 W within 0.1%), W_t = −D_f, remainder 0.
- [ ] Plastic impact (e = 0) at a normal approach speed of exactly 2 m/s, solved as a located event: D_e = 2 J
      within 10⁻⁹ J, booked in exactly one interval, with X = 0 on the event rows.
- [ ] Σᵢ Sᵢ equals T(v⁺) − T(v*) within 10⁻¹² relative on 10⁴ random contact configurations.
- [ ] A state-function row always reports `algorithmic = 0`; a compliant contact at static equilibrium reports
      D_n = 0 and ΔU = 0 exactly (binary-exact fixture).
- [ ] No dissipation component is ever computed from S or X (a source-level test: the functions in `laws.rs` do
      not take `stage` or `algorithmic` as inputs).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-contact` lists the classification, the formulas and the remainder rule.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/row_energy.sh` exits 0 on a correct build, with its three controls caught; its log passes the
      shared lint.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `resting_normal_row`: λ = m·g·h, u⁻ = 0, u* = −g·h, u⁺ = 0 → S = −½·m·g²·h², W = 0, X = S, D = 0. At
  h = 1/32: X = −0.03125 J; at h = 1/64: −0.0078125 J; at h = 1/2,000: −8·10⁻⁶ J.
- `driven_slide_rows`: tangential λ_t = −4·h, u_t⁻ = 1, u_t* = 1 + 4·h, u_t⁺ = 1 → S_t = −4·h − 8·h²,
  W_t = −4·h, X_t = −8·h², D_f = 4·h; the normal row as in the resting case.
- `plastic_event_row`: λ = 2 N·s, u⁻ = u* = −2, u⁺ = 0 → S = W = −2 J, X = 0, D_e = 2 J; m_eff = 1 kg.
- `restitution_half`: e = 0.5, v_n = 2 m/s, m = 1 kg → D_e = ½·(1 − 0.25)·4 = 1.5 J; λ = 3 N·s; W = 3·½·(−2 + 1)
  = −1.5 J.
- `sticking_contact_zero_friction_heat`.
- `compliant_equilibrium_exact`: k = 2¹⁷ N/m, d = 2⁻¹⁴ m, m = 1 kg, g = 8 m/s² (k·d = 8 N exactly) → D_n = 0,
  ΔU = 0, algorithmic = 0.
- `compliant_storage_not_dissipation`: compressing by d = 1 mm at k = 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J in ΔU.
- `decelerating_slide_remainder`: an unpowered block sliding from 1 m/s with μ·g = 4 m/s² at h = 1/32:
  u_t⁺ = 0.875, λ_t = −0.125, D_f = 0.109375 J, W_t = −0.1171875 J, remainder −0.0078125 J, reported to the
  residual line and not to D.
- `stage_sum_identity` (property, 10⁴ seeded configurations with 1 to 8 contacts).
- `conveyor_boundary_term`: a block carried without slip by a belt at 1 m/s: the row's term λ·w appears as
  boundary work and D_f = 0.
- `row_x_matches_step`: on the three fixtures and the random configurations, the record's X equals the step's
  Xᵢ (N29b-21) bit for bit.
- `state_function_rows_zero_exchange` (property, 10⁴ seeded compliant rows away from equilibrium, where
  u* ≠ u⁻): `algorithmic` is exactly 0 on every row. The equilibrium fixture alone cannot catch a state-function
  row given the impulse-level formula, because there u* = u⁻ and that formula also gives 0.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The plan's three known answers: resting block, algorithmic exchange 1 J/s at 32 Hz and 0.5 J/s at 64 Hz,
  constitutive dissipation 0, heat 0; the same block sliding at 1 m/s on μ = 0.5, 4 W of friction heat at both
  rates; a plastic drop arriving at 2 m/s, 2 J of impact dissipation, booked once at the located event.
- Derived here (hand-checkable from the formulas, not stated by the plan): the sliding fixture's tangential
  algorithmic exchange is −8·h J/s (−0.25 J/s at 32 Hz); restitution 0.5 gives 1.5 J.
- Negative controls: D_f computed as −S_t (it then reads 4·h + 8·h² and changes with the rate); restitution loss
  booked on a non-located contact every interval (the resting block then dissipates); a state-function row
  reporting a non-zero exchange.
- Metamorphic: halving h halves the resting block's exchange per second and leaves every D unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; the binary-exact fixtures are compared exactly
  across platforms.

## Considerations

- Classification is by law, not by tier: a tier that runs a compliant law has state-function rows; the same tier
  under the hard-contact batch law has impulse-level rows. The class is recorded per row.
- An alternative reading would give a compliant row a non-zero exchange at touchdown (the implicit correction to
  its start-of-interval force). It was not taken, because it would make the line depend on solver internals;
  under either reading the binding rule and the gate's fixtures are unchanged, since thermal states read D only.
- Friction heat uses the solved slip velocity, as the plan specifies; the mean-velocity form differs by the
  remainder, which is first order in h and is visible on its own line instead of hidden in the heat.
- The referee's impulses come through the airlock from the sibling library; the three row velocities come from
  our own transaction, so this bead needs nothing new upstream.
