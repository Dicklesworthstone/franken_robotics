## Background

GATE-CHRON-14 belongs to the record subsystem (crate `frob-chronicle`) and guards one rule: the energy that an
impulse-level contact step exchanges with the velocity predictor (the *algorithmic exchange*) is a ledger line of
its own, and only *constitutive dissipation*, computed from a law with a dissipation function, feeds a thermal or
a wear state. The motivating case: a 1 kg block resting on a frictionless support under g = 8 m/s², stepped by
forces, then contact, then drift, has ½·m·(g·h)² of kinetic energy pass through the predictor every step, 1 J/s at
32 Hz and 0.5 J/s at 64 Hz, with zero displacement and no heat. Booked as contact dissipation it would warm the
tyres of a parked robot at a rate set by the step size.

The gate, verbatim in substance (claim Known answer): **the three fixtures at 32, 64 and 2,000 Hz on `referee`
and `strict`: constitutive dissipation of the resting block is exactly 0; sliding heat is within 0.1% of 4 W;
impact loss is within 10⁻⁹ J of 2 J; a mutant that feeds algorithmic exchange into the film temperature is
caught, because its temperature rise differs between 32 and 64 Hz by a factor of 2. First milestone on the
referee; second milestone on the batch tiers.**

Known answers of the subsection: resting block, algorithmic exchange 1 J/s at 32 Hz and 0.5 J/s at 64 Hz,
constitutive dissipation 0, heat 0. The same block sliding at 1 m/s on μ = 0.5: 4 W of friction heat at both
rates. A plastic drop arriving at 2 m/s: 2 J of impact dissipation, booked once at the located event.

This bead is the end-to-end script and the first-milestone record (the referee rows). The mechanisms are
N29b-20 (contact rows), N29b-21 (predictor and joint rows) and N29b-22 (ledger line, binding rule, refusal). The
`strict` rows and the lockstep kernel are N29b-24.

## Technical approach

**1. Fixtures (registered in N24b-03's fixture registry as `chron14/<name>`, each with its manifest
`assets/fixtures/chron14/<name>/fixture.toml`, kind `synthetic`, so that `sim.run --fixture` runs them; every
parameter except the step 1/2,000 s is exactly
representable in binary floating point. The bit-exact assertion does not need it to be: on a frictionless
resting contact no dissipation component is active, so the resting block's constitutive dissipation is 0.0 at
every rate by construction, and the impact and sliding assertions have tolerances).** Gravity 8 m/s² (a fixture parameter); a 1 kg rigid block; a
stationary horizontal support; step h ∈ {1/32, 1/64, 1/2,000} s; duration 4 s (128, 256 and 8,000 steps).

| Fixture | Setup | Expected, at every rate unless a rate is named |
|---|---|---|
| `rest-block` | Frictionless support; the block starts at rest in contact | Constitutive dissipation exactly 0.0 J (every component, every interval). Algorithmic exchange, constraint side: −1 J/s at 32 Hz, −0.5 J/s at 64 Hz, −0.016 J/s at 2,000 Hz; predictor side equal and opposite; net 0. Heat 0 |
| `slide-block` | μ = 0.5 (friction force μ·m·g = 4 N); a constant horizontal push of 4 N; initial speed 1 m/s, so the block slides at a steady 1 m/s | Sliding friction heat 4 W within 0.1% (16 J over 4 s); the normal row's exchange as in `rest-block`; the tangential exchange −8·h J/s (−0.25 J/s at 32 Hz) |
| `plastic-drop` | The block starts with zero gap and a normal approach speed of exactly 2 m/s; restitution 0; frictionless | One located event at t = 0 with restitution loss ½·m·(1 − e²)·v_n² = 2 J within 10⁻⁹ J, booked in exactly one interval; algorithmic exchange 0 on the event; afterwards the block rests as in `rest-block` |

Why the impact fixture starts in contact: the gate's tolerance of 10⁻⁹ J is tighter than a first-order
integrator's error in the fall before the impact. "Arriving at 2 m/s" is therefore an initial condition, and the
impact is the first event. A companion row (reported, not part of the gate) drops the block from 0.25 m, where
v² = 2·g·H gives 2 m/s, and records the impact loss with the event-location error stated.

**2. The thermal test node.** At the first milestone no tyre or film model exists. Each fixture declares one
lumped thermal node `film` at the contact interface with heat capacity 1 J/K, no path to ambient, and bindings
`film ← contact sliding friction` and `film ← restitution loss`, both with gain 1. Its temperature rise in
kelvin therefore equals the constitutive heat in joules: 0 K for `rest-block`, 16 K for `slide-block`, 2 K for
`plastic-drop`, at every rate. The node is labelled `synthetic` in the envelope.

**3. The mutant (conformance build only; feature `mutant-chron-exchange-heat`).** This is the plant of the
chapter's appended catalogue entry `chron-exchange-heat` (N29-CX, label C1); its control row in
`controls.toml` is written by the seeding tool of N38b-05 from that entry, and this bead builds the feature and
runs it, never a second row under another name (an earlier draft called it `chron14-heat-from-exchange`). The
binding validator is bypassed and the `film` node is fed the magnitude of the constraint-side algorithmic
exchange.
Expected on `rest-block` over 4 s: +4 K at 32 Hz, +2 K at 64 Hz, +0.064 K at 2,000 Hz. The gate's detection rule
is rate invariance of heat: abs(ΔT(32 Hz) − ΔT(64 Hz)) ≤ 10⁻³·max(ΔT) + 10⁻⁹ K. The correct build gives 0 and 0;
the mutant gives 4 and 2, a factor of 2, and fails the rule.

**4. Steps.** For each fixture and rate: `frob sim run --tier referee --fixture chron14/<name> --step <h> --seconds
4 --seed 0 --telemetry T2 --key c14-<name>-<rate> --json` (the 4 s of item 1 are passed, since a run without a
length lasts the row's default 2 s and would halve every expected total; the level is written in upper case: `t2` is FROB-REF-802; each
run has its own key, and the determinism repeat of a run carries `c14-<name>-<rate>-r2`, since a repeated key
would return the stored receipt); then `frob telemetry energy <run> --json` (line totals, the algorithmic pair,
the residual) and `frob telemetry query <run> --stream energetics --json` (the per-row records at level T2, keyed
by feature pair and row kind, N29b-22 item 7; and the node's temperature). Step groups:
- `rows-*`: per-row S, W, X and D against the hand values of N29b-20;
- `predictor-*`: N29b-21's script `scripts/e2e/predictor_exchange.sh` as a stage, then, on this script's runs,
  the predictor side equals minus the constraint side; inconsistent intervals 0;
- `ledger-*`: the identity closes inside the referee's bound (10⁻⁹ relative) with both lines shown;
  `energy-created` (N29b-02's monitor) is clear on all three fixtures at every rate, so the algorithmic pair is
  not mistaken for created energy; the contact-level flags read `not-run` until M2;
- `heat-*`: node temperature rises 0, 16 and 2 K with the stated tolerances, and rate invariance holds across
  32, 64 and 2,000 Hz for all three fixtures;
- `binding-*`: N29b-22's script `scripts/e2e/energy_bindings.sh` as a stage: its resting block whose `film` node
  is bound to the algorithmic line is refused at admission (the refusal "non-physical energy bound to a physical
  state"), exit 3, before any allocation, and the correctly bound one is admitted;
- `mutant-*`: the conformance build's runs of `rest-block` at 32 and 64 Hz (keys `c14-mutant-32`, `c14-mutant-64`)
  must fail `heat-*`'s rate-invariance assertion; the script asserts that failure (a control that is not caught
  fails the gate);
- `receipt`: the gate's tier run (item 7).

**5. Pass rule (referee rows).** For each of the three rates: resting constitutive dissipation is bit-exactly
0.0; sliding heat is within 0.1% of 4 W; impact loss is within 10⁻⁹ J of 2 J and booked once; the mutant is
caught at 32 against 64 Hz with a measured ratio of 2 within 1%; the binding refusal fires.

**6. Logging.** The script sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/gate_chron_14/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line: `ts`, `script`, `bead`
("N29b-23"), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block
(claim Known answer, class Tested, tier referee, determinism class, identities, seed, `synthetic` for the node).
`metric` lines carry, with unit and threshold: `rest_constitutive_J` (= 0 exactly, compared as bits),
`alg_constraint_J_per_s` (−1, −0.5, −0.016 within 10⁻⁹), `slide_heat_W` (4 ± 0.004), `impact_loss_J` (2 ± 10⁻⁹),
`impact_booked_intervals` (= 1), `film_dT_K` per rate, `rate_invariance_K`, `mutant_ratio` (2 ± 0.02),
`ledger_residual_rel` (≤ 10⁻⁹). On failure: the first interval and row whose value differs, both values, the run
identifier and the exported `.frun` path. A final `summary` line: assertions passed and failed, controls caught
of controls run, and `gates: ["GATE-CHRON-14"]`. Fixtures are synthetic; nothing private can be logged.

**7. Gate record.** GATE-CHRON-14's row in `registries/gates.toml`, with its two parts (M1 on the referee, M2 on
the batch tiers) and claim kind `known-answer`, is written by N40-10 (the late gates folded into the register).
The script writes no gate record (one recorder per gate; a log line that runs the recorder fails the harness
lint `self-recording`). Its last step group, `receipt`, runs the gate's tier run through the shared helper,
`e2e_tier g1_known_answers GATE-CHRON-14 --mutations`, that is `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-CHRON-14`: the entries this bead registers against
the gate on the site `referee` (the three fixtures at the three rates, asserting the pass rule of item 5 in
process), and the gate's three registered controls, which the tier runner runs in the same run. The script
asserts the receipt's GATE-CHRON-14 line and logs the receipt's hash. This bead then records the M1 part from
that receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-CHRON-14 --receipt <hash>
--part M1` (the gate recorder, N38b-19): status `pass` only if every control above
was caught in the same run, `measured = "rest D = 0 exactly at 32/64/2000 Hz; slide <w> W; impact <j> J booked
once; mutant ratio <r>"`, tier `G1:referee`, the evidence (ledger sequence numbers and trace identifiers), the
claim identifier and closure manifest, recorded by a session that did not implement N29b-20 to N29b-22. The M2
part stays `not-run` until N29b-24 records it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 on a correct build and non-zero on any failed assertion or if the mutant is not caught.
- [ ] Referee, at 32, 64 and 2,000 Hz: `rest_constitutive_J` is 0.0 bit for bit; `slide_heat_W` within 0.1% of
      4 W; `impact_loss_J` within 10⁻⁹ J of 2 J, booked in one interval.
- [ ] The algorithmic pair reads ∓1 J/s at 32 Hz and ∓0.5 J/s at 64 Hz on `rest-block`, net 0.
- [ ] The `film` node rises 0, 16 and 2 K on the three fixtures at every rate; rate invariance holds.
- [ ] The mutant's rise on `rest-block` is 4 K at 32 Hz and 2 K at 64 Hz and is caught; the binding refusal fires
      on the mis-bound model.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-14's M1 part is recorded from the receipt of the tier run with `record_gate -- --gate
      GATE-CHRON-14 --receipt <hash> --part M1`, with measured values, commit, hardware fingerprint and seed, by
      a fresh-context session, with the three controls caught in the same run; the M2 part reads `not-run`. No
      line of the script's log runs the recorder.
- [ ] The script's three known-answer assertions use the truth, tolerance and control of N29-KA's rows K1 to K3
      (`rest-block`, `slide-block`, `plastic-drop`), which this bead carries for the referee site.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Fixture self-checks in `crates/frob-conformance/tests/chron14_fixtures.rs`: every parameter is exactly
representable (`m·g = 8`, `μ·m·g = 4`, `v_n = 2`, `h·32`, `h·64` integers); the expected values are recomputed
from the formulas (½·m·g²·h per second; μ·m·g·v; ½·m·v_n²) rather than typed in; `assert_rate_invariant` fails
on the pair (4, 2) and passes on (16, 16.008).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. `--tier` selects the tier (referee here; N29b-24 calls the same script with `strict`
and the lockstep tiers); `--list` prints the matrix of fixtures, rates and assertions; the first-milestone exit
review re-runs it from a clean checkout.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The three known answers of the subsection, as above, with tolerances: exact 0; 0.1%; 10⁻⁹ J.
- Derived values asserted by the script: −0.016 J/s at 2,000 Hz; the tangential exchange −8·h J/s; the node's
  16 K; the mutant's 4 K and 2 K.
- Negative controls of the gate: `mutant-chron-exchange-heat` (the plan's mutant; seeded from N29-CX's C1, not
  registered here); and two registered here as new `[[control]]` rows (`kind = "mutation"`, a `feature` each,
  `gate = ["GATE-CHRON-14"]`): `mutant-chron14-friction-from-stage-energy` (friction heat computed as the energy
  the solve removed: it reads 4 + 8·h W, 4.25 W at 32 Hz, and fails the 0.1% bound) and
  `mutant-chron14-impact-every-step` (restitution loss booked on a resting contact).
- Live-dependency proof: changing μ to 0.25 with the push at 2 N halves the sliding heat to 2 W.
- Metamorphic: doubling the block's mass doubles every energy in all three fixtures; the rate-invariance
  assertion is itself the relation "constitutive heat does not depend on h".
- Determinism lanes: each run twice on one machine with equal state digests; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The gate was added after the gate register's main table was assembled; the register lists it among the late
  gates with milestones M1 and M2, and says that until its tables are regenerated the row in the defining
  subsection governs the threshold. Its registry row is N40-10's (first milestone zero), so the status tables
  show it as `not run` from the start; this bead turns its M1 part into a measurement.
- "Film temperature" in the gate text refers to a state that exists from the third milestone; the test node is
  the smallest stand-in that exercises the same binding path, and the mutant is re-pointed at the real film node
  when it exists.
- The 2,000 Hz rows cost 8,000 steps each on one world; the whole script is seconds on the referee.
- If the referee's step structure changes (a variational integrator is a later, Frontier option on the referee),
  the resting block's exchange may be zero by construction; the constitutive rows and the mutant test are
  unchanged, and the pair's expected values are re-derived for that structure.
