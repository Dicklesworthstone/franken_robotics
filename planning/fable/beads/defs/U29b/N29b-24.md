## Background

The rule guarded by GATE-CHRON-14: the energy that an impulse-level contact step exchanges with the velocity
predictor (the *algorithmic exchange*; ½·m·(g·h)² per step for a block at rest, 1 J/s at 32 Hz and 0.5 J/s at
64 Hz for 1 kg under g = 8 m/s²) is a ledger line of its own, and only *constitutive dissipation* (computed from
a law with a dissipation function) feeds a thermal or a wear state. The gate's text: the three fixtures at 32, 64
and 2,000 Hz on `referee` and `strict`: constitutive dissipation of the resting block is exactly 0; sliding heat
is within 0.1% of 4 W; impact loss is within 10⁻⁹ J of 2 J; a mutant that feeds algorithmic exchange into the
film temperature is caught, because its temperature rise differs between 32 and 64 Hz by a factor of 2. First
milestone on the referee; **second milestone on the batch tiers**.

The referee rows are N29b-23. This bead is the second half. It has three parts, because "the batch tiers" covers
three different executions of the step:

1. **`strict`** (the `batch-cpu` tier under the transactional profile: `f64`, one world at a time per worker,
   events located, the compliant law by default with located events). The gate names it.
2. **`lockstep`** on `batch-cpu` (the ten-stage batched step, the reference for the GPU). The plan's mechanism
   says stage 9, the kernel `K-ledger-step`, accumulates the two quantities separately for each contact row and
   each joint-limit row, and that the energy view prints the two lines separately on every tier.
3. **`lockstep` on the device back ends**, where the same kernel must agree with its CPU reference.

On the compliant tiers the algorithmic exchange of a resting contact is zero, because the contact force is a
function of state; the line matters on the referee's impulse step and on the hard-contact batch law. So on these
tiers most of the work is making the *constitutive* quantities right in single precision and making sure nothing
else can reach a thermal state.

## Scope

In: the per-row outputs of `K-ledger-step` for the two quantities; the row classification on batched tiers; the
solve-stage energy as a diagnostic that is never a ledger line; the `strict` rows of the gate; lockstep rows of
the three fixtures on `batch-cpu` in `f64` and `f32` and on each admitted device; the mutant on batch tiers; the
gate's second-milestone record.

Out: the rest of `K-ledger-step` (the ledger's other terms, the created-energy accumulators: seed BD-208); the
per-row formulas (N29b-20, N29b-21); the line, binding rule and refusal (N29b-22); the script (N29b-23, reused).

## Technical approach

**1. What stage 9 accumulates per row (in addition to the ledger terms of BD-208).** For each contact row and
each joint-limit row slot of each world, in double-float:

```
alg[row]   += Xᵢ      impulse-level rows:  λᵢ·½·(uᵢ* − uᵢ⁻);   state-function rows: 0
diss[row][component] += Dᵢ      by law component: sliding friction ‖λ_t‖·‖u_t⁺‖; normal damping c·(u_n⁺)²·h;
                                restitution (event rows only); joint damping b·(q̇⁺)²·h; stiction; backlash
stored[row] (as a state, not an accumulator): U(d⁺) of the row's elastic store
```

and per world `alg_pred` (the predictor side) from the stage-6 forces and the stage-8 result. The row velocities
u⁻ and u⁺ are available in the step (stage 1 twists before and after the solve); u* is the free velocity that
stage 8's solve starts from. Fixed shapes: one slot per row capacity, masked for inactive rows, no data-dependent
control flow across worlds.

**2. Row classes on batched tiers.** Under the default compliant law every contact row, the compliant
joint-limit rows, and the stiction and backlash rows of the convex tiers are state-function rows: `alg` stays 0
and the energy view prints the pair as zeros with the note `no impulse-level rows`. Under the hard-contact batch
law (when admitted) contact rows are impulse-level and `alg` is live by the formula above. The class is a
per-row constant of the step plan, so the kernel's shape does not depend on it.

**3. The solve-stage energy is a diagnostic, not a line.** A batched solve that starts from a free velocity and
adds row impulses has, for every row, the stage energy Sᵢ = λᵢ·½·(uᵢ* + uᵢ⁺): for a block resting on a compliant
contact it is −½·m·(g·h)² per step, the same number as on the referee. This is the quantity a careless
implementation would call "contact dissipation". The kernel may export Sᵢ for debugging at trace level under the
channel name `solve_stage_energy` with the declaration `non_physical: true`; it is not a ledger line, is not in
any identity, and the binding validator of N29b-22 refuses it as a source for any state.

**4. `strict` rows of the gate.** The script of N29b-23 run with `--tier batch-cpu --profile strict`:
- `rest-block`: with the compliant law, the block is initialized at static equilibrium with binary-exact
  parameters (stiffness k = 2¹⁷ N/m, penetration d = 2⁻¹⁴ m, so k·d = m·g = 8 N exactly); the normal rate is
  exactly 0 in every interval; constitutive dissipation is bit-exactly 0.0; the pair is zero.
- `slide-block`: regularized Coulomb friction at 1 m/s (far above the stiction speed): heat within 0.1% of 4 W at
  the three rates.
- `plastic-drop`: the impact is a located event solved as a coupled impulse on the pre-event state, so the
  restitution loss is ½·m·(1 − e²)·v_n² = 2 J within 10⁻⁹ J, booked once on the restitution component. The
  sustained contact after it is compliant: the block then settles to the equilibrium penetration d = 2⁻¹⁴ m,
  releasing m·g·d ≈ 4.88·10⁻⁴ J of gravity work, of which ½·k·d² ≈ 2.44·10⁻⁴ J is stored and the same amount is
  dissipated as normal-damping heat. That settling heat is real, belongs to the normal-damping component, is
  asserted against 2.44·10⁻⁴ J within 1%, and is not part of the impact loss.
- mutant: on `strict` the same feature `mutant-chron-exchange-heat` (the plant of N29-CX's entry C1, whose
  seeded control already names GATE-CHRON-14; no second control row) binds the `film` node to the solve-stage
  energy of item 3 (the batch-tier form of "feeds algorithmic exchange into the film temperature"). Expected on `rest-block` over 4 s:
  4 K at 32 Hz and 2 K at 64 Hz, a factor of 2; the rate-invariance assertion catches it.

**5. Lockstep rows (reported with the gate; single precision is not held to the `f64` tolerances).** The same
three fixtures as batches of 64 identical worlds on `batch-cpu` (`f64` and `f32`), `gpu-cuda` and `gpu-metal`:
- resting constitutive dissipation: exactly 0.0 in both precisions (the equilibrium is binary-exact);
- sliding heat: within 0.1% of 4 W in `f64`; within 0.1% in `f32` as well (4 W over 4 s is 16 J against an `f32`
  resolution near 10⁻⁶ J per step, accumulated in double-float);
- impact: the lockstep step does not locate events; the fixture's impact is absorbed by the compliant law over
  several steps as normal-damping heat. The row reported is the total normal-damping heat of the impact against
  2 J plus the gravity work during compression, with the tolerance of the fidelity envelope, and it is labelled
  as a fidelity row, not as the gate's 10⁻⁹ J row;
- all 64 worlds of a batch report identical bits (a world does not depend on its batch).

**6. Device differential.** `K-ledger-step`'s per-row outputs on each device are compared with the CPU reference
kernel on the fixtures and on the standard battery's first 1,000 world-steps: bit-identical where the backend
attains the bitwise class for this kernel, otherwise inside the recorded tolerance envelope, and the first
divergence is kept in the divergence ledger.

**7. Record.** Through the gate recorder (N38b-19), this bead records the M2 part of GATE-CHRON-14 (the row and
its two parts are N40-10's; the M1 part is N29b-23's): `strict` (the gate's three assertions and the mutant
caught in the same run) as the part's measurement, and the lockstep rows per tier as reported measurements in
its `detail`. `pass` on the M2 part requires the `strict` rows and the mutant; the lockstep rows must be present
and inside their stated tolerances.

**8. INV-CHRON-14 on the batch tiers.** This bead turns on the batch-tier rows of the invariant module `inv_14`
(N29bx-01), which report `not exercised (due M2)` until it closes: the static property against the step plan's
wiring table of each batch tier, and the dynamic property (step-rate invariance of the film node) on `strict`
and on the lockstep tiers in both precisions.

**9. Layout.** Kernel outputs in `crates/frob-kernels/src/ledger_step.rs` (the file BD-208 owns; this bead adds
the row-quantity section and its CPU reference test); fixtures shared with N29b-23.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `ledger_step_rows_cpu_reference`: for 10³ seeded worlds with 1 to 16 active rows, the kernel's `diss` and `alg`
  equal the host formulas of N29b-20 and N29b-21 (exactly in `f64`; within 4 ulp of the double-float sum in
  `f32`).
- `state_function_rows_have_zero_alg` (property over random compliant contacts).
- `settling_heat_after_impact`: m·g·d = 8 × 2⁻¹⁴ = 4.8828·10⁻⁴ J; stored ½·2¹⁷·2⁻²⁸ = 2.4414·10⁻⁴ J; heat the
  difference.
- `resting_equilibrium_exact_f32_f64`: k = 2¹⁷, d = 2⁻¹⁴, m = 1, g = 8 → normal rate 0.0, D_n = 0.0.
- `slide_heat_f32_accumulation`: 8,000 steps at 2,000 Hz of 4·h J each accumulate to 16 J within 0.1% in
  double-float `f32` (and drift visibly with a plain `f32` accumulator, which is the planted control).
- `stage_energy_is_not_a_line`: the ledger identity has no term fed by `solve_stage_energy`; the validator
  refuses it as a binding source.
- `batch_independence`: a world's row quantities are identical alone and in a batch of 64.

### End-to-end script and logging

The script of N29b-23 with `--tier` and `--profile` arguments; this bead adds the step group `lockstep-*` and the
device loop (a missing device is logged `not-run` with the reason). NDJSON lines per the shared helper: command,
exit code, wall time, evidence block (tier, profile, precision, determinism class, seed), the gate metrics per
tier with units and thresholds, the differential's maximum deviation against its envelope, per-batch digest
equality; on failure the first world, row and step that differ and the chunk range to replay on `batch-cpu`.
A `summary` line names GATE-CHRON-14.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The gate's three assertions on `strict`; the known answers of the subsection (heat 0; 4 W at both rates; 2 J
  booked once).
- Negative controls: the batch-tier mutant; a plain single-precision accumulator for the row quantities (caught
  by the 2,000 Hz sliding row); an impact loss booked every step on the compliant law (the resting block then
  dissipates).
- Metamorphic: constitutive heat is invariant to the step (32, 64, 2,000 Hz) on every tier; the solve-stage
  energy is proportional to the step.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; `batch-cpu` against each device, each against
  its class; batch-composition invariance.

## Considerations

- The gate's 10⁻⁹ J impact tolerance is meaningful only where impacts are located events (referee, `strict`).
  Reporting the lockstep impact as a fidelity row, not as a gate row, is this bead's reading of "on `referee` and
  `strict`"; the unit's coverage notes record it.
- When the hard-contact batch law is admitted, its resting-block pair must reproduce the referee's values
  (∓1 J/s at 32 Hz); that row is added to the gate then.
- Per-row accumulators cost up to four double-float pairs per row slot (the exchange and three dissipation
  components); with 24 contact slots and 12 joint rows that is about 1.2 kB per world in `f32` pairs. Level-T0 keeps per-world totals; per-row detail is level T2.
- If KILL-CONTACT fires at this milestone and the alternative compliant law becomes default, the fixtures are
  re-pinned under it; nothing in this bead depends on which compliant law is default.
