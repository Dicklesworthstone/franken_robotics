## Background

Ground is the subsystem that owns calibration against reality: the one claim of the seven (Proof, Known answer,
Parity, Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality, Performance) that the first release mostly cannot make. Its crate is
`frob-ground`, layer 5, cluster `ground`. Two of its parts are Solid: the admission lane for real data and the
`reality:` line that every report prints. The rest (identification, card updates, reality bands, drift detection,
as-built ingestion) is Frontier. Nothing in the subsystem is on the critical path of a milestone before M5, and
no gate of the subsystem needs hardware. The crate must exist from M1 because the `reality:` line is due at M1.

What Ground owns: (1) the Reality claim: its statuses, its scope key (the cell), the report line, and the
refusals that stop a simulated number from being read as a measured one; (2) admission of real data: logs from a
robot or bench rig, single bench measurements and scans of built parts, each with licence, provenance,
measurement uncertainty and privacy mark; (3) a sim-to-real procedure in ten steps: clock fitting,
identifiability analysis and excitation design, identification in a fixed order, Bayesian card updates, frozen
predictions, sealed trials, holdout retirement, conformal bands per calibrated cell, drift monitoring, and the
feedback of discrepancy into later studies; (4) the replication kit and the synthetic rehearsal on which every
gate is decided. What it does not own: hardware (no driver, no bus, no real-time loop, no hardware-in-the-loop
mode; the project's non-goal on deployment stacks stands); scans of real places (the scene subsystem's lane).
Ground handles robots, parts and rigs.

**The ten decisions** (each with its reason; rejected alternatives in brackets).

| ADR | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-GROUND-01 | Ground is file-in, file-out. It never commands or reads a device. | Scope and safety: a simulator's gate cannot depend on hardware the project does not have. [Rejected: a live bench mode.] |
| ADR-GROUND-02 | The unit of calibration is the cell. A band belongs to exactly one cell. | Coverage holds for exchangeable draws only, and pooling hides a failing regime. [Rejected: one band per robot class.] |
| ADR-GROUND-03 | Identification order is fixed: clocks, actuators, contact and surface, inertia and geometry, sensors. | The literature places the gap in the actuator, and each stage's error aliases into the next. [Rejected: one joint fit of everything.] |
| ADR-GROUND-04 | Identifiability is analysed before any fit. A fit on an unidentifiable combination is refused, never regularized quietly. | A regularized fit returns the prior and labels it a measurement. |
| ADR-GROUND-05 | A prediction is frozen before its trial data is readable. A refit opens a new prediction version. A scored holdout is retired. | Otherwise the comparison is a fit, and its error rate is unknown. |
| ADR-GROUND-06 | A posterior is a new card version with provenance `measured`. Cards are never mutated. A new card version is a new `ModelId`, and evidence attached to the old identity does not carry over. | The immutability invariant of design data (INV-SKEL-01) and the model-identity rule of the decision cores. |
| ADR-GROUND-07 | Randomization width derived from a card narrows only after validation, never from a fit alone. | Shrinking a card without evidence overstates what is known. |
| ADR-GROUND-08 | Sibling estimators are reached through the airlock as whole jobs. Every Ground output has evidence class Estimated. | The siblings expose estimated candidates only and require an external authenticated authority for more. |
| ADR-GROUND-09 | Drift is tested on ranks and misses, not on raw distributions by kernel discrepancy or optimal transport. | Those tests have no power at about twenty scalar residuals. |
| ADR-GROUND-10 | Bench work is optional, is packaged separately and blocks no milestone. | Reality is an import lane. |

## Scope

In: the crate skeleton and module layout; the decision register as `docs/adr/ADR-GROUND-01.md` to
`docs/adr/ADR-GROUND-10.md` (the one decision directory, flat, as every subsystem's records are); the
dependency firewall (invariant INV-GROUND-07); the type-level rule that every Ground output is Estimated; the
crate's `CONTRACT.md`. Out: the status type and report line (N30a-02, BD-139); admission (BD-387); every
mechanism (its own bead).
The invariant battery INV-GROUND-01 to -07 as a set is N30b-23: it re-runs two of this bead's three firewall
mutants (the serial-port crate and the `std::net` use) and adds the source check for `extern "C"` blocks to the same deny-list and test file.

## Technical approach

1. **Layout** (DESIGN). `crates/frob-ground/src/`: `status.rs` (status transitions, N30a-02), `reality.rs` and `records.rs` (the status guard and the record
   tables it reads, BD-139), `cell.rs`
   (N30a-07), `dataset.rs` and `seal.rs` (BD-387), `instrument.rs` (N30a-08), `clock.rs` (N30a-11 to -13),
   `excite/` (BD-390, N30a-15 to -18), `identify/` (N30a-20 to -25), `card.rs` (N30a-27 to -30), `predict.rs`,
   `score.rs` (N30a-31 to -34), `band.rs` (N30a-36 to -38), `drift.rs` (N30a-40 to -43), `asbuilt/` (N30a-45 to
   -47), `procedure.rs` (N30a-09), `sibling/` (the airlock adapters, N30a-05, N30a-06), `kit.rs`. Dependencies
   allowed: `frob-core`, the model, actuator, sensing, dynamics and chronicle crates of lower layers, and the
   airlock crate `frob-fsim` with only the feature groups `frob-ground` is listed for.
2. **The firewall (INV-GROUND-07: Ground performs no device input or output).** `xtask check-deps` gains a rule
   for `frob-ground`: its resolved dependency graph (all features) contains no crate from a denylist of socket,
   serial, USB, CAN, EtherCAT, Bluetooth and HTTP-client crates (`registries/deny/ground-io.toml`, DESIGN:
   names such as `serialport`, `socketcan`, `rusb`, `tokio::net` via feature, `reqwest`, `hyper`), and the crate
   source contains no `std::net`, `std::os::unix::net` or `std::process::Command` use (a source lint in the same
   check). Rule identifiers in the `frob.xtask.report/1` report (DESIGN, in the form of BD-003's rules):
   `INV-GROUND-07.io-crate` (a denied crate in the resolved graph, naming it and the feature path that pulls
   it in) and `INV-GROUND-07.io-source` (a denied use in the source, naming file and line). File access goes
   through the chunk store and the artifact reader only.
3. **Estimated on every output (ADR-GROUND-08).** `frob-ground` exposes one constructor for results,
   `GroundResult<T>::estimated(value, inputs)`, which builds the claim product with class `Estimated` and takes
   the tier and determinism class as the weakest among its inputs. There is no constructor that takes a class. A
   compile-fail test pins that no `Tested`, `Certified` or `Proved` value can be built in this crate. Open
   question OPEN-GROUND-5 (who is the authenticated authority that could promote a sibling's estimated
   candidate; owner: the proof-subsystem lead) keeps its fail-closed default: nobody, and every Ground output
   stays Estimated.
4. **`CONTRACT.md`** with the ten sections every crate ships: purpose, inputs, outputs, invariants (INV-GROUND-01
   to -07, each with the bead that tests it), refusals (the eleven codes of the chapter: 812, 814, 873, 876, 879,
   883, 886, 889, 895, 897, 899), determinism class (admission and arithmetic D0; fits on the referee
   deterministic per platform; the ensemble lane without a cross-machine claim), evidence class of outputs
   (Estimated), validity domain (inside a calibrated cell only), oracles (the synthetic rehearsal), known gaps
   (no real data; the sibling caps).
5. **Tier labels.** The crate map marks the crate F. The two Solid parts (admission, the line) are labelled in
   the contract so that their gates are not read as Frontier pilots.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `crates/frob-ground` builds on the workspace toolchain with `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and passes
      `xtask check-layers` as a layer-5 crate.
- [ ] `xtask check-deps` passes on the crate and fails on a planted mutant that adds a serial-port crate behind
      a feature, on a mutant that opens a `std::net::TcpStream` in a test helper compiled into the crate, and on
      a mutant that spawns a process with `std::process::Command`.
- [ ] No public function of the crate returns a claim product with a class above `Estimated`; the compile-fail
      test exists and fails to compile as intended.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has ten sections and `xtask check-contracts` accepts it.
- [ ] The ten decision files exist, each with decision, reason, rejected alternative and the bead that
      enforces it.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/ground_firewall.sh` passes on the workspace and exits non-zero under each of its three
      planted mutants; its log carries the denied-crate count (0) from both the checker and the independent
      `cargo tree` count.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/charter.rs`: `outputs_are_estimated` (every public result type's class is
`Estimated` for inputs of class `Tested` and `Estimated`); `class_is_weakest_input_tier_and_determinism`
(inputs at D0 and D2 give D2); trybuild case `no_constructor_takes_a_class`. `xtask/tests/ground_firewall.rs`:
`clean_graph_passes`; `serial_crate_behind_feature_fails`; `std_net_use_fails`; `process_spawn_fails`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_firewall.sh [--controls]` (this bead; M1, so that the firewall is checked from the user's
side before any Ground mechanism exists). Steps: (1) `cargo xtask check-deps --json` on the workspace (the
xtask surface is `cargo xtask <check> [--root <dir>] [--json]`, BD-003; it has no per-crate option): assert
exit 0 and no `INV-GROUND-07.*` finding, and log the number of deny-list crates found in `frob-ground`'s
resolved graph with all features (expected 0) and the deny-list's digest; (2) `cargo tree -p frob-ground
--all-features -e normal,build` and the script's own grep of its output against
`registries/deny/ground-io.toml`, asserting the same count as step 1 (an independent check of the checker);
(3) `cargo xtask check-contracts --json`: assert the ten sections for `frob-ground`; (4) `cargo xtask
check-layers --json`: assert `frob-ground` is layer 5 with no upward edge; (5) with `--controls`, copy the
workspace to `target/e2e-work/ground_firewall/` and run `cargo xtask check-deps --root <copy> --json` on
three planted mutants of the crate (a `serialport` dependency behind a feature; a `std::net::TcpStream` in a
test helper compiled into the crate; a `std::process::Command` use): assert each fails with its rule
identifier (`INV-GROUND-07.io-crate`, then `INV-GROUND-07.io-source` twice).
Log `target/e2e-logs/ground_firewall/<utc>.ndjson` through the `STD-E2E` helper: per command a `cmd` line
(exact command, exit code, wall time), a `metric` line with the denied-crate count and its threshold (0), one
`assert` line per mutant with the rule that caught it, and a final `summary`. At M5,
`scripts/e2e/ground_ingest.sh` (bead N25a-29) repeats step 1 and also runs an ingest under a sandbox that
denies sockets.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls registered with `STD-TEST`: the three firewall mutants (the serial-port crate behind a
feature, the `std::net` use, the `std::process::Command` use); a mutant constructor that accepts a class. Invariant carried: INV-GROUND-07 (tested by the dependency firewall in `xtask`; no socket, serial or
bus crate in the graph). Determinism: the checks are static and exact.

## Considerations

- Risk: an optional bench package (a later, separately packaged import) tempts someone to add a logger driver
  to this crate. The firewall is the answer; a bench tool, if ever written, is a separate program outside the
  workspace's production graph whose only product is a file.
- The crate map gives `frob-ground` a scope cap of 10 thousand lines including tests; the mechanism list above
  is large for that cap, and reaching 115% of it triggers a scope conversation, not a silent overrun.
- Sibling estimators depend on a units crate whose nightly-toolchain coupling is a named program risk; Ground's
  jobs are whole jobs of seconds to minutes, so the worker-process fallback of the airlock costs them nothing.
- "Ground work beyond the rehearsal waits if no dataset has been admitted" is the program's stop rule for this
  lane at the M5 exit; the skeleton and the Solid parts are not affected by it.
