## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) maps each source clock of a real dataset to a reference clock by a fitted affine
(or piecewise-affine) map with a covariance (N30a-11). This bead governs how the fitted map may be used. Three
of the five clock rules of the calibration procedure live here; the other two (never fit the clock to the event
being scored; cross-correlation is not a clock fit) are N30a-13.

- **Rule 1. Scored instants lie inside the bracket, or the score is refused.** The uncertainty of an aligned
  instant is at most the mark jitter inside the interval spanned by the marks and grows quadratically outside
  it: an extrapolated instant looks precise and is not.
- **Rule 4. Raw timestamps are kept.** Aligned times are derived data carrying the fit's uncertainty. A test of
  whether a packet arrived before a decision uses the interval and answers `undecided` when it straddles.
- **Rule 5. Each instrument names which of the seven instants its timestamp denotes.** A timestamp with no
  declared instant is refused. The seven instants of the simulator's clock contract are: sample (the sensor's
  clock fires), exposure (the close of the integration window), timestamp (what the device writes, in its own
  clock), arrival (the packet becomes readable by its consumer), decision (a controller tick reads its inputs),
  command (the tick's output is issued) and actuation (the command takes effect). Conflating any two hides a
  class of failure: stale data, mis-stamped data, or a command applied before it could have been computed.

Rejected alternatives: replacing raw timestamps with aligned ones at ingest (the fit could then never be
revisited, and its uncertainty would be lost); a two-valued "before" test with a tolerance (the tolerance hides
exactly the cases where the answer is unknown).

## Scope

In: the aligned-instant type; bracket enforcement at scoring; three-valued ordering; derived durations with
uncertainty; the instant declaration and like-with-like comparison. Out: the fit (N30a-11); misuse detection
(N30a-13); the score itself (N30a-32).

## Technical approach

1. **Type** (DESIGN).
   ```rust
   pub struct AlignedInstant { raw: RawTime, clock: ClockRef, fit: ClockFitId,
                               t_ref: f64, sigma: f64, inside_bracket: bool, denotes: Instant }
   pub enum TimeOrder { Before, After, Undecided }
   ```
   `ClockFit::align(raw) -> AlignedInstant` computes t_ref and σ_t(raw) from the fit's covariance; it never
   fails, and sets `inside_bracket` by comparing the raw timestamp with the first and last mark of the fit.
2. **Bracket rule.** Any function that turns aligned instants into a scored quantity
   (`score::time_metric`, `score::latency`, `score::crossing_time`) takes `InBracket<AlignedInstant>`, a
   wrapper whose only constructor checks `inside_bracket`. A scored instant outside the bracket is refused
   with `FROB-REF-876` (clock alignment under-determined, or a scored instant outside the bracket; repair
   offered: the marks needed; exit code 3); the payload names the instant, the bracket, and the mark that
   would extend it (an earlier or a later one). Diagnostics and plots may use out-of-bracket instants and
   print them with their (large) σ.
3. **Intervals and ordering.** The interval of an instant is [t_ref − k·σ, t_ref + k·σ] with k = 3 (TARGET,
   recorded in the calibration artifact). Ordering is decided on the difference, not on the overlap of the two
   intervals, so that correlated errors are used: `order(a, b)` computes Δ = t_b − t_a and
   σ_Δ² = σ_a² + σ_b² − 2·cov(a, b); `Before` if Δ > k·σ_Δ, `After` if Δ < −k·σ_Δ, `Undecided` otherwise. For
   instants mapped by two independently fitted clocks cov(a, b) = 0, so σ_Δ = √(σ_a² + σ_b²); when a and b come
   from the same clock fit their errors are correlated and the difference's variance comes from the fit's
   covariance (var(t_a − t_b) = (x_a − x_b)²·var(ŝ) for the affine model), so two nearby samples of one clock
   are ordered exactly even when each has a wide interval. (DESIGN: an overlap test of the two ±k·σ intervals
   would be needlessly blind, at 6σ instead of 3·√2·σ, and could never order two samples of one clock.) A
   consumer that needs a yes-or-no answer (did this packet
   arrive before that decision?) must handle `Undecided` explicitly: the scoring code counts such trials in
   their own column and never assigns them to either side.
4. **Durations.** `duration(a, b) -> (Δ, σ_Δ)` with the same correlation handling. A registered tolerance on a
   time metric is derived from σ_Δ and the decision margin (N30a-31); a metric whose σ_Δ exceeds its
   tolerance is reported "not resolvable with these marks".
5. **Declared instants and like-with-like.** `Instrument.instant` is required in the manifest (a missing one is
   refused at admission with the missing-field refusal 873). When a real reading stamped at instant I is
   compared with simulation, the simulated side is the simulated packet's value **at the same instant I**:
   the simulator's sensor chain produces sample, exposure, timestamp and arrival instants for each packet, so
   a reading stamped at arrival is compared with the simulated arrival-stamped packet, not with the physical
   state at that time. Converting a real stamp from one instant to another (arrival to sample) needs a declared
   transport latency with its uncertainty; without it the conversion is refused, because the difference would
   otherwise be absorbed into an identified delay.
6. **Raw data.** Datasets keep source timestamps as recorded integers. Aligned instants are never written back
   into the dataset; they are recomputed from (raw, fit) and cached under the fit's identity, so that a new
   fit (more marks, a piecewise model) changes every derived time and nothing else.
7. **When there is no fit.** If the clock fit was refused, time-free metrics (success, energy, peak values)
   remain scorable and every time-based metric returns the 876 refusal: the step fails closed to "no
   time-based score".

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A scored instant 0.1 s past the last mark is refused with 876 and the payload names "a later mark"; the
      same instant is available to diagnostics with its σ.
- [ ] With marks 10 s apart and 50 µs jitter, a packet arrival and a decision instant on different clocks that
      differ by 60 µs are `Undecided`; differing by 1 ms they are ordered.
- [ ] Two samples of one clock 100 µs apart are ordered `Before` although each has σ = 50 µs (correlated errors:
      σ_Δ = 10⁻⁴ s × 7.07 ppm ≈ 7·10⁻¹⁰ s; treated as independent, k·σ_Δ would be 212 µs and the pair
      `Undecided`).
- [ ] An instrument with no declared instant is refused at admission; a reading stamped at arrival is compared
      with the simulated arrival-stamped value.
- [ ] After a refit with an added mark, no raw timestamp in the dataset has changed (chunk hashes equal).
- [ ] With a refused clock fit, a time-free metric scores and a crossing-time metric is refused.

`instant_required`; `like_with_like_arrival`; `conversion_without_latency_refused`;
`refit_leaves_raw_untouched`; `no_fit_time_free_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Part of `scripts/e2e/ground_clock.sh` (N30a-14): a scored crossing placed 0.5 s after the last mark is refused;
a packet-before-decision query on a straddling pair prints `undecided`. Logged: the instant, its σ, the
bracket, the order result and the refusal payload.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-721 requires "aligned instants cover the truth" in at least 99% of resamples: coverage is evaluated with the
interval defined here (k = 3). Negative controls: a build that scores out-of-bracket instants silently (the
script's extrapolation case must fail); a two-valued comparison that maps `Undecided` to `Before` (the
straddle test fails); a comparison that treats same-clock errors as independent (`same_clock_100us_ordered`
fails: 100 µs is inside 3·√2·50 µs = 212 µs, so it reads `Undecided`; at 1 ms apart both builds order the
pair, which is why that separation is not the control).
Determinism: per-platform double precision, identical twice.

## Considerations

- k = 3 is conservative for ordering questions and is a TARGET; the choice is recorded per calibration artifact
  so that changing it is visible.
- `Undecided` trials reduce the effective sample of a time-based study; the reachability pre-analysis of a
  registered prediction (N30a-33) should assume some will occur when marks are sparse.
- If the kill gate KILL-CLOCK fires, step 7 becomes the permanent state: time-free metrics only.
- The like-with-like rule depends on the simulator's sensor chain producing the same instant kinds as the real
  instrument; an instrument whose stamp point is unknown (start, middle or end of exposure) must say so, and
  the comparison then carries half the exposure as added uncertainty.
