## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) designs the excitation before it fits anything: step 2 of its procedure returns an
experiment plan chosen so that the parameters to be identified are in fact determined. Candidate experiments
come from a small grammar. Each has a duration, a safety envelope (limits on torque, speed, travel and
temperature) and a predicted Jacobian J = ∂(readings)/∂(parameters). A small closed grammar, not free-form
trajectories, for three reasons: each kind is a standard bench procedure a person can run and check; each has a
known reason to inform particular parameters, so the plan is explainable; and the plan must be shippable as
data in a replication kit.

| Experiment | Instruments | Informs |
|---|---|---|
| Slow torque ramp from rest, both directions | Current, encoder | Static friction, Stribeck speed |
| Velocity steps and coast-down | Encoder | Coulomb and viscous friction |
| Multisine torque, unloaded and with a known payload | Current, encoder | Armature, torque-constant scale |
| Step command with a shared mark | Command log, encoder | Delay, first-order lag |
| Stall and speed sweep at two bus voltages | Current, voltage, encoder | Torque–speed envelope |
| Thermal soak at fixed current | Winding and case temperature | Thermal resistances and capacitances |
| Incline to first slip; sled drag at two normal loads | Angle, force | Friction coefficient per material pair |
| Drop from two heights | Force or acceleration | Contact stiffness and dissipation |
| Scale, balance and bifilar pendulum | Mass, period | Mass, centre of mass, inertia |

Ground never commands a device (it is file-in, file-out). An experiment here is a description for a person or a
separately packaged bench tool, plus a simulation of itself. It authorizes nothing: every exported plan carries
the project's no-authorization flags (`execution_authorized`, `hardware_safety_signoff`,
`sim_to_real_certified`, all false, with no setter).

## Scope

In: the schema of an experiment; the nine kinds with parameters and defaults; compilation to a simulated twin
on the referee; feasibility on a rig; the envelope check; closed-form reading maps for the bench kinds. Out:
choosing among candidates (N30a-17); the Fisher analysis (BD-390); running anything on hardware (nobody).

## Technical approach

1. **Schema** `frob.ground.experiment/1` (DESIGN):
   ```toml
   [experiment]
   kind = "torque-ramp"            # one of the nine kinds below
   target = "joint:knee-L"         # a joint, a material pair, a part, or a contact pair
   duration_s = 90
   instruments = ["current-knee-L", "encoder-knee-L"]
   [experiment.params]             # per kind
   rate_Nm_s = 0.05; max_torque_Nm = 2.0; directions = ["+", "-"]; dwell_s = 1.0
   [experiment.safety]             # the envelope
   torque_Nm = 3.0; speed_rad_s = 2.0; travel_rad = [-0.5, 0.5]; temperature_C = 70
   ```
2. **The nine kinds and their parameters** (defaults are DESIGN and recorded in the plan output).
   - `torque-ramp`: ramp rate, peak, both directions, dwell at zero between ramps. Reading of interest: the
     torque at which motion starts (breakaway) and the speed–torque curve just after.
   - `velocity-steps`: a list of speeds of both signs held to steady state, then a coast-down with the drive
     off; steady torque gives b·q̇ + τ_c·sgn(q̇); the coast-down gives (I + J_a)·q̈ = −b·q̇ − τ_c·sgn(q̇).
   - `multisine`: a sum of sines over a band (default 0.5 to 20 Hz, 12 lines, Schroeder phases to limit crest
     factor), run unloaded and again with a payload of known inertia.
   - `step-with-mark`: a command step issued together with a hardware pulse visible on the command logger's and
     the encoder's clocks; informs delay and first-order lag.
   - `stall-and-sweep`: stall torque and a speed sweep at two bus voltages; informs the envelope
     i ≤ min(I_max, (V_lim − k_e·N·ω)/R).
   - `thermal-soak`: fixed current until thermal steady state, then cool-down; steady rise is
     P·(1/g_winding-housing + 1/g_housing-ambient) at the winding, and the two time constants give the
     capacitances.
   - `incline-and-drag`: tilt to first slip (μ_s = tan θ_slip) and drag a sled at two normal loads
     (F = μ_k·N + F₀: two loads separate μ_k from a load-independent offset).
   - `drop`: a body dropped from heights h and 2·h onto the surface, with a force or acceleration trace.
   - `mass-properties`: scale (mass), balance on two supports (centre of mass), bifilar pendulum (inertia
     about the vertical axis I = m·g·d²·T²/(16·π²·L), with filament separation d, length L, period T).
3. **Feasibility.** `grammar::candidates(design, cards, rig) -> Vec<Candidate>` instantiates each kind for each
   target that has parameters to identify. A candidate is dropped, with its reason kept in the report, when
   the rig lacks an instrument it needs (for example no voltage channel), lacks a shared mark
   (`step-with-mark`), or lacks a known payload.
4. **Simulated twin.** `grammar::compile(candidate) -> SimProgram`: a scene (a stand, an incline, a drop
   rig), a command program and the instrument list, runnable on the referee tier with the design's cards. It
   yields the predicted readings y(θ) at the instants the instruments declare, as a function of θ that BD-390
   differentiates (this bead's own tests use plain one-sided differences of the readings, so it does not wait
   on the analysis). The bench kinds with closed-form reading maps (incline, drag, pendulum, scale) are evaluated in
   closed form and cross-checked against their simulated twin.
5. **Envelope check.** The twin is run at the prior mean and at eight prior draws; the predicted peak torque,
   speed, travel and winding temperature are compared with `experiment.safety`. A candidate that exceeds its
   envelope at any of the nine points is excluded with the first exceeded limit and the margin. This is a
   screening of the plan against the stated limits under the model, not a safety sign-off, and the plan says so.
6. **Plan as data.** A selected plan is a list of experiment records plus the rig file's hash and the predicted
   bounds, written as an artifact (`frob.ground.plan/1`) with the three no-authorization flags.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the nine kinds instantiates on a single-joint stand rig and a cart rig fixture, compiles to a
      twin, and produces finite readings of the declared shape (instruments × instants).
- [ ] Each kind's readings change under a one-sided perturbation of 10⁻³·σ_i of every parameter its row of the
      table says it informs, and (for the designed smallest cases) do not change under static friction when the
      experiment never dwells.
- [ ] A rig without a voltage channel drops `stall-and-sweep` with the reason; one without a shared mark drops
      `step-with-mark`.
- [ ] A ramp whose predicted peak torque exceeds its envelope is excluded and the report names the limit.
- [ ] Closed-form and simulated readings agree for the incline (tan θ), the sled (two loads) and the bifilar
      pendulum to the referee's tolerance.
- [ ] Every plan artifact carries the three flags, all false, and a reader rejects a plan in which one is
      missing or true.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/grammar.rs`: `nine_kinds_instantiate`; `schema_roundtrip`;
`ramp_informs_breakaway` (non-zero ∂y/∂τ_s; zero for `velocity-steps` with |q̇| ≥ 5·v_s);
`steps_and_coastdown_inform_b_and_tau_c`; `multisine_with_payload_informs_kt_scale` (rank rises by one against
the unloaded run); `step_with_mark_requires_linked_clocks`; `incline_mu_is_tan_theta` (θ = 21.8° gives
μ_s = 0.400); `sled_two_loads_separate_offset` (F = 4.5 N at 10 N and 8.5 N at 20 N give μ_k = 0.40,
F₀ = 0.5 N); `bifilar_inertia_formula` (m = 2 kg, d = 0.2 m, L = 1 m, T = 1.5 s gives I = 0.01118 kg·m²);
`thermal_soak_steady_rise` (P = 20 W, g = 2 W/K and 1 W/K gives 30 K); `envelope_exclusion_names_limit`;
`missing_instrument_drops_candidate`; `plan_flags_all_false`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_excite.sh` (N30a-19) prints the candidate list for two rig files and asserts the dropped
candidates and reasons. Logged: per candidate the kind, target, duration, feasibility, envelope margins and
the Jacobian's column norms.

### Known answers and negative controls

The closed forms above. KA-723's separating run (3 A under 10 N) is an `incline-and-drag`-family candidate on
the cart rig. Negative controls: a compile that ignores the instrument's declared instant (the delay column
shifts; the step-with-mark test fails); an envelope check evaluated at the prior mean only (a planted draw
that exceeds the limit must still exclude the candidate). Determinism: twins run on the referee, bitwise twice
on one machine; closed forms exact or double precision.

## Considerations

- The bench work packages that would run these experiments (an actuator stand; a ramp and drag sled; an
  instrumented cart; one built link; a calibrated release; a revalidation) are optional imports outside the
  critical path; this grammar is exercised on the synthetic rehearsal whether or not any bench exists.
  Whether any bench hardware is acquired is open question OPEN-GROUND-2 (owner: project lead; default: none,
  and Reality stays `no-claim`; it blocks nothing; sourcing may start at M0).
- A free-form trajectory optimizer could beat the grammar on information per second; it is rejected for now
  because its output is hard to run by hand and hard to check, and because the plan's cautions favour a direct
  measurement of the decision quantity over a cleverer probe.
- Safety: the envelope check is model-based; a real rig's limits are the operator's responsibility, and the
  exported plan authorizes nothing.
