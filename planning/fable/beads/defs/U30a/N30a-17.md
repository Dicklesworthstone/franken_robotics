## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) proposes which bench experiments to run before any parameter is fitted. The
candidates come from a grammar of nine experiment kinds (N30a-16), each with a duration and a predicted
Jacobian of its readings with respect to the parameters. This bead chooses among them. Two rules govern the
choice.

**Information.** Selection maximizes log det(I + Σ_g J_gᵀ·J_g/ridge) greedily over whole experiments under a
time budget, using the sibling routine `fs-uq::experimental_design`. Where instruments share a calibration
error, the cost-budgeted plan of `fs-assimilate::groups::stream::design` is used instead, because it conditions
on the shared nuisance: repeating an experiment through one mis-zeroed encoder does not buy what the naive
objective says it buys.

**Decision relevance** (a caution that binds the design). An experiment is recommended for the decision it
could change, not for the parameter it would sharpen. Ranking uses value of information per unit cost. If
every admissible parameter value implies the same design decision, the recommendation is to measure nothing.
This is construction C-39 (the value of an experiment): for two finalists with posterior score difference
Δ ~ N(m, s²), an experiment that removes variance s̃² from Δ has expected value of sample information
EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃ (φ and Φ the standard normal density and distribution function), in
the units of the objective; where all mechanisms compatible with what is known imply one decision,
identification is skipped; and measuring the target directly at mission conditions is always an admitted probe.

Rejected alternatives: maximizing information alone (it sharpens parameters nobody's decision depends on);
choosing experiments one sample at a time (bench work comes in whole set-ups, so the unit is the experiment).

## Scope

In: the objective; the budgeted greedy loop and the choice between the two sibling routines; stopping; the
search for an experiment that separates each remaining alias; the value ranking, the direct-measurement
candidate and the "measure nothing" outcome. Out: candidates (N30a-16); the checks on a plan (BD-390); the
profile check and the model-class caution (N30a-18); the experiment table for real batches and the exchange
rate between operator time and compute (the decision-model bead).

## Technical approach

1. **Objective.** Jacobians are whitened by the instrument covariance and scaled by prior standard deviations
   (J̃_g, as in BD-390), so the prior is the identity and ridge = 1: Φ(S) = log det(I + Σ_{g∈S} J̃_gᵀ·J̃_g),
   twice the Gaussian information gain of the set S. Φ is monotone and submodular in S.
2. **Budgeted greedy** (Ground drives the loop; the sibling scores).
   1. Remaining budget B (seconds of bench time, from `--budget-s`); S empty.
   2. Among candidates whose duration fits B, ask the sibling for marginal gains ΔΦ_g = Φ(S ∪ {g}) − Φ(S);
      re-compute each gain locally (a Cholesky update) and require agreement to 10⁻⁹ relative.
   3. Pick the largest ΔΦ_g per second of duration; add it; subtract its duration.
   4. Stop when the budget is spent, when the best gain is below 10⁻³ nats (DESIGN), or when every requested
      parameter passes the precision check and no alias remains among them (DESIGN: do not spend bench time
      past the point of identification unless a decision asks for more).
   5. Run the plain largest-gain greedy as well, and keep whichever final plan has the larger Φ (the standard
      safeguard for budgeted submodular selection; the constant-factor guarantee is to be re-read from its
      source before it is quoted in the contract).
   A replicate of an experiment is a separate candidate (same J̃, same duration).
   The identification test of step 4 and the alias test of the separator search below are passed in by the
   caller as functions of the plan's information matrix F = Σ_{g∈S} J̃_gᵀ·J̃_g (`ground.excite` passes BD-390's
   checks, so the selector and the report can never disagree). DESIGN: this keeps the selector finishable
   before the verb that depends on it; its own tests pass the two threshold rules written out here: parameter i
   is identified when √((F⁻¹)_ii) ≤ 0.5 (scaled units, half the prior standard deviation), and a pair is
   aliased when |ρ_ij| ≥ 0.95 with ρ_ij = (F⁻¹)_ij/√((F⁻¹)_ii·(F⁻¹)_jj), on the pseudo-inverse over the
   identified subspace when F is singular.
3. **Shared calibration.** If the noise model (N30a-08) has a common-mode group whose members span more than
   one candidate experiment, the loop calls `groups::stream::design` (outcome-free probe scoring, greedy,
   cost-budgeted) in place of `experimental_design`, with the groups declared; the local re-computation then
   uses the dense R with the group term. The report states which routine was used and why.
4. **Separating experiments.** For each alias still present under the final plan, try each grammar candidate
   not in the plan, including ones excluded by budget or by the rig, as a single addition; report the
   cheapest that removes the alias, marked `in budget`, `over budget by <s>` or `needs <instrument>`; if none
   in the grammar removes it, say so and name the alias table's separator (N30a-15).
5. **Value ranking** (when `--decision` is given). Input: either a partition of parameter space by the design
   choice each value implies, or two finalists with m, s and the sensitivity g = ∂Δ/∂θ of the score difference
   to the parameters. For an experiment with Fisher information F (scaled units, prior Σ = I):
   Σ_post = (I + F)⁻¹; variance removed from Δ: s̃² = gᵀ·(I − Σ_post)·g; value = EVSI(m, s̃); rank by value
   per second. The candidate list always contains `direct-measurement`: measure the decision quantity itself
   at mission conditions (its s̃² is the whole parameter-borne variance of Δ plus the model-form part an
   identification cannot remove).
6. **Outcomes.**
   - `measure-nothing`: every parameter value in the prior region falls in one block of the partition (or
     EVSI of every candidate is below the stop price). Exit 0; the report says the decision does not depend on
     the parameters asked about.
   - `direct-measurement`: recommended whenever its cost is within a factor of two (DESIGN reading of "about
     what probing a parameter costs") of the best parameter plan with comparable value.
   - a ranked plan otherwise. With no `--decision`, the D-optimal plan is returned with the note "ranked by
     information, not by value".
7. **Costs.** Bench seconds only. The exchange rate between operator time and GPU-seconds is an open question
   (OPEN-GROUND-4, owner: project lead; default: real episodes are recommended, never scheduled, and no rate is
   assumed), so bench experiments are never ranked against compute here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the toy of three one-row experiments with scaled Jacobians (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), unit durations and
      budget 2, the plan contains both directions and Φ = 2·ln 2.
- [ ] With a common-mode group spanning two replicates, the gain of the second replicate is strictly smaller
      than under the independent objective, and the routine switch is reported.
- [ ] On the single-joint stand rig with a 30-minute budget, the plan identifies every actuator parameter of
      the stage (precision check) and stops before the budget when it does.
- [ ] For each of the eight structural aliases left in a deliberately poor plan, the report names a separating
      experiment or states that the grammar has none.
- [ ] With a partition in which both friction values imply the same wheel, the outcome is `measure-nothing`.
- [ ] EVSI reproduces the worked values: m = 0.03, s̃ = 0.096 gives 0.0251; s̃ = 0.05 gives 0.0084.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/selection.rs`: `toy_plan_is_orthogonal_pair`; `objective_monotone_submodular`
(property over random Jacobians: gains are non-negative and shrink as S grows);
`local_gain_matches_sibling`; `budget_never_exceeded`; `per_second_and_plain_greedy_best_is_kept`;
`stops_at_identification`; `stops_at_small_gain`; `shared_calibration_switches_routine`;
`replicate_gain_shrinks_under_common_mode`; `separator_search_reports_cheapest_or_none`;
`evsi_values` (0.0251 and 0.0084 to 10⁻⁴; zero when s̃ = 0; increasing in s̃; decreasing in |m|);
`variance_removed_formula` (one parameter, g = 1, F = 3: s̃² = 1 − 1/4 = 0.75);
`measure_nothing_when_partition_is_single_block`; `direct_measurement_always_listed`;
`no_decision_note_present`; `bench_cost_not_ranked_against_compute`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_excite.sh` (N30a-19): the plan for the stand rig at budgets of 5, 30 and 120 minutes, the
decision-relevance cases and the shared-calibration case. Logged: the picks in order with duration, ΔΦ, Φ and
which routine scored them; the stop reason; EVSI per candidate with m, s̃ and cost; the outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-187 (the value is 0 when s̃ = 0, increases in s̃ and decreases in |m|; a campaign in which no experiment's
ratio clears the price stops and decides). The toy plan above. Negative controls: a selector that ignores
durations (exceeds the budget on a fixture with one long experiment); a selector that treats replicates as
independent under a shared bias (the common-mode test fails); a ranker that recommends the most informative
experiment when the partition has one block. Determinism: ties are broken by experiment identifier, so a plan
is a pure function of its inputs; twice on one machine bit for bit.

## Considerations

- The value model only ranks experiments; it never issues a verdict. A wrong variance model costs efficiency
  and never validity.
- Greedy D-optimality is not optimal; the report prints Φ of the chosen plan and an upper bound (Φ of all
  candidates together), so the reader sees how much was left.
- The planner chooses which experiments and how many, never the conditions of a registered real trial: those
  come from a pre-registered draw (the interference test of the decision model).
- Kill gate KILL-VOI (owned by the studio's experiment-suggestion work, BD-351, which this bead does not call)
  falls back to a fixed order of experiments; here the fallback is the D-optimal plan with no value ranking.
  The EVSI of step 5 is this bead's own evaluation of C-39's closed form (SPEC-C-39), checked by `evsi_values`.
