## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) analyses identifiability before any fit. Three of its four checks are local: they
read a Jacobian at the prior mean and at eight prior draws (rank, the Cramér–Rao precision bound, correlation in
the inverse Fisher matrix). A local bound can look good where the likelihood is in fact flat: across a regime
switch (a force that saturates), for a parameter that acts only through a threshold (breakaway), or for a
delay, whose effect is not smooth. The fourth check is therefore global, on the parameter's own axis: **a
profile likelihood on the synthetic twin of the experiment, for finalists; a flat profile overrides a good
local bound.**

The second subject of this bead is the first of the two cautions that bind excitation design, the **model
class**. A probe that is perfect inside its assumed law class can be wrong outside it. In one executed study a
probe built for three material laws classified 675 of 675 cases inside the class and 0 of 225 under an omitted
delayed law, while measuring the target quantity directly was right in 900 of 900 (FACT, a planning pilot, to
be re-run). Consequences adopted (DESIGN): where measuring the decision quantity directly costs about what
probing a parameter costs, Ground recommends the direct measurement; every report says which law class it is
conditional on; and the rehearsal always contains an omitted-law control.

## Scope

In: the profile computation, its flatness rule and its override of the local checks; the law-class statement;
the omitted-law control at plan time. Out: the local checks (BD-390); selection and the direct-measurement
recommendation's ranking (N30a-17); the system-level omitted-law known answer, where a candidate fitted in the
wrong class is rejected at validation on sealed data (N30a-26, N30a-32).

## Technical approach

1. **Synthetic twin.** For a finalist plan P (the top plan, or the top three when their objectives are within
   5%, DESIGN), simulate every experiment of P on the referee at the prior mean θ*, giving noise-free readings
   y*. The twin is labelled `synthetic` and never leaves the analysis.
2. **Profile.** For each requested parameter θ_i and each grid value v on 13 points spanning θ*_i ± 3·σ_i
   (DESIGN; a delay is profiled on its own grid of rational fractions of the control period):
   PL_i(v) = min over the other parameters of χ²(θ) with θ_i = v, where
   χ²(θ) = (y(θ) − y*)ᵀ·R⁻¹·(y(θ) − y*) and R is the instrument covariance with its common-mode groups. The
   inner minimization is a Gauss–Newton iteration on the referee through the sibling's iterated update
   (N30a-05), warm-started from the neighbouring grid value, at most 20 iterations per point.
3. **Flatness rule.** The 95% likelihood-ratio set is L_i = {v : PL_i(v) − min PL_i ≤ 3.84} (the 95% point of
   χ² with one degree of freedom; TARGET level). The profile is **flat** if L_i reaches either end of the grid
   (unbounded within ±3·σ_i on that side), or if its half-width exceeds 1.96·0.5·σ_i = 0.98·σ_i. That is the
   half-prior criterion of the precision check expressed at the 95% level (DESIGN): the precision check
   bounds a one-standard-deviation quantity, CRB_i ≤ 0.5·σ_i, while L_i is a 95% set, whose half-width for a
   model linear in θ is √3.84·CRB_i = 1.96·CRB_i. Comparing the 95% half-width with 0.5·σ_i directly would
   overturn every linear parameter whose bound lies between 0.26·σ_i and 0.5·σ_i, which the precision check
   has just passed. A flat profile sets the parameter to `not-identified (profile)` whatever the local
   bound said, names the side on which it is unbounded, and is treated by the fit guard as unidentified.
   For a model linear in θ the profile is the parabola (v − θ*_i)²/CRB_i² and the two checks agree exactly;
   the report prints both half-widths so that a disagreement is visible.
4. **Cost control.** Profiles run only for finalists and only for the requested parameters; each is p·13 inner
   solves. `ground.excite` is a synchronous `read` verb with no job handle (BD-390, after the registry row),
   so these two restrictions are what bound its cost; the contract records the measured wall time of the
   profiles on the alias and stand rigs, and the fallback below applies if whole-robot plans prove too slow.
5. **Law-class statement.** Every report carries `conditional_on`: the list of laws assumed for the parameters
   in question (for an actuator stage: friction law "Coulomb + viscous + Stribeck with stiction row",
   first-order delay and lag, actuator level L1; for contact: the contact law in force), with their identities
   from the model, and, where the fit is restricted to a class of the registry of step 6, that class by its
   key. The report's first sentence about any plan is "identifiable within this law class".
6. **Omitted-law control at plan time.** A registry `registries/ground/omitted_laws.toml` (DESIGN) keys its law
   classes by name, one `[class.<name>]` table each; the names are the values `ground.identify --law-class`
   takes (VERBS-ground), so the option and the `conditional_on` statement share one spelling. The
   Coulomb-plus-viscous class (Coulomb plus viscous friction, τ_s = τ_c, no Stribeck term) is
   `coulomb-viscous`. Per class it lists the variants the class lacks: a Stribeck term for
   `coulomb-viscous`; a dwell-time (delayed) dependence of static friction; a load-dependent friction
   coefficient; backlash; a
   temperature-dependent resistance. For each variant: simulate the plan with the variant as truth at a
   declared magnitude; fit the best in-class parameters; compute the residual statistic
   χ²_res against its degrees of freedom. The variant is `detectable` if χ²_res exceeds the 99% quantile, else
   `silent`: this plan could not tell that the law is missing. The report lists silent variants, and when a
   silent variant would change the pending decision (when one was supplied), the recommendation becomes the
   direct measurement of the decision quantity.
7. **What the control does not do.** It tests the listed variants only. The report says so: a law nobody
   listed defeats any probe, which is why sealed validation exists.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Regime-edge fixture: drawbar force F = min(k_t·I/r, μ·N) with k_t = 0.2 N·m/A, r = 0.06 m, one run at 3 A
      under 14 N, prior μ = 0.6 ± 0.15. Locally ∂F/∂μ = 14 N at the mean (F = 8.4 N). With force noise
      σ = 1 N the profile is flat above μ = 0.714 (χ² there is (10 − 8.4)² = 2.56 < 3.84): the parameter is
      `not-identified (profile)`, unbounded above. With σ = 0.5 N (χ² = 10.24) the set is bounded.
- [ ] For a linear fixture the profile half-width equals 1.96·CRB within 2%, and the profile and precision
      checks give the same verdict on either side of CRB = 0.5·σ_i.
- [ ] A delay profiled on its grid returns the set of grid delays inside the bound, never a derivative-based
      bound.
- [ ] Omitted-law control: on the velocity-steps plan with a Coulomb-plus-viscous class, a Stribeck truth is
      `silent` when no step dwells below 5·v_s and `detectable` when a slow torque ramp is added.
- [ ] Every report prints its `conditional_on` list, and a report with an empty list is refused by the schema.
- [ ] `registries/ground/omitted_laws.toml` keys its classes by name and holds `[class.coulomb-viscous]`; a
      report on a plan restricted to that class names `coulomb-viscous` in `conditional_on`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/profile.rs`: `linear_profile_is_parabola`; `lr_threshold_is_3p84`;
`linear_checks_agree_at_the_boundary` (one linear parameter: with CRB = 0.49·σ_i both checks pass, the profile
half-width being 0.96·σ_i; with CRB = 0.51·σ_i both fail; with CRB = 0.40·σ_i the profile is not flat, which a
rule comparing the 95% half-width with 0.5·σ_i would get wrong);
`regime_edge_flat_at_sigma_1` and `regime_edge_bounded_at_sigma_0p5` (the fixture above; the switch is at
μ = 10/14); `flat_profile_overrides_local_bound` (the local precision check passes, the final status is
not-identified); `unbounded_side_is_named`; `delay_profile_is_a_grid_set`;
`warm_start_matches_cold_start` (same minima to 10⁻⁸); `stribeck_silent_without_dwell`;
`stribeck_detectable_with_ramp`; `conditional_on_required`; `silent_variant_triggers_direct_measurement`
(with a decision partition that the variant crosses).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_excite.sh` (N30a-19) runs the regime-edge rig and the stand rig with and without the ramp.
Logged per parameter: the local half-width, the profile half-width, the likelihood-ratio set's ends, the
flatness verdict; per omitted-law variant: χ²_res, its degrees of freedom, `detectable` or `silent`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-726 (omitted law: the truth has a Stribeck term the fit class lacks; the candidate is rejected at
validation; control: the same fit scored on its own calibration data passes) is the system-level case this
check anticipates. KA-188 and counterexamples CX-092 and CX-093 (two friction laws μ₁(v) = 0.5 and
μ₂(v) = 0.3 + 0.4·v agree at 0.5 m/s and imply opposite designs at 1.5 m/s; a probe at 1.0 m/s reads 0.5 or 0.7
and separates them exactly when the reading error is below 0.1; a third law that follows μ₁ up to 1.0 m/s and
departs later defeats that probe, so a certificate is conditional on the admitted set). CX-331 (score a fit on
its own calibration data). Negative controls: a profile that holds the other parameters fixed (a conditional
slice, not a profile: it is too narrow on a correlated pair and the test of an aliased pair must catch it); a
flatness rule that looks only at the half-width and misses the unbounded side. Determinism: referee, bitwise
twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- A noise-free twin gives the expected profile; a noisy twin would give one realization. The noise-free form
  is used so that the result is a property of the plan, and R carries the noise.
- The grid of 13 points and the ±3·σ range are DESIGN; a parameter with a hard physical bound (a friction
  coefficient cannot be negative) is profiled on the bounded range.
- The planning pilot behind the 675, 225 and 900 counts is marked "to be re-run"; the rule adopted does not
  depend on the exact counts.
- If the profile cost proves too high on whole-robot plans, the fallback is to profile only parameters whose
  local bound is within a factor of two of the threshold or whose model term contains a switch.
