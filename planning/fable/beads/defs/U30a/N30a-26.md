## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) identifies actuator parameters from logs in a fixed order and in three passes
(a linear regressor fit; an output-error fit on the referee through the sibling's iterated Gauss–Newton Kalman
update; a delay profile and an ensemble estimate of static friction and Stribeck speed), and writes the
posterior as candidate card versions. Whether this works is decided without hardware, on the synthetic
rehearsal: a truth simulator with planted parameters emits logs through the sensor models, labelled
`synthetic`.

**GATE-GROUND-04** (claim kind: known answer; milestone M5; TARGET), three parts: (a) planted actuator
parameters within 10% on at least 95 of 100 synthetic rigs; (b) identified cards cut held-out trajectory error
by at least 30% against prior cards; (c) the omitted-law candidate is rejected in at least 90 of 100.

**KILL-GROUND** (decided at M5): if this gate fails after one repair cycle, there is no identification. Ground
keeps admission, clocks, frozen predictions and bands on direct measurements with prior cards, and a card
field changes only by direct bench measurement of that field.

## Scope

In: the script, the rig population, the three measurements, the associated known answers and controls, the log
and the gate record. Out: the mechanisms; the kill-gate decision.

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/ground_identify.sh [--seed S] [--rigs 100] [--controls] [--quick]`, plain shell over
`frob`, `frobd` and the rehearsal generator, under `target/e2e-work/ground_identify/`; default seed 4.
`--quick` runs 10 rigs for development and never records the gate.

1. **Population A (recovery and error cut).** 100 single-joint stand rigs. Per rig, planted
   θ = (J_a, b, τ_c, τ_s, v_s, d) drawn by address from ranges spanning the generic actuator classes
   (each parameter between 0.5 and 2 times its class value; delay between 2 and 12 ms on the grid), a prior
   card whose fields are the class values with their stated uncertainty, clocks with planted skew and two
   shared marks, encoder and current channels with the rehearsal's noise and one shared encoder bias. Data:
   the excitation plan returned by `frob ground excite --budget-s 1800` for that rig, simulated by the truth
   simulator; split by day into calibration, validation and holdout; validation and holdout sealed.
2. **Per rig:** `frob ground ingest`; `frob ground excite`; `frob ground identify --stage actuators`; then for
   part (b) two predictions of the validation trajectory (a command sequence not in the calibration split),
   one made with the prior card and one with the candidate card, both frozen with `frob ground predict`
   before `frob ground score` opens the validation part.
3. **Part (a).** A rig passes when each of J_a, b, τ_c, τ_s and v_s is within 10% of its planted value and the
   delay is within one grid step. Assert passes ≥ 95 of 100. Also log, per parameter, the fraction within 10%
   and the fraction of errors inside 3 posterior standard deviations (expected near 0.997; below 0.95 is a
   finding that the posterior is too narrow).
4. **Part (b).** Per rig, e_prior and e_cand: the root-mean-square encoder error over the validation
   trajectory, as scored. The gate statistic is 1 − (Σ e_cand)/(Σ e_prior) over the 100 rigs; assert ≥ 0.30.
   Logged as well: the median per-rig reduction and the count of rigs where the candidate was worse.
5. **Population B (omitted law).** 100 rigs whose truth has a Stribeck term while the fit class is Coulomb plus
   viscous only (the class is selected by `--law-class coulomb-viscous`). Per rig: identify, freeze a
   prediction for a validation trajectory that dwells at low speed, score. The candidate is *rejected* when
   the registered interval does not cover. Assert rejections ≥ 90 of 100. Control built into the gate's known
   answer: the same fit scored on its own calibration data (through the conformance binary, never through the
   verb, which cannot read calibration data as if it were sealed) passes, which shows why sealing matters.
6. **Further known answers asserted once each.** Unidirectional data: a request for τ_c is refused with
   `FROB-REF-879`. Wrong reason: a candidate forced to a compensating pair (torque constant and friction) is
   labelled `wrong-reason` and cannot be admitted. Shared bias: doubling the trace does not push the bias-torque
   uncertainty below the planted floor. Stage order: `--stage contact` before `actuators` is refused. Card
   immutability: the prior card's content address is unchanged after `identify`, and each candidate is a new
   version with provenance `measured`, the dataset hash and the cell.
7. **Controls (`--controls`).** Builds on which the script must exit non-zero: an estimator with a diagonal
   noise model; a delay fitted by finite differences; a score path that reads calibration data in place of
   the sealed part (part (c) then accepts the omitted-law candidates); an `identify` that mutates the prior
   card in place.
8. **Determinism.** Ten rigs of population A twice with one seed: identical posteriors bit for bit on one
   machine; the airlock in process and through the worker gives equal digests; the ensemble part's machine
   fingerprint is logged and no cross-machine bit comparison is made.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Part (a): at least 95 of 100 rigs recover all six parameters within tolerance (TARGET), measured value
      recorded.
- [ ] Part (b): pooled held-out trajectory error reduced by at least 30% against prior cards (TARGET).
- [ ] Part (c): the omitted-law candidate rejected on at least 90 of 100 rigs (TARGET); the
      calibration-data control passes, as it should.
- [ ] The five single assertions of step 6 hold.
- [ ] Each control build makes the script exit non-zero.
- [ ] The gate record `GATE-GROUND-04` holds the three measured values, the seed, the commit, the machine and
      the airlock mode, and is the record the KILL-GROUND decision reads. A failing value is recorded as such.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own. The population generator has `rig_population_reproducible_by_seed` and
`planted_ranges_cover_classes`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. Log `target/e2e-logs/ground_identify/<utc>.ndjson` through the helper of `STD-E2E`,
with a transcript. Per rig one `metric` line: rig index, each parameter's planted value, posterior mean,
standard deviation and relative error; delay and its 95% set; iterations and evaluations used; e_prior and
e_cand; pass or fail. Per command template a `cmd` line with exit codes, wall time and the evidence block
(class `Estimated`, tier referee, determinism, dataset, cell and model identities, seed; `reality: synthetic`).
`metric` lines for the three gate statistics with thresholds 95, 0.30 and 90. `refusal` lines for step 6. On
failure: the first failing rig, its calibration record's identity and the paths of its dataset and record.
Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed, the gate touched and its three values.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-724 (actuator recovery within 10%, delay within one grid step; control: unidirectional data, τ_c refused);
KA-726 (omitted law rejected at validation; control: the same fit on its own calibration data passes); KA-727
(wrong reason). Counterexamples CX-331 (score a fit on its own calibration data) and CX-332 (matching the
observed speed validates the model). Negative controls: the four builds of step 7. Metamorphic: relabelling
rigs permutes the per-rig lines and leaves the three statistics unchanged. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; debug and release on ten rigs; both development machines inside the recorded tolerance.

## Considerations

- Cost: 200 rigs, each a plan, three passes and two predictions: hours of CPU. The script fans rigs out as
  daemon jobs and is resumable (each rig's result is keyed by seed and index).
- "One repair cycle": if the first full run fails, one round of fixes is allowed and the second run decides
  the kill gate. Both runs are recorded.
- The 10% tolerance on v_s is the hardest row: the Stribeck speed is weakly determined when ramps are fast.
  The excitation plan is part of what is being tested, so the script uses the plan `ground.excite` returns,
  not a hand-made one.
- Everything is `synthetic`. Passing shows the loop is sound on the model class it was given and nothing
  about any real actuator.
