## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) writes the posterior of an identification as a new card version. A fitted
posterior is narrower than the prior, and training randomizes each card field over its stated uncertainty, so
a careless pipeline would narrow randomization the moment a fit finishes. That is exactly the failure this
bead prevents. Decision ADR-GROUND-07: randomization width derived from a card narrows only after validation,
never from a fit alone, because shrinking a card without evidence overstates what is known. A fit can be
confidently wrong: with a law missing from the fit class, the posterior is tight around values that reproduce
the calibration data and mispredict the next trial (the rehearsal's omitted-law case: such a candidate passes
on its own calibration data and is rejected on sealed data).

The rule (plan text): `ground.identify` writes a `candidate`. It becomes `admitted` only when `ground.score`
has scored a frozen prediction made with it on sealed data and the registered interval covered. Randomization
for training keeps the prior width until then (invariant INV-GROUND-05: randomization width from a card does
not narrow without an admitted validation; tested by: a candidate posterior leaves the training manifest
unchanged).

## Scope

In: the status of a card version and its ledger events; the admission and rejection rules; the single
interface through which any consumer obtains a field's width, including the `CardWidthSource` trait in
`frob-actuate` that lets lower-layer consumers reach it; behaviour when a candidate is bound explicitly;
behaviour after a retirement. Out: writing the version (N30a-27); freezing and scoring (N30a-31, N30a-32); the
floor applied at admission (N30a-29); the reality status line (N30a-02); the randomization compiler's own
change to take its band through the trait (BD-200, with N30a-27).

## Technical approach

1. **Status.** `CardStatus = Candidate | Admitted | Rejected`, held as ledger events
   `ground.status { version, from, to, cause }` that reference the immutable card version and its calibration
   record. `card::status(version, ledger)` folds them. A version with no Ground event (a data-sheet or generic
   card) has no status and is used as it always was.
2. **Admission.** On a scoring event for prediction p, each candidate version v listed in p's `cards` becomes
   `Admitted` if and only if all hold: (a) p was frozen before the sealed chunk was read (its ledger sequence
   precedes the read); (b) the sealed part scored had not been spent for this cell and model lineage; (c) the
   prediction's registered coverage rule returned "covered" (for a single trial: every registered metric's
   outcome lies inside its registered interval; for n trials: the rule and its allowance fixed at
   registration by the reachability pre-analysis); (d) v's calibration record has no admission blockers
   (`CalibrationRecord::admission_blockers`, N30a-24: no `WrongReason`, nor `EnergyUnchecked` under
   `--strict-data`); (e) p's model identity is the one built from v.
   Otherwise v becomes `Rejected` when (c) failed, and stays `Candidate` when the score was refused for a
   protocol reason (the step fails closed: the candidate stays a candidate).
3. **The width interface.** `ground::effective(version, field) -> FieldPrior { mean, width, source }` is the
   only function randomization, sensitivity analysis and robust synthesis may use to read a Ground-touched
   field:
   - `Admitted`: mean = the version's value; width = max(posterior standard deviation, discrepancy floor);
     source `measured-admitted`. The floor is read from the calibration record's current view (the
     `ground.floor` event that scoring writes, N30a-29); this bead's tests plant that event. A floor that is
     `not-estimated` is N30a-29's case (the prior width comes back).
   - `Candidate` or `Rejected`: walk `parent` links to the nearest version that is admitted or has no Ground
     status; return that version's mean and width; source `prior (candidate not admitted)`.
   The raw uncertainty stored in a candidate version is readable for display only; `xtask` lints forbid its
   use outside `frob-ground` (a deny-list of the field accessor in other crates).
   **Where the interface lives (DESIGN, layering).** The randomization compiler is in `frob-learn` (layer 4)
   and sensitivity analysis in `frob-analyze` (layer 4), below `frob-ground` (layer 5), and a crate never
   depends on a higher layer (`xtask check-layers`). So `effective` reaches them by dependency inversion: this
   bead adds to `frob-actuate` (layer 2, the card crate, which every consumer already reads) the trait
   `CardWidthSource { fn effective(&self, version: CardId, field: &FieldPath, cell: Option<CellId>) ->
   Result<FieldPrior, Refusal>; fn sampler(&self, version: CardId, cell: Option<CellId>) ->
   Option<Box<dyn CorrelatedDraw>> }` (`CorrelatedDraw` is N30a-27's trait in the same crate) and its plain
   implementation `CardOwnWidths` (the card's stated band; no correlated sampler). `frob-ground` implements it as `GroundWidths`, which reads the status ledger and
   returns N30a-27's correlated sampler for a version with a covariance block; `frob-verbs` (layer 6), which
   composes every training, sensitivity and synthesis run, injects `GroundWidths`. Fail closed:
   `CardOwnWidths` refuses any field of provenance `measured` or `measured-class` with the randomization
   compiler's refusal `randomize-no-interval` (BD-200), detail `width source has no status ledger`, so a run
   composed without Ground can never narrow on a candidate.
4. **Training manifests.** A randomization specification references a card lineage, and resolves to its latest
   admitted version (or the original). Writing a candidate therefore changes no training manifest: its
   resolved cards, means, widths and hence its hash are identical before and after `ground.identify`.
   Admission produces a new resolved manifest (a new hash) for runs started afterwards; runs in flight keep
   theirs.
5. **Explicit binding.** A user may bind a candidate version into a model on purpose (that is how the frozen
   prediction is made). Such a model uses the candidate's means; any randomization over it uses the prior's
   widths; every result carries the label `candidate-card` and `reality: candidate(cell)`.
6. **After a retirement.** If the cell in which a version was admitted is retired (a scope change, drift, or
   expiry), `effective` returns the prior width again for manifests resolved after the retirement, with source
   `prior (calibration retired: <reason>)` (DESIGN: the admission was evidence about that regime; a replaced or
   re-lubricated part is the plan's own example of a scope change).
7. **Reality status.** Admission alone does not make a cell `calibrated`: that needs a band (N30a-36). The
   transitions are: candidate written → `candidate(cell)`; rejected → `no-claim`; admitted and band issued →
   `calibrated`.
8. **Folded from the superseded bead N30b-04.** Admission is per cell: `ground::effective` takes the consumer's
   cell as a third argument, `effective(version, field, cell)`. A version admitted in one cell is unknown in
   another, and a consumer running outside the admitting cell receives the prior width with source
   `prior (admitted in another cell)` (DESIGN, from the rule that a Reality result has no meaning outside its
   scope; this is what the randomization compiler must ask, with a cell). Recorded alternative, not adopted:
   admission as a further card version that differs only in a `state` field and in the score's identifier.
   Status stays a ledger event over an immutable version, the same reading as for the calibration record
   (N30a-24).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-GROUND-05: for a training task whose randomization covers an actuator card, the resolved training
      manifest's bytes and hash are identical before and after `ground.identify` writes a candidate.
- [ ] After `ground.score` reports coverage on sealed data, the newly resolved manifest has the narrowed width
      for exactly the identified fields, and runs started before admission are unaffected.
- [ ] The omitted-law candidate is `Rejected` at validation; its widths never appear in any manifest.
- [ ] A candidate whose record carries `WrongReason` is not admitted even when its prediction covers.
- [ ] A refused score (no frozen prediction; spent holdout) leaves the version `Candidate`.
- [ ] A model that binds a candidate explicitly randomizes with prior widths and labels its results.
- [ ] No crate outside `frob-ground` reads a candidate's raw uncertainty (lint passes; a planted use fails).
- [ ] `frob-learn` and `frob-analyze` reach widths only through `CardWidthSource` and name no `frob-ground`
      item (`xtask check-layers` passes; a planted `use frob_ground::…` in `frob-learn` fails it); with
      `CardOwnWidths` injected, randomizing a `measured` field is refused and the manifest is not written.
- [ ] A consumer asking with a cell other than the admitting one receives the prior width and the source
      `prior (admitted in another cell)`; after a retirement of the admitting cell the width reverts for every
      cell.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-726 (omitted law: the candidate is rejected at validation; control: the same fit scored on its own
calibration data passes). Counterexample CX-331 (score a fit on its own calibration data). Negative controls:
a randomization path that reads the candidate's stored uncertainty (the manifest-hash test fails); an
admission rule that omits condition (a), so a prediction frozen after the read admits its card (must be
caught); an `effective` that returns the candidate mean with the prior width for unbound lineages.
Determinism: status and manifests are class D0.

## Considerations

- "The registered interval covered" for a prediction with several metrics and several trials is defined by
  the rule frozen in the prediction record; this bead does not choose it after the fact.
- A long chain of rejected candidates makes the parent walk longer, not riskier; the walk always ends at a
  version with no Ground status.
- Kill gate KILL-GROUND removes identification; cards then change only by direct bench measurement of a
  field, which follows the same candidate-then-admitted path with a prediction of that field's consequence.
- Reverting widths at retirement (step 6) is a DESIGN reading; the plan states only that reports revert to
  `no-claim` and that verdicts citing the band are marked stale.
