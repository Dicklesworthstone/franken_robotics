## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) fits card parameters to logs and stores the posterior with its uncertainty. A
Gaussian posterior's variance falls like 1/n with the amount of data. That is correct for sampling error and
false for model error: if the model's form is slightly wrong (a friction law without a term the real joint
has), ten times more data makes the fit ten times more confident in parameters that still mispredict by the
same amount. The plan's rule: **the posterior variance of a field is never reported below the variance that
validation residuals attribute to model form. The floor is the common-mode term of the sibling estimator's
`groups` module, estimated on validation data. More samples shrink sampling error and leave the floor.**

The sibling crate `fs-assimilate` models an error shared by many rows as a declared common-mode source, so
that repeated samples cannot push uncertainty below it. Instrument calibration error is one such source
(N30a-08). Model-form error is another, and it can only be sized on data the fit did not see: the validation
split, which is sealed and read once, by scoring.

Rejected alternatives: inflating the posterior by the calibration-data reduced χ² (the fit has already bent
itself to that data, so its residuals understate model error; this is the same mistake as scoring a fit on its
own calibration data); a fixed minimum relative uncertainty per field (arbitrary, and unrelated to how the
model actually fails).

## Scope

In: the estimator of the model-form variance from validation residuals; the floored posterior; where the floor
is stored and how it is reported and consumed. Out: the instrument common-mode floor (N30a-08); the reality-gap
variance per cell used by the experiment-value model (N30a-09), which is a statistic of outcomes, not of card
fields; admission (N30a-28).

## Technical approach

1. **When and where.** Inside the scoring of a frozen prediction on the validation part: scoring already holds
   the validation readings y_j, the candidate's predictions ŷ_j at the instruments' instants, the instrument
   covariance R_val, and the sensitivity rows h_j = ∂ŷ_j/∂θ of the identified fields (from the referee). The
   floor computation sees residual summaries only and writes no validation sample anywhere.
2. **Model-form variance per channel** (method of moments). For channel c with m_c validation rows (window
   features, as in the fit): σ_mf,c² = max(0, (1/m_c)·Σ_j [e_j² − R_val,jj − h_jᵀ·P·h_j]), where e_j = y_j − ŷ_j
   and P is the candidate's posterior covariance. In words: residual power left after instrument noise and
   the parameters' own posterior uncertainty are accounted for. A channel with fewer than 8 rows (DESIGN)
   contributes no estimate, and the floor is then `not-estimated` for fields informed only by that channel.
3. **Floored posterior.** Recompute the posterior covariance at the candidate's mean with one added
   common-mode source per channel of variance σ_mf,c², shared by all calibration rows of that channel:
   P_floor = (P₀⁻¹ + Hᵀ·(R + Σ_c σ_mf,c²·1_c·1_cᵀ)⁻¹·H)⁻¹, through the sibling's `groups` mechanism (one
   more shared parameter per channel; the cap of 64 shared parameters is checked). Because the added term is
   positive semi-definite, P_floor ≥ P in the matrix order.
4. **The floor of field i** is floor_i = (P_floor)_ii. The reported variance of the field is
   max(P_ii, floor_i) = floor_i. As the calibration data grows, P_ii falls like 1/n and floor_i tends to a
   positive constant set by σ_mf² and the sensitivities: for a field that acts as the level of one channel
   (sensitivity 1 on n rows of noise variance σ²), floor = σ²/n + σ_mf².
5. **Storage.** The card version is immutable and keeps the fit's covariance. The floor is written as a ledger
   event `ground.floor { version, record, sigma_mf: per channel, sigma_mf2_se: per channel (the standard error
   of σ_mf,c², √(2/m_c)·mean(e_j²) under a normal residual), floor: per field, validation_reduced_chi2, rows:
   per channel }` and as an addendum artifact referenced by the calibration record's current view
   (`discrepancy_floor`). Before scoring, the view shows `not-estimated`.
6. **Consumption.** `ground::effective(version, field, cell)` (N30a-28) returns
   width = √max(posterior variance, floor) for an admitted version. Correlated draws use P_floor in place of
   P for the identified block. Reports print both numbers: "posterior σ = …; floor σ = …; reported σ = …".
   A field of an admitted version whose floor is `not-estimated` (every channel that informs it had fewer than
   8 validation rows) gets the prior width back, with source `prior (floor not estimated)` (DESIGN, fail
   closed: validation that cannot size the model-form term cannot justify a narrower width). This bead adds
   that case to `effective`.
7. **Diagnostics.** The event also stores the validation reduced χ² before flooring. A value near 1 gives
   σ_mf ≈ 0 and no visible floor; a large value with a covering interval is legitimate (the registered
   interval was wide enough) and the floor records how much of the spread is model form.
8. **Before any validation (folded from the superseded bead N30b-04).** While the floor is `not-estimated`,
   the uncertainty displayed for a candidate field is max(posterior standard deviation, the model-form term of
   the prior card's class), so a candidate is never shown tighter than its class admits. DESIGN definition of
   that term: the floor σ of the field in the most recent `ground.floor` event of an admitted version of the
   same card lineage, else of the same class (N30a-30); when no such event exists the posterior σ is shown with
   the note `floor not yet estimated`. This display rule changes no width any consumer uses, since a
   candidate's width is never used (N30a-28).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Closed form: a one-parameter level model with n calibration rows of noise variance 1 and a planted
      model-form error of standard deviation 0.5, drawn independently per validation row, gives σ_mf² = 0.25
      (within sampling error) and floored variance 1/n + 0.25: 0.26 at n = 100 and 0.2501 at n = 10⁴.
- [ ] With validation residuals consistent with instrument noise alone, σ_mf² = 0 and the floor equals the
      posterior variance.
- [ ] Doubling the calibration data twice lowers the posterior variance by about 4 and leaves the reported
      variance within 2% of the floor's limit.
- [ ] P_floor − P is positive semi-definite on every fixture.
- [ ] The floor is absent (`not-estimated`) before scoring and cannot be computed by any path other than
      scoring (no public function takes validation data).
- [ ] An admitted version's `effective` width is the floored one, and its correlated draws use P_floor; a
      field whose floor is `not-estimated` returns the prior width with source `prior (floor not estimated)`.
- [ ] On a fixture with 1,000 windows sharing one bias, the reported variance of the field equals the floor
      (posterior variance 10⁻⁸, floor 10⁻⁶: reported 10⁻⁶).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/discrepancy_floor.rs`: `mom_estimate_level_model` (10⁴ validation rows: 0.25 within
0.06; the estimate's standard error is √(2·1.25²/10⁴) ≈ 0.018, so 0.06 is about 3.4 standard errors and the
test does not depend on a lucky seed); `mom_floors_at_zero`; `not_estimated_floor_returns_prior_width`; `floored_variance_closed_form` (n = 100: 0.26; n = 10⁴: 0.2501);
`floor_survives_more_data`; `p_floor_dominates_p` (property over random H, R, σ_mf); `few_rows_not_estimated`;
`shared_parameter_cap_respected` (60 instrument groups plus 5 channels is refused with the cap named);
`floor_event_roundtrip`; `effective_uses_floor`; `draws_use_p_floor`;
`no_public_path_takes_validation_rows` (an API test: the only caller is `score`).

### End-to-end script and logging

Part of `scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh` (N30a-35): the rehearsal's instrumented-cart dataset, whose truth
carries a small planted model-form difference (a contact-dissipation variant the fit class lacks, BD-388,
sized so that the interval still covers). After `score` the script reads the floor event and logs σ_mf per
channel with its row count and standard error, posterior σ, floor σ and reported σ for each identified field,
and asserts reported ≥ posterior, reported ≥ floor, and σ_mf > 0 on at least one channel.

### Known answers and negative controls

The closed forms above. Negative controls: a floor estimated on calibration residuals (on the planted case it
reads near zero, and the test asserts the validation-based value instead); a floored posterior computed with
an independent (diagonal) model-form term in place of the common-mode one (its variance still falls like 1/n,
and `floor_survives_more_data` fails). Counterexample CX-331 (score a fit on its own calibration data).
Determinism: double precision with fixed summation order; bitwise twice on one machine.
Folded from N30b-04: test `floor_applies` (the case above); planted failure: a floor of zero (the shared-bias
fixture then reports an impossible precision); case CX-G-10 of the chapter's counterexample registry.

## Considerations

- One common-mode group per channel is the most conservative reading (model-form error perfectly correlated
  across rows of a channel). A per-episode grouping would be less conservative and needs evidence; the choice
  is recorded in the event.
- The floor uses the validation part, which is read once. A refit after scoring needs a new prediction version
  and fresh sealed data, and with them a new floor; an old floor never transfers to a new card version.
- The floor never raises an evidence class and never makes a rejected candidate admissible; it only widens.
- If the validation part is small the floor is noisy; the event stores the row count and a standard error for
  σ_mf² so that a reader can see it.
