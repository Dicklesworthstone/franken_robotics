## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) compares simulation with measured trials. Decision ADR-GROUND-05: a prediction is
frozen before its trial data is readable; a refit opens a new prediction version; a scored holdout is retired.
Otherwise the comparison is a fit, and its error rate is unknown. A real trial is therefore preceded by a
**prediction record**. It extends the studio's registered predictions (a prediction hashed and frozen before a
run starts and scored afterwards with a proper scoring rule) with what a comparison against reality needs: the
cell, the cards and model identity the prediction was made with, a recorded draw of trial conditions, a
replayable address for the simulated side, a declared study and registered tolerances.

Invariant INV-GROUND-03: a prediction's ledger sequence precedes the first read of the sealed chunk it
predicts (tested by the ledger's total order; a planted early read is refused). The admission bead seals the
validation and holdout parts of a dataset so that only scoring can read them; this bead supplies the thing
scoring must be shown first.

Rejected alternatives: timestamps as proof of order (clocks can be set; a ledger sequence cannot be reordered);
a prediction kept in a notebook or a chat log (unhashed, editable); one global tolerance (see below).

## Scope

In: the record's schema and validation; how intervals are produced; the conditions draw; tolerance
registration and its refusals; freezing; tamper detection. Out: the proper score and the scoring report
(N30a-32); study type and reachability (N30a-33); versions, holdout retirement and error spending (N30a-34);
the verbs' envelopes (BD-392).

## Technical approach

1. **Schema** (the plan's, DESIGN):
   ```toml
   # frob.ground.prediction/1
   [prediction]
   version = 1; cell = "…"; model = "ModelId"; cards = ["…"]; scene = "SceneId"; task = "TaskId"
   conditions_draw = "philox:seed=…"       # trial conditions are drawn and recorded before the trial
   world_addr      = "…"                   # the simulated side is replayable at its recorded determinism class
   metrics = [ { name = "crossing_time_s", interval = [4.05, 4.42], level = 0.90 },
               { name = "peak_current_A",  interval = [4.8, 6.1],   level = 0.90 } ]
   scoring   = "interval-score"
   decision  = "B beats A by at least 5 points"
   tolerance = "from instrument sigma and the decision margin"
   study     = { type = "fixed-n", n = 20, reachable = ["promote", "inconclusive"] }
   frozen_at = "ledger-seq:…"              # precedes the first read of the sealed chunk
   ```
   Typed form: `metrics: Vec<MetricPrediction { name, unit, interval: [lo, hi], level, tolerance:
   Tolerance }>`; `tolerance` in the sketch is realised per metric (step 4); `decision` is a typed comparison
   (designs or a threshold, a metric, a margin with unit); `lineage` (the card lineage the version belongs
   to) and `parent` (the previous version, if any) are added.
2. **Intervals.** `ground.predict` either computes them or accepts the caller's. Computed: simulate the task
   in the scene under each drawn condition with the bound cards, over 256 correlated draws of the cards'
   identified block (by address) and the simulator's sensor noise; the interval of a metric at level 1 − α is
   its central (α/2, 1 − α/2) sample quantile interval; `source = "simulated"`. Supplied: `source = "caller"`,
   stored as given. Either way the simulated side is replayable from `world_addr` at its recorded determinism
   class, and the record stores the digest of that replay.
3. **Conditions draw.** The trial conditions (payload, speed set-point, start pose, surface patch, whichever
   the task randomizes) are drawn inside the cell's envelope from the recorded seed before the trial and
   listed in the record. At scoring, each trial's recorded conditions must equal its registered draw within
   the instruments' tolerance; a trial whose conditions differ is marked `interfered` and is not scored as
   confirmatory. (The planner chooses how many real episodes, never which conditions: assigned conditions
   equal the registered draw, and that equality is the interference test.)
4. **Registered tolerances.** Tolerances are registered and are derived from measurement uncertainty and the
   decision margin. Per metric: `tolerance = { value, unit, instrument_sigma, margin }`, where
   `instrument_sigma` is the metric's measurement standard deviation propagated from the instrument model
   (including time-alignment uncertainty for time metrics) and `margin` is the decision margin expressed in
   that metric's unit. Registration checks (DESIGN): the unit matches the metric's dimension;
   value ≥ 2·instrument_sigma (a tolerance tighter than the instrument can resolve is refused); if
   2·instrument_sigma exceeds the margin the metric is registered `unresolvable` for the decision and cannot
   support a `promote` verdict. **One unitless epsilon across angles, currents and temperatures is refused**:
   a record whose metrics of different dimensions share one bare number, or any tolerance without a unit,
   fails validation.
5. **Freezing.** Canonicalize; hash; append ledger event `ground.frozen { prediction: hash, cell, lineage,
   version }`; write `frozen_at` (the event's sequence) into the stored record's envelope, not into the hashed
   body. From then on the body is immutable: scoring recomputes the hash and compares.
6. **Refusals.** A read of sealed data with no frozen prediction, or with a prediction whose body no longer
   matches its frozen hash, is `FROB-REF-883` (repair offered: register a new version; exit code 3). A record
   that fails validation (missing cell, unknown card, interval with lo > hi, a level outside (0, 1), a
   unitless tolerance) is refused at `ground.predict` with the schema refusal and the offending field.
7. **What the record does not contain.** No outcome, no fitted quantity from validation or holdout data, no
   free text that scoring interprets. `decision` and `study` are typed so that the verdict is computed, not
   read.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A valid record freezes: the ledger holds one `ground.frozen` event whose sequence is returned as
      `frozen_at`, and re-reading the record reproduces the hash.
- [ ] INV-GROUND-03: for every scored trial the frozen sequence is less than the sequence of the sealed read;
      a planted read before freeze is refused with 883.
- [ ] Editing any byte of a frozen record's body (an interval end moved by 0.01) makes scoring refuse with 883.
- [ ] A record with one bare tolerance `0.01` for `crossing_time_s`, `peak_current_A` and a temperature metric
      is refused; a tolerance below twice the instrument sigma is refused; a metric whose instrument sigma
      exceeds half the margin is registered `unresolvable`.
- [ ] Computed intervals are reproducible from `world_addr` (same digest) and contain the stated share of the
      256 simulated draws.
- [ ] A trial whose recorded payload differs from its registered draw by more than tolerance is `interfered`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh` (bead N30a-35): `frob ground predict` for a rehearsal trial; the script logs
the record hash, `frozen_at`, the metrics with intervals, levels and tolerances, the conditions drawn, and
later the sequence of the sealed read, asserting freeze < read.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-734 (protocol: read before freeze; refit without a new version; holdout reuse: each refused): the first is
decided here. The crossing-time interval [4.05, 4.42] at level 0.90 is the fixture used throughout. Negative
controls: a scoring path that compares timestamps instead of ledger sequences (a planted clock set back must
not license an early read); a freeze that hashes after inserting `frozen_at` (the record can then never
verify); a validator that accepts a unitless tolerance. Determinism: record bytes and hash are class D0;
computed intervals are by address and identical twice.

## Considerations

- A prediction may be registered before the data exists (a planned trial) or after admission of a sealed
  dataset; in both cases the sealed chunk has not been read. Data acquired and admitted unsealed can never be
  used for a confirmatory score.
- The tolerance rule (at least twice the instrument sigma, at most the margin) is DESIGN; the plan fixes only
  the sources (instrument sigma and decision margin) and the refusal of a single unitless epsilon.
- The refusal registry has no code for "invalid prediction at registration"; the generic schema refusal is
  used and the gap is listed in this unit's coverage notes.
- Kill gate KILL-CLOCK is triggered by the failure of the freeze-before-read gate as well as of the clock
  gate: without a trustworthy order there are no time-based scores.
