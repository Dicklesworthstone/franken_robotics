## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) scores a prediction that was frozen in the ledger before the trial data could be
read. Two rules of the plan govern the score itself.

**Scoring is proper.** The interval score of a registered interval [l, u] at level 1 − α against an outcome y
is S = (u − l) + (2/α)·(l − y)₊ + (2/α)·(y − u)₊, where (x)₊ = max(x, 0). It is reported with bias, coverage,
and residual structure against condition. A proper score is one whose expected value is minimized by stating
one's true belief: a forecaster cannot improve it by widening intervals to look safe (the width term grows) or
narrowing them to look sharp (the miss terms grow, at a rate 2/α).

**A reversed ranking is a result.** If the real ranking of two designs contradicts the simulated one, the
outcome is `ranking-falsified`: the cell's domain narrows to where the ranking held, and the pair, its
conditions and its logs become a regression case in the divergence ledger. The project's most common statement
is "design B is better than design A"; a simulator that predicts each design's metric within its band and
still orders them wrongly has failed at the thing it is used for, and that failure is kept, not explained away.

Rejected alternatives: scoring by mean absolute error against a point prediction (it rewards a forecaster for
hiding uncertainty); a pass or fail on coverage alone (a forecaster covers everything with infinite intervals);
treating a reversed ranking as noise unless it is large (the registered study decides what is large).

## Scope

In: the per-trial and aggregate score; bias, coverage and structure; what scoring emits (residuals for the
cell's pool, the score report); the comparison of rankings and the three consequences of a falsified one. Out:
freezing (N30a-31); the study's verdict and reachability (N30a-33); holdout retirement (N30a-34); admission of
cards (N30a-28); the floor (N30a-29); the band (N30a-36).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** A frozen record (hash verified), a sealed part opened once through a score token, the clock
   artifacts (verified to be pure functions of the committed marks, N30a-13). Time-based metrics use aligned
   instants inside the bracket only (N30a-12); a trial whose conditions differ from the registered draw is
   `interfered` and excluded from confirmatory statistics, with its count reported.
2. **Per trial t and metric m.** Outcome y_t from the sealed data by the metric's registered definition;
   α = 1 − level; S_t = (u − l) + (2/α)·(l − y_t)₊ + (2/α)·(y_t − u)₊; signed residual e_t = y_t − c with c
   the interval's midpoint (or the registered median when the record carries one); `covered_t` = l ≤ y_t ≤ u;
   `near_miss_t` = not covered and outside by at most the registered tolerance.
3. **Aggregates per metric.** n scored trials; mean score S̄; bias = mean(e_t) with its standard error;
   coverage k/n with the exact (Clopper–Pearson) 95% interval; near misses counted separately. The registered
   coverage rule (frozen in the record: `strict`, or `within-tolerance`, which counts near misses as covered;
   default `within-tolerance`, DESIGN, because the tolerance is the measurement's own resolution) decides
   "covered" for the purposes of admission.
4. **Residual structure against condition.** For each registered condition (payload, speed, temperature and
   so on): the least-squares slope of e_t on the condition with its standard error, and Spearman's rank
   correlation with a permutation p-value (10⁴ permutations, by address). A condition with |slope|/se > 3 is
   reported `structured`: the error depends on where in the envelope the trial sat, which pooled coverage
   would hide.
5. **Outputs.** (a) The score report artifact `frob.ground.score/1`: per metric the numbers above, the trial
   table (trial id, conditions, y, interval, S, covered), the study verdict, labels. (b) Residuals for the
   band: r_t = |y_t − ŷ_t| (two-sided) or ŷ_t − y_t (one-sided), pushed to the cell's pool, which refuses any
   other cell, model identity or origin. (c) Ledger event `ground.scored { prediction, dataset, part, read_seq,
   report }`. Evidence class Estimated; status of the comparison follows the data's origin (`synthetic` data
   never yields another status).
6. **Ranking comparison.** When the record's `decision` compares designs A and B on a metric with a margin:
   simulated ranking from the frozen record; real ranking from paired real trials under the registered study
   (N30a-33). Outcomes: `ranking-confirmed` (same sign, the study's verdict reached), `ranking-inconclusive`
   (the study could not decide), `ranking-falsified` (the real difference has the opposite sign and the study
   decides against the simulated ranking).
7. **Consequences of `ranking-falsified`.**
   1. The label `RankingFalsified` is added to the score report and to the calibration record's current view.
   2. **Narrowing.** For each registered condition dimension, sort trials by the condition and find the
      longest contiguous range (at least 5 paired trials, DESIGN) in which every paired difference agrees in
      sign with the simulated ranking. Take the dimension with the largest share of trials in its range and
      form `cell.narrow(dimension, range)` (a new cell identity that inherits nothing). If no range of 5
      exists, the pair has no domain in this cell. The narrowing is found after seeing the data, so it is
      exploratory: the narrowed cell supports nothing until a new prediction is frozen for it and scored on
      fresh sealed data.
   3. **Regression case.** The pair, the registered conditions, the simulated world addresses and the hashes
      of the real logs are written to the divergence ledger with kind `reality` (hashes only for a private
      dataset), so that every later change to the simulator is replayed against the case.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For [4.05, 4.42] at level 0.90: y = 4.20 scores 0.37; y = 4.50 scores 0.37 + 20·0.08 = 1.97; y = 4.00
      scores 0.37 + 20·0.05 = 1.37.
- [ ] Properness, by simulation: for outcomes drawn from a standard normal and level 0.90, the mean score of
      the interval ±1.645 is lower than those of ±1.2 and ±2.2 (10⁵ draws, differences beyond 5 standard
      errors).
- [ ] Coverage 18 of 20 reports 0.90 with the exact interval [0.683, 0.988]; near misses are counted apart and
      follow the registered rule.
- [ ] A planted dependence of the residual on payload (slope 0.05 s/kg, noise 0.02 s, 20 trials over 0 to 2 kg)
      is reported `structured`.
- [ ] A planted reversed ranking gives `ranking-falsified`, a narrowed cell where the ranking held (a new
      `CellId` with no band), and one regression case that replays to its recorded digest.
- [ ] An `interfered` trial is excluded and counted; a time metric outside the clock bracket is refused, not
      scored.

`private_dataset_case_has_hashes_only`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh` (bead N30a-35): `frob ground score` on rehearsal trials, including a planted
miss and a planted reversed ranking. Logged per metric: n, S̄, bias with its standard error, coverage k of n
with its interval, near misses, the structure table; for the ranking case the outcome, the narrowed range and
`CellId`, and the regression case's identity.

### Known answers and negative controls

The three interval-score values (exact). KA-726's control (a fit scored on its own calibration data passes) is
impossible through this function by construction: it accepts only a sealed part opened by a token.
Negative controls: a score with factor 1/α in place of 2/α (the 1.97 case reads 1.17); a coverage statistic
that counts `interfered` trials; a ranking comparison that reports `inconclusive` whenever the signs differ.
Metamorphic: permuting trials leaves every aggregate unchanged; shifting interval and outcome together leaves
S unchanged. Determinism: exact sums in a fixed order; permutation tests by address; bitwise twice.

## Considerations

- The narrowing rule is DESIGN: the plan says the domain narrows to where the ranking held, not how the
  region is found. A one-dimensional contiguous range is the simplest reading that cannot be tuned much; the
  result is exploratory and says so.
- With small n the Clopper–Pearson interval is wide (18 of 20 is compatible with 68% coverage); the report
  prints it so that "90% coverage" is not over-read.
- A losing result is reported as completely as a winning one, and a replication kit is built for a falsified
  prediction as readily as for a confirmed one.
- Kill gate KILL-BAND leaves scoring in place: reports then print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual
  table this bead produces.
