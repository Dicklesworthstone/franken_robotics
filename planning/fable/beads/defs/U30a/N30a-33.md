## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) scores predictions against real trials, and real trials are few. The plan's rule:
**the study type is declared: fixed-n exact with a pre-analysis of reachable verdicts, or anytime-valid, never
both. For real trials n is small and the pre-analysis decides whether the trial is worth running.**

The two study types are the task subsystem's. An *anytime-valid* study uses a betting e-process or a confidence
sequence, valid at every stopping time; it declares its null hypothesis, effect margin, bound on outcomes,
betting rule, level and budget before the first episode. A *fixed-n exact* study uses an exact interval or test
at a sample size fixed in advance; it declares n, the rule, the level and a pre-analysis: which verdicts are
reachable at that n, and the power at declared alternatives. The types are never mixed inside one study: a
fixed-n interval computed when an anytime-valid study stops has no coverage guarantee, and a fixed-n study that
stops when its running p-value first looks good loses its level (an exact one-sided sign test at nominal 5%
looked at after every pair rejects falsely 5.08% of the time after 8 looks and 17.4% after 64).

The arithmetic that makes the pre-analysis matter (FACT): with zero failures in n trials, the one-sided 95%
upper bound on the failure probability is 1 − 0.05^(1/n): 13.9% at n = 20, 9.5% at n = 30, 4.95% at n = 59. A
claim of "under 5%" is unreachable below 59 failure-free trials. Twenty clean real trials feel like strong
evidence and support only "under 14%".

## Scope

In: the study block of a prediction record; validation against the task subsystem's study library; the
reachability pre-analysis for the claims real trials make (a failure-rate bound; a paired ranking; interval
coverage); refusal at registration of an unreachable claim; the "worth running" output. Out: the study
machinery itself (BD-124); the score (N30a-32); error spending across repeated attempts (N30a-34).

## Technical approach

1. **The block.** `study = { type = "fixed-n", n = 20, rule = "…", level = 0.95, reachable = ["promote",
   "inconclusive"] }` or `study = { type = "anytime", null = "…", margin = …, bound = […], betting = "…",
   level = …, budget = … }`. It is validated by the same library that validates the task subsystem's studies;
   a block that declares both types, or switches type in a later version of the same prediction, is refused
   (`FROB-REF-552`, the task subsystem's refusal of a mixture).
2. **Pre-analysis (fixed-n).** `reach::analyse(study, claim) -> Reachability` enumerates every possible
   outcome of the n trials under the rule and returns: the set of verdicts that can occur; for each claimed
   verdict the outcomes that produce it; the smallest n at which it becomes reachable; and the power at each
   declared alternative. The record's `reachable` list must equal the computed set; a record that names a
   verdict its rule cannot reach at the declared n and level is refused at registration (`FROB-REF-532`, the
   task subsystem's refusal; repair: the smallest sufficient n, or the strongest claim reachable at this n).
3. **Failure-rate claims.** Claim "failure probability below p*" at one-sided level 1 − α with k failures in n
   trials: exact upper bound U(k, n) = the (1 − α) quantile of Beta(k + 1, n − k); for k = 0,
   U = 1 − α^(1/n). The claim is reachable iff U(0, n) < p*, that is n ≥ ⌈ln α/ln(1 − p*)⌉: 59 for p* = 5%,
   29 for 10%, 299 for 1% at α = 0.05. The pre-analysis prints the strongest claim reachable: at n = 20,
   "under 13.9%".
4. **Ranking claims.** Claim "B beats A" from n paired trials by an exact one-sided sign test at level α: the
   smallest attainable p-value is 2⁻ⁿ (all pairs favour B), so `promote` is unreachable below
   n = ⌈log₂(1/α)⌉ pairs: 5 at α = 0.05 (2⁻⁵ = 0.03125; 2⁻⁴ = 0.0625). With a margin, pairs inside the margin
   count as ties and the same enumeration is done on the non-tied count.
5. **Coverage claims.** Claim "the registered intervals cover at the registered level" over n trials: the
   pre-analysis fixes the allowance m_max, the largest number of misses for which the exact binomial test does
   not reject nominal coverage at level α (for n = 20, nominal miss rate 0.10, α = 0.05: m_max = 4, since
   P(misses ≥ 5) = 0.043 and P(misses ≥ 4) = 0.133), and reports the power to detect a miss rate of twice
   nominal (0.37 here). "Covered", for the admission of a card version, means misses ≤ m_max, fixed here before
   any data is read.
6. **Worth running.** The output carries a recommendation: `run` when the claimed verdict is reachable and its
   power at the declared alternative is at least 0.5 (DESIGN); `not worth running` with the reason
   otherwise, for example "at n = 20 the only reachable verdict for 'under 5%' is inconclusive". Under the
   decision model's budgeted mode at most 30 real episodes are recommended per decision; a claim that needs
   more is answered "inconclusive, no-claim" at registration.
7. **At scoring.** A fixed-n verdict is computed once, on n complete, non-interfered trials, never earlier;
   with fewer the verdict is `inconclusive (incomplete: k of n)`. An anytime-valid study's e-process is read
   after every trial and stops at its threshold or its budget.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Zero failures in n trials: the bound prints 13.9% at n = 20, 9.5% at n = 30, 4.95% at n = 59 and 5.03% at
      n = 58.
- [ ] A registered claim of "under 5%" at n = 20 is refused at registration with the repair "n ≥ 59, or claim
      under 13.9%".
- [ ] A ranking claim at α = 0.05 with 4 pairs is refused; with 5 pairs it registers with reachable verdicts
      `promote` and `inconclusive`.
- [ ] The coverage allowance at n = 20 and nominal 0.90 is m_max = 4, and the record stores it.
- [ ] A study block declaring both types is refused; a version 2 that changes the type is refused.
- [ ] A fixed-n study scored with 17 of 20 trials complete returns `inconclusive (incomplete: 17 of 20)`.
- [ ] CX-333's fixture `cx-333.toml` exists with the `synthetic` header and its `removed_check`; offered through
      the registrar it is refused with 532 and the repair above.

## Considerations

- The power threshold of 0.5 for "worth running" is a DESIGN default; the pre-analysis prints the power so
  the operator can decide otherwise, and the decision is recorded.
- `reach::analyse` evaluates at the level it is given. Through `ground.predict` that is the attempt's level
  α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] (N30a-34), which is α/2 on a first attempt: a ranking claim with nominal α = 0.05
  registered as attempt 1 runs at 0.025 and needs 6 pairs (2⁻⁶ = 0.0156), not 5. The worked values of this
  bead are at the level stated beside them. Through the verb, KA-732's fixture therefore declares nominal
  α = 0.10, so that its first attempt runs at 0.05 and gives the 59 trials and the 13.9% of the known answer
  (N30a-35 step 7 does so).
- For real trials the exchangeable unit matters: twenty episodes on one unit bound that unit's failure rate in
  that cell, not the design's across units.
- The two refusal codes used here belong to the task subsystem, which owns the study types; Ground adds none.
- Anytime-valid studies suit drift monitoring and sequential confirmation; for a small planned batch on a
  bench the fixed-n form is usually the honest choice because n is set by cost.
