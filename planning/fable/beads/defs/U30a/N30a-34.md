## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) compares frozen predictions with sealed real data. Freezing protects one
comparison. This bead protects the sequence of comparisons, where the same error returns by another door: look
at the validation result, adjust the model, look again. Decision ADR-GROUND-05 has three clauses; the second
and third are here: **a refit opens a new prediction version; a scored holdout is retired.**

The plan's rules:

- **A refit opens a new version.** A posterior changed after validation data was seen needs version 2 of the
  prediction and fresh sealed data. A published failure keeps its original threshold.
- **A scored holdout is spent** for that cell and model lineage. It moves to the calibration pool or the
  regression set and is never a holdout again (invariant INV-GROUND-04: a holdout chunk is scored at most once
  per cell and model lineage; tested by: a planted reuse is refused). Repeated confirmation attempts spend
  error as in the blinded qualification lane: the i-th attempt on fresh data runs at α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)].

Why the level series: Σ_{i=1..m} α/[i·(i + 1)] = α·m/(m + 1), which stays below α for any number of attempts,
so a team that keeps trying until something confirms still has a total false-confirmation probability under α.
The price is that each later attempt needs more evidence.

Rejected alternatives: allowing a holdout to be re-scored "for diagnosis" (a diagnosis that changes the next
fit is a use); a constant level for every attempt (the error rate of the procedure then grows with
persistence); deleting a failed prediction when its successor passes (a published failure is a result).

## Scope

In: model lineage; the spent set and refusal 886; what a spent part becomes; detection of a refit and the
version rule; immutability of a failed version and its threshold; the attempt counter and the level series.
Out: the record and its freeze (N30a-31); scoring (N30a-32); reachability at the reduced level (N30a-33, called
from here); the sealed-area read guard (BD-387).

## Technical approach

1. **Model lineage.** `LineageId = hash(root ModelId, CellId)`, where the root is the model built from cards
   with no Ground status; every card version written by identification stays in the lineage of its parent.
   A different design, or a different law class registered as a rival model, is a different lineage.
2. **The spent set.** Scoring appends, in one ledger transaction with `ground.scored`, the event
   `ground.spent { dataset, part, chunks: [hash], cell, lineage, prediction }`. Before a score token is minted
   for (part, cell, lineage), the guard checks that no chunk of the part is in the spent set for that pair. A
   hit is refused with `FROB-REF-886` (reuse of a spent holdout; repair offered: acquire fresh sealed data;
   exit code 3). The check is on chunk hashes, so re-admitting the same bytes under a new dataset name is
   caught.
3. **What a spent part becomes.** The score call says `--then calibration` (default) or `--then regression`.
   `calibration`: the part is unsealed into the cell's calibration pool; from then on it is a holdout for no
   lineage (the guard treats it as spent for all). `regression`: it stays sealed and becomes part of the
   regression set, readable only by score tokens minted for regression replay of an already-frozen
   prediction; another lineage that has not scored it may still score it once.
4. **Refit detection and the version rule.** A card version written after any `ground.scored` event of its
   lineage in its cell is a *refit*. `ground.predict` for a model that binds a refit version must produce a
   record with `version = v + 1` and `parent` = the hash of the lineage's latest frozen record for the same
   claim; a version-1 record is refused. `ground.score` for a refit's record on a part already spent for the
   lineage is refused with 886; on any data with no record of the right version, with `FROB-REF-883` (a read
   of sealed data with no frozen prediction, or a prediction changed after freezing; repair offered: register
   a new version). "Refit without a new version" is therefore refused at whichever verb is tried first.
5. **A published failure keeps its original threshold.** A frozen record and its score report are immutable.
   Version v + 1 of a claim carries the same claim identity and must restate the decision margin, the
   tolerances and the nominal level α; `ground.predict` diffs them against version v and refuses a version
   whose margin or tolerance is looser under the same claim identity. A loosened threshold is a new claim,
   with a new identity and attempt 1, and every report of the design lists the old claim as failed at its
   original threshold beside it.
6. **Attempt counter and levels.** Per claim identity in a cell and lineage, attempts are counted by scored
   records: the i-th scored attempt on fresh data runs at α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] (α/2, α/6, α/12, α/20, …),
   computed as exact rationals. `ground.predict` for attempt i writes α_i into the study block, re-runs the
   reachability pre-analysis at α_i (a claim reachable at α/2 with n trials may be unreachable at α/6), and
   the record's intervals for admission purposes are evaluated at that level. The ledger stores i with the
   frozen event; the level cannot be chosen by the caller.
7. **Reports.** `frob ground band --status` and the design report list, per claim: every version, its attempt
   index and level, its outcome, and the parts spent.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INV-GROUND-04: a second score of one holdout part for the same cell and lineage is refused with 886,
      also when the same chunks are re-admitted under another dataset identity.
- [ ] After `--then calibration` the part is readable as calibration data and is refused as a holdout for
      every lineage; after `--then regression` it stays sealed and a different lineage may score it once.
- [ ] A card version written after a scored event is detected as a refit; a version-1 prediction with it is
      refused; scoring it on the spent part is refused with 886; with version 2 and fresh sealed data it scores.
- [ ] A failed version's report is unchanged by later versions and is listed with its original threshold; a
      version that loosens the margin under the same claim identity is refused.
- [ ] Levels: with α = 0.10 the first three attempts run at 0.05, 0.016667 and 0.008333, and the sum over m
      attempts equals α·m/(m + 1) exactly.
- [ ] Attempt 2 of a claim that was reachable at n = 5 pairs (2⁻⁵ = 0.03125 ≤ 0.05) is refused as unreachable
      at α₂ = 0.0167 and offered n = 6 (2⁻⁶ = 0.0156).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh` (bead N30a-35) performs the three protocol mutations from the user's side and
a legitimate second attempt. Logged: the spent-set entries (chunk hash prefixes), the lineage identity, each
attempt's index and level, refusal codes with repairs, and the ledger sequences of freeze, read and spend.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-734 (read before freeze; refit without a new version; holdout reuse: each refused). The level identity
Σ α/[i·(i + 1)] = α·m/(m + 1) (a law tested exactly). Negative controls: a spent set keyed by dataset name
instead of chunk hash (the re-admission case passes and the test must fail); an attempt counter the caller can
reset by renaming the claim without changing its content (claim identity is a content hash of the decision,
metric and margin); a constant level for every attempt. Chaos (tier G4): kill between scoring and spending.
Determinism: class D0 throughout.

## Considerations

- The first attempt runs at α/2, not α: the series is the price of allowing any retry at all, and it is paid
  from the start, as in the qualification lane.
- A refit is detected from ledger order alone; a refit done outside the system and imported as a "new prior"
  would evade it. Card versions without Ground provenance that appear in a lineage after a scored event are
  therefore also treated as refits for that cell.
- Fresh sealed data is expensive on a bench; the reachability pre-analysis at the reduced level tells the
  operator before acquiring it whether the attempt can succeed.
- A spent holdout released to calibration improves the next fit; one kept in the regression set guards against
  regressions of the simulator. The default favours calibration because real data is scarce.
