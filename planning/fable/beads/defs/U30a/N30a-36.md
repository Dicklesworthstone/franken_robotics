## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) can attach a **reality band** to a simulated metric: an interval around the
simulated value that covers the real outcome with a stated probability. It is the one quantitative statement
about reality the project makes, and it is deliberately narrow (bet BET-28: within one calibrated robot and
task family, conformal bands on the gap between simulation and measurement reach their stated coverage;
nothing is claimed for designs that were never built).

**Definition.** Inside one cell (one set of physical units, controller, firmware, surface, task family and
envelope of conditions), calibration pairs (ŷ_i, y_i) are a simulated prediction and the real outcome under the
same registered conditions. The residual is r_i = |y_i − ŷ_i| for a two-sided band, or r_i = ŷ_i − y_i for a
one-sided statement "real is at least sim minus q̂" on a performance metric. The half-width q̂ is the
⌈(n+1)·(1−α)⌉-th smallest residual, and the band is infinite when that index exceeds n. The implementation is
the sibling's `fs-eproc::hardening::MondrianConformal` with the cell as the bucket and a minimum calibration
count of 19 (TARGET; the sibling's own fixture uses 19, FACT).

**What the evidence supports** (FACT: split conformal prediction, https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511, and
arithmetic from its coverage law).
- Marginal coverage of at least 1 − α holds for any simulator quality, provided test draws are exchangeable
  with the calibration draws. A poor simulator gives a wide band, not a wrong one.
- Conditional on the calibration set, realized coverage is distributed Beta(n + 1 − l, l) with l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋:

  | n | Target | Mean coverage | Standard deviation | P(coverage < 0.80) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 20 | 0.90 | 0.905 | 0.063 | 0.069 |
  | 200 | 0.90 | 0.900 | 0.021 | below 0.001 |

- A finite band needs n ≥ (1 − α)/α: 9 at 90%, 19 at 95%, 99 at 99%. At n = 20 a 95% band is the largest
  residual seen, and any level above 20/21 = 95.2% is unbounded.

Rejected alternatives: a Gaussian interval from the residual mean and variance (wrong when residuals are
skewed, and it claims coverage it cannot guarantee); a band from simulation-only uncertainty (tier agreement
and randomization say nothing about reality); a learned residual model that extrapolates to other cells.

## Scope

In: residual definitions; the quantile and its three outcomes; the band artifact; the coverage law as a
function; proof obligation PO-GROUND-01. Out: who may be given a band and the exchangeable unit (N30a-37); how

## Technical approach

1. **Calibration set.** The cell's pool (N30a-07: keyed by cell, `ModelId` and origin) for one metric and
   band scope: residuals
   emitted by scoring from non-interfered trials of frozen predictions, whose conditions came from a
   pre-registered draw. The calibration set is the band's only calibration source; nothing from simulation
   alone enters it.
2. **Residuals.** ŷ_i is the simulated point prediction registered for trial i (the registered median, or the
   interval midpoint). Two-sided: r_i = |y_i − ŷ_i|. One-sided (performance metric, higher is better):
   r_i = ŷ_i − y_i, which is positive when the simulation over-predicts and may be negative.
3. **Quantile** (`band::quantile(residuals, α, min_count) -> Quantile`). n = count; k = ⌈(n+1)·(1−α)⌉ computed
   on α as an exact rational. The minimum count is a parameter because the two band scopes of N30a-37 differ:
   19 for a `within-units` pool of episodes (TARGET, the sibling's fixture value), and ⌈(1 − α)/α⌉ for an
   `across-units` pool of one residual per unit (9 at 90%, the plan's "at least 9 calibrated units"). It is
   passed to `MondrianConformal` as its minimum calibration count. Outcomes:
   - n < min_count: `Refused` with `FROB-REF-889` (a band for an uncalibrated cell, or with too few calibration
     points; repair offered: samples present and samples needed; exit code 3).
   - k > n: `Infinite` (the level is above n/(n+1)); the band is unbounded.
   - otherwise `Finite(q̂)`, q̂ the k-th smallest residual (ties need no special handling).
   The call goes through `ConformalJob` (N30a-06) with the cell's bucket string and is cross-checked against
   the local reference implementation; a mismatch is a hard error.
4. **The statement.** Two-sided: the real outcome lies in [ŷ − q̂, ŷ + q̂] with probability at least 1 − α for
   a new draw exchangeable with the calibration draws. One-sided: the real outcome is at least ŷ − q̂.
5. **Artifact** `frob.ground.band/1` (DESIGN): cell, metric with unit, side, α, n, k, q̂ (or `unbounded`), the
   hash of the sorted residual list, dataset prefix, `ModelId` and its model lineage, the calibration epoch, and the coverage
   law's parameters. Ledger event `ground.band`. A `Finite` band moves the cell's status to
   `calibrated(cell, n, level)`; an `Infinite` one is recorded and printed as `band=unbounded` and leaves the
   status at `candidate` (DESIGN: an unbounded band calibrates nothing).
6. **Coverage law** (`band::coverage_law(n, α) -> CoverageLaw { a, b, mean, sd, p_below(x) }`): with
   l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋, a = n + 1 − l, b = l; mean a/(a + b); variance a·b/((a + b)²·(a + b + 1)); p_below(x) the
   Beta distribution function. At n = 20, α = 0.10: Beta(19, 2), mean 19/21 = 0.9048, sd 0.0626,
   p_below(0.80) = 0.8¹⁹·(20·0.2 + 0.8) = 0.0692. At n = 200: Beta(181, 20), mean 0.9005, sd 0.0211.
7. **PO-GROUND-01** (proof obligation): the quantile index ⌈(n+1)·(1−α)⌉ and the infinite-band rule give
   coverage at least 1 − α for exchangeable draws. Argument on file (`docs/derivations/ground_band.md`): for
   n + 1 exchangeable residuals the rank of the last among all is uniform on 1..n+1, so it is at most the k-th
   smallest of the first n with probability at least k/(n+1) ≥ 1 − α; when k > n the bound is +∞ and coverage
   is 1. Tested over 10⁴ exchangeable resamples; it is a candidate for a theorem card of the proof subsystem.
8. **Class.** A band is class Estimated, deterministic and exact given its residuals (a sort and an index).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Index arithmetic: n = 20 gives k = 19 at α = 0.10 and k = 20 at α = 0.05; α = 0.04 gives k = 21 > 20,
      `Infinite`; n = 8 is refused with 889 reporting 8 present and 19 needed.
- [ ] PO-GROUND-01: over 10⁴ resamples of n + 1 exchangeable residuals (independent draws from a skewed
      distribution, and a non-independent exchangeable construction with a shared random scale), the fraction
      of test residuals at or below q̂ is at least 1 − α minus three standard errors, for (n, α) in
      {(19, 0.05), (20, 0.10), (200, 0.10), (19, 0.01)}; the last is an infinite-band case (k = 20 > 19),
      where coverage is 1.
- [ ] Coverage-law numbers: 0.9048, 0.0626 and 0.0692 at n = 20; 0.9005 and 0.0211 at n = 200, to the digits
      shown.
- [ ] Simulated known answer: calibrating on n = 20 and measuring realized coverage on a large test set, over
      1,000 resamples: mean within 0.01 of 19/21 and the fraction below 0.80 within 0.03 of 0.069.
- [ ] The sibling's quantile and the local reference agree on every call; a one-sided band can have a negative
      q̂ and prints the statement correctly.
- [ ] A finite band changes the status to `calibrated`; an unbounded one does not.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-728 (n = 20, target 0.90, 1,000 resamples: mean coverage 19/21 = 0.905; fraction below 0.80 about 0.069;
control: a pooled shifted regime covers 16 of 20). Negative controls: the index ⌈n·(1−α)⌉ (18 at n = 20: mean
coverage 18/21 = 0.857, caught by the 0.01 tolerance); a quantile that returns the largest residual when
k > n in place of infinity (PO-GROUND-01 fails at (19, 0.01): realized coverage 19/20 = 0.95 against a bound
of 0.99 minus three standard errors, about 0.987); a band computed from calibration-fit residuals
instead of scored predictions. Determinism: class D0 given the residual list; resampling tests are seeded and
identical twice.

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-BAND (decided at M5): if the band-coverage gate or the drift gate fails, bands are withdrawn
  and reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual table.
- Coverage is marginal: on average over calibration draws. At n = 20 any one calibration set realizes a
  coverage that varies by about six points; the report sentence says so (N30a-38).
- The minimum count of 19 is a TARGET that also makes a 95% band finite; at 90% a finite band exists from
  n = 9, and cells with 9 to 18 episodes are still refused for `within-units`. An `across-units` pool uses the
  finite-band minimum itself (9 units at 90%), as the plan states; its draws are units, which are scarcer.
- The band says nothing conditional on a particular condition inside the cell; residual structure against
  condition (reported by scoring) is the warning that the cell is too wide.
