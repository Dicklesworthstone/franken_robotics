## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) issues conformal reality bands per calibrated cell. A correct band can still
mislead through the way it is printed: without its n a band at n = 20 reads like one at n = 2,000; without its
level it reads as certain; beside a result of a stronger evidence class it borrows that class; and twenty bands
on one scorecard are each right "90% of the time" while the scorecard as a whole is almost surely wrong
somewhere. Three of the plan's four band rules are about printing, and they are here.

- **Rule 2.** A simulated metric in a calibrated cell is reported as [ŷ − q̂, ŷ + q̂] with n, the level, and
  the sentence "coverage is marginal over calibration draws; at this n realized coverage varies by about ±(one
  standard deviation from the table)".
- **Rule 3.** A band never raises an evidence class. It is Estimated, and in a claim product the weakest input
  wins.
- **Rule 4.** A scorecard with many banded metrics spends an explicit false-coverage budget: each claim
  reserves its share up front (the sibling's `admission_alpha`) and violations are flagged by e-BH (the
  sibling's `fcr_flag`).

A fourth point is about what the band is worth. **Paired simulation buys little hardware time.** One study
reports savings of 20–25% of hardware evaluation effort for similar bounds, and that with low correlation
between simulation and reality, real-only evaluation gives the tightest bounds
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354; FACT, source register). The ceiling of the benefit on real-trial count is
therefore about 1.3, stated before any test. Ground reports the measured correlation in each cell and
recommends real-only evaluation when it is below 0.5 (TARGET). More generally: Ground has no lever on time or
compute. It is an honesty mechanism, and its only possible saving, on real-trial count, is bounded near 1.3.

## Scope

In: the printed form of a banded metric; the coverage sentence; class handling in the claim product; the
false-coverage budget and its flags; the per-cell correlation and the real-only recommendation; the ceiling
statement. Out: computing q̂ (N30a-36); authorization (N30a-37); the monitors that produce the e-values
(N30a-41, N30a-42, a dependency: the miss monitor's e-value is each claim's input to the flags); the
retirement a flag causes and the status listing that prints the correlation (N30a-43); the `reality:` line's
grammar (N30a-02).

## Technical approach

1. **Printed form** (`band::render(prediction ŷ, band) -> BandedMetric`). Two-sided:
   `<metric> = ŷ, reality band [ŷ − q̂, ŷ + q̂] <unit> (n = <n>, level <1−α>)`. One-sided:
   `real <metric> ≥ ŷ − q̂ <unit> (n = <n>, level <1−α>)`. An unbounded band prints `band unbounded at this n
   and level`. The statement's line is
   `reality: calibrated [cell=<id> n=<n> level=<1−α> band=<q̂><unit> dataset=<prefix>]`.
2. **The sentence.** Appended to every banded statement, generated from the coverage law (N30a-36): "coverage
   is marginal over calibration draws; at this n realized coverage varies by about ±<sd>", with sd the
   standard deviation of Beta(n + 1 − l, l), l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋, printed in percentage points (±6.3 at n = 20 and
   level 0.90; ±2.1 at n = 200). The JSON form carries `coverage_mean` and `coverage_sd` as numbers.
3. **Class.** `band::claim(metric_claim, band) -> ClaimProduct` combines the simulated metric's claim product
   with the band's (kind Reality, class Estimated) by the workspace's `combine`, so the banded statement's
   class is the weakest of its inputs, which is at most Estimated. The simulated metric's own claim (for
   example a Fidelity result of class Tested) is still printed separately with its own class; nothing lets
   the banded statement inherit it. A compile-fail test pins that `BandedMetric` has no constructor taking a
   class.
4. **False-coverage budget.** A scorecard or report with m banded metrics declares a total budget α_total
   (default 0.10). Before any band is computed, each claim j reserves a share α_j through `admission_alpha`
   with Σ_j α_j ≤ α_total (default equal shares; a declared weighting is allowed and recorded). Each band is
   then issued at level 1 − α_j. Consequence shown to the user at reservation time: the smallest n that gives
   a finite band at α_j is ⌈(1 − α_j)/α_j⌉ (five metrics at α_total = 0.10 need n ≥ 49 each); a claim whose
   cell is below that but at or above the pool's minimum count prints `band unbounded` and is not counted as
   banded (below the minimum count, 19 for a `within-units` pool, the quantile of N30a-36 refuses with
   FROB-REF-889 first, and the claim prints that refusal). Requesting m bands at 1 − α_total
   each on one scorecard is refused with the reservation it should have made.
5. **Flags (e-BH).** As outcomes arrive, each claim j has an e-value e_j against "this band covers at its
   level" (the miss-counting e-process of N30a-42). `fcr_flag` applies the e-BH rule at level δ (default
   α_d = 0.05): sort the e-values in decreasing order e_(1) ≥ … ≥ e_(m); let k* be the largest k with
   e_(k) ≥ m/(k·δ); flag the k* claims with the largest e-values. The scorecard prints flagged claims as
   `coverage-violated`, and each flag is appended as the ledger event
   `ground.flagged { claim, band, e, rank, m, delta }`. The retirement a flag causes is performed by the
   retirement transaction of N30a-43, which depends on this bead and reacts to the event with reason
   `coverage-flag`; this bead only flags (so that the monitors, the flags and the retirement form a chain,
   N30a-42 then this bead then N30a-43, and not a cycle).
6. **Correlation.** Per cell and metric, from the calibration pairs (ŷ_i, y_i): Pearson's r with a 90%
   interval by the Fisher transform (atanh r ± 1.645/√(n − 3)) and Spearman's ρ. Printed by
   `ground.band --status` and in the band artifact. If the point estimate of r is below 0.5 (TARGET), the
   result carries `next_actions`: "real-only evaluation recommended in this cell: simulation and reality
   correlate at r = <r>; pairing with simulation will not tighten the bound". A band is still issued if
   authorized; the recommendation concerns how further trials are best used.
7. **The ceiling statement.** `ground.band --status` and the replication kit's documentation print, once:
   "Reported savings from pairing real trials with simulation are 20–25% of hardware evaluation effort; the
   benefit on real-trial count is at most about 1.3." For orientation (arithmetic of a control-variate
   estimator, not a result of the cited study): a paired estimator's variance scales with 1 − r², so r = 0.5
   saves 25% of trials, a factor of 1.33.
8. **Folded from the superseded bead N30b-07.** A share too small for a finite band at the cell's n
   (α_j < 1/(n + 1)) gives an unbounded band, shown as unbounded and never as a narrower one. Under equal
   shares the number of metrics that can carry finite bands is at most α_total·(n + 1): two at n = 20 and
   α_total = 0.10, issued at level 0.95. A flagged claim stays flagged on every later rendering of the
   scorecard. The page-level arithmetic that motivates the budget: five independent bands at 0.90 would miss
   somewhere with probability 1 − 0.9⁵ = 0.41; the e-BH flag itself is valid under arbitrary dependence. A
   report that states a saving in real trials from pairing with simulation prints the ceiling 1.3 and the
   measured correlation beside it, and a claimed saving above the ceiling is refused by the report lint. A
   correlation near zero does not make the band wrong; it makes it as wide as the outcome's own spread, and
   the recommendation says so in those words.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A crossing time ŷ = 4.21 s with q̂ = 0.12 s at n = 20, level 0.90 prints
      `crossing_time_s = 4.21, reality band [4.09, 4.33] s (n = 20, level 0.90)` followed by the sentence with
      ±6.3 points, and the line `reality: calibrated [… n=20 level=0.90 band=0.12s …]`.
- [ ] The banded statement's class is Estimated when the simulated metric's class is Tested, and no public
      constructor can make it otherwise.
- [ ] Five banded metrics at α_total = 0.10 are issued at level 0.98 each; `within-units` cells with
      19 ≤ n < 49 print `band unbounded` and cells with n < 19 print the 889 refusal; a request for five bands
      at 0.90 on one scorecard is refused with the reservation.
- [ ] e-BH on e-values (50, 25, 5, 1) with m = 4 and δ = 0.1 flags exactly the first two (thresholds 40, 20,
      13.3, 10).
- [ ] A cell with r = 0.31 carries the real-only recommendation; one with r = 0.82 does not; the interval is
      printed in both.
- [ ] The ceiling statement appears in the status listing.
- [ ] A scorecard with two banded metrics at α_total = 0.10 and n = 20 shows two finite bands at level 0.95;
      with five it shows five unbounded bands and says why.
- [ ] A planted claim that misses on later data is flagged, and the flag persists on later renderings.
- [ ] A report asserting a 2× saving in real trials from pairing is refused by the lint.

### Known answers and negative controls

The coverage standard deviations (from KA-728's law); the e-BH toy case; the Fisher interval. Negative
controls: a renderer that omits n and the level (the exact-text test fails); a claim combiner that returns the
simulated metric's class; a scorecard that issues every band at 1 − α_total (the budget test fails); an e-BH
that flags every e-value above 1/δ regardless of m. Determinism: text and arithmetic are class D0.
Folded from N30b-07: planted failure: a scorecard that issues all five metrics at 0.90 (page-level false
coverage about 0.41 if independent, against a budget of 0.10).

## Considerations

- Equal shares are the default because a weighting chosen after seeing which cells are well calibrated would
  itself be a selection; a declared weighting must be frozen with the scorecard's definition.
- The budget makes multi-metric scorecards expensive in real trials (49 per cell for five metrics at 0.10).
  That is the true price of the joint statement, and the alternative offered is fewer banded metrics.
- The 0.5 correlation threshold and the statement of the ceiling are TARGETs from one study, to be re-read
  when a second source or the project's own data exists.
- If the kill gate KILL-BAND fires, the renderer's fallback is the raw residual table under
  `reality: no-claim`; rules 3 and 4 then have nothing to apply to.
