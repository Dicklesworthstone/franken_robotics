## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) attaches a reality band to a cell: particular physical units, a controller and
firmware, a surface, a task family, an envelope of conditions, compared against a particular model identity
through particular instruments. The band's guarantee is about draws exchangeable with the calibration draws.
Some events end that exchangeability for certain, with no statistics needed: the firmware was updated; a
gearbox was replaced. Step 9 of the calibration procedure retires a calibration when such an event is seen.
The plan's trigger table has four rows; two are statistical (rank drift and miss counting, N30a-41 and
N30a-42) and two are deterministic, and those are this bead:

| Trigger | Mechanism | Fires |
|---|---|---|
| Scope change | Controller or firmware identity changes; a part is replaced or re-lubricated; the surface class changes; a recorded condition leaves the envelope; `ModelId` changes, including a kernel change that alters bits; an instrument's calibration identity changes | Immediately and deterministically |
| Expiry | Age or operating hours since calibration | At the limit |

Rejected alternatives: waiting for the statistical monitors to notice a firmware change (at n = 20 they see
only gross shifts, and a firmware change is known, not inferred); treating a re-lubrication as "the same
robot" (joint friction is exactly what identification measured).

## Scope

In: detection of each scope-change kind, at ingest and at use; the maintenance declaration; expiry; the
retirement reason emitted. Out: what retirement does (N30a-43); the statistical monitors; the cell type and its
diff (N30a-07).

## Technical approach

1. **What a calibration records** (in the band artifact and the cell's calibration epoch): the `CellKey`; the
   `ModelId` of the simulated side and the digest of its replay (which changes if a kernel change alters
   bits); the calibration identity of every instrument that feeds the banded metric; the latest acquisition
   date of the calibration data; each unit's operating-hours counter at that date.
2. **At ingest** (automatic for every dataset whose declared cell overlaps a calibrated one in design and
   units). `drift::scope_check(calibration, manifest) -> Vec<RetireReason>`:
   - `CellKey::diff` between the calibrated cell and the dataset's declared cell: a difference in
     `controller`, `firmware` or `surface` fires `ScopeChange(Controller | Firmware | Surface)`;
   - any episode whose recorded conditions are not contained in the envelope fires
     `ScopeChange(Envelope { name, value })`;
   - a maintenance entry for a calibrated unit, declared in the manifest as
     `[[rig.maintenance]] unit = "unit-07"; part = "knee-L gearbox"; action = "replaced" | "re-lubricated";
     on = "2027-04-02"` (DESIGN field), dated after the calibration, fires `ScopeChange(Part { unit, part,
     action })`;
   - an instrument that feeds the banded metric with a `calibration` identity different from the recorded one
     fires `ScopeChange(Instrument { name, from, to })`.
   A dataset that differs in `design` or adds a unit not in the cell is not a scope change of this
   calibration; it belongs to another cell and its data lands there.
3. **At use** (every `ground.band` request and every report that cites a band).
   `drift::model_check(calibration, request)`: the request's `ModelId` must equal the recorded one and the
   replay digest of the band's reference world address must equal the recorded digest. A different `ModelId`
   (a new card version, a design edit) or a changed digest (a kernel build that alters bits) fires
   `ScopeChange(ModelId)`. Kernel-library updates also trigger a sweep: every calibration whose replay digest
   no longer reproduces is retired at once, not on its next use.
4. **Expiry.** Limits from `registries/ground/expiry.toml`: `max_age_days = 90`, `max_operating_hours = 200`,
   `on_part_replacement = "always"` (the defaults of open question OPEN-GROUND-3, marked `default` until the
   Ground lead decides). `drift::expiry_check(calibration, now, hours_now)`: fires `Expired { age_days }` when
   the ledger date minus the calibration date exceeds the age limit, or `Expired { hours }` when a unit's
   operating-hours counter (from the latest manifest for that unit) minus its value at calibration exceeds the
   hours limit, whichever comes first. "Now" is the ledger's date, an explicit input, so the check is
   reproducible. Evaluated at ingest, at use, and by a daily daemon sweep.
5. **Determinism and order.** All checks are exact (class D0) and total: given the same calibration record
   and the same manifest or request they return the same list. When several fire, all are recorded and the
   first in the order Part, Firmware, Controller, Surface, Instrument, Envelope, ModelId, Expired is the
   headline reason.
6. **Hand-off.** Each fired reason is passed to `status::advance` (N30a-02) as a retirement event; the effects
   are N30a-43's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every scope change fires (seven planted cases, each on its own calibrated synthetic cell): controller
      hash changed; firmware `2.4.1` to `2.4.2`; a part replaced; a part re-lubricated; surface
      `sealed-concrete/dry` to `sealed-concrete/wet`; an episode at 26 °C against an envelope of [18, 24] °C;
      an instrument calibration `cal-2027-02` to `cal-2027-05`. Each retires immediately with its reason.
- [ ] A new card version, and a kernel build whose replay digest differs, each fire `ScopeChange(ModelId)`; a
      kernel build with an identical digest does not.
- [ ] Expiry: at 91 days the calibration is retired by age; at 201 operating hours by hours; at 89 days and
      199 hours it is not.
- [ ] A dataset from a different unit of the same design retires nothing.
- [ ] The same inputs give the same reasons twice, and the checks read no clock other than the ledger date.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/scope_triggers.rs`: `controller_change_fires`; `firmware_change_fires`;
`part_replaced_fires`; `part_relubricated_fires`; `surface_change_fires`; `condition_leaves_envelope_fires`
(26.0 against [18, 24]; 24.0 does not); `instrument_calibration_change_fires`;
`unrelated_instrument_change_does_not_fire` (an instrument that does not feed the banded metric);
`new_card_version_fires_model_id`; `kernel_digest_change_fires`; `same_digest_kernel_update_does_not_fire`;
`other_unit_is_other_cell`; `expiry_by_age_91_days`; `expiry_by_hours_201`; `no_expiry_at_89_days_199_hours`;
`maintenance_before_calibration_does_not_fire`; `headline_reason_order`; `checks_are_pure_functions`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_drift.sh` (bead N30a-44) performs the seven scope changes and the two expiry cases through
`frob ground ingest` and `frob ground band`. Logged per case: the calibrated cell, the changed component with
old and new values, the retirement reason, the ledger sequence of the retirement, and the reality line before
and after.

### Known answers and negative controls

The gate's clause "every scope change fires" (GATE-GROUND-07). Negative controls: a scope check that compares
`CellId`s only and reports "changed" without the component (the reason assertions fail); a check that ignores
the maintenance log; an expiry check that reads the wall clock (a test that advances the ledger date and not
the system clock must still fire). Determinism: class D0; twice, debug and release.

## Considerations

- Open question OPEN-GROUND-3 is not settled. The defaults (90 days, 200 hours, always on part replacement)
  hold until the Ground lead decides; the question blocks only the first real cell (GATE-GROUND-10), not the
  rehearsal.
- Operating hours and maintenance are declared by the operator in manifests. An undeclared replacement cannot
  be seen by this bead; the statistical monitors are the backstop, and they are weak at small n. The dataset
  manifest's trial counter and the maintenance log are therefore required fields for a calibrated cell's
  later datasets (missing: refused at admission, as for any missing provenance).
- A kernel change that alters bits retires every band at once. That is intended: the simulated side of every
  residual has changed. The old real data has already been read, so new predictions against it would not be
  frozen before the read; a bit-changing kernel update therefore costs a recalibration on fresh sealed data,
  and the sweep's report states that cost (which cells, how many trials) before the update is adopted.
