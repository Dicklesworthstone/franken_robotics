## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) issues a reality band whose guarantee holds while new episodes are exchangeable
with the calibration episodes. Robots drift: bearings wear, lubricant ages, floors get dusty. Step 9 of the
calibration procedure watches for that and retires the calibration when it is seen. Deterministic triggers
(scope changes, expiry) are N30a-40. This bead is the first of two statistical monitors.

**Rank drift.** Each new residual is ranked in the frozen calibration residuals. Under exchangeability the
normalized rank is uniform. The sibling's `DriftMonitor` bets against that in both directions and averages the
two e-processes; the calibration is retired when the e-value reaches 1/α_d, with α_d = 0.05 (TARGET).

An *e-process* is a nonnegative process whose expectation is at most 1 at every stopping time under the null
hypothesis; by Ville's inequality the probability that it ever reaches 1/α is at most α. That is why **the
monitors are read after every episode without inflating error**, and why a monitor can be stopped the moment
it alarms.

Decision ADR-GROUND-09: drift is tested on ranks and misses, not on raw distributions by kernel discrepancy or
optimal transport, because those tests have no power at about twenty scalar residuals. A fact about power that
must be printed so that a quiet monitor is not misread: the rank monitor's slack is δ = max(1/√n, 0.02): 0.224
at n = 20 and 0.071 at n = 200 (FACT). At n = 20 it sees only gross shifts.

## Scope

## Technical approach

1. **Monitoring stream.** After a band is issued for (cell, metric, lineage), every later episode admitted for
   that cell contributes one monitoring residual: r_new = |y − ŷ| (or the one-sided form), with y the measured
   outcome and ŷ the simulated prediction at the episode's recorded conditions under the band's model
   identity. This is automatic on ingest. Monitoring residuals never enter the calibration pool: the pool is
   fed only by scored frozen predictions.
2. **Normalized rank.** With frozen sorted calibration residuals r_(1) ≤ … ≤ r_(n):
   u = (L + V·(T + 1))/(n + 1), where L = #{i : r_(i) < r_new}, T = #{i : r_(i) = r_new}, and V is a uniform
   draw on (0, 1) keyed by the episode's address (randomized tie-breaking, reproducible). Under
   exchangeability u is uniform on (0, 1).
3. **The monitor** (through `DriftJob` and `DriftStep`, N30a-06). State: the frozen sample's identity, n, the
   slack δ = max(1/√n, 0.02), two wealth values E⁺ (bets that ranks are high: residuals growing) and E⁻ (bets
   that ranks are low: residuals shrinking, which also signals a changed regime), and the count of steps. The
   form Ground expects (DESIGN; the adapter's fact re-check pins the sibling's actual betting rule against
   it): E⁺ ← E⁺·(1 + λ⁺·(u − ½ − δ)) and E⁻ ← E⁻·(1 + λ⁻·(½ − δ − u)), with predictable bets
   0 ≤ λ± < 1/(½ + δ) chosen before u is seen; the reported e-value is E = (E⁺ + E⁻)/2, an average of two
   e-processes and therefore an e-process. The slack absorbs the fact that ranks are computed against a
   finite calibration sample.
4. **Alarm.** When E ≥ 1/α_d (20 at the default α_d = 0.05) the monitor emits `RetireReason::RankDrift` with
   the step, E⁺, E⁻ and the direction. The level α_d is a parameter of the cell's calibration (from the
   decision model's loss ratio when one is declared; otherwise 0.05) and is frozen with the band.
5. **State and reads.** One monitor per (cell, metric, lineage, band). Its state after each episode is a
   ledger event `ground.monitor { band, step, e_plus, e_minus, e }`; `procedure::state` and
   `ground.band --status` read the latest. A new band starts a new monitor at E⁺ = E⁻ = 1; nothing carries
   over a recalibration.
6. **PO-GROUND-03 (this monitor's half).** The false-alarm rate is at most α_d under optional stopping.
   Tested by 10⁴ null runs stopped adversarially: calibration of size n and a stream of 500 further residuals
   from the same continuous distribution (three shapes: half-normal, lognormal, uniform), the run stopped at
   the first step with E ≥ 1/α_d; the fraction of runs that alarm must be at most α_d plus three standard
   errors (0.0565 at 10⁴ runs), at n = 20 and n = 200.
7. **Power, stated.** `drift::rank_power_note(n)` returns the text printed by the status listing: "rank
   monitor slack δ = <δ>; at this n it detects only shifts that move the mean normalized rank beyond
   <½ + δ>". Arithmetic on the expected form at n = 20: even if every new residual exceeds every calibration
   residual (u ≈ 1), the per-step factor is at most 1 + 0.276/0.724 = 1.38, so E⁺ needs at least
   ln 40/ln 1.38 ≈ 12 such episodes for the average to reach 20 (more when the betting rule starts below its
   largest bet; the 15-episode acceptance below is checked against the sibling's actual rule by the fact
   re-check).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Slack values: 0.2236 at n = 20, 0.0707 at n = 200, 0.02 at n = 10⁴.
- [ ] Normalized ranks of 10⁵ exchangeable draws are uniform (Kolmogorov distance below 0.01), with ties
      handled by address-keyed randomization that is reproducible.
- [ ] PO-GROUND-03: false-alarm fraction at most 0.0565 over 10⁴ adversarially stopped null runs, for each of
      the three distributions and both n.
- [ ] A gross upward shift (every new residual above the calibration maximum) alarms at n = 20 within 15
      episodes; a gross downward shift alarms through E⁻; a 10% scale increase at n = 20 does not alarm within
      100 episodes, and the power note says why.
- [ ] Monitoring residuals never appear in the calibration pool; a new band resets the monitor.
- [ ] The alarm carries step, direction, E⁺ and E⁻, and the state is recoverable from ledger events alone.

re-check: on a scripted rank sequence the sibling's wealth stays within the bounds the expected form implies).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_drift.sh` (bead N30a-44): the planted friction increase and the null runs through
`frob ground ingest`. Logged per episode: the residual, its normalized rank, E⁺, E⁻, E and the threshold; at
the alarm, the step and the reason; for the null batch, the false-alarm fraction.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-730 (drift: friction raised by 30% after episode k; the calibration is retired within 20 further episodes;
control: null runs, false retirement at most 5%) is decided by the two monitors together. Negative controls:
a monitor with no slack (δ = 0), whose null false-alarm rate exceeds α_d because ranks against a small frozen
sample are not independent; a monitor read as a fixed-sample p-value at each step and alarmed on any step
below 0.05; ranks computed without tie randomization on discretized residuals. Determinism: draws by address;
wealth arithmetic in double precision in a fixed order; bitwise twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- This monitor cannot tell drift from an unlucky calibration draw; both mean the band is not doing its job,
  and both call for recalibration.
- A downward alarm (the simulation is now better than the band says) also retires: the regime changed, and a
  tighter band may be justified only by recalibrating.
- The expected form in step 3 is this bead's reading of "bets against that in both directions and averages
  the two e-processes"; if the sibling's rule differs in detail, the property tests (null rate, alarms on
  gross shift) are what must hold, and the contract records the sibling's actual rule.
- Kill gate KILL-BAND (decided at M5): if the drift gate fails, bands are withdrawn; no band, no monitor.
