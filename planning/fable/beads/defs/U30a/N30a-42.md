## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) watches each calibrated cell for evidence that its reality band has stopped
covering. Two statistical monitors run on the stream of new episodes; both are e-processes, read after every
episode without inflating error. The rank monitor (N30a-41) looks at where new residuals fall among the
calibration residuals. This bead is the second, which looks only at whether each new outcome fell outside the
band:

**Miss counting.** A likelihood-ratio e-process on band misses, with null hypothesis "miss rate ≤ p₀(n)" and
alternative p₁ = 0.5; the calibration is retired when the e-value reaches 1/α_d (α_d = 0.05, TARGET).

Why the null is not the nominal miss rate α: conditional on the calibration set that happened to be drawn, a
band's true miss rate is random. It follows Beta(l, n + 1 − l) with l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋. A band at a 90% target from
n = 20 points honestly has a miss rate anywhere from a few percent to over 20%. Testing against 10% would retire
perfectly healthy calibrations. So p₀(n) is the 95th percentile of the conditional miss rate: about 0.22 at
n = 20 and about 0.14 at n = 200 for a 90% band (FACT, arithmetic from the Beta law).

The arithmetic at n = 20 (FACT): a miss multiplies the evidence by 2.27 and a cover by 0.641, so four misses in
a row give 26.7 and retire the calibration, and three give 11.7 and do not. This monitor cannot tell drift from
an unlucky calibration draw. Both mean the band under-covers, and both call for recalibration.

## Scope

In: p₀(n, α); the e-process, its alarm and state; its proof obligation; its power statement; the e-value it
supplies to the false-coverage flags. Out: the monitoring stream and rank monitor (N30a-41); retirement
effects (N30a-43); the e-BH rule (N30a-38).

## Technical approach

1. **Miss indicator.** For each monitoring episode t after the band was issued (the stream defined in
   N30a-41): m_t = 1 if the new residual exceeds q̂ (the outcome is outside the band), else 0. With an
   unbounded band no monitor runs.
2. **The null rate.** `drift::p0(n, α) -> Rational`: with l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋, the 0.95 quantile of
   Beta(l, n + 1 − l), computed to 10⁻¹² by bisection on the regularized incomplete beta function and then
   rounded **up** to two decimals (DESIGN: rounding up is conservative and reproduces the plan's figures).
   n = 20, α = 0.10: Beta(2, 19); the distribution function is 1 − (1 − x)¹⁹·(1 + 19·x), which reaches 0.95 at
   x = 0.2161, so p₀ = 0.22. n = 200: Beta(20, 181), quantile near 0.136, so p₀ = 0.14. For l = 0 (a level so
   high that the band is unbounded) the function is undefined and returns an error.
3. **The e-process.** E₀ = 1; E_t = E_{t−1}·(p₁/p₀) if m_t = 1, and E_{t−1}·((1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀)) if m_t = 0,
   with p₁ = 0.5. At p₀ = 0.22 the factors are 0.5/0.22 = 2.2727 and 0.5/0.78 = 0.6410. For any true miss rate
   p ≤ p₀ the expected factor is p·p₁/p₀ + (1 − p)·(1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀) ≤ 1 (it is linear and increasing in p and
   equals 1 at p = p₀), so E is a nonnegative supermartingale under the whole null, and by Ville's inequality
   the probability that it ever reaches 1/α_d is at most α_d.
4. **Alarm.** E_t ≥ 1/α_d (20 by default) emits `RetireReason::MissDrift { step, misses, covers, e }`.
5. **State.** One monitor per (cell, metric, lineage, band); ledger event
   `ground.monitor { band, kind: "miss", step, misses, e }` per episode; reset by a new band. The current
   e-value is also the claim's e-value for the scorecard's false-coverage flags (N30a-38).
6. **PO-GROUND-03 (this monitor's half).** The false-alarm rate is at most α_d under optional stopping:
   10⁴ runs of independent Bernoulli misses at rate exactly p₀ (the boundary of the null), each run of length
   2,000, stopped at the first step with E ≥ 1/α_d; the alarm fraction must be at most α_d plus three standard
   errors (0.0565). A second battery draws a fresh calibration set per run and uses its realized miss rate
   (the unconditional null of exchangeability) with streams of 100 episodes: the retirement fraction must be
   at most 0.05 (the gate's figure).
7. **Power, stated.** The log e-value grows on average only when the true miss rate exceeds
   p* = ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) / ln(p₁·(1 − p₀)/(p₀·(1 − p₁))): 0.351 at n = 20, 0.299 at n = 200 (p₀ = 0.14).
   `drift::miss_power_note(n, α)` returns the text for the status listing: "miss monitor: null miss rate
   p₀ = <p₀>; a miss multiplies evidence by <a>, a cover by <b>; <k> straight misses retire; it gains evidence
   only when the true miss rate exceeds <p*>". At n = 20: 2.27, 0.641, k = 4, p* = 0.35.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] p₀(20, 0.10) = 0.22 (from 0.2161) and p₀(200, 0.10) = 0.14.
- [ ] Miss-monitor arithmetic at n = 20, p₀ = 0.22, p₁ = 0.5: four straight misses give 26.7 and retire; three
      give 11.7 and do not; miss, cover, miss, miss, miss gives 17.1 and does not; one more miss gives 38.9.
- [ ] PO-GROUND-03: alarm fraction at most 0.0565 over 10⁴ adversarially stopped runs at miss rate p₀; at most
      0.05 retirement over 10⁴ exchangeable null runs with fresh calibration sets.
- [ ] With a true miss rate of 0.6 the monitor retires within 20 episodes in at least 90% of 10³ runs; with
      0.3 at n = 20 it does not grow on average, and the power note says so.
- [ ] No monitor runs on an unbounded band; a new band resets the e-value to 1.
- [ ] The state is recoverable from ledger events, and permuting a fixed set of misses and covers leaves the
      final e-value unchanged.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-731 (n = 20, p₀ = 0.22, p₁ = 0.5: four straight misses give 26.7; three give 11.7; the plan states no
control; DESIGN control: a monitor that uses the nominal miss rate 0.10 as its null has factors 5 and 0.556,
retires after two straight misses at e = 25, and fails the exchangeable-null battery). KA-730 (planted drift
retired within 20 episodes; null false retirement at most 5%). Negative controls: the nominal-rate monitor
above; a monitor that resets E to 1 after every cover (it is no longer an e-process; its null alarm rate over
long runs exceeds α_d); a p₀ taken from the mean of the Beta law. Determinism: exact rational factors;
products in double precision in sequence order; bitwise twice.

## Considerations

- At n = 20 this monitor gains evidence only when true coverage has fallen below about 65%. It is a guard
  against gross failure, and the status listing prints that plainly. Larger calibration sets sharpen it.
- Rounding p₀ up is a DESIGN choice that matches the plan's printed figures (0.22, 0.14) and the known-answer
  arithmetic exactly; the unrounded quantile would give 28.7 and 12.4 for four and three misses.
- The alternative p₁ = 0.5 is fixed by the plan. A mixture over alternatives would have more power against
  mild under-coverage and is a recorded option, not adopted.
- "A monitor at or above 20" is also one of the decision model's triggers for the deterministic fallback
  `reality: no-claim`; the two statements are the same event.
