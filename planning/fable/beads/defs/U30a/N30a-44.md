## Background

Ground (crate `frob-ground`) retires a calibration, and with it the cell's reality band, when new episodes
stop being exchangeable with the calibration episodes. Four triggers exist: a scope change (controller or
firmware identity, a replaced or re-lubricated part, the surface class, a recorded condition leaving the
envelope, a changed model identity including a kernel change that alters bits, an instrument's calibration
identity), which fires immediately and deterministically; a rank-drift e-process on each new residual's rank
in the frozen calibration residuals; a miss-counting e-process on band misses against the null miss rate
p₀(n); and expiry by age or operating hours. The statistical monitors alarm at an e-value of 1/α_d, α_d = 0.05.

**GATE-GROUND-07** (claim kind: known answer; milestone M5; TARGET): the planted drift retires the calibration
within 20 episodes in at least 90% of resamples; false retirement at most 5% over 10⁴ null runs; every scope
change fires. With the band-coverage gate it feeds the kill gate **KILL-BAND**: if either fails, bands are
withdrawn and reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual table.

Everything runs on the synthetic rehearsal: a truth simulator stands in for the robot, and all data is
labelled `synthetic`.

## Scope

In: the script, its three batteries (planted drift, null runs, scope changes), expiry cases, the effects of
retirement seen from the user's side, controls, log and gate record. Out: the mechanisms (N30a-40 to N30a-43).

## Technical approach

Script `scripts/e2e/ground_drift.sh [--seed S] [--resamples 1000] [--null-runs 10000] [--controls] [--quick]`,
plain shell over `frob`, `frobd` and the rehearsal generator, under `target/e2e-work/ground_drift/`; default
seed 8. `--quick` uses 50 resamples and 500 null runs and never records the gate.

1. **Calibrated cells.** For each resample: a one-unit cell of the instrumented cart taken through ingest,
   predict, score and band at level 0.90 with n = 20 (as in the band script), giving a frozen residual list,
   q̂, and two fresh monitors.
2. **Battery A: planted drift.** The truth simulator's joint friction is raised by 30% after episode k (k
   drawn in 5..15 by address). Monitoring episodes are then admitted one at a time with
   `frob ground ingest` (each feeds the monitors automatically). For each resample record the number of
   post-change episodes until the status becomes `retired`, and which monitor fired. Gate statistic: the
   fraction of resamples retired within 20 post-change episodes; assert ≥ 0.90. Also logged: the median
   detection delay, the split between rank and miss alarms, and the fraction of post-change episodes that
   missed the band (the effect size the 30% change actually produced on the metric).
3. **Battery B: null runs.** 10⁴ runs with no change: a calibrated cell and 100 further exchangeable
   monitoring episodes each. To keep 10⁶ ingests affordable, this battery drives the monitors through the
   conformance binary on residual streams produced by the same generator (the code path from residual to
   retirement is the production one; only the verb layer is bypassed), and a subset of 200 runs goes through
   `frob ground ingest` end to end and must agree bit for bit with the binary on the same streams. Gate
   statistic: the fraction of runs with any retirement; assert ≤ 0.05.
4. **Battery C: every scope change fires.** On separate calibrated cells, one planted change each, admitted
   through `frob ground ingest` or requested through `frob ground band`: controller hash; firmware `2.4.1` to
   `2.4.2`; a part replaced; a part re-lubricated; surface dry to wet; an episode at 26 °C against [18, 24];
   an instrument calibration identity; a new card version; a kernel build with a different replay digest.
   Assert for each: retirement in the same call, the reason naming the component, exit code 0 with a warning
   on ingest (the data is admitted; the calibration is retired) and `FROB-REF-897` on the next band request.
   Gate statistic: fired count over 9; assert 9 of 9. Counter-case: a dataset from another unit of the same
   design retires nothing.
5. **Expiry.** With the ledger date advanced 91 days: retired by age; with a manifest reporting 201 operating
   hours since calibration: retired by hours; at 89 days and 199 hours: not retired. The limits are the
   defaults and the log says so.
6. **Effects.** After one retirement of each kind: the design report prints
   `reality: no-claim [reason=retired:…]`; `frob result revalidate` lists the verdict that cited the band as
   stale and leaves the one that did not untouched; `frob ground band --status` prints the reasons and, for a
   live n = 20 cell, the power notes (δ = 0.224; p₀ = 0.22; factors 2.27 and 0.641; four straight misses
   retire). A scripted sequence of misses reproduces 11.7 after three and 26.7 after four.
7. **Controls (`--controls`).** Builds on which the script must exit non-zero: a miss monitor with the nominal
   null 0.10 (battery B exceeds 5%); a rank monitor with no slack (battery B); monitors that are never fed on
   ingest (battery A never retires); a scope check that ignores firmware (battery C reads 8 of 9); a
   retirement that submits no invalidation (step 6).
8. **Determinism.** Fifty resamples of battery A twice with one seed: identical detection delays and e-value
   trajectories.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Battery A: retired within 20 episodes in at least 90% of 1,000 resamples (TARGET), measured value
      recorded.
- [ ] Battery B: false retirement at most 5% over 10⁴ null runs (TARGET), measured value recorded; the 200
      end-to-end runs agree with the binary.
- [ ] Battery C: 9 of 9 scope changes fire with the right reason; the other-unit counter-case retires nothing.
- [ ] Expiry and effects behave as stated; the miss arithmetic prints 11.7 and 26.7.
- [ ] Each control build makes the script exit non-zero.
- [ ] The gate record `GATE-GROUND-07` holds the three statistics, the resample and run counts, the seed, the
      commit and the machine, and is what the KILL-BAND decision reads.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

None of its own. The generator has `friction_step_changes_only_after_k` and
`null_stream_is_exchangeable_by_construction`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. Log `target/e2e-logs/ground_drift/<utc>.ndjson` through the helper of `STD-E2E`, with
a transcript. Battery A: per resample a `metric` line (k, detection delay, monitor that fired, final E⁺, E⁻
and miss e-value). Battery B: per 1,000 runs a `metric` line with the running alarm fraction, split by
monitor. Battery C and expiry: per case a `cmd` line (exact command, exit code, wall time, evidence block,
`reality` text before and after) and a `refusal` line for the follow-up band request. `metric` lines for the
three gate statistics with thresholds 0.90, 0.05 and 9 of 9. On failure: the resamples not retired in time
with their e-value trajectories and residual lists' hashes. Final `summary`: assertions passed and failed,
gate touched (GATE-GROUND-07) with its three values.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-730 (drift retired within 20 further episodes; null false retirement at most 5%) and KA-731 (miss
arithmetic). PO-GROUND-03 (each monitor's false-alarm rate at most α_d under optional stopping) is the
unit-level form of battery B. Negative controls: the five builds of step 7. Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; debug and release on the quick sizes.

## Considerations

- Whether a 30% friction increase is detected within 20 episodes at n = 20 depends on how much it moves the
  banded metric relative to q̂. The monitors gain evidence only under gross under-coverage (a true miss rate
  above about 0.35 for the miss monitor). If the planted change produces a smaller effect on the chosen
  metric, the gate fails for a reason that is about power, not about a bug; the log's effect-size line makes
  that visible, and the response (a more sensitive metric such as peak current, a larger n) is a design
  decision for the Ground lead, recorded with the gate.
- Battery B's bypass of the verb layer is a cost decision; the 200 end-to-end runs are the check that the
  bypass changes nothing.
- A passing gate says the monitors behave as specified on synthetic drift. It is not evidence that real
  drift looks like a friction step.
