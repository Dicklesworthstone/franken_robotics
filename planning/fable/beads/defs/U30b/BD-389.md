## Background

A robot log carries several clocks: the motor controller's, each sensor's, the logging computer's. Before any
quantity that depends on time can be compared with a prediction (a crossing time, a latency, an actuator delay)
every source clock has to be mapped onto one reference clock. The map is t_ref = a + (1 + s)·t_src, with offset a
and skew s. There are two unknowns, so **one shared pulse fixes only one combination of them**. At a skew of
100 parts per million, two clocks that coincide at the start differ by 0.8 ms after 8 s (FACT, arithmetic),
which is most of a tick at 1 kHz and enough to move a packet across a decision instant.

Clock fitting is step 1 of Ground's procedure and stage 0 of its fixed identification order (clocks, actuators,
contact and surface, inertia and geometry, sensors: each stage's error aliases into the next, so a clock error
left in becomes a delay error, then a friction error). It runs inside `ground.ingest` and fails closed: no
fit, no time-based score.

The danger specific to this stage is that a clock is a free parameter that can absorb any timing error. The
five rules below exist to stop that. Rejected alternatives: fitting the offset by cross-correlating command and
response (it estimates offset plus actuator delay, and the two are not separable that way); a single
synchronization pulse (one equation, two unknowns); replacing raw timestamps by aligned ones (the fit's
uncertainty is lost and cannot be recomputed).

Tier: Frontier. Kill gate KILL-CLOCK, named in advance: if GATE-GROUND-02 or GATE-GROUND-05 fails, real logs are
scored on time-free metrics only (success, energy, peak values), and no crossing time, latency or delay is
reported.

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** For each source clock in the manifest: its marks, each a pair (raw source timestamp, reference
   timestamp) of one physical event seen by both clocks (a shared hardware pulse), with a timing error σ_m per
   mark. Raw timestamps are integers in device ticks and are never rewritten.
2. **Affine fit.** Weighted least squares over m ≥ 2 independent marks that bracket the trial. Unknowns
   (a, 1 + s); rows [1, t_src,i]; weights 1/σ_m,i². Times are centred on the weighted mean before the solve.
   Output: â, ŝ, their 2 × 2 covariance C, residuals, the largest residual, and the bracket [t₁, t_m].
   For two marks at t₁ < t₂ with independent timing error σ_m each (proof obligation PO-GROUND-02):
   - skew uncertainty σ_s = √2·σ_m/(t₂ − t₁);
   - uncertainty of an aligned instant σ_t(t)² = σ_m²·[(t₂ − t)² + (t − t₁)²]/(t₂ − t₁)², which is at most σ_m²
     inside the bracket (σ_m/√2 at the midpoint) and grows quadratically outside it.
   With σ_m = 50 µs and marks 10 s apart, σ_s = 7.1 ppm (FACT, arithmetic). In general
   σ_t(t)² = [1, t]·C·[1, t]ᵀ.
3. **Piecewise affine.** If the manifest declares that skew may change during the trial (`model = "piecewise"`
   on the `[[clock]]` entry, k segments), or the affine fit fails the adequacy test of N30a-11 (with more than
   two marks, a weighted residual sum above the 99.9% quantile of its χ² law), the fit is continuous piecewise
   linear with knots at the declared instants or else at interior marks; it needs at least k + 1
   marks and reports its largest residual (zero, and labelled `no redundancy`, when exactly k + 1 are given).
   A trial with fewer marks than its model needs is refused with FROB-REF-876 (clock alignment
   under-determined, or a scored instant outside the bracket), whose first repair lists the marks needed.
4. **Rules, each enforced by code.**
   1. *Scored instants lie inside the bracket.* `clock::in_bracket(fit, t)` is called by scoring; outside the
      bracket the score is refused with FROB-REF-876.
   2. *The clock is never fitted to the event being scored.* The fit's only inputs are the marks declared in the
      manifest, which are hash-committed at admission. The record lists the mark identifiers. On every use in
      scoring, `clock::verify` (N30a-13) recomputes the fit from the committed marks and compares it with the
      stored record (bit for bit on one platform, within 10⁻¹² relative on the parameters across platforms);
      a record that differs, such as one whose offset was adjusted to an event, is refused with FROB-REF-876
      and the label `clock-not-from-marks`. Counterexample: the prediction says the wheel
      crosses the sill at 4.212 s and the log shows 4.180 s; the offset that aligns the two reports a
      crossing-time error of zero and an offset of 32 ms: the whole error has been moved into the clock.
   3. *Cross-correlating command and response is not a clock fit.* It estimates offset plus actuator delay: a
      delay of 8 ms with an offset of −8 ms produces the same logs as no delay and no offset. For a clock with
      no shared hardware mark the record holds one quantity, `delay_plus_offset` with its uncertainty, and a
      request to update the card's delay from it is refused with FROB-REF-879 (repair: a shared hardware mark).
   4. *Raw timestamps are kept.* Aligned times are derived data carrying σ_t. The predicate
      `arrived_before(fit, t_packet, t_decision)` uses the interval t̂ ± 3·σ_t (TARGET multiplier) and answers
      `before`, `after` or `undecided` when the interval straddles.
   5. *Each instrument names which of the seven instants its timestamp denotes* (sample, exposure, timestamp,
      arrival, decision, command, actuation). A timestamp with no declared instant is refused: at admission
      under FROB-REF-873 (a required instrument field is missing), and under FROB-REF-876 if a time-based metric
      is requested on it later.
5. **Record.** `frob.ground.clockfit/1`, one per dataset and source clock: model, â, ŝ, covariance, mark
   identifiers with raw and reference timestamps, σ_m, residuals, largest residual, bracket, the
   `delay_plus_offset` field where rule 3 applies, and the fit's content hash. Ledger event
   `ground.clock` (the event N30a-11 appends). The result of `ground.ingest` gains `clocks: [{name, model, skew_ppm, offset_s,
   sigma_skew_ppm, bracket, max_residual_s}]`.
   **Failing closed inside the verb** (DESIGN; the parts state the pieces, this is the envelope). Admission and
   clock fitting are two steps of one call, and a refused clock fit does not undo admission: the dataset stays
   admitted and sealed, because time-free metrics (success, energy, peak values) remain scorable on it, which
   is the second repair of 876 and the whole of the KILL-CLOCK fallback. The call then completes with
   `ok: true` and exit code 2 (completed with findings); the entry of that clock is `{name, refused: <the
   refusal object of FROB-REF-876 in full: pointer `clock[<name>]`, marks present, marks needed, repairs>}`,
   the result carries `time_based_metrics: "unavailable"` for the instruments on that clock, and the
   `ground.clock` event records the refusal. Every later time-based use is refused outright with exit 3:
   876 at `ground.score` (N30a-12, N30b-06), and 879 with pointer `stage` naming `clocks` at `ground.identify`
   (N30a-20). A dataset in which every clock is fitted exits 0.
6. **Gate measurement** (defined here; run by the gate script's bead N30a-14 as the `GATE-GROUND-02` entry of
   the conformance crate's known-answer tier, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<S> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
   g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-GROUND-02`, on fixture R-01 of the rehearsal: planted skew 100 ppm, offset 3.7 ms, jitter 50 µs, marks
   10 s apart). Per resample (1,000 jitter seeds): pass
   when |ŝ − 100 ppm| ≤ 3·σ_s = 21 ppm **and** the registered scored instant t* = 4.212 s satisfies
   |t̂(t*) − truth| ≤ 3·σ_t(t*). Gate: at least 990 of 1,000 pass, and both negative controls are caught.

Determinism: double precision on the CPU, class D2; identical across worker counts. Evidence class Estimated;
on the rehearsal the claim kind is Known answer and the status `synthetic`.

## Considerations

- **The reading of "aligned instants cover truth" decides whether the gate is passable.** With Gaussian jitter,
  a correct fit passes one resample with probability 0.9973 on the skew clause alone, 0.9945 with coverage at
  one registered instant, and 0.990 if coverage is required simultaneously at every instant of the bracket. At
  0.990 the count falls below 990 of 1,000 in 42% of runs of a correct implementation (P(X ≤ 989) = 0.417); at
  0.9945 in about 2.5% (0.0249).
  DESIGN: coverage is asserted at the registered scored instant, the seed is registered before the run, and the
  expected count (about 994.5) is written beside the measured one.
- Timing error is assumed independent between marks. Marks that share a trigger line share error; such marks are
  declared as one group and the group's common term is not divided by the number of marks.
- A clock with a temperature-dependent oscillator needs the piecewise model; with more than two marks the
  affine model on such a clock fails the adequacy test and the piecewise model is tried; with exactly two
  marks nothing can show it, and the record says `no redundancy`.
- If KILL-CLOCK fires, this bead's record survives as provenance, and every time-based metric is removed from
  predictions, scores and bands.
