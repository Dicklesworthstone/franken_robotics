## Background

A reality band (BD-393) covers at its stated level only while new episodes remain exchangeable with the ones it
was calibrated on. Robots do not stay exchangeable with their past: bearings wear, grease migrates, firmware is
updated, a floor is refinished. A band that outlives its regime is the most expensive failure the subsystem can
have, because it is a wrong number that looks calibrated. Ground (crate `frob-ground`, the subsystem that
compares simulation with measured reality) therefore watches every calibrated cell and retires its calibration
when the evidence says the regime has ended. The alarm ends the regime, and calibration restarts inside the new
one.

Four triggers exist. Two are deterministic (a change of scope; an expiry limit) and are in N30a-40 and N30a-43. This bead
builds the two statistical ones. Both are **e-processes**: nonnegative processes whose expectation never exceeds
one under their null, so the probability that they ever reach 1/α is at most α. They can be read after every
episode without inflating error, which is exactly how a monitor is used.

Decision **ADR-GROUND-09:** drift is tested on ranks and misses, not on raw distributions by kernel discrepancy
or optimal transport, because those tests have no power at about twenty scalar residuals.

What the sibling provides (FACT, FrankenSim `crates/fs-eproc`, feature `conformal-hardening`): `DriftMonitor`, a
two-sided betting e-process on the rank of each new value in a frozen sample, with slack
δ = max(1/√n, 0.02). The slack makes the monitor insensitive at small n; that is a fact to print, not to hide.

Tier: Frontier. Pilot: N30b-14. Kill gate KILL-BAND, named in advance: if GATE-GROUND-06 or GATE-GROUND-07 fails,
bands are withdrawn and reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual table.

## Scope

This bead is the capstone of drift detection: it feeds the monitors on every admitted episode, retires a
calibration when a trigger fires, and closes GATE-GROUND-07. The parts are built in their own beads, which are
dependencies of this one:

- the deterministic triggers (every scope change of a calibrated cell, and expiry): N30a-40;
- the rank-drift monitor (a two-sided betting e-process on new residuals' ranks): N30a-41;
- the miss-counting monitor (a likelihood-ratio e-process on band misses against p₀(n)): N30a-42;
- what retirement does (the transaction, reports back to no-claim, citing verdicts stale, refusal 897, the
  status listing): N30a-43.

In this bead: the feeding of both monitors from `ground.ingest`; the monitors' stored state record; the firing
rule that hands an alarm to the retirement transaction; the power facts printed with every status; the
definition of the gate batteries for drift and for null runs; switching the `model-drift` failure class of the
chronicle taxonomy from `pending: BD-394` to `available`, and recording that wave of GATE-CHRON-08's fixture
list (item 9). The Technical approach below states the
integrated behaviour the parts must show together; each part's own bead is where its internals are specified
and built.

Out: the band (BD-393); the core record (N30b-08); the proof-obligation discharge record (N30b-24); the gate
script (`scripts/e2e/ground_drift.sh`, N30a-44, which depends on this bead); the kill decision (N30b-18).

## Technical approach

1. **Triggers** (α_d = 0.05, TARGET; thresholds are 1/α_d = 20).

   | Trigger | Mechanism | Fires |
   |---|---|---|
   | Scope change | Controller or firmware identity changes; a part is replaced or re-lubricated; the surface class changes; a recorded condition leaves the envelope; the model identity changes, including a kernel change that alters bits; an instrument's calibration identity changes | Immediately and deterministically |
   | Rank drift | Each new residual is ranked in the frozen calibration residuals. Under exchangeability the normalized rank is uniform. `DriftMonitor` bets against that in both directions and averages the two e-processes | At e ≥ 1/α_d |
   | Miss counting | A likelihood-ratio e-process on band misses, null "miss rate ≤ p₀(n)", alternative p₁ = 0.5 | At e ≥ 1/α_d |
   | Expiry | Age or operating hours since calibration | At the limit |
2. **Rank monitor.** At calibration the cell's n residuals are frozen (their identifiers and values hashed into
   the monitor record). For a new residual r_t the normalized rank is u_t = (1 + #{i : r_i ≤ r_t})/(n + 1). The
   sibling's monitor consumes u_t and returns its upward and downward e-values; Ground stores both and their
   average e_rank. The slack is δ = max(1/√n, 0.02): 0.224 at n = 20 and 0.071 at n = 200 (FACT). At n = 20 it
   sees only gross shifts.
3. **Miss monitor.** m_t = 1 if the new outcome falls outside the band, else 0.
   e_miss(t) = Π_{j ≤ t} (p₁/p₀)^{m_j}·((1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀))^{1 − m_j}.
   p₀(n) is the 95th percentile of the conditional miss rate, whose law is Beta(l, n + 1 − l) with
   l = ⌊(n + 1)·α⌋: 0.2161 at n = 20 and 0.1363 at n = 200 for a 90% band, registered rounded up to two
   decimals, 0.22 and 0.14 (FACT, arithmetic from the Beta law; rounding up is conservative for the null). At
   n = 20 a miss multiplies the evidence by 0.5/0.22 = 2.27 and a cover by 0.5/0.78 = 0.641, so four misses in a
   row give 26.7 and retire the calibration, and three give 11.7 and do not. Because p₁ > p₀, each factor has
   expectation at most one under any miss rate at or below p₀, so the process is valid for the composite null.
   This monitor cannot tell drift from an unlucky calibration draw. Both mean the band under-covers, and both
   call for recalibration.
4. **Feeding.** Automatic on ingest (step 9 of Ground's procedure). When `ground.ingest` admits an episode whose
   cell is `calibrated`, it simulates the prediction under the episode's recorded conditions with the cell's
   fixed model, forms the residual, updates both monitors and appends `ground.monitor.update` to the ledger.
   Episodes are consumed in admission order; an episode updates each monitor once (idempotent by episode
   identity). Sealed parts of a new dataset are not read for monitoring.
5. **State record.** `frob.ground.monitor/1`: cell, metric, model identity, hash of the frozen residuals, n, δ,
   p₀, p₁, α_d, the two rank e-values and their average, e_miss, episodes seen, misses, and, once fired, which
   monitor, at which episode and ledger sequence.
6. **Firing.** When e_rank or e_miss reaches 20 the cell's state becomes `calibrated-shifted` and the
   retirement transaction of N30a-40 and N30a-43 runs with reason `RankDrift` or `MissDrift` (the variants of
   `RetireReason` in N30a-02 that the two monitors emit; printed `rank-drift` and `miss-drift`) and the e-value.
7. **Power facts, printed by `ground.band --status`** so that a quiet monitor is not misread: the slack at this
   n; p₀(n); the per-miss and per-cover factors; the number of consecutive misses that retire (four at n = 20);
   the post-drift miss probability needed for the miss monitor to retire within 20 episodes with probability
   0.9 from a fresh start (about 0.6 at n = 20: computed power 0.70, 0.92 and 0.99 at miss probabilities 0.5,
   0.6 and 0.7, starting from e-value 1); and the same power computed from the monitor's current e-value,
   because covered episodes shrink it. After ten covered episodes at n = 20 the e-value is 0.641¹⁰ = 0.0117,
   thirteen or more misses in the next twenty are then needed, and the power is 0.43, 0.78 and 0.97 at 0.6,
   0.7 and 0.8. (The rehearsal's drift fixture quotes 0.58, 0.86 and 0.98 for the same three rates: that is
   the average over its calibration draw, in which the ten clean episodes are not all covered, BD-388.)
8. **Gate battery** (defined here; run by the gate script's bead N30a-44 as the `GATE-GROUND-07` entry of the
   conformance crate's tier targets, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<S> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test <tier>
   [--features gauntlet-mutations] -- GATE-GROUND-07`, with
   the tier each known-answer row names: KA-730 and KA-731 are tier G0, target `g0_laws`). Drift: fixture R-10 of the rehearsal (20
   calibration episodes, 10 clean, then joint friction raised by 30%), 1,000 resamples: the fraction retired
   within 20 episodes after the change. Null: 10⁴ runs, each a fresh calibration of 20 exchangeable residuals
   followed by 200 exchangeable episodes (TARGET horizon), stopped at the first crossing of either monitor:
   the fraction retired.
9. **The `model-drift` failure class** (the row N24a-38 entered in `registries/failure-classes.toml` ahead of
   its monitor, with `status = "pending: BD-394"`). While the row is pending, a run that cites a calibrated
   cell is not refused: it is admitted, and every result is stamped `unmonitored: model-drift (pending
   BD-394)` (N24a-38's rule for a row entered ahead of its monitor). This bead switches the row to
   `available`: predicate, a run that cites a calibrated cell is classed `model-drift` when a drift monitor of
   that cell fired inside the run's interval of validity, or the cell is `retired` at the time the run is
   cited (read from the monitor and retirement records); fixture, a run citing a rehearsal cell with a planted
   drift event (R-10); control, the same cell before the event, which must not be classed. From the switch on
   the stamp is gone, and a build without the monitor computes the row `unavailable`, so that N24a-38's
   admission rule then refuses such a run with `failure-monitor-unavailable` unless it is waived.
   The pair is wave 3 of GATE-CHRON-08's fixture list: this bead appends it to
   `fixtures/e2e/chron08/list.toml` (N29b-06's list, with `wave = 3`) and is that wave's recorder, as N29b-06's
   table of waves names it. The gate keeps its one part. This bead's script runs N29b-06's
   `scripts/e2e/gate_chron_08.sh` as a stage of its own on the whole list as it then stands (the stage
   `gate-chron-08` below); that script ends with the tier run `FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance
   --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-CHRON-08`, with the referee receipt exported as
   its entry requires. A session that did not implement the monitors then re-records the part from that
   run's receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-CHRON-08 --receipt
   <hash> --part M2`, so every earlier class is proved again and a regression fails the part. No script writes
   a gate row.

Determinism: the miss process is a product of two registered rationals (class D0 as exact rationals, D2 in
double precision); the rank process has the sibling's class, D2 on one machine. Evidence class Estimated.

## Considerations

- Each monitor is bounded at α_d = 0.05 by proof obligation PO-GROUND-03; two monitors together are bounded by
  0.10 in the worst case, while the gate asks for at most 5% measured on the union. The miss monitor
  contributes about 1% at a 200-episode horizon (computed), so a correct build should pass. The union is
  measured first by the null calibration of the proof obligation (N30b-24), before the gate; if it exceeds 5%
  there, the pre-declared adjustment is to run each monitor at α_d/2 (threshold 40), which is a new version of
  the decision policy and changes the hand case: four misses (26.7) then no longer retire and five (60.6) do.
- The drift clause depends on the fixture's registered signal. From a fresh start, 0.6 would give 90% power in
  20 episodes at n = 20; R-10's ten clean episodes shrink the e-value first, so a post-drift miss probability
  of 0.7 gives only 0.86 and the fixture needs at least 0.8 (0.98). The rehearsal registers that before the run
  (BD-388).
- A quiet monitor at n = 20 is weak evidence of no drift. The status output says so with numbers; a reader who
  wants sensitivity needs a larger calibration set (δ = 0.071 at n = 200).
- If KILL-BAND fires, the monitors are withdrawn with the bands; the raw residual table is still printed.
