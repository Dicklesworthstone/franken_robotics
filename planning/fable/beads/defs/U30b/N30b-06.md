## Background

`ground.score` is the single place where Ground reads sealed data. Ground is the subsystem that compares
simulation with measured reality; every dataset it admits has its validation and holdout parts sealed at entry,
readable only here, once, with a ledger event. A prediction must have been frozen first (decision
ADR-GROUND-05: a prediction is frozen before its trial data is readable; a refit opens a new prediction
version; a scored holdout is retired). Scoring does four things in one transaction: it compares the frozen
prediction with what happened; it spends the holdout so that it can never be a holdout again; it decides
whether the candidate cards used in the prediction become `admitted`; and it feeds what it learned back, as
calibration pairs, as the cell's reality-gap variance and as regression cases.

Why one verb and one transaction: if reading and spending were separate steps, a crash or a careless script
between them would leave data that has been seen and can still be used as a holdout. The counterexample that
motivates sealing is in the rehearsal: a fit class without the truth's Stribeck friction term passes when
scored on its own calibration data and is rejected on sealed data.

## Scope

This bead owns the verb `ground.score` (kind run): the one path that opens sealed data. Its parts are built in
their own beads, which are dependencies of this one:

- the scoring functions (the per-trial and aggregate interval score, bias, coverage, residual structure, the
  comparison of rankings and the `ranking-falsified` outcome): N30a-32;
- the declared study and its reachability pre-analysis, evaluated at the declared type and level: N30a-33;
- the spent set, refusal 886, the version rule after a refit and the error-spending series
  α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]: N30a-34;
- admission or rejection of candidate cards and the width interface (N30a-28), and the discrepancy floor
  computed inside scoring (N30a-29).

In this bead: the verb, its arguments, envelope and job behaviour; the score token and the single sealed read
as one ledger transaction (spend event, read, score record) with its crash behaviour; metric extraction from
the sealed log with the clock checks (the bracket predicate of BD-389); the order in which the parts are
called; what is fed back (residuals to the cell's pool; the reality-gap variance event of N30a-09). The
Technical approach below states the integrated behaviour the parts must show together; each part's own bead is
where its internals are specified and built.

Out: the prediction record and `ground.predict` (BD-392); the sealed store itself (BD-387); band issue
(BD-393); monitors fed by later ingests (BD-394); the gate script (`scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh`,
N30a-35).

## Technical approach

1. **Verb.** `frob ground score <prediction> --key <k> --json`; kind `run` (the idempotency key `--key`,
   without which the call is FROB-REF-805; budget; bounded wait or a job handle); capability `ground:run`, the
   scope the verb registry gives it (a separate `ground:score` scope, which an earlier text of this bead named,
   was proposed to the scope registry and is not adopted); schemas `frob.ground.score.in/1`, `.out/1`, record
   `frob.ground.score/1`. Refusals: FROB-REF-883 (a read of sealed data with no frozen prediction, or a
   prediction changed after freezing), FROB-REF-886 (reuse of a spent holdout), FROB-REF-876 (a scored instant
   outside the clock bracket, or an under-determined clock).
2. **Transaction, in order.**
   1. Load the prediction; recompute its hash; compare with the frozen hash (mismatch: 883).
   2. Check each chunk in `sealed` against the spent table for this cell and model lineage (hit: 886).
   3. Ask the sealed store for a score token: granted only if `frozen_at` precedes every read event of those
      chunks. Write `ground.sealed.read` and `ground.holdout.spent` to the ledger **before** any byte is
      returned.
   4. Read the chunks; check the recorded trial conditions against the registered draw (a mismatch beyond the
      condition instruments' tolerance marks the trial `conditions-not-as-drawn`: it is scored, reported, and
      excluded from calibration pairs).
   5. Extract each metric's outcome y. A time-based metric is taken only at instants inside the clock bracket,
      with the aligned-time uncertainty carried; outside it the metric is refused with 876 and the others are
      still scored. Every attempted trial is a row, aborted ones included.
   6. Score, decide, admit or reject, feed back (steps 3 to 7 below), write the score record, commit.
   A crash after step 3 leaves the holdout spent and unscored; recovery re-enters at step 4 with the same token
   (idempotent by key), never with a fresh holdout.
3. **Score.** Per metric and trial the interval score S = (u − l) + (2/α)·(l − y)₊ + (2/α)·(y − u)₊ for the
   registered interval [l, u] at level 1 − α. Reported with: bias (mean of y − midpoint); coverage (share of
   trials with l ≤ y ≤ u, as a count over all attempted trials); residual structure against condition (for each
   recorded condition the slope of the residual on it with its interval; a slope whose interval excludes zero
   is the finding `condition-dependent`).
4. **Decision.** The registered decision is evaluated by the declared study type at its registered n and level.
   For the i-th confirmation attempt of the same claim on fresh data the level is α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)]
   (0.025, 0.00833, 0.00417, 0.0025 for α = 0.05), so repeated attempts spend error and the total stays at most
   α. The attempt index advances for every scored attempt, whatever its outcome.
5. **Admission.** A candidate card version named by the prediction becomes `admitted` in this cell when (a)
   every registered interval covered at the registered coverage count, and (b) its calibration record is not
   labelled `wrong-reason`. Otherwise it is `rejected` for this cell and stays visible. Fail-closed default: a
   candidate stays a candidate (for example when a metric was refused for clock reasons).
6. **Retirement of the holdout.** The scored chunks move to the calibration pool or the regression set of the
   cell and are never a holdout again (invariant INV-GROUND-04: a holdout chunk is scored at most once per cell
   and model lineage). "Model lineage" is the chain of card versions descending from one prior by refits; a
   refit does not get a second look at the same holdout.
7. **A reversed ranking is a result.** If the real ranking of two designs contradicts the simulated one, the
   outcome is `ranking-falsified`: the cell's domain narrows to the conditions where the ranking held (the
   envelope box is cut along the condition that separates the two), and the pair, its conditions and its logs
   become a regression case in the divergence ledger.
8. **Fed back automatically.** Calibration pairs (ŷ, y) per metric into the cell's band pool; the cell's
   reality-gap variance update; the prediction's score into the studio's prediction ledger.
9. **Result.** `score` (per metric), `coverage`, `bias`, `findings`, `decision` and its level, `attempt`,
   `cards` (admitted, rejected, unchanged), `holdout` (spent hashes), `labels`, and `reality` (`candidate` until
   a band is issued; `synthetic` on rehearsal data).

Determinism: class D2. Evidence class Estimated; claim kind Reality on real data, Known answer on the rehearsal.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-GROUND-05 (TARGET): the freeze-before-read order holds for every trial; the three protocol mutations
      (read before freeze; refit without a new version; holdout reuse) are refused, three of three. Each has a
      unit test of this bead (`spend_event_precedes_bytes`, `read_before_freeze_refused_883`,
      `hash_mismatch_refused_883`, `spent_holdout_refused_886`). The gate is measured from the user's
      side, and its receipt and record written, by N30a-35 (one recorder per gate), which depends on this bead;
      the KILL-CLOCK decision (N30b-19) reads that record. This bead closes on its own criteria, whichever way
      that measurement falls.
- [ ] A second `ground.score` on the same holdout for the same cell and lineage is refused with FROB-REF-886,
      also after a refit of the cards.
- [ ] The omitted-law candidate of the rehearsal is rejected on sealed data, and the same candidate scored by a
      mutant that reads the calibration split passes: the control shows why sealing matters.
- [ ] A candidate whose record is `wrong-reason` is not admitted even with full coverage.
- [ ] A kill of the process between the spend event and the score leaves the holdout spent; the retried call
      completes the same score; no path returns sealed bytes without the two ledger events.
- [ ] A crossing-time metric whose instant lies outside the clock bracket is refused with 876 while time-free
      metrics in the same call are scored.
- [ ] The attempt levels follow α/[i·(i + 1)] and sum to α·m/(m + 1) after m attempts.
- [ ] A planted reversed ranking yields `ranking-falsified`, a narrowed cell domain and one regression case.
- [ ] Reachable through `frob ground score <prediction> --key <k> --json` and the MCP tool `ground_score` with
      the capability `ground:run`; a call without `--key` is refused with FROB-REF-805; cancellation before the
      spend event leaves the holdout unspent, after it leaves it spent.

`scripts/e2e/ground_protocol.sh` (bead N30a-35, which owns the script and depends on this bead) scores every

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-726 (omitted law: rejected at validation; the same fit scored on its own calibration data passes) and KA-734
(protocol). CX-331. Planted failures: a store that returns bytes before writing the spend event; a spent table
keyed by card version instead of lineage (a refit then reuses the holdout); a scorer that drops aborted trials
from the denominator; an admission rule that ignores `wrong-reason`. Chaos lane: kill at each of the six
transaction steps, then recover. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- A real trial is expensive and a holdout can be read once. The reachability pre-analysis at registration is
  what protects the operator from spending a holdout on a trial that could not have decided anything.
- Coverage at small n is coarse: with one metric and 20 trials, a 90% interval covers 15 or fewer with
  probability about 4% for a perfect model (binomial arithmetic). The registered coverage count, not the nominal level, is the
  admission criterion, and it is part of the frozen record.
- If the clock kill gate fires, step 5 of the transaction drops every time-based metric and the rest is
  unchanged.
- Scores computed on private data inherit the private mark; the score record holds no raw samples.
