## Background

The design studio has eleven recurring decisions, each of which moves compute or changes what is reported as
established. Each is a **decision core**: one mathematical family compiled ahead of time into a small runtime
artifact, with a loss model, proof obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode, registered as a
record `registries/cores/<id>.toml` and shipped in shadow behind its baseline. Core DC-07 decides whether a
simulated result may carry a reality band and when the band is void. Its family is a conformal band with an
exchangeability alarm, its owner is `frob-ground`, and its timescale is once per real batch. This bead is the
owner's specification and implementation of that core. Its deterministic fallback is already the default state
of the whole subsystem: `reality: no-claim`.

The reason for writing the decision down as a core, instead of leaving it implicit in the band code, is that
the costs are lopsided and someone will be tempted to trade them: a band that under-covers and is relied on is
far more expensive than a valid band withheld. Reporting an artifact-dependent result as established costs
more than a wasted episode. A core makes that asymmetry a declared constant with a version, so changing it is a
visible act.

Rejected method, recorded so the question is not reopened without new evidence: kernel two-sample and
optimal-transport drift tests have no power at about twenty scalar residuals (decision ADR-GROUND-09: drift is
tested on ranks and misses).

## Scope

In: the core record; states, actions and loss; the alarm level; the four artifacts; the guard table and its
regeneration; obligations; failure modes; fallback triggers; budgeted mode; timescale; decision evidence
records; adoption. The record `registries/cores/dc-07.toml` already exists from M4 (N28a-28: shadow, the table
compiled and calibrated on synthetic residuals, note `mechanism: M5`); this bead amends that record as a new
decision-policy version with the full cell key, the two monitors, the draw dimension of the table and the
30-episode cap, and never creates a second record. Out: the band computation (BD-393); the monitors (BD-394);
the planner that buys real episodes (N30b-09); the M4 conformance row for DC-07 on synthetic residuals, which
the studio's own gate measures.

## Technical approach

1. **State, per cell.** `uncalibrated`; `calibrated-exchangeable`; `calibrated-shifted`.
2. **Actions.** Issue a band; refuse; recommend k more real episodes; retire.
3. **Loss.** A band that under-covers and is relied on costs C_false. A valid band withheld costs C_refuse. A
   real episode costs c_real, expressed in GPU-second equivalents at a declared exchange rate (open question
   OPEN-GROUND-4: default, real episodes are recommended, never scheduled, and no rate is assumed). C_false is
   far larger than C_refuse. The alarm level follows from the loss as α_d = c_recal/C_stale (the cost of a
   recalibration over the cost of relying on a stale band), with 0.05 as the default. These constants live in
   the core's contract; changing one is a new version of the decision policy.
4. **Artifacts.**
   (a) The residual quantile per cell, whose calibration set is its only calibration source and whose
       conditions come from a pre-registered draw.
   (b) A **guard table** (n, level, draw) → `finite(index)`, `infinite` or `refuse`, compiled as
       `frob.core-table/1`, regenerated in CI and diffed. Rule: `draw = episode`: refuse for n < 19; otherwise
       index = ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉, `infinite` when the index exceeds n. `draw = unit`: refuse for
       n < ⌈(1 − α)/α⌉, otherwise the same index rule. Levels tabulated: 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99; n from 0 to
       400 (TARGET range); beyond the table the rule is evaluated directly and the row marked `computed`.
       Sample rows: (8, 0.90, episode) refuse; (19, 0.95, episode) finite(19); (20, 0.90, episode) finite(19);
       (20, 0.95, episode) finite(20); (20, 0.99, episode) infinite; (99, 0.99, episode) finite(99);
       (3, 0.90, unit) refuse; (9, 0.90, unit) finite(9).
   (c) The two monitors: the rank e-process and the miss e-process, each firing at 1/α_d.
   (d) Rows of the experiment table for real batches: cost, expected value of sample information, and their
       ratio (N30b-09).
5. **Automaton.** `uncalibrated` → `calibrated-exchangeable` when a frozen prediction has been scored on sealed
   data and the guard table returns `finite`; `calibrated-exchangeable` → `calibrated-shifted` when a monitor
   reaches 1/α_d, an audited miss rate exceeds its bound, any scope component changes, or the calibration
   expires by age or operating hours under the limits of `policy_version` (DESIGN: expiry is the fourth
   retirement trigger of §30.13, retired by N30a-40; the plan's DC-07 trigger list names the other three, and
   without this edge the core would read `calibrated-exchangeable` for a cell whose status is already
   `retired`); `calibrated-shifted`
   → action `retire`, status `retired(cell, reason)`, and calibration restarts inside the new regime as a new
   `uncalibrated` cell. There is no edge from `calibrated-shifted` back to `calibrated-exchangeable`.
6. **Obligations.** Coverage at least 1 − α for exchangeable draws. Type-I error of each monitor at most α_d
   under optional stopping. The planner never issues a verdict, so a wrong variance model costs efficiency and
   never validity. The planner chooses how many real episodes, never which conditions: assigned conditions
   equal the registered draw, and that equality is the interference test.
7. **Failure modes, each with its guard.** Survivor-only calibration (the `attempts = "all"` check at
   admission); starved cells (the minimum count); conditions chosen by the operator (the registered draw
   compared at scoring); drift (the monitors).
8. **Deterministic fallback.** `reality: no-claim`. Triggers: fewer than 19 calibration points; a monitor at or
   above 20; an audited miss rate above its bound; any scope change; expiry (step 5); a guard table that is missing, stale
   against its contract or not the registry version (the core-table code `FROB-REF-871`: on a call of
   `ground.band` it is not a refused call, the verb completes on the fallback with 871 in `warnings` and in the
   decision record; `cores.show` shows it as a finding, exit 2; repair: `cargo xtask check-generated`
   regenerates and compares).
9. **Budgeted mode.** At most 30 real episodes are recommended per decision (TARGET); beyond that the answer is
   "inconclusive, no-claim". Below the calibration floor no band is issued: there is no smaller band for a
   smaller budget.
10. **Timescale.** Once per real batch. Kernel builds and solver-effort profiles are constants to it; the
    per-episode promotion core treats the band as a constant.
11. **Record and evidence.** `registries/cores/dc-07.toml` with the fields of the core contract (decision,
    owner, timescale, family and table hash, loss constants, proof obligations PO-GROUND-01 and PO-GROUND-03,
    fallback and triggers, budgeted mode, adoption status, replay scenario, calibration report, demonstration
    manifest, and the `policy_version` of the expiry limits in `registries/ground/expiry.toml`, whose values
    OPEN-GROUND-3 sets, N30b-27). The amendment keeps what other beads have written into the record: the
    proof-obligation entries are an identifier with, beside it, the address of its discharge record where one
    has been written (N30b-24 adds the addresses of PO-GROUND-01 and PO-GROUND-03 to the M4 record; that bead
    and this one have no order between them), so the amendment rewrites the fields listed here and leaves
    every address it finds in place, whichever of the two runs first. Every decision writes an evidence record: cell, pool hash, table version, ledger frontier, state
    before and after, action, and, while in shadow, the action the baseline (`no-claim`) took.
12. **Adoption.** `shadow` until the Ground gates for bands and drift have recorded values; then `default` for
    cells of origin `real`. On the rehearsal the core runs and every output is `synthetic`.

Determinism: the table and the automaton are class D0. Evidence class of every action: Estimated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/cores/dc-07.toml` validates against the core contract; `cores.show dc-07` lists states,
      actions, constants, triggers and the table hash.
- [ ] Exactly one DC-07 record exists: the M4 record of N28a-28, amended here to a new decision-policy version
      whose history keeps the M4 version. A record that carries discharge-record addresses beside PO-GROUND-01
      and PO-GROUND-03 before the amendment carries the same two addresses after it.
- [ ] The guard table regenerates byte-identically in CI; a hand-edited table fails the diff; the sample rows
      above are in it.
- [ ] Each fallback trigger, forced on a fixture, yields `reality: no-claim` and an evidence record naming the
      trigger: 18 points; a monitor at 20; an audited miss rate above bound; one test per scope component;
      expiry at 91 days; a stale table.
- [ ] No sequence of inputs moves a cell from `calibrated-shifted` to `calibrated-exchangeable` (property test
      over random event sequences).
- [ ] While in shadow, every decision record holds both the core's action and the baseline's.
- [ ] A request beyond 30 recommended episodes returns "inconclusive, no-claim".
- [ ] Changing α_d in the contract changes the decision-policy version, and records written before and after
      carry different versions.

release.

## Considerations

- The studio's own description of this core keys the band by (robot, firmware, floor, policy) and names a test
  martingale on conformal p-values with a trigger at 20; the owner's specification here is the same core with
  the full cell key and the two named monitors. Where the two differ in wording, the cell key and the monitors
  of this bead are what the code implements, and the studio's conformance row is measured against them.
- Open question OPEN-GROUND-4 (the exchange rate between operator time and GPU-seconds; owner: project lead;
  blocks nothing): until a rate is declared, cost columns for real batches are in episodes and no ratio against
  simulation is printed.
- A core never issues a verdict about a design; it decides whether a band may be attached and when it is void.
- For a Frontier core the fallback is also its kill fallback: if calibration fails after one repair cycle the
  core stays on `no-claim`, and the registry says so (KILL-BAND).
