## Background

Real episodes are the scarcest resource the program will ever have: each needs an operator, a robot and a
floor. Simulation episodes are nearly free by comparison. The question "should the next unit of effort be a
real batch or more simulation?" therefore has a real answer, and it depends on where the remaining uncertainty
sits. The studio's experiment-choice core values each candidate experiment by the expected reduction in
opportunity loss of the current choice. This bead supplies the piece of that calculation that only Ground can:
the **reality-gap variance** σ_r² of a cell, and the rows of the experiment table for real batches.

The point, in one sentence: calibration does not make simulation faster; it changes which experiment is worth
buying. In an uncalibrated cell the reality gap is the largest unknown and real episodes are worth several
times all remaining simulation; in a calibrated cell the same batch is worth less than more simulation.

Two cautions bind the design, both carried from the excitation-design rules. **Model class:** a probe that is
perfect inside its assumed law class can be wrong outside it (in one executed study a probe built for three
material laws classified 675 of 675 cases inside the class and 0 of 225 under an omitted delayed law, while
measuring the target directly was right in 900 of 900, FACT), so the direct measurement is always listed.
**Decision relevance:** an experiment is recommended for the decision it could change, not for the parameter it
would sharpen; if every admissible value implies the same decision, the recommendation is to measure nothing.

Rejected alternative: expected information gain. It buys knowledge that cannot change the choice.

## Scope

In: the reading of σ_r² per cell from the ledger (the estimator and its event `ground.feedback` are step 10a
of the procedure bead N30a-09, a dependency); the expected value of sample information for a real batch;
the table rows; the recommendation "k more real episodes" with its cap; the rule that the planner never picks
conditions; the wall between this advice and any verdict. Out: the ranking and stop rule of
`experiment.suggest` (the studio's bead); the core record (N30b-08); scheduling of anything (real episodes are
recommended, never scheduled).

## Technical approach

1. **Model.** For two finalists, Δ ~ N(m, s²) is the posterior on their score difference. s² is the sum of
   sampling variance, tier-bias variance and the reality-gap variance σ_r² of the cell. An experiment removes
   variance s̃² from Δ, and its expected value of sample information is
   EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃,
   where φ and Φ are the standard normal density and distribution function. A measurement of variance v on a
   component of prior variance w removes w²/(w + v).
2. **Real batch.** k real pairs with per-pair noise σ_p measure Δ itself, so v = σ_p²/k and w = s²:
   s̃² = (s²)²/(s² + σ_p²/k). **Simulation** can remove at most the sampling and tier-bias terms:
   s̃² ≤ s² − σ_r².
3. **Substituted (illustrative).** m = 0.03, sampling 0.03², tier bias 0.04². In an uncalibrated cell with
   σ_r = 0.10, twenty real pairs with per-pair noise 0.3 remove s̃ = 0.096 and are worth 0.0251; all remaining
   simulation can remove at most the first two terms (s̃ = 0.05) and is worth 0.0084. In a calibrated cell with
   σ_r = 0.02 the same real batch is worth 0.0035 and the simulation 0.0084.
4. **Reality-gap variance ledger.** Computed and written by step 10a of the procedure bead (N30a-09; ledger
   event `ground.feedback`) and read here; the estimator is restated for the reader. It is fed automatically
   by scoring (step 10 of Ground's procedure: feed
   discrepancy back). For each cell and metric, from the scored pairs: σ̂_r² = max(0, mean((y_i − ŷ_i)²) −
   mean instrument variance − mean simulation sampling variance), stored with n and the pool hash. For an
   uncalibrated cell the value is a declared prior from the core's contract, marked `assumed`; the report
   prints which it used.
5. **Rows.** For each candidate real batch size k ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30}: cost (k episodes; in GPU-second
   equivalents only if an exchange rate is declared), s̃, EVSI, and the ratio EVSI/cost when a rate exists.
   The rows are handed to `experiment.suggest` as experiments of kind `real-batch`, each with its model class
   stated and with the direct measurement of the decision quantity listed beside it as a comparator.
6. **Recommendation.** The action "recommend k more real episodes" of core DC-07 returns the smallest k such
   that (a) the cell reaches its calibration floor (n + k ≥ 19) when a band is what is asked for, and (b) for a
   decision between finalists, the fixed-n pre-analysis shows the registered verdict reachable at k and the
   row's value clears the studio's budget price when a rate exists. At most 30 episodes are recommended per
   decision (TARGET); beyond that the answer is "inconclusive, no-claim". If |m| is so large that no outcome
   could change the choice, EVSI is zero to working precision and the recommendation is to measure nothing.
7. **Conditions are not the planner's.** The planner chooses how many, never which: the conditions of the k
   episodes are the next k draws of the registered counter-based stream for the cell. The interference test of
   the studio's core composition, "assigned conditions equal the draw", is evaluated on every recommended
   batch that is later ingested.
8. **Advisory only.** Every number here has result kind `advisory`. It cannot be passed where a verdict or an
   establishable result is required. A wrong variance model costs efficiency and never validity.

Determinism: closed forms, class D2. Evidence class Estimated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The substituted case reproduces: s̃ = 0.0959 and EVSI = 0.0251 (uncalibrated, 20 real pairs);
      EVSI = 0.0084 for simulation; EVSI = 0.0035 for the same real batch in the calibrated cell.
- [ ] After a scoring run the ledger holds σ̂_r² for the cell with its n; before any, the row is marked
      `assumed`.
- [ ] On a fixture where every admissible parameter value implies the same decision, the recommendation is to
      measure nothing and the value is zero.
- [ ] A request that would need more than 30 episodes returns "inconclusive, no-claim".
- [ ] With no declared exchange rate the cost column is in episodes and no ratio is printed.
- [ ] The conditions of a recommended batch equal the registered draw; a batch whose logged conditions differ
      fails the interference test and is reported.
- [ ] An attempt to cite a row as a verdict is refused by the advisory type wall.
- [ ] Every row lists the direct measurement as a comparator with its cost.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/planner.rs`: `evsi_closed_form` (z = 0.6, s̃ = 0.05: 0.008434);
`variance_removed_by_measurement` (w = 0.0125, v = 0.0045: 0.009191, s̃ = 0.09587);
`uncalibrated_real_batch_value` (0.02510); `calibrated_real_batch_value` (w = 0.0029: s̃ = 0.03371, 0.003449);
`simulation_cannot_remove_reality_gap`; `evsi_zero_when_choice_fixed` (m = 0.5, s̃ = 0.05: below 10⁻¹²);
`gap_variance_estimate_floored_at_zero`; `assumed_marked_before_any_score`; `recommend_smallest_k_to_floor`
(n = 12: k = 7); `cap_at_30`; `no_rate_no_ratio`; `conditions_are_next_draws`; `rows_are_advisory_kind`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_drift.sh` (bead N30a-44) asks `frob experiment suggest` for a rehearsal cell before and after
calibration and logs the real-batch rows (k, s̃, EVSI), the simulation row, which is larger in each state, the
recommended k and the interference-test result.

### Known answers and negative controls

The worked card for experiment value: 0.0251 against 0.0084 uncalibrated; 0.0035 against 0.0084 calibrated.
Planted failures: a planner that lets simulation remove σ_r² (simulation then always wins: caught by
`simulation_cannot_remove_reality_gap`); a planner that picks the conditions with the largest predicted gap
(the interference test fails); a row emitted as a verdict (refused). Metamorphic relation: scaling m and every
standard deviation by one factor scales EVSI by the same factor. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug
and release; equal to 10⁻¹² across platforms.

## Considerations

- Open question OPEN-GROUND-4 (the exchange rate between operator time and GPU-seconds in the experiment
  table; owner: project lead; blocks nothing). Fail-closed default: real episodes are recommended, never
  scheduled, and no rate is assumed.
- The Gaussian model ranks experiments. Its intervals are scored against registered predictions, and if stated
  80% intervals cover outside 65 to 92% over thirty experiments the studio's core falls back to its fixed order;
  this bead's rows are then listed unranked.
- If the kill gate on value-of-information ranking has fired in the studio, the rows are still produced (cost,
  s̃, EVSI) and are listed without ranking; the calibration-floor part of the recommendation is unaffected.
- σ̂_r² from twenty pairs is itself uncertain by roughly a third; the row prints the n behind it.
