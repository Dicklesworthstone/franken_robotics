## Background

A calibration in Ground belongs to one cell: (design, set of physical units, controller identity, firmware,
surface class, task family, envelope box over the recorded conditions). A reality band and the `admitted` state
of identified cards are valid only inside it. Two of the four ways a calibration ends need no statistics at
all. If any component of the cell changes, the new episodes are not draws from the calibrated population, by
definition: this is a **scope change**, and it fires immediately and deterministically. And a calibration
grows old: lubricant ages and parts wear whether or not anyone is looking, so a calibration **expires** by age
or operating hours. The other two triggers, the rank and miss monitors, are in BD-394.

This bead also owns what happens when any trigger fires. Retirement has to be one transaction with visible
consequences: the status becomes `retired(cell, reason)`, reports revert to `reality: no-claim`, randomization
widths go back to the prior, and every verdict that cited the band is marked stale. A retirement that only
flips a flag, leaving old reports and narrowed randomization in use, would be worse than no monitoring.

Rejected alternatives: treating a firmware update as "probably fine" unless a monitor fires (at twenty
calibration points the monitors see only gross shifts); retiring silently; deleting the retired record (the
reason and the history are evidence).

## Scope

In: the scope-change detector and its seven components; the expiry rule; the manual `--retire` form; the
retirement transaction; reversion of reports and randomization; stale marking; restart inside the new regime.
Out: the statistical monitors (BD-394); the default expiry limits as a decision (N30b-27); the revalidation
engine itself.

## Technical approach

1. **Scope change.** Evaluated at every `ground.ingest` and whenever a consumer asks for a band or an admitted
   card with a context. The context's cell components are compared with the calibrated cell's:

   | Component | Change that fires | How it is observed |
   |---|---|---|
   | Controller | The controller identity (content hash) differs | Manifest `rig.controller`; the model's bound controller |
   | Firmware | The firmware string as reported by the device differs | Manifest `rig.firmware` |
   | Units and parts | A unit leaves or joins the set; a part is replaced or re-lubricated | Manifest `rig.units`; a maintenance event declared in the dataset |
   | Surface | The surface class or its condition differs | Manifest `rig.surface` |
   | Envelope | A recorded condition leaves the box | Each episode's recorded conditions |
   | Model identity | The `ModelId` differs, including a new card version and a kernel change that alters bits | The identity of the simulating side |
   | Instruments | An instrument's calibration identity differs | Manifest `instrument.calibration` |

   A difference retires the calibration for that context at once, with reason `scope:<component>` and the old
   and new values. An episode outside the envelope does not retire the cell for episodes inside it: the
   episode is refused as a member of the cell (it belongs to another cell) and no band is offered for it.
2. **Expiry.** A calibration expires at the earlier of an age limit and an operating-hours limit since its
   calibration event, and always on part replacement. The limits are contract constants of the decision core;
   the fail-closed default while open question OPEN-GROUND-3 is undecided is 90 days or 200 operating hours,
   whichever comes first. Age is measured against the acquisition dates in manifests and the ledger's recorded
   time of the query, not the host clock at render time; operating hours come from the datasets' declared
   hours. A dataset that declares no operating hours makes the hours limit `unknown`, and the age limit alone
   applies, with a finding.
3. **Manual retirement.** `frob ground band --retire --cell <c> --reason <text>` needs capability
   `ground:retire` and an idempotency key. It runs the same transaction with reason `manual:<text>`.
4. **Retirement transaction** (one ledger transaction; idempotent by cell and trigger):
   1. append `ground.cell.retired` with the cell, reason, trigger values and the monitor state;
   2. write the `retirement` record; the cell's status becomes `retired(cell, reason)`;
   3. mark the cell's band records and the `admitted` state of its cards as not usable: a consumer that asks
      for them is refused with FROB-REF-897 (use of a retired calibration), whose payload is the retirement
      reason and whose first repair is to recalibrate;
   4. hand the retired band and card identifiers to the revalidation engine as changed inputs: every verdict,
      scorecard and report that cited them is marked stale, and a later citation of a stale item is refused
      until it is revalidated;
   5. randomization widths that had narrowed revert to the prior cards' widths for new training manifests.
5. **Reports.** Any report generated after retirement prints `reality: no-claim` and, in its warnings, the
   retirement reason and date. A report generated before retirement is not rewritten; it is stale, and a
   reader who resolves its citations sees that.
6. **Restart.** Calibration restarts inside the new regime: the new cell key (with the changed component) is
   `uncalibrated`; pairs from the old cell never enter the new pool (pooling across cells is refused with
   FROB-REF-814). The retired cell's data moves to the regression set.

Determinism: scope comparison and expiry are class D0. Evidence: retirement is a ledger fact, not a claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-GROUND-07, last clause (TARGET): every scope change fires. Seven planted changes, one per component,
      each retire the calibration at the first ingest or query that shows the change, seven of seven.
- [ ] An out-of-envelope episode is refused as a member of the cell and does not retire it for in-envelope
      episodes.
- [ ] At 91 days, or at 201 operating hours, or on a declared part replacement, the calibration is retired with
      reason `expiry`; at 89 days and 199 hours it is not.
- [ ] After any retirement: the status line of a new report is `reality: no-claim`; a request for the band is
      refused with FROB-REF-897 carrying the reason; every verdict that cited the band is listed stale by the
      revalidation engine; a training manifest compiled afterwards has the prior widths.
- [ ] The transaction is atomic: a kill at any point leaves either no retirement or a complete one.
- [ ] `--retire` without the capability is refused; with it, the record carries `manual:` and the caller's
      identity.
- [ ] No pair of the retired cell appears in the pool of the restarted cell.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/retire.rs`: `scope_change_controller`, `_firmware`, `_unit_set`,
`_part_replaced`, `_surface`, `_model_identity`, `_instrument_calibration` (each fires with the right reason);
`kernel_bit_change_is_model_identity_change`; `out_of_envelope_episode_not_member`;
`expiry_age_boundary` (89, 90, 91 days); `expiry_hours_boundary` (199, 200, 201 h);
`expiry_uses_recorded_time_not_host_clock`; `missing_hours_applies_age_only_with_finding`;
`retired_band_refused_897_with_reason`; `dependants_marked_stale` (three verdicts citing the band);
`randomization_reverts_to_prior`; `retirement_idempotent`; `manual_retire_needs_capability`;
`restart_pool_is_empty`; `retired_data_moves_to_regression_set`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_band.sh` (bead N30b-34) plants each scope change through a second rehearsal bundle,
ingests it, and logs the retirement reason, the old and new component values, the status line of a report made
before and after, the refusal of the band with its code, and the list of items marked stale. It then advances
the fixture's recorded time past the expiry limit and logs the same.

### Known answers and negative controls

The "every scope change fires" clause of the drift gate: seven of seven. Planted failures: a detector that
ignores instrument calibration identity (one of seven missed: the gate fails); a retirement that updates the
status but skips stale marking (the stale list is empty: caught); an expiry computed from the host clock (a
test with a shifted host clock passes when it should not). Chaos lane: kill at each of the five transaction
steps. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- Open question OPEN-GROUND-3 (calibration expiry by age and operating hours; owner: Ground lead; blocks the
  first real cell). The limits above are defaults until N30b-27 records a decision; they are contract
  constants, so changing them is a new decision-policy version.
- Scope change is deliberately strict. A firmware patch that "only fixes logging" still retires; the repair is
  cheap if nothing changed (recalibration episodes will be exchangeable and a new band of similar width
  appears) and essential if something did.
- A maintenance event that nobody declared cannot be detected here; the monitors are the backstop, and their
  power at small n is printed.
- Stale marking can be wide: one retired band may touch many reports. The revalidation engine reports the
  count and the cost before anything is rerun.
