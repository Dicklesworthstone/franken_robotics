## Background

Every Frontier mechanism of the program needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded: a
small experiment with existing tools, outside the workspace, registered before it runs, whose outcome is not
known in advance. A pilot that cannot fail is not a pilot. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded,
and a work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded: its fallback ships.

This is the pilot for two mechanisms of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality:
split-conformal reality bands per calibrated cell, and the drift monitors that retire a calibration. Both rest
on one premise, exchangeability inside a cell, and the pilot tests whether the machinery behaves as the
arithmetic says when the "reality" is a simulator whose contact model differs from the one doing the
predicting. It needs no hardware and costs hours of CPU and one to two engineer-days.

What the arithmetic says (FACT): with n = 20 calibration pairs at a 90% target, the half-width is the 19th
smallest residual, and realized coverage conditional on the calibration set is Beta(19, 2): mean 19/21 = 0.905,
standard deviation 0.063, below 0.80 with probability 0.069.

## Scope

In: the protocol, frozen before execution; the falsifier in operational form; the ceiling; the fallback; the
record. Out: the production mechanisms (BD-393, BD-394), which depend on this bead; the synthetic rehearsal of
the subsystem (BD-388), which is larger and comes later.

## Technical approach

1. **Program.** A standalone program under `pilots/ground-bands/` (outside the workspace crates), using the
   referee through the airlock and `fs-eproc` at its recorded pin. No production crate depends on it.
2. **Stand-in reality.** Simulator P (predicting) is the referee with nominal contact parameters. Simulator T
   (truth) is the referee with a perturbed contact model: friction coefficient scaled by 0.85 and contact
   dissipation scaled by 1.3 (registered values). Task family A: the loaded cart crossing a 12 mm sill, metric
   crossing time, conditions (payload offset, approach speed) drawn by address. Shifted family B: the same task
   on a surface whose friction is a further 30% lower in T only.
3. **Resample** (1,000 times, seeds registered): draw 20 calibration conditions of A; pair P's prediction with
   T's outcome; residual r = |y − ŷ|; q̂ = 19th smallest. Then (a) 200 held-out A conditions: record coverage;
   (b) ask for a band on B with zero B pairs: record whether one is issued; (c) feed 20 B episodes into the A
   monitors (rank monitor with slack max(1/√20, 0.02) = 0.224; miss monitor with p₀ = 0.22, p₁ = 0.5, threshold
   20): record whether either fires. The monitors are armed fresh at e-value 1 for (c); the 200 held-out A
   conditions of (a) are scored for coverage only and are not fed to them, so the alarm clause measures power
   from a fresh start (covered episodes fed first would shrink the e-value and lower it).
4. **Falsifier, operational** (any one holds → the mechanisms are not funded as specified):
   - the mean held-out coverage on A over the 1,000 resamples differs from 19/21 by more than 0.01;
   - a band is issued for B without B data in any resample;
   - B episodes fed as A raise no alarm: an alarm within 20 episodes in fewer than 90% of resamples.
5. **Ceiling, computed before the run.** The mechanisms have no lever on time or compute. Their only possible
   saving is in real-trial count through pairing with simulation, bounded near 1.3 (published savings of 20 to
   25% of hardware evaluation effort). No speed claim is tested.
6. **Fallback and kill gate.** KILL-BAND: bands are withdrawn; reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw
   residual table.
7. **Record.** One entry in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`,
   N38a-09): mechanism and tier (bands and drift, `F`), the protocol hash, the falsifier, the ceiling, the
   fallback and kill gate, the cost, the tools and pins, and then the appended result: the three measured
   quantities with their intervals, the verdict (the register's `falsifier-holds` or `falsifier-rejected`),
   and what was revised if it held. The protocol entry's ledger sequence precedes
   the first resample.

Everything the pilot produces is labelled `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol entry exists with its hash before any resample runs.
- [ ] The result entry holds: mean coverage on A with its standard error (expected about 0.002); the count of
      bands issued for B (falsifier if above zero); the fraction of resamples with an alarm within 20 B
      episodes.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded whichever way it falls; if the falsifier holds, BD-393 and BD-394 are not started
      as specified and the review log states the revision or the adoption of the fallback.
- [ ] The pilot re-runs from the registry entry and reproduces every count exactly on the same machine.
- [ ] No output of the pilot carries a status other than `synthetic`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's own test module: `quantile_is_19th_of_20`; `beta_reference_values` (0.9048, 0.0626, 0.0692);
`miss_arithmetic` (four misses 26.68, three 11.74); `resample_seeds_are_registered_list`;
`b_request_with_zero_pairs_is_refused_by_bucket_minimum`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_ground_bands.sh`, header `bead=N30b-14 milestone=M5 labels=ground,pilot seed=1`: runs the
pilot from its registry entry, sourcing `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_ground_bands/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` the protocol hash, per falsifier clause the measured
value against its threshold (`e2e_metric coverage_A <v> 1 >= 0.894762`, `<= 0.914762`, which are 19/21 ∓ 0.01,
the falsifier's own bounds; `bands_for_B <count> bands ==
0`; `alarm_within_20 <v> 1 >= 0.90`), and a summary with the verdict. The script exits zero when the
measurement is complete, whatever the verdict; it exits non-zero only if the pilot could not be run or its
controls failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

Controls that show the pilot can fail: a variant with the quantile index ⌈n·(1 − α)⌉ = 18 must trip the first
clause (mean coverage about 0.857); a variant whose bucket key ignores the task family must trip the second; a
variant in which B equals A must trip the third (alarms then occur at the null rate). Each control is run and
its trip recorded beside the result.

## Considerations

- The third clause is the informative one. At n = 20 the rank monitor sees only gross shifts and the miss
  monitor needs a post-shift miss probability of about 0.6 to retire within 20 episodes with probability 0.9.
  If a 30% friction change in T does not move crossing-time residuals that far, the clause fails, and the
  honest revision is to state the detectable shift size instead of promising detection.
- The pilot's stand-in reality shares the referee's integrator with the predicting side. It can show the
  machinery is sound; it cannot show that real residuals are exchangeable.
- Open question OPEN-GROUND-1 (no public paired dataset with enough robots per class was identified) is why
  the stand-in is a second simulator.
