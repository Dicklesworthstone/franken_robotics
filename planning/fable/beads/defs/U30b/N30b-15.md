## Background

Every Frontier mechanism needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its work is funded: small, run with
existing tools outside the workspace, registered before it runs, with an outcome not known in advance. A work
package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded and its fallback ships.

This is the pilot for Ground's identification of actuator parameters from logs. Ground is the subsystem that
compares simulation with measured reality. The full mechanism identifies a joint in three passes: a linear
regression away from sticking and saturation; an output-error fit by an iterated Gauss–Newton update on windows
of summary features; and a third pass for parameters with no useful derivative, in which delay is profiled on a
grid and static friction with the Stribeck speed is estimated by an ensemble. The pilot asks the cheapest
version of the question that could still come out badly: on one simulated joint with known planted parameters,
with excitation chosen by the design rules, do the three passes recover what was planted, and do they refuse
when asked for something the data cannot give? It needs no hardware and costs hours of CPU and one to two
engineer-days.

The joint model: (I(q) + J_a)·q̈ + b·q̇ + τ_f(q̇) + g(q) = N·η·k_t·i(t − d), with friction
τ_f(q̇) = [τ_c + (τ_s − τ_c)·exp(−(q̇/v_s)²)]·sgn(q̇) while moving and a stiction row holding q̇ = 0 while the
applied torque is inside ±τ_s. J_a is the armature, b viscous friction, τ_c Coulomb torque, τ_s static torque,
v_s the Stribeck speed, d the delay.

## Scope

In: the protocol, frozen before execution; the falsifier in operational form; the ceiling; the fallback; the
record. Out: the production mechanism (BD-391, N30a-23, N30a-24), which depends on this bead; the 100-rig gate
battery, which is the mechanism's own measurement.

## Technical approach

1. **Program.** Standalone, under `pilots/ground-identify/`, using the referee at actuator level L1 with the
   stiction row, and `fs-assimilate` at its recorded pin. No production crate depends on it.
2. **Planted joint** (registered): vertical axis, known link inertia 0.020 kg·m², N = 9, η = 0.9, nominal
   k_t = 0.10 N·m/A; planted J_a = 6.0 × 10⁻³ kg·m², b = 0.050 N·m·s/rad, τ_c = 0.30 N·m, τ_s = 0.45 N·m,
   v_s = 0.10 rad/s, d = 2.625 ms on a 1/8 ms grid at a 1 ms control period. Encoder noise 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ rad with a
   shared bias of up to 3 × 10⁻⁴ rad; current noise 0.02 A. Priors off by registered factors between 0.5 and 2.
3. **Designed excitation** (from the experiment grammar): slow torque ramps from rest in both directions;
   velocity steps and coast-down; multisine torque unloaded and with a known payload of 0.010 kg·m²; a step
   command with a shared mark.
4. **Runs.** (a) The three passes on the designed excitation, 50 noise seeds. (b) Bait 1: the same without the
   known payload and without a second moving joint, asking for J_a separately from link inertia (only
   I_link + J_a appears). (c) Bait 2: unidirectional motion only, asking for τ_c (τ_c·sgn(q̇) + bias is one
   number). (d) Bait 3: no shared mark, asking for d (only delay plus clock offset is identified).
5. **Falsifier, operational** (either holds → identification is not funded as specified):
   - a planted parameter is missed by more than 10% after the designed excitation: in run (a), for any of J_a,
     b, τ_c, τ_s, v_s the median relative error over the 50 seeds exceeds 10%, or the delay minimizer is more
     than one grid step from the truth in more than 5 of 50 seeds;
   - an aliased pair is fitted without refusal: any of the three bait runs returns a value for the baited
     parameter instead of the identified combination and a refusal.
6. **Ceiling, computed before the run.** No speed or compute benefit is claimed. The benefit, if any, is model
   accuracy: published studies report 46% more travel distance and 75% less rotational deviation after
   identifying armature, friction and delay, and that is context, not a target of this pilot.
7. **Fallback and kill gate.** KILL-GROUND: no identification; Ground keeps admission, clocks, frozen
   predictions and bands on direct measurements with prior cards; a card field changes only by direct bench
   measurement of that field.
8. **Record.** One entry in `frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` with mechanism and tier, protocol hash,
   falsifier, ceiling, fallback and kill gate, cost, tools and pins, then the result: per parameter the median
   and worst relative error; the delay hit count; per bait the returned object; the verdict.

Everything the pilot produces is labelled `synthetic`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol entry exists with its hash before any run.
- [ ] The result entry holds per-parameter errors over 50 seeds, the delay hit count and the three bait
      outcomes, each against its clause.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded whichever way it falls; if the falsifier holds, BD-391 is not started as
      specified and the review log states the revision or the adoption of the fallback.
- [ ] The pilot re-runs from the registry entry and reproduces its counts on the same machine fingerprint (the
      ensemble part is recorded with the fingerprint and not compared across machines).
- [ ] The summary-feature window definition used is stored with the result, because the sibling's update
      admits at most 256 rows and the windowing is part of what is being piloted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the pilot's own test module: `planted_joint_simulates_stick_and_slip` (the ramp shows breakaway at
0.45 N·m within 2%); `regressor_recovers_noise_free` (I + J_a, b, τ_c to 10⁻⁶ relative on noise-free data);
`rows_at_most_256_per_update`; `bait_runs_built_as_registered`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_ground_identify.sh`, header `bead=N30b-15 milestone=M5 labels=ground,pilot seed=1`: runs the
pilot from its registry entry and logs to `target/e2e-logs/pilot_ground_identify/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` the
protocol hash, each parameter's median relative error against 0.10, the delay hits against 45 of 50, each
bait's outcome (`refused-with-combination` or `value-returned`), and a summary with the verdict. It exits zero
when the measurement is complete, whatever the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls

Controls that show the pilot can fail: a variant whose excitation omits the torque ramps must miss τ_s and v_s
(first clause trips); a variant with a ridge-regularized fit must return values on all three baits (second
clause trips); a variant that differentiates through the delay must leave the delay at its prior.

## Considerations

- The pilot's truth and its fit share one model class, so success shows the estimator is sound inside the
  class. The omitted-law behaviour (a truth with a term the fit lacks) is tested later, at the mechanism's
  gate, on sealed data.
- If only the ensemble part fails (τ_s, v_s), the honest revision is narrower than the kill fallback: identify
  the smooth parameters and hold stick–slip parameters at measured-by-bench values.
- The 256-row cap may force windows so coarse that the multisine carries too little information; the stored
  window definition makes that visible.
