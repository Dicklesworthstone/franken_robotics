## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at the milestone named for it. A trigger, a threshold or a fallback
changes only by a dated amendment with its reason, made before the measurement it governs. A kill gate that
fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan, and from then on the capability is described
by the fallback's name.

KILL-GROUND governs the most ambitious mechanism of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with
measured reality: identifying actuator parameters from logs by a staged fit (a linear pass, an output-error
pass, a delay profile and a stick–slip ensemble) and writing them as new card versions.

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-04 fails after one repair cycle. GATE-GROUND-04 (claim kind: Known answer; TARGET):
  planted actuator parameters within 10% on at least 95 of 100 synthetic rigs; identified cards cut held-out
  trajectory error by at least 30% against prior cards; the omitted-law candidate is rejected in at least 90 of
  100.
- **Fallback:** no identification. Ground keeps admission, clocks, frozen predictions and bands on direct
  measurements with prior cards; a card field changes only by direct bench measurement of that field.
- **Decided at:** M5.

Why the fallback is acceptable: a wrong identification is worse than none, because it writes `measured` on a
number that was fitted for the wrong reason. Direct measurement of one field (a resistance with a meter, a
torque constant at stall with a torque sensor, a mass on a scale) needs no estimator to be trusted.

## Scope

In: how the measurement is read; the repair cycle; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until
decided; the record. Out: running the battery (N30b-32); the mechanisms (BD-391 and its parts).

## Technical approach

1. **Measurement read.** The row of GATE-GROUND-04 written by the end-to-end battery on the rehearsal's
   100-rig fixture: three counts or ratios (rigs within 10%: k₁ of 100; error ratio identified/prior: ρ; omitted
   law rejected: k₃ of 100), each with its caught negative controls. A row whose controls were not caught is
   not a measurement.
2. **Decision rule.** Pass: k₁ ≥ 95 and ρ ≤ 0.70 and k₃ ≥ 90. Otherwise one repair cycle: a single round of
   changes addressing a defect that the controls or the per-rig log localize, declared in writing before the
   re-run, with the fixture, seeds and thresholds unchanged. The second measurement decides. Tuning the
   estimator's settings against the 100 rigs until the counts pass is not a repair; the declaration must name a
   defect, not a parameter sweep.
3. **Not fired.** The KILL-GROUND row records `not fired` with both measurements if there were two;
   GATE-GROUND-04 is `pass`; identification is described as implemented (Frontier), with its known gaps.
4. **Fired** (status `killed: fallback adopted`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.identify` keeps only direct-measurement forms: each call names one card field and one bench
     measurement of that field; passes B and C are removed from the build; a request for a fitted parameter is
     refused with FROB-REF-879 and the repair "measure the field directly", naming the bench experiment.
   - `ground.excite` stays as a report of which experiment measures which field.
   - Card versions are still written through the same writer, with provenance `measured`, the dataset hash and
     the cell, one field at a time; the joint covariance block is diagonal.
   - Frozen predictions, scoring and bands continue on prior cards plus directly measured fields; bands will be
     wider, and that width is the honest statement.
   - The energy cross-check stays as a finding on predictions; the label `wrong-reason` no longer has a fit to
     attach to.
   - Beads that exist only on the surviving branch of identification are closed as not built: the delay profile
     and stick–slip ensemble (N30b-02), the output-error part of BD-391, the whole-robot contact refinement in
     N30b-05; the upstream work items for windowed updates and the ensemble's cross-machine statement lose their
     consumer and are marked so.
   - The inventory row "actuator identification from logs" is described as "direct bench measurement of card
     fields".
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Identification runs only on the synthetic rehearsal; no card
   version identified by a fit is `admitted` in any cell of origin `real`.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-GROUND.md`: the measurement(s), the repair declaration if any, the decision, the
   downstream list as executed. Rows of `registries/gates.toml` for KILL-GROUND and GATE-GROUND-04, written by a
   session that did not implement identification.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M5 exit with the trigger evaluated against its numbers.
- [ ] If a repair cycle was used, its declaration is dated before the second measurement and names a defect;
      fixture, seeds and thresholds are unchanged between the two measurements (hashes equal).
- [ ] If fired: `frob ground identify --stage actuators` without a direct measurement is refused; the closed
      beads carry a typed reason citing this decision; the README's status table names the fallback.
- [ ] If not fired: GATE-GROUND-04 is `pass` with measured values and caught controls.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_ground.rs`: `decision_table` (95, 0.70, 90 pass; 94 fails; 0.71 fails; 89
fails); `second_measurement_decides`; `repair_declaration_must_precede_rerun`;
`fixture_hash_unchanged_between_measurements`; `row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ground.sh`, header `bead=N30b-17 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads the GATE-GROUND-04 rows from the registry, recomputes the three clauses from the
battery's log, checks the repair declaration's date if present, and prints the decision. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/kill_ground/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the three measured values against their thresholds,
the control results, the fixture and seed hashes, and a summary with the trigger evaluation and
`gates: ["KILL-GROUND", "GATE-GROUND-04"]`. It exits zero when the decision can be made, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls for the decision itself: a second measurement on a changed fixture (rejected by the hash
check); a registry row marked `pass` with an uncaught control (rejected); a repair declaration dated after the
re-run (rejected).

## Considerations

- The three clauses test different things: recovery inside the model class, usefulness on held-out data, and
  honesty outside the class. A build that passes the first and fails the third is the dangerous one, and the
  rule treats it as a failure.
- The decision is scoped to the rehearsal. Real data may later show that identification inside the class is
  not enough; that reopens the question through a new pilot and a new decision, not by editing this one.
- If the kill fires, risk RISK-42 (a model that fits for the wrong reason) is reduced, not removed: prior cards
  can also be wrong, and the report's share of guessed card fields stays high.
