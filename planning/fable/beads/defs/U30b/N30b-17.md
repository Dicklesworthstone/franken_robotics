## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at the milestone named for it. A trigger, a threshold or a fallback
changes only by a dated amendment with its reason, made before the measurement it governs. A kill gate that
fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan, and from then on the capability is described
by the fallback's name.

KILL-GROUND governs the most ambitious mechanism of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with
measured reality: identifying actuator parameters from logs by a staged fit (a linear pass, an output-error
pass, a delay profile and a stick–slip ensemble) and writing them as new card versions.

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-04 fails after one repair cycle. GATE-GROUND-04 (claim kind: Known answer; TARGET):
  planted actuator parameters within 10% on at least 95 of 100 synthetic rigs; identified cards cut held-out
  trajectory error by at least 30% against prior cards; the omitted-law candidate is rejected in at least 90 of
  100.
- **Fallback:** no identification. Ground keeps admission, clocks, frozen predictions and bands on direct
  measurements with prior cards; a card field changes only by direct bench measurement of that field.
- **Decided at:** M5.

Why the fallback is acceptable: a wrong identification is worse than none, because it writes `measured` on a
number that was fitted for the wrong reason. Direct measurement of one field (a resistance with a meter, a
torque constant at stall with a torque sensor, a mass on a scale) needs no estimator to be trusted.

## Scope

In: how the measurement is read; the repair cycle; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until
decided; the record. Out: running the battery (N30a-26); the mechanisms (BD-391 and its parts).

## Technical approach

1. **Measurement read.** The record of GATE-GROUND-04, entered by its one recorder (N30a-26) from the receipt
   of the gate's tier run on the rehearsal's 100-rig fixture: three counts or ratios (rigs within 10%: k₁ of
   100; error ratio identified/prior: ρ; omitted law rejected: k₃ of 100), each with its caught negative
   controls. A row whose controls were not caught is not a measurement. This bead reads the row and its
   receipts and writes neither.
2. **Decision rule.** Pass: k₁ ≥ 95 and ρ ≤ 0.70 and k₃ ≥ 90. Otherwise one repair cycle, in the program's one
   reading of those words (the kill-record bead N40-03): a single bounded attempt to fix the cause, opened
   before the re-run by a `note` line in the measuring bead's log (N30a-26) with `cause = "repair-cycle:
   <failed receipt hash>: <hypothesis>"`, the hypothesis naming a defect that the controls or the per-rig log
   localize; and closed by a re-measurement with the same thresholds and controls on the same fixture
   construction. Because the first measurement exposes per-rig results that a fix could be tuned to, the
   re-measurement runs on a fresh population of 100 rigs drawn by R-04's registered construction with a new
   seed, and the opening line records that seed (an earlier text of this bead kept the rigs and seeds
   unchanged, which is exactly what would let a tuned fix pass). The cycle changes the mechanism, never the
   fixture's construction, the thresholds or the controls. The second measurement decides: a value still
   outside its bound is recorded `fail`, N30a-26 closes `recorded`, and this decision becomes ready. Tuning
   the estimator's settings until the counts pass is not a repair; the hypothesis must name a defect, not a
   parameter sweep. A second cycle is not this bead's to grant: it is a `deferred` decision with its own ADR.
3. **Not fired.** The `decision` entry of the KILL-GROUND row has outcome `not-fired` and cites the receipts
   (both measurements if there were two); GATE-GROUND-04 reads `pass` in its own record; identification is
   described as implemented (Frontier), with its known gaps.
4. **Fired** (outcome `fired`; GATE-GROUND-04, recorded `fail`, then reads `killed` with `detail =
   "KILL-GROUND: <fallback>"`, printed "killed: fallback adopted", through the register's `apply_decisions`,
   the one place a gate becomes `killed`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.identify` keeps only direct-measurement forms: each call names one card field and one bench
     measurement of that field; passes B and C are removed from the build; pass A stays only as a diagnostic
     (its residual plot against time and velocity) and none of its estimates is ever written to a card; a
     request for a fitted parameter is
     refused with FROB-REF-879 and the repair "measure the field directly", naming the bench experiment.
   - `ground.excite` stays as a report of which experiment measures which field.
   - Card versions are still written through the same writer, with provenance `measured`, the dataset hash and
     the cell, one field at a time; the joint covariance block is diagonal.
   - Frozen predictions, scoring and bands continue on prior cards plus directly measured fields; bands will be
     wider, and that width is the honest statement.
   - The energy cross-check stays as a finding on predictions; the label `wrong-reason` no longer has a fit to
     attach to.
   - Beads that exist only for identification by fitting (the branch the kill removes) are closed as not built: the delay profile
     and stick–slip ensemble (N30a-23), the output-error part of BD-391, the whole-robot contact refinement in
     N30a-25; the upstream work items for windowed updates and the ensemble's cross-machine statement lose their
     consumer and are marked so.
   - The inventory row "actuator identification from logs" is described as "direct bench measurement of card
     fields".
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Identification runs only on the synthetic rehearsal; no card
   version identified by a fit is `admitted` in any cell of origin `real`.
6. **Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GROUND-M5.md` (the one form of a kill-gate decision; an
   earlier text of this bead named `docs/adr/KILL-GROUND.md`), with the required fields: the kill gate; the
   milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash
   beside the trigger's number (both measurements if there were two, with the cycle's opening line); the
   outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from then on; the beads re-read (each
   "stands on the fallback", "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"); the downstream
   list of item 4 as executed; and the recording session, which did not implement identification. The kill
   row's entry `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` in `registries/gates.toml`, with an
   outcome from the closed set: `not-fired` (at least one receipt cited), `fired`, `deferred` (with
   `deferred_to` and the reason, when a second cycle was granted by ADR or the measurement is not complete at
   the M5 exit) or `not-reached` (only if identification was never funded, its pilot's falsifier having held).
   This bead writes that entry and the ADR; it never writes GATE-GROUND-04's row, and no status `killed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-GROUND-M5.md` exists at the M5 exit with the required fields of item 6 and the
      trigger evaluated against its numbers, and the KILL-GROUND row's `decision` entry cites it with an
      outcome of the closed set; an ADR at any other path fails the register's load (`decision-adr-path`).
- [ ] If a repair cycle was used, its opening `note` line precedes the second measurement and names a defect;
      the thresholds, the controls and the fixture's construction (generator version and recipe hash) are
      equal between the two measurements, and the second ran on fresh rigs under the new seed the line records.
- [ ] If fired: `frob ground identify --stage actuators` without a direct measurement is refused; the closed
      beads carry a typed reason citing this decision; the README's status table names the fallback, and
      GATE-GROUND-04 prints "killed: fallback adopted" from `apply_decisions`, its stored row still `fail`.
- [ ] If not fired: GATE-GROUND-04 is `pass` with measured values and caught controls, and the entry cites its
      receipt.
- [ ] The decision can be recorded whichever way the measurement falls: nothing this bead depends on needs
      the gate to pass in order to close (BD-391 closes on its own criteria; N30a-26 closes `pass` or
      `recorded`).
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_ground.rs`: `decision_table` (95, 0.70, 90 pass; 94 fails; 0.71 fails; 89
fails); `second_measurement_decides`; `repair_note_must_precede_rerun` (a `repair-cycle` line dated after the
second receipt is rejected); `rerun_uses_fresh_rigs_same_construction` (the second receipt's rig seed differs
from the first and equals the one in the opening line; generator version, recipe hash, thresholds and control
list are equal; a re-run on the first population is rejected); `row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`;
`outcome_from_closed_set` (pass → `not-fired` with a receipt; fail after the cycle → `fired`; a granted second
cycle → `deferred` with `deferred_to`; "passed" and "killed: fallback adopted" are rejected as outcomes);
`adr_path_is_the_registered_form` (`docs/adr/KILL-GROUND.md` is refused `decision-adr-path`);
`fired_pass_a_is_diagnostic_only` (with the kill fired, pass A still prints its residual plot and no card
version carries a value it estimated).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ground.sh`, header `bead=N30b-17 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate
gates=KILL-GROUND,GATE-GROUND-04 seed=1 plants=late-repair-note,uncaught-control`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads the GATE-GROUND-04 record from the registry, recomputes the three clauses from the
receipts the record cites, checks the repair cycle's opening line and seed if present, and prints the outcome
the rule gives. It reads only: it writes no register row and no ADR, which are the recording session's. The
two plants are the negative controls below, on scratch copies of the receipts. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/kill_ground/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: each command with its exit code and
wall time; the three measured values against their thresholds with the receipt hashes; the control results;
the generator version, recipe hash and rig seed of each measurement; on failure the first clause or check that
could not be evaluated and the receipt path; and a summary with the trigger evaluation and
`gates: ["KILL-GROUND", "GATE-GROUND-04"]`. It exits zero when the decision can be made, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls for the decision itself: a second measurement on a changed fixture construction, or on the
first population of rigs (rejected); a registry row marked `pass` with an uncaught control (rejected); a
repair-cycle line dated after the re-run (rejected).

## Considerations

- The three clauses test different things: recovery inside the model class, usefulness on held-out data, and
  honesty outside the class. A build that passes the first and fails the third is the dangerous one, and the
  rule treats it as a failure.
- The decision is scoped to the rehearsal. Real data may later show that identification inside the class is
  not enough; that reopens the question through a new pilot and a new decision, not by editing this one.
- If the kill fires, risk RISK-42 (a model that fits for the wrong reason) is reduced, not removed: prior cards
  can also be wrong, and the report's share of guessed card fields stays high.
