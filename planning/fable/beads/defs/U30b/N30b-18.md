## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at its milestone. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from then on.

KILL-BAND governs the quantitative Reality statement of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with
measured reality: a split-conformal band per calibrated cell, watched by two drift monitors that retire it.

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-06 or GATE-GROUND-07 fails. GATE-GROUND-06 (Known answer; TARGET): mean in-cell
  coverage within 0.01 of 19/21; fraction of resamples below 0.80 within 0.03 of 0.069; zero bands for the
  shifted cell, the short cell or the unbuilt design. GATE-GROUND-07 (Known answer; TARGET): the planted drift
  retires the calibration within 20 episodes in at least 90% of resamples; false retirement at most 5% over
  10⁴ null runs; every scope change fires.
- **Fallback:** bands are withdrawn. Reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual table.
- **Decided at:** M5. This is also the decision point of bet BET-28 (calibration bands reach their stated
  coverage inside one calibrated robot and task family), whose own fallback is `reality: no-claim`.

Why the fallback is acceptable: a residual table is a fact (these predictions, these outcomes, these
differences), and it lets a reader judge the simulator without the program asserting a coverage level it could
not hold. A band that under-covers and is relied on is the most expensive failure the subsystem has.

## Scope

In: how the two measurements are read; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until decided;
the record. Out: running the batteries (N30a-39, N30a-44); the mechanisms; the verdict of bet BET-28 on its
three falsifier clauses (coverage on real held-out episodes; a band issued after the exchangeability alarm; a
band for a design never built), its pre-registration, its readings on real data and its row in the bet ledger
(BET-28, which reads this decision and never re-decides it).

## Technical approach

1. **Measurements read.** The records of GATE-GROUND-06 and GATE-GROUND-07, each entered by its one recorder
   (N30a-39 and N30a-44) from the receipt of the gate's tier run on the rehearsal, and read here, never
   written: mean coverage and its standard error; fraction below 0.80; counts of bands issued for the three
   forbidden cases; fraction retired within 20 episodes; null false-retirement fraction over 10⁴ runs; scope
   changes fired of the nine planted by the drift battery (N30a-44, battery C: controller, firmware, part
   replaced, part re-lubricated, surface, condition outside the envelope, instrument calibration, card version,
   kernel build); each with its caught negative controls.
2. **Decision rule.** Not fired only if every clause of both gates passes: |mean − 0.9048| ≤ 0.01;
   |fraction − 0.069| ≤ 0.03; zero forbidden bands; retirement fraction ≥ 0.90; null fraction ≤ 0.05; nine of
   nine (the count is read from the gate row, so a battery that plants more changes requires all of them). The register grants this kill gate no repair cycle: the gate's first complete recorded measurement
   decides. One pre-declared adjustment is not a re-scope, because it is settled before the gate is measured:
   if the null calibration of the monitors' proof obligation (N30b-24, 10⁴ null runs, run before the gate)
   shows the union of the two monitors above 5% false retirement, each runs at half the alarm level (threshold
   40) under a new decision-policy version, and both clauses of the drift gate are then measured under that
   version.
3. **Not fired.** The `decision` entry of the KILL-BAND row has outcome `not-fired` and cites the receipts of
   both gates; both gates read `pass` in their own records; the decision core for bands may leave shadow for
   cells of origin `real`; BET-28 stays open until real data reads it (the first real cell).
4. **Fired** (outcome `fired`; each of the two gates that was recorded `fail` then reads `killed` with
   `detail = "KILL-BAND: <fallback>"`, printed "killed: fallback adopted", through the register's
   `apply_decisions`, the one place a gate becomes `killed`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.band` issues nothing: its answer is the cell's residual table (prediction, outcome, residual,
     conditions, per pair) with n, and a refusal of any level; `--list` and `--status` report pools and no
     bands.
   - The status `calibrated` is never produced; a scored cell stays `candidate`, and the reality line reads
     `no-claim`.
   - The drift monitors are withdrawn with the bands. Scope change and expiry still retire the `admitted`
     state of cards.
   - The decision core stays on its fallback and the registry says so; the real-episode planner drops the
     calibration-floor part of its recommendation and keeps the value rows.
   - Identification, card updates, frozen predictions and scoring are unaffected: a candidate can still be
     admitted by a covering score.
   - The North-Star scenario of a calibrated band is not demonstrated; the false-coverage budget and the
     across-unit band are closed as not built.
   - BET-28's row in the bet ledger records the bet as decided against at the rehearsal stage. That row and
     the bet's own record (`docs/adr/bet-28-m5.md`) are written by the bet's decision bead, BET-28, which
     depends on this bead and cites this ADR; this bead writes the kill decision's ADR and the kill row's
     `decision` entry only.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** The decision core is in shadow: it records the band it would
   issue, and every report prints `reality: no-claim` (or `synthetic` on the rehearsal).
6. **Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BAND-M5.md` (the one form of a kill-gate decision, and the
   path BET-28 cites; an earlier text of this bead named `docs/adr/KILL-BAND.md`), with the required fields:
   the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; each measured value
   with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number, and the policy version in force; the outcome; the
   fallback in force and the name the capability carries from then on; the beads re-read (each "stands on the
   fallback", "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"); the downstream list of item 4 as
   executed; and the recording session, which implemented neither bands nor monitors. The kill row's entry
   `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }` in `registries/gates.toml`, with an outcome from the
   closed set: `not-fired` (the receipts of both gates cited), `fired`, `deferred` (with `deferred_to` and the
   reason, when a measurement is not complete at the M5 exit) or `not-reached` (only if bands were never
   funded, the pilot's falsifier having held). This bead writes that entry and the ADR; it never writes the
   two gates' rows, and no status `killed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-BAND-M5.md` exists at the M5 exit with the required fields of item 6 and each clause
      evaluated against its number, and the KILL-BAND row's `decision` entry cites it with an outcome of the
      closed set; an ADR at any other path fails the register's load (`decision-adr-path`).
- [ ] The decision can be recorded whichever way the measurements fall: nothing this bead depends on needs a
      gate to pass in order to close (BD-393 and BD-394 close on their own criteria; N30a-39 and N30a-44 close
      `pass` or `recorded`).
- [ ] If the half-level adjustment was used, the policy version changed before the measurement it governs, and
      both clauses of GATE-GROUND-07 were measured under the same version.
- [ ] If fired: `frob ground band --cell … --level 0.90 --json` returns the residual table and no band; no
      artifact in the store carries status `calibrated`; the closed beads carry a typed reason citing this
      decision; the failed gate prints "killed: fallback adopted" from `apply_decisions`, its stored row still
      `fail`.
- [ ] If not fired: both gates are `pass` with measured values and caught controls, and the entry cites their
      receipts.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_band.rs`: `decision_table` (mean 0.9148 passes, 0.9149 fails; fraction
0.099 passes, 0.100 fails; one forbidden band fails; retirement 0.899 fails; null 0.051 fails; eight of nine
scope changes fails); `either_gate_failing_fires`; `policy_version_consistent_across_clauses`;
`row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`; `outcome_from_closed_set` (all clauses pass → `not-fired`
with both receipts; any clause fails → `fired`; "passed" and "killed: fallback adopted" are rejected as
outcomes); `adr_path_is_the_registered_form` (`docs/adr/KILL-BAND.md` is refused `decision-adr-path`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_band.sh`, header `bead=N30b-18 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate
gates=KILL-BAND,GATE-GROUND-06,GATE-GROUND-07 seed=1 plants=mixed-policy-version,uncaught-control`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads both gate records, recomputes every clause from the receipts they cite, and prints
the outcome the rule gives. It reads only: it writes no register row and no ADR, which are the recording
session's. The two plants are the negative controls below, on scratch copies of the receipts. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/kill_band/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: each command with its exit code and wall
time; each measured value against its threshold with its receipt hash; the standard errors; the control
results; the policy version; on failure the first clause that could not be evaluated and the receipt path;
and a summary with the trigger evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-BAND", "GATE-GROUND-06", "GATE-GROUND-07"]`. It
exits zero when the decision can be made, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values the decision checks against: 19/21 = 0.9048; P(coverage < 0.80) = 0.0692 at n = 20. Negative
controls for the decision: a row in which the drift clause was measured at threshold 20 and the null clause at
40 (rejected); a row marked `pass` whose pooled-regime control was not caught (rejected).

## Considerations

- The tolerances of GATE-GROUND-06 are about five and four standard errors at 1,000 resamples, so a correct
  build fails by chance with negligible probability; a failure there means a defect, and the fallback is the
  right response until it is understood.
- The drift clause depends on a fixture property registered in advance (the post-drift miss probability); a
  failure caused by an under-powered fixture is a failure of the registered design and still fires. The
  honest alternative, stating the detectable shift size, is then proposed as a new mechanism with its own
  pilot.
- A fired KILL-BAND does not touch the reality line's gate: that line then reads `no-claim` everywhere, which
  is its resting state.
