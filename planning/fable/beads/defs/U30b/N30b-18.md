## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at its milestone. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from then on.

KILL-BAND governs the quantitative Reality statement of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with
measured reality: a split-conformal band per calibrated cell, watched by two drift monitors that retire it.

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-06 or GATE-GROUND-07 fails. GATE-GROUND-06 (Known answer; TARGET): mean in-cell
  coverage within 0.01 of 19/21; fraction of resamples below 0.80 within 0.03 of 0.069; zero bands for the
  shifted cell, the short cell or the unbuilt design. GATE-GROUND-07 (Known answer; TARGET): the planted drift
  retires the calibration within 20 episodes in at least 90% of resamples; false retirement at most 5% over
  10⁴ null runs; every scope change fires.
- **Fallback:** bands are withdrawn. Reports print `reality: no-claim` with the raw residual table.
- **Decided at:** M5. This is also the decision point of bet BET-28 (calibration bands reach their stated
  coverage inside one calibrated robot and task family), whose own fallback is `reality: no-claim`.

Why the fallback is acceptable: a residual table is a fact (these predictions, these outcomes, these
differences), and it lets a reader judge the simulator without the program asserting a coverage level it could
not hold. A band that under-covers and is relied on is the most expensive failure the subsystem has.

## Scope

In: how the two measurements are read; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until decided;
the record. Out: running the batteries (N30b-34); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

1. **Measurements read.** The rows of GATE-GROUND-06 and GATE-GROUND-07 written by the end-to-end battery on the
   rehearsal: mean coverage and its standard error; fraction below 0.80; counts of bands issued for the three
   forbidden cases; fraction retired within 20 episodes; null false-retirement fraction over 10⁴ runs; scope
   changes fired of seven; each with its caught negative controls.
2. **Decision rule.** Not fired only if every clause of both gates passes: |mean − 0.9048| ≤ 0.01;
   |fraction − 0.069| ≤ 0.03; zero forbidden bands; retirement fraction ≥ 0.90; null fraction ≤ 0.05; seven of
   seven. The register grants this kill gate no repair cycle: the gate's first complete recorded measurement
   decides. One pre-declared adjustment is not a re-scope, because it is settled before the gate is measured:
   if the null calibration of the monitors' proof obligation (N30b-24, 10⁴ null runs, run before the gate)
   shows the union of the two monitors above 5% false retirement, each runs at half the alarm level (threshold
   40) under a new decision-policy version, and both clauses of the drift gate are then measured under that
   version.
3. **Not fired.** KILL-BAND `not fired`; both gates `pass`; the decision core for bands may leave shadow for
   cells of origin `real`; BET-28 stays open until real data reads it (the first real cell).
4. **Fired** (status `killed: fallback adopted`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.band` issues nothing: its answer is the cell's residual table (prediction, outcome, residual,
     conditions, per pair) with n, and a refusal of any level; `--list` and `--status` report pools and no
     bands.
   - The status `calibrated` is never produced; a scored cell stays `candidate`, and the reality line reads
     `no-claim`.
   - The drift monitors are withdrawn with the bands. Scope change and expiry still retire the `admitted`
     state of cards.
   - The decision core stays on its fallback and the registry says so; the real-episode planner drops the
     calibration-floor part of its recommendation and keeps the value rows.
   - Identification, card updates, frozen predictions and scoring are unaffected: a candidate can still be
     admitted by a covering score.
   - The North-Star scenario of a calibrated band is not demonstrated; the false-coverage budget and the
     across-unit band are closed as not built.
   - BET-28's row in the bet ledger records the bet as decided against at the rehearsal stage.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** The decision core is in shadow: it records the band it would
   issue, and every report prints `reality: no-claim` (or `synthetic` on the rehearsal).
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-BAND.md` with the measurements, the policy version in force, the decision and the
   downstream list as executed; rows of `registries/gates.toml` for KILL-BAND and both gates, written by a
   session that did not implement bands or monitors.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M5 exit with each clause evaluated against its number.
- [ ] If the half-level adjustment was used, the policy version changed before the measurement it governs, and
      both clauses of GATE-GROUND-07 were measured under the same version.
- [ ] If fired: `frob ground band --cell … --level 0.90 --json` returns the residual table and no band; no
      artifact in the store carries status `calibrated`; the closed beads carry a typed reason citing this
      decision.
- [ ] If not fired: both gates are `pass` with measured values and caught controls.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_band.rs`: `decision_table` (mean 0.9148 passes, 0.9149 fails; fraction
0.099 passes, 0.100 fails; one forbidden band fails; retirement 0.899 fails; null 0.051 fails; six of seven
scope changes fails); `either_gate_failing_fires`; `policy_version_consistent_across_clauses`;
`row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_band.sh`, header `bead=N30b-18 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads both gate rows, recomputes every clause from the battery's log, and prints the
decision. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_band/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with each measured value against its
threshold, the standard errors, the control results, the policy version, and a summary with the trigger
evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-BAND", "GATE-GROUND-06", "GATE-GROUND-07"]`. It exits zero when the decision can
be made, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values the decision checks against: 19/21 = 0.9048; P(coverage < 0.80) = 0.0692 at n = 20. Negative
controls for the decision: a row in which the drift clause was measured at threshold 20 and the null clause at
40 (rejected); a row marked `pass` whose pooled-regime control was not caught (rejected).

## Considerations

- The tolerances of GATE-GROUND-06 are about five and four standard errors at 1,000 resamples, so a correct
  build fails by chance with negligible probability; a failure there means a defect, and the fallback is the
  right response until it is understood.
- The drift clause depends on a fixture property registered in advance (the post-drift miss probability); a
  failure caused by an under-powered fixture is a failure of the registered design and still fires. The
  honest alternative, stating the detectable shift size, is then proposed as a new mechanism with its own
  pilot.
- A fired KILL-BAND does not touch the reality line's gate: that line then reads `no-claim` everywhere, which
  is its resting state.
