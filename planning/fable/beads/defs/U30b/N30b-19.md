## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at its milestone. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from then on.

KILL-CLOCK governs every comparison with real logs that depends on time. Ground, the subsystem that compares
simulation with measured reality, maps each source clock onto a reference clock from shared hardware marks and
scores frozen predictions on sealed data. A crossing time, a latency or an actuator delay is meaningful only if
both of those work: the clock map with its uncertainty, and the order "prediction frozen, then data read".

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-02 or GATE-GROUND-05 fails. GATE-GROUND-02 (Known answer; TARGET): clock recovery on
  the rehearsal (planted skew 100 ppm, offset 3.7 ms, mark jitter 50 µs, marks 10 s apart: skew within
  3·σ_s = 21 ppm and the scored instant covered) in at least 99% of 1,000 resamples; both negative controls
  caught (one pulse only: refused; an offset fitted to the scored event: detected). GATE-GROUND-05 (Known
  answer; TARGET): the freeze-before-read order holds for every trial; the three protocol mutations (read
  before freeze; refit without a new version; holdout reuse) are refused.
- **Fallback:** real logs are scored on time-free metrics only (success, energy, peak values); no crossing
  time, latency or delay is reported.
- **Decided at:** M5.

Why the fallback is acceptable: a time-free metric cannot be bent by a clock. Success, energy over a trial and
a peak current are the same whatever offset the logger had.

## Scope

In: how the two measurements are read; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until decided;
the record. Out: running the batteries (N30b-32, N30b-33); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

1. **Measurements read.** The row of GATE-GROUND-02: resamples passing of 1,000, with the registered reading of
   coverage (the registered scored instant, inside the bracket) and the two controls. The row of GATE-GROUND-05:
   trials with freeze sequence before read sequence, of all trials; the three mutations refused of three; each
   with its caught controls.
2. **Decision rule.** Not fired only if: at least 990 of 1,000; both clock controls caught; zero order
   violations; three of three mutations refused. The register grants no repair cycle: the first complete
   recorded measurement of each gate decides.
3. **Not fired.** KILL-CLOCK `not fired`; both gates `pass`; time-based metrics may be registered in
   predictions for datasets whose clocks have bracketing marks.
4. **Fired** (status `killed: fallback adopted`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.predict` refuses to register a time-based metric (FROB-REF-876, repair "score time-free metrics");
     the set of time-free metric kinds is closed in the schema: success, counts, energy over the trial, peak
     and extreme values, final-state quantities.
   - `ground.score` extracts no instant-dependent outcome; `arrived_before` answers `undecided`.
   - Identification keeps the card's delay and lag at their priors; the delay profile is not reported; the
     quantity "delay plus offset" is not reported either.
   - Clock-fit records are still written at ingest as provenance, labelled `not used for scoring`.
   - Bands exist only for time-free metrics.
   - The exposure-delay alias on a straight pass has nothing to act on: `ground.excite` still flags it, and no
     delay or mount offset is updated from any pass.
5. **A failure of GATE-GROUND-05 needs more than the named fallback.** Time-free metrics do not repair a
   protocol in which sealed data can be read before a prediction is frozen. DESIGN, fail closed: if the trigger
   was GATE-GROUND-05, then in addition to the fallback above no candidate is admitted and no band is issued
   from any score until the invariant "a prediction's ledger sequence precedes the first read of the sealed
   chunk it predicts" is shown to hold again by its own test; the ADR states this explicitly and lists the
   defect.
6. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Time-based metrics are scored only on the synthetic rehearsal.
7. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-CLOCK.md` with both measurements, which gate triggered if any, the decision and
   the downstream list as executed; rows of `registries/gates.toml` for KILL-CLOCK and both gates, written by a
   session that did not implement clock fitting or scoring.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at the M5 exit with each clause evaluated against its number, and the registered reading of
      "aligned instants cover truth" cited.
- [ ] If fired: registering `crossing_time_s` in a prediction is refused with FROB-REF-876; a score record
      holds no time-based outcome; card delays equal their priors; if GATE-GROUND-05 was the trigger, no card is
      `admitted` and no band exists until the invariant's test passes again.
- [ ] If not fired: both gates are `pass` with measured values and caught controls.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_clock.rs`: `decision_table` (990 passes, 989 fails; one uncaught clock
control fails; one order violation fails; two of three mutations refused fails); `either_gate_failing_fires`;
`gate_05_trigger_suspends_admission`; `time_free_metric_kinds_closed_list`;
`row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_clock.sh`, header `bead=N30b-19 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads both gate rows, recomputes the counts from the batteries' logs, and prints the
decision. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_clock/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the resample count against 990, the
order-violation count against zero, the mutation count against three, the control results, and a summary with
the trigger evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-CLOCK", "GATE-GROUND-02", "GATE-GROUND-05"]`. It exits zero when the
decision can be made, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values: σ_s = 7.07 ppm for σ_m = 50 µs and marks 10 s apart, so 3·σ_s = 21 ppm; the expected count of
passing resamples for a correct fit is about 994.5 under the registered reading. Negative controls for the
decision: a row measured under the simultaneous-coverage reading without saying so (rejected: the reading is
part of the row); a row with the event-fitted-offset control missing (rejected).

## Considerations

- The clock gate's threshold of 99% leaves little room: under the registered reading a correct fit falls below
  990 of 1,000 in about 2% of seeds. The seed is registered before the run, and a failure at a count such as
  988 fires the gate as written. That risk is stated here so that it is accepted knowingly; changing the
  reading or the threshold requires a dated amendment before the measurement.
- The pairing of GATE-GROUND-05 with this kill gate is the register's. Item 5 is the conservative addition this
  decision makes so that a protocol defect is not papered over by dropping time-based metrics.
- With the fallback in force the cart fixture's headline metric (crossing time) cannot be compared with
  reality; success and energy can.
