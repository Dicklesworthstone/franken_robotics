## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. Its decision is
recorded as an architecture decision record at its milestone. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program: the fallback is the plan, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from then on.

KILL-KIT governs the replication kit of Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality.
A kit lets a third party rebuild a result without access to the originating machine or to anything private. It
carries the design, cards, scene, task and prediction records, the dataset or its hash and acquisition
protocol, the bench protocol, the analysis configuration, expected outputs with tolerances and determinism
classes, cited certificates, the no-authorization flags, and the synthetic rehearsal. Its self-test passes when
a clean machine reproduces its numbers: bit for bit on the same backend, inside the recorded envelope
otherwise.

- **Trigger:** GATE-GROUND-09 fails. GATE-GROUND-09 (Known answer; TARGET): a kit rebuilds the rehearsal on a
  clean machine inside its class; a private input is refused.
- **Fallback:** the kit ships as an archive of inputs, records and hashes, with no reproduction claim.
- **Decided at:** M5.

Why the fallback is acceptable: an archive with hashes still lets a reader see exactly what was predicted, when
it was frozen and what was measured. What it gives up is the statement "you can rerun this and get these
numbers", and that statement must not be made unless it has been shown.

## Scope

In: how the measurement is read; the decision; what changes on each branch; the posture until decided; the
record. Out: building and checking kits (BD-396, N30b-12); running the gate script, the gate's tier run and
the recording of GATE-GROUND-09 (N30b-36, the gate's one recorder).

## Technical approach

1. **Measurement read.** The record of GATE-GROUND-09, entered by its one recorder (N30b-36) from the one
   receipt of the gate's tier run, and read here, never written. That receipt cites by hash the two check
   receipts of the rehearsal kit: the one made in a fresh checkout and empty store on the building machine
   (calls compared by bit, failures) and the one made on the second platform (calls compared inside the
   envelope, failures, decision fields equal); it also holds the refusal of a kit whose lineage has a planted
   private input, with zero files written, and the controls caught.
2. **Decision rule.** Not fired only if: zero failures in both check receipts the gate's receipt cites; every
   decision field equal; the private input refused with FROB-REF-899 and nothing written. No repair cycle is
   granted: the first complete recorded measurement decides. A tier receipt that cites one check receipt only
   has the gate line `not-run` (`second-platform-receipt-missing`, N30b-36) and is no measurement: nothing is
   recorded from it, and if that is still the state at the M5 exit the outcome is `deferred` (item 7).
3. **The privacy clause is not waivable by the fallback.** If the reproduction part fails, the fallback
   applies. If the refusal of a private input fails, the fallback applies **and** the builder is disabled for
   any result whose lineage is not entirely `synthetic` until the screen's own tests pass again: an archive
   that leaks private data is not an acceptable fallback (DESIGN, fail closed).
4. **Not fired.** The `decision` entry of the KILL-KIT row has outcome `not-fired` and cites the gate's
   receipt; GATE-GROUND-09 reads `pass` in its own record; kits carry `reproduction_claim = true` with the
   classes under which the claim was shown.
5. **Fired** (outcome `fired`; GATE-GROUND-09, recorded `fail`, then reads `killed` with `detail =
   "KILL-KIT: <fallback>"`, printed "killed: fallback adopted", through the register's `apply_decisions`, the
   one place a gate becomes `killed`). Downstream changes:
   - `ground.kit build` writes the same directory with `reproduction_claim = false` in its manifest and without
     the `expected/` section; the screen for private and non-redistributable inputs is unchanged.
   - `ground.kit check` is reduced to manifest verification (files, hashes, flags) and says so in its verdict:
     `verified-archive`, never `pass`.
   - Reports and documentation describe the artifact as an archive; the words "replication kit" are not used
     for it.
   - The first-record bead of the Reality lane lists "archive, no reproduction claim" where it would have
     listed a kit.
6. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Kits are built only for the synthetic rehearsal.
7. **Record.** The decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-KIT-M5.md` (the one form of a kill-gate decision; an
   earlier text of this bead named `docs/adr/KILL-KIT.md`), with the required fields: the kill gate; the
   milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash
   beside the trigger's number (the two failure counts against zero, the decision-field comparison, the
   refusal's code and the count of files written); the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the
   capability carries from then on ("archive", item 5); the beads re-read (each "stands on the fallback",
   "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"); the downstream list of item 5 as executed,
   with the builder's suspension of item 3 when the privacy clause was the failing one; and the recording
   session, which did not implement the kit. The kill row's entry `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session,
   receipts }` in `registries/gates.toml`, with an outcome from the closed set: `not-fired` (the gate's
   receipt cited), `fired`, `deferred` (with `deferred_to` and the reason, when the measurement is not
   complete at the M5 exit: the second platform's check receipt is missing) or `not-reached` (only if the kit
   was never funded). This bead writes that entry and the ADR; it never writes GATE-GROUND-09's row, and no
   status `killed`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-KIT-M5.md` exists at the M5 exit with the required fields of item 7, both check
      receipts and the refusal evaluated, and the KILL-KIT row's `decision` entry cites it with an outcome of
      the closed set; an ADR at any other path fails the register's load (`decision-adr-path`).
- [ ] The decision can be recorded whichever way the measurement falls: nothing this bead depends on needs
      the gate to pass in order to close (BD-396 and N30b-12 close on their own criteria; N30b-36 closes
      `pass` or `recorded`).
- [ ] If fired: a built directory has `reproduction_claim = false` and no `expected/`; `frob ground kit check`
      returns `verified-archive`; if the privacy clause was the failing one, a build for a result with any
      non-synthetic lineage is refused; GATE-GROUND-09 prints "killed: fallback adopted" from
      `apply_decisions`, its stored row still `fail`.
- [ ] If not fired: GATE-GROUND-09 is `pass`, recorded from the one tier receipt that cites both check
      receipts, with every control caught, and the entry cites that receipt.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback is dated after the first measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_kit.rs`: `decision_table` (zero failures and a refusal pass; one bit-mode
failure fails; one differing decision field fails; an accepted private input fails);
`privacy_failure_disables_non_synthetic_builds`; `fallback_manifest_has_no_reproduction_claim`;
`row_without_caught_controls_is_not_a_measurement`; `outcome_from_closed_set` (every part holds → `not-fired`
with the receipt; any part fails → `fired`; a gate line `not-run` at the exit → `deferred` with `deferred_to`
and a reason; "not fired", "passed" and "killed: fallback adopted" are rejected as outcomes);
`adr_path_is_the_registered_form` (`docs/adr/KILL-KIT.md` is refused `decision-adr-path`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_kit.sh`, header `bead=N30b-21 milestone=M5 labels=ground,kill-gate
gates=KILL-KIT,GATE-GROUND-09 seed=1 plants=build-store-receipt,one-platform-receipt`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`, reads the gate record, the tier receipt it cites and the two check receipts that receipt
cites, recounts failures, and prints the outcome the rule gives. It reads only: it writes no register row and
no ADR, which are the recording session's. The two plants are the negative controls below, on scratch copies
of the receipts. Log: `target/e2e-logs/kill_kit/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: each command with
its exit code and wall time; the failure counts against zero per comparison mode with the receipt hashes; the
fingerprints of both machines; the refusal's code and the count of files written against zero; the control
results; on failure the first part that could not be evaluated and the receipt path; and a summary with the
trigger evaluation and `gates: ["KILL-KIT", "GATE-GROUND-09"]`. It exits zero when the decision can be made,
whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls for the decision: a check receipt produced in the building store (`build-store-receipt`;
rejected: the receipt records the store's object count at start, which must be zero); a tier receipt that
cites one platform's check receipt only (`one-platform-receipt`; its gate line is `not-run`, so the rule
gives `deferred`, never `not-fired`).

## Considerations

- The most likely cause of a reproduction failure is a determinism gap between platforms in a component the kit
  replays (the ensemble lane of identification is recorded per machine for that reason). A failure there is
  informative about the product, and the fallback keeps the kit honest while it is understood.
- A kit for a result on real data may be `partial` by design when its dataset cannot ship; that is not this
  gate's failure mode, and the gate is measured on the rehearsal, where everything ships or regenerates from
  its recipe.
