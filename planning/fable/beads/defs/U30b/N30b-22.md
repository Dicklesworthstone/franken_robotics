## Background

A gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold. The record is a row of
`registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and that resolves in
the artifact graph. A gate with no record has the status `not-run`, which is not a pass. Statuses are the
seven of N40-01: `not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed` (printed "killed: fallback adopted"), `blocked` (the open
question's identifier in `detail`), `no-claim` (terminal, only for a gate whose claim is Reality) and
`superseded`. Each gate names exactly
one of the seven kinds of claim. Every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches, and
a gate whose control has not been shown to fail has not passed. Where a subsystem's own text and the central
register differ on a gate's identity, claim, milestone or numbers, the register governs.

Ground, the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality, has eleven convergence gates and five
kill gates. Their measurements are spread over many beads. The record of each gate and kill gate is created
once, by the register bead that owns it (Scope names each); this bead supplies the subsystem's side of every
one, so that each measurement is recorded against a row whose full threshold, fixture, controls and measuring
bead are already stated, and so that the status table can show the subsystem honestly from M1 on (one row
measured, nine `not-run`, and GATE-GROUND-10 `blocked` on OPEN-GROUND-1).

Ceiling, stated with the rows: Ground has no lever on time or compute. It is an honesty mechanism, and its
only possible saving, on real-trial count, is bounded near 1.3. No row below is a Performance claim.

## Scope

This bead is the Ground chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the eleven gates: the full threshold of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as
  the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the
  chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its fixture, measuring bead, script and kill linkage.
- For the five kill gates: the map from each to its decision bead, and the check that each has its ledger record
  and exactly one owner declaration.
- The chapter's statement of the recurring rule for the first gate and the unscheduled rule for the tenth, and
  the check that the records carry them; the lint that keeps declarations and measuring scripts in agreement.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-GROUND-01 to -10 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts, `recurring` and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-GROUND-11: N40-10.
- The kill records of KILL-GROUND, KILL-BAND, KILL-CLOCK, KILL-ASBUILT and KILL-KIT, with trigger, fallback,
  decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback ledger, N40-27.
- Measuring anything; the control records (N30-CX); the kill decisions (N30b-17 to N30b-21).

3. **What this bead writes, and what it does not** (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
   reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart). `registries/gates.toml`
   holds exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-GROUND-01 to
   -10 are created by N40-02 and that of GATE-GROUND-11 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`,
   `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
   `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The five kill records are created by the fallback ledger
   (N40-27) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and writes
   none of those fields. It writes three things:
   - `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers from the control registry, and the
     tiers the gate is measured on.
   - The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
     `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers` (the numbers of
     the threshold, with units), in the `contract.toml` of `frob-ground` (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
     until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the
     chapter-side data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare:
     `criterion`, the full threshold of the table above, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief;
     `fixture`; `measured_by`; `script`, the end-to-end script of the measuring bead; and `kill`, the kill gate
     the gate triggers, if any.
   - Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its decision bead as data of its
     lint. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written
     by the decision bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

   The Claim, Earliest, Trigger, Fallback and Decided columns above are the chapter's statement of fields the
   register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10 and N40-27 when the beads were reconciled, and they
   agree; where a fallback above says more than the ledger's (the rule for card fields under KILL-GROUND; the
   examples of time-free metrics under KILL-CLOCK), the ledger's text is the one a declaration carries. If the
   two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or
   N40-30; it is never settled by a second record. Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`,
   `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`, `superseded`). The record of a part is its `commit`,
   `fingerprint`, `seed`, `measured`, `receipt`, `recorded` and `status` fields; a recurring gate gains one
   part per later exit, so an earlier record is never overwritten.
4. **Rules the records carry (stated here by the chapter, entered by N40-02 and N40-10, checked by this bead).**
   - GATE-GROUND-01 is `recurring = true`: every milestone exit after M1 has a part of its own with a new
     record; an exit with no new record for it is incomplete.
   - GATE-GROUND-03 has one part, at M5. The subsystem's own text tags it M4 and it may be recorded there; that
     note is in the declaration and adds no part.
   - GATE-GROUND-10 has no milestone and no part tied to an exit. It never blocks an exit: from M5 on the exit
     report prints it in a section of its own (N40-04), with its record or its resting state. Its status reads
     `blocked` with `detail = "OPEN-GROUND-1"` while the dataset question (OPEN-GROUND-1 and OPEN-GAUNT-1) is
     undecided, then the terminal `no-claim` with reason `no-dataset-admitted` once the default is recorded,
     and `pass` only on a real cell (the same words as N30b-25). The bead that owns the Reality lane's first
     record (BD-397) is its recorder: its tier entry writes the receipt, and a session that did not implement
     the subsystem records the row from it.
   - GATE-GROUND-11 belongs to a subsection added after the main tables were compiled; it is a full row, created
     by N40-10.
   - A gate with a kill gate does not block the exit when it fails: the kill decision with its fallback adopted
     is the record. GATE-GROUND-01, -03 and -11 have no kill gate and block.
5. **Lint.** A test compares each declaration's `script` with the scripts under `scripts/e2e/`: the script
   exists, its header names a bead listed in the declaration's `measured_by`, and its `summary` line can emit
   the gate identifier. A record whose controls are not all present in the control registry cannot take the
   status `pass`. The same test asserts that each of the sixteen identifiers is the `id` of exactly one record,
   that this bead has added none, and that each kill gate has a ledger record and a decision bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sixteen records load from `registries/gates.toml`, one per identifier (created by N40-02, N40-10 and
      N40-27; none by this bead); each convergence record names exactly one claim; ten are Known answer and one
      is Reality. Each gate has an owner declaration whose claim and milestone parts equal the record's.
- [ ] At M1 the status table generated from the registry shows GATE-GROUND-01 with its measured record, nine
      rows as `not-run` and GATE-GROUND-10 as `blocked: OPEN-GROUND-1`, and never prints a pass for a row with
      no record.
- [ ] Every convergence row has at least one control that resolves in the control registry.
- [ ] The lint fails when a script named by a row is missing, and when a script claims a gate no row assigns to
      it.
- [ ] A planted `pass` record written by the same session that implemented the measured bead is refused (no
      self-certification), and a planted `pass` with an uncaught control is refused.
- [ ] The numbers in the declarations equal the numbers in this bead, and every number in a record's brief
      appears among them; a change to a kill gate's trigger, threshold or fallback is accepted only as a dated
      amendment entry that precedes the measurement it governs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/gates_ground.rs`: `sixteen_rows`; `claims_one_each`; `milestones_per_register`
(03 at M5; 10 unscheduled; 01 recurring); `kill_linkage` (02 and 05 → KILL-CLOCK; 06 and 07 → KILL-BAND; 04 →
KILL-GROUND with `repair_cycles = 1`; 08 → KILL-ASBUILT; 09 → KILL-KIT); `rows_have_controls`;
`scripts_match_rows`; `self_certified_pass_refused`; `pass_with_uncaught_control_refused`;
`late_amendment_refused`; `one_record_per_identifier` (a planted second record for GATE-GROUND-02 fails the load
with `duplicate-id`); `declarations_agree_with_records`; `gate_10_status_sequence` (no decision record:
`blocked` with detail `OPEN-GROUND-1`; default recorded: `no-claim` with reason `no-dataset-admitted`; never
`pass` on a `synthetic` form A).

### End-to-end script and logging

No script of its own: this bead delivers registry fields, declarations and a lint, none of which a `frob`
command reaches; its tests above are its check (KNOWN_DRIFTS 11). Each declaration's `script` is the script of
its gate's one recorder; that script runs the gate's tier entry with `e2e_tier`, asserts on the receipt
(`E2E_LAST_RECEIPT`) and logs its hash, emits `e2e_metric … --gate <id>` and ends with `e2e_gate <id>`. A
session that did not implement the measured beads then records the part from that receipt with `record_gate`
(table note above). No script writes a gate record (N40-01).

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values present in rows: 19/21 = 0.9048; 0.069; 21 ppm; 990 of 1,000; 95, 0.70 and 90; 20 episodes;
10⁴ null runs; 80 mm to 10⁻⁹. Negative controls: a declaration in which GATE-GROUND-03 is tagged M4 only (the
milestone comparison fails against the register); a record for GATE-GROUND-10 with a part due at M5 (it would
block the exit: refused by the lint).

## Considerations

- The gate for the eight structural aliases is measured by the bead that owns the identifiability analysis.
  This bead writes its declaration and the end-to-end rehearsal re-asserts it; if that owner bead were to
  change scope, the declaration would still name a measuring script.
- Four of the eleven thresholds are statistical. Their parts record the seed, and their declarations the
  expected value, beside the measured one, so a reader can tell a near-miss from a defect.
- The pairing of GATE-GROUND-05 with KILL-CLOCK is the register's and is kept; the kill decision adds a
  fail-closed rule for that case.
