## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality. Its machinery is tested on a synthetic
rehearsal, and its one Reality gate, GATE-GROUND-10 (the first real cell: a frozen prediction scored on sealed
data and a band issued), waits for data. OPEN-GROUND-1 asks the engineering half of the data question: **which
public paired simulation-and-reality dataset, if any, has enough robots per class to test exchangeability
across units.** None was identified at planning time (FACT, source register). The owner's half, which dataset
is admitted first with what licence and provenance, is a separate open question of the verification program
(OPEN-GAUNT-1); the two are closed together at the M5 exit, this one first, because the owner's decision reads
this bead's finding.

Why "enough robots per class" is the hard part. A reality band for a unit nobody has measured treats units as
the exchangeable draws, and at a 90% level it needs at least nine calibrated units of one design under one
controller, firmware, surface and task family. Public datasets typically hold many episodes of one or two
machines. Twenty episodes on one machine are one cluster.

- **Fail-closed default:** the second-simulator stand-in, labelled `synthetic`. Every report prints
  `reality: no-claim` (or `synthetic` on the stand-in).
- **Owner:** Ground lead. **Blocks:** GATE-GROUND-10 only. **Decided at:** M5.

## Scope

This bead is the engineering half: it finds out what exists and what each candidate could support, and it
answers OPEN-GROUND-1. In: the candidate survey and its one table (the only candidate table of the program:
the owner's decision and the owner page read it and do not keep their own); the admission checklist a
candidate must pass; the classification of what a candidate could support; the finding and the Ground lead's
recommendation; the record `docs/adr/OPEN-GROUND-1.md` and the register row OPEN-GROUND-1.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the owner's decision which dataset, if any, is admitted to the Reality lane first, with what licence and
  provenance (OPEN-GAUNT-1), its record `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-06-reality-dataset.md`, the status of the Reality
  gates under each outcome and the tombstone: N38b-26, which depends on this bead;
- the page that puts the survey's table to the owner beside the bench-hardware question, and the checks that
  the defaults are in force: N41-10 (the owner packet), which depends on this bead;
- admitting the chosen dataset (`ground.ingest`, BD-387) and the Reality lane's record and gate row (BD-397);
- acquiring bench data (the bench packages, governed by the decision N30b-26).

## Technical approach

1. **Survey, twice dated** (DESIGN: the owner-input calendar asks each open question one milestone before its
   deadline, so the table must exist at the M4 exit, and the decision must not rest on a stale search, so the
   survey is re-run before the M5 exit; the record keeps both dates). A dated survey of public datasets that
   pair logged real-robot episodes with a simulation model of the same robot, recorded as one table. One row
   per candidate, with these fields (DESIGN: the union of what the survey, the owner's decision and the owner
   page each need, held once, here):
   1. name and source;
   2. licence, and what it permits: internal use, redistribution of derived artifacts, redistribution in a
      replication kit, or none of these;
   3. the robot: its class, the number of distinct serial-level units, the episodes per unit, and whether a
      public model of it exists among the reference robots;
   4. what was measured (joint torques, motor currents, encoders, base motion or whole-body pose, contact
      forces, energy) and at what rates;
   5. the instruments with their stated uncertainty, or "not stated";
   6. provenance: who measured, when, with what instruments;
   7. clocks: whether they can be aligned (shared marks or a sync signal), and the number of marks per clock;
   8. whether attempts that failed are included (the full denominator);
   9. whether conditions were assigned or chosen;
   10. whether a held-out portion can be sealed before anyone reads it;
   11. the cell it would calibrate: which units, controller and firmware, surface, task family, envelope of
       conditions;
   12. what could not be learned from it;
   13. the checklist result (item 2 below) and the class of support (item 3 below).
   The table is kept in machine-readable form as the front-matter block of the record of step 5, so that the
   owner page (N41-10) and the owner's decision (N38b-26) are generated from it and nobody retypes a row.
2. **Admission checklist** (a candidate that fails any item is not admitted; each of the first six items is a
   refusal the admission path would raise anyway, so the checklist predicts the outcome of `ground.ingest`; the
   seventh is not an admission check and is settled by compiling the model with the program's own compiler,
   the compiler's diagnostics recorded as the item's result):
   - licence known and at least `research-only` for internal use; `redistributable` or `attribution` for a kit;
   - provenance, rights and per-instrument uncertainty present or derivable from documentation;
   - serial-level unit identity for every episode;
   - a split by hardware, condition or day is possible; no random-row split;
   - the full denominator of attempts is recorded;
   - at least two timing marks per clock for any time-based metric;
   - a simulation model of the robot that this program can compile, with card provenance stated.
3. **Classification of what a candidate could support.** `per-unit`: at least 19 usable episodes of one unit in
   one cell (a band for that unit). `across-units`: at least nine units of one class in one cell (the
   exchangeability test this question asks for). `neither`: usable for identification or as a published anchor
   only.
4. **Finding and recommendation.** The answer to OPEN-GROUND-1 is a finding, signed by the Ground lead, in
   one of three forms: (a) a named dataset passes the checklist, with its class of support, and is recommended
   to the owner as the first real cell; (b) no candidate passes: the default stands; (c) no public dataset
   passes, and a bench package is recommended instead (then the decision N30b-26 governs). The finding does
   not admit anything: whether a dataset recommended under (a) enters the Reality lane is the owner's decision
   (N38b-26). Under (b) and (c), and under (a) if the owner declines, the owner's decision records the default,
   the Reality lane's record (BD-397) is its form B with the reason `no-dataset-admitted`, and
   GATE-GROUND-10 takes the terminal status `no-claim`.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-GROUND-1.md` with the survey table (both dates), each candidate's checklist
   result, the finding, the Ground lead's sign-off, and the date; the open-questions registry row
   OPEN-GROUND-1 updated; the owner's decision record (N38b-26) and the Reality lane's record (BD-397) cite it.
6. **Gate row until then** (stated here so that this bead, N38b-26 and N41-10 use the same words). The gate
   register's statuses are `not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked` (with the open question's identifier
   in the row's `detail`, printed "blocked: OPEN-…"), `no-claim` (terminal, only for a gate whose claim is
   Reality) and `superseded`. GATE-GROUND-10 reads `blocked` with `detail = "OPEN-GROUND-1"` while the two
   halves of the dataset question are undecided, `no-claim` once the default is recorded, and `pass` only on a
   real cell. The row is written by BD-397, not by this bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The survey table exists with a date at the M4 exit and a re-run date at or after the opening of M5, and
      has the thirteen fields above per candidate, or states that no candidate was found.
- [ ] Each candidate has a checklist result, and each failed item names the refusal code admission would raise.
- [ ] The finding is one of the three forms and is signed by the Ground lead, the owner named for the question.
- [ ] Under form (b) the record states the reason `no-dataset-admitted` in the words the Reality lane's record
      and the owner's decision use, and nothing else in the program is blocked.
- [ ] Under form (a) the dataset's licence class and the class of support are recorded before any ingest.
- [ ] The table's machine-readable block parses, and the owner page generated from it has one row per
      candidate (checked in N41-10).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_ground_1.rs`: `default_is_synthetic_stand_in` (with no decision record the
lane's state is `no-claim` and the stand-in's outputs are `synthetic`); `checklist_items_map_to_refusals` (each
of the first six items maps to a registry code: 873 for licence, provenance and uncertainty, units, splits and
denominator; 876 for marks; the seventh maps to `compile` and records the compiler's result, never a refusal
code); `support_classification` (19 episodes of one unit → `per-unit`; nine units → `across-units`;
eight units → not `across-units`); `gate_status_blocked_until_decided` (with neither record on file the row of
GATE-GROUND-10 reads `blocked` with `detail = "OPEN-GROUND-1"`); `candidate_table_has_thirteen_fields` (a row
missing any field is rejected by the table's loader, with the field named).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_first_record.sh` (bead BD-397) prints the state of this question from the registry at the
top of its log and, when no decision record exists, asserts the default: the lane's record is form B with the
reason `no-dataset-admitted`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values: minimum 19 episodes for a per-unit band; ⌈(1 − α)/α⌉ = 9 units at 90%. Negative control: a
candidate described with "many robots" and no serial-level identity must fail the checklist (units
undetermined), not pass on the description.

## Considerations

- A dataset can be valuable without answering this question: a single well-instrumented unit supports a
  per-unit band and the whole procedure. The finding records which use is intended so that a per-unit band is
  not later read as a statement about the class.
- Private data offered under a non-disclosure agreement is admissible for a per-unit cell and cannot enter a
  kit; that trade is the owner's to make (N38b-26).
- If nothing is admitted, the pre-mortem's story "calibration data never arrived" has occurred as predicted,
  and its residual is stated: the Reality claim stays empty.
