## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality. It owns no hardware: it reads files and
writes files. Whether it ever has real data to read depends on a question that engineering cannot answer:
**whether any bench hardware is acquired.** Six optional bench packages are defined (an actuator stand; a ramp
and drag sled for material pairs; the instrumented cart and sill fixture; one built link; a calibrated release
of named robot, surface and task cells; an independent revalidation). Decision **ADR-GROUND-10:** bench work is
optional, is packaged separately and blocks no milestone, because Reality is an import lane. None of the
packages is scheduled against a milestone, none gates a release, and each leaves `reality: no-claim` in place
if it never runs. Sourcing may start at M0, and nothing waits for it.

- **Fail-closed default:** none acquired; Reality stays `no-claim`.
- **Owner:** project lead. **Blocks:** nothing. **Owner input wanted at:** M5.

The decision bead exists, although the question blocks no milestone, because the real runs of the six packages
exist only on one branch of it and because the owner's input is explicitly asked for at the M5 exit. The
packages' protocols and their dry runs on synthetic twins exist on both branches: they need no hardware, the
replication kit ships the protocol, and the protocol format takes its format row at M5 (N34-27).

## Scope

This bead is the decision bead of OPEN-GROUND-2 and holds everything specific to the question. In: the
options table (the six packages with rig, measures, completion condition, what each makes sayable and what
stays `no-claim`), the value statement, the cost side with its cost bands and lead times, all written as the
options file `docs/decisions/OPEN-GROUND-2.md`; the forms of the decision; the decision record
`docs/adr/ADR-PROG-07-bench-hardware.md` and the register row OPEN-GROUND-2; what changes on each outcome
(which packages' real runs are funded; the check that no milestone gate and no bead's close waits on a rig).

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the one page that puts this question to the owner beside the dataset question, by including the options
  file unchanged, and the end-to-end check that under the default every report prints `reality: no-claim`,
  a band request is refused and rehearsal artifacts are labelled `synthetic`: N41-10 (the owner packet). That
  bead first carried its own table of the six packages and its own record name; the table's two columns this
  bead lacked (measures, completion condition) and its rule on cost bands are now here, and N41-10 keeps
  none of them;
- the packages themselves, with their rigs, protocols and completion conditions as work: N30b-38 to N30b-40,
  which close at M5 on their protocols and dry runs whatever this decision says, and read it only for whether a
  real run follows;
- the survey of public datasets and its finding (OPEN-GROUND-1): N30b-25; the owner's decision on a dataset
  (OPEN-GAUNT-1): N38b-26.

## Technical approach

1. **Options, cheapest first, with what each makes sayable** (all optional, none scheduled against a
   milestone, none gating a release; each leaves `reality: no-claim` in place if it never runs). The table is
   the content of the options file `docs/decisions/OPEN-GROUND-2.md`, written when the question is put, at
   the M4 exit, and is the only table of the six packages as options; the owner page of N41-10 includes the
   file and does not restate it.

   | Option | Rig | Measures | Completion condition of the package added | What becomes sayable | What stays `no-claim` |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | None | — | — | — | Nothing measured; the rehearsal shows the machinery is sound | Everything |
   | A (actuator stand) | One actuator of a catalogue class on a stand with a known payload | Current, encoder, optionally torque | A sealed prediction of a held-out trajectory is scored; the card gains `measured` fields | One card of one unit with `measured` fields, admitted by a sealed prediction of a held-out trajectory | Contact, whole robots, any class statement |
   | A + B (ramp and material pairs) | Adds an incline and a drag sled | Slip angle, drag at two normal loads | Friction per pair with its interval; a named identifiability alias (friction against torque limit) shown separated | Friction per material pair with its interval; the friction-against-torque-limit alias shown separated | Whole robots |
   | A + B + C (instrumented cart) | Adds the cart and sill fixture with 60 mm and 75 mm wheels | Current, encoder, crossing time | A frozen prediction and the A/B ranking are scored on a sealed day | A frozen prediction and the A/B ranking scored on a sealed day: the first whole-system cell | Legged robots; any other task |
   | D (one built link) | One printed or machined link with declared fiducials, a scanner and a scale | A scan and a scale reading | The as-built loop closes and the tolerance audit is produced | The as-built loop closed on one part, with its tolerance audit | Assemblies |
   | E (calibrated release), F (revalidation) | Named robot, surface and task cells; for F, independently acquired traces for the cells of E | Whole-robot logs | E: bands issued per cell from held-out predictions, with limits stated. F: each ranking reversal and calibration limit reported | Bands per cell from held-out predictions with limits stated; each ranking reversal and calibration limit reported | Units and cells not measured |
2. **Value statement for the owner** (from the real-episode planner): in an uncalibrated cell the reality gap
   is the largest unknown, and twenty real pairs are worth about three times all remaining simulation for a
   decision between two close finalists (0.0251 against 0.0084 in the worked case); in a calibrated cell the
   order reverses. The first batch of real data is the valuable one.
3. **Cost side.** No rate between operator time and compute is assumed (a separate open question, default: real
   episodes are recommended, never scheduled). The owner sees costs in episodes, parts and days. The options
   file lists for each package an estimated cost band and lead time supplied by the Ground lead, labelled
   `assumed` until quoted, and which catalogue cards would move from `assumed` to `measured`. Package C uses
   the program's first fixture and is the cheapest path to one calibrated whole-system cell.
4. **Decision.** One of: none (default); a named subset of packages with a budget and an operator; or defer
   with a date. A decision to acquire does not create a milestone dependency: the packages stay imports.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-07-bench-hardware.md` (DESIGN: the owner questions take the program's
   consecutive numbers, so that one identifier names the record in every bead; this bead first named the file
   `docs/adr/OPEN-GROUND-2.md`, which is not used) with the option chosen, the budget if any, the owner's
   sign-off and the date, and the options file as its supporting file; the open-questions registry row
   OPEN-GROUND-2 moved to `decided` or `defaulted` and naming the ADR. The record lists, per package, whether
   its real run is funded. On "none" no rig is acquired and no real run is funded; the three package beads still
   close at M5 on their protocols and dry runs (their close never waits on hardware, which is how they stay
   ancestors of the M5 exit review, N41-16 check 3(b), without a milestone ever waiting on a rig), and a later
   decision can fund a package's real run, which then goes through that bead's `--bundle` mode whenever its
   bundle returns.
6. **What an acquisition switches.** The package's rig and protocol are registered; its logs are admitted
   like any dataset, through the admission verb with a manifest; the package's completion condition is the
   acceptance of that package's bead; nothing outside the cells it measures changes its reality line.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The options file exists at the M4 exit with the six packages, each with rig, measures, completion
      condition, what becomes sayable, what stays `no-claim`, a cost band and a lead time (`assumed` on every
      unquoted cost), and the cards that would move to `measured`.
- [ ] The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-07-bench-hardware.md` exists at the M5 exit, signed by the owner
      named for the question, in one of the three forms; the register row names it.
- [ ] Under the default, the record states that no package's real run is funded, and the Reality lane's record
      (BD-397) states the default of this question.
- [ ] Under any acquisition, no milestone waits on a rig: no gate row with a milestone reads a bench package's
      real result (GATE-GROUND-10, tied to no milestone, is the only gate a real run can feed), and no package
      bead's acceptance needs a returned bundle (a check).
- [ ] The record states, per chosen package, what becomes sayable and what stays `no-claim`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_ground_2.rs`: `default_is_none`; `packages_never_block_milestones` (no gate
row of `registries/gates.toml` that carries a milestone lists a bench package's real result among the records it
reads; GATE-GROUND-10's row carries none); `record_lists_funded_real_runs` (the ADR names each package as funded
or not; the default ADR names none); `options_file_has_six_packages` (the options file's table has the
rows A to F with every column filled, and an unquoted cost without the label `assumed` is rejected).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_first_record.sh` (bead BD-397) prints the state of this question at the top of its log; with
no decision record it asserts the default and that every report surface prints `reality: no-claim`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Reference values in the value statement: 0.0251, 0.0084 and 0.0035 (real batch uncalibrated; all remaining
simulation; the same real batch calibrated). Known answers: the six packages with their completion conditions,
as in the table. Negative controls: a planted gate row with milestone M5 that reads a bench package's real
result must fail `packages_never_block_milestones`; a cost printed without `assumed` and without a quotation
must fail the options file's check.

## Considerations

- A bench rig is also a liability: it needs calibration identities for its instruments, an operator who records
  every attempt including aborted ones, and conditions assigned from a registered draw. A package acquired
  without that discipline produces data that admission will refuse.
- Borrowed time on someone else's robot is a form of acquisition and goes through the same record, with the
  data's licence and privacy mark decided first.
- The subsystem's value does not depend on this decision: with no hardware it still prevents a simulated number
  from being read as measured.
