## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality. A calibration (a reality band and the
`admitted` state of identified cards, valid inside one cell) ends in one of four ways: a change of scope, a
rank-drift alarm, a miss-count alarm, or expiry. Expiry exists because the statistical monitors are weak at
small calibration sets (at twenty points the rank monitor sees only gross shifts) and because wear and
lubricant ageing proceed whether or not anyone runs an episode. OPEN-GROUND-3 asks what the expiry limits
should be: **calibration expiry by age and operating hours.**

- **Fail-closed default:** 90 days or 200 operating hours, whichever comes first, and always on part
  replacement.
- **Owner:** Ground lead. **Blocks:** GATE-GROUND-10 (the first real cell cannot be recorded without a stated
  expiry). **Decided at:** M5, or when the first real cell is prepared.

The question is open because no data exists from which to estimate how fast a calibrated cell drifts. The
default is a deliberately short engineering guess; an expired calibration costs a recalibration batch, and a
stale one that is relied on costs far more.

## Scope

In: the options, the evidence that would change the default, the decision and its record, where the constants
live. Out: the expiry mechanism (N30b-10); the monitors (BD-394).

## Technical approach

1. **What is being set.** Three contract constants of the reality-band decision core: `expiry_days`,
   `expiry_hours`, and the rule `expire_on_part_replacement = true` (not negotiable: a replaced part is a scope
   change). They may be set per part class (a strain-wave gear and a plain bearing age differently) as a table
   keyed by actuator and transmission class, with the default as the fallback row.
2. **Options.**
   - (a) Keep the default for every class.
   - (b) A per-class table from manufacturer service intervals where a cited datasheet gives one (a relubrication
     interval is a natural upper bound for `expiry_hours`), with the default elsewhere.
   - (c) Data-driven, once at least one cell has been recalibrated twice: set the limit so that the band's
     half-width changed by less than its own sampling variation between calibrations in at least nine of ten
     recalibrations; until such data exists this option is unavailable.
3. **Evidence that would lengthen a limit.** Successive recalibrations of one cell whose residual distributions
   pass the rank monitor against each other at the 0.05 level for the whole interval. **Evidence that would
   shorten it:** any retirement by a statistical monitor before expiry in more than one of ten calibrations of
   a class.
4. **Decision.** The owner picks (a) or (b) at M5, records the table, and states the review date. Option (c) is
   revisited at each milestone exit after real recalibrations exist.
5. **Mechanics of a change.** The constants live in `registries/cores/dc-07.toml`. A change is a new
   decision-policy version; calibrations made under the old version keep the expiry they were issued with
   (a limit is never lengthened retroactively); the change is recorded with its reason.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-GROUND-3.md` with the option, the table, the citations for any datasheet-derived
   row, the owner's sign-off and the date; the open-questions registry row updated; the Reality lane's record
   cites the limits in force.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Until a decision is recorded, the mechanism uses 90 days and 200 hours and retires on part replacement,
      and the Reality lane's record says the limits are defaults.
- [ ] The decision record exists before the first form-A record of the Reality lane is written.
- [ ] A per-class row cites its source; a row without a citation is rejected and the default applies.
- [ ] Changing a limit changes the decision-policy version; a calibration issued earlier keeps its original
      expiry date.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/expiry_policy.rs`: `defaults_90_days_200_hours`; `part_replacement_always_expires`;
`per_class_row_overrides_default`; `uncited_row_rejected`; `policy_version_changes_with_limit`;
`issued_calibration_keeps_its_expiry` (a calibration issued at 90 days is not extended by a later change to
180).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_band.sh` (bead N30b-34) prints the expiry limits in force with their source (default or
decision record) in the status output of a calibrated rehearsal cell, and asserts expiry at the boundary by
advancing the fixture's recorded time.

### Known answers and negative controls

Boundary cases: 89 and 91 days; 199 and 201 hours. Negative control: a policy file that sets `expiry_days = 0`
(never expires) is rejected by the contract check: zero and absent are not permitted values.

## Considerations

- "Operating hours" must come from the datasets' declared hours; a robot whose logs do not record them is
  governed by age alone, and the status output says so.
- A short limit is cheap only if recalibration is cheap. For a whole-robot cell, twenty episodes every 90 days
  is a real cost, and the owner may prefer a longer limit with a larger calibration set, which also makes the
  monitors sensitive (slack 0.071 at n = 200 against 0.224 at n = 20).
- Temperature and season are conditions of the envelope, not of expiry: a calibration made in winter does not
  cover a summer floor, whatever its age.
