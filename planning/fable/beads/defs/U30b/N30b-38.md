## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality. It owns no hardware and has no driver:
it reads files. Decision **ADR-GROUND-10:** bench work is optional, is packaged separately and blocks no
milestone, because Reality is an import lane. Six bench packages are defined. They are imports: none is
scheduled against a milestone, none gates a release, and each leaves `reality: no-claim` in place if it never
runs. Sourcing may start at M0; nothing waits for it. Whether any of them is funded is the owner's decision
(N30b-26). This bead and its two siblings close at M5 on work that needs no hardware (the protocol format, the
protocols and their dry runs on synthetic twins), which is written whatever that decision says: a replication kit
ships the protocol in its `protocol/` section, and the format takes its format row at M5 (N34-27). Only a
package's real run waits for funding, and it is never a condition of this bead's close.

Packages A and B are the two component benches. They are the cheapest way to turn a `generic` card field into
a `measured` one, and they follow the fixed identification order (actuators first, then contact and surface).

| Package | Rig | Measures | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Actuator stand | One actuator of a catalogue class on a stand with a known payload | Current, encoder, optionally torque | A sealed prediction of a held-out trajectory is scored; the card gains `measured` fields |
| B. Ramp and material pairs | An incline and a drag sled | Slip angle, drag at two normal loads | Friction per pair with its interval; the friction-against-torque-limit alias shown separated |

What "package" means here: a protocol that an operator follows away from this software, and a dataset bundle
that comes back. Nothing in the package commands a device.

## Scope

In: the bench protocol format; for each of A and B the rig description, the instrument list with required
fields, the experiments, the split, the verbs run on the returned bundle, and the completion check. Out: buying
or building a rig; any software that talks to it; the mechanisms the returned data exercises (the beads in
`deps`).

## Technical approach

1. **Bench protocol** (`frob.ground.protocol/1`, a TOML file produced by `frob ground excite` plus operator
   fields; it is what a replication kit ships in its `protocol/` section):
   - `[rig]`: description, units (serial-level identities), controller identity, firmware, surface or fixture
     materials, envelope of conditions, mounting notes;
   - `[[instrument]]`: name, quantity, unit, `sigma`, `bias_bound`, timestamp instant, clock, calibration
     identity and date;
   - `[[clock]]`: nominal rate and the shared hardware marks the operator must record (at least one before and
     one after every trial);
   - `[[experiment]]`: kind from the grammar, parameters, duration, safety envelope (limits on torque, speed,
     travel and temperature), the predicted information per parameter;
   - `[split]`: by day; which days are calibration, validation and holdout; validation and holdout sealed;
   - `[conditions]`: the counter-based draw from which each trial's conditions come;
   - `[attempts]`: the rule that every attempted trial is logged, aborted ones included, with a running counter.
2. **Package A, actuator stand.**
   - Rig: one actuator on a rigid stand, output shaft carrying a payload of known inertia (stated with its
     uncertainty), axis vertical so gravity torque is zero, or horizontal with a known mass moment.
   - Instruments: phase or bus current; output encoder; bus voltage (needed for the energy cross-check);
     optionally an in-line torque sensor.
   - Experiments, both with and without the payload where stated: slow torque ramps from rest in both
     directions (static friction, Stribeck speed); velocity steps and coast-down (Coulomb and viscous
     friction); multisine torque unloaded and with the payload (armature; torque-constant scale); a step
     command with a shared mark (delay, lag); stall and speed sweep at two bus voltages (torque–speed envelope);
     a thermal soak at fixed current (thermal resistances and capacitances).
   - Days: two calibration days, one validation day, one holdout day; the held-out trajectory is a multisine
     with a different seed, registered before the validation day is read.
   - On return: `ground.ingest`; `ground.excite` on the recorded experiments; `ground.identify --stage
     actuators`; `ground.predict` for the held-out trajectory with prior cards and with the candidate;
     `ground.score`.
   - Completion: the sealed prediction is scored; the card version is `admitted` in the stand's cell, or
     `rejected` with its reason; either outcome completes the package.
3. **Package B, ramp and material pairs.**
   - Rig: a tilting plane with an inclinometer; a sled with interchangeable sole and floor samples; a force
     gauge or a driven pull at constant speed; weights for at least two normal loads.
   - Experiments per material pair and condition (dry, wet, dusty as declared): incline to first slip, both
     directions of travel, ten repeats; sled drag at two normal loads, ten repeats each; one driven traction
     run in the torque-limited regime and one in the traction-limited regime.
   - On return: `ground.ingest` (`kind = "bench"`); `ground.identify --stage contact`.
   - Completion: friction per pair with its interval, written as candidate interface-card versions keyed by
     pair and condition; and the alias shown separated: the identifiability report on the torque-limited runs
     alone refuses μ, and with the traction-limited run added it does not.
4. **Dry run.** Before any hardware time, each protocol is executed on its synthetic twin (the rehearsal's rig
   and bench fixtures): the same verbs, the same completion check, all outputs labelled `synthetic`. A protocol
   that does not complete on its twin is not taken to a bench.
5. **What changes in the product.** After A: one card's guessed-field share falls for that unit; the reality
   line for results in the stand's cell may read `candidate` and, with enough validation pairs, `calibrated`.
   After B: interface cards for the measured pairs carry `measured` fields. Nothing else changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each protocol file validates against `frob.ground.protocol/1` and names marks, instruments with
      uncertainty, a day split with sealed parts, a conditions draw and the attempts rule.
- [ ] The dry run of each package completes on its synthetic twin with every output `synthetic`.
- [ ] The `--bundle` mode applies each package's completion check, exercised now on the twin bundles through
      the same code path: Package A: the returned bundle is admitted without refusal; a frozen prediction of
      the held-out trajectory is scored; the card gains `measured` fields with the dataset hash and the cell;
      the calibration record's energy verdict is present. Package B: friction per pair is reported with its
      interval from at least two normal loads; the torque-limited-only request is refused and the combined
      request is not.
- [ ] The bead closes at M5 on the items above (typed close `pass` citing the dry-run log), whatever OPEN-GROUND-2
      decides; every report still prints `reality: no-claim` while no real bundle has been admitted. A real
      bundle of a funded package is run through the `--bundle` mode whenever it returns, and its result is
      written to the lane's own records (the admission record, the candidate card versions), never as a
      condition of this bead's close.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/protocol.rs`: `protocol_roundtrip`; `protocol_without_marks_rejected`;
`protocol_without_instrument_uncertainty_rejected`; `protocol_with_row_split_rejected`;
`package_a_experiments_cover_theta` (every parameter of the joint model is informed by at least one listed
experiment, per the identifiability report on the twin); `package_b_requires_two_loads`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_bench_ab.sh`, header `bead=N30b-38 milestone=M5 labels=ground,e2e seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`; with no arguments it performs the dry run of both packages on their synthetic twins;
with `--bundle <path>` it runs the same sequence on a returned real bundle. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/ground_bench_ab/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead` ("N30b-38"), `step`, `event`: each command with its exact command line, exit code and wall time, the
evidence block, the protocol's validation result and hash, the completion check's result per package with the
planted values against the identified ones (actuator parameters within 10%; 3.33 N, 6.67 N, 4 N and 8 N for the
friction row); one `assert` per negative control with `caught`; on failure the first failing check and the
protocol or bundle path; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed; for real bundles identifiers, counts and
intervals only, never sample content.

### Known answers and negative controls

On the twins: the planted actuator parameters within 10% (the rehearsal's actuator row); 3.33 N and 6.67 N
indistinguishable and 4 N against 8 N separated (the friction row). Negative controls: a protocol with one
normal load (package B's completion check fails); a returned bundle whose trial counter exceeds its row count
(admission refuses it: a survivor-only log).

## Considerations

- A stand measures one unit. The class card's unit-to-unit spread stays at its prior until several units have
  been on the stand.
- Bus voltage is listed as required because without it the energy cross-check is not evaluable and the card
  carries the flag `energy-unchecked`.
- Material samples age; the dataset's condition field and the calibration's expiry apply to interface cards as
  to any other.
- Private premises: a dataset recorded where the owner marks the place private inherits the mark and cannot
  enter a kit.
