## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality; it reads files and never commands a
device. Bench work is optional, is packaged separately and blocks no milestone (decision ADR-GROUND-10). Six
packages are defined as imports; each leaves `reality: no-claim` in place if it never runs, and whether any is
funded is the owner's decision (N30b-26). The bench protocol format and packages A and B are in N30b-38. As
there, this bead closes at M5 on its protocols and dry runs, which need no hardware; a funded package's real run
is never a condition of its close.

Packages C and D are the two fixture-level benches. C is the first whole-system comparison: the program's
first task fixture, built for real. D closes the loop between a design and a manufactured part.

| Package | Rig | Measures | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| C. Instrumented cart | The cart and sill fixture with 60 mm and 75 mm wheels | Current, encoder, crossing time | A frozen prediction and the A/B ranking are scored on a sealed day |
| D. One built link | A printed or machined link with declared fiducials | A scan and a scale reading | The as-built loop closes and the tolerance audit is produced |

The fixture behind C asks one question: for a fixed motor and a current-limited cart, does a 75 mm front wheel
improve loaded delivery across a 12 mm door sill relative to a 60 mm wheel? Design A has 60 mm front wheels
(total mass 8.000 kg); design B has 75 mm front wheels at the same density and width (8.225 kg, the front axle
15 mm higher). The cart has a wheelbase of 0.32 m, a 5.0 kg chassis and a 2.2 kg payload at a fore-aft offset
that is a condition of the trial; one motor per front wheel (torque constant 0.2 N·m/A, resistance 1 Ω, current
limit ±6 A, 24 V bus); an outer loop at 100 Hz. In simulation the ranking of A and B depends on load
distribution, motor lag, impact and momentum. A real cart either agrees with that ranking or does not, and a
reversed ranking is a result.

## Scope

In: for each of C and D the rig description, instruments, experiments or measurements, split, the verbs run on
the returned bundle, and the completion check. Out: building the cart or the link; the mechanisms exercised
(the beads in `deps`).

## Technical approach

1. **Package C, instrumented cart.**
   - Rig: one cart buildable in both designs (swappable front wheels), a level floor of a declared surface
     class, a 12 mm sill across the lane (sharp-edged or filleted, declared), a dock position.
   - Units and cell: one cart with one controller and firmware; the cell's design component differs between A
     and B, so A and B are two cells sharing units, controller, surface and task family.
   - Instruments: current per driven wheel; wheel encoders; bus voltage; a crossing detector (light gate or
     contact strip at the sill's far edge) on a clock that shares hardware marks with the controller clock;
     scale for the payload; a rule or gauge for the payload offset.
   - Conditions, drawn in advance: payload offset and approach speed within the envelope; sill stratum.
   - Days: two calibration days per design, one validation day, one sealed holdout day on which both designs
     run under the same drawn conditions, interleaved.
   - Registered metrics: success (docked inside the time limit), energy over the trial, peak current, and
     crossing time (time-based: requires bracketing marks; dropped if time-based comparison has been withdrawn
     by its kill gate).
   - Study: fixed-n, with the reachability pre-analysis run at registration. With zero failures in n trials the
     95% upper bound on a failure probability is 1 − 0.05^(1/n) (13.9% at n = 20, 4.95% at n = 59); for a paired
     A-against-B decision with a five-point margin, the pre-analysis states which verdicts are reachable at the
     planned n, and the trial is not run for a verdict it cannot return.
   - On return: `ground.ingest`; identification of the two motors (package A's method, on the cart with wheels
     lifted); `ground.predict` for each sealed trial of both designs, including the registered decision "B
     beats A by at least the margin"; `ground.score`.
   - Completion: the frozen prediction and the A/B ranking are scored on the sealed day. Outcomes that
     complete the package: ranking confirmed; ranking `inconclusive` at the registered n; `ranking-falsified`
     (the cell's domain narrows to where the ranking held, and the pair with its conditions and logs becomes a
     regression case).
2. **Package D, one built link.**
   - Part: one link of a reference design, printed or machined from a build packet that declares its datum
     scheme and at least three non-collinear fiducials.
   - Instruments: a scanner with stated accuracy; a scale; optionally calipers for two reference lengths.
   - Measurements: a scan with fiducial correspondences (or registered by an identified external tool whose
     identity is recorded); a mass reading; the support used during scanning.
   - On return: `ground.ingest` of the scan; registration with pose covariance; deviation and thickness fields;
     posteriors on the link's design parameters; effective density from mass over computed volume; the as-built
     variant with its own `ModelId`; a batch with the as-designed and as-built robots; the tolerance audit.
   - Completion: the as-built loop closes (a variant exists and ran beside the as-designed model) and the
     tolerance audit is produced, listing each measured feature as within, outside, undecided or not
     allocated.
3. **Dry run.** Each package is first executed on its synthetic twin: for C, the cart fixture with a truth
   simulator whose contact model is perturbed; for D, the rehearsal's link with planted pose and wall
   deviation. A package that does not complete on its twin is not taken to a bench.
4. **What changes in the product.** After C: two cells of origin `real` exist; each holds at most one sealed
   day of trials, typically too few for a band (below 19 pairs the band is refused, and that refusal is the
   expected state). After D: one unit has an as-built variant; the part class's card is unchanged.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each protocol validates against the bench protocol schema; package C's protocol lists the shared marks
      for the crossing detector and a conditions draw common to both designs on the sealed day.
- [ ] The dry run of each package completes on its synthetic twin with every output `synthetic`.
- [ ] The `--bundle` mode applies each package's completion check, exercised now on the twin bundles through
      the same code path: Package C: both designs' predictions are frozen before the sealed day is read; the
      registered decision is evaluated at the registered n and level; one of the three outcomes is recorded; a
      reversed ranking produces `ranking-falsified` and a regression case. Package D: registration is
      well-posed; the variant's `ModelId` differs from the as-designed one; the audit table exists.
- [ ] The bead closes at M5 on the items above (typed close `pass` citing the dry-run log), whatever OPEN-GROUND-2
      decides. A real bundle of a funded package is run through the `--bundle` mode whenever it returns, and its
      result is written to the lane's own records (cells of origin `real`, the as-built variant), never as a
      condition of this bead's close.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/protocol_cd.rs`: `cart_cells_differ_only_in_design`;
`sealed_day_conditions_shared_by_both_designs`; `crossing_time_requires_bracketing_marks`;
`preanalysis_attached_to_registered_decision`; `link_packet_has_three_noncollinear_fiducials`;
`audit_lists_every_allocated_feature`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_bench_cd.sh`, header `bead=N30b-39 milestone=M5 labels=ground,e2e seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`; with no arguments, the dry run of both packages on their twins; with `--bundle <path>`,
the same sequence on a returned real bundle. Calls are in the registry's forms (VERBS-ground). Package C, one
bundle per design (the two cells): `frob ground ingest <bundle> --design <cart design> --task-family <sill task
family> --key … --json`; `frob ground identify <dataset> --stage actuators --key … --json`; `frob ground predict
--dataset <dataset> --part holdout --cell <cell> --model <id> --scene <id> --task <id> --metric
crossing_time_s:0.90 --decision <spec> --study fixed-n:<n> --key … --json`; `frob ground score <prediction> --key …
--json`. Package D: `frob ground ingest <scan bundle> --design <design> --task-family tf:none --emit-variant --key
… --json` (a scan of one part has no task; `--emit-variant` is what builds the as-built variant and returns its
`ModelId` and the audit). Every `run` and `mutate` call carries its own key, written `--key "$(e2e_key
<label>)"`. Log: `target/e2e-logs/ground_bench_cd/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N30b-39"), `step`, `event`: each command with its exact
command line, exit code and wall time, the evidence block, freeze and read sequences for package C (ledger
sequence numbers, freeze before read), the decision and its level with the planned n and the reachable verdicts,
the two model identities for package D, the completion check per package; one `assert` per negative control
with `caught`; on failure the first failing check and the protocol or bundle path; a `summary` with assertions
passed and failed; identifiers and counts only for real bundles.

### Known answers and negative controls

On the twins: the protocol row (freeze before read; three mutations refused) and the as-built row (pose and
0.3 mm deviation inside stated uncertainty). Negative controls: a sealed day on which A and B ran under
different conditions (the paired decision is refused as unpaired); a scan of the link with two fiducials
(refused as ill-posed).

## Considerations

- Package C will usually end below the calibration floor for a band. Its value is the scored ranking, not a
  band; the report says "no band: n below 19" in those words.
- The fixture in simulation is synthetic with explicit values; a built cart is a new fixture identity with
  measured profiles, never a silent replacement of the synthetic one.
- A printed link's effective density is the informative number in package D; the scale reading is not optional.
- Operator discipline decides whether the data is admissible: every attempt logged, conditions as drawn, marks
  recorded. The protocol file is the checklist.
