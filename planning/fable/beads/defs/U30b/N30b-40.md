## Background

Ground is the subsystem that compares simulation with measured reality; it reads files and never commands a
device. Bench work is optional, is packaged separately and blocks no milestone (decision ADR-GROUND-10). Six
packages are defined as imports; each leaves `reality: no-claim` in place if it never runs, and whether any is
funded is the owner's decision (N30b-26). The bench protocol format is in N30b-38.

Packages E and F are the whole-robot ones, and E is the only route by which the subsystem's one Reality gate
(the first real cell: a frozen prediction scored on sealed data and a band issued) can be recorded as a pass.

| Package | Rig | Measures | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| E. Calibrated release | Named robot, surface and task cells | Whole-robot logs | Bands issued per cell from held-out predictions, with limits stated |
| F. Revalidation | Independently acquired traces for cells of E | Whole-robot logs | Each ranking reversal and calibration limit reported |

"Calibrated release" means a release of the software in which some reports, for some named cells, print a
reality line other than `no-claim`. The North-Star picture: bench measurements of one quadruped on one floor
are imported with their provenance; predictions for held-out episodes are frozen before the comparison; for
that robot and task family a cost of transport is reported with a band at 90% nominal coverage from 20
calibration episodes, with the statement that coverage at this sample size varies by about six points; the
same question about a design that was never built returns a refusal that names the missing calibrated family.

F exists because a calibration checked only by the people who made it has not been checked. It is the
subsystem's own version of "no self-certification".

## Scope

In: for E and F the cells, data requirements, protocol, the verbs run, the limits that must be stated, and the
completion checks. Out: acquiring a robot; the mechanisms (the beads in `deps`); the record of the first real
cell (BD-397), which package E feeds.

## Technical approach

1. **Package E, calibrated release.**
   - Cells are named before any data is taken: for each, the design, the unit or units by serial identity, the
     controller identity and firmware, the surface class and condition, the task family and the envelope box.
     A cell that cannot be named cannot be calibrated.
   - Data per cell, minimum: identification experiments for the stages the task needs; at least 19 episodes
     whose predictions were frozen before reading and whose conditions were drawn in advance (the calibration
     floor for a per-unit band at any level up to 0.95; a finite band at 0.90 needs 9 and the minimum count is
     19); one sealed holdout day beyond those; every attempted episode logged.
   - Sequence per cell: admit and seal; clocks; identifiability; identification in order; candidate cards;
     frozen predictions; scoring, holdout retirement, admission or rejection; band issue or refusal; monitors
     armed; discrepancy fed back; a replication kit, or the reason none could be built.
   - Limits that must be stated with every band, in the release notes and on every report in the cell: n, the
     level, the half-width with its unit, the sentence that coverage is marginal over calibration draws with
     its standard deviation at this n (0.063 at n = 20 for 90%), the draw type (episodes of this unit; never
     another unit unless nine or more units were calibrated), the envelope, the expiry date and hours, the
     measured sim-to-real correlation with the recommendation `real-only evaluation` where it is below 0.5, the
     monitors' power facts, and the list of cells for which a band was refused with the reason.
   - Completion: bands issued per cell from held-out predictions, with limits stated; a cell that ends in a
     refusal or a rejected candidate is reported as such and counts toward completion as a stated limit.
2. **Package F, revalidation.**
   - Independence: traces acquired by a different operator, on different days, with instruments under their own
     calibration identities; where possible on another unit of the same design (which then tests the claim's
     boundary: a per-unit band does not cover another unit, and the request is refused).
   - Procedure: admit the new dataset; the scope check runs first (a changed component retires or separates the
     cell); for cells still in scope, feed the monitors episode by episode; score the predictions frozen in
     package E's release against the new sealed data as a fresh confirmation attempt at level
     α/[i·(i + 1)].
   - Report: each ranking reversal (`ranking-falsified`, with the narrowed domain and the regression case);
     each calibration limit found (a retirement with its reason; an under-covering band with its miss count;
     an expiry reached; an envelope edge at which residuals grow); and each cell that held.
   - Completion: each ranking reversal and calibration limit is reported. A revalidation that finds nothing
     reports that too, with the monitors' power at the calibration size so that "nothing found" is not
     over-read.
3. **Dry run.** Both packages are executed on synthetic twins first: E on the rehearsal's whole-robot cell; F on
   a second generation of that cell with a planted drift and a planted ranking reversal, which the report must
   contain.
4. **What changes in the product.** After E: the Reality lane's first record can take its real-cell form; the
   bet on calibration bands is read on real data for the first time; reports in the named cells print
   `calibrated` lines. After F: the limits of those cells are public, including the ones that failed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every cell of package E is named in the protocol before its first dataset is admitted (the protocol's
      ledger sequence precedes the first ingest).
- [ ] The dry runs complete on the twins: E issues a band with all stated limits; F's report contains the
      planted drift's retirement and the planted ranking reversal.
- [ ] Package E, if run: for each cell either a band with every listed limit printed, or a refusal or rejection
      with its code and reason; the first real-cell record is written from one of them.
- [ ] Package F, if run: the report lists reversals, limits and cells that held, with the confirmation-attempt
      level used and the monitors' power facts.
- [ ] No band of package E is offered for a unit, firmware, surface or task outside its cell (attempts are
      refused with the uncalibrated-cell or retired-calibration refusal).
- [ ] If neither runs, the bead closes as not funded with a reason citing the owner's decision, and the Reality
      lane's record is the no-claim form.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-ground/tests/protocol_ef.rs`: `cells_named_before_first_ingest`;
`release_note_lists_every_limit` (a band rendered without its expiry or draw type fails the note's lint);
`revalidation_attempt_level_sequence` (0.025, 0.00833 for the first two attempts at α = 0.05);
`other_unit_request_refused`; `nothing_found_report_prints_power`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/ground_bench_ef.sh`, header `bead=N30b-40 milestone=M5 labels=ground,e2e seed=1`: sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh`; with no arguments, the dry runs on the twins; with `--bundle <path>` and `--package E|F`,
the same sequence on returned real bundles. Log: `target/e2e-logs/ground_bench_ef/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with
each command, exit code and evidence block; per cell the status line, n, level, half-width, the stated limits
as structured fields, monitor states; for F the list of reversals and limits; identifiers and counts only for
real bundles. When package E completes on real data the summary lists `gates: ["GATE-GROUND-10"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

On the twins: mean coverage 19/21 at n = 20 (the band row); retirement within 20 episodes of the planted drift
(the drift row). Negative controls: a release note generated with a band's expiry omitted (lint fails); a
revalidation that pools the new traces into the old calibration set before scoring (refused: the predictions
must be scored on data the calibration never saw).

## Considerations

- Twenty calibration episodes on one robot support one sentence about that robot. The package's deliverable is
  as much the stated limits as the bands.
- Package E answers, by acquisition, the question of which real dataset is admitted first; the owner's
  decisions on that and on expiry limits are prerequisites.
- If the kill gate on bands has fired, package E ends in scored predictions and raw residual tables with
  `no-claim` lines; F still reports reversals.
- A revalidation on a second unit of the design is the cheapest evidence the program could ever get about
  exchangeability across units, and it will usually be a refusal followed by two per-unit bands to compare.
