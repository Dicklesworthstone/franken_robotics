## Background

Foundry is the subsystem that turns a qualified robot design into something that could be built: it extracts
structural load cases from simulated telemetry, sizes and optimizes links, checks them independently for stress,
buckling, modes and cumulative fatigue damage, feeds the resulting mass and inertia back into the model, checks
manufacturability, allocates tolerances, exports geometry and writes the build packet `.fbuild`. Two Moonshot
items sit beside it: export of fixed-point controller code with overflow checks, and lifecycle compression. Two
crates hold all of it: `frob-foundry` and `frob-lifecycle`, both layer 5, cluster `foundry`. Tiers: the structure
loop, manufacturability, tolerance allocation, export and the build packet are Frontier [F]; firmware export and
lifecycle compression are Moonshot [M], behind feature flags, and nothing depends on them.

**Foundry fabricates nothing, buys nothing, flashes nothing and signs nothing off.** Every artifact it writes
says so in machine-readable flags (INV-FOUNDRY-01: every exported artifact carries the no-authorization flags,
all false). It is an output-quality subsystem: it makes the answer better and does not make it arrive sooner, so
no part of the program's ten-fold thesis rests on it.

This bead creates the crate that every other Foundry bead writes into, and fixes four things that must exist
before any mechanism lands: the module map; the type that caps every Foundry result at evidence class Estimated;
the decision record; and the firewall that keeps the Moonshots out of the build packet.

**Decisions recorded here (each is restated, with its reason, in the bead it governs).**

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-FOUNDRY-01 | Optimization and build output run after the design decision, on qualified finalists. The beam rung also runs at preflight and after the first rollouts as a validity check that can block promotion. An exploratory run is allowed, is labelled, and cannot enter a packet. | Structure adds CPU-hours per design and at most about 8% of robot mass; it cannot shorten a decision. | Structure inside screening as an optimization: it lengthens the loop for every candidate. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-02 | Two rungs: closed-form beam sizing by default and as fallback; three-dimensional topology optimization for finalists. | The beam rung costs milliseconds, answers the structural-margin analysis A-15 and is the engineered baseline every optimized part is judged against. | Topology optimization only: no baseline, no fallback. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-03 | A load case is one instant of one episode. Peaks from different instants are never assembled. | The assembled case can be unsafe. | A per-channel peak envelope. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-04 | Symmetric limbs share one optimization only after their load histories are transformed into one frame and unioned. | An offset payload loads nominally identical limbs unequally. | Optimize one limb and copy. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-05 | The optimizer never checks its own result. Stress, buckling, modes and fatigue are evaluated on a separately generated body-fitted mesh. | The optimizer's stress aggregate is not a bound, and thresholding changes the structure. | Accepting the optimizer's final stress field. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-06 | Fatigue is reported as utilization under a named empirical law with card provenance, class Estimated. The word "life" is not used for a result. | The sibling library states the same limit; curves for printed and cast material are model claims. | A predicted life in hours. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-07 | Actuator cards are held fixed inside the structure loop. Re-selecting an actuator is an outer step with full revalidation. | Combining the two loops raises the loop gain and can cycle between catalogue sizes. | One combined loop. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-08 | Exported STEP is a faceted solid and is described as interchange evidence. No curved-surface re-fit is attempted. | That is what the sibling exporter emits and checks. | A fitted spline surface model. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-09 | Every exported artifact carries the no-authorization flags; the exporter has no code path that sets one true. | A packet must not be mistaken for a release. | A flag an operator can set. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-10 | No priced bill of materials without a pinned, licensed catalogue. | No licensed catalogue is chosen yet (OPEN-FOUNDRY-2). | Scraped or assumed prices. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-11 | No gate depends on a reviewer, a machine shop or a board. Those are import lanes that rest at `no-claim`. | A simulator cannot gate on what it lacks. | A human sign-off gate. |
| ADR-FOUNDRY-12 | Firmware export and lifecycle compression live behind features `firmware-export` and `lifecycle`; the latter is its own crate. | Moonshots stay off the critical path and out of the build packet's dependency graph. | Modules of the main crate, always compiled. |

half of the firewall (the rules R1 to R4 in `xtask check-deps` with their planted violations, and the

## Technical approach

1. **Manifest and features.** `frob-foundry` depends on `frob-core`, `frob-model`, `frob-chronicle` (read side)
   and `frob-fsim` with feature `structure`. It names no FrankenSim type: only `frob-fsim` may. Cargo features:
   `structure` (default for this crate; forwards to the airlock group), `moonshot-firmware-export` (off;
   compiles `firmware/`), and no feature for lifecycle in this crate, which is the separate crate
   `frob-lifecycle` (linked only by the verb layer under `moonshot-lifecycle`). Cargo feature names follow
   workspace rule L-12 (a feature that selects a tier of ambition is named `frontier-*` or `moonshot-*` and no
   default build enables one); the chapter's short names `firmware-export` and `lifecycle`, as in
   ADR-FOUNDRY-12, remain the names at the user surface (documentation, the flag `--with-firmware`, refusal
   messages). N31b-03 checks the rule; the workspace owner may still rename.
2. **Module map (DESIGN).**
   ```
   src/lib.rs  class.rs  admission.rs  ceiling.rs  refusal.rs  verbs.rs  structure_loop.rs
   src/sib/        typed wrappers over the airlock traits, one file per sibling crate
   src/loads/      campaign sample freebody traction unit influence envelope symmetry variants separation
                   spectra declared
   src/beam/  src/margin.rs          the beam rung and analysis A-15 (seed bead BD-401)
   src/topo/       region formulate bisect plate extract classes
   src/check/      stress buckling modes fatigue gauges card
   src/feedback/   massprops edit converge outer
   src/fab/        process dispatch corner voids access overhang inopt cost
   src/tol/        qoi sens allocate stack robust structured report
   src/export/     repair mesh step decimate
   src/packet/     manifest flags writer reader parts geometry mass harness schedule evidence assemble
   src/firmware/   (feature moonshot-firmware-export) scope emit qformat range bv induct quant vectors
   ```
3. **Shared vocabulary (DESIGN sketch; fixes intent, not signatures).**
   ```rust
   pub struct LinkClassId(Blake3);   // links equal up to the mechanical symmetry group; mirror images are one class
   pub enum PortKind { Joint, ContactPatch, Cable }
   pub struct PortId { pub link: BodyId, pub kind: PortKind, pub index: u8 }
   pub enum StructLabel { Exploratory, ModelConditional, CheckSameFamily, FatigueNoClaim, Provisional,
                          TierSensitive, SingularFilletRequired, NoClaimNearInterface }
   /// A Foundry number. The class cannot exceed Estimated.
   pub struct FoundryValue<T> { value: T, unit: Unit, class: FoundryClass, labels: Vec<StructLabel>,
                                weakest: InputRef }
   pub struct FoundryClass(());      // one value; `evidence()` returns EvidenceClass::Estimated
   ```
   `FoundryClass` has no constructor that takes a class. A claim product built from Foundry values therefore
   has class Estimated at most (INV-FOUNDRY-06: Foundry and lifecycle results are at most class Estimated,
   except instance certificates of the firmware exporter). The one exception type, `InstanceCertificate`, lives
   in `firmware/bv.rs` and is created only by a replayed bit-vector proof.
4. **The firewall (INV-FOUNDRY-07: the build packet does not depend on `frob-lifecycle` or on the firmware
   feature).** The crate's side of two rules that N31b-03 enters in the dependency checker's table: `frob-foundry`
   (any feature set) has no path to `frob-lifecycle`; `src/packet/**` compiles with `moonshot-firmware-export` off and its item graph names nothing
   under `firmware/` (enforced by a `cfg`-off build of the packet tests plus a source-level check that
   `packet/` contains no `use crate::firmware`).
5. **`CONTRACT.md`, ten sections:** purpose; inputs (design, model, T2 campaign, cards); outputs (load cases,
   sizes, checked geometry, typed edits, tolerances, exports, packet); invariants (INV-FOUNDRY-01 to -07);
   refusals (the fourteen codes of the cluster); determinism class (D0 for the beam rung and exact comparisons;
   same bits twice on one machine for finite-element jobs; nothing claimed across machines); evidence class of
   outputs (Estimated); validity domain (small-strain linear elasticity; rate-independent stress-life fatigue; no
   bolted joints, press fits, threads or welds; stress within one boss diameter of an interface is `no-claim`);
   oracles (hand derivations under `docs/derivations/foundry/`); known gaps (the sibling gaps of the adapter
   bead N31a-04).
6. **What is recorded in the ledger.** Every Foundry job writes a record with design, model, campaign identity,
   stack-lock digest, mesh hashes and the labels above; mechanism beads extend it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `cargo check -p frob-foundry` passes with default features, with `--no-default-features`, and with `--features moonshot-firmware-export`; the layer and dependency checks pass.
- [ ] A compile-fail test shows that no public path builds a `FoundryValue` with a class above Estimated, and that `FoundryClass` cannot be converted into `Certified` or `Proved`.
- [ ] The firewall test fails when a planted `use frob_lifecycle` is added to `frob-foundry`, and when a planted `use crate::firmware` is added under `packet/`.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` has the ten sections; `docs/adr/foundry.md` lists ADR-FOUNDRY-01 to -12 with reason and rejected alternative.
- [ ] The crate documentation's first paragraph states that Foundry fabricates, buys, flashes and signs off nothing.
- [ ] Line count of the crate is reported against its cap of 12 thousand lines by the workspace report.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry class firewall`:
- `foundry_value_is_estimated`: combining a `FoundryValue` with a Certified input yields a claim product of class Estimated whose `weakest` names the Foundry input.
- `no_public_constructor_for_higher_class` (compile-fail, three cases: struct literal, `From`, setter).
- `labels_union_on_combine`: `ModelConditional` on one input appears on the product.
- `packet_module_builds_without_firmware`: the packet test module compiles and runs with `moonshot-firmware-export` off.
- `firewall_rejects_lifecycle_edge` and `firewall_rejects_firmware_use_in_packet` (planted defects, each caught).
- `contract_has_ten_sections`; `adr_file_lists_twelve`.

### End-to-end script and logging
None of its own. The scripts of beads N31a-53 to N31a-59 source the shared end-to-end helper and assert, as
their first step, that `frob doctor --json` lists the `foundry` cluster with the feature set compiled in; the
line is logged with `event: "assert"` and the build identity.

### Known answers and negative controls
No numeric known answer. Negative controls are the three planted defects above. Determinism: the firewall and
class tests are host tests and identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The cap of 12 thousand lines is a scope cap that triggers a conversation at 115%; the module map above is
  sized for it only if the sibling crates carry the mechanics. If an upstream gap forces a local evaluator to
  grow (overhang, tool access), the growth is reported against the cap.
- `frob-foundry` exists as its own crate because the `structure` group adds 13 FrankenSim crates and about 412
  thousand lines beyond what earlier milestones compile, and no other crate needs them.
- Foundry jobs are whole jobs of seconds to minutes, so the worker-process mode of the airlock suits them; no
  module may assume in-process access to a sibling.
- The same class ceiling must hold in `frob-lifecycle`; that crate re-exports `FoundryClass` and does not define
  its own.
