## Background

Structural load cases for a robot's links should come from what the robot did, not from an assumed static load.
The simulator already records what is needed: Chronicle's level-T2 telemetry holds, at control rate, joint
constraint wrenches, contact wrenches and body twists. (Telemetry levels: T0 per-episode metrics; T1 events; T2
traces at control rate; T3 full snapshots. By default T2 is kept only for an address-keyed sample of worlds and
for failing worlds.)

A **load campaign** is the qualified task set plus push-recovery and drop tasks, run with T2 on *every* world
at *fixed world addresses*, so that the campaign is reproducible and is paired across iterations of the
structure loop. Push recovery and drops are added because the tasks a design was qualified on may never load a
link near its limit, while the events that do (a shove, a fall from a step) are exactly the ones a structure
must survive; rare overloads dominate fatigue damage, so a campaign that never contains them certifies nothing.

Fixed addresses matter twice. Random draws in this simulator are keyed by world address, step and stream, so
two designs run at the same addresses see the same worlds; that makes the loads–mass map deterministic and the
loop's convergence test meaningful. And a load case can be replayed to its sample, which is how invariant
INV-FOUNDRY-02 (a load case is a single instant of a single episode at a recorded world address) is tested.

Rule 6 of load extraction: **missing samples refuse the metric (FROB-REF-169). Nothing is interpolated across a
telemetry gap.** A peak that fell in a dropped interval is exactly the sample a structure needs.

## Scope

In: the campaign specification and its identity; running a campaign through the simulation verbs with T2 forced
on; reading a finished campaign's traces as a stream of per-tick link samples in episode order; gap detection
and FROB-REF-169; campaign statistics (hours, counts).
Out: the free-body check on each sample (N31a-07); envelope cases and spectra (N31a-10, N31a-09); the variants
re-run under other contact stiffnesses and laws (N31a-12); hand-declared load sets (N31a-13).

## Technical approach

1. **Specification** (`loads/campaign.rs`; schema `frob.foundry.campaign/1`, DESIGN):
   ```rust
   pub struct CampaignSpec {
       tasks: Vec<TaskRef>,               // the design's qualified task set, by content hash
       push_recovery: Vec<TaskRef>,       // impulses by direction and magnitude, from the fixture library
       drops: Vec<TaskRef>,               // drop heights and attitudes
       worlds: WorldAddrSet,              // explicit addresses: (run_seed, world, episode) ranges
       tier: Tier, profile: Profile, contact_variant: ContactVariant,   // nominal unless a variant run
       channels: Vec<ChannelRef>,         // the T2 channels of step 2
       controller: ControllerPolicy,      // "baseline, re-synthesized per design"
   }
   ```
   The campaign identity is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical bytes of the specification. A campaign *run* is
   the pair (specification hash, `DesignId` + `ModelId`).
2. **Channels requested**, all in the link frame of the link they load:
   - `joint_constraint_wrench[joint]`: the wrench the joint transmits to the child link, at the joint frame,
     kind Interval mean (constraint impulse over the tick ÷ tick length); the reaction on the parent is the
     negative, transported to the parent's frame by the reader;
   - `contact_wrench[patch]`: kind Impulse summed over the tick, ÷ tick length, at the patch frame;
   - `link_twist[link]`: kind State, with the value before and after any jump in the tick;
   - `link_accel[link]`: spatial acceleration of the link-frame origin and angular acceleration, as the tick
     mean (V⁺ − V⁻) ÷ tick length reported by the stepper; gravity is not included in it.
3. **Running.** `foundry.loads --campaign <spec>` submits the campaign as one job: the simulation verb is
   called with telemetry policy `T2: all worlds` and the listed channels. Cost is estimated before admission
   from worlds × ticks × channels × 4 bytes. Illustrative sizing (DESIGN arithmetic): 12 links with two ports
   each need 12 × (2 × 6 + 12) = 288 scalars per tick; 1,000 episodes of 20 s at 500 Hz are 10⁷ ticks; raw
   storage is 288 × 4 × 10⁷ ≈ 11.5 GB before chunk compression. The estimate is printed and charged against
   the caller's budget.
4. **Reading.** `LinkSampleStream::open(run, link)` yields, in episode order and tick order, records
   `{addr: WorldAddr, tick, ports: [(PortId, Wrench)], twist, accel, flags}`. Episode order is the campaign's
   declared duty sequence (task order within a world, worlds in address order); it is part of the history
   because fatigue cycles that span tasks are counted. At a jump the reader emits the pre-jump and post-jump
   states as two records with the same tick and a `jump` flag.
5. **Gaps.** Chronicle marks dropped telemetry with its interval and reason. The reader compares the committed
   frontier of every (world, episode) with the planned tick count and scans the dropped-interval index. Any
   missing tick, missing channel or missing world → FROB-REF-169 with payload: the channel, the worlds and tick
   intervals missing, the telemetry level found, and the repair "re-run these worlds at T2 with channels X"
   as an exact command. There is no option to skip, pad or interpolate.
6. **Statistics** returned with every read: worlds, episodes, ticks, campaign hours H_c = total simulated
   time ÷ 3,600 s (the denominator of fatigue utilization), the share of ticks with any contact, peak port
   force and moment per link (as plain statistics, never as a load case).
7. **Determinism.** A campaign run is identified by addresses; re-running it on the same backend gives the same
   trace digests (class D2). The reader's output order is fixed, so every downstream reduction is reproducible.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A campaign specification round-trips through canonical JSON with a stable hash; changing one world address changes the hash.
- [ ] `foundry.loads --campaign` forces T2 on every world of the specification and the run's telemetry record shows 100% T2 coverage of the listed channels.
- [ ] Reading a campaign with one world's T2 chunk removed returns FROB-REF-169 (exit 3) naming that world, the tick interval and the channel, with the re-run command as repair.
- [ ] Reading a run recorded with sampled T2 (the default policy) returns FROB-REF-169 listing the untraced worlds.
- [ ] Two runs of one campaign for one design on one backend give identical trace digests; two designs run on one specification are traced in the same worlds.
- [ ] H_c equals worlds × episode length summed, to the tick.
- [ ] A jump in a tick yields two records flagged `jump`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry loads::campaign`:
- `spec_hash_stable_and_sensitive`.
- `stream_order_is_duty_sequence`: a two-task, two-world `synthetic` run yields records in (world, task, tick) order.
- `parent_reaction_is_negative_transported`: a child wrench (f, m) at the joint frame gives the parent the wrench (−f, −m + r × (−f)) about the parent origin, r being the joint position in the parent frame. With r = (0.1, 0, 0) m, f = (0, 0, 10) N, m = 0: force (0, 0, −10) N and moment (0, +1.0, 0) N·m.
- `gap_refuses_169` (missing tick; missing channel; missing world): three cases, each with the right pointer.
- `sampled_t2_refuses_169`.
- `no_interpolation_path`: the reader type has no method returning an estimate for a missing tick (compile-level).
- `campaign_hours_exact`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `pad-gap-with-last-value`, `skip-untraced-world`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_loads.sh` (bead N31a-53): runs a small campaign on the reference quadruped, deletes one
T2 chunk in a scratch copy of the store, and asserts the refusal; logs the specification hash, worlds, ticks,
H_c, storage bytes against the estimate, and trace digests for the determinism lane.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic: 12 × (2 × 6 + 12) = 288; 1,000 × 20 s × 500 Hz = 10⁷ ticks. Negative controls: the two planted
defects; the sampled-T2 run. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts and shard counts
(the address-keyed design makes traces independent of sharding).

## Considerations

- KILL-LOADS (decided at M5; may be recorded at M4): if the load-extraction gate fails after one repair cycle,
  load cases are declared by hand from statics with a stated dynamic factor (N31a-13) and spectra and fatigue
  read `no-claim`.
- A campaign runs on a fast tier, because it needs every world traced and many episodes; peak contact wrenches
  are what a compliant fast-tier contact gets wrong. Cases are therefore provisional until re-extracted on the
  referee (a rule owned by unit U31b); this bead stores the replay address that makes it possible.
- Storage for full-T2 campaigns is large; retention follows Chronicle's policy, but the samples that became
  envelope cases must be pinned for as long as a packet cites them.
- Privacy: campaign traces of a private scene never appear in logs; only digests and counts do.
