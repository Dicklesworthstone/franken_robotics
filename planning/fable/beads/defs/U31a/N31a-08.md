## Background

Telemetry gives the load at a joint as a wrench: a force and a moment at a point. A finite-element model needs
tractions on a surface. The sibling solid-mechanics library applies consistent surface loads, but "a port
wrench is not turned into interface tractions" there (FACT), so Foundry owns the conversion.

Rule 5 of load extraction: **a port wrench becomes tractions by a declared interface pattern on the port boss:
a traction field affine in position whose resultant force and moment equal the wrench exactly. Stress within
one boss diameter of an interface is `no-claim`; bolted joints and press fits are outside this chapter.**

A *port boss* is the solid region of a link around a port (a joint interface, or a foot or wheel contact
patch) that the optimizer may not remove. Its *interface surface* Γ is the face, bore or patch through which
the neighbour loads the link.

Why an affine field with exact resultant, and why the no-claim zone. By Saint-Venant's principle the stress
far from where a load is applied depends only on the load's resultant, so any statically equivalent
distribution gives the same answer beyond about one characteristic dimension. The real distribution at a
bolted flange or a pressed bearing depends on preload, fit and friction, none of which this chapter models.
The honest design is therefore the simplest field that carries the wrench exactly, plus a declared zone near
the interface where no stress number is claimed. The rejected alternatives: point loads at a node (singular
stress, mesh-dependent); a rigid spider element (stiffens the boss and changes the mode shapes); a contact
model of the joint (out of scope, and it would import bolt and fit parameters no card supplies).

Proof obligation PO-FOUNDRY-02: the interface traction pattern returns the port wrench exactly; tested as
resultant force and moment to rounding.

## Scope

In: the affine traction field for a wrench on a surface; its discrete form as consistent nodal loads; the
declared patterns; the no-claim mask; the resultant self-check on every application.
Out: which wrenches are applied (N31a-09, N31a-10); the solve (the sibling, through N31a-04); bolted joints,
press fits, threads, welds (not modelled anywhere in Foundry).

## Technical approach

1. **Continuous definition.** Let the wrench be (F, M) with M taken about the port origin r_p. For the
   interface surface Γ with area A and centroid x̄, write r = x − x̄. Define
   - M_c = M − (x̄ − r_p) × F, the moment about the centroid;
   - J_Γ = ∫_Γ (‖r‖²·I − r·rᵀ) dΓ, the second-moment tensor of the surface about its centroid;
   - w = J_Γ⁻¹·M_c;
   - **t(x) = F/A + w × r.**
   The field is affine in x. Its resultant force is F, because ∫ r dΓ = 0. Its resultant moment about x̄ is
   ∫ r × (w × r) dΓ = J_Γ·w = M_c. J_Γ is symmetric positive definite for any surface not contained in a line,
   so w exists for every face, bore or patch.
2. **What the field looks like on a flat circular face** of radius R with normal z (the hand cases of the
   tests): a normal force gives uniform pressure F_z/(π·R²); a bending moment M_x gives normal traction
   (M_x/I)·y with I = π·R⁴/4, peak 4·M_x/(π·R³); a torque M_z gives shear of magnitude (M_z/J)·ρ at radius ρ
   with J = π·R⁴/2, peak 2·M_z/(π·R³); a shear force gives uniform shear.
3. **Declared patterns** (`loads/traction.rs`, DESIGN). The pattern names the surface region the field acts on:
   `face` (a flange or mounting face), `bore` (a cylindrical bearing seat), `face+bore` (their union), `patch`
   (a foot or wheel contact region, taken as the declared patch outline on the outer surface). The pattern is
   part of the port declaration and is recorded with every load case. The formula is the same for all; only Γ
   changes.
4. **Discrete form.** On the triangulated Γ of a tetrahedral mesh with linear elements, the nodal load is
   f_i = ∫_Γ N_i·t dΓ with N_i the linear shape functions. The integrand is quadratic, so a three-point
   degree-2 rule per triangle is exact. A, x̄ and J_Γ are computed from the same triangles with the same rule.
   Because the shape functions sum to one and reproduce position, Σ f_i = F and Σ x_i × f_i equals the
   moment of t exactly; the discrete resultant therefore equals the wrench to rounding, on any mesh.
5. **Self-check on every application.** After assembling nodal loads the function recomputes (Σ f_i,
   Σ (x_i − r_p) × f_i) in compensated summation and compares with (F, M). A relative difference above
   10⁻¹² (`f64`) is an internal error, not a refusal: the job fails with the surface, the mesh hash and both
   wrenches recorded.
6. **The no-claim zone.** Let D_p be the boss diameter of port p (the diameter of the smallest sphere
   containing Γ_p; DESIGN). Elements whose centroid lies within D_p of Γ_p are masked with
   `StructLabel::NoClaimNearInterface`. Masked elements are excluded from utilization peaks, from gauge
   placement and from the refinement criterion of the stress check, and their share of the link volume is
   reported. A link whose masked share exceeds one half is flagged `mostly-interface`: the continuum result
   says little about it.
7. **Body loads** are not this bead's: the density-weighted acceleration field is a volume load applied by the
   sibling's consistent volume-load routine; its resultant is checked the same way in N31a-09.
8. **Determinism.** Pure `f64` arithmetic in a fixed triangle order (sorted by the mesh's canonical face
   index); identical bits twice on one machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] PO-FOUNDRY-02: for 10³ seeded random wrenches on each of the four patterns, on three meshes of different density, the discrete resultant force and moment equal the wrench with relative error at most 10⁻¹².
- [ ] On a flat circular face of radius 10 mm: a 100 N normal force gives pressure 0.3183 MPa at every node; M_x = 1 N·m gives normal traction ±1.273 MPa at y = ±R; M_z = 1 N·m gives shear 0.6366 MPa at the rim.
- [ ] A wrench given about a port origin offset from the centroid is transported correctly (the test uses an offset of 5 mm and a transverse force).
- [ ] The no-claim mask covers exactly the elements within one boss diameter, and those elements never appear as a governing location in any check result.
- [ ] A degenerate interface surface (all triangles collinear, a planted defect in a fixture) is rejected with a typed error before a solve.
- [ ] The traction field at nodes is affine: second differences along any straight line of nodes vanish to rounding.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry loads::traction`:
- `resultant_exact_face`, `resultant_exact_bore`, `resultant_exact_face_plus_bore`, `resultant_exact_patch`: 10³ random wrenches each, three meshes; tolerance 10⁻¹².
- `circular_face_pressure`: 100 N on R = 10 mm: A = 3.1416 × 10⁻⁴ m²; 3.183 × 10⁵ Pa.
- `circular_face_bending`: I = π·R⁴/4 = 7.854 × 10⁻⁹ m⁴; peak 1.2732 × 10⁶ Pa.
- `circular_face_torsion`: J = 1.5708 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴; peak 6.366 × 10⁵ Pa.
- `offset_port_origin`: F = (10, 0, 0) N at r_p = x̄ + (0, 0, 0.005) m, M = 0: (x̄ − r_p) × F = (0, −0.05, 0), so M_c = (0, +0.05, 0) N·m.
- `mesh_independence_of_resultant`: coarse and fine meshes give the same resultant to 10⁻¹².
- `no_claim_mask_radius`; `mostly_interface_flag`.
- `degenerate_surface_rejected`.
- Planted defects, each caught by the resultant self-check: `one-point-quadrature` (moment wrong on coarse meshes), `moment-about-port-not-centroid` (omits the transport term), `nodal-lumping` (applies t at nodes times a lumped area).

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised through `scripts/e2e/foundry_loads.sh` (N31a-53) and `scripts/e2e/foundry_check.sh` (N31a-55): every
structural job's log line carries `traction_resultant_rel_err` per port (threshold 1e-12) and the masked volume
share per link.

### Known answers and negative controls
Hand values above (0.3183 MPa; 1.273 MPa; 0.6366 MPa). Negative controls: the three planted defects.
Metamorphic: rigidly moving surface and wrench together leaves the nodal loads' resultant unchanged in the
moved frame; scaling the wrench by k scales every nodal load by k; reversing the wrench reverses every nodal
load. Determinism: nodal load vectors are bitwise identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The affine field is not the true contact pressure; nothing within one boss diameter is claimed. A reader who
  needs bolt or bearing stresses needs a joint model, which is outside this chapter by decision.
- A contact patch under a foot moves and changes size in reality; the `patch` pattern uses the declared patch
  outline, which is one more reason foot stresses near the sole are `no-claim`.
- If the upstream library later provides a wrench-to-traction routine, this function becomes its differential
  test and then its wrapper; the resultant self-check stays.
