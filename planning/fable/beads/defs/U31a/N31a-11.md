## Background

A symmetric quadruped has four legs but three to five distinct link shapes: left and right legs are mirror
images, front and hind may differ. Optimizing each shape once and reusing it saves most of the cost. The
shortcut that makes this dangerous is to assume that identical limbs carry identical loads and to take the
loads from one of them.

**Counterexample (CX-337).** A payload offset sideways breaks equal loading. Numeric case used by the tests
(`synthetic`, hand-computed): a 15 kg robot stands on four feet at (±0.20, ±0.15) m with a 2 kg payload
0.10 m to the left of the centre line. Total weight 17 × 9.80665 = 166.71 N. Moment balance about the
fore–aft axis gives left-side load minus right-side load = 2 × 9.80665 × 0.10/0.15 = 13.08 N, so each left
foot carries 44.95 N and each right foot 38.41 N: the left legs carry 17% more. A structure sized from the
right leg and copied is under-designed for the left.

ADR-FOUNDRY-04: **symmetric limbs share one optimization only after their load histories are transformed into
one frame and unioned.** Rule 2 of load extraction: each limb's history is transformed into the canonical limb
frame, with forces reflecting as polar vectors and moments as axial ones under a mirror, and the cases are
unioned.

The parity rule is the content. Under an isometry with linear part P (P·Pᵀ = I, det P = ±1), a polar vector v
(force, linear acceleration, position) becomes P·v, and an axial vector u (moment, angular velocity, angular
acceleration) becomes det(P)·P·u. For a rotation the two agree; for a mirror they differ by a sign, and a
moment transformed as if it were polar has the wrong sign. The same parity map T(P) = diag(det(P)·P, P) on
twists preserves the Lie bracket and the power pairing (theorem card T-16), which is why transformed loads are
still loads of a mechanical system.

GATE-FOUNDRY-02 includes "the equal-limb mutation refused".

## Scope

In: link classes from the mechanical symmetry group; the transform of a `LinkLoad` under a proper or improper
isometry; the union of sample streams for envelope selection; per-instance spectra and the class rule for
fatigue; coverage checking; the re-run of the free-body check in the canonical frame.
Out: computing the symmetry group (the algebra crate); the optimization of a class as one problem with a
quotient chart (N31a-15); envelope selection itself (N31a-10).

## Technical approach

1. **Link classes** (`loads/symmetry.rs`, `topo/classes.rs`). Two links are in one class when an element g of
   the mechanical symmetry group maps one to the other, including geometry, material, fill and port layout. The
   class picks a canonical instance (the first in the design's body order) and stores, for every instance i,
   the isometry (P_i, d_i) with x_canonical = P_i·x_i + d_i in link frames. A design with no declared symmetry
   has singleton classes and this bead is the identity.
2. **Transform of a sample** from instance i into the canonical frame:
   - port positions: r' = P·r + d (ports are matched by the class's port correspondence);
   - forces f' = P·f; moments m' = det(P)·P·m, taken about the transformed port origins;
   - body field: a₀' = P·a₀ + (terms from d: a₀ is the acceleration at the link-frame origin, so
     a₀' = P·(a₀ + α × o + ω × (ω × o)) with o the instance-frame point that maps to the canonical origin);
     α' = det(P)·P·α; ω' = det(P)·P·ω. The product coordinates transform as Q' = P·Q·Pᵀ with Q = ω·ωᵀ, in
     which the determinant cancels;
   - the address, tick and instance identity are kept: the transformed sample is still one real instant of one
     real limb.
3. **Check of the transform.** The free-body check is re-run on every transformed sample with the canonical
   instance's mass properties. Because rigid-body equilibrium is invariant under isometries when vectors are
   transformed by their correct parity, the residual must stay within tolerance; a parity error shows up as a
   residual of order one in the moment equation and is refused as FROB-REF-167.
4. **Union for envelope cases.** The class stream is the merge of all instances' transformed streams in a
   fixed order (instance order, then duty order). Envelope selection runs on the union; each case records the
   instance it came from. The scale vector is the maximum over the union.
5. **Spectra per instance; the class takes the worst.** Gauge histories are computed per instance in the
   canonical frame and are not concatenated: joining the histories of two limbs would create cycles between
   their stress levels that no limb experienced, and averaging would hide the more heavily loaded limb. The
   class's fatigue utilization at a gauge is the maximum over instances, and the governing instance is named
   (DESIGN).
6. **Coverage.** An envelope set or a spectrum set records `instances_covered`. Every consumer (sizing,
   optimization, check, packet) requires it to equal the class membership computed from the design. A set
   built from a strict subset of the instances is refused with FROB-REF-169 (telemetry without the samples a
   load metric needs), naming the missing limbs; the repair is to read those limbs' traces. This is how the
   equal-limb mutation is refused.
7. **Material orientation.** A mirrored link made of an anisotropic material (a printed part with a build
   direction) needs its material frame mirrored too. The class record states the material frame per instance;
   classes whose instances would need different orientations of an anisotropic card are split.
8. **Output.** `foundry.loads` reports classes, members, isometries with their determinants, per-instance peak
   statistics, and for each class the ratio of the largest to the smallest instance peak per coordinate, which
   makes unequal loading visible.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the offset-payload stance fixture the per-foot loads are 44.95 N (left) and 38.41 N (right); the class envelope's governing vertical-force case comes from a left leg.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-02: the equal-limb mutation (class loads taken from the canonical instance only) is refused with FROB-REF-169 naming the three missing limbs.
- [ ] A mirrored sample transformed with moments treated as polar fails the canonical-frame free-body check and is refused (FROB-REF-167).
- [ ] For a pure rotation (a radial constructor) polar and axial rules coincide and the transformed residual equals the original to rounding.
- [ ] Class fatigue utilization equals the maximum over instances; concatenating instance histories (planted defect) gives a different, larger count and is caught.
- [ ] A design without symmetry yields singleton classes and byte-identical results with and without this stage.

### Known answers and negative controls
The stance arithmetic above; the parity vectors. CX-337 (symmetric limbs carry equal loads) is the shortcut
under test. Negative controls: the three planted defects. Metamorphic: relabelling which instance is canonical
changes the frame of every result by the corresponding isometry and changes no scalar (projections,
utilizations). Determinism: the merge order is fixed, so the union stream and its envelope are identical
across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Geometric symmetry of the limbs does not imply symmetric use: gaits, payloads and task direction all break
  it. The union is over what happened, so nothing has to be assumed.
- Front and hind legs of the same shape are one class even though their duty differs; the per-instance
  statistics show whether one geometry for both is costing mass, which is information for the designer.
- If the symmetry group is declared but an edit has broken it on one side (a sensor mount on one leg), the
  class splits; the report says why.
