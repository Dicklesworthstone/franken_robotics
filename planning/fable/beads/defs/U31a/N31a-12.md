## Background

Two ways in which simulated loads can be wrong for a structure are specific to impacts.

**Rule 3: impact loads carry a model-form label.** Peak contact forces depend on contact stiffness, which in a
compliant contact model is a modelling parameter, not a measured property of the robot. A peak that doubles
when the stiffness doubles is a statement about the model. The campaign is therefore re-extracted with
stiffness halved and doubled and under the alternative contact law. The envelope over variants is the load
set, and a peak that moves by more than 20% (TARGET) marks the case `model-conditional`, a label the
structural margin inherits. The rejected alternative, trusting the nominal stiffness, would let a number that
nobody measured decide a wall thickness without saying so.

**Rule 4: quasi-static superposition needs separation of frequencies.** The unit-load method computes stress as
if each instant's load were applied slowly. That is valid only when the structure's own dynamics are much
faster than the load. It is used only when the link's first natural frequency f₁ is at least five times the
highest frequency f carrying 99% of load power (TARGET); the dynamic amplification of a single mode,
1/(1 − (f/f₁)²), is then at most 1/(1 − 1/25) = 25/24 ≈ 1.042 (known answer KA-754). Otherwise impact windows
go through the transient path of the solid-mechanics sibling (sparse average-acceleration dynamics with an
energy check), and if that path is not enabled the request is refused (FROB-REF-262: quasi-static
superposition where the frequency separation fails; repair: enable the transient path for impact windows).

## Scope

In: the variant campaigns and their identities; the envelope over variants; the 20% test and the label; the

stresses replace the quasi-static ones; FROB-REF-262.
Out: the nominal campaign and its reader (N31a-06); envelope selection (N31a-10); the modal solve (N31a-20);

## Technical approach

1. **Variants** (`loads/variants.rs`). Four campaign runs on the same specification and addresses:
   `nominal`, `k×0.5`, `k×2`, `alt-law`. Each is a distinct `ModelId` (the contact parameters are part of it)
   and a distinct run of the same campaign identity. All four run at every iteration of the structure loop, so
   the loads–mass map is the same function each time (DESIGN). The option `--variants nominal-only` exists for
   exploration; every case then carries `model-form: unchecked` and the result is admitted as exploratory only.
2. **Envelope over variants.** For each direction d, p_v = max over variant v's samples of d·z. The case is the
   arg-max over all four variants; it records its variant. Movement m_d = (max_v p_v − min_v p_v)/p_nominal.
   If m_d > 0.20 the case gets `StructLabel::ModelConditional` with the four values attached. The label
   propagates to every stress, utilization and margin computed from the case, to the fed-back mass, and to the
   packet's structural evidence.
3. **Spectra under variants.** Gauge histories are computed per variant. For fatigue the reported damage is the
   largest over variants; with S-N exponent m the equivalent stress ratio is (D_max/D_min)^(1/m), and a ratio
   above 1.20 marks the gauge's utilization `model-conditional` (DESIGN: the same 20% criterion, expressed in
   stress).
4. **Load-power spectrum** (`loads/separation.rs`). Per link class and load coordinate, per episode: remove
   the mean (DESIGN: the static part needs no dynamic argument and would hide the impact band), compute a
   Welch periodogram with a Hann window of 1 s and half overlap, sum across episodes, and take f₉₉ as the
   frequency below which 99% of the fluctuating power lies. The class's f₉₉ is the largest over coordinates
   whose fluctuating power is at least 1% of the class total (so a silent coordinate cannot set it). If
   f₉₉ > 0.4 × control rate the telemetry cannot resolve the band: the class is `under-sampled`, treated as
   failing separation, and the repair names a higher trace rate.
5. **Separation test.** Pass when f₁ ≥ 5·f₉₉. The result reports f₁, f₉₉, the ratio, and the bound
   1/(1 − (f₉₉/f₁)²). f₁ comes from the candidate geometry of the current iteration (beam formula with end
   masses on the beam rung; the modal check on an extracted geometry), so the test is repeated whenever the
   geometry changes: a lightened link is also a softer one.
6. **Impact windows.** When the test fails: an impact window opens at each T1 contact-made event on the link
   or its distal chain and at each push event, and closes after max(50 ms, 10/f₁) (DESIGN). Overlapping
   windows merge.
7. **Transient path.** For each window one sibling job: the body-fitted mesh with consistent mass, the port
   tractions and body field of each tick applied as piecewise-constant loads (their telemetry kind is interval
   mean), time step at most 1/(20·f₁) with sub-steps inside a tick, initial state from the quasi-static
   solution at the window's first tick, the sibling's fatigue observers on the inertial trajectory. Outputs:
   gauge stress histories for the window, which replace the quasi-static values of those ticks in the
   spectrum, the sibling's energy-check residual, and the instant of peak stress per gauge, which joins the
   check's case list tagged `transient`. Outside windows the quasi-static values stand.
8. **Refusal.** Separation fails and the transient path is not enabled (flag `--transient impact-windows`
   absent, or the airlock build lacks it) → FROB-REF-262. Payload: class, f₁, f₉₉, ratio, required ratio 5,
   the amplification the quasi-static result could be hiding at that ratio, the number of impact windows and
   the estimated cost of the transient jobs. Exit code 3; `retry: after-change`.
9. **Class.** Estimated. Quasi-static and transient results are both labelled with the path that produced them.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-754: at f₁ = 5·f the reported amplification bound is 25/24 = 1.0417 (printed 1.042).
- [ ] On a `synthetic` campaign whose foot-strike peak is 100 N nominal, 80 N at half stiffness and 130 N at double stiffness, the vertical-force case is taken from the `k×2` variant, movement is 0.50 and the case is `model-conditional`; with peaks 100, 95, 108 the movement is 0.13 and it is not.
- [ ] The label reaches the structural margin and the packet evidence of a link sized from that case.
- [ ] A link with f₁ = 400 Hz under loads with f₉₉ = 60 Hz passes (ratio 6.67); the same link lightened to f₁ = 250 Hz fails (ratio 4.17) and, without the transient flag, returns FROB-REF-262 with the amplification 1/(1 − 0.24²) = 1.061.
- [ ] With the transient flag the failing class completes, its spectrum contains transient values inside impact windows and quasi-static values outside, and the energy-check residual is reported.
- [ ] A single-degree-of-freedom check: a half-sine force pulse of duration equal to the natural period gives a transient peak response above the quasi-static one by the textbook shock-spectrum factor (about 1.7 for an undamped oscillator), and the quasi-static path under-predicts by that factor.
- [ ] f₉₉ above 0.4 × control rate yields `under-sampled`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry loads::variants loads::separation`:
- `amplification_bound_25_over_24`.
- `movement_and_label` (the two triples above); `argmax_across_variants_records_variant`.
- `label_propagates_to_margin`.
- `f99_of_two_tone_signal`: a mean-removed signal with 99.5% of power at 10 Hz and 0.5% at 200 Hz has f₉₉ in the 10 Hz band; with 95% and 5% it is in the 200 Hz band.
- `silent_coordinate_cannot_set_f99`.
- `separation_pass_and_fail` (400/60 and 250/60).
- `refusal_262_payload`.
- `impact_windows_merge`.
- `transient_replaces_only_window_ticks`.
- `sdof_half_sine_shock` (feature `structure`): quasi-static against transient peak.
- `under_sampled_detected`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `nominal-only-silently` (skips variants without the label), `include-mean-in-power` (f₉₉ collapses to the lowest bin and a failing class passes), `separation-from-stale-geometry` (uses f₁ of the previous iteration).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_loads.sh` (N31a-53): runs the four variants on the drop task of the reference quadruped;
asserts that at least the foot class reports movement and a decision on the label; runs a deliberately soft
link fixture without and with `--transient impact-windows` and asserts FROB-REF-262 and then completion; logs
per class f₁, f₉₉, ratio, bound, windows, transient cost and the energy residual.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-754 (bound 1.042 at a ratio of five). The shock-spectrum factor for a half-sine pulse of duration equal to
the period is a textbook value (FACT to recheck against a derivation filed under `docs/derivations/foundry/`
before the test is frozen); the test accepts a ratio between 1.6 and 1.8. Negative controls: the three planted
defects. Metamorphic: scaling all loads by k changes neither f₉₉ nor any movement m_d. Determinism: variant
runs are addressed, so their envelopes are reproducible; the transient job is identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- Four campaigns per iteration quadruple the simulation cost of the loop. It is the price of not letting an
  unmeasured stiffness decide a wall. The cost appears in the estimate before admission.
- Variants bound only the part of the error that moves with contact stiffness and law. Tier bias in peak
  wrenches is handled by referee re-extraction (unit U31b), which is a separate rule.
- The 20% and 5× thresholds are TARGETs; the pilot's stage 2 reports how many cases each marks.
- A link that fails separation after optimization is telling the designer that it rings under the gait; the
  refusal's numbers are design information, not only an obstacle.
