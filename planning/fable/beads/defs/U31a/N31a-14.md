## Background

Topology optimization distributes material inside a region. If the region is wrong, the best structure inside
it is a part that collides with its neighbour at full joint flexion, grows through the motor, or blocks the
cable. The design region is therefore built from what the robot already knows about itself:

**the design region is the keep-in envelope, minus the volumes swept by neighbouring bodies over the joint
ranges (the swept charts of the self-collision and clearance analysis A-13), minus actuator, bearing and cable
keep-outs, with port bosses frozen solid.**

A *port boss* is the solid around a joint interface or contact patch that carries the interface loads; the
optimizer may not remove it, so the part always has its mounting faces. A *keep-out* is a volume the part may
not occupy. The *keep-in envelope* is the outer bound the designer allows.

The construction can fail in two ways, and both are refusals with a repair rather than a silent empty answer.
FROB-REF-164: **an empty design region, or ports with no load path inside the envelope**; the repair offered is
the blocking keep-outs.

The alternative of letting the designer draw the region by hand was rejected as the default because the
swept volumes change with every edit of a joint range, and a hand-drawn region goes stale without anyone
noticing; a hand-drawn keep-in envelope remains an input.

## Scope

In: assembling the region as signed distance functions; clearance margins; frozen bosses; classes with several
instances; the emptiness and connectivity tests; the ranked list of blocking obstacles; the region record.
Out: the swept charts themselves (the analysis crate, seed bead BD-104); the optimization inside the region
(N31a-15); process constraints (N31a-29); the traction pattern on boss faces (N31a-08).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs** (`topo/region.rs`), all in the canonical link frame of the class:
   - `keep_in`: the declared envelope; default (DESIGN) the link's primitive Region offset outward by the
     declared margin, zero if none;
   - `swept[i]`: for each neighbouring body i (parent, children, and any body the self-collision analysis lists
     as able to approach), the swept chart over the declared joint ranges;
   - `keepout[j]`: actuator housings, bearing seats, cable corridors (a tube of the declared diameter along the
     declared route between cable ports), sensor bodies;
   - `clearance`: a distance added to every swept and keep-out volume (DESIGN default 2 mm, declared per
     design);
   - `boss[p]`: the frozen solid of each port.
2. **Assembly.** With signed distance functions negative inside:
   - obstacle = min over i, j of (swept[i] − clearance, keepout[j] − clearance);
   - design = max(keep_in, −obstacle), which is the keep-in with the obstacles removed;
   - frozen = min over p of boss[p];
   - admissible = min(design, frozen): the region where material may exist; bosses win over obstacles only
     where the two were declared to overlap, and any such overlap is reported as a design lint, since a boss
     inside a neighbour's sweep is an interference.
   The functions are sampled on the optimizer's background grid with the sweep's own error bound added to the
   clearance, so the sampled region is inside the true one.
3. **Classes with several instances.** Obstacles of every instance are mapped into the canonical frame by the
   instance isometry and all are applied: the admissible region is the intersection over instances, so one
   geometry fits every limb of the class.
4. **Emptiness test.** Volume of (design minus frozen) by the outward-rounded volume enclosure of the geometry
   sibling. If its upper bound is below a floor (DESIGN: the volume of a strut of the minimum length scale
   between the two nearest bosses) the region is empty → FROB-REF-164.
5. **Load-path test.** Erode the admissible region by half the minimum length scale (a path thinner than the
   smallest feature that can be made is not a path), then label connected components on the grid
   (26-connectivity). All port bosses must lie in one component. Otherwise → FROB-REF-164.
6. **Blocking obstacles (the repair).** For the failing case, test each obstacle in turn: remove it (or its
   clearance only) and repeat step 5. Obstacles whose removal connects the ports are listed first, with the
   smallest uniform shrink of that obstacle that restores a path (found by bisection on an offset), then pairs
   of obstacles up to a budget of 100 tests. Payload: ports disconnected, components found, the ranked list
   `{obstacle, kind, shrink_needed_mm}`, and typed-edit suggestions where one exists (narrow a joint range by
   the angle that frees the path; move a cable port; enlarge the keep-in on a named side). Exit code 3;
   `retry: after-change`.
7. **Region record** (`frob.foundry.region/1`): hash, grid spacing, the four volumes (keep-in, removed by
   sweeps, removed by keep-outs, frozen), the clearance, the list of obstacles with identities, the component
   count, and labels. The record is an input identity of the optimization, so an edit that changes any
   obstacle invalidates the optimized part.
8. **Class.** Estimated: swept charts and housings come with estimate authority; the outward rounding is on
   the safe side but is not a certificate of non-interference, which remains the clearance analysis's claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A box keep-in of 100 × 40 × 40 mm with a cylindrical keep-out of radius 10 mm through its 40 mm depth has design volume 1.6000 × 10⁻⁴ − 1.2566 × 10⁻⁵ = 1.4743 × 10⁻⁴ m³ (clearance zero), and the reported enclosure contains that value.
- [ ] A keep-out slab across the whole cross-section between two end bosses gives FROB-REF-164 naming the slab.
- [ ] With a 10 mm channel left open and a minimum length scale of 4 mm the ports are connected; with a 3 mm channel they are not, and the repair states the shrink needed (at least 1 mm).
- [ ] A swept chart from a joint range of ±90° that blocks the path and one of ±60° that does not: the refusal's edit suggestion narrows the range and the suggested angle restores connectivity.
- [ ] For a mirrored class, an obstacle present on only one instance is removed from the shared region.
- [ ] Bosses are present in the region record as frozen volume and never appear as removable.
- [ ] Changing the clearance changes the region hash.

- `region_hash_tracks_inputs`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `ignore-clearance`, `connectivity-without-erosion` (accepts the 3 mm channel), `union-instead-of-intersection-over-instances`.

### End-to-end script and logging
A step of `scripts/e2e/foundry_structure_loop.sh` (N31a-54): `frob foundry optimize` on a fixture whose knee
actuator keep-out severs the shank region; asserts exit 3, `FROB-REF-164`, and that the first repair names the
actuator housing; logs the four volumes, the component count and the obstacle list.

### Known answers and negative controls
Volumes: box 0.1 × 0.04 × 0.04 = 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ m³; cylinder π × 0.01² × 0.04 = 1.2566 × 10⁻⁵ m³. Negative controls:
the three planted defects. Metamorphic: enlarging any obstacle never enlarges the admissible region; moving
link, obstacles and bosses by one rigid motion changes no volume and no connectivity. Determinism: the region
record is bitwise identical twice on one machine; grid labelling uses a fixed scan order.

## Considerations

- The swept charts cover the declared joint ranges, not the ranges the controller uses. A design with generous
  declared ranges pays for them in structure; the region record lists volume removed per neighbour so the cost
  of each range is visible.
- Cable corridors are straight or piecewise-straight tubes here; cable sag and whip are `no-claim` in this
  chapter.
- Grid sampling loses features smaller than the spacing; the spacing is tied to the minimum length scale
  (DESIGN: at most one third of it).
- The region is one of the things an agent will be tempted to enlarge to get a lighter part. Because it is
  built from other analyses' outputs and hashed, enlarging it means editing the design, which invalidates what
  depended on the old one.
