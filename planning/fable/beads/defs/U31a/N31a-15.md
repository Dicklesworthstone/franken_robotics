## Background

The topology rung is the second of Foundry's two sizing rungs (ADR-FOUNDRY-02): closed-form beam sizing by
default and as fallback, three-dimensional topology optimization for finalists only. It asks: inside the
design region of a link, what is the lightest distribution of material that survives the loads the robot met?

The mathematics is construction C-35, part 1. Densities ρ_e ∈ [ρ_min, 1] on a mesh give stiffness
K(ρ) = Σ ρ_eᵖ·K_e with penalty p = 3 (DESIGN); the classical problem minimizes compliance c = FᵀU subject to
K(ρ)·U = F and Σ ρ_e·v_e ≤ V*. The sensitivity is ∂c/∂ρ_e = −p·ρ_eᵖ⁻¹·u_eᵀ·K_e·u_e, with no extra solve
because the problem is self-adjoint, and a density filter fixes the length scale. The load cases are the
envelope of interface wrenches recorded over qualified missions, not an assumed static load.

Foundry's formulation is the engineering form of that problem: **minimize projected volume subject to a stress
cap S_y/SF on the relaxed measure, a stiffness cap per envelope case from the control budget, a first-mode
floor above actuator bandwidth, a minimum length scale, and the process constraints.** Where a sibling driver
takes compliance at fixed volume, an outer bisection on volume fraction finds the lightest feasible design.
Mirror-image links are one problem.

What it is worth is stated before it runs: reported savings against engineered parts are 7 to 30% of link
mass, about 2 to 8% of robot mass on the one quadruped with a published breakdown. It improves the built
object and shortens no decision. The bet (BET-29) fails if the optimized part is less than 7% lighter than the
engineered part at equal stiffness and fatigue margin.

The optimizer's own stress number is not evidence: the sibling's documentation says cut volumes, discrete
sensitivities and the stress aggregate are not continuum certificates and the aggregate is not a bound on the
maximum. ADR-FOUNDRY-05 follows: the optimizer never checks its own result.

## Scope

In: the problem statement handed to the sibling optimizers; the two drivers (direct least-volume; fixed-volume
compliance with bisection); constraint construction from envelope cases, budgets and cards; coarse and
finalist resolutions; one problem per link class; the result record; budgets and their measurement.
Out: the region (N31a-14); process constraints inside the optimization (N31a-29); plate parts (N31a-16);
surface extraction and meshing (N31a-17); the independent check (N31a-19 to N31a-21); the loop (N31a-05).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs per link class** (`topo/formulate.rs`): the region record; the envelope set over all instances
   (each case a real instant, converted to port tractions and a body load); the material card (E, ν, ρ, S_y)
   and the safety factor SF; the deflection budget; the first-mode floor; the minimum length scale; the
   process declaration.
2. **Constraints.**
   - *Stress*: relaxed stress measure ≤ S_y/SF in every case (the sibling's relaxed measure and sampled peak
     bound; ε-relaxation so that void elements do not carry a stress constraint).
   - *Stiffness*: for each case, compliance Fᵀu ≤ ‖F‖·δ_max, where δ_max is the allowed deflection of the
     distal port relative to the proximal one. δ_max comes from the design's control budget for the class; the
     default when none is declared is 1/500 of the port-to-port distance (DESIGN, printed as an assumption).
   - *First mode*: f₁ ≥ f_floor, with end masses equal to the reflected inertia of the neighbours; f_floor is
     the larger of twice the actuator bandwidth from analysis A-12 (DESIGN margin) and five times the load
     band f₉₉ of the frequency-separation rule, so that an optimized link does not fall out of the
     quasi-static regime it was sized in.
   - *Length scale*: the robust erode/dilate formulation with the declared minimum feature size.
   - *Process*: as supplied by N31a-29 (envelope, symmetry, overhang filter for additive parts).
3. **Driver A, direct least volume** (preferred): the sibling's three-dimensional cut-cell lane (minimum volume
   under a stress cap, with modal design and material body loads), reached behind its feature through the
   airlock at the pinned commit (interim posture of upstream item FD-FSIM-28). One job per class.
4. **Driver B, fixed volume with bisection** (fallback, and the cross-check): the sibling's multi-load
   compliance driver at volume fraction v, then evaluation of all constraints on the result. Bisect v on
   [v_lo, 1] (v_lo = frozen volume ÷ region volume) until the bracket is at most 1% of region volume: at most
   ⌈log₂(1/0.01)⌉ = 7 solves. Feasibility is assumed monotone in v (HYPOTHESIS, since the problem is not
   convex); every bisection step records its feasibility, and a feasible point below an infeasible one sets
   the flag `non-monotone` and widens the search by one extra solve on each side.
5. **Continuation and solver settings** are those of the sibling pipeline (penalty continuation to p = 3,
   projection sharpening by doubling), fixed in the job description so that the job hash determines the
   result. The sibling's finite-difference gradient check runs once per job on a sample of 32 design variables
   and its largest relative error is recorded; above 10⁻⁴ the job fails.
6. **Resolutions and budgets (TARGET, unmeasured).** *Coarse*: grid spacing one third of the minimum length
   scale or the spacing that gives about 10⁵ cells in the region, whichever is larger; at most 120 s per link
   class. *Finalist*: goal-driven octree refinement from the coarse result; at most 10 minutes per class. Both
   on the CPU cores of the M5 Ultra. A symmetric quadruped has three to five classes, solved in parallel.
7. **One problem per class.** Loads of all instances are already in the canonical frame; the class is solved
   once. Where the link itself has a mirror plane and its load set is invariant under it, the sibling's
   quotient chart halves the problem; otherwise the full domain is used and the reason recorded.
8. **Infeasibility.** If the stress cap fails at full volume (v = 1) the class has no feasible design in its
   region: the result is a finding `infeasible-in-envelope` with the governing case and ranked repairs (enlarge
   the keep-in on the side of the governing stress; a stronger material; a lower load from a gait change), and
   the loop uses the beam section for that class.
9. **Result record** (`frob.foundry.topo/1`): job hash, driver, resolution, iterations, volume, mass, the
   bisection table, constraint values per case, the gradient-check error, wall time and core-seconds against
   budget, the density field's hash. The optimizer's stress aggregate is stored under the key
   `optimizer_internal` with the note "not a bound; not evidence" and is never printed as a utilization.
10. **Class.** Estimated. Determinism: the same bits twice on one machine; no claim across machines.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the two-bar fixture (load P at distance L from a wall, domain height 2L) both drivers return two bars at 45° ± 5° with compliance within 10% of 4·P²·L²/(E·V) at the achieved volume V (measured by bead N31a-18).
- [ ] Driver B's bisection ends within 7 solves with a bracket of at most 1% of region volume, and its final design is feasible in every constraint.
- [ ] Drivers A and B agree on mass within 5% on the tube-replacement fixture (a straight two-port link under bending and torsion); a larger difference is reported, not hidden.
- [ ] The coarse solve of each link class of the reference quadruped completes within 120 s and the finalist solve within 10 minutes; measured times are recorded per class (the budget clause of GATE-FOUNDRY-05).
- [ ] A class whose stress cap fails at full volume returns `infeasible-in-envelope` with the governing case, and the loop falls back to the beam section.
- [ ] The gradient check passes at 10⁻⁴; a planted sign error in the body-load sensitivity fails it.
- [ ] No output field presents the optimizer's stress aggregate as a utilization or a margin.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry topo::formulate topo::bisect` (feature `structure`):
- `compliance_sensitivity_formula`: one element, ρ = 0.5, p = 3, u_eᵀ·K_e·u_e = 8 J: ∂c/∂ρ = −3 × 0.25 × 8 = −6 J.
- `stiffness_cap_from_budget`: port distance 0.25 m, default δ_max = 0.5 mm; a case with ‖F‖ = 300 N caps compliance at 0.15 J.
- `mode_floor_is_max_of_two_rules`: bandwidth 40 Hz and f₉₉ = 25 Hz give max(80, 125) = 125 Hz.
- `bisection_seven_steps`: on a `synthetic` monotone feasibility oracle with threshold 0.37 the bracket after 7 steps has width below 0.01 and contains 0.37.
- `non_monotone_flagged` (oracle feasible at 0.30, infeasible at 0.35, feasible above 0.40).
- `infeasible_at_full_volume_reports_finding`.
- `job_hash_determines_result`: the same job twice gives the same density hash.
- `quotient_only_when_loads_symmetric`.
- `optimizer_stress_not_exposed_as_utilization` (schema test).
- Planted defects, each caught: `body-load-sensitivity-sign` (gradient check), `assembled-peak-case-as-input` (refused by the envelope reader before the job), `bisection-on-mass-not-feasibility` (returns an infeasible design; caught by the final feasibility assertion).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_structure_loop.sh` (N31a-54): `frob foundry optimize <finalist> --rung topology --json`
with `sync_wait_ms` shorter than the job, asserting exit 8 and then completion by `job.wait`; logs per class
driver, resolution, iterations, volume, mass against the beam baseline, time against 120 s and 600 s, the
bisection table and the gradient-check error.

### Known answers and negative controls
Two-bar optimum (KA-739; C-35's KA-168: at fixed stress the volume of a symmetric two-bar truss is
proportional to 1/(sin θ·cos θ), least at 45°): bars at 45°; compliance 4·P²·L²/(E·V); minimum volume
2·P·L/σ_allow; control: swapping the support and the load port is caught. Negative controls: the three
planted defects. Metamorphic: scaling all loads by k leaves the optimal layout unchanged for the compliance
driver and scales compliance by k²; mirroring region and loads mirrors the result. Determinism: twice on one
machine, and across airlock modes.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOPOPT (M5): trigger GATE-FOUNDRY-05 fails (saving under 7%, no convergence, or over budget). Fallback:
  beam-rung sizing supplies mass and inertia feedback; three-dimensional optimization becomes an offline option
  or is dropped; analysis A-15 is unaffected.
- The three-dimensional lane is feature-gated and tagged Frontier upstream. A move of the pin can change
  results; the job hash includes the stack-lock digest, and the sibling fact probes of N31a-04 run first.
- Budgets are unmeasured TARGETs. Three-dimensional optimization of 12 to 20 links is CPU-hours in the
  literature; the 120 s coarse budget is what makes the loop's five iterations affordable, and missing it is
  one of the three kill triggers.
- Stress-constrained topology optimization is sensitive to the aggregate's parameters; the independent check
  decides, and a failure there returns a tighter cap (FROB-REF-261), which is the designed feedback path.
