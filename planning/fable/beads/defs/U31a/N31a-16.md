## Background

Many robot parts are plates: brackets, side plates of a leg, sheet-metal frames. For a part of constant
thickness cut from stock, the design freedom is its outline and its holes, which is a two-dimensional shape
problem. The sibling level-set optimizer does exactly that: a bilinear grid level set with exact enclosures,
high-order advection, redistancing, velocity extension, topological derivatives for nucleating holes,
compliance descent with volume control and checkpoints, multi-load robust selection, sampled stress admission
and hard-area projection. It is cheaper than a three-dimensional solve and gives crisp boundaries.

The rule of the topology rung: **plate parts use the two-dimensional level-set optimizer for shape and are
checked in three dimensions.**

The reason for the second half is a fact about the sibling: it is two-dimensional only, and its sampled stress
is plane strain, while a thin plate loaded in its plane is a plane-stress problem. Under plane strain the
effective modulus is E/(1 − ν²); for ν = 0.33 that is 1.122·E, so plane-strain compliance is about 11% too low
and plane-strain stresses carry an out-of-plane component a thin plate does not have. A plane-stress option is
upstream work item FD-FSIM-27. Interim posture: no stress or compliance number from the two-dimensional solver
is reported as evidence; the shape is extruded and every number comes from the independent three-dimensional
check. Switch-over rule: when the option lands, add the plane-stress known answer and allow the sibling's
stress admission to steer the shape, still followed by the three-dimensional check.

## Scope

In: deciding that a part is a plate problem; reduction of envelope cases to in-plane load cases; the
two-dimensional job; thickness selection from stock; extrusion to a solid; hand-off to the check; the refusal
to treat a part with significant out-of-plane loading as a plate.
Out: the level-set mathematics (the sibling); bending of sheet parts into three-dimensional shapes (not in
this chapter); the check itself (N31a-19); process limits for sheet parts (N31a-26).

## Technical approach

1. **When a part is a plate problem** (`topo/plate.rs`). The link or bracket declares process `sheet`, or its
   keep-in is a slab (constant thickness t_max between two parallel planes) and the designer asks for
   `--plate`. The mid-plane and its normal n define the two-dimensional domain: the projection of the
   admissible region onto the mid-plane, with through-holes where any keep-out crosses the slab and frozen
   areas at the projected bosses.
2. **In-plane reduction of loads.** For each envelope case, port forces and moments are split into in-plane
   parts (force components in the plane, moment about n) and out-of-plane parts (force along n, moments about
   in-plane axes). The body load is split the same way. An estimate of bending stress from the out-of-plane
   part on the solid slab section is compared with the in-plane membrane stress estimate; if out-of-plane
   stress exceeds 10% of the total in any case (DESIGN threshold) the part is not a plate problem: the request
   is redirected to the three-dimensional rung with a finding that says which case and why.
3. **The two-dimensional job.** Fixed-area compliance descent under the in-plane cases with robust multi-load
   selection, hole nucleation by topological derivative, minimum feature size from the process card (for sheet
   cutting: the larger of the stock thickness and the cutter's kerf limit). The area fraction is found by the
   same bisection as the three-dimensional fallback driver: at most 7 solves to a 1% bracket, with feasibility
   judged by the three-dimensional check of the extruded candidate at the coarse resolution.
4. **Thickness.** Stock thicknesses come from the sheet stock card. For each candidate thickness, in
   increasing order, run step 3; the result is the lightest (thickness, area) pair that passes. Mass is
   ρ·t·A. At most three thicknesses are tried (the thinnest that passes the beam-rung estimate and its two
   neighbours; DESIGN), to bound cost.
5. **Extrusion.** The zero contour of the level set is extracted as closed polygons with the exact enclosure
   the sibling provides, simplified under a Hausdorff tolerance of one tenth of the minimum feature size, and
   extruded by t along n. Bosses are re-inserted exactly. The solid goes to surface validation and meshing
   (N31a-17) and then to the independent check (N31a-19), including buckling: a thin plate in compression
   buckles out of plane, which the two-dimensional solve cannot see.
6. **What is recorded.** The two-dimensional job hash, the label `shape: 2D level set (plane strain;
   shape only)`, thickness, area, the out-of-plane share per case, and the three-dimensional check's results
   as the only stress, stiffness and buckling evidence.
7. **Class.** Estimated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A flat bracket fixture with two bolt bosses and an in-plane load produces an outline with at least one nucleated hole, a mass below the solid blank's, and passes the three-dimensional check.
- [ ] No number from the two-dimensional solver appears in the result as stress, utilization or compliance evidence; the schema has no field for one.
- [ ] A case with out-of-plane bending above 10% of total stress redirects the part to the three-dimensional rung with the case named.
- [ ] The plate-with-hole known answer: an extruded plate with a central circular hole (hole diameter one tenth of the width) under uniaxial tension shows a peak stress of 3.0 times the remote stress within 5% after refinement in the three-dimensional check.
- [ ] A thin plate fixture in in-plane compression that passes stress but has a buckling factor below 2 is rejected by the check, and the next stock thickness is tried.
- [ ] The plane-stress switch-over test exists, ignored, tagged FD-FSIM-27.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry topo::plate` (feature `structure`):
- `plane_strain_modulus_ratio`: ν = 0.33 gives 1/(1 − ν²) = 1.1222.
- `in_plane_split`: force (10, 5, 2) N and moment (0.1, 0.2, 0.3) N·m with n = z give in-plane (10, 5, 0) N and 0.3 N·m about n; out-of-plane 2 N and (0.1, 0.2) N·m.
- `out_of_plane_share_redirects`.
- `extrusion_volume`: polygon area 1,200 mm² at t = 3 mm gives 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ m³; with ρ = 2,700 kg/m³ mass 9.72 g.
- `thickness_search_order_and_bound` (at most three thicknesses).
- `kirsch_factor_three` (the plate-with-hole case through the real check).
- `compressed_plate_buckles_and_is_rejected`.
- `no_2d_stress_in_schema`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `report-2d-stress-as-utilization` (schema test), `skip-3d-check-for-plates`, `ignore-out-of-plane-loads`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_plate.sh` (this bead's own, built only on its dependencies; it sources the STD-E2E helper
library). Steps: (1) `cargo test -p frob-foundry --features structure -- topo::plate` through `e2e_cmd`

two-dimensional stress, utilization or compliance field in the output; (3) the plate-with-hole case: peak

compression: rejected for a buckling factor below 2 and the next stock thickness tried; (5) a case with

is called, so the evidence block is recorded as absent with reason `no-verb`. Logs to

script as a stage: `frob foundry optimize --fixture bracket-plate --plate --exploratory --key "$(e2e_key
bracket-plate)" --json`, asserting the label, the stock thickness, the three-dimensional check results and
class Estimated.

### Known answers and negative controls
The stress concentration at a small circular hole in a wide plate under uniaxial tension is 3 (a classical
elasticity result; registered in the chapter's known-answer registry as an additional case with its
derivation filed under `docs/derivations/foundry/`). The modulus ratio 1.1222. Negative controls: the three
planted defects. Metamorphic: mirroring the plate and its loads mirrors the outline; doubling all loads at
fixed thickness cannot reduce the area. Determinism: twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- Shape from plane-strain compliance and shape from plane-stress compliance are expected to be close for one
  isotropic material (HYPOTHESIS: the difference is a uniform stiffness factor when Poisson's ratio does not
  vary; hole placement can still differ slightly). The three-dimensional check, not this expectation, carries
  the evidence.
- The sibling's stress admission is unused in the interim, so stress feasibility is found by the outer
  bisection, which costs three-dimensional checks. That cost is why at most three thicknesses are tried.
- Bent sheet-metal parts are outside this bead; a designer models each flat panel as its own plate part.
