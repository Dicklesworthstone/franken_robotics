## Background

An optimizer that removes material makes parts slender, and slender parts fail in two ways that a stress check
does not see. They buckle: a thin strut or wall in compression loses stiffness and collapses at a stress far
below yield. And they ring: a link whose first natural frequency falls toward the actuator's bandwidth or the
gait's load band stops behaving as a rigid body, the controller excites it, and the quasi-static load method
used to size it stops being valid.

The independent check therefore has, after stress:

- **Buckling.** The first positive load factor from the sibling's stability analysis is at least 2 (TARGET) in
  every envelope case. The load factor λ is the multiple of the case's loads at which the linearized
  structure first loses stability: (K + λ·K_g)·φ = 0, with K the elastic stiffness and K_g the geometric
  stiffness of the case's stress state. Only positive factors count: each case is a real instant with real
  signs, and a negative factor describes buckling under the reversed load, which did not occur in that case
  (if it occurs in the campaign, another case has it).
- **Modes.** The first frequency clears its floor.

The margin of 2 on buckling also protects the superposition used for stress and fatigue, which assumes
stiffness does not depend on load. Both checks run on the same body-fitted mesh as the stress check, never on
the optimizer's grid (ADR-FOUNDRY-05). Class Estimated.

## Scope

In: the buckling eigenproblem per case and its acceptance rule; the modal model (boundary condition and end
masses) and its acceptance rule; the floor; what the results feed; the result records.
Out: the stress solves they reuse (N31a-19); the beam-rung closed forms for Euler buckling, wall buckling and
the first bending frequency (BD-401); the frequency-separation rule that consumes f₁ (N31a-12).

## Technical approach

1. **Buckling** (`check/buckling.rs`). For each envelope case (and each transient peak case): take the stress
   field of the stress check at the converged mesh level, form the geometric stiffness, and ask the sibling's
   stability analysis for the smallest positive eigenvalue λ₁ and its mode (it provides buckling pencils with
   eigenvalue derivatives). Acceptance: λ₁ ≥ 2 in every case. A case with no positive eigenvalue below a
   search bound of 100 (DESIGN) is reported `> 100`. The governing case, λ₁, the mode's location (centroid of
   the region with the largest modal displacement) and whether the mode is global (member-level) or local
   (wall-level, judged by the mode's wavelength against the member length) are recorded.
2. **Failure.** λ₁ < 2: for an optimized part this is the same refusal as a stress failure, FROB-REF-261, with
   repairs ranked: (1) increase the minimum length scale by one stock step (thicker members); (2) re-run with
   the sibling's buckling-constrained allocation enabled; (3) for a local wall mode, add a wall-thickness floor
   at the mode's location. For other geometry: a finding, exit 2.
3. **Modes** (`check/modes.rs`). Model: the body-fitted mesh with consistent mass; the proximal boss's
   interface surface held; at each other port a lumped mass and rotational inertia equal to the reflected
   inertia of what hangs from it at the nominal stance pose (DESIGN: this is the configuration in which the
   link acts as a cantilever carrying the distal chain, and it matches the beam rung's formula with end
   masses). Compute the first six modes; f₁ is the lowest. Acceptance: f₁ ≥ f_floor.
4. **The floor.** f_floor = max(2 × actuator bandwidth of the joints at the link's ports, 5 × f₉₉), where the
   bandwidth comes from the modes analysis A-12 and the actuator card, the factor 2 is a DESIGN margin, and
   f₉₉ is the highest frequency carrying 99% of load power for the class. The second term is exactly the
   frequency-separation requirement, so a link that passes the modal check may be loaded quasi-statically.
   Both terms and which one governs are printed.
5. **Failure.** f₁ < f_floor: FROB-REF-261 for an optimized part with repairs: (1) re-run with the first-mode
   constraint tightened to the floor times 1.05; (2) stiffer material; (3) accept and enable the transient
   path if only the 5·f₉₉ term fails (then the label `quasi-static: not separated` is attached and the
   transient rule takes over). A finding for other geometry.
6. **What the results feed.** f₁ goes to the separation test of the current iteration. The mode shapes'
   strain-energy density marks where stiffness is missing and is stored as a field for the viewer. The first
   mode's frequency and λ₁ go into the packet's `structure/` record.
7. **Records** (`frob.foundry.check.buckling/1`, `frob.foundry.check.modes/1`): mesh hash, per case λ₁ and mode
   class, f₁ to f₆, end masses used, the floor and its two terms, labels inherited from governing cases, cost.
8. **Cost and determinism.** A few eigenpairs per case on the factorization already computed; the eigen-solver
   runs with a fixed start vector and iteration cap so that the same job gives the same bits on one machine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Euler column: a pinned–pinned tube of mean radius 10 mm, wall 1 mm, length 0.5 m, E = 69 GPa has P_cr = π²·E·I/L² = 8,558 N with I = π·r³·t = 3.1416 × 10⁻⁹ m⁴; the check returns λ₁ within 3% of 8,558/P for P = 4,000 N (2.14, pass) and P = 5,000 N (1.71, fail).
- [ ] A case in pure tension reports no positive factor below the search bound.
- [ ] Cantilever mode: the 0.20 m × 20 × 10 mm aluminium beam (ρ = 2,700 kg/m³) clamped at one end has f₁ = 204 Hz within 3% (bending about the weak axis); with a 0.5 kg tip mass, 45.6 Hz within 3%.
- [ ] The floor is the larger of its two terms; for bandwidth 40 Hz and f₉₉ = 25 Hz it is 125 Hz, and the report names the second term as governing.
- [ ] An optimized part with λ₁ = 1.6 returns FROB-REF-261 with the buckling repairs; one with f₁ below the floor returns it with the modal repairs.
- [ ] Buckling uses the stress field of the converged mesh level, and both records carry that mesh hash.
- [ ] A local wall mode and a global strut mode are distinguished on two fixtures built for the purpose.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry check::buckling check::modes` (feature `structure`):
- `euler_tube_pinned`: I = 3.1416 × 10⁻⁹ m⁴; P_cr = 8,558 N; λ₁ = 2.14 at 4,000 N and 1.71 at 5,000 N.
- `tension_has_no_positive_factor`.
- `negative_factor_ignored`: a case whose reversed load would buckle at |λ| = 1.2 passes when the positive factor is 3.
- `cantilever_first_mode`: E·I = 115.0 N·m², ρ·A = 0.54 kg/m: f₁ = (1.875²/(2π))·√(E·I/(ρ·A·L⁴)) = 204.2 Hz.
- `cantilever_with_tip_mass`: effective beam mass 0.2427 × 0.108 kg: 45.6 Hz.
- `floor_is_max_of_two_terms`.
- `optimized_buckling_failure_refuses_261`; `optimized_modal_failure_refuses_261`.
- `local_versus_global_mode_classification`.
- `end_masses_from_reflected_inertia`.
- `eigen_solver_is_reproducible`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `accept-smallest-absolute-eigenvalue` (takes a negative factor), `free-free-modes` (omits the proximal restraint and reports a rigid-body mode or a far higher f₁), `geometric-stiffness-from-coarse-level`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_check.sh` (N31a-55): `frob foundry check --fixture euler-tube --load 4000 --json` and
`--load 5000`; `--fixture cantilever --modes`. Logged: λ₁ against 2, P_cr, f₁ to f₃, the floor with both
terms, the governing term, mesh hash.

### Known answers and negative controls
Euler load 8,558 N; first cantilever mode 204.2 Hz; 45.6 Hz with the tip mass (hand derivations filed under
`docs/derivations/foundry/`; registered as additional known answers of the chapter). Negative controls: the
three planted defects. Metamorphic: scaling the loads by k scales λ₁ by 1/k; scaling the density by k scales
every frequency by 1/√k; scaling E by k scales λ₁ by k and frequencies by √k. Determinism: twice on one
machine.

## Considerations

- Linearized buckling over-predicts the capacity of imperfection-sensitive shells; the factor of 2 is a
  margin, not a proof of stability, and printed lattice-like members deserve more. The class stays Estimated.
- The modal boundary condition is a modelling choice. A link in a closed chain or in double support is
  stiffer than a cantilever; the cantilever is the conservative choice for the first mode.
- If the 5·f₉₉ term governs and fails after optimization, the optimizer has traded stiffness for mass past the
  point where the quasi-static loads that sized it are valid; this is the designed interaction with the
  frequency-separation rule.
- The stability analysis and its eigen-solver are sibling code reached as whole jobs; nothing here calls it
  per element.
