## Background

Lighter links mean lower joint loads, which may allow smaller actuators, which are lighter still. This is the
mass spiral read backwards: with actuator mass proportional to required torque and required torque
proportional to total mass, total mass is m = m_f/(1 − κ), where m_f is the mass that does not scale and κ the
loop gain (κ = n·c·g·L/ρ_τ for n actuators, torque coefficient c, leg length L and torque density ρ_τ;
illustrative values give κ = 0.59 and m = 2.43·m_f). Read forwards it promises that a structural saving is
multiplied by 1/(1 − κ), here 2.4.

The promise holds **only if actuators are then re-selected from a discrete catalogue, which is a separate
decision with its own revalidation** (HYPOTHESIS; this bead measures the realized multiplier). A catalogue has
a few sizes. A structural saving that moves the required knee torque from 21.0 to 20.4 N·m changes nothing if
the catalogue steps are at 20 and 30 N·m; one that moves it from 20.5 to 19.9 N·m crosses a step.

ADR-FOUNDRY-07: **actuator cards are held fixed inside the structure loop. Re-selecting an actuator is an
outer step with full revalidation.** Reason: the structure loop's gain is λ and the actuator spiral's is κ; in
one loop the gain is about κ + λ. At κ = 0.59 and λ = 0.15 it is 0.74: five iterations contract the first step
only to 0.74⁴ = 0.30 of itself, the fixed point amplifies a structural change by 3.8 against 2.4 for the
actuator spiral alone, and a discrete catalogue can make the combined loop cycle between sizes. Two geometric
series in one loop converge slowly; nested, the inner one converges in a few passes and the outer one moves in
a few discrete steps. The rejected alternative is the combined loop.

## Scope

In: enforcement that the inner loop never changes an actuator; the outer step (proposal, edit, revalidation,
re-entry into the structure loop); the cycle guard across catalogue sizes; the realized multiplier and its
report; the snap rule shared with the parts list.
Out: the inner loop and its test (N31a-05, N31a-24); actuator envelope analysis itself (the analysis crate);
mission-level co-design synthesis (seed bead BD-360), which may call this step but is not required by it.

## Technical approach

1. **Inner-loop invariant.** Every iteration's design must have the same actuator, transmission and battery
   bindings as iteration 0; the orchestrator checks the binding hash at each feedback edit and the feedback
   stage rejects edit sets containing `swap-actuator`, `regear` or `swap-battery`.
2. **Proposal** (`feedback/outer.rs`), after the inner loop has converged on design d with actuators a:
   for each joint class, evaluate the actuator envelope analysis on d for each catalogue card lighter than
   the current one, under the loads of the converged campaign (peak and continuous torque, speed, voltage,
   thermal steady state). A card is a candidate when every envelope margin is non-negative. The proposal is
   the lightest candidate per joint class; if no joint class has one, the outer step ends with
   `no-step-available` and the realized multiplier is 1.
3. **The step.** Apply `swap-actuator` (and any implied `regear`) under the symmetry constructor: a new design
   d′. Then **full revalidation**, in order, each a hard stop on failure with the proposal withdrawn:
   1. `design.check` on d′ (all eight preflight stages);
   2. controller synthesis for d′;
   3. the structure loop from its start on d′ (new campaign runs on the same specification; the previous
      optimized geometry is the starting candidate, never the answer);
   4. the snap rule: a continuous optimum snapped to a catalogue part, stock size or gear ratio is revalidated
      after the snap, meaning hard constraints and the controller are re-checked on the snapped design;
   5. the result is a new finalist with no qualification verdict; it must be qualified before a packet.
4. **Cycle guard.** The outer loop keeps the set of visited states (actuator binding hash, rounded structural
   mass per class). A revisit, or more than three outer steps (DESIGN cap), stops the loop with the heaviest
   visited state that passed revalidation and the reason `catalogue-cycle`.
5. **Realized multiplier.** With M the total robot mass:
   - structural saving ΔS = (structural mass with beam-rung sections) − (structural mass after the inner
     loop), both at fixed actuators a;
   - total saving ΔM = M(beam sections, a) − M(final design after outer steps);
   - realized multiplier μ = ΔM/ΔS; predicted 1/(1 − κ̂), with κ̂ estimated for the design as (actuator mass
     that scales with load) ÷ (total mass), per the spiral's closed form.
   The report prints ΔS, ΔM, μ, the prediction, the catalogue steps crossed (joint class, from card, to card,
   mass difference) and the ceiling line of the ceiling ledger. μ = 1 with no step crossed is the expected
   common case and is printed as such.
6. **Family pruning is not done here by peaks.** If an outer step is refused by a failed simulation, only that
   candidate is discarded; a whole part family is removed only when an algebraic bound shows the failure holds
   across the family (the rule and its two-finger counterexample are carried by the parts-list bead N31a-39).
7. **Records.** The outer loop record lists proposals, revalidation outcomes with their refusal codes, the
   states visited, μ and its inputs. Class Estimated. The step is run only on request (`foundry.optimize
   --outer actuators`) and is never implied.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An inner-loop edit set containing `swap-actuator` is rejected, and the loop record shows identical binding hashes for all iterations.
- [ ] On a `synthetic` design where the converged loads drop the knee requirement from 20.5 to 19.9 N·m with catalogue sizes of 20 and 30 N·m, the outer step proposes the 20 N·m card, revalidates and reports μ > 1 with the step listed.
- [ ] With the requirement moving from 21.0 to 20.4 N·m no step is available and μ = 1.00 is reported with the reason.
- [ ] A proposal that fails `design.check` (a thermal margin under the lighter card) is withdrawn; the result is the pre-step design.
- [ ] A planted two-size catalogue cycle (the lighter actuator's design needs the heavier, and the reverse) stops with `catalogue-cycle` at the heavier state within three outer steps.
- [ ] The structure loop is re-run from its start after a swap; a run that reuses the previous loop's geometry without re-checking (planted defect) is caught by the stage-record hash mismatch.
- [ ] The report prints ΔS, ΔM, μ and 1/(1 − κ̂), and never prints the prediction as a result.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry feedback::outer` (with `synthetic` envelope and loop stages):
- `inner_loop_rejects_actuator_edits`.
- `step_crossed_and_multiplier`: ΔS = 0.30 kg; twelve actuators each 0.05 kg lighter: ΔM = 0.90 kg; μ = 3.0.
- `no_step_multiplier_one`.
- `prediction_from_kappa`: κ̂ = 0.59 gives 2.44; κ̂ + λ = 0.74 gives 3.85 (arithmetic only).
- `revalidation_order_and_hard_stops` (each of the five stages failing in turn withdraws the proposal).
- `catalogue_cycle_stops_at_heavier`.
- `outer_cap_is_three`.
- `structure_loop_restarts_after_swap`.
- `snap_is_revalidated`: a continuous gear ratio 1.83 snapped to stock 1.8 triggers re-check of hard constraints and controller.
- Planted defects, each caught: `swap-inside-inner-loop`, `skip-revalidation-on-lighter-card`, `report-predicted-multiplier-as-realized`.

### End-to-end script and logging
A step of `scripts/e2e/foundry_structure_loop.sh` (N31a-54): `frob foundry optimize <finalist> --outer
actuators --json` on the two torque fixtures; asserts `result.outer.multiplier` (greater than 1 with a step
listed; exactly 1 with `no-step-available`), and logs ΔS, ΔM, μ, the prediction, steps crossed, outer
iterations and each revalidation stage's outcome and time.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic from the mass-spiral card: κ = 0.59 gives m = 2.43·m_f, so a 6 kg structure is a 14.6 kg robot;
0.74⁴ = 0.30; amplification 3.8 against 2.4. The two torque fixtures are hand-built. Negative controls: the
three planted defects and the catalogue cycle. Metamorphic: adding a catalogue card heavier than the current
one changes nothing; refining the catalogue (more sizes) never lowers μ. Determinism: the outer loop is a
deterministic function of the catalogue order and the inner loop's results.

## Considerations

- The outer step is expensive: each step is a full structure loop plus preflight plus, eventually, a
  qualification run. It is offered because the multiplier is the only way structure could matter beyond its
  2 to 8% ceiling, and measuring it is the test of that hypothesis.
- With today's coarse actuator catalogues the common answer will be μ = 1. That is a finding about catalogues,
  and the report says which torque margin was closest to a step.
- Nothing here changes the decision-time thesis: structure and its spiral improve the built object.
- If mission-level co-design later proposes actuator changes itself, it must call this step's revalidation
  sequence; the sequence is exported as a function for that purpose.
