## Background

Two manufacturability features have no measure in the sibling libraries (FACT: the geometry-constraint sibling
has no corner-radius measure; the fabrication sibling only compares a supplied number with a limit), so
Foundry measures them itself.

**Internal corner radius** (milling). A rotating cutter of radius r_t cannot cut an internal corner sharper
than r_t: it leaves a fillet of its own radius. A part whose internal corners are sharper than the smallest
available cutter cannot be milled as drawn. The plan's measure is the curvature of the distance field; the
limit is the tool radius from the process card.

**Enclosed voids** (all processes). An internal cavity with no opening cannot be milled at all. In a powder
process (`sls`, `slm`) it fills with unfused powder that cannot be removed without a drain hole. The plan's
measure is the connected components of the complement of the part; the limit is none for milling (any void is
a violation) and a drain hole for powder processes.

Both are checked after extraction, not inside the optimization, because neither has a usable derivative.
GATE-FOUNDRY-07 requires planted violations of wall, corner radius, tool access, overhang and undercut each to
be detected with location and repair, and clean parts to pass.

## Scope

In: the two evaluators on a sampled signed distance field; locations and regions of violations; the measured
values handed to the limit check; accuracy statements; a second, independent measure of corner sharpness as a
cross-check.
Out: thickness, draft and envelope (sibling measures through N31a-26); tool access and overhang (N31a-28); the
fixture battery (N31a-30).

## Technical approach

1. **Field.** The part's signed distance field φ (negative inside) is sampled on a regular grid of spacing h,
   with h at most one quarter of the smallest tool radius or minimum feature size on the card (DESIGN). The
   outward unit normal is n = ∇φ/‖∇φ‖.
2. **Corner radius by curvature** (`fab/corner.rs`). At surface points (cells where φ changes sign,
   interpolated to φ = 0), form the Hessian H of φ by central differences and the shape operator
   S = (I − n·nᵀ)·H·(I − n·nᵀ)/‖∇φ‖; its two non-trivial eigenvalues are the principal curvatures κ₁ ≥ κ₂. With
   this sign convention a sphere of radius R has κ = +1/R (convex), and an internal (concave) fillet of radius
   r has κ₂ = −1/r. The *internal corner radius* at a point is r_int = −1/κ₂ where κ₂ < 0.
   - `tool_type = "ball"`: both principal curvatures are constrained: violation where r_int < r_t.
   - `tool_type = "flat"`: only curvature in planes normal to the setup direction d constrains the cutter (a
     flat end mill leaves a sharp floor edge without difficulty): evaluate the curvature of the level curve of
     φ in the plane normal to d, for each declared setup, and take the best (largest radius) over setups.
   Curvature from finite differences cannot resolve radii below about 2·h: such points are reported as
   `r < 2h` and are violations whenever 2·h ≤ r_t.
3. **Cross-check by tangent ball.** At each surface point p, a ball of radius r_t tangent to the surface on
   the outside is centred at q = p + r_t·n; the cutter can touch p only if the ball does not cut into the
   part: φ(q) ≥ r_t − 2·h. Points failing this test are unreachable by a cutter of that radius. The two
   measures must agree on the violating region up to a band of 2·h; a disagreement larger than that on a
   fixture is a test failure, and in production both regions are reported.
4. **Violation record.** Connected violating surface regions with: minimum r_int, its location, the region's
   area, the limit r_t (the smallest cutter on the card), the difference r_t − r_int, and the repair from the
   limit check (target r_t). Edit hint: "fillet this corner to at least r_t" or "add a cutter of radius ≤ r".
5. **Enclosed voids** (`fab/voids.rs`). Label the empty cells (φ > 0) of a grid padded by two cells. The
   component touching the padding is the outside. Every other empty component is a void: report its volume
   (cell count × h³, with the bound ± surface cells × h³), centroid and bounding box.
   - Milling and `sheet`: each void is a violation (measured: void volume; limit: none allowed; repair: open
     the cavity to a setup direction or fill it).
   - `sls`, `slm`: a cavity is drained only through an opening at least as wide as the card's
     `drain_hole_min_m`. Erode the empty space by half that diameter and relabel: a component that is cut off
     from the outside after erosion is `undrained`, with the widest existing opening measured by bisection on
     the erosion radius. Repair target: an opening of the drain diameter; edit hint with the nearest outer
     surface point.
   - `fdm`: voids are reported as information (trapped support is possible) and are not violations.
6. **Accuracy and class.** Both evaluators state their resolution h in every finding; results are Estimated.
   Radii are accurate to about ± h for r ≥ 4·h on the fixtures (a tested statement, not a bound).
7. **Determinism.** Fixed scan order for labelling; curvature in `f64`; findings sorted by location.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A pocket with internal vertical fillets of radius 2.0 mm against a 3.0 mm flat cutter is flagged at all four corners with measured radius 2.0 ± 0.25 mm (h = 0.25 mm), limit 3.0 mm, difference 1.0 mm, repair target 3.0 mm.
- [ ] The same pocket with 3.5 mm fillets passes; with sharp corners it is flagged as `r < 2h`.
- [ ] With a flat cutter the sharp edge between pocket floor and wall is not flagged; with a ball cutter it is.
- [ ] The tangent-ball cross-check agrees with the curvature measure on every corner fixture within the 2·h band.
- [ ] A sphere of radius 10 mm reports κ = +100 m⁻¹ within 3% everywhere and no internal corner.
- [ ] A cube with a spherical cavity of radius 5 mm reports one void of volume 5.236 × 10⁻⁷ m³ within the grid bound; for milling it is a violation.
- [ ] For `slm` with a 3 mm drain minimum: the cavity with a 2 mm channel to the outside is `undrained` with widest opening 2 mm ± h; with a 4 mm channel it passes.
- [ ] For `fdm` the cavity is reported and is not a violation.

### Known answers and negative controls
Sphere curvature 100 m⁻¹; bore curvature −250 m⁻¹; cavity volume 5.236 × 10⁻⁷ m³. Negative controls: the three
planted defects, and the clean fixtures (no finding). Metamorphic: rotating the part by a grid-incommensurate
angle changes measured radii by less than h; refining h never turns a clean fixture into a violating one;
scaling part and tool radius together preserves the set of findings. Determinism: identical findings twice on
one machine.

## Considerations

- Curvature of a sampled distance field is noisy near sharp features; the tangent-ball test is the robust
  one, and the curvature gives the number a designer wants (the radius). Reporting both is deliberate.
- The flat-cutter rule assumes machining along the declared setups only; a corner that is internal in every
  setup's plane is flagged, one that some setup sees as an outside edge is not.
- Grid spacing drives cost: a 100 mm part at h = 0.25 mm is 6.4 × 10⁷ cells; the evaluators work on a narrow
  band around the surface (DESIGN) to keep memory at tens of megabytes.
- KILL-FAB fallback: these findings become advisory lints.
