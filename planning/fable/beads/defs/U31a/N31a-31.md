## Background

Tolerances cost money where they are tight and cost function where they are loose. The usual practice, one
tolerance grade everywhere, is tight where it does not matter and sometimes loose where it does. A robot model
knows something a drawing does not: how much each dimension moves the things the robot must do. Tolerance
allocation driven by sensitivities uses that knowledge: loosen where sensitivity is low and cost is high,
tighten where a small error is amplified.

The mathematics is construction C-35, part 3: for a functional dimension f(x) with sensitivities aᵢ = ∂f/∂xᵢ
from one adjoint pass over the kinematic chain, the worst-case stack is Σ|aᵢ|·tᵢ and the statistical stack is
(Σ aᵢ²·tᵢ²)^½.

This bead supplies the two inputs every later step needs: what is being protected, and how sensitive it is.

**Quantities of interest.** Foot or tool position error; joint-axis offset and parallelism; closure error of a
loop; backlash; minimum clearance; wheel radius as an odometry scale.

**Sensitivities.** For kinematic quantities they are exact derivatives of the product-of-exponentials map with
respect to geometric parameters, class Certified when evaluated in exact or interval arithmetic. For
simulated task metrics they come from the sensitivity analysis A-16, class Estimated. The allocation inherits
the weaker class.

The alternative of estimating all sensitivities by perturbing and re-simulating was rejected for the kinematic
quantities: an exact derivative exists, costs one pass, and carries a stronger evidence class.

## Scope

In: the catalogue of quantities of interest and how each is defined on a compiled model; the feature model
linking a toleranced feature to geometric parameters; exact sensitivities of kinematic quantities over a pose
set; estimated sensitivities of simulated metrics; the sensitivity table with classes.
Out: turning sensitivities into tolerances (N31a-32); zero or unknown sensitivities, band extremes and
mode-switching quantities (N31a-33); the analysis A-16 itself (N33b-18, `analyze.sensitivity`: exact
derivatives and Morris screening; the seed bead BD-243 only checks A-09, A-14 and A-16 side by side in the
registry and is not needed here).

## Technical approach

1. **Features** (`tol/qoi.rs`, DESIGN):
   ```rust
   pub struct Feature { id: FeatureId, part: PartId, kind: FeatureKind,   // Length, Position, Angle, Parallelism, Radius
                        params: Vec<(ParamRef, f64)>,   // how a unit deviation of the feature moves model parameters
                        cost: CostCoeff, group: Option<SetupOrDatumId> }
   ```
   A feature is something a shop controls (a bore position, a face-to-face length, an axis direction). Its
   `params` map says which geometric parameters of the model move, and by how much, per unit of feature
   deviation: a bore position error moves a joint axis's point; a face angle error tilts an axis direction.
2. **Quantities of interest**, each a function q(p) of the geometric parameters p at a pose:

   | Quantity | Definition on the model | Unit |
   |---|---|---|
   | Foot or tool position error | Displacement of a declared point of the end body from its nominal position, as a vector, or its component along a declared direction | m |
   | Joint-axis offset | Distance between a joint's actual axis and its nominal axis at the joint centre | m |
   | Joint-axis parallelism | Angle between two axes that are nominally parallel | rad |
   | Loop closure error | The six-component gap at the cut joint of a closed chain when the others are at nominal | m, rad |
   | Backlash | Free angle at a joint output | rad |
   | Minimum clearance | Smallest distance between two declared bodies over a pose set | m |
   | Wheel radius as an odometry scale | Travelled distance per wheel revolution: distance error = (δr/r)·distance | m per m |
3. **Exact sensitivities** (`tol/sens.rs`). The forward map of a chain is
   T(θ; p) = exp(ξ̂₁(p)·θ₁) ⋯ exp(ξ̂_n(p)·θ_n)·M(p), with screw axes ξ_i and home pose M depending on p. For a
   parameter p_j, the body-fixed-at-base derivative is
   (∂T/∂p_j)·T⁻¹ = Σ_i Ad(exp(ξ̂₁θ₁) ⋯ exp(ξ̂_{i−1}θ_{i−1}))·D_i(p_j) + Ad(exp(ξ̂₁θ₁) ⋯ exp(ξ̂_nθ_n))·(∂M/∂p_j·M⁻¹),
   where D_i(p_j) = (∂ exp(ξ̂_iθ_i)/∂p_j)·exp(−ξ̂_iθ_i) is a twist computed from the derivative of the screw
   axis. One backward pass over the chain gives the derivative of the end pose with respect to every
   parameter (the adjoint pass of the construction). A scalar quantity's sensitivity is its directional
   derivative: for position error along a unit direction u at point x, s_j = u·(v_j + ω_j × x) for the twist
   (ω_j, v_j); for the error magnitude, s_j = ‖v_j + ω_j × x‖.
   The pass runs in exact rational arithmetic when parameters and the trigonometric values of the pose are
   rational (poses from a rational parametrization of rotations), and otherwise in outward-rounded interval
   arithmetic, giving an enclosure [s_lo, s_hi]. Class: Certified in both cases; the enclosure's upper
   magnitude is the value used.
4. **Pose set.** Sensitivities depend on pose. Each quantity declares a pose set (the task's key poses, or a
   fixed grid over joint ranges); the sensitivity used for allocation is the largest magnitude over the set,
   and the governing pose is recorded.
5. **Feature sensitivity.** s(feature) = Σ over its params of (map coefficient × parameter sensitivity), with
   signs kept for the stack and the magnitude used for allocation.
6. **Estimated sensitivities.** For a simulated task metric (success rate, energy per metre, tracking error
   under the controller), the value and standard error come from analysis A-16 on the campaign's world
   addresses. Class Estimated. A sensitivity whose standard error exceeds half its magnitude is `unknown`
   (DESIGN threshold) and is handled by the refusal rule of N31a-33.
7. **Sensitivity table** (`frob.foundry.sens/1`): per (quantity, feature): value or enclosure, unit, governing
   pose, class, source (exact pass or A-16 run identity), evidence colour for the sibling's report (green for
   Certified, amber for Estimated, red for unknown).
8. **Class rule.** A quantity's allocation takes the weakest class among the sensitivities it used; mixing one
   Estimated sensitivity into a stack makes that stack's closure statement Estimated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Planar two-link arm (lengths l₁ = 0.3 m, l₂ = 0.25 m, at θ₁ = 30°, θ₂ = 60°): the tool-position sensitivity to l₁ has magnitude 1 and direction (cos 30°, sin 30°); to l₂ magnitude 1 and direction (cos 90°, sin 90°) = (0, 1); to a zero-offset error of joint 2 magnitude l₂ = 0.25 m/rad.
- [ ] The exact pass agrees with central finite differences of the forward map to 10⁻⁷ relative on a six-joint spatial chain at ten random poses, and its interval enclosures contain the exact rational values at rational poses.
- [ ] At a rational pose (cos θ = 3/5, sin θ = 4/5) the sensitivities are exact rationals, bitwise identical on every platform.
- [ ] Wheel odometry: radius 0.05 m over 10 m gives sensitivity 200 m per m, so a 0.1 mm radius error is 20 mm of distance error.
- [ ] Joint-axis parallelism: a face-angle feature that tilts one of two nominally parallel axes by δ gives sensitivity 1 rad per rad.
- [ ] A simulated-metric sensitivity is returned with its standard error and class Estimated; one with standard error above half its magnitude is marked `unknown`.
- [ ] A stack with five Certified sensitivities and one Estimated one is labelled Estimated, naming the Estimated feature as the weakest input.
- [ ] The governing pose of each sensitivity is recorded.

filters go to the test harness after it):

- `poe_derivative_matches_finite_differences` (six-joint chain, 10 poses, every parameter).
- `rational_pose_is_exact`: with (3/5, 4/5) the l₁ sensitivity along x is exactly 3/5.
- `interval_enclosure_contains_exact`.
- `adjoint_pass_cost_is_linear`: operation count grows linearly with the number of joints (counted, not timed).
- `worst_pose_selected_and_recorded`.
- `feature_map_combines_parameters`: a bore-position feature that moves an axis point by 1 and tilts nothing has the axis-offset sensitivity 1.
- `loop_closure_gap_sensitivity` on a planar four-bar: the gap's sensitivity to the coupler length equals the unit vector along the coupler.
- `wheel_radius_scale_200`.
- `estimated_sensitivity_carries_standard_error`; `noisy_sensitivity_is_unknown`.
- `weakest_class_rule`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `drop-home-pose-term` (omits ∂M/∂p), `adjoint-order-reversed` (applies the adjoints of later joints), `float-pass-labelled-certified` (plain `f64` without outward rounding is given class Certified; the class test fails).

### Known answers and negative controls
The two-link values; the rational-pose value 3/5; the odometry scale 200. Negative controls: the three planted
defects. Metamorphic: a rigid motion of the whole mechanism rotates vector sensitivities and leaves magnitudes
unchanged; scaling all lengths by s scales length-to-position sensitivities by 1 and angle-to-position
sensitivities by s. Determinism: D0 at rational poses; identical interval endpoints twice on one machine and
across platforms where the interval library's rounding is the same.

## Considerations

- A first-order sensitivity is a local statement. Where it vanishes or where the quantity switches modes
  (clearance, backlash), the first-order model is wrong, and those cases are routed elsewhere by design.
- The feature-to-parameter map is authored per part family; a wrong map gives a confident wrong allocation.
  The as-built audit, which compares measured features with their allocations, is the eventual check.
- Certified here means the derivative of the model is enclosed; it says nothing about whether the model
  describes the built robot (`reality: no-claim`).
- KILL-TOLERANCE fallback (worst-case boxes per interface) still needs this bead's quantities and interval
  evaluation; only the statistical allocation is dropped.
