## Background

Given what each feature's deviation does to a quantity of interest (its sensitivity) and what it costs to hold
the feature tighter, there is a cheapest set of tolerances that still meets the specification. For independent
deviations and a statistical stack the answer is closed-form (construction C-35, part 3): the statistical
stack is (Σ aᵢ²·tᵢ²)^½; minimizing the cost Σ kᵢ/tᵢ under a stack limit gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓; for the
worst-case stack Σ|aᵢ|·tᵢ it gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/|aᵢ|)^½. Tolerance is cheapest to loosen where sensitivity is low
and cost is high, and the cube root keeps the allocation from being extreme.

The tolerance-allocation sibling implements this (`allocate`, `variance_budget`), together with first-order
correlated stacks aᵀ·C·a up to 128 terms (`propagate_correlated_stack`) and grouped allocation
(`allocate_grouped`). Its sensitivities and cost coefficients are inputs, and it provides no datum frames:
its report is a list of suggestions, not a drawing annotation.

The plan's steps 1 to 3:

1. `variance_budget(spec_margin, target)` turns the specification into a variance budget.
2. `allocate(features, budget, k)` returns t_i ∝ (c_i/s_i²)^(1/3), normalized to meet the budget.
3. Features that share a setup or a datum go through `propagate_correlated_stack` or `allocate_grouped`.

**Worked case.** Two features with sensitivities 1 and 4, equal cost coefficients, k = 3, budget √B = 10 µm.
The allocation is 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm. Equal tolerances meeting the same budget are 7.28 µm each, and the
allocation costs 19.9% less under the 1/t cost model (FACT, arithmetic).

Gate (TARGET): GATE-FOUNDRY-08, Known answer, M5: the worked allocation to 10⁻⁹; the zero-sensitivity and
quadratic cases handled as specified; a correlated stack equals its hand value. The ceiling of the mechanism is
stated in advance: cost under an assumed 1/t model only.

## Scope

In: the budget; the closed-form allocation and its normalization; the cost comparison against equal
tolerances; correlated stacks; grouped features; several quantities of interest sharing features; the
allocation record. The verb `foundry.tolerance` as far as these steps.
Out: sensitivities (N31a-31); zero and unknown sensitivities, the band-extreme check, worst-case boxes,
mode-switching quantities and the report into the packet (N31a-33).

## Technical approach

1. **Budget** (`tol/allocate.rs`). A specification is a margin T on a quantity (|q − q_nominal| ≤ T) and a
   target expressed as a coverage factor k (the tolerance band of each feature is ±k standard deviations,
   t_i = k·σ_i; k = 3 by default, DESIGN). The variance budget is B with √B = T/k_spec, where k_spec is the
   coverage demanded of the quantity itself (default equal to k). In the worked case √B = 10 µm and k = 3.
   The sibling's `variance_budget(spec_margin, target)` computes B; Foundry records both arguments.
2. **Allocation.** Minimize Σ c_i/t_i subject to Σ s_i²·(t_i/k)² = B. The stationarity condition
   −c_i/t_i² + 2·μ·s_i²·t_i/k² = 0 gives t_i³ ∝ c_i/s_i², and normalizing to the budget:

   t_i = k·√B·(c_i/s_i²)^(1/3) / √(Σ_j (c_j·s_j)^(2/3)).

   Worked case: Σ (c_j·s_j)^(2/3) = 1 + 4^(2/3) = 3.519842; its root is 1.876124; t₁ = 30/1.876124 =
   15.9904 µm; t₂ = 15.9904 × (1/16)^(1/3) = 6.3458 µm. Check: (15.9904² + 16 × 6.3458²)/9 = 100 µm².
   Foundry calls the sibling's `allocate(features, budget, k)` and verifies its output against this closed
   form to 10⁻⁹ relative on every call with independent features (a cheap self-check that also guards against
   an upstream change of convention).

   **Worst-case method** (`--method worst-case`). The stack is Σ|s_i|·t_i ≤ T, with the margin T itself as the
   limit (no coverage factor: a worst-case band is the band). Minimizing Σ c_i/t_i under it, the stationarity
   condition −c_i/t_i² + μ·|s_i| = 0 gives t_i ∝ (c_i/|s_i|)^(1/2), and normalizing:

   t_i = T·(c_i/|s_i|)^(1/2) / Σ_j (|s_j|·c_j)^(1/2).

   Sensitivities 1 and 4, equal costs, T = 10 µm: Σ_j (|s_j|·c_j)^(1/2) = 1 + 2 = 3, so t₁ = 3.333 µm and
   t₂ = 1.667 µm, and the stack is 3.333 + 4 × 1.667 = 10 µm. The sibling allocates only to a variance
   budget; this closed form is Foundry's own (`tol/allocate.rs::worst_case`).
3. **Cost comparison.** Equal tolerances meeting the same budget: t_eq = k·√B/√(Σ s_j²) (7.276 µm in the
   worked case). Relative saving = 1 − (Σ c_i/t_i)/(Σ c_i/t_eq) (19.9% in the worked case). Printed with the
   words "under the 1/t cost model"; cost coefficients are inputs of provenance `assumed` unless a process
   card supplies them, and the saving's class is Estimated.
4. **Correlated stacks.** Features that share a setup or a datum do not deviate independently. With a
   correlation matrix C over feature deviations (unit diagonal; correlations declared per setup or datum
   group), the stack variance is aᵀ·Σ·a with Σ_ij = C_ij·σ_i·σ_j, computed by the sibling's
   `propagate_correlated_stack` (at most 128 terms). Used to *verify* any allocation against its budget; an
   allocation computed as if independent that fails under the declared correlations is rescaled by the common
   factor √(B/(aᵀ·Σ·a)) and the rescaling is recorded.
5. **Grouped features.** Features cut in one setup are assigned one tolerance per group
   (`allocate_grouped`). A group's effective sensitivity is √(Σ s_i²) for independent members and Σ|s_i| for
   members that move together (a shared datum shift); the group's cost is the sum of its members' costs. The
   closed form of step 2 is applied over groups.
6. **More than 128 correlated terms.** Features sharing a setup are merged into groups first. If a stack still
   exceeds 128 terms, the correlated verification for that quantity is replaced by the worst-case stack
   Σ|s_i|·t_i, which needs no correlation model, and the result says so.
7. **Several quantities sharing features.** Each quantity q has its own budget B_q and sensitivities s_qi.
   Step 2 is solved per quantity; a feature's tolerance is the smallest over the quantities it appears in
   (DESIGN: feasible for every quantity, not jointly cost-optimal); each quantity's achieved stack and its
   slack against the budget are then reported, and the label `per-quantity optimum, conservative combination`
   is attached.
8. **Process floor.** The process card of the part (N31a-26) may carry an optional table
   `tolerance_floor_m`, keyed by feature kind (`length`, `bore-diameter`, `position`, `flatness`, `angle` in
   radians), each value with its provenance (DESIGN: the process-card schema of N31a-26 carries the optional
   table; a card without it is valid). Where the table is absent for a feature's kind, no floor check is made and the
   record says `process floor: not declared`. A tolerance tighter than the declared floor is flagged
   `beyond-process-capability`, with the repair to redistribute (fix that feature at the floor and re-solve
   the others against the remaining budget B − s_f²·(t_floor/k)²) or to change process. If the remaining
   budget is not positive, the redistribution is impossible and the flag carries the repair "change process"
   only.
9. **Record** (the result of the verb, schema `frob.foundry.tolerance.out/1`, the name the verb registry
   gives it): per feature: tolerance, sensitivity with class, cost coefficient with provenance, group,
   governing quantity; per quantity: budget, achieved statistical stack, worst-case stack, slack, class (the
   weakest sensitivity class used); the equal-tolerance comparison; k; the method. The robustness results
   and the packet report `tolerances.json` are added to the same result by N31a-33.
10. **Verb.** `foundry.tolerance <design> [--qoi …] [--k 3] [--method auto|statistical|worst-case]`, kind
    `read`. `auto` (the default) is the statistical allocation of this bead with worst-case boxes for the
    features N31a-33 routes to its robust path; `statistical` refuses instead of boxing (FROB-REF-165, N31a-33);
    `worst-case` uses the closed form of step 2's worst-case method for every feature. The held-out check
    `--verify monte-carlo` is N31a-33's. Class of the result is Estimated, the ceiling of every Foundry result
    (INV-FOUNDRY-06; the verb row's evidence), and its `weakest` names the weakest class among sensitivities
    and inputs (N31a-31 step 8: a stack of only Certified sensitivities is still Estimated and lists them as
    Certified inputs); determinism D0 when inputs are rational and the cube roots are reported as enclosures, otherwise
    deterministic `f64`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-08: the worked case returns 15.9904 µm and 6.3458 µm, matching the closed form to 10⁻⁹ relative; printed as 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm.
- [ ] Equal tolerances for the same budget are 7.276 µm and the allocation's cost is 19.9% lower under the 1/t model.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-08: a correlated stack equals its hand value: a = (1, 1), σ = (3, 4) µm, correlation 0.5 gives variance 37 µm² and standard deviation 6.083 µm (5.000 µm at correlation 0, 7.000 µm at correlation 1).
- [ ] Two dimensions of equal sensitivity with cost weights 1 and 8 under a statistical limit of 0.1 mm get tolerances in the ratio 1 : 2, namely 0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm.
- [ ] An independent-feature allocation that violates its budget under declared correlation 0.5 is rescaled by the stated factor and then meets the budget exactly.
- [ ] A group of three features in one setup receives one tolerance, and the stack with that tolerance meets the budget.
- [ ] A feature appearing in two quantities takes the smaller of its two tolerances; both quantities report non-negative slack.
- [ ] A tolerance below the process floor is flagged with the redistribution repair, and the redistributed allocation meets the budget; a card without `tolerance_floor_m` yields `process floor: not declared` and no flag.
- [ ] `--method worst-case` on sensitivities 1 and 4 with equal costs and T = 10 µm returns 3.333 µm and 1.667 µm, and the worst-case stack equals T to 10⁻¹².
- [ ] The result names its class (Estimated, also when every sensitivity is Certified) and the weakest input; the cost saving is labelled with the cost model.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-745 (tolerance allocation, worked case: 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm; 19.9% cheaper than equal tolerances under the
1/t cost model; control: q = x² at nominal zero is refused and then fails the band-extreme check, carried by
bead N31a-33). KA-171 (equal sensitivity, cost weights 1 and 8, limit 0.1 mm: 0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm).
Negative controls: the four planted defects. Metamorphic: multiplying every cost coefficient by a constant
changes no tolerance; multiplying every sensitivity by λ divides every tolerance by λ; doubling the budget's
root doubles every tolerance. Determinism: identical everywhere for rational inputs up to the stated cube-root
enclosure.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOLERANCE (M5): trigger GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of
  features in the pilot. Fallback: worst-case boxes per interface (N31a-33).
- The 1/t cost model is an assumption; real cost has steps at process changes. The saving is a comparison
  under the model, never a price, and no priced output exists without a licensed catalogue.
- Independence of feature deviations is an assumption that setups and datums violate; step 4 is where the
  declared correlations are honoured, and undeclared ones are a risk the as-built audit can expose.
- The conservative combination across quantities gives away some cost; a joint convex solve is possible and
  is left out until the pilot shows it matters.
