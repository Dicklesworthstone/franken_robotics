## Background

First-order tolerance allocation assumes that a quantity of interest responds linearly to each feature's
deviation inside its tolerance band. That assumption fails in three recognizable ways, and each has a rule.

**A zero first-order sensitivity is not insensitivity (counterexample CX-340).** For q(x) = x² at nominal zero
the sensitivity is zero and a first-order allocator would loosen without limit, while the deviation at
tolerance t is t². The sibling requires positive sensitivities; Foundry refuses the feature (FROB-REF-165) and
routes it to the band-extreme check.

**The linearization can be wrong at the edge of the band.** Step 4 of the plan: `robustness_check` evaluates
the true quantity at band extremes. Features that fail fall back to worst-case boxes by interval evaluation,
which is also the fallback of KILL-TOLERANCE.

**Some quantities switch modes.** Step 5: clearances and backlash, which switch modes, go through
`structured_propagation` (finite-population propagation through piecewise-quadratic clearance laws, with a
gear-backlash consumer). A clearance is the minimum of several gaps, and which gap is smallest changes with the
deviations; backlash is zero until a clearance opens.

Then the results leave Foundry. Step 6: `gdt_report` enters the packet; it is a list of suggested tolerances
with their justification, not a drawing annotation, and datum frames are declared by Foundry in the packet.
Step 7: the as-built audit compares each measured feature with its allocation.

Refusal FROB-REF-165: an allocation with a zero or unknown sensitivity, or a failed band-extreme check; repair
offered: worst-case boxes for the named features.

## Scope

In: detection of zero and unknown sensitivities; the band-extreme check and its pass rule (all corners for
small stacks); worst-case boxes by interval evaluation and the split of the margin between boxed and
statistical features; the structured path for clearance and backlash; when the outcome is a fallback inside a
result and when it is a refusal; the tolerance report and `tolerances.json`; the hand-off record for the
as-built audit; the share of features failing the band-extreme check; the held-out Monte Carlo coverage
option `--verify monte-carlo` of the verb.
Out: the first-order allocation (N31a-32); sensitivities (N31a-31); datum and fiducial placement (N31a-40);
the as-built comparison itself (Ground).

## Technical approach

1. **Screening before allocation** (`tol/robust.rs`). A feature is removed from the first-order allocation and
   marked for the robust path when its sensitivity enclosure contains zero, its magnitude is below 10⁻⁹ of the
   largest sensitivity in the stack (DESIGN), or its sensitivity is `unknown` (an estimate whose standard
   error exceeds half its magnitude).
2. **Band-extreme check** (a check of the linearization, which is what the sibling's `robustness_check` is).
   For the allocated tolerances, evaluate the true quantity q (the model, not its linearization) at: each
   feature at +t_i and at −t_i with the others nominal (2n evaluations); and the two corners that the linear
   model predicts to be worst (every feature at sign(s_i)·t_i, and the opposite). For a stack of n ≤ 12
   features (at most 4,096 corners, DESIGN) every corner of the box Π[−t_i, +t_i] is evaluated instead of
   those two. The sibling's `robustness_check` performs the evaluations; Foundry supplies q as a closure over
   the compiled model. What is tested is the linearization, not the margin: a statistical allocation's
   corners lie outside the margin by design (in the worked case of N31a-32 the linearly worst corner is
   15.99 + 4 × 6.35 = 41.4 µm against T = k·√B = 30 µm), so a corner beyond the margin is not a failure.
   - A feature *fails* when the true change at its single-feature extreme differs from s_i·t_i by more than
     10% of the specification margin T (DESIGN threshold).
   - At a corner c (c_i = ±t_i) the discrepancy is d(c) = |q(c) − q_nom − Σ s_i·c_i|. A corner with
     d(c) > 0.1·T fails the features whose return to nominal at that corner, one at a time, lowers d below
     0.1·T; when no single return does, the smallest set taken greedily in order of the reduction each gives
     (the attribution rule, DESIGN). This catches an interaction that is bad only at a corner.
   Above 12 features the report says `corners: sampled (2n + 2)`.
3. **Worst-case boxes by interval evaluation.** Let R be the features on the robust path (screened in step 1
   or failed in step 2) and S the others. The margin is split (a conservative combination, DESIGN): the
   boxed features get the worst-case share T_R = φ·T and the statistical features the rest,
   √B_S = (1 − φ)·T/k_spec. For a given φ:
   1. *Box.* Start from the worst-case first-order split t_i ∝ (c_i/|s_i|)^(1/2) normalized so that
      Σ_{i∈R} |s_i|·t_i = T_R where s_i ≠ 0, and from the process default tolerance where s_i = 0; then
      find the largest common scale in (0, 1] by bisection (at most 20 halvings; a scale is never above 1, so
      a feature is never loosened beyond its start) such that the interval enclosure of q − q_nom over
      Π_{i∈R}[−t_i, +t_i], with S at nominal, lies inside [−T_R, +T_R].
   2. *Statistical part.* Allocate S by the closed form of N31a-32 against B_S.
   The split φ is chosen by golden-section search on [0, 1] minimizing the total cost Σ c_i/t_i (at most 30
   evaluations, DESIGN); φ = 0 when R is empty, and φ = 1 when S is empty. A boxed feature whose enclosure is
   zero at its start (a true insensitivity) keeps the process default and lets φ go to 0, so S keeps the full
   budget. The combined allocation is then re-checked by step 2 with every feature at its final band; a
   feature that fails again halves its box (at most 5 times) before the quantity is refused (step 6). The
   interval evaluation is the uncertainty sibling's `product_epistemic`; its result is an enclosure, so a box
   that passes is a guaranteed statement about the model whose inputs are Certified when q's sensitivity
   inputs are. The box itself, a Foundry result, is reported with class Estimated (INV-FOUNDRY-06, the
   ceiling N31a-01's `FoundryValue` enforces; N31b-18 reports it the same way) and lists those Certified
   inputs as its justification.
4. **Quadratic example carried as a fixture.** q(x) = x²/L with L = 1 mm and margin 10 µm: the sensitivity at
   zero is zero; a first-order allocator has no finite answer; the band-extreme check fails at any t above
   √(0.1·T·L) = √(1 µm × 1 mm) ≈ 31.6 µm, where the discrepancy t²/L exceeds 10% of the margin (at the 200 µm
   process default it reads 40 µm); the worst-case box, from the interval enclosure t²/L ≤ T, is
   t = √(10 µm × 1 mm) = 100 µm. The two thresholds differ because the box tests the quantity against the
   margin while the band-extreme check tests the linearization.
5. **Clearance and backlash.** Quantities declared as `clearance` or `backlash` skip the variance stack. The
   sibling's `structured_propagation` takes the finite population of feature deviations (each at its band
   ends and centre) through the piecewise-quadratic clearance law and returns the distribution of the
   quantity and its extremes; `gear_backlash` is the consumer for gear pairs. Acceptance is on the extreme:
   minimum clearance above its floor and backlash below its ceiling at every population point.
6. **Fallback or refusal.**
   - Default: features on the robust path receive worst-case boxes automatically; the result is complete,
     exits with code 2 (completed with findings), lists those features with reason (`zero-sensitivity`,
     `unknown-sensitivity`, `band-extreme-failed`), and labels the quantities involved `mixed: statistical and
     worst-case`.
   - FROB-REF-165 (exit 3) when the caller asked for a purely statistical allocation
     (`--method statistical`) and any feature needs the robust path; or when no worst-case box exists (the
     enclosure cannot be brought inside the margin at any tolerance above the process floor); or when a
     feature's sensitivity is unknown and its quantity cannot be evaluated in interval arithmetic (a simulated
     metric). Payload: the named features with reason, the quantity, the margin, the best enclosure found;
     repairs ranked in the order of the registry row (N31-REF Table 1, rendered by N31a-52's rule: the row's
     repairs first, then the mechanism's, a repair that does not apply to the reason omitted): (1) worst-case
     boxes for the named features (the exact command; omitted when no box exists or the quantity cannot be
     evaluated in interval arithmetic); (2) a second-order sensitivity, or for an unknown simulated
     sensitivity the number of additional episodes the sensitivity analysis needs; (3) tighten the named
     feature (omitted when no box exists above the process floor); then (4) a wider margin or a different
     process.
7. **The fifth.** The record reports `band_extreme_failed_share` = failed features (by the single-feature
   rule or the corner attribution of step 2) ÷ features checked. Above one fifth in the pilot is a trigger of
   KILL-TOLERANCE; the number is printed on every run so the trigger is visible outside the pilot too.
8. **Report** (`tol/report.rs`). The sibling's `gdt_report`, in which each tolerance carries its sensitivity
   and that sensitivity's evidence colour, is wrapped into the report object, schema
   `frob.foundry.tolerances/1` (named after its file, as the other packet sections are). The verb's result
   (`frob.foundry.tolerance.out/1`) carries it under `report`, and the packet assembler writes that object
   verbatim as `tolerances.json`. Per feature: `tolerance`; `action` (`loosen`, `keep` or `tighten`,
   relative to the process default); `path` (`allocated`, `grouped`, `worst-case-box` or `refused`: how the
   tolerance was obtained); `enclosure` (on `worst-case-box` features only: `product-epistemic`, the sibling's
   entry admitted by N33bx-06, or `local-bisection`, the local interval path of N33bx-06 step 5 when that
   admission failed); `beyond_process_capability` (boolean, from N31a-32's process floor);
   `sensitivity`; `class`; `governing_quantity`; `reason` (for `worst-case-box` and `refused`:
   `zero-sensitivity`, `unknown-sensitivity` or `band-extreme-failed`). Then per quantity the stack results,
   the split φ where boxes were used, the robustness results (`corners: all` or `corners: sampled (2n + 2)`),
   `band_extreme_failed_share` and, when run, the held-out coverage of step 10. Its header carries the design
   and model identities, the four no-authorization flags and the statement: "suggested tolerances with
   justification; not a drawing annotation; datum frames are those declared in this packet".
9. **Hand-off to the as-built audit.** `tolerances.json` lists each feature with the measurable definition the
   as-built registration can evaluate (which datums, which fiducials, which surface region), so that a scan
   can be compared feature by feature.
10. **Held-out coverage** (`--verify monte-carlo [--samples N] --seed s`, `tol/verify.rs`). N draws (default
    10⁵) of the feature deviations: normal with σ_i = t_i/k for allocated features, correlated inside declared
    groups through the Cholesky factor of the group's correlation matrix; uniform over the box for boxed
    features (DESIGN: a box carries no distribution, and the report says so). The true quantity is evaluated
    for each draw. Coverage p̂ is the fraction of draws with |q − q_nom| ≤ T; it passes when
    p̂ ≥ p* − 2.326·√(p*·(1 − p*)/N), where p* is the coverage of k standard deviations (0.9973 for k = 3), so
    for N = 10⁵ the threshold is 0.9973 − 2.326 × √(0.9973 × 0.0027/10⁵) = 0.99692 (the pilot's rule,
    N31b-08). The seed is recorded and the result is identical for the same seed. Class Estimated (a
    sample). Available for kinematic and closed-form quantities only, so the verb stays a cheap `read`
    (10⁵ evaluations of a product-of-exponentials map take milliseconds); for a simulated metric the option
    returns the finding `verify: not available for simulated metrics` and points to the pilot's script.
    Coverage below its threshold is a finding (exit 2) naming the quantity, not a refusal.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-08, zero sensitivity: a feature with sensitivity zero is not given an unbounded tolerance; it is routed to the robust path and listed with reason `zero-sensitivity`.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-08, quadratic case: q = x²/L at nominal zero is refused by the first-order allocator (FROB-REF-165 under `--method statistical`), fails the band-extreme check at t = 200 µm, and receives the worst-case box t = 100 µm ± 1%.
- [ ] A linear quantity passes the band-extreme check at its allocated tolerances with zero discrepancy, although its linearly worst corner lies beyond the margin (the worked case: 41.4 µm against 30 µm is not a failure).
- [ ] For a stack of at most 12 features every corner is evaluated, and an interaction that departs from the linearization only at an off-diagonal corner fails the features the attribution rule names.
- [ ] A mixed quantity (boxed and statistical features) meets its margin as T_R + k_spec·√B_S ≤ T, with φ recorded; a true insensitivity keeps the process default and leaves the statistical features the full budget.
- [ ] `--verify monte-carlo --samples 100000 --seed s` on a linear two-feature fixture with k = 3 reports a coverage at or above 0.99692, identical for the same seed.
- [ ] A quantity with strong curvature (a feature near a kinematic singularity in the fixture) fails the check for that feature only, and the fallback box for it is tighter than the first-order tolerance.
- [ ] Minimum clearance of two gaps (nominal 0.30 mm and 0.35 mm, tolerances ± 0.10 mm and ± 0.20 mm) is evaluated on the population: the extreme minimum is 0.15 mm, from the second gap, which a first-order stack on the nominally smallest gap would miss (0.20 mm).
- [ ] An unknown simulated-metric sensitivity yields FROB-REF-165 naming the feature and the number of extra episodes as a repair.
- [ ] `tolerances.json` (schema `frob.foundry.tolerances/1`) validates, every feature has action, path, sensitivity and class (and `enclosure` exactly when its path is `worst-case-box`), and its header carries the identities, the four flags and the not-a-drawing statement; the verb's result carries the same object under `report`.
- [ ] `band_extreme_failed_share` is reported.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-340 (a zero first-order sensitivity means the feature may be loosened: q(x) = x² at nominal zero; the
deviation at tolerance t is t²). The negative control of KA-745 (q = x²: refused, then fails the band-extreme
check). The two-gap clearance values. Negative controls: the five planted defects. Metamorphic: tightening
the margin never loosens a worst-case box; a feature that passes at tolerance t passes at any smaller
tolerance. Determinism: interval endpoints and the bisection are deterministic.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOLERANCE (M5): if GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails or more than a fifth of features fail the band-extreme check
  in the pilot, allocation is by worst-case boxes per interface: step 3 applied to every feature of an
  interface, with no statistical stack. The code path already exists here; the kill switch makes it the only
  one.
- Interval evaluation over many features widens quickly (the dependency problem); boxes can be far tighter
  than necessary. That conservatism is the cost of a guaranteed statement and is reported as the ratio of box
  to first-order tolerance.
- Above 12 features the band-extreme check samples 2n + 2 points, not all 2ⁿ corners. It then detects
  curvature in each feature and at the linearly-worst corners; an interaction that is bad only at another
  corner is missed, and that limit is stated in the report (`corners: sampled`). Grouping features that share
  a setup (N31a-32) keeps most stacks at or below 12.
- The tolerance pilot (N31b-08) must count a band-extreme failure by the same rule as this bead (the
  discrepancy from the linearization, not a corner beyond the margin), or the kill gate's second clause
  would fire on a perfectly linear linkage whose statistical corners exceed the margin by design.
- The split of the margin between boxes and the statistical stack adds a worst case to a k-sigma bound; it
  is conservative, and the report shows φ so a designer can see what the boxes cost.
- The report is advice to a designer. Real drawing annotations need datum reference frames and feature control
  frames that the sibling does not produce; the packet declares datums and says the rest is `review:
  no-claim`.
