## Background

The first section of a build packet is the parts list (`parts.json`): **made parts with geometry, material,
process and mass; purchased parts by card reference and quantity.** Three rules govern it.

**Revalidation after a snap.** A continuous optimum snapped to a catalogue part, stock size or gear ratio is
revalidated after the snap: hard constraints and the controller are re-checked on the snapped design. An
optimizer returns a wall of 1.85 mm and a gear ratio of 1.83; the shop stocks 2.0 mm and the catalogue has
1.8. The snapped design is a different design, and the evidence must be about it.

**Family pruning needs a bound.** A failed simulation removes a whole part family only when an algebraic bound
shows the failure holds across the family. One failed candidate says nothing about its neighbours unless
something monotone connects them.

**The prune rule has a counterexample that must be kept (CX-341).** Two gripper fingers each need one powered
tick and the bus powers one finger per tick. With a one-tick deadline no schedule works; with two ticks
exactly two do, and the motor limits are identical in both cases. A rule that adds both peaks prunes a feasible
design. **A prune is rejected if any discarded design is independently feasible under the same mission.**

**No prices without a pinned, licensed catalogue** (ADR-FOUNDRY-10; FROB-REF-818: a priced parts list without a
pinned, licensed catalogue; repair: omit prices). Which catalogue and price data, under what licence, is
OPEN-FOUNDRY-2 (owner: project lead; blocks nothing); the default is card references and no prices.

## Scope

In: the `parts.json` schema; derivation of made and purchased parts from the design; quantities and mass
roll-up; the snap record and its revalidation requirement; the prune proposal type, its bound and its
independent-feasibility test; the price rule and refusal.
Out: geometry files and mass properties of made parts (N31a-34, N31a-35, N31a-40); the actuator and sensor
schedule (N31a-42); the outer actuator re-selection that produces snaps (N31a-25); catalogue ingestion itself
(the exchange sibling's schema-checked catalogue reader).

## Technical approach

1. **Schema** (`packet/parts.rs`; `frob.foundry.parts/1`):
   ```json
   { "header": { "design": "…", "model": "…", "authorization": { … four false … } },
     "made": [ { "part": "lower-leg", "class": "LinkClassId", "instances": ["FL.shank", "FR.shank", …],
                 "mirrored": ["FR.shank", "HR.shank"], "quantity": 4,
                 "quantity_by_hand": { "as_designed": 2, "mirrored": 2 },
                 "geometry": "geometry/lower-leg", "mirror_plane": "xz of the part frame",
                 "mirrored_geometry": "geometry/lower-leg-mirrored",
                 "part_frame_to_link": [ … ],
                 "material": { "card": "hash", "state": "6061-T6 wrought" },
                 "process": { "card": "hash", "name": "cnc-3axis" },
                 "mass_kg": { "value": 0.141, "rel_uncertainty": 0.01, "class": "Estimated" },
                 "provenance": { "rung": "topology", "loop_record": "structure/loop.json" } } ],
     "purchased": [ { "role": "actuator", "card": "hash", "card_version": "…", "name": "qdd-m",
                      "quantity": 12, "used_at": ["*.hip", "*.thigh", "*.knee"],
                      "mass_kg": { "value": 0.48, "provenance": "datasheet" } } ],
     "snaps": [ … ], "prices": null }
   ```
   A mirrored instance of a class is listed under the same part with `mirrored`, since a mirror image is a
   different physical part made from the mirrored geometry; quantities count both hands separately in
   `quantity_by_hand`. The packet carries that geometry so that no shop has to mirror a file by hand (DESIGN):
   when the class's export-exact polyhedron, reflected about `mirror_plane` (a coordinate plane of the part
   frame, so the reflection negates one coordinate and stays exact in single precision), is not identical to
   itself after canonical ordering, `mirrored_geometry` names a second geometry entry, produced by that exact
   reflection with triangle orientation reversed and exported like any made part's geometry (four files,
   datums, fiducials and the cross-export identity, N31a-34, N31a-35, N31a-40); when it is identical,
   `mirrored_geometry` is `null` and one geometry serves both hands.
2. **Derivation.** Made parts: every body of the design whose fill is structural and which has a made
   geometry, grouped by link class. Purchased parts: every card-backed component (actuators, transmissions,
   encoders, sensors, battery, drivers, bearings and fasteners where modules declare them), grouped by card
   hash, with the places they are used. A body with neither a made geometry nor a card is a finding
   (`unsourced part`) that blocks the packet.
3. **Mass roll-up.** Σ quantity × mass over both lists must equal the assembly mass of `mass.json` and of the
   model within 10⁻⁶ relative; the comparison is part of cross-consistency (N31a-43) and is computed here.
4. **Snaps.** `snaps` lists every place a continuous value was replaced by a discrete one:
   `{ what: "wall" | "stock-size" | "gear-ratio" | "catalogue-part", where, continuous, snapped, revalidated:
   { design: "DesignId", checks: ["design.check", "control.synth", "foundry.check"], verdict: "VerdictId" } }`.
   The assembler refuses a packet containing a snap whose `revalidated.design` is not the packet's design, or
   whose listed checks did not run on it. (The mechanical pre-review of unit U31b re-checks this as its item
   "every snapped part was revalidated after the snap".)
5. **Prune proposals** (`packet/parts.rs::prune`). Whenever a stage wants to discard a family (all actuator
   sizes below a failed one; all walls below a failed wall), it must construct
   `PruneProposal { family, discarded: Vec<DesignId or ParamRange>, bound: AlgebraicBound }`, where the bound
   is a checkable statement that failure is monotone across the family (for example: required torque is
   non-decreasing in payload and capacity is non-decreasing in size, hence every smaller size fails), with the
   exact-arithmetic check that validates it. A proposal without a valid bound discards only the one failed
   candidate. Before a proposal is accepted, a sample of up to 8 discarded members (the extremes and a
   deterministic spread) is tested with the mission's exact feasibility oracle; **if any is feasible the prune
   is rejected** and the rule that proposed it is reported as unsound, with the witness.
6. **The two-finger fixture.** Bus capacity one powered actuator per tick; two fingers each needing one
   powered tick. The additive-peak rule computes a peak of two against a capacity of one and proposes to prune
   the two-finger design. The oracle enumerates schedules: with a deadline of one tick, none; with two ticks,
   exactly two (finger A then B; B then A). At a two-tick deadline the discarded design is feasible, so the
   prune is rejected.
7. **Prices.** `prices` is `null` by default and the envelope says `prices: omitted (no pinned, licensed
   catalogue)`. `--priced` requires a catalogue record with identity, licence terms, retrieval date and
   content hash, ingested through the sibling's schema-checked catalogue reader; then each purchased part
   carries a price with the catalogue identity, and made parts carry the parametric cost estimate of the
   manufacturability bead with its uncertainty, labelled as an estimate and not a price. `--priced` without
   such a catalogue → FROB-REF-818, repair "omit prices" (the same command without the flag), exit code 3.
   Under `--strict-data` a priced list whose catalogue is marked `assumed` is refused the same way
   (`--strict-data` on `foundry.export` is this bead's option, listed in the VERBS-foundry export row with
   this bead as the one that fixes its meaning there).
8. **Class.** Masses carry the class of their source (card provenance; Estimated for made parts). The list
   itself is structural data.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The parts list of the reference quadruped has one made-part entry per link class with correct quantities by hand, and one purchased entry per distinct card with every joint accounted for.
- [ ] A chiral link class carries `mirrored_geometry` naming exported files that equal the exact reflection of the class geometry; a class symmetric about its mirror plane carries `null`.
- [ ] Σ quantity × mass equals the model's total mass within 10⁻⁶ relative.
- [ ] A body with neither geometry nor card blocks the packet with the finding `unsourced part`.
- [ ] A snap whose revalidation ran on a different design is refused by the assembler, naming the snap.
- [ ] The two-finger prune is rejected at a two-tick deadline with the witness schedule, and accepted at a one-tick deadline only if a valid bound accompanies it.
- [ ] A prune proposal with no bound discards exactly one candidate.
- [ ] `foundry.export --priced` without a pinned, licensed catalogue returns FROB-REF-818 with the omit-prices repair; without the flag the list has `prices: null` and the envelope says why.
- [ ] With a catalogue record, prices carry the catalogue identity and licence, and made parts carry cost only as a labelled estimate.

### Known answers and negative controls
CX-341 (prune a design whose component peaks add past the bus limit: two gripper fingers; no schedule at a
one-tick deadline, exactly two at two ticks, with identical motor limits). Negative controls: the three
planted defects. Metamorphic: reordering bodies in the design leaves the list unchanged (canonical order by
class and card hash); adding an unused card to the catalogue changes nothing. Determinism: the list is a pure
function of the design, the model and the cards.

## Considerations

- A parts list is what a buyer reads first, so it is where an authorization-looking artifact is most
  dangerous; its header carries the four flags and the words `prices: omitted` or the catalogue's licence.
- Prices go stale. A priced list records the retrieval date and the catalogue hash, and nothing in the
  evidence depends on a price.
- The prune rule protects design-space search far beyond Foundry (the mission-synthesis lanes use the same
  fixture); it lives here because the parts list is where a pruned family first becomes visible to a person.
- Fasteners and bearings appear only where modules declare them; bolted joints are not modelled, and the list
  says `fasteners: as declared by modules; joint design is out of scope`.
