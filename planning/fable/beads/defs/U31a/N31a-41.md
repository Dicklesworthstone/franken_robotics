## Background

A robot that can be built has wires. The packet's wiring-harness section (`harness.json`: nets, connectors,
conductor sizes, lengths with slack, checks) is derived from the design, not drawn by hand:

**a netlist from the electrical ports of the design; routes along the kinematic tree through declared cable
ports; conductor size, length and slack per net.**

Every module in a design declares ports, including electrical ones (the port structure of construction C-12:
power flows across ports as effort and flow pairs, here voltage and current). Connecting the electrical ports
that must be connected gives the netlist.

Routing has an exact answer here: **the kinematic tree makes the shortest connecting tree the union of paths,
so no approximation is needed.** In a general graph the shortest tree connecting a set of terminals is a hard
problem; in a tree it is simply the union of the unique paths between the terminals.

**Checks:** voltage drop I·ρ·L/A against the bus budget; current density against a declared limit; bend
radius against the cable card; slack across a joint of at least ρ_c·Δθ for a cable at offset ρ_c from the axis.
**Sag and dynamic whip need a rod model, which is Frontier scope elsewhere in this plan, and are `no-claim`
here.**

OPEN-FOUNDRY-3 (which current-capacity table governs conductor sizing; owner: Foundry lead) is undecided. The
default: current density against a limit declared on the cable card, with no capacity claim.

## Scope

In: net extraction; routing over the tree and length computation; slack per joint crossing; conductor
selection from the cable card; the four checks; the `no-claim` statements; `harness.json`.
Out: the cable corridor as a keep-out in the structural design region (N31a-14); cable dynamics, sag and whip
(rod models, not in this chapter); connector mechanical design; electromagnetic compatibility.

## Technical approach

1. **Nets** (`packet/harness.rs`). From the design's electrical ports: for each power bus, one net from the
   battery (or supply) port to every driver's supply port; for each actuator, a phase net from its driver to
   its motor (when the driver is not integrated); for each sensor and encoder, a signal net to its consumer.
   Net record: `{ id, kind: bus | phase | signal, ends: [(body, port)], voltage, current: {continuous, peak} }`.
   Currents come from the actuator and bus analyses on the qualified task set. A net with fewer than two ends
   is a finding that blocks the packet ("every net has two ends").
2. **Routing.** Each body with an electrical port has cable ports (where a cable enters and leaves the body).
   For a net with terminals on bodies b₁ … b_k: take the union of the tree paths between them; the cable runs
   through each body on that union from its entry cable port to its exit cable port (or to the terminal).
   Length inside a body: the declared corridor length between the two cable ports, or the straight-line
   distance when none is declared. Branch points occur only at bodies, where a splice or connector is listed.
3. **Slack at a joint crossing.** A cable crossing a revolute joint at perpendicular offset ρ_c from the axis
   must change length by up to ρ_c·Δθ over the joint's range Δθ; slack ≥ ρ_c·Δθ. A cable through the axis
   (ρ_c = 0) needs no length slack and twists by Δθ; the twist is compared with the cable card's twist limit
   per unit length where the card has one. For a prismatic joint the slack is the stroke (DESIGN). Total
   length of a net segment = Σ in-body lengths + Σ slack + connector allowances from the card.
4. **Conductor selection.** For each net choose, from the cable card's stock cross-sections, the smallest A
   that passes checks (a) and (b); record A, the cable type and outer diameter.
5. **Checks**, each reported with value, limit and pass or fail:
   - (a) *Voltage drop*: ΔV = I·ρ·L/A with ρ the conductor resistivity from the card at its reference
     temperature (copper: 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ Ω·m), L the conductor length there and back, I the continuous current
     (and separately the peak); against the bus budget (a fraction of bus voltage declared on the design,
     DESIGN default 3%);
   - (b) *Current density*: J = I/A against the limit declared on the cable card;
   - (c) *Bend radius*: the smallest bend radius along the route (at cable ports and at joint crossings, from
     the declared geometry) against the card's minimum for its outer diameter;
   - (d) *Slack*: provided slack against ρ_c·Δθ at every crossing.
   Dissipated power I²·R per net is reported, because it is heat inside a limb.
6. **No-claim statements** written into the section: `sag: no-claim`, `dynamic whip: no-claim`,
   `current capacity: no-claim (current density against the card's declared limit only)`,
   `flex life: no-claim`.
7. **`harness.json`** (`frob.foundry.harness/1`): header with identities and flags; nets with ends, route
   (ordered list of bodies and cable ports), length and its breakdown, slack per crossing, conductor,
   connectors and splices, the four check results; totals (cable length by type, harness mass from the card's
   mass per metre). Harness mass is compared with the mass the design budgeted for wiring and the difference
   is a finding when it exceeds the budget.
8. **Class.** Estimated (currents come from simulation; resistivity and limits from cards). Determinism: exact
   rational arithmetic for lengths and slack where inputs are rational.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Voltage drop known answer: copper, 1 m each way, 0.5 mm², 10 A, 24 V bus: resistance 0.0688 Ω; drop 0.688 V (2.9%); dissipation 6.9 W; current density 20 A/mm². Halving the area doubles the drop.
- [ ] Cable slack known answer: offset 15 mm, joint range 2.4 rad: 36 mm.
- [ ] On a four-leg tree with a battery in the torso and drivers at each knee, the bus net's route is the union of the four torso-to-knee paths, and its total length equals the sum of the in-body lengths plus slack at every joint crossed.
- [ ] A net with one end blocks the packet.
- [ ] The smallest stock cross-section that passes both the drop budget and the current-density limit is chosen; with a 3% budget on the 24 V example, 0.5 mm² passes at 2.9% and 0.35 mm² fails at 4.1%.
- [ ] A cable through the joint axis has zero length slack and a twist check when the card has a twist limit.
- [ ] `harness.json` carries the four no-claim statements and validates.
- [ ] Harness mass above the design's wiring budget is reported as a finding.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry packet::harness`:
- `voltage_drop_copper_example`: R = 1.72 × 10⁻⁸ × 2/(0.5 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.0688 Ω; ΔV = 0.688 V; 0.688/24 = 2.87%; P = 10² × 0.0688 = 6.88 W; J = 10/0.5 = 20 A/mm².
- `halving_area_doubles_drop`: 0.25 mm² gives 1.376 V.
- `slack_15mm_2_4rad_is_36mm`.
- `conductor_selection_smallest_passing`: 0.35 mm² gives 0.983 V (4.1%), fails; 0.5 mm² passes.
- `route_is_union_of_tree_paths`: terminals on three leaves of a star-shaped tree give exactly the three spokes.
- `length_breakdown_sums`.
- `single_ended_net_blocks`.
- `axis_routed_cable_twist_check`.
- `prismatic_slack_is_stroke`.
- `bend_radius_check`: a 6 mm cable with a minimum of ten diameters fails at a 40 mm bend and passes at 60 mm.
- `harness_mass_against_budget`.
- `no_claim_statements_present`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `one-way-length` (uses L one way: the drop is halved), `slack-uses-degrees` (ρ_c·Δθ with Δθ in degrees), `route-by-straight-line-between-terminals` (ignores the tree).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_export_packet.sh` (bead N31a-57): asserts on the exported `harness.json` that every net
has two or more ends, every check has a value and a limit, the no-claim statements are present; runs the two
known-answer fixtures through `frob foundry export --fixture harness-24v --json`; logs per net length, slack,
conductor, drop against budget and current density against limit.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-746 (voltage drop: 0.0688 Ω; 0.688 V (2.9%); 6.9 W; 20 A/mm²; control: halving the area doubles the drop).
KA-747 (cable slack: 36 mm; the plan states no negative control; registered here: the degrees-for-radians
mutation, which gives 2,063 mm). Negative controls: the three planted defects. Metamorphic: doubling the
current doubles the drop and quadruples the dissipation; lengthening any link never shortens a route.
Determinism: identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- Current density against a declared limit is not a current-capacity (ampacity) claim: capacity depends on
  bundling, insulation and ambient temperature. Until OPEN-FOUNDRY-3 is decided the section says so and makes
  no capacity statement.
- Cable routes also occupy space the structure wants; the corridor keep-outs of the design region and this
  section read the same declared cable ports, so they cannot disagree.
- Slack is a minimum for geometry. Real harnesses need strain relief and service loops, which a reviewer adds;
  that is what `review: no-claim` covers.
- KILL-PACKET fallback defers the harness; the reduced profile omits this section.
