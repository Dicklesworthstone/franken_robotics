## Background

A person assembling a robot needs to know, joint by joint, what goes where and how it is to be set up. The
packet's schedule (`schedule.json`) is that table:

**Per joint: actuator card, gear ratio, mounting interface, encoder, driver rate, limits, thermal derating.
Per sensor: card, mount frame, extrinsic tolerance, clock contract (rational period and phase), cable.**

Rule: **every joint has an actuator card and every net has two ends.**

Every entry is something the model already contains: the design binds an actuator card to each actuated
joint, the compiled model holds each sensor's mount frame and its clock contract (in this system sensor
timing is a rational period and phase, so that clocks are exact), and the analyses hold the limits and the
thermal steady state. The schedule adds nothing; it gathers, and it refuses to be incomplete. A schedule with
an empty row is how a build discovers on the bench that a joint was never specified.

It is derived from the model identity the packet names, so it cannot describe a different robot than the one
whose evidence is in the packet.

## Scope

In: the joint rows and the sensor rows; where each field is read from; the completeness rules; the outputs
the cross-consistency check needs; `schedule.json`.
Out: the cards themselves (BD-069, BD-099); the parts list (N31a-39); nets and cables (N31a-41); the
comparison of this section with the parts list and the harness (N31a-43); tolerance values for mounts
(N31a-32, N31a-33); firmware parameters (N31a-44).

## Technical approach

1. **Joint row** (`packet/schedule.rs`; `frob.foundry.schedule/1`):

   | Field | Source | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | `joint`, `kind` | the design | revolute, prismatic, and so on; instances under a symmetry constructor are listed individually |
   | `actuator` | the joint's bound actuator card: hash, version, name | a passive joint must say `actuator: "passive"` with the design's reason; an empty binding is a violation |
   | `gear_ratio` | the transmission binding | as a rational where the design gives one (tooth counts), with the stage list |
   | `mounting_interface` | the module's mechanical port | bolt circle, pilot diameter, datum references into the part's datum scheme |
   | `encoder` | the sensor card bound to the joint | resolution in counts per revolution, placement (motor side or output side) |
   | `driver_rate` | the control binding | current-loop and command rates as rationals in hertz |
   | `limits` | the design and the card | position range, velocity, continuous and peak torque at the output, current limit |
   | `thermal_derating` | the thermal analysis | continuous output torque at the stated ambient, as a fraction of the card's rating, with the ambient and the winding limit used |
2. **Sensor row:**

   | Field | Source | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | `sensor` | the design | name and body |
   | `card` | the bound sensor card | hash, version |
   | `mount_frame` | the compiled model | pose in the body's link frame and in the part's datum frame |
   | `extrinsic_tolerance` | the tolerance allocation, when the mount is a toleranced feature; otherwise the card's requirement | translation and rotation bands, with class |
   | `clock` | the clock plan | rational period and phase, and the clock domain |
   | `cable` | the harness | the net identifier of its signal net |
3. **Completeness rules** (each failure blocks the packet and names the row):
   - every joint of the model has a row; every actuated joint has an actuator card; every passive joint is
     declared passive;
   - every sensor of the model has a row with a card, a mount frame and a clock;
   - every sensor and every non-integrated actuator has a `cable` entry naming a signal or phase net
     identifier derived from the model's electrical ports by the same naming rule the harness uses
     (`<kind>:<body>.<port>→<body>.<port>`, DESIGN), so the schedule can be built without the harness;
   - gear ratios, rates and clock values are those of the packet's `ModelId` (the schedule builder reads the
     compiled model, never the editable design text).
   The two rules that compare this section with others (every actuator row's card appears in the parts list
   with a quantity equal to the number of rows using it; every `cable` is a net of the harness with two
   ends) are evaluated by the packet's cross-consistency function (N31a-43, rows "Parts ↔ schedule" and
   "Nets") on the outputs of step 5, so this bead and the parts list and harness beads proceed in parallel.
4. **Derating.** `thermal_derating = τ_continuous(ambient)/τ_rated`, where τ_continuous is the largest output
   torque whose steady-state winding temperature stays at or below the card's limit at the stated ambient, as
   computed by the thermal analysis with temperature-dependent resistance. Class Estimated, with the card
   field that is the weakest input.
5. **Consistency outputs.** The builder emits, for cross-consistency, the multiset of actuator cards and the
   set of cable net identifiers it referenced.
6. **Class and determinism.** A table of references and of values that carry their own classes; rational
   fields are exact; the file is a pure function of model and cards.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The schedule of the reference quadruped has twelve joint rows, each with an actuator card, gear ratio, interface, encoder, driver rate, limits and derating, and one row per sensor with card, mount frame, tolerance, clock and cable.
- [ ] A joint with no actuator binding and no passive declaration blocks the packet, naming the joint.
- [ ] A sensor without a clock contract blocks the packet.
- [ ] The consistency outputs (the multiset of actuator cards with their row counts, the set of cable net identifiers) are emitted and equal the counts and names of the model: for the reference quadruped twelve actuator rows over their cards, and one cable identifier per sensor and non-integrated actuator; the comparisons with `parts.json` and `harness.json` (including a joint re-bound after the parts list was built, and a dangling cable reference) are asserted by N31a-43's consistency tests on these outputs.
- [ ] A sensor or non-integrated actuator without a `cable` entry blocks the packet, naming the row.
- [ ] Gear ratios given by tooth counts appear as rationals (for example 36/20 = 9/5), not as rounded decimals.
- [ ] Clock entries are rationals (a 1 kHz encoder has period 1/1000 s and a stated phase).
- [ ] The schedule validates against its schema and its header carries identities and the four flags.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry packet::schedule`:
- `joint_rows_complete_for_quadruped` (12 rows).
- `missing_actuator_blocks`; `passive_joint_must_be_declared`.
- `sensor_without_clock_blocks`; `sensor_without_cable_blocks`.
- `consistency_outputs_card_multiset` (twelve rows over the bound cards, counts equal to the model's bindings); `consistency_outputs_cable_ids` (one identifier per sensor and non-integrated actuator, by the stated naming rule).
- `cable_id_naming_rule`: an encoder on `knee.enc` read by `knee.drv` gives `signal:knee.enc→knee.drv`, the identifier the harness rule of N31a-41 gives the same ports.
- `gear_ratio_is_rational`: 36/20 reduces to 9/5; a two-stage 9/5 × 4/1 gives 36/5.
- `clock_is_rational`: period 1/1000 s, phase 1/4000 s.
- `derating_fraction`: rated 30 N·m, continuous 21 N·m at 40 °C ambient: 0.70.
- `mount_frame_in_datum_frame` (a known transform composed correctly).
- `reads_compiled_model_not_design_text`: editing the design text after compilation does not change the schedule for that `ModelId`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `skip-symmetric-instances` (lists one leg's joints only), `decimal-gear-ratio` (writes 1.8 with no rational), `actuator-row-for-passive-joint-filled-with-default`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic: 36/20 = 9/5; 9/5 × 4 = 36/5; 21/30 = 0.70. Negative controls: the three planted defects.
Metamorphic: renaming a joint changes only that row's name; swapping an actuator card changes exactly the rows
of the joints it is bound to and the parts-list quantities. Determinism: identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The schedule is the most likely section to be read alone, pinned to a bench. Its header repeats the flags
  and the statement that limits and deratings are model values of class Estimated.
- Mounting interfaces come from module ports; a custom actuator with no module port has no interface data,
  and its row then says `mounting_interface: undeclared`, which is a finding.
- Extrinsic tolerances for sensors matter to perception; where the tolerance allocation did not cover a mount,
  the card's requirement is shown and labelled as a requirement, not an allocation.
- KILL-PACKET fallback keeps the parts list and mass properties and drops this section.
