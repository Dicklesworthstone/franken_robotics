## Background

The build packet's sections are produced by different mechanisms: parts list, geometry, tolerances, mass
properties, harness, schedule, structural evidence, manufacturability report. The assembler is the step that
makes them one object about one design: it gathers them, refuses to proceed if any is missing, mislabelled or
about a different model, checks that they agree with each other, and hands them to the atomic writer. It is
the body of the verb `foundry.export`.

The last row of the packet's content table is carried here: **structural and manufacturing evidence** (the
results of the independent check and of manufacturability), with the rule that **classes are printed per
number.**

GATE-FOUNDRY-10 (Known answer, M5) names the assembler's contribution: "cross-consistency of parts, geometry,
nets and schedule".

This is the capability behind North-Star scenario NS-20, "a build packet": before, the loop ends with a scored
design; after, from a qualified design and task, Foundry emits a `.fbuild` packet: a bill of materials against
a pinned catalogue, faceted solid geometry, a tolerance stack that closes, fatigue margins computed from the
simulated load histories, and one link re-optimized to be 18% lighter at equal margin. The packet's claim
product is Estimated, because its material cards are, and every file in it carries the three
no-authorization flags and the packet flag `fabrication_released`, all false.

The kill gate KILL-PACKET decides at M5 whether the full packet ships: if GATE-FOUNDRY-09 or -10 fails after
one repair cycle, the packet is reduced to a parts list, mass properties and STL only, with STEP and harness
deferred.

## Scope

In: the input contract for each section; the `structure/` directory's content; preconditions; the
cross-consistency checks; per-number classes and the packet's claim product; the full and reduced profiles;
the verb's packet mode; the result envelope.
Out: each section's own production (N31a-33, N31a-34, N31a-35, N31a-39 to N31a-42, N31a-26); the container and
writer (N31a-36); flags (N31a-37); the offline audit (N31b-44); the firmware directory (N31a-44); the
mechanical pre-review and the review record (unit U31b).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs** (`packet/assemble.rs`). `PacketInputs { design, model, verdict, parts, geometry[], tolerances,
   mass, harness, schedule, structure, fab, certs[], firmware: Option<…> }`. Each section arrives as a typed
   value with its own header (design, model, flags) and its own record of evidence classes.
2. **Preconditions**, in order; the first failure refuses the export with its own code (exit 3), and no file
   is written:
   1. admission (bead N31a-03): a passed qualification verdict for exactly this design and model, else
      FROB-REF-166; no input record labelled `exploratory` (FROB-REF-166, pointer to the record);
   2. no envelope case labelled `provisional` in `structure/` (the rule of N31b-36): refusal
      `provisional-load-case-in-packet`, carried under the interim code FROB-REF-820 with the pointer at the
      case until the registry row is allocated (decision bead N31b-45);
   3. no unresolved `model-conditional` or `tier-sensitive` selection record behind the design (the
      precondition N31ax-01 registers through the admission hook): FROB-REF-830, or FROB-REF-819 for
      `tier-sensitive`; when it passes, the selection block it returns (`model-robust` or `none-recorded`,
      the records read and their frontier) is the manifest's `selection` field (N31a-36);
   4. every made part's geometry passed the independent check on a mesh the optimizer did not use, and the
      evidence cites that mesh hash (INV-FOUNDRY-03), else FROB-REF-261;
   5. the cross-export identity holds for every made part (bead N31a-40), else `packet-inconsistent` (below);
   6. every section's header names this design and model, else `packet-inconsistent`.
3. **`structure/` content** (`packet/evidence.rs`): `loads.json` (campaign identity or declared-load label;
   per link class the envelope cases with address, tick, variant, labels and re-extraction records);
   `stress.json`, `buckling.json`, `modes.json`, `fatigue.json` (the check records, per class: utilizations,
   buckling factors, first frequencies and floor, fatigue utilization or `no-claim`, each with mesh hash and
   labels); `loop.json` (the convergence record); `baseline.json` (the beam-rung section and mass per class
   and the saving against it, preceded by the ceiling line: the saving text is produced only by
   `format_saving(ceiling, saving, baseline)` of N31a-02, which emits the ceiling line first and refuses a
   baseline that is not a beam-rung result; the assembler formats no saving itself). Every number is an object
   `{ value, unit, class, labels, weakest }`: classes are printed per number.
4. **Cross-consistency** (`packet/consistency.rs`), a function reused by `foundry.check <packet>`:

   | Check | Rule |
   |---|---|
   | Parts ↔ geometry | every made part, and the mirrored hand of every chiral class (`mirrored_geometry`), has its four geometry files and its geometry record; every geometry belongs to a made part |
   | Parts ↔ cards | every purchased part resolves to a card of the stated hash and version |
   | Parts ↔ schedule | every joint has an actuator card (or a passive declaration); card multiplicities in the schedule's consistency output (N31a-42) equal quantities in the parts list |
   | Nets | every net has two ends; every end is a port of a listed part; every `cable` identifier in the schedule's consistency output is a net of `harness.json` |
   | Mass | Σ parts = assembly in `mass.json` = the model, within 10⁻⁶ relative |
   | Structure ↔ geometry | every structural record's mesh hash belongs to a geometry in the packet; every made part has a stress, buckling, modal and fatigue entry (numeric or `no-claim` with reason) |
   | Tolerances ↔ parts | every toleranced feature belongs to a listed part and refers to that part's datum scheme |
   | Fab ↔ parts | `fab.json` has an entry for every made part under its declared process |
   | Snaps | every snap was revalidated on this design |
   | Identities | one design, one model, one verdict across all sections |
   Each failed check is reported with the files and entries at fault; any failure blocks the export
   (the packet is not written). The refusal is `packet-inconsistent` (DESIGN; the chapter states the rule and
   no code): exit 3, `retry: after-change`, `pointer` the row and entry, `observed` and `required` the two
   disagreeing values with their files, `repairs` ranked: (1) re-run the verb that produces the named section
   for this design, with its exact command; (2) for a mass or identity row, re-export the geometry of the
   named part. The refusal is row 10 of the decision bead N31b-45, which holds it with no fitting interim
   code: until the ledger assigns its number (N35x-01) the assembler still refuses (exit 3, nothing written)
   and carries no code, only a `detail` naming `packet-inconsistent` behind one named constant in
   `packet/consistency.rs`, and this bead stays open on that point: it is not shipped as passing until the
   number is assigned, at which change only the constant's value moves. Tests assert the `detail` value
   through the constant.
   *Plant hook* (DESIGN; in `frob-foundry`'s `conformance` feature only, which the umbrella
   `gauntlet-mutations` enables, BD-025): the assembler reads `FROB_TEST_PACKET_PLANT=<plant>`, one of the ten
   row plants of the acceptance criteria or the three parallel-section plants, and applies it to its typed
   inputs before the checks, exactly as the unit tests construct it; the envelope of such a build carries the
   warning `test-packet-plant`, and a build without the feature ignores the variable. The verb takes a design
   and has no input through which a section could be planted, so this hook is how the plants reach the
   assembler through the binary (N31a-57 step 9).
5. **Claim product.** The packet's product is the combination of its sections': class the weakest (Estimated,
   because material and process cards are), tier the lowest among the evidence, conditions the union of labels
   (`model-conditional`, `check: same-family`, `loads: declared`, `fatigue: no-claim` are carried, not hidden),
   `reality: no-claim`. The manifest's `evidence` table lists each section's weakest input.
6. **Profiles.** `Full`: all sections. `Reduced` (the KILL-PACKET fallback, selectable by `--profile reduced`
   and forced when the kill switch is set): `parts.json`, `mass.json` and `geometry/*.stl` only; the manifest
   says `profile: "reduced"` and lists what is deferred (STEP, 3MF, GLB, harness, schedule, tolerances,
   structure, fab). Cross-consistency runs the rows that apply.
7. **Writing.** The verified inputs go to the atomic writer; the result envelope carries the packet identity,
   the file table, the claim product, the four flags (the envelope's `authorization` block also carries the
   three program-wide flags), findings of non-blocking checks, and next actions (`frob-verify --packet`,
   `foundry.check <packet>`, import a review record).
8. **Verb.** `foundry.export <design> --packet <dir> [--profile reduced] [--priced] [--with-firmware]
   [--replace <identity>]`, kind `run`, idempotency key required; long exports return a job handle (exit 8)
   after the bounded wait. Refusals reachable: FROB-REF-166, -261, -818, -824, -860, and with
   `--with-firmware` -827 (the registry row's list); from the preconditions of other beads, FROB-REF-820
   (interim for `provisional-load-case-in-packet`), -830 and -819 (N31ax-01); -169 when a symmetric class's
   load set in `structure/` does not cover every instance (the coverage rule of N31a-11, step 6, which every
   consumer applies); `packet-inconsistent` (held, above); and through the writer's export door (N31a-36,
   N37-18) FROB-REF-731, -733 and -734 (exit 5) and -860 for the packet's own flags. The verb cluster bead
   N31a-51 lists the same codes on the row of `foundry.export`. `--profile reduced` is this bead's option
   and `--replace` the writer's (N31a-36); VERBS-foundry's export row lists both, naming these beads.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-10, cross-consistency: on the reference packet every row of the table passes; for each row a planted inconsistency (a missing geometry file; an unresolved card; a joint without an actuator; a single-ended net; a mass off by 10⁻⁵; a structural record with a foreign mesh hash; a feature on an unlisted part; a made part missing from `fab.json`; an unrevalidated snap; a section naming another model) blocks the export and names the entry; so do three further plants on the rows that compare sections built in parallel (a joint re-bound after the parts list was built; a schedule `cable` identifier that is no net of the harness; a chiral class with no mirrored geometry). Each block is the refusal `packet-inconsistent` with exit 3, the row and entry as pointer, and the re-run of the producing verb as first repair.
- [ ] No packet directory exists after a blocked export.
- [ ] An unqualified design, an exploratory input, a provisional envelope case, an unresolved `model-conditional` selection and an unchecked geometry are each refused with the stated code (166, 166, the interim 820, 830, 261) before any file is written.
- [ ] Every number under `structure/` carries its class and labels; a schema test rejects a bare number.
- [ ] The packet's claim product is Estimated with the weakest input named, and carries every label present in its sections.
- [ ] `--profile reduced` writes exactly the parts list, mass properties and STL files, and the manifest lists what is deferred.
- [ ] NS-20 is demonstrable: from a qualified reference design the verb produces a packet that the reader and `frob-verify` accept, whose tolerance stack closes, whose fatigue entries are numeric where cards allow, and whose `baseline.json` reports the saving of the re-optimized link against its beam-rung section with the ceiling line first.
- [ ] Assembling and writing the same inputs twice gives the same packet identity, and the second write is the writer's idempotent success that adds no file. (The stored-envelope replay of one idempotency key is the verb layer's, asserted through `foundry.export` by BD-408 and N31a-57, which depend on this bead.)

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry -- packet::assemble packet::consistency packet::evidence`:
- `consistency_row_<name>` × 10: the clean packet passes; the planted inconsistency for that row fails with the entry named.
- `schedule_rebind_mismatch_caught`; `dangling_cable_caught`; `missing_mirrored_geometry_caught`.
- `packet_inconsistent_payload` (exit 3, no code, `detail` equal to the named constant, pointer, observed and required with files, the re-run command first).
- `preconditions_in_order`: with several of the six faults present the refusal reported is the first in the stated order.
- `blocked_export_writes_nothing`.
- `numbers_carry_class_and_labels` (schema).
- `claim_product_is_weakest_and_union`: sections Certified, Tested and Estimated give Estimated; labels are united.
- `reduced_profile_file_set`.
- `structure_directory_complete`: one entry per link class in each of the five records.
- `no_claim_entries_have_reasons`.
- `same_inputs_same_identity_idempotent_write`.
- `packet_plant_hook`: in a build with `conformance`, each of the thirteen plant names blocks the assembly with its row and entry; in a build without it, `FROB_TEST_PACKET_PLANT` changes nothing.
- `saving_reported_against_beam_baseline_with_ceiling_first`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `write-then-check` (writes the packet before consistency runs), `drop-labels-in-product`, `consistency-skips-mirrored-instances`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_packet_assemble.sh` (this bead's own, built only on its dependencies; it sources the
STD-E2E helper library; header `bead=N31a-43 milestone=M5 gates=GATE-FOUNDRY-10 seed=0 needs=cpu`). Steps: (1) `cargo test -p
frob-foundry -- packet::assemble packet::consistency packet::evidence` through `e2e_cmd` (including the three
planted defects); (2) `cargo run -p frob-foundry --example packet_assemble -- --fixture packet-inputs-ref --out
<scratch>/a --json`, an example program of this bead that loads typed section inputs recorded from a
qualified reference design (`synthetic`), runs the preconditions and the ten consistency rows, and writes
through the writer of N31a-36: every row passes, the claim product is Estimated with its weakest input named
and every section label carried, every number under `structure/` has a class, and `frob-verify --packet` on
the result exits 0; the same into `<scratch>/b`: the same identity; (3) for each of the ten rows and the three
parallel-section plants, `--plant <row>`: exit 3, `detail: "packet-inconsistent"`, pointer at the row and entry,
the re-run of the producing verb as first repair, no packet directory; (4) the preconditions in order, one
fault each: unqualified design and an exploratory record → FROB-REF-166; a provisional envelope case →
FROB-REF-820 with `detail: "provisional-load-case-in-packet"`; an unresolved `model-conditional` selection →
FROB-REF-830; an unchecked geometry → FROB-REF-261; several faults together → the first in the stated order; no
packet directory after any; (5) `--profile reduced`: exactly `parts.json`, `mass.json` and `geometry/*.stl`, with
the deferred list in the manifest. No `frob` verb is called (the packet half of `foundry.export` is BD-408's,
which depends on this bead), so the evidence block is recorded as absent with reason `no-verb`. Logs to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_packet_assemble/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: per line `ts`, `script`, `bead` =
N31a-43, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; per consistency row pass or fail with the entry
at fault, the refusal's code or `detail`, pointer and first repair, the packet identity and the claim product;
on failure the row, the entry and both disagreeing values with their files and the scratch paths; a final
`summary` (the gate is touched, not recorded: N31a-57 records GATE-FOUNDRY-10). Through the binary, steps 5,
9, 12, 13 and 14 of `scripts/e2e/foundry_export_packet.sh` (N31a-57), which runs this script as a stage, export
the qualified design (`--key "$(e2e_key packet-a)"`) and replay every planted inconsistency through `frob
foundry export` on a `frob` built with `--features gauntlet-mutations` under `FROB_TEST_PACKET_PLANT`, each
re-run under its own label.

### Known answers and negative controls
The NS-20 statement is the capability's story; its 18% figure is an illustration in the scenario and is not a
threshold (the threshold on saving is the 7% of GATE-FOUNDRY-05). Negative controls: the ten planted
inconsistencies and the three planted defects. Metamorphic: the order in which sections are supplied does not
change the packet identity; adding an unused card to the catalogue does not change it. Determinism: the same
inputs give the same identity on every platform.

## Considerations

- KILL-PACKET is decided at M5; the reduced profile ships under its own name ("parts list, mass properties and
  STL"), and a capability that fell back is described by the name of the fallback.
- The assembler is where an agent under pressure to "just produce the packet" will look for a bypass. There
  is none in the interface: every precondition is checked on the typed inputs' own records, and the writer
  takes only the assembler's verified value.
- A packet with `no-claim` entries is a valid packet. What is not valid is a packet that omits the entry.
- Review, quotation and first-article inspection are imported records that leave the packet's bytes
  unchanged; until one exists, no text may describe a packet as reviewed.
