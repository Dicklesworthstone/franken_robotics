## Background

Lifecycle compression is Foundry's second Moonshot, in its own crate `frob-lifecycle` behind the feature
`lifecycle` (ADR-FOUNDRY-12: Moonshots stay off the critical path and out of the build packet's dependency
graph; INV-FOUNDRY-07 forbids the packet from depending on this crate).

**The question it answers.** Whether the design that passes today still passes after months of its duty
cycle; which component reaches its limit first, and when; what maintenance interval that implies; and what a
lighter link costs in service duration.

Simulating months directly is out of reach: at 500 Hz, 1,000 hours is 1.8 × 10⁹ steps (FACT, arithmetic). But
wear, damage and ageing change slowly compared with a gait cycle. **Method.** The state splits into fast x and
slow z with ẋ = f(x, z, u) and ż = ε·g(x, z). Two-timescale averaging advances z with macro steps and
estimates the averaged rate ⟨g⟩ from short micro windows at fixed z: a settling phase with a periodicity
residual, then about twenty gait cycles of averaging (TARGET), or for a mixed duty an ergodic sample of the
mission profile with its confidence interval. Macro steps use step doubling for error control.

**Ceiling, stated before any test.** A run of 100 macro steps with three micro evaluations each, of 30 gait
cycles at 0.5 s, simulates 2.25 × 10⁶ steps for 1,000 hours, a ratio near 800 in simulated steps (DESIGN,
illustrative). It is a count of steps, not of wall time and not of field experience. Every output is class
Estimated (INV-FOUNDRY-06), and the laws that define g are empirical with stated validity domains.

Gate (TARGET): GATE-FOUNDRY-12, claim Fidelity [M]: a 100-hour reference (1.8 × 10⁸ steps) compressed at least
50-fold in steps with at most 5% error on every slow state; the planted backlash-induced gait collapse
caught. The gate register places it at M6 (the chapter's own table says M8; the register governs). It reads as
Fidelity: a compressed run agrees with a reference within a stated error; it is not a performance claim.

## Scope

In: the crate; the fast/slow interface; the micro window (settling, averaging, mixed-duty sampling); the macro
integrator with step doubling and step-size control; step accounting; the run record; the toy systems used as
known answers.
Out: the laws g for wear, fatigue, insulation and cells (N31a-48); return to full resolution and rare
overloads (N31a-49); outputs, aged variants, the verb and the 100-hour gate measurement (N31a-50).

## Technical approach

1. **Crate.** `crates/frob-lifecycle/` (layer 5, cap 6 thousand lines), depending on `frob-core`,
   `frob-model`, the simulation verbs' library interface, and `frob-fsim` with group `spectral` (for the
   monodromy analysis used by N31a-49). `frob-foundry` does not depend on it; it may read Foundry's records
   (gauge definitions, spectra) as data. Modules: `split`, `micro`, `macro_step`, `laws/`, `domain`,
   `resolve`, `rare`, `outputs`. It re-exports Foundry's class ceiling type: nothing it returns is above
   Estimated.
2. **Interface** (`split.rs`, DESIGN):
   ```rust
   pub trait SlowLaw {                       // one per slow state family
       fn rate(&self, window: &MicroWindow) -> Result<RateEstimate, Refusal>;   // averaged ż over the window
       fn apply(&self, z: &SlowState, model: &mut ModelVariant);                // how z enters the fast model
       fn domain(&self) -> ValidityDomain;
   }
   pub struct SlowState { values: Vec<f64>, names: Vec<SlowName>, units: Vec<Unit> }
   pub struct RateEstimate { mean: Vec<f64>, half_width: Vec<f64>, cycles: u32, residual: f64 }
   ```
3. **Micro window** (`micro.rs`). At a fixed slow state z: build the per-world model variant with z applied;
   run the design's duty on fixed world addresses.
   - *Periodic duty* (a steady gait of period T): a settling phase of up to 10 cycles, ended when the
     periodicity residual r = ‖x(t + T) − x(t)‖/‖x(t)‖ (on a declared set of state channels, sampled at a
     fixed phase) is below 10⁻³ for two consecutive cycles; then an averaging phase of 20 cycles (TARGET).
     Each law integrates its own rate over the averaging phase; the estimate is the time average of the
     *rate*, never a rate evaluated at averaged inputs. Half-width from batch means over the 20 cycles.
     If settling fails within 10 cycles the window reports `not-periodic`, which is a trigger for full
     resolution.
   - *Mixed duty*: an ergodic sample of the mission profile: tasks drawn by duty fraction from a registered
     stream; each sampled task contributes its rate weighted by duration; the estimate is the weighted mean
     with a confidence interval across samples (default 95%).
4. **Macro step** (`macro_step.rs`). From z_n with step H:
   - G₀ = ⟨g⟩(z_n); the Euler proposal z_E = z_n + H·G₀;
   - G₁ = ⟨g⟩(z_n + ½·H·G₀); two half steps give z₂ = z_n + ½·H·G₀ + ½·H·G₁;
   - error estimate e = ‖z₂ − z_E‖ in the componentwise-relative norm; accepted value z_{n+1} = 2·z₂ − z_E
     (which is the midpoint rule, second order);
   - a third evaluation G₂ = ⟨g⟩(z_{n+1}) is the consistency check (it becomes G₀ of the next step).
   Accept when e ≤ tol (default 1%; DESIGN) and the micro half-widths are below tol; otherwise halve H and
   retry. After an accepted step, H grows by at most a factor 2 when e < tol/4. H is also capped so that no
   slow state moves by more than 10% of its threshold distance in one step.
5. **Step accounting.** The run record counts simulated fast steps (settling plus averaging, every micro
   evaluation, including rejected macro steps) and reports the compression ratio = (horizon ÷ fast step) ÷
   (simulated fast steps), with the words "ratio of simulated steps; not wall time; not field experience".
   The illustrative count: 100 × 3 × 30 × 0.5 s × 500 Hz = 2.25 × 10⁶ steps against 1.8 × 10⁹, a ratio of 800.
6. **Run record** (`frob.lifecycle.run/1`): per macro step z, H, the three rate estimates with half-widths,
   e, accept or reject, triggers raised; totals; class Estimated; determinism: world addresses fixed per
   micro window, so a run is reproducible on one backend.
7. **Toy systems for known answers** (host-only implementations of the same traits): the averaged-growth
   oscillator ż = ε·a·x², x = sin ωt; the forced oscillator with slow wear of the pilot.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Averaged growth: for ż = ε·a·x² with x = sin ωt, the compressed run gives z(T) = ε·a·T/2 over whole periods within 5% at a compression of at least 50 in steps; with exact micro averaging over whole periods the value is exact to rounding.
- [ ] The illustrative step count is reproduced by the accounting: 100 macro steps × 3 evaluations × 30 cycles × 250 steps = 2.25 × 10⁶, ratio 800 against 1.8 × 10⁹.
- [ ] Step doubling: on ż = ε·z the error estimate tracks the true local error within a factor of 2, and the accepted solution has second-order convergence (error ÷ 4 when H is halved).
- [ ] A micro window that does not settle within 10 cycles reports `not-periodic` and no rate.
- [ ] Mixed duty returns a weighted mean with a confidence interval whose empirical coverage on a `synthetic` two-task profile is within 3 points of 95% over 1,000 replications.
- [ ] No rate is computed from averaged inputs: the interface gives a law the window's samples, and a law that requests pre-averaged channels does not compile.
- [ ] Every output carries class Estimated; the crate cannot be linked from `frob-foundry` (the firewall test).
- [ ] The compression ratio is always printed with its qualifying words.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-lifecycle split micro macro_step`:
- `averaged_growth_closed_form`: ε = 10⁻⁴, a = 2, ω = 2π rad/s, T = 1,000 s: z = 10⁻⁴ × 2 × 1,000/2 = 0.1.
- `mean_of_sin_squared_is_half` over whole periods; `partial_period_bias_detected`: the mean of sin² over a window of length τ from phase zero is ½ − sin(2·ω·τ)/(4·ω·τ); at 20.125 periods that is 0.4% below ½, and the batch-means half-width must cover it.
- `midpoint_from_step_doubling`: z₂ and z_E combine to z_n + H·G₁.
- `second_order_convergence`; `error_estimate_tracks_truth`.
- `step_halves_on_reject_and_doubles_on_easy`.
- `threshold_distance_cap`.
- `settling_detects_periodicity`; `not_periodic_reported`.
- `mixed_duty_weighted_mean_and_ci_coverage`.
- `step_accounting_counts_rejected_steps`; `illustrative_ratio_800`.
- `arithmetic_1_8e9`: 500 × 3,600 × 1,000 = 1.8 × 10⁹; 100 h gives 1.8 × 10⁸.
- `firewall_foundry_cannot_link_lifecycle`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `average-inputs-then-apply-law` (the registered shortcut; caught on the two-temperature case), `drop-rejected-steps-from-count` (inflates the ratio), `euler-without-doubling` (first-order convergence fails the test).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_lifecycle.sh` (bead N31a-59): `frob foundry lifecycle --fixture averaged-growth --json`
asserting z(T) within 5% and compression at least 50; logs per macro step z, H, e and triggers, the simulated
step count and the ratio with its wording.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-750 (averaged growth: z(T) = ε·a·T/2 over whole periods; tolerance 5% at a compression of 50 in steps; the
plan states no negative control; registered here: averaging over a non-integer number of periods without a
half-width, which is caught by the bias test). Arithmetic: 1.8 × 10⁹; 2.25 × 10⁶; 800. Negative controls: the
three planted defects. Metamorphic: scaling ε by k and the horizon by 1/k leaves z unchanged; splitting a run
into two consecutive runs gives the same final z within tolerance. Determinism: identical records twice on
one backend.

## Considerations

- KILL-LIFECYCLE (M6): trigger GATE-FOUNDRY-12 fails. Fallback: aged model variants at hand-specified wear
  states, with no time axis and no compression claim (N31a-50).
- Averaging theory needs a separation of time scales and a fast system that is periodic or ergodic at frozen
  z. Where either fails, the method must return to full resolution; that is its own bead, and it is half of
  the gate.
- Micro windows cannot see rare events; they enter as rate times damage per event from another subsystem.
- The count of steps is the only thing compressed. Wall time depends on the tier and the batch; a ratio of 800
  in steps is not a statement about hours of computing or about years in the field.
