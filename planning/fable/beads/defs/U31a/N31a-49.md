## Background

Two-timescale averaging replaces months of simulation by a few short windows. It is valid while the fast
system, at a frozen slow state, settles to a periodic or ergodic regime and the slow state changes little per
cycle. A robot's gait can stop satisfying that: as backlash grows the gait's stability margin shrinks, and at
some wear state the gait fails. An averaging method that does not notice will extrapolate smoothly through a
collapse.

The plan's rule: **return to full resolution when a Floquet multiplier of the gait orbit approaches the unit
circle, when the macro error estimate fails, or when the slow rate is no longer small.**

A *Floquet multiplier* is an eigenvalue of the monodromy matrix, the linearization of one gait cycle's return
map; the periodic gait is stable while all non-trivial multipliers lie inside the unit circle. A multiplier
approaching modulus one announces loss of stability before it happens.

The second rule concerns what short windows cannot contain: **rare overloads are invisible to short windows:
their damage enters as rate times damage per event, with rates from the adversarial-worlds subsystem.** A
stumble once an hour will not occur in twenty gait cycles, yet with fatigue exponent 3 a thousand cycles at
five times the amplitude carry as much damage as an eighth of a million ordinary cycles.

GATE-FOUNDRY-12's second clause is this bead's: "the planted backlash-induced gait collapse caught". The
negative control of the wear-feedback known answer says the same: a planted gait collapse at β* must force
full resolution before β*.

## Scope

In: the three triggers and their thresholds; how the multiplier is obtained inside a micro window; what "full
resolution" means and how the run re-enters compression; the rare-event term; the planted-collapse fixture;
the trigger log.
Out: the averaging core and macro error estimate themselves (N31a-47); the laws (N31a-48); computing gait
orbits and multipliers in general (seed bead BD-333); estimating rare-event rates (seed bead BD-276).

## Technical approach

1. **Trigger A: stability margin** (`resolve.rs`). In each micro window of a periodic duty, after settling,
   estimate the monodromy matrix of the gait's return map on a reduced set of fast coordinates (DESIGN: the
   base's lateral and sagittal velocity, roll and pitch and their rates, at a fixed gait phase) by
   central finite differences of the return map over one cycle from seeded perturbations on fixed world
   addresses (2 × 6 short rollouts, batched as variants of the window). The sibling's monodromy analysis
   returns the multipliers. Let ρ be the largest modulus among non-trivial multipliers.
   - ρ ≥ 0.9 (DESIGN threshold): *approaching*. Macro steps are capped so that the linear extrapolation of ρ
     over the next step stays below 0.97.
   - ρ ≥ 0.97, or the extrapolation reaches 1 within the next step: *return to full resolution*.
   For a mixed duty with no single orbit, the trigger uses the failure fraction of the sampled tasks instead:
   any task failure in a window that had none at the previous slow state is a return.
2. **Trigger B: macro error.** The step-doubling error estimate exceeds tolerance at the minimum step size
   H_min (DESIGN: ten micro windows' worth of simulated time), so the stepper cannot halve further. Return.
3. **Trigger C: slow rate no longer small.** For any slow state, the change over one gait cycle exceeds 1% of
   its current value or of its threshold distance (DESIGN): the separation of time scales is gone. Return.
   Also raised when a micro window reports `not-periodic`.
4. **Full resolution.** The run switches to direct simulation of the duty with the slow laws integrated
   step by step (every law's rate applied each control tick, slow states updated in the model variant at
   episode boundaries, since slow model parameters advance between episodes, never within a step). It
   continues until either the run ends (a threshold or the horizon is reached, or the task fails), or all
   three triggers have been clear for a stretch of 50 gait cycles (DESIGN), after which compression resumes
   from the current slow state. Every switch is recorded with its trigger and the slow state.
5. **A failure found at full resolution is an output**, not an error: "at backlash β = … on joint … the gait
   loses stability; time … with interval …". It is a component reaching its limit, of a kind no threshold on
   the card would have named.
6. **Rare overloads** (`rare.rs`). For each rare event class e supplied with a rate λ_e (events per hour of
   duty, with its bound) and a set of recorded example episodes: compute the damage per event d_e for each
   affected slow state by running the laws on the examples (fatigue damage of the event's stress history per
   gauge, including the cycle it closes against the ordinary duty envelope; wear and insulation increments),
   as mean and spread across examples. The macro rate gains the term Σ_e λ_e·d_e. Rates come with class and
   interval from their source; the term's uncertainty is carried into the time-to-threshold interval. With
   no rates supplied, the output says `rare overloads: not included` on every affected result; it is never
   silently zero.
7. **Planted-collapse fixture** (`synthetic`, host-level and one referee-level). A hopping or walking toy
   whose return-map multiplier depends on backlash as ρ(β) = ρ₀ + κ·β with ρ₀ = 0.6 and κ chosen so that
   ρ(β*) = 1 at β* = 0.02 rad, combined with the wear feedback law β̇ = ε·(c₀ + c₁·β). The time t* at which
   β reaches β* follows from the closed form: t* = ln((β* + c₀/c₁)/(β₀ + c₀/c₁))/(ε·c₁).
8. **Trigger log.** Per macro step: ρ, the macro error, the largest per-cycle relative slow change, which
   triggers are raised, the mode (compressed or full), simulated steps in each mode.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-12, second clause: on the planted-collapse fixture the run returns to full resolution before β reaches β*, and reports the collapse at a time within 5% of t*.
- [ ] With trigger A disabled (a planted defect) the compressed run steps past β* and reports no collapse; the test catches it.
- [ ] The estimated multiplier on a linear return map with known eigenvalues 0.5 and 0.8 is within 0.02 of each.
- [ ] Trigger B fires on a fixture whose rate changes discontinuously with z; trigger C fires when ε is raised until a slow state moves 2% per cycle.
- [ ] After a return, compression resumes only after 50 clear cycles, and each switch is in the log.
- [ ] Rare overloads: with ordinary damage rate 10⁻⁶ per hour and an event of damage 10⁻⁵ at 0.2 events per hour, the total rate is 3 × 10⁻⁶ per hour; without supplied rates the result is labelled `rare overloads: not included`.
- [ ] A mixed-duty window in which a task newly fails triggers a return.
- [ ] Slow states are updated in the model only at episode boundaries during full resolution.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-lifecycle resolve rare`:
- `monodromy_estimate_linear_map` (eigenvalues 0.5, 0.8 recovered within 0.02).
- `approaching_caps_macro_step`: with ρ rising 0.05 per step from 0.90 the cap keeps the extrapolated value below 0.97.
- `return_at_0_97`; `resume_after_50_clear_cycles`.
- `macro_error_at_min_step_returns`; `fast_slow_separation_lost_returns`; `not_periodic_returns`.
- `collapse_time_closed_form`: c₀ = 1 × 10⁻³, c₁ = 50, β₀ = 0, β* = 0.02, ε = 10⁻⁴: c₀/c₁ = 2 × 10⁻⁵; t* = ln((0.02 + 0.00002)/0.00002)/(10⁻⁴ × 50) = ln(1001)/0.005 = 1,381.8 s.
- `planted_collapse_caught_before_beta_star`.
- `collapse_missed_without_trigger_a` (planted).
- `rare_event_rate_term`: 10⁻⁶ + 0.2 × 10⁻⁵ = 3 × 10⁻⁶ per hour.
- `rare_event_closes_cycle_against_duty_envelope`: an overload to 300 MPa over a duty envelope of [0, 100] MPa is credited with a 300 MPa range, not 200.
- `no_rates_is_labelled_not_zero`.
- `slow_state_updates_only_between_episodes`.
- `mixed_duty_new_failure_returns`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `ignore-multiplier`, `resume-immediately-after-return` (oscillates between modes), `rare-term-silently-zero`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_lifecycle.sh` (bead N31a-59): `frob foundry lifecycle --fixture planted-collapse --json`,
asserting that `result.collapse.time_s` is within 5% of 1,381.8 s, that the mode switched to `full` before
β = 0.02 rad, and that the trigger named is the multiplier; logs per macro step ρ, the mode, the triggers, and
the simulated steps in each mode.

### Known answers and negative controls
The control of KA-751 (a planted gait collapse at β* must force full resolution before β*). The closed-form
collapse time 1,381.8 s. The rare-overload arithmetic of the fatigue construction (KA-169: 10⁶ cycles at an
amplitude of life 2 × 10⁶ give 0.5; 10³ cycles at five times the amplitude add 0.0625). Negative controls: the
three planted defects. Metamorphic: halving ε doubles t* and leaves β at collapse unchanged; adding an event
class with rate zero changes nothing. Determinism: perturbation rollouts are on fixed addresses, so ρ and the
switch points are reproducible on one backend.

## Considerations

- The reduced monodromy is an estimate from finite differences through contact; near a change of contact
  sequence it is noisy. The thresholds 0.9 and 0.97 are DESIGN values to be tuned on the pilot, and trigger C
  and the `not-periodic` report are the backstops.
- Full resolution is expensive; a run that spends most of its horizon there has little compression, and the
  step accounting says so. Meeting the gate's factor of 50 with the collapse fixture present is part of the
  test.
- Rare-event rates are themselves bounded estimates from another subsystem; a lifecycle interval can be no
  tighter than they are.
- KILL-LIFECYCLE fallback removes the time axis, and with it this bead's triggers; aged variants at
  hand-specified wear states can still be checked for gait stability one at a time.
