## Background

Lifecycle compression exists to answer four questions: whether the design that passes today still passes
after months of its duty cycle; which component reaches its limit first, and when; what maintenance interval
that implies; and what a lighter link costs in service duration.

**Outputs:** time to first threshold per component with an interval; aged model variants at stated ages for
the evaluation and adversarial subsystems, as per-world variants in one batch; and a front of mass against
service duration. **Every output is Estimated.**

The aged variants are what make the first question a single run: per-world model variants let one batch hold
the robot as new and as aged at several ages, so "does it still pass at 1,000 hours" is evaluated on the same
worlds, side by side.

The verb is `foundry.lifecycle` (kind `run`; a lifecycle run under the feature `lifecycle`). The registry
dates it M8; the gate register and the milestone plan place the work and its gate at M6, which governs.

GATE-FOUNDRY-12 (Fidelity [M]): a 100-hour reference (1.8 × 10⁸ steps) compressed at least 50-fold in steps
with at most 5% error on every slow state; the planted backlash-induced gait collapse caught. KILL-LIFECYCLE:
if it fails, the fallback is aged model variants at hand-specified wear states, with no time axis and no
compression claim.

## Scope

In: thresholds; time to first threshold and its interval; the maintenance-interval statement; aged variants
and their hand-off; the mass–duration front; the verb's surface; the 100-hour reference run and the gate
record; the fallback mode.
Out: the averaging core, laws and triggers (N31a-47 to N31a-49); evaluation and adversarial search on the
variants (other subsystems); structural variants themselves (the structure loop).

## Technical approach

1. **Thresholds** (`outputs.rs`). Per slow state, from cards or the design: backlash limit per joint
   (controller tolerance or the card's service limit); fatigue D·scatter = 1 per gauge; insulation Λ = 1;
   capacity at 80% of new and resistance at a declared multiple. Plus the dynamic threshold found by the
   full-resolution trigger: loss of gait stability or task failure.
2. **Time to first threshold.** For each component, the time at which its slow state crosses its threshold,
   interpolated within the macro step (linear in the step's rate). The interval combines: the micro windows'
   confidence intervals on rates (propagated by running the macro integration at the lower and upper rate
   envelopes); the macro error estimate; and coefficient uncertainty from the cards where they state one. A
   threshold not reached within the horizon is reported as `> horizon`, never extrapolated. The *first*
   threshold over all components is the headline: component, mechanism, time, interval, the law and its card
   as weakest input.
3. **Maintenance interval.** For components with a serviceable threshold (backlash adjustment, battery
   replacement), the time to that threshold's lower interval end, rounded down to a declared service
   granularity, is offered as "service at or before"; wording is fixed and carries class Estimated and
   `reality: no-claim`.
4. **Aged model variants.** At the requested ages (default 0, ¼, ½ and 1 × horizon) the slow state is written
   as a parameter-variant row: backlash widths per joint, f_Q and f_R, and any derating the laws imply; each
   variant has its own `ModelId` with provenance `aged: <hours> under <law set>`. The verb returns the variant
   set as an object that `eval.run` and `nemesis.search` accept, so that all ages run as per-world variants
   in one batch on the same world addresses. Fatigue damage and insulation consumption do not change the
   fast model and are carried as annotations.
5. **Mass against service duration.** Given a set of structural candidates for a link class (the beam-rung
   stock sizes around the chosen one, or topology results at several stress caps), run the lifecycle for each
   with the fatigue law on its own gauges and influence matrix, and report (mass, time to first threshold)
   pairs with intervals, marking the non-dominated ones. Candidates share world addresses. The output states
   the number of lifecycle runs it cost.
6. **Verb.**
   `frob foundry lifecycle <design> --duty <profile> --horizon-h <H> [--ages …] [--laws wear,fatigue,insulation,cell]
   [--structural-candidates <set>] [--rare-rates <file>] [--strict-data] [--json]`.
   Kind `run`; idempotency key and budget required; bounded synchronous wait, otherwise a job handle (exit 8).
   Output: the threshold table; the first threshold; aged variants; the front when requested; the run record
   with step accounting and the trigger log; labels (`rare overloads: not included`, `cell ageing: user-set
   constants`, `loads: declared`); evidence block with class Estimated and an empty claims list. Refusals:
   FROB-REF-831 (a law outside its validity domain, or cited above Estimated), the card refusal under strict
   data, FROB-REF-169 if a micro window's traces are incomplete, and the feature-absent refusal (exit 5) when
   the binary lacks `lifecycle`.
7. **The gate run** (`tests/gate_foundry_12.rs` and the script). Reference: direct simulation of 100 hours of
   the fixture duty at 500 Hz, 1.8 × 10⁸ steps, with all four laws integrated every tick (a long job on the
   CPU tiers, run once per gate recording and stored with its digest). Compressed: the same duty and laws
   through the averaging core. Measurements: simulated steps (must be at most 3.6 × 10⁶, a ratio of at least
   50); relative error of every slow state at 100 hours and at 25, 50 and 75 hours (each at most 5%); and the
   planted-collapse fixture's outcome. The fixture duty is chosen so that at least one slow state moves by
   more than 10% of its threshold in 100 hours (otherwise a 5% relative error is trivially met); the record
   states each state's movement.
8. **Fallback mode (KILL-LIFECYCLE).** `--mode hand-specified`: the caller supplies wear states (backlash per
   joint, f_Q, f_R); the verb returns the corresponding model variants for evaluation, with no time axis, no
   thresholds in time and no compression figure; the output says `lifecycle: hand-specified wear states`.
   With the kill switch set this is the only mode.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-12: on the gate fixture the compressed run uses at most 3.6 × 10⁶ simulated steps against the 1.8 × 10⁸ of the reference (ratio at least 50) and every slow state is within 5% of the reference at 25, 50, 75 and 100 hours; measured ratio and errors are recorded.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-12: the planted backlash-induced gait collapse is caught (bead N31a-49's measurement, recorded here).
- [ ] At least one slow state of the gate fixture moves by more than 10% of its threshold over the 100 hours, and the record shows each state's movement.
- [ ] Time to first threshold is reported with an interval that contains the reference crossing time on the wear-feedback fixture.
- [ ] A threshold beyond the horizon is reported as `> horizon`.
- [ ] Aged variants at four ages run in one evaluation batch on the same world addresses, and the per-variant results show the new robot and the aged ones side by side.
- [ ] The mass–duration front on three stock walls reports three points with intervals and marks the non-dominated ones.
- [ ] Every number carries class Estimated; the verb's schema has no field named `life`; the compression ratio carries its qualifying words.
- [ ] `--mode hand-specified` returns variants and no time axis; with the kill switch set, a time-axis request is refused naming the fallback.
- [ ] Without the `lifecycle` feature the verb refuses with exit 5.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-lifecycle outputs` and `--test gate_foundry_12`:
- `threshold_crossing_interpolated`: z from 0.8 to 1.1 of threshold over a step of 100 h crosses at 66.7 h.
- `interval_from_rate_envelopes`: rate 1.0 ± 0.1 per 1,000 h to a threshold of 1 gives 1,000 h with interval [909, 1,111] h.
- `beyond_horizon_not_extrapolated`.
- `first_threshold_is_minimum_over_components`.
- `aged_variant_rows`: backlash widths, f_Q and f_R at each age; provenance string; distinct `ModelId`s.
- `variants_share_world_addresses`.
- `front_marks_non_dominated`: (0.10 kg, 800 h), (0.12 kg, 1,500 h), (0.14 kg, 1,400 h): the third is dominated.
- `gate_compression_and_error` (long; gate lane only).
- `gate_fixture_moves_enough`.
- `hand_specified_mode_has_no_time_axis`; `kill_switch_forces_fallback`.
- `feature_absent_refuses_exit_5`.
- `schema_has_no_life_field`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `extrapolate-past-horizon`, `variants-on-fresh-worlds` (ages are not paired), `report-ratio-as-speedup` (a message says "800 times faster"; the wording lint fails).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_lifecycle.sh` (bead N31a-59) is this capability's script: the known-answer fixtures, the
gate fixture against the stored reference, the aged-variant batch, the fallback mode.

### Known answers and negative controls
Arithmetic: 100 h × 3,600 s × 500 Hz = 1.8 × 10⁸; ÷ 50 = 3.6 × 10⁶. The interval example 1/1.1 and 1/0.9 of
1,000 h. KA-750 to KA-753 through the beads that carry them. Negative controls: the three planted defects.
Metamorphic: doubling the horizon never moves an already-reported crossing time; adding an age to the list
changes no other variant. Determinism: reproducible on one backend from the world addresses.

## Considerations

- The 100-hour reference is 1.8 × 10⁸ steps of a full simulation with traces: hours of machine time on CPU
  tiers. It is run when the gate is recorded and its result is stored; the routine lane compares against the
  stored reference and re-runs a 1-hour slice directly.
- A 5% error on a slow state that barely moves means nothing; the movement requirement is what gives the gate
  teeth.
- The front costs one lifecycle run per candidate. It answers "what does a lighter link cost in service
  duration", and it is the only place where structure and lifecycle meet.
- Nothing here is a warranty or a predicted failure date; outputs are times to a threshold under named laws,
  class Estimated, `reality: no-claim`.
