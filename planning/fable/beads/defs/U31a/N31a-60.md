## Background

Foundry sizes links on two rungs (ADR-FOUNDRY-02): **closed-form beam sizing by default and as fallback;
three-dimensional topology optimization for finalists.** The reason given for the decision: the beam rung costs
milliseconds, serves the structural-margin analysis A-15, and is the engineered baseline every optimized part
is judged against. One rung only (topology optimization everywhere) was rejected: it is CPU-hours for 12 to 20
links, it leaves nothing to compare a saving with, and it leaves no fallback if the topology rung is killed
(KILL-TOPOPT: beam-rung sizing then supplies mass and inertia feedback).

The beam rung as the plan states it: each link is idealized between its ports as a thin-walled tube or a
rectangular section. For a tube of mean radius r and wall t: area 2π·r·t, second moment π·r³·t, section modulus
π·r²·t, torsion constant 2π·r³·t; stress σ = N/A ± M/Z and τ = T/(2π·r²·t); the check is
√(σ² + 3·τ²) ≤ S_y/SF (S_y the yield strength, SF the safety factor); Euler buckling P_cr = π²·E·I/(K·L)²;
local wall buckling with a card knock-down; and a first bending frequency from the beam formula with end
masses. Sizing picks the lightest stock size that passes every envelope case.

Two beads already hold the closed-form kernel: the member-sizing kernel delivered early for the screening
check (one wrench at the distal port, checked at the root) and seed bead BD-401 (the verb `foundry.size`,
analysis A-15, the finite-element form). Neither says how the internal forces N, V, M and T along a member
follow from an *envelope case*. An envelope case is not a tip wrench. It is one instant of one episode
(ADR-FOUNDRY-03: peaks from different instants are never assembled) with a wrench at every port **and the
rigid-body acceleration field** a(x) = a₀ + α × x + ω × (ω × x), gravity folded into a₀, acting on the link's
own mass. A leg link in swing is loaded mostly by that field; a link with three ports carries a wrench
part-way along; a link accelerated sideways has its largest bending moment in mid-span, where a root-only
check sees nothing. Nor do the two beads say how a rectangular section is checked in torsion and biaxial
bending. This bead supplies the load model and the section checks; its companion (N31a-61) builds sizing,
fatigue at notches and the baseline on it.

## Scope

In: the member model of a link; the mass layout along the member; section forces at a station from one
`LinkLoad`; closure against the free-body check; the linear form in the load coordinates; the station set;
section checks for the tube and the rectangle; member checks (column buckling, first bending frequency with
end masses); the option `--diagram` of `foundry.size`. For a campaign load set this model replaces the
reduction BD-401 starts with (each envelope case presented to the kernel as the wrench at the distal port and
checked at the root): after this bead `foundry.size` evaluates every case at every station.
Out: the section properties, the four criteria for a tip wrench, the stock search with its tie rule and the
exact decisions (the early kernel N31b-38, a dependency: this bead reuses them and gives no second definition);
the verb itself, its load-set reader, the value of the frequency floor it passes, the A-15 margin and the
finite-element form (BD-401, a dependency); stock sizing under campaign loads, fatigue at notches, the baseline
record and the saving (N31a-61, which depends on this bead);
envelope selection (N31a-10); the free-body check (N31a-07); card fields (N31a-22); the finite-element check
(N31a-19, N31a-20).

## Technical approach

Modules `beam/{member,diagram,rect}.rs` in `frob-foundry`, beside the kernel's modules.

1. **Member** (DESIGN). Ports of the class: p₀ (the proximal joint port, which is also the restraint port of
   the unit-load basis) and p₁ … p_{P−1}. The member runs from x_A = r_{p₀} to x_B = r_{p_e}, where the end
   port p_e is the link's `member_end` declaration or, absent one, the port farthest from p₀ (ties: lowest
   port identifier). L = ‖x_B − x_A‖; e = (x_B − x_A)/L; ŷ is the link-frame axis least aligned with e (ties:
   the first of x, y, z), made orthogonal to e; ẑ = e × ŷ. A station is s ∈ [0, L] with x(s) = x_A + s·e.
   Every other port p attaches at s_p = clamp((r_p − x_A)·e, 0, L) through a rigid arm d_p = r_p − x(s_p).
   The free span is [s₀, s₁], between the two boss faces. A class is *beam-like* when L is at least four
   times the larger section dimension and no arm exceeds L/2; otherwise the result is
   `NotBeamLike { reason }` (a torso plate, a hub) and the beam rung makes no statement about that class.
2. **Mass along the member.** The body load uses the mass of the model that produced the sample (the
   `ModelId` in the `LinkLoad`), never a candidate section's: a candidate's mass belongs to the next
   iteration of the loads–mass loop, and mixing the two would be a load state no robot was in. The model's
   link mass m with centre of mass c is laid out as (a) a line mass μ = ρ·A_model over the free span, for the
   section the fill currently declares; (b) one lump (m_j, x_j, optional inertia I_j) per other entry of the
   mass roll-up, attached at s_j like a port; (c) a remainder lump m_r = m − μ·(s₁ − s₀) − Σ m_j, placed so
   that total mass and centre of mass equal the model's exactly. A remainder below −1% of m means roll-up
   and model disagree: `NotBeamLike { reason: mass-roll-up }`.
3. **Section forces at station s**: the force and moment that the part of the link distal of the cut exerts
   on the cut face whose outward normal is +e, which is the sum of the external loads on that part. With
   ℓ_s the length of line mass beyond s, x_m its midpoint and g = α × e + ω × (ω × e) the gradient of a along
   the axis:
   - R⁺(s) = Σ_{p: s_p > s} f_p − μ·ℓ_s·a(x_m) − Σ_{j: s_j > s} m_j·a(x_j);
   - Q⁺(s) = Σ_{p: s_p > s} [(r_p − x(s)) × f_p + m_p] − μ·[ℓ_s·(x_m − x(s)) × a(x_m) + (ℓ_s³/12)·e × g]
     − Σ_{j: s_j > s} [(x_j − x(s)) × m_j·a(x_j) + I_j·α + ω × (I_j·ω)],
   where (f_p, m_p) is the wrench on the link at port p about r_p. The line terms are exact because a(x) is
   affine in x. Components: N = R⁺·e (tension positive), V_y = R⁺·ŷ, V_z = R⁺·ẑ, T = Q⁺·e, M_y = Q⁺·ŷ,
   M_z = Q⁺·ẑ; V = √(V_y² + V_z²), M = √(M_y² + M_z²). The axial stress at the section point (y, z) is
   σ_x = N/A + M_y·z/I_y − M_z·y/I_z. Rotary inertia of the cross-section is neglected (order (r/L)²); the
   bound μ·r²·ℓ_s·(‖α‖ + ‖ω‖²) on the neglected moment is reported per case.
4. **Closure.** At s = 0 the distal part is the whole link less p₀, so R⁺(0) + f_{p₀} is the force residual
   of the free-body check, exactly (the layout reproduces m and c). The moment closure differs from the
   free-body one by the inertia the line-and-lump layout does not reproduce; when that difference exceeds 2%
   of the governing bending moment the class is labelled `beam: coarse`, and above 10% it is
   `NotBeamLike { reason: inertia }` (DESIGN thresholds).
5. **Linear form.** R⁺ and Q⁺ are linear in the load coordinates ℓ of the link (six per port other than p₀,
   then a₀, α and the six products ω_i·ω_j; 18 for two ports, 24 for three), because ω × (ω × x) =
   (ω·ωᵀ − ‖ω‖²·I)·x. One 6 × n matrix B(s) per station is assembled per class and model; the forces of a case
   are B(s)·ℓ, and a history at a section point is a row times the campaign's sample stream.
6. **Stations.** s₀ and s₁; both sides of every port and lump station inside the span; every declared notch
   station; and 16 equal sub-stations per span between consecutive point loads, because the body load can put
   the largest moment in mid-span. Each sub-station's M is increased by h²·μ·a_max/8 (h the sub-station
   spacing, a_max the largest ‖a‖ on the span), which bounds what sampling can miss: 1/256 of the peak for a
   uniform load.
7. **Section checks**, each a utilization u that passes when u ≤ 1, per case and station.
   *Tube* (A = 2π·r·t, I = π·r³·t, Z = π·r²·t, J = 2π·r³·t): at the extreme fibre σ = |N|/A + M/Z,
   τ = |T|/(2π·r²·t), u = √(σ² + 3·τ²)/(S_y/SF); at the neutral axis (DESIGN addition, since the plan's check
   omits transverse shear) σ = |N|/A, τ = |T|/(2π·r²·t) + 2·V/A; wall buckling σ_c = max(0, −N/A + M/Z)
   against η·E·t/(r·√(3·(1 − ν²))), η the card's knock-down factor.
   *Rectangle* b along ŷ by h along ẑ, with ẑ set by `section_up` (required; without it the family is not
   offered for the class): A = b·h, I_y = b·h³/12, I_z = h·b³/12, Z_y = b·h²/6, Z_z = h·b²/6. With a and c
   half the long and half the short side (DESIGN: the standard closed forms for a solid rectangle),
   J = a·c³·[16/3 − 3.36·(c/a)·(1 − c⁴/(12·a⁴))] and the largest torsional shear, at the middle of the long
   faces, τ_T = (3·|T|/(8·a·c²))·[1 + 0.6095·(c/a) + 0.8865·(c/a)² − 1.8023·(c/a)³ + 0.9100·(c/a)⁴]. Points
   checked: the corners, σ = |N|/A + |M_y|/Z_y + |M_z|/Z_z with τ = 0; the faces z = ±h/2,
   σ = |N|/A + |M_y|/Z_y with τ = τ_T + 1.5·|V_y|/A; the faces y = ±b/2, σ = |N|/A + |M_z|/Z_z with
   τ = τ_T + 1.5·|V_z|/A (τ_T is the long-face value, used on both: conservative).
8. **Member checks**, per case. Column buckling: u = SF_b·N_c/P_cr, with N_c the largest compressive −N along
   the span, P_cr = π²·E·I_min/(K·L)², K as the kernel sets it (1 for pinned ends, 2 for a cantilever, 2 when
   undeclared) and SF_b = 2, the factor the independent check requires. First bending frequency, with the
   boundary and end masses of the modal check (proximal boss held; reflected mass m_tip at the end port):
   f₁ = (1/2π)·√(3·E·I_min/((m_tip + (33/140)·m_b)·L_f³)), m_b the member's mass and L_f = s₁ − s₀. The
   one-mode value is an upper bound on the true frequency (1.5% high with no end mass), so the test is
   f₁/1.015 ≥ f_floor, with f_floor = max(2 × actuator bandwidth, 5 × f₉₉) as in the modal check (f₉₉ is the
   highest frequency carrying 99% of load power). The same test, written u = 1.015·f_floor/f₁ ≤ 1, is the
   kernel's frequency criterion (N31b-38), and the same floor is the one BD-401's verb passes (2 × actuator
   bandwidth alone for a `statics` load set, which has no spectra): DESIGN, one margin and one floor in the
   three beads, so that the root check and the member check cannot disagree on the definition.
9. **Decisions, class, determinism.** Recorded loads are binary fractions; forces and utilizations are
   evaluated with outward-rounded intervals, a check passes only when the upper bound is at most 1, and
   `at-limit` (1 inside the interval) counts as not passing. Results are class Estimated and inherit
   `model-conditional`, `provisional` and `loads: declared` from their cases. Evaluation order is fixed:
   identical bits twice on one machine and across worker counts.
10. **Surface.** `foundry.size --diagram` returns, per class and case, N, V_y, V_z, T, M_y, M_z at every
    station, the governing station and point, the closure residuals and the labels (schema
    `frob.foundry.beam.diagram/1`). A nominal section stress at a boss face is what a hand calculation of a
    cantilever root evaluates; it is reported as `nominal` and is not the local stress field near an
    interface, which stays `no-claim` within one boss diameter in the finite-element check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A tip force of 600 N across a 0.3 m member gives V = 600 N and M = 180 N·m at the root, equal to the kernel's values.
- [ ] The self-weight, spin, angular-acceleration, offset-port and mid-span fixtures reproduce the hand values of the test plan to 10⁻⁹ relative.
- [ ] B(s)·ℓ equals the direct evaluation of step 3 on 10³ seeded loads to 10⁻¹² relative.
- [ ] R⁺(0) + f_{p₀} equals the free-body force residual bit for bit on every sample of the reference campaign.
- [ ] Forces are computed only from one `LinkLoad`; no function takes N from one case and M from another.
- [ ] The rectangle values are reproduced: corner stress 27.5 MPa; torsion 2.037 MPa per N·m for 20 × 10 mm and 4.811 MPa for 10 × 10 mm.
- [ ] A plate-like link returns `NotBeamLike` with its reason; a link whose mass roll-up exceeds its model mass by more than 1% returns `NotBeamLike { reason: mass-roll-up }`.
- [ ] `frob foundry size --diagram --json` returns the diagram with closure residuals for every beam-like class of the reference quadruped.

## Considerations

- A straight prismatic member is a coarse picture of a real link: no taper, no curvature, no joints. The
  closure labels say when it is too coarse; if more than a third of link classes in the design corpus are
  `NotBeamLike`, a frame idealization should be considered.
- The neutral-axis point, the rectangle's torsion formula and the 1.015 margin are DESIGN additions to the
  plan's tube check; each makes a check stricter, never looser.
- KILL-LOADS: with hand-declared load sets a case still has port wrenches and a body field, so the diagram is
  unchanged; only the label `loads: declared` is added.
- Cost: stations × cases × a 6 × n product; well under a millisecond per class.
