## Background

The beam rung is the first of Foundry's two sizing rungs (ADR-FOUNDRY-02). The plan's rule for it: **sizing
picks the lightest stock size that passes every envelope case and the fatigue utilization at declared
notches.** It names three jobs for the result: it answers the structural-margin analysis A-15, it **is the
engineered baseline** every optimized part is judged against, and it is **the fallback of KILL-TOPOPT**: if
the topology rung is killed, beam-rung sizing supplies the mass and inertia that the structure loop feeds back
into the model.

The baseline is what gives a saving its meaning. Reported savings of topology optimization against engineered
parts are 7 to 30% of link mass; against primitive shapes the saving is larger and means nothing, because
nobody ships a solid capsule. Bet BET-29 is falsified if the optimized part is less than 7% lighter than the
engineered part *at equal stiffness and fatigue margin*; the structure-loop pilot is falsified by a saving
under 5% against a hand-sized tube *at equal first bending frequency and equal fatigue utilization*; and
GATE-FOUNDRY-05 (claim: Design comparison; TARGET) requires a saving of at least 7%, with 15% the target, the
convergence test passing within five iterations and a coarse solve of at most 120 s per link class. In every
one of these the denominator is this bead's output, so its definition has to be fixed before any optimized
part exists. Two ways of manufacturing a saving are closed here: a baseline rounded up to the next stock
size, and a comparison in which the optimized part is allowed to be floppier or closer to its fatigue limit
than the tube.

Fatigue follows ADR-FOUNDRY-06: it is reported as utilization under a named empirical law with card
provenance, class Estimated, and the word "life" is not used for a result. Signed stress only; residues are
carried between episodes and the counter is finished once.

The section forces along a member, the section checks and the member checks come from bead N31a-60. The
loop's rung interface is `Rung::size(class, loads, previous, job) → Candidate { geometry, baseline,
provenance }`; this bead is its `beam` implementation.

## Technical approach

Modules `beam/{notch,rung,baseline}.rs` in `frob-foundry`.

1. **Gauges in beam form.** Each declared notch: station s_n, section point (y_n, z_n) on the perimeter,
   fatigue notch factor K_f. So that a baseline always has a fatigue number, default gauges are added at both
   boss faces with K_f = 1 and the label `nominal` (DESIGN): eight points at 45° spacing for a tube (y = r·cos θ,
   z = r·sin θ); the four corners and four face centres for a rectangle. That is 16 default gauges per section.
2. **Gauge row.** With B_R(s) and B_Q(s) the force and moment blocks of the section-force matrix B(s), and A,
   I_y, I_z the properties of the trial section: h = K_f·[eᵀ·B_R(s_n)/A + (z_n·ŷᵀ/I_y − y_n·ẑᵀ/I_z)·B_Q(s_n)].
   For a load-coordinate sample ℓ the value h·ℓ is the axial stress at the gauge, signed (tension positive),
   of type `SignedStress`. Rows are appended to the class's gauge matrix so that one pass over the campaign
   stream serves them with the finite-element gauges.
3. **Counting and utilization.** One counter per (gauge, limb instance, contact variant), fed in duty order
   across all episodes, residues carried, finished once. Damage D over H_c campaign hours gives
   U_f = (D/H_c)·H for the declared service duration H, against a limit of 1 divided by the card's scatter
   factor. The section's result is the largest U_f over gauges, instances and variants; instances are never
   concatenated. Without a curve valid for the part's material state, process and finish, or with declared
   load sets (no spectra), the result is `fatigue: no-claim` with its reason.
4. **Sizing** (`BeamRung::size`):
   1. obtain the member, the mass layout and B(s) of the class; a `NotBeamLike` class returns that outcome
      and the loop keeps its declared fill;
   2. walk the stock card in ascending mass (ties: smaller outer dimension, then card order); for each entry
      evaluate every static check in every envelope case at every station; keep the first four that pass;
   3. one pass over the campaign stream with the gauge rows of those four gives U_f for each; the choice is
      the lightest whose U_f is within the limit;
   4. if none of the four passes, take the next four; after three batches return `no-stock-passes` with the
      nearest entry, its governing criterion and utilization;
   5. with `fatigue: no-claim` the choice is the lightest entry of step 2 and carries the label.
   The envelope set of a class with symmetric limbs is the union over its instances, so one section serves
   the class.
5. **Candidate.** `Candidate { geometry: BeamSection, baseline: the same section, provenance: Beam }`. The
   feedback stage turns the section into mass properties (hollow cylinder or cuboid) and a typed edit, as for
   any other candidate.
6. **Baseline record** (schema `frob.foundry.beam/1`, DESIGN):
   ```rust
   pub struct BeamBaseline {
       class: LinkClassId, family: SectionFamily,          // Tube | Rectangle
       stock: StockSection, m_stock: Mass,                 // what the loop feeds back
       continuous: Option<ContinuousWall>,                 // tube only: t_req and m_cont
       f1: Frequency, fatigue: FatigueOutcome,             // Utilization { u_f, gauge, instance, variant } | NoClaim { reason }
       governing: Governing,                               // case (world address, tick), station, point, criterion, u
       checked: Option<CheckedBaseline>,                   // f1 and U_f of the reference solid from the independent check; mesh hash
       loads: EnvelopeSetId, campaign: CampaignId, card: CardRef, labels: Vec<StructLabel>,
   }
   ```
7. **Continuous wall** (tube family). t_req is the smallest wall, at the chosen entry's mean radius, that
   passes every criterion; every criterion is monotone in the wall at fixed radius, so bisection between the
   process card's minimum wall and the stock wall finds it (to 10⁻⁶ relative). Fatigue needs no new pass: at
   fixed r both N/A and M/Z scale as 1/t, so each counted cycle of the reference entry scales by t_ref/t in
   range and in mean, and D(t) is re-summed from the stored cycle table. m_cont = ρ·2π·r·t_req·L_f, with L_f
   the free length. For the rectangle no continuous reference is defined, and the record says so.
8. **The saving** (DESIGN reading of "equal margin"). Saving = 1 − m_opt/m_ref, with m_ref = m_cont for a tube
   (never heavier than the stock section, so the step between stock sizes cannot inflate the saving) and
   m_stock for a rectangle. The saving against stock is printed beside it and labelled.
9. **Equal margin.** The reference section, as a solid between the frozen port bosses, is run through the
   same independent check as the optimized part (body-fitted mesh, modal model with the same end masses,
   fatigue at gauges); its values are `checked`. The saving counts for a gate only when
   f₁,opt ≥ 0.98·f₁,base and U_f,opt ≤ U_f,base, both from that check. Otherwise the record says
   `not-counted: frequency` or `not-counted: fatigue`. To let the optimizer meet the rule, the comparison
   run of the topology rung receives `EqualMarginTargets { f1_floor: max(f_floor, 0.98·f₁,base),
   fatigue_cap: min(1/scatter, U_f,base) }`. When the baseline's fatigue is `no-claim` the comparison is
   labelled `equal frequency and stress only`.
10. **Cross-check of the idealization.** The closed-form f₁ and nominal stresses of the reference are compared
    with the checked solid's (TARGET: f₁ within 5%; nominal stress outside the boss zones within 10%). A wider
    gap is a finding against the beam picture of that class and is printed with the baseline.
11. **Fallback mode.** With the topology rung unavailable (feature absent, or KILL-TOPOPT in force),
    `foundry.optimize` runs the loop on this rung alone: the candidate is the stock section, the feedback is
    its mass and inertia, the convergence test is unchanged, and the result names the rung. Stock steps make
    the loop's map piecewise constant, which is the cycling case the convergence test detects.
12. **Class, labels, determinism.** Everything is class Estimated. Labels of the governing case and of the
    spectra are inherited (`model-conditional`, `provisional`, `loads: declared`, `damage rate not
    stabilized`). Trial order, batch size and tie rules are fixed; results are bitwise identical twice on one
    machine and across worker counts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sizing returns the lightest stock entry that passes every static check in every envelope case at every station and whose fatigue utilization is within 1/scatter.
- [ ] On the fatigue-governed fixture (walls 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 mm with utilizations 1.555, 0.656, 0.336, 0.194 against a limit of 0.25) the choice is 3.0 mm and the continuous wall is 2.759 mm.
- [ ] With a card lacking a valid curve the choice is the lightest statics-passing entry labelled `fatigue: no-claim`; with a declared load set the same, labelled `loads: declared`.
- [ ] Instances of a symmetric class are counted separately and the largest utilization is reported with its instance.
- [ ] The baseline record holds the stock section, the continuous wall (tube), f₁, the fatigue outcome, the governing case with its world address and tick, and the checked values.
- [ ] For the 0.3 m tube case (stock 2.0 mm, 0.15268 kg; continuous 1.845 mm, 0.14087 kg) a 0.125 kg part reports a saving of 11.3%, with 18.1% against stock printed beside it.
- [ ] A part with f₁ 3% below the checked baseline's, or with fatigue utilization above it, has its saving marked `not-counted` with the reason and cannot satisfy GATE-FOUNDRY-05.
- [ ] A saving is never formatted for a `NotBeamLike` class or against the design's primitive Region.
- [ ] With the topology rung unavailable `foundry.optimize` completes or refuses on this rung, and the loop record names it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry beam::notch beam::rung beam::baseline`:
- `default_gauges_sixteen_per_section`; `declared_notch_adds_gauge_with_factor`.
- `notch_row_value`: tube r = 15 mm, t = 2 mm (A = 1.88496 × 10⁻⁴ m², Z = 1.41372 × 10⁻⁶ m³), N = 300 N, M_y = 30 N·m, gauge at z = +r, K_f = 2: 2 × (1.5915 + 21.2207) = 45.62 MPa; at z = −r: 2 × (1.5915 − 21.2207) = −39.26 MPa.
- `gauge_history_is_signed_stress` (a von Mises history does not type-check as counter input).
- `fatigue_governs_stock_choice`: curve N(S) = 10⁶·(100 MPa/S)³, fully reversed; 3,600 cycles in a campaign hour; H = 2,000 h; scatter 4; amplitude 60 MPa at the 1.5 mm wall. Utilizations 1.5552, 0.6561, 0.3359, 0.1944 at walls 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 mm; choice 3.0 mm.
- `continuous_wall_by_cycle_scaling`: the same fixture gives t_req = 1.5 mm × (1.5552/0.25)^(1/3) = 2.759 mm without a second pass over the stream; a direct recount at 2.759 mm agrees to 10⁻⁹.
- `continuous_wall_static_case`: the 0.3 m tube under 600 N across its tip, S_y = 276 MPa, SF = 2, r = 15 mm: t_req = 1.845 mm, m_cont = 0.14087 kg at 2,700 kg/m³.
- `instances_not_concatenated`: two limbs with different histories give the larger of two utilizations, not the utilization of the joined history.
- `batches_of_four`; `no_stock_passes_after_three_batches`; `tie_break_is_deterministic`.
- `fatigue_no_claim_still_sizes`; `declared_loads_label`.
- `saving_reference_is_continuous_wall`: 11.3% and 18.1%.
- `equal_margin_not_counted_frequency`; `equal_margin_not_counted_fatigue`; `equal_margin_targets_values`.
- `no_saving_for_not_beam_like`; `no_saving_against_primitive`.
- `idealization_gap_is_a_finding` (a planted 12% gap in f₁).
- `fallback_mode_names_rung`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `saving-against-stock-only` (reports 18.1%); `skip-equal-margin`; `concatenate-instances`; `finish-per-episode` (loses cycles that span tasks); `fatigue-from-envelope-cases` (uses case peaks, not spectra); `baseline-from-primitive`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_beam_rung.sh`, part 2: (1) `frob foundry optimize <design> --rung beam --json` on the
reference quadruped, asserting one `frob.foundry.beam/1` record per beam-like class, a stock section, a
continuous wall no heavier than it, a fatigue outcome, and a loop outcome of `converged` or a refusal with
its code; (2) `frob foundry size <design> --loads <campaign id> --json`, asserting the same stock choices;
(3) the fatigue-governed fixture, asserting 3.0 mm and 2.759 mm; (4) a card stripped of its stress-life
curve, asserting exit 0 with `fatigue: no-claim`; (5) a design whose torso is plate-like, asserting
`NotBeamLike` and no saving line for it. Logged per the shared helper: command, exit code, wall time,
evidence block; metrics `m_stock_kg`, `m_cont_kg`, `f1_Hz`, `fatigue_utilization` against its limit,
`u_governing`, `loop_iterations`; on failure the class, the governing case's address and both values. The
summary lists GATE-FOUNDRY-05 as supported, not measured: the gate's script is the structure-loop script.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fatigue block of KA-741 supplies the curve (2,000 cycles at 200 MPa give damage 0.016). The hand values
above are entered as derived values in the chapter's known-answer registry. Negative controls: the six planted
defects; the primitive-baseline saving. Metamorphic: doubling the service duration doubles every utilization;
appending an entry heavier than the choice to the stock card changes nothing; permuting limb instances changes
nothing. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOPOPT (decided at M5; trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-05 fails by a saving under 7%, no convergence, or a
  solve over budget): this rung then supplies mass and inertia feedback, three-dimensional optimization
  becomes an offline option or is dropped, and analysis A-15 is unaffected. Milestone M5's fallback for
  BET-29 is the same: beam sizing and the structural-margin analysis.
- KILL-LOADS: with hand-declared load sets there are no spectra; fatigue reads `no-claim` and a comparison is
  at equal frequency and stress only. OPEN-FOUNDRY-4 has the same effect for printed parts until stress-life
  data by process and orientation exists.
- OPEN-SKEL-4 (owner: Foundry lead; blocks nothing before this chapter): whether the `lattice` fill needs
  anisotropy. Default: isotropic fraction only, with the assumption printed in the check report. This rung
  sizes a lattice-filled member as its equivalent section and prints that assumption.
- The continuous-wall reference and the equal-margin rule are DESIGN readings; they can only lower a reported
  saving. They are listed for the owner in the unit's coverage notes.
- A beam is a floor on mass, not a design: no joints, no bolted interfaces. Actuator cards stay fixed while
  this rung runs in the loop (ADR-FOUNDRY-07).
- Cost: static checks are under 10⁷ section evaluations per class; fatigue shares the campaign pass; the
  checked baseline adds one independent-check job per class per comparison.
