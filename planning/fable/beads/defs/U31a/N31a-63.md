## Background

Foundry (crate `frob-foundry`, the subsystem that turns a qualified robot design into checked, makeable
parts) reads several things about a link that the designer must state and that nothing else in the program
uses. The Foundry chapter names them where it uses them and never says where they are written:

- "The design region is the *keep-in envelope*, minus the volumes swept by neighbouring bodies, minus
  actuator, bearing and *cable keep-outs*, with *port bosses* frozen solid" (topology optimization).
- "A port wrench becomes tractions by a declared interface pattern on the *port boss*", and stress within one
  boss diameter is `no-claim` (load extraction).
- Fatigue gauges sit at the highest-stress locations "and at *declared notches*"; the beam rung passes "the
  fatigue utilization at declared notches".
- "Each made part *declares a process*: `cnc-3axis`, `cnc-5axis`, `sheet`, `fdm`, `sls` or `slm`."
- The wiring harness routes "along the kinematic tree through *declared cable ports*".
- A stiffness cap per envelope case comes "from the control budget" (a deflection the designer allows).

The design language (file format `.fbot`, schema `frob.design/1`, crate `frob-design`) has no field for any of
them: a body has a Region (its mass support), a fill, a material, ports, and collision and visual shapes. Eight
Foundry mechanism beads were therefore drafted against inputs "declared in the design" that no bead defined.
This bead defines them once, as one optional block on a body.

Terms. A *port* is a named mount frame on a body (a joint interface, or a foot or wheel contact patch). A
*port boss* is the solid region of a link around a port that an optimizer may not remove; its *interface
surface* is the face, bore or patch through which the neighbour loads the link. The *keep-in envelope* is the
outer bound of the volume the designer allows a link to occupy. A *cable port* is where a cable enters or
leaves a body. A *notch* is a declared stress raiser (a hole, a shoulder, a thread relief) with a fatigue
notch factor K_f that multiplies the nominal stress at its location. A *made part* is manufactured from
stock by a declared process; a *purchased part* is a card reference.

Why a block in the design and not a file beside it (DESIGN). The plan says datums, fiducials and processes are
set "at design time", and a keep-in envelope is a statement about the design as much as its Region is. A
sidecar would need its own identity, its own symmetry handling under the mirror and radial constructors, and
its own typed edits, all of which the design term already has. Rejected: fields on the process card (a card
is shared by many parts and cannot hold a link's notch positions); inference only (a boss can be derived from
a joint module's mechanical port, and that derivation is kept as the default, but a keep-in envelope, a notch
or a cable corridor cannot be inferred from a capsule).

Tier: Frontier [F], with Foundry. It serves the generative-structure bet BET-29 and North-Star scenario NS-20
only as their input format; it claims nothing itself.

## Scope

In: the `structure` block of a Body (schema, typing, canonical form, defaults); its behaviour under the
`mirror` and `radial` constructors; the two typed edits that set and clear it; its place in the design and
model identities; the design lints over it; the typed accessor Foundry reads; a minor-version entry of the
schema; fixtures.
Out: every use of the declarations (the design region N31a-14; interface tractions N31a-08; the stiffness cap
of the topology rung N31a-15; fatigue gauges N31a-21 and N31a-61; the manufacturability dispatcher N31a-26; the
harness router N31a-41; the member model N31a-60); keep-outs taken from actuator and bearing cards (read from
the cards by N31a-14); datums and fiducials, which are placed by the packet geometry bead N31a-40 and recorded
in the packet, not in the design; process cards and stock cards themselves (card kinds on the schema of
BD-069, used by N31a-26 and N31b-38); bolted-joint, press-fit and weld models (outside the program).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/blocks/structure.rs`; lints in the existing lint registry; fixtures
under `crates/frob-design/tests/fixtures/structure/`.

1. **The block.** One optional member `structure` of a Body. Every member of the block is optional except
   `part`. All lengths are exact quantity atoms; frames, Regions and directions use the grammar of the Body
   block (a direction is a vector with exact rational components, normalized on read).

   | Member | Shape | Meaning | Default when absent |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `part` | `"made"` or `"purchased"` | Whether Foundry sizes, checks and exports this body | Required when the block is present. A body with no block is treated as `purchased` by Foundry and is never optimized |
   | `process` | `{name, card, setups: [direction…], build_direction, finish}` | The manufacturing process of a made part: `name` is one of `cnc-3axis`, `cnc-5axis`, `sheet`, `fdm`, `sls`, `slm`; `card` names a process card; `setups` are the tool approach directions of a milled part; `build_direction` is the layer normal of an additive part; `finish` names the surface-finish state the fatigue curve must be valid for | None. A made part without a process cannot be checked for manufacturability or packed (the consuming bead refuses) |
   | `keep_in` | a Region in the body frame | The outer bound of the link's material | The body's Region offset outward by `clearance` (zero if none) |
   | `clearance` | a length | The margin added to every swept and keep-out volume | 2 mm (DESIGN) |
   | `bosses` | `[{port, region, interface: {kind: "face", "bore" or "patch", at: frame}, diameter}]` | The frozen solid around a port, its interface surface and its diameter | Derived from the mechanical port of the attached joint module (its flange Region and diameter, BD-060); a port with neither a declared nor a derivable boss gets the lint `structure-boss-missing` |
   | `cable_ports` | `[{name, at: frame, corridor}]` | Position and exit direction (the frame's z) of a cable entry or exit, and the diameter of the corridor a cable needs | None; a body with an electrical port and no cable port gets the lint `structure-cable-port-missing` |
   | `cable_routes` | `[{from, to, length}]` | The length of the corridor between two cable ports inside the body | The straight-line distance between the two ports |
   | `notches` | `[{name, at: point, normal: direction, kf, source}]` | A stress raiser: location in the body frame, the gauge plane normal, the fatigue notch factor K_f ≥ 1 and where the factor comes from | None |
   | `deflection` | `[{port, delta_max}]` | The deflection the control budget allows at a port under an envelope case | None; the topology rung then runs without a stiffness cap and says so |
   | `member` | `{end: port, section_up: direction}` | For the beam idealization: the port that ends the member, and the direction of a rectangular section's depth | `end`: the port farthest from the proximal port (ties: lowest name); `section_up`: none, and a rectangular section family is then not offered |

   A notch may also be written in member coordinates, `{station, section_point: [y, z]}`, which the accessor
   converts to a body-frame point with the member frame; both spellings canonicalize to the body-frame form.
2. **Typing and refusals.** No new refusal code. An unknown member refuses with FROB-REF-103 (unknown,
   misspelt or duplicate field); a length without a unit, a zero or negative diameter, `kf` below 1 or a
   zero direction refuses with FROB-REF-102 (unit or dimension error); a `port` name that the body does not
   have refuses with FROB-REF-103 naming the port. A process `name` outside the six is FROB-REF-103.
3. **Canonical form and identity.** The block is part of the design term: it is written in the canonical form
   (members sorted, lists sorted by `name` or `port`, defaults not written), so it enters the `DesignId`. No
   compile functor of the model reads it: mass properties, kinematics, contact and actuation are unchanged, so
   the `ModelId` and every compiled digest are the same with and without the block. Both facts are tested.
4. **Symmetry.** Under the `mirror` and `radial` constructors the block is copied with the constructor's
   isometry applied: points and frames move as positions; directions (`normal`, `section_up`, `setups`,
   `build_direction`, a frame's axes) as polar vectors; `kf`, `diameter`, `corridor`, `delta_max` and the
   process are unchanged. Mirror images therefore declare the same structure in the canonical limb frame, which
   is what lets mirror-image links form one optimization class. A block set on one instance only is legal:
   the instances then differ, and the consumer's class table splits them (recorded, never refused).
5. **Typed edits** (in the edit engine of BD-058): `structure.set {body, member, value}` and
   `structure.clear {body, member}`, each with its inverse and the precondition that the body exists and the
   value types. Declared symmetry effect: applied to every member of the body's orbit unless `--instance` names
   one. Both yield a new `DesignId` and the same `ModelId`.
6. **Lints** (warnings of `design.check`, each with a triggering and a clean fixture and a ranked repair as a
   typed edit): `structure-boss-outside-region` (a boss Region not inside the keep-in); `structure-keep-in-
   excludes-port` (a port frame outside the keep-in); `structure-bosses-overlap`; `structure-cable-port-off-
   surface` (farther than `clearance` from the body's surface); `structure-notch-outside-keep-in`;
   `structure-boss-missing`; `structure-cable-port-missing`; `structure-process-direction-missing` (a milled
   part without `setups`, an additive part without `build_direction`); `structure-member-end-is-proximal`.
7. **Accessor.** `Design::structure(body) -> Option<&StructureDecl>` returns typed values with defaults
   applied and each value tagged `declared`, `derived` (from a module port) or `default`, so that a consumer
   can print which of its inputs the designer actually stated. `frob design show --section structure` prints
   the same table.
8. **Schema.** The block is added by a minor version of `frob.design/1` (an optional field whose absence
   means exactly the previous behaviour); a reader of the previous minor version refuses a design that carries
   it, as the versioning rule requires.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A `.fbot` body with a full `structure` block parses, canonicalizes and round-trips byte for byte; the same body without the block has a different `DesignId` and the same `ModelId` and compiled digests.
- [ ] Each member's default is applied by the accessor and tagged `default` or `derived`; a declared value is tagged `declared`.
- [ ] A boss is derived from a joint module's mechanical port when none is declared, with the module's diameter.
- [ ] `mirror` and `radial` copies carry the block with the isometry applied; a mirrored notch has its mirrored position and normal and the same K_f.
- [ ] Every refusal of step 2 is raised with its code and the pointer at the offending member; no new code is introduced.
- [ ] Each of the nine lints has a triggering fixture and a clean fixture, and its first repair, applied, clears it.
- [ ] `structure.set` followed by its inverse restores the original `DesignId`.
- [ ] The lossless S-expression view of a design shows the block and reads it back identically.
- [ ] The eight consuming beads read the block only through the accessor (a source check finds no second parser of these members).
- [ ] Standing conditions: the code is in `frob-design`; its `CONTRACT.md` gains the block in the ten sections; outputs of `design.show` carry their evidence block; the work is reachable through `frob --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-design structure` (fixtures labelled `synthetic`):
- `block_round_trips_canonically`; `defaults_not_written`; `lists_sorted_by_name`.
- `design_id_changes_model_id_does_not`: a lower-leg capsule with and without a block.
- `boss_derived_from_module_port`: a joint module with a 40 mm flange gives a boss of diameter 40 mm tagged `derived`.
- `keep_in_default_is_region_plus_clearance`: capsule radius 30 mm, clearance 2 mm → a capsule of radius 32 mm.
- `mirror_moves_notch`: a notch at (0.010, 0.020, 0.100) m with normal (0, 1, 0) under the mirror y → −y is at (0.010, −0.020, 0.100) m with normal (0, −1, 0), and K_f is unchanged.
- `radial_copies_block`: three instances at 120° carry the rotated cable port frames.
- `station_form_converts_to_body_frame`: station 0.05 m, section point (0, 0.015) m on a member along z.
- `unknown_member_103`; `kf_below_one_102`; `zero_diameter_102`; `unknown_port_103`; `unknown_process_103`.
- `lint_<name>_fires` and `lint_<name>_clean` for the nine lints; `first_repair_clears_lint` for each.
- `set_then_inverse_restores_id`; `instance_edit_splits_class`.
- `previous_minor_reader_refuses_block`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `block-read-by-model-compile` (the `ModelId` changes with a notch), `mirror-copies-without-isometry`, `defaults-written-to-canonical-form` (two spellings, two identities).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_structure_block.sh`: writes the fixture design `quad-structure` (the reference quadruped
with a block on each lower leg); runs `frob design check --json` (assert no lint), `frob design show --section
structure --json` (assert the table with its `declared`, `derived` and `default` tags), the view round trip,
then each planted-lint variant (assert the lint and that its first repair clears it) and each refusal variant
(assert exit 3, the code and the pointer). It compares the `DesignId` and `ModelId` of the design with and
without the block. Logs per the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/design_structure_block/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31a-63`), `step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block, both identities as digests, the lint names seen against those expected,
and on failure the first differing member; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the mirrored notch; the 32 mm default keep-in; the 40 mm derived boss; identity unchanged for
the model and changed for the design. Negative controls: the three planted defects and the lint and refusal
variants. Metamorphic: reordering the members of the block or of its lists changes no identity. Determinism:
canonical bytes identical twice, across worker counts, in debug and release and across platforms (exact).

## Considerations

- A change to a `structure` block is a new `DesignId` with an unchanged `ModelId`. Foundry admits work on a design whose qualification verdict names its `DesignId` and `ModelId`, so declarations are best written before qualification. Whether a verdict may be inherited across an edit that provably leaves the `ModelId` unchanged is a question for the owner of the qualification lane (BD-259); the fail-closed default is no inheritance, the same rule the loop's own mass edit follows.
- The names of the block and of its members are DESIGN: the plan uses the nouns and gives no syntax. The consuming beads were drafted before this one and name `keep_in`, `clearance`, `boss`, `member_end` and `section_up`; `member.end` is the canonical spelling of `member_end`, and the accessor exposes both names.
- Derived bosses depend on joint modules carrying a flange Region; a module without one leaves the lint standing, and the design-region bead decides what an undeclared boss means for optimization.
- An open question of the design language (lattice anisotropy of the `lattice` fill) is unrelated to this block and is carried where the fill is.
- If the owner prefers a sidecar file, the accessor is the only interface the consumers use, so the change is confined to this bead.
