## Background

A design that passes today may not pass after months of its duty cycle: joints wear and gain backlash, gauges
accumulate fatigue damage, winding insulation ages with temperature, cells lose capacity and gain resistance.
Lifecycle compression asks four questions: does the design that passes today still pass after months of its
duty cycle; which component reaches its limit first, and when; what maintenance interval that implies; and
what a lighter link costs in service duration. Its outputs are the time to first threshold per component with
an interval, aged model variants at stated ages for the evaluation and adversarial-search subsystems (as
per-world variants in one batch), and a front of mass against service duration. Every output is class
Estimated.

The obstacle is scale: at 500 Hz, 1,000 hours is 1.8 × 10⁹ steps. The method is two-timescale averaging: the
slow states are advanced with large macro steps using rates averaged over short windows of full simulation.

It is a Moonshot [M]: off the critical path, in its own crate `frob-lifecycle` behind feature `lifecycle`
(ADR-FOUNDRY-12), and nothing depends on it; the build packet's dependency graph must not include it
(INV-FOUNDRY-07). If its pilot fails the crate is deleted, not absorbed. Ceiling, stated before any test: a
run of 100 macro steps with three micro evaluations each, of 30 gait cycles at 0.5 s, simulates 2.25 × 10⁶
steps for 1,000 hours, a ratio near 800 in simulated steps (DESIGN, illustrative). That is a count of steps,
not of wall time and not of field experience. Accelerated estimates can also change the failure mechanism.

FACT (FrankenSim at commit `483d09c682`): `fs-contact` has Archard wear volume advanced from caller-supplied
sliding distance and compressive load, with no recession geometry and no map from volume to backlash.
`fs-orbit` has periodic orbits by harmonic balance and shooting, Floquet multipliers and pseudo-arclength
continuation, but its problem shape is a nonlinear island on a linear port, not an articulated robot in
contact: an oracle for toy known answers only. `fs-phs` has a finite state-of-charge battery with open-circuit
voltage and polarization and no capacity fade or resistance growth; a search of the workspace found no
cell-ageing law and no insulation-life law. `fs-spectral::floquet::analyze_monodromy` analyses a supplied
monodromy matrix. So the laws, the stepping and the feedback are this project's code, and the coefficients
come from cards.

## Technical approach

**The short plan (the seed's first done-condition).** Order of work: pilot → (if positive, and funded) data
decision → mechanism → gate → kill decision.

**1. Model.** The state splits into fast x and slow z with ẋ = f(x, z, u) and ż = ε·g(x, z), ε small. Slow
states and their laws (empirical; coefficients from cards with provenance):

| Slow state | Law | Feeds back into |
|---|---|---|
| Backlash β per joint | Archard volume k·F_n·s/H (k wear coefficient, F_n normal load, s sliding distance, H hardness) mapped to recession by a card geometry factor | The backlash rows of the contact model |
| Fatigue damage per gauge | Rainflow with Miner's sum, residues carried across windows; cycles between duty modes counted at the macro level | A threshold only |
| Winding insulation | An Arrhenius rate in winding temperature (the common form: life halves per 10 K) | A threshold only |
| Cell capacity and resistance | Calendar fade in √t plus fade per unit of charge throughput: capacity Q = Q₀·(1 − f_Q), resistance R₀ = R₀₀·(1 + f_R) | Bus voltage sag in the actuator model |

**2. Micro windows.** At fixed z: a settling phase run until a periodicity residual ‖x(t + T) − x(t)‖ falls
under tolerance, then about twenty gait cycles of averaging (TARGET) to estimate ⟨g⟩; for a mixed duty, an
ergodic sample of the mission profile with its confidence interval. *Micro windows average the rate, never the
input.* Counterexample: with insulation life halving per 10 K, a duty that spends half its time at T_ref and
half at T_ref + 20 K consumes life at (1 + 4)/2 = 2.5/τ_ref; evaluating the law at the mean temperature gives
2.0/τ_ref and overstates service duration by 25%.

**3. Macro steps.** z advances by z ← z + H·ε·⟨g⟩(z) with step doubling for error control: one step of H is
compared with two of H/2 (three micro evaluations per accepted step); the difference is the error estimate; a
step is rejected and halved when it exceeds tolerance.

**4. Return to full resolution** when a Floquet multiplier of the gait orbit approaches the unit circle (the
gait is about to change character), when the macro error estimate fails, or when the slow rate is no longer
small. Planted case: a backlash-induced gait collapse at β* must force full resolution before β*.

**5. Rare overloads** are invisible to short windows: their damage enters as rate times damage per event,
with rates from adversarial search.

**6. Validity.** Each law carries a validity domain and refuses outside it, and refuses to be cited above
class Estimated (FROB-REF-831; repair: the domain and the card needed). Generic cards are marked `assumed`
and `--strict-data` refuses them.

**7. Outputs and verb.** `foundry.lifecycle` (kind `run`; the verb registry dates it M8; the work lands with
M6): time to first threshold per component with an interval; aged model variants (new `ModelId`s with
provenance `aged@<hours>`); the mass against service-duration front; the count of simulated steps and the
compression ratio in steps. Evidence: class Estimated (INV-FOUNDRY-06).

**8. Pilot before funding** (N31b-11). A forced oscillator with slow wear and ε = 10⁻⁴, against direct
integration. Falsifier: error above 5% at a compression of 50 in steps. If the falsifier holds, nothing else
in this epic is built.

**9. Gate.** GATE-FOUNDRY-12 (claim Fidelity, TARGET): a 100-hour reference (1.8 × 10⁸ steps) compressed at
least 50-fold in steps with at most 5% error on every slow state; the planted backlash-induced gait collapse
caught. The gate register places it at M6 with this epic (the chapter printed M8; the register governs).

**10. Kill gates.** KILL-LIFECYCLE: GATE-FOUNDRY-12 fails. Fallback: aged model variants at hand-specified
wear states, with no time axis and no compression claim. KILL-SINEW-AGE (the actuator chapter's): the ageing
law fails its pilot, or no card with ageing coefficients better than `assumed` exists when this work is
funded. Fallback: f_Q and f_R are user-set constants per scenario, with no evolution law; the cell row of the
table above then runs in that mode.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Carried by the children; the epic names the ones that must exist: `averaged_growth` (ż = ε·a·x², x = sin ωt:
z(T) = ε·a·T/2 over whole periods); `wear_feedback_closed_form` (β̇ = ε·(c₀ + c₁·β): β(t) = (β₀ + c₀/c₁)·
exp(ε·c₁·t) − c₀/c₁); `rate_not_input_is_averaged` (2.5/τ_ref, not 2.0/τ_ref); `cell_fade_sum` (1% per √month
calendar and 0.5% per 100 cycles over 9 months and 300 cycles: 3% + 1.5% = 4.5%); `step_doubling_error_estimate`;
`floquet_trigger_forces_full_resolution`; `law_outside_domain_refuses_831`; `residues_carried_across_micro_windows`;
`step_count_arithmetic` (500 Hz × 1,000 h = 1.8 × 10⁹; 100 × 3 × 30 × 0.5 s × 500 Hz = 2.25 × 10⁶; ratio 800).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_lifecycle.sh` (bead N31a-59): runs `frob foundry lifecycle` (a run verb: each call under
its own `--key`) on the 100-hour reference fixture and on the planted gait-collapse fixture, compares every
slow state with the direct reference, and logs per STD-E2E the exact command lines, exit codes and wall time,
the compression ratio in steps (threshold 50), the largest slow-state error (threshold 5%), the trigger events,
the cell-ageing mode in force and the evidence block; the summary names GATE-FOUNDRY-12. The pilot (N31b-11)
and the kill decision (N31b-23) have their own scripts.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-750 (averaged growth; tolerance 5% at a compression of 50). KA-751 (wear feedback; control: a planted gait
collapse at β* must force full resolution before β*). KA-752 (rate averaging; control: averaging the input is
caught). KA-753 (cell fade 4.5%). Counterexample CX-344. Supplied controls for the rows printed without one
(DESIGN): for KA-750, averaging over a non-integer number of periods; for KA-753, summing calendar fade
linearly in t.

## Considerations

- Moonshot priority 4 whatever the milestone; funded only on a positive pilot and the owner's approval; an epic that has not passed its pilot within its planned weeks returns to the register of deferred items.
- OPEN-FOUNDRY-5: wear, insulation and cell-fade coefficients with provenance do not exist yet. Default: generic cards marked `assumed`, which `--strict-data` refuses. It blocks GATE-FOUNDRY-12 (N31b-28).
- Averaging assumes the fast motion is near-periodic or ergodic at fixed slow state; near a gait bifurcation it is not, which is why the Floquet trigger exists.
- The compression ratio is a ratio of simulated steps. No wall-time ratio and no field-life claim is ever printed.
- Milestone note: the chapter tags the gate, the kill gate and the verb M8; the gate register and the milestone chapter place the work, gate and kill gate at M6. This bead follows the register.
