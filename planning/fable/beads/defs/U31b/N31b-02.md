## Background

A proof obligation, in this program, is a mathematical statement a mechanism relies on, together with the
means by which it is discharged: a derivation on file, a test with a stated acceptance bound, a certificate,
or a proof. Foundry has four. They differ from invariants (properties of every artifact) and from gates
(thresholds measured on fixtures): an obligation is the reason a computation is allowed to be used as it is.

Without them three shortcuts would be unjustified: computing stress histories by multiplying an influence
matrix with load coordinates instead of solving each sample; replacing a joint's wrench by a traction field;
and stopping the loads–mass loop on an error bound computed from the last two iterates. The fourth concerns
exported fixed-point code and is a Moonshot.

This bead writes each obligation's derivation, fixes its acceptance bound with a reason, runs its test, and
records the discharge. Two of the obligations are clauses of gates (PO-FOUNDRY-01 in GATE-FOUNDRY-02;
PO-FOUNDRY-03 in GATE-FOUNDRY-06), so their measured values are also written to those gate rows.

## Scope

In: four derivation files; four tests with bounds; the discharge ledger rows.
Out: the mechanisms themselves (BD-400, BD-404, BD-438); the hand derivations of known answers (N31b-04).

## Technical approach

Derivations live in `docs/derivations/foundry/po-foundry-0N.md` (model, statement, derivation, error budget
of the test). The discharge ledger (DESIGN) is a table `frob-conformance/registry/obligations.toml` with one
row per obligation: `id`, `statement`, `discharged_by` (`derivation+test`, `certificate`), `bound`,
`measured`, `commit`, `status` (`open`, `discharged`, `not-applicable: <reason>`).

**PO-FOUNDRY-01. The unit-load superposition reproduces a direct solve of any campaign sample.**
- *Statement.* Let the load on a link at a sample be the coordinate vector L (six per port except the
  statically determinate 3-2-1 restraint port, plus twelve for the body field: a₀, α and the six products
  ω_i·ω_j). Let σ_j be the stress field of the unit load e_j. Then the stress of the sample is Σ_j L_j·σ_j.
- *Derivation.* Small-strain elasticity with fixed restraints is linear: K·u = F, and F is linear in L because
  port tractions are linear in the port wrench (PO-FOUNDRY-02's pattern is linear in f and m) and the body
  load −ρ·a(x) is linear in the twelve field coordinates (ω × (ω × x) = (ω·ωᵀ − ‖ω‖²·I)·x is linear in the
  products). Stress is linear in u.
- *Test and bound.* 10³ random campaign samples; relative difference between superposed and directly solved
  gauge stresses at most 10⁻⁹ on the referee (TARGET). Reason for the bound: double precision with a direct
  sparse solve leaves residuals near 10⁻¹² relative; three orders of margin cover conditioning.
- *Negative control.* A body field that omits the ω-product terms: the difference exceeds 10⁻⁹ on spinning
  samples.

**PO-FOUNDRY-02. The interface traction pattern returns the port wrench exactly.**
- *Statement.* On the port boss patch Γ with area A, centroid x̄ and J = ∫_Γ r·rᵀ dA, r = x − x̄, the field
  t(x) = f/A + w × r with w = (tr(J)·I − J)⁻¹·m_c has resultant force f and resultant moment m_c about x̄.
- *Derivation.* ∫ t dA = f because ∫ r dA = 0. ∫ r × (w × r) dA = ∫ (‖r‖²·w − r·(r·w)) dA = (tr(J)·I − J)·w.
  The matrix tr(J)·I − J has eigenvalues equal to pairwise sums of the eigenvalues of J, so it is positive
  definite unless the patch degenerates to a line or a point, which the interface declaration refuses.
- *Test and bound.* Random wrenches on annular, rectangular and three-lug bosses, with A, x̄ and J computed by
  the same quadrature the load vector uses, so the identity is exact in the discrete setting: resultant force
  and moment equal the wrench to rounding (relative 10⁻¹², TARGET).
- *Negative control.* J computed by a different quadrature than the load assembly: the moment misses by the
  quadrature error and the test fails.

**PO-FOUNDRY-03. The a-posteriori bound of the loads–mass loop holds for a contraction.**
- *Statement.* If G is a contraction with factor λ < 1 and x_{k+1} = G(x_k), Δ_k = x_k − x_{k−1}, then
  ‖x* − x_k‖ ≤ λ/(1 − λ)·‖Δ_k‖.
- *Derivation.* ‖x_{k+j} − x_{k+j−1}‖ ≤ λʲ·‖Δ_k‖; summing the telescoping series for x* − x_k gives
  Σ_{j≥1} λʲ·‖Δ_k‖ = λ/(1 − λ)·‖Δ_k‖. The loop uses the estimate λ̂_k = ‖Δ_k‖/‖Δ_{k−1}‖ in place of λ, which
  is exact for a scalar linear map and an estimate otherwise; the convergence test therefore also requires two
  successive small changes and a passing independent check.
- *Test and bound.* The planted-gain fixture (S ↦ 0.6 + 0.15·(12 + S), start 4.0): at every iteration the true
  error |2.823529 − x_k| is at most the bound; property test over random linear contractions in up to 12
  dimensions with factors in (0, 0.9).
- *Negative control.* The bound computed with λ̂/(1 + λ̂): violated on the fixture.

**PO-FOUNDRY-04. For each exported controller, no intermediate overflows on the declared input ranges**
(theorem card T-14). Discharged per controller by replayed bit-vector certificates: an unsatisfiable overflow
query whose proof stream the independent checker replays; stateful elements by a base query and a step query.
Class Certified, not Proved, because the upstream step that would derive a kernel proof term is not built.
Status until the firmware epic is funded: `not-applicable: feature not built`. The row is created now so the
obligation is not lost.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry obligations`: `po_foundry_01_superposition_1e_9` (feature `structure`);
`po_foundry_01_omega_products_needed`; `po_foundry_02_traction_resultant` (annulus of radii 8 and 12 mm; f =
(10, −20, 30) N; m = (0.5, 0.2, −0.1) N·m; both returned within 10⁻¹²); `po_foundry_02_degenerate_patch_refused`;
`po_foundry_03_bound_on_fixture` (errors 0.1765, 0.02647, 0.003971, 0.000596 against bounds 0.1765, 0.02647,
0.003971, 0.000596 at k = 1 to 4: equality for the scalar linear map); `po_foundry_03_random_contractions`;
`po_foundry_03_wrong_bound_violated`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_obligations.sh`: runs the test target; then `frob foundry loads quad-ref --campaign
gate-foundry-02 --check-superposition 1000 --json` and `frob foundry optimize --fixture planted-gain --loop
--json`, reads the measured values and compares them with the ledger rows. Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_obligations/`: per obligation its identifier, bound, measured value and status; the
summary names GATE-FOUNDRY-02 and GATE-FOUNDRY-06.

### Known answers and negative controls
The planted-gain numbers of the loads–mass known answer (fixed point 2.8235 kg; λ̂ = 0.15). The three
negative controls above. Metamorphic: PO-FOUNDRY-01 is invariant under scaling of L; PO-FOUNDRY-02 under rigid
motion of the boss with its wrench. Determinism: identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- PO-FOUNDRY-01 holds only inside the frequency-separation rule of load extraction; samples routed to the transient path are outside it and are excluded from the test by construction.
- PO-FOUNDRY-02 says nothing about the stress near the boss: within one boss diameter of an interface the stress is `no-claim`.
- PO-FOUNDRY-03's λ̂ is an estimate; a map that is not a contraction can show a small λ̂ once. The loop's other three convergence conditions are what protect against that.
