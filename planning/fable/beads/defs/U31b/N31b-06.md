## Background

Every Frontier and Moonshot mechanism in this program names a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier that must
run before its work is funded. A pilot is small enough to run on a CPU in a day, uses existing tools outside
the workspace, is registered before it runs, and has an outcome not known in advance; a pilot that cannot fail
is not a pilot. Its result, including a negative one, is recorded, and the mechanism's tier and scope are
revised to match. A work package whose pilot's falsifier holds is not funded; its fallback ships.

The mechanism here is Foundry's *structure loop*: load cases from simulation → sizing → topology optimization
→ independent check → mass and inertia fed back → repeat. The bet behind it (generative structure) is that
links optimized under simulated load histories are lighter than engineered parts at equal margin. The honest
prior is modest: reported savings against engineered parts are 7 to 30% of link mass, legs are about 27% of
one published quadruped's mass, so the ceiling is a few percent of robot mass (0.27 × 0.30 ≈ 0.08). Against
primitive shapes the saving is larger and means nothing. The pilot therefore compares against an *engineered*
baseline: a thin-walled tube sized by hand to the same peak load.

The pilot has two stages. Stage 1 (this bead) is runnable on the sibling libraries today, with three analytic
envelope cases from stance and trot statics; it needs no simulator of ours. Stage 2 (N31b-07) replaces the
analytic cases by cases extracted from 1,000 simulated episodes and adds five loop iterations. Together they
are two to three engineer-days.

## Scope

In: the frozen stage-1 protocol; its registration; the run with sibling tools; the record; the reading against
the falsifier.
Out: stage 2; any product code in `frob-foundry`; the gate measurement on the product pipeline (N31b-13).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture** (DESIGN choices, frozen in the registration). The lower-leg link of the reference quadruped:
ports at the knee and the foot; port spacing, keep-in envelope, material card (an aluminium alloy with yield
strength, modulus, density and a stress-life curve) and robot weight W taken from the reference robot's
design file at a named revision.

**2. Three analytic envelope cases from statics**, each one consistent instant: (a) four-leg stance, foot
force W/4 along the leg's support direction; (b) trot, diagonal-pair support, foot force W/2; (c) trot with
traction, W/2 normal plus a tangential component μ·W/2 at the friction coefficient of the reference floor.
Each case is the foot-port wrench with the knee port as the statically determinate restraint and the body
field of gravity alone. The cases are not combined into one.

**3. Engineered baseline.** A thin-walled tube between the ports, sized by hand to the same peak load with
the closed forms: for mean radius r and wall t, section modulus π·r²·t, area 2π·r·t; stress σ = N/A ± M/Z and
the check √(σ² + 3·τ²) ≤ S_y/SF; Euler buckling π²·E·I/(K·L)²; first bending frequency from the beam formula
with end masses; fatigue utilization at the root under an assumed duty of the three cases. The lightest stock
wall that passes is the baseline mass m_tube; its first bending frequency f_tube and fatigue utilization
U_tube are recorded.

**4. Optimized candidate.** Using the sibling libraries directly (a script outside the workspace, pinned by
commit): density optimization in three dimensions inside the keep-in envelope with frozen port bosses, the
three load cases, a stress cap S_y/SF, a first-mode floor equal to f_tube, and a minimum length scale;
surface extraction at level ½; a body-fitted mesh; an independent stress, modal and fatigue evaluation on
that mesh. The candidate's mass m_opt is read at equal first bending frequency (f_opt ≥ f_tube) and equal
fatigue utilization (U_opt ≤ U_tube); if either inequality fails the candidate is re-run with a tighter
constraint, at most three times.

**5. Falsifier (TARGET), stage-1 clause.** Saving s = (m_tube − m_opt)/m_tube under 5% against the tube at
equal first bending frequency and equal fatigue utilization. (The second clause of the pilot's falsifier,
mass still changing by more than 2% between iterations four and five, is read in stage 2.) The construction's
own pilot line is stricter and is read in the same record: a saving below 7% at equal stiffness and peak
stress, or a damage figure that changes by more than 5% when the window length is halved. The record states
the saving against both thresholds.

**6. Controls that show the pilot can fail and can see an effect.** Potency: against a solid rod of the
tube's outer radius the same pipeline must report a large saving (above 30%), which is recorded as
meaningless and proves the pipeline measures mass. Null: with the stress cap relaxed tenfold and the mode
floor removed, the saving must rise, showing the constraints bind. Independence: the candidate's stress is
taken from the body-fitted mesh, not from the optimizer's aggregate; the planted overstress fixture (a root
thinned so that stress is 300 MPa against 240 MPa yield) must be reported with utilization 1.25 by the same
evaluation script.

**7. Ceiling, computed before the run.** Structure fraction f_L times saving s: at most 0.27 × 0.30 ≈ 0.08
of robot mass; expected 2 to 8%; a positive pilot changes no decision time.

**8. Record.** One entry in the pilot register: mechanism and tier (structure loop, Frontier); the protocol
hash (fixture, cases, baseline rule, constraints, thresholds) registered before execution; falsifier; ceiling;
fallback (catalogue sections with a beam check) and kill gate (KILL-TOPOPT); cost in engineer-hours and CPU
time; tool and pin; result: m_tube, f_tube, U_tube, m_opt, f_opt, U_opt, s, wall time per solve, whether the
extracted surface meshed, and the verdict `positive`, `negative` or `inconclusive` with the reason.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol is registered with its hash before any solve runs; the register's sequence number precedes the first result.
- [ ] The baseline tube is sized by hand and its derivation is on file (inputs, formulas, chosen stock wall, mass, frequency, utilization).
- [ ] The record holds every number of step 8 and a verdict against the 5% clause and against the 7% line.
- [ ] The three controls ran: the potency control reports a large saving, the relaxed-constraint run reports a larger saving than the constrained one, and the planted overstress is reported at 1.25 ± 5%.
- [ ] A negative or inconclusive result is recorded as such, and the downstream consequence (stage 2 proceeds, or the topology rung is not funded and the beam rung ships) is written into the record.
- [ ] No product crate was created or changed by this pilot.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The pilot is a registered experiment, not product code. Its script carries self-checks that run before the
solves: `tube_section_formulas` (r = 15 mm, t = 2 mm: A = 1.885 × 10⁻⁴ m², Z = 1.414 × 10⁻⁶ m³);
`three_cases_are_distinct_instants` (no case is a combination); `baseline_is_lightest_passing_stock`;
`saving_definition` (m_tube = 0.20 kg, m_opt = 0.18 kg → 10%).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_structure_stage1.sh` (outside the product's end-to-end suite; it sources the same
logging helper): registers the protocol, runs the baseline sizing, the optimization, the extraction and the
independent evaluation, then the three controls. It writes newline-delimited JSON to
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_structure_stage1/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with: the protocol hash, tool pins,
per step command and wall time, `m_tube_kg`, `m_opt_kg`, `saving` against thresholds 0.05 and 0.07,
`f_tube_hz`, `f_opt_hz`, `u_fatigue_tube`, `u_fatigue_opt`, `meshed`, control results, and a `summary` with
the verdict. It exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers used by the self-checks and the evaluation: the cantilever (1.159 mm; 30 MPa), the planted
overstress (300 MPa; 1.25), the two-bar optimum (45°; minimum volume 2·P·L/σ_allow). Negative controls: the
three of step 6; a protocol edited after registration (the hash check must refuse the run). Determinism: the
script is seed-free; two runs on one machine give the same masses.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOPOPT is decided at M5 on the product pipeline (saving under 7%, no convergence, or over budget); this pilot is its first reading. Fallback named in advance: beam-rung sizing supplies mass and inertia feedback; three-dimensional optimization becomes an offline option or is dropped.
- Threshold difference: the chapter's pilot row uses 5%, the construction's pilot line and the gate's kill threshold use 7%. The record reports against both; funding of the topology rung follows the chapter's row, and the gate later applies 7%.
- Stage 1 uses statics only, so it says nothing about impact loads or about convergence of the loop. A positive stage 1 funds stage 2, not the work package.
- The three-dimensional lane of the sibling is feature-gated and tagged Frontier upstream; if it cannot run the fixture, that is a finding and is recorded as `inconclusive: tool`.
- "Equal fatigue utilization" depends on a stress-life curve for the material; for a printed material there is none, and the comparison is made for a wrought alloy only.
