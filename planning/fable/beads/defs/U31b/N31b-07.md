## Background

Foundry's structure loop is a Frontier mechanism and must pass a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before its
work package is funded. Stage 1 of the pilot (N31b-06) compared one optimized quadruped lower-leg link with a
hand-sized thin-walled tube under three analytic load cases. Stage 1 cannot answer two things: whether load
cases taken from simulated episodes (with impacts and inertial loads) still leave a saving, and whether the
loop converges once the new mass is fed back, since link mass sized to a stress cap grows with the loads and
the loads grow with total mass.

Stage 2 answers both by hand, with scripts, before any product code exists: three envelope cases from 1,000
simulated episodes, and five iterations of optimize → new mass and inertia → re-simulate → re-extract. It can
run once the batched simulator and control-rate telemetry exist (after M2). It is the experiment that
GATE-FOUNDRY-05 later repeats on the product pipeline.

In the linear picture the loop is S ↦ A + λ·(M₀ + S), with error contracting by λ per iteration. An expected
structural gain around 0.15 would leave 0.15⁴ = 5 × 10⁻⁴ of the first step after four contractions; a gain
near 1, or a stock-size step, would show as a mass still moving at iteration five. Fresh random worlds at each
iteration would put a noise floor above the 2% test, so the same world addresses are used throughout.

## Scope

In: the frozen stage-2 protocol; its registration; the run; the record; the reading against both clauses of
the falsifier and against the construction's window-halving clause.
Out: product code; the gate measurement (N31b-13); the kill decision (N31b-15).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture.** The link, material, baseline tube and thresholds of stage 1, unchanged, so the two stages are
comparable. The reference quadruped under its baseline legged controller on the velocity-tracking task of the
M2 task family, plus a push-recovery variant (DESIGN: lateral impulses at fixed addresses).

**2. Campaign.** 1,000 episodes at fixed world addresses with control-rate traces on every world (joint
constraint wrenches, contact wrenches, body twists). The address list is hashed into the registration.

**3. Extraction by script** (the hand version of the product's rules). For the link: port wrenches in the
link frame and the body acceleration field a(x) = a₀ + α × x + ω × (ω × x) per sample; the free-body residual
per sample (samples above 10⁻³ relative dropped and counted; more than 0.1% dropped stops the pilot); 18 load
coordinates; three envelope cases chosen as the samples maximizing the projection on three registered
directions (DESIGN: foot normal force, foot tangential force, knee bending moment). Each case is one instant.
A signed stress history at the root gauge for the fatigue comparison.

**4. One iteration.** (i) Optimize the link under the three cases with the constraints of stage 1; (ii)
extract and evaluate on a body-fitted mesh; (iii) compute mass, centre of mass and inertia; (iv) write them
into the design as a typed edit (a `declared` body with provenance `pilot`), giving a new design identity; (v)
re-run the 1,000 episodes at the same addresses with the baseline controller re-tuned by its own synthesis
rule; (vi) re-extract. Actuator cards do not change.

**5. Five iterations.** Record after each: link mass m_k, principal inertias, Δ_k, the contraction estimate
λ̂_k = |Δ_k|/|Δ_{k−1}|, the change of the three case magnitudes, wall time of each step.

**6. Falsifier (TARGET).** Either clause falsifies: (a) saving under 5% against the tube at equal first
bending frequency and equal fatigue utilization, read on the final iterate; (b) mass still changing by more
than 2% between iterations four and five. Also read and recorded: the construction's clause, a damage figure
that changes by more than 5% when the window length is halved (the root-gauge damage computed over all 1,000
episodes and over the first 500, per episode), and the saving against the gate's 7% kill threshold and 15%
target.

**7. Controls.** Potency: with the load cases scaled by 2 the optimized mass must rise (the loop responds to
load). Noise floor: iteration 1 repeated with a different address set, to measure the mass scatter that fresh
worlds would cause; the record states it beside the 2% test. Planted gain: the scripted loop driver run on
the scalar map S ↦ 0.6 + 0.15·(12 + S) from 4.0 must return 3.0, 2.85, 2.8275, 2.8241, 2.8236.

**8. Ceiling, computed before the run.** A few percent of robot mass (at most 0.27 × 0.30 ≈ 0.08); the pilot
makes no decision faster.

**9. Record.** A pilot-register entry as for stage 1, with the per-iteration table, both falsifier clauses,
the window-halving ratio, the noise floor, the cost (CPU-hours of optimization and GPU-seconds of simulation
per iteration) and the verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, including the address list digest and the three directions, is registered before the first campaign runs.
- [ ] Five iterations are recorded with m_k, Δ_k, λ̂_k and case changes, all on the same addresses (digest equal across iterations).
- [ ] The record states the saving on the final iterate against 5%, 7% and 15%, the change between iterations four and five against 2%, and the window-halving change against 5%.
- [ ] The three controls ran and their results are in the record; the planted-gain driver check reproduces the five iterates.
- [ ] The verdict is `positive` only if neither falsifier clause holds; otherwise the record states that the topology rung is not funded and that the beam rung supplies mass and inertia feedback.
- [ ] The cost per iteration is recorded, as the first estimate of the 120 s per link class and five-iteration budgets.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the pilot's scripts: `free_body_residual_static` (a link at rest: zero); `eighteen_coordinates`;
`case_is_single_sample`; `loop_driver_planted_gain` (the five iterates above; λ̂ = 0.15);
`window_halving_ratio_definition` (damage per episode over 500 against over 1,000).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_structure_stage2.sh`: registers the protocol; for k = 1 to 5 runs `frob sim run` on
the address list with control-rate telemetry, the extraction script, the optimization and evaluation scripts
and `frob design edit`; then the controls. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_structure_stage2/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with, per iteration, the design
identity, address-list digest, `dropped_fraction`, the three case magnitudes, `m_k_kg`, `delta_rel`,
`lambda_hat`, wall times and device seconds; then `saving` (thresholds 0.05, 0.07, 0.15), `delta_4_5_rel`
(0.02), `window_halving_change` (0.05), `noise_floor_rel`, and a `summary` with the verdict. Exit zero when
the measurement is complete.

### Known answers and negative controls
The planted-gain iterates and fixed point 2.8235 kg (the loads–mass known answer). Negative controls: the
potency and noise-floor runs; a run with re-sampled addresses per iteration, which must be flagged by the
digest check; a protocol edited after registration. Determinism: iteration k re-run on the same machine gives
the same m_k.

## Considerations

- This pilot is the first reading of KILL-TOPOPT. The decision itself is taken at M5 on the product pipeline; a negative stage 2 means the topology rung is not funded and the M5 decision is taken on this record.
- Cases come from a fast tier here and are not re-extracted on the referee; the record labels them provisional. A saving that depends on a fast-tier impact peak is noted as such.
- A discrete stock size in the baseline can make the comparison jump; the baseline is fixed at stage 1's choice for all five iterations.
- Five iterations of 1,000 episodes plus optimization is the largest pilot cost in Foundry: two to three engineer-days for both stages.
