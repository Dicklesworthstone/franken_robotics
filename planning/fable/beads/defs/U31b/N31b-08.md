## Background

Foundry's tolerance step allocates tolerances by sensitivity: for a functional quantity with first-order
sensitivities sᵢ and cost coefficients cᵢ, tolerances tᵢ ∝ (cᵢ/sᵢ²)^(1/3) meet a statistical variance budget at
least cost under an assumed 1/t cost law. It is a Frontier mechanism, so a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier
must run before its work is funded.

What could be wrong with it on a real mechanism: the allocation is first-order. A linkage's output is a
nonlinear function of its dimensions, deviations within a tolerance band are not infinitesimal, and features
are not always independent. If the linearization is poor, a design toleranced by the formula meets its
specification less often than promised. The pilot measures exactly that, on a mechanism small enough to check
by brute force, with data the allocation never saw.

Ceiling, stated first: the benefit is a lower cost under an assumed 1/t model only. In the two-feature worked
case (sensitivities 1 and 4) the allocation is 19.9% cheaper than equal tolerances. No currency figure exists
and none is claimed.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; registration; the run with the sibling allocator; the record; the reading against
the falsifier and against the kill gate's one-fifth clause.
Out: product code in `frob-foundry`; correlated stacks and mode-switching clearances (covered by the product
gate, not by this pilot).

## Technical approach

**1. Fixture** (DESIGN, frozen at registration). A planar four-bar linkage with a coupler point. Eight
toleranced features: the four link lengths; the x and y coordinates of the second ground pivot; the two
coordinates of the coupler point in the coupler frame. Nominal dimensions are chosen away from singular
configurations (a Grashof crank-rocker; transmission angle between 40° and 140° over the crank range used).
Quantity of interest: the coupler-point position at five registered crank angles; the functional error is the
largest of the five position errors along a registered direction. Specification: error within ± M with
coverage target p* (DESIGN: M such that the allocated budget is √B = M/3; p* = 99.73% at k = 3).

**2. Sensitivities.** Exact derivatives of the closure-constrained coupler position with respect to the
eight features at each crank angle, computed symbolically or in exact rational arithmetic at rational nominal
values; the governing sensitivity vector per angle is recorded.

**3. Two arms.** (a) Allocated: `variance_budget` then `allocate` with equal cost coefficients and k = 3, for
the worst of the five angles. (b) Equal tolerances meeting the same variance budget.

**4. Band-extreme check** (the rule of the product, N31a-33 step 2, so that the pilot's share and the kill
gate's clause count the same thing). At each of the five angles the true (nonlinear) error is evaluated at
each feature's single extremes (±tᵢ, the others nominal) and at all 2⁸ = 256 corners of the tolerance box
(n = 8 ≤ 12, so every corner). What is tested is the linearization, not the margin: a statistical allocation's
corners lie outside the specification by design, so a corner beyond ± M is not by itself a failure. A feature
*fails* when the true change at its single-feature extreme differs from sᵢ·tᵢ by more than 10% of the margin
M; or when a corner c has discrepancy d(c) = |q(c) − q_nom − Σ sᵢ·cᵢ| > 0.1·M and the attribution rule names
it (the features whose return to nominal, one at a time, lowers d below 0.1·M; when no single return does,
the smallest set taken greedily in order of the reduction each gives). A feature that fails at any of the
five angles counts once. The share of failing features is recorded: the kill gate's second clause fires
above one fifth (more than 1.6 of 8, that is two or more).

**5. Held-out Monte Carlo.** 10⁵ draws of the eight deviations, independent normal with σᵢ = tᵢ/3, from a
seed registered before the allocation is computed and not used for anything else; the true error is evaluated
by solving the loop closure for each draw at each angle. Coverage is the fraction of draws inside ± M at all
five angles. Target: at least p* minus the one-sided 99% binomial tolerance for 10⁵ draws (for p* = 0.9973
that is 0.9973 − 2.326·√(0.9973 × 0.0027/10⁵) = 0.99692).

**6. Falsifier (TARGET).** Held-out Monte Carlo coverage of the specification below target, for the allocated
arm.

**7. Controls.** Known answer: the two-feature worked case run through the same script must give 16.0 µm and
6.35 µm (15.990412, 6.345799) and a cost ratio of 0.8008. Potency: a linkage placed near a singular
configuration (transmission angle 10°) must fail coverage, showing the test can fail. Null: the equal-tolerance
arm must also meet coverage (same budget), so a difference between arms is a cost difference and not a
coverage difference. Zero sensitivity: adding a ninth feature whose first-order sensitivity is zero at the
nominal (a symmetric offset) must be refused by the allocator, not loosened.

**8. Record.** Pilot-register entry (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09): protocol hash; sensitivities; both arms' tolerances and costs under 1/t
(cost ratio); corner results and the failing-feature share; coverage of both arms with intervals; the
controls; cost of the pilot; verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, the nominal dimensions and the Monte Carlo seed are registered before the allocation is computed.
- [ ] The record holds both arms' eight tolerances, the cost ratio, the 256-corner result per angle, the failing-feature share against one fifth, and the held-out coverage against 0.99692.
- [ ] The worked two-feature case reproduces 15.990412 µm and 6.345799 µm through the pilot's script.
- [ ] The near-singular potency control fails coverage; the zero-sensitivity feature is refused.
- [ ] The verdict is `positive` only if coverage meets the target; a negative verdict, or a failing-feature share above one fifth, is recorded with its consequence (worst-case boxes per interface).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the pilot's script: `four_bar_closure_solves` (a registered configuration closes to 10⁻¹²);
`sensitivities_match_finite_differences` (central differences at step 10⁻⁶ agree to 10⁻⁶ relative);
`worked_case_allocation`; `budget_met_by_both_arms` (Σ sᵢ²·(tᵢ/3)² equal to B to 10⁻¹²);
`binomial_tolerance` (0.99692 for 10⁵ draws at 0.9973); `band_extreme_rule_matches_product`: on the
synthetic q = x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₁·x₃/L (L = 1 mm, M = 10 µm, every tᵢ = 40 µm) the corner discrepancy is 1.6 µm
against 1 µm and x₁ and x₃ fail by attribution while x₂ passes; on the two-feature worked case no feature
fails although its linearly worst corner (41.4 µm) lies beyond the 30 µm margin.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_tolerance.sh`: registers the protocol; computes sensitivities, both arms, the corner
check and the Monte Carlo; runs the controls. It sources the STD-E2E logging helper: every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N31b-08`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; a failed self-check
or control logs the first differing value and the inputs to repeat it. It exits zero when the measurement is
complete, whatever the verdict. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_tolerance/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the protocol hash, seed, tool pin,
`tolerances_allocated_um`, `tolerances_equal_um`, `cost_ratio`, `corner_failures`,
`failing_feature_share` (threshold 0.2), `coverage_allocated` and `coverage_equal` (threshold 0.99692), the
control results, and a `summary` with the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
The worked allocation (16.0 µm and 6.35 µm; 19.9% cheaper). The quadratic counterexample: for q(x) = x² at
nominal zero the sensitivity is zero and a first-order allocator would loosen without limit while the
deviation at tolerance t is t². Negative controls: the near-singular linkage; a Monte Carlo that reuses the
seed used to tune anything (refused by the registration check). Determinism: fixed seed; identical results on
every machine in double precision within 10⁻¹² of each other, and identical counts.

## Considerations

- KILL-TOLERANCE (decided at M5): the product gate fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of features in this pilot. Fallback: worst-case boxes per interface.
- Normal, independent deviations are an assumption of the pilot; real processes are neither. The pilot tests the linearization, not the process model.
- A positive pilot supports the allocation law on one small linkage; spatial chains and mode-switching clearances are tested by the product gate.
- The 1/t cost law is assumed in both arms; the cost ratio is meaningful only under it.
