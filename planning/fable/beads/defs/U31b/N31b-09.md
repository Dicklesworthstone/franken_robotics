## Background

Foundry's build packet (`.fbuild`) is one directory with a parts list, per-part geometry in four formats,
tolerances, mass properties, a wiring harness, a schedule and evidence, every file hashed, with four
no-authorization flags that are always false. It is a Frontier mechanism and needs a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier before its work is funded.

The risk the pilot addresses is not conceptual; it is plumbing. The sibling's STEP exporter accepts only a
solid produced by its own decoder, so a mesh must be taken through a write, parse, decode and re-emit chain
that refuses unless every coordinate bit replays. Four file formats must describe the same triangles. Mass
properties recomputed from each file must equal the model's. A purchased part must resolve to a card. If any
of that cannot be made to work by hand on two parts, the packet work package should not be funded as planned.

Ceiling, stated first: no time claim. The pilot shows that a packet can be produced and checked; it says
nothing about how long a person would take to produce one by other means.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; registration; a scripted, by-hand export of two parts with the sibling libraries; the
record; the reading against the falsifier.
Out: product code; harness routing and schedule beyond one net and one joint; packet review; the offline
auditor's checker.

## Technical approach

**1. Parts** (DESIGN, frozen at registration).
- *Made part:* a faceted tube: outer radius 15 mm, inner radius 13 mm, length 0.20 m, 256 sides, density
  2,700 kg/m³, process `cnc-3axis`. Its triangle mesh has 8 × 256 = 2,048 triangles and 4 × 256 = 1,024
  vertices (a closed surface of genus 1). Its faceted cross-section area is (256/2)·sin(2π/256)·(r_o² − r_i²)
  = 1.75912 × 10⁻⁴ m², so the model mass is 0.094992 kg (the smooth tube is 0.0950 kg).
- *Purchased part:* one actuator, by card reference and quantity 1, with no price.

**2. Steps, each by script against the sibling libraries.**
1. Validity of the made part's mesh: watertight, manifold, oriented.
2. Write STL, 3MF and GLB; read each back; recompute triangle count and volume.
3. STEP: build the syntax-level document of the faceted-solid entity chain; write it; parse and decode it
   with limits; re-emit the decoded solid; confirm every coordinate bit and triangle index replays. Expected
   instance count 3·F + V + 2 = 6,144 + 1,024 + 2 = 7,170.
4. Mass properties (mass, centre of mass, inertia) from the model and from each of the four files.
5. A manufacturability report for the made part from the limit checks of the sibling (minimum feature size
   against the 2 mm wall; tool radius against the internal radius).
6. A minimal packet directory written by the script: `manifest.json` (schema `frob.build/1`, identities,
   four flags all `false`, `reality: "no-claim"`, `review: "no-claim"`, `units: "SI"`, BLAKE3 of every file),
   `parts.json`, `geometry/`, `mass.json`, `tolerances.json` (one feature), `harness.json` (one net with two
   ends), `schedule.json` (one joint with the actuator card), `fab.json`.
7. Verification by a second, independent script: recompute every hash; check the four flags; check that the
   purchased part resolves to a card; check that the net has two ends and the joint has a card; compare mass
   properties among model, `mass.json` and the four files.

**3. Falsifier (TARGET).** Any cross-export mismatch (mass, centre of mass or inertia differing by more than
10⁻⁶ relative among the model, `mass.json` and the four files; a triangle count that differs) or a failed
round trip (the STEP replay refuses, or a mesh format does not read back).

**4. Controls.** Known answer: a tetrahedron through step 3 gives 18 instances and a bit-exact replay.
Potency: an open shell (one triangle removed) must be refused at step 1 or 3; one flipped coordinate bit
after the write must make the re-emit refuse; a manifest with `fabrication_released: true` must be rejected by
the verification script; an STL written in millimetres must show a volume ratio of 10⁹ at step 7.

**5. Record.** Pilot-register entry: protocol hash; tool pin; per file its digest, triangle count and
recomputed mass properties; the largest relative difference; the STEP instance count; control results; every
place where the sibling had to be worked around (these become the interim postures of upstream work items);
cost in engineer-hours; verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol and both parts are registered before the first file is written.
- [ ] The record lists four geometry files with digests and triangle counts, the STEP instance count (7,170 expected) and the largest relative mass-property difference against 10⁻⁶.
- [ ] The four controls ran: tetrahedron 18 instances and replay; open shell refused; flipped bit refused; true flag rejected; millimetre file reported at 10⁹.
- [ ] The verification script is separate from the writing script and recomputes every hash itself.
- [ ] The verdict is `positive` only with no mismatch and no failed round trip; a negative verdict states which link failed and that the reduced packet (parts list, mass properties and STL only) is the plan.
- [ ] Every workaround of a sibling limitation is listed with the upstream work item it belongs to.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the pilot's scripts: `tube_mesh_counts` (2,048 triangles; 1,024 vertices; Euler characteristic
0); `faceted_area` (1.75912 × 10⁻⁴ m²); `instance_formula` (7,170 for the tube; 18 for the tetrahedron);
`volume_from_triangles_matches_prism` (divergence-theorem volume equals area × length to 10⁻¹²).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_packet.sh`: registers the protocol, runs steps 1 to 7 and the controls. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_packet/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the protocol hash, tool pin, per file
`format`, `blake3`, `triangles`, `mass_kg`, `com_m`, `inertia_kgm2`; `mass_rel_diff_max` (threshold 1e-6);
`step_instances`; `replay_bit_exact`; control results; `summary` with the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
The STEP round trip of a tetrahedron (18 instances; bit-exact replay; an open shell is refused). The tube's
mass properties for the smooth tube (0.0950 kg; axial 1.87 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m²; transverse 3.26 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m²) as a
plausibility check on the faceted values. Negative controls: section 4. Determinism: the same files, byte for
byte, on a second run.

## Considerations

- KILL-PACKET (decided at M5): the geometry or packet gate fails after one repair cycle. Fallback: parts list, mass properties and STL only; STEP and the harness are deferred. A negative pilot is an early reading of that gate.
- Binary STL and GLB store single-precision coordinates; the pilot exports the tube about its centroid and records whether 10⁻⁶ holds with margin, which decides the export frame rule of the product.
- The pilot's packet is hand-made and is not a build packet: it is labelled a pilot artifact and kept out of any store of real packets.
- This pilot also serves as the pilot for the manufacturability checks, through step 5; they have no pilot row of their own.
