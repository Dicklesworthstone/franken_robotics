## Background

Firmware export is a Moonshot of Foundry: emit fixed-point source for joint-level controllers such that the
arithmetic run in simulation is, integer for integer, the arithmetic that would be shipped, with certificates
that no intermediate overflows on the declared input ranges. Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier before any of its work is funded; if the falsifier holds, the epic is not built.

The idea stands or falls on one capability: can overflow questions about a small controller be *decided*, by
a bit-vector decision procedure, quickly enough, with a certificate that an independent checker replays?
Interval arithmetic alone is too coarse for correlated terms and says nothing useful about an integrator,
whose range grows every tick. The sibling proof tool has a bit-blaster, a solver, a proof stream with an
independent streaming checker, and a decision entry point that returns a re-decoded counterexample or a
replayed unsatisfiability candidate; its last step toward a kernel proof term is not built, so a positive
answer is a checked certificate (class Certified), not a proof.

Fixed-point arithmetic has traps that a pilot can plant. In Q15 (a 16-bit signed word with 15 fractional
bits, range [−1, 1 − 2⁻¹⁵]): (−1)·(−1) is 0x8000·0x8000 = 0x40000000, which shifts right by 15 to 0x8000 and
reads as −1; the negation of −32768 overflows; an integrator adding 400 counts per tick from zero overflows
sixteen bits at tick 82. Ceiling, stated first: none on speed; the benefit is arithmetic identity between the
simulated and the exported controller.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; registration; hand-built bit-vector queries for one controller in two variants; the
run; the record; the reading against the falsifier.
Out: an emitter, a range analyser or any product code; controllers other than the proportional–integral loop;
timing on any board.

## Technical approach

**1. Controller** (DESIGN, frozen at registration), all values Q15 in 16-bit words, products in 32 bits
shifted right by 15:
- error e = r − y, with r and y declared in [−0.49, 0.49] (so e stays in [−0.98, 0.98] and cannot overflow; a
  declaration of [−0.5, 0.5] would allow e = +1, which Q15 cannot represent);
- proportional term p = (K_p·e) >> 15;
- integrator I ← I + d, where d = (K_i·e) >> 15 and the declared ranges give |d| ≤ 400 counts;
- output u = p + I.
Variant A has no clamp on I. Variant B clamps I to [−32,000, 32,000] after the addition.

**2. Obligations**, each posed as "does an input in the declared range exist for which this intermediate
overflows?":
- O1 product: with K_p and e over the full Q15 range, can (K_p·e) >> 15 leave [−32768, 32767]? Expected:
  satisfiable, with the counterexample K_p = e = 0x8000.
- O2 product with declared ranges (K_p in [0, 0.9], e in [−0.98, 0.98]): expected unsatisfiable.
- O3 negation: can −y overflow for y over the full range? Expected satisfiable at y = −32768; with y declared
  in [−0.49, 0.49], unsatisfiable.
- O4 integrator, base case: from I = 0, does one addition overflow? Unsatisfiable.
- O5 integrator, step, variant A: for I anywhere in the 16-bit range, can I + d overflow? Expected
  satisfiable; the documented witness is I = 32,400 with d = 400 (any I of at least 32,368 serves).
- O6 integrator, step, variant B: with the invariant |I| ≤ 32,000, can I + d overflow before the clamp, and
  does the clamp restore the invariant? Expected unsatisfiable for both parts (32,000 + 400 = 32,400 ≤ 32,767).
- O7 unrolled check of variant A: with d = 400 every tick from zero, the first overflow is at tick 82
  (400 × 81 = 32,400; 400 × 82 = 32,800).
- O8 output: u = p + I under the proved ranges of p and I for variant B. Expected satisfiable: p reaches
  about 28,900 counts (K_p = 0.9, e = 0.98; the exact count depends on the rounding of the declared bounds to
  Q15, which the protocol fixes) while I may stand at 32,000 from earlier ticks, and the two are independent,
  so u can reach about 60,900. The pilot records the counterexample and the largest clamp bound C for which O8
  is unsatisfiable (C = 32,767 − p_max, about 3,867), decided by the query and compared with that interval
  figure. Its role: to show that a certified state invariant does not by itself certify the output, so the
  exporter must also bound or clamp the output (an input to the range analysis of the mechanism, N31a-45).

**3. Procedure.** Each obligation is written as a bit-vector formula by a script, bit-blasted and decided by
`bv_decide` under a time budget of 2 s per obligation (the default of the open question on budgets). For each
unsatisfiable answer the proof stream is replayed by the independent checker. For each satisfiable answer the
counterexample is re-decoded and checked by executing the controller's integer arithmetic on it.

**4. Falsifier (TARGET).** A missed overflow (a planted overflow in O1, O3, O5 or O7 not found, or an
unsatisfiable verdict on any of them), or an obligation undecided inside its budget.

**5. Controls.** A widened input range must break a certificate: O2 with K_p widened to the full range must
flip to satisfiable. A corrupted proof stream (one record altered) must be rejected by the independent
checker. A trivially false query (an 8-bit addition of two values in [0, 10]) must be unsatisfiable in
milliseconds, showing the harness is not slow by construction.

**6. Record.** Pilot-register entry: protocol hash; tool pin; per obligation the verdict, the counterexample
or the certificate digest, the wall time against 2 s, and the checker's replay result; the controls; the
distribution of query times (the input to the budget decision N31b-29); cost; verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol, the controller and the eight obligations are registered before any query runs.
- [ ] The record holds, for each obligation, a verdict, a counterexample or a replayed certificate, and a wall time.
- [ ] O1, O3 (full range), O5 and O7 each return the planted overflow; O2, O3 (declared range), O4 and O6 are unsatisfiable with replayed proof streams.
- [ ] The three controls behave as stated.
- [ ] The verdict is `positive` only if no overflow was missed and no obligation was undecided within 2 s; otherwise the record states that the epic is not funded and that its fallback (parameters, Q formats and test vectors, no code) stands.
- [ ] No product code was written.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the pilot's scripts, in plain integer arithmetic: `q15_mul_minus_one_squared` (0x40000000 >> 15
= 0x8000, read as −32768); `q15_negate_min` (wraps to −32768); `integrator_first_overflow_tick` (82);
`step_witness_32400` (32,400 + 400 = 32,800 > 32,767); `clamp_restores_invariant` (32,400 clamps to 32,000);
`counterexample_replay` (every satisfiable answer overflows when executed); `output_bound_interval` (p_max from
the registered Q15 bounds; C = 32,767 − p_max).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_firmware.sh`: registers the protocol; builds the eight queries and the controls; runs
the decision procedure and the independent checker. It sources the STD-E2E logging helper: every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-10`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; a verdict
that differs from the expected one logs the obligation, the expected and observed verdicts and the path of the
formula file to repeat it; it exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the verdict. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_firmware/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the protocol hash, tool pin, and per
obligation `id`, `verdict`, `counterexample` or `certificate_digest`, `wall_ms` (threshold 2,000),
`replayed`; the control results; `summary` with missed overflows (0 required), undecided obligations (0
required) and the verdict.

### Known answers and negative controls
The fixed-point known answer: each counterexample found and the clamped controller certified; its control: a
widened input range breaks a certificate. The two counterexamples "Q15 multiplication cannot overflow" and
"an integrator without a clamp is safe if each step is small". Negative controls: section 5. Determinism: the
solver is deterministic for a fixed formula; verdicts and counterexamples are identical across runs, wall
times are not and are reported as a distribution.

## Considerations

- KILL-FIRMWARE (decided at M8): the product gate fails. Fallback: controller parameters, Q formats and the test-vector manifest only; no code. A negative pilot ends the epic before that gate.
- A certificate is for one controller, one set of ranges and one word length; the pilot does not show that query time stays small for larger controllers, and its record says so.
- The proof-term step is not built upstream; nothing in the record may use the word proved.
- The pilot runs on a Linux host where the sibling checker is admitted; it needs no device.
