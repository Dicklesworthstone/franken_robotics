## Background

Lifecycle compression is a Moonshot of Foundry. It asks what a robot's slow states (joint backlash from wear,
fatigue damage, winding insulation, cell capacity) look like after months of duty, without simulating months
of 500 Hz steps: 1,000 hours is 1.8 × 10⁹ steps. The method is two-timescale averaging. The state splits into
fast x and slow z with ẋ = f(x, z, u) and ż = ε·g(x, z); z is advanced by large macro steps, and the averaged
rate ⟨g⟩ is estimated from short micro windows of full simulation at fixed z (a settling phase, then about
twenty cycles of averaging), with step doubling for error control.

Every Moonshot needs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any of its work is funded. The pilot must
test the one thing the method assumes: that the slow evolution computed from averaged rates agrees with the
slow evolution of the full system, including when the slow state feeds back into the fast motion. A forced
oscillator with slow wear has both features and can be integrated directly for comparison.

Ceiling, stated first: the compression is a ratio of simulated steps, not of wall time and not of field
time. A positive pilot says nothing about whether the wear laws are right.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; registration; a stand-alone program (double precision, CPU, no workspace crates);
the record; the reading against the falsifier.
Out: robots, contact, cards with real coefficients, any product code in `frob-lifecycle`.

## Technical approach

**1. System** (DESIGN, frozen at registration). A damped oscillator forced at 2 Hz (period 0.5 s, like a gait
cycle): ẍ + 2·ζ·ω₀(z)·ẋ + ω₀(z)²·x = F·cos(Ω·t), Ω = 2π·2 rad/s. Slow wear: ż = ε·a·x², ε = 10⁻⁴. Feedback:
the natural frequency drifts with wear, ω₀(z)² = ω₀₀²·(1 − κ·z), so the forced amplitude, and with it the wear
rate, changes as z grows. Constants ζ, ω₀₀, F, a and κ are chosen so that z changes by order one over the
reference duration and so that, in the planted-collapse variant only, ω₀(z) crosses Ω at a known z*
(resonance: the amplitude rises sharply and the fast motion changes character).

**2. Reference.** Direct integration at 500 Hz for 100 hours: 1.8 × 10⁸ steps, by a fixed-step fourth-order
method, recording z every simulated hour.

**3. Compressed run.** Macro steps on z by the method's explicit rule z ← z + H·ε·⟨g⟩(z), with step doubling
(one step of H against two of H/2; three micro evaluations per accepted step; the difference of the two is the
error estimate, of order H² per step). Each micro evaluation: integrate the fast system at fixed z, settle until
the periodicity residual |x(t + T) − x(t)| is below tolerance, then average a·x² over twenty cycles. The
*rate* is averaged, never the input. All fast steps are counted, including settling and rejected macro steps.
Return to full resolution when the macro error estimate fails or the averaged rate changes by more than a
registered fraction between micro evaluations.

**4. Measures.** Compression = reference steps divided by compressed steps. Error = the largest relative
difference in z between the compressed run and the reference at the hourly marks.

**5. Falsifier (TARGET).** Error above 5% at a compression of 50 in steps: the compressed run is tuned (macro
tolerance) to use at most 3.6 × 10⁶ fast steps, and the error is read there.

**6. Known answers inside the pilot.**
- No feedback (κ = 0) and unit steady amplitude, x = sin ωt: z(T) = ε·a·(T/2 − sin(2ωT)/(4ω)), which is
  ε·a·T/2 over whole periods. The micro average must return a/2.
- Linearized feedback: where the squared amplitude is affine in z over the range used, ż = ε·(c₀ + c₁·z) and
  z(t) = (z₀ + c₀/c₁)·exp(ε·c₁·t) − c₀/c₁.

**7. Controls.**
- Averaging the input: a variant that averages x over the window and then squares it gets zero growth and
  must be reported as wrong. The hand case behind the rule: with a life law that halves per 10 K, half the
  time at T_ref and half at T_ref + 20 K consumes life at 2.5/τ_ref, while the law at the mean temperature
  gives 2.0/τ_ref.
- Planted collapse: in the variant where ω₀(z) reaches Ω at z*, the compressed run must leave macro stepping
  and return to full resolution before z*; a run that steps across z* is a failure even if the final error is
  small.
- Potency: at a compression of 500 the error must exceed 5% or the run must refuse the step size, showing the
  comparison can fail.
- Non-integer window: averaging over 20.125 cycles must be flagged by the periodicity check or corrected by the
  window rule. For a pure sine the uncorrected average of a·x² is biased by 1/(2·Ω·T_w) = 1/(4π·20.125) = 0.4%
  (0.3954%), the case N31-KA registers as KA-750's control. A window of 20.5 cycles would not serve: x² has
  half the period of x, so 20.5 cycles of x are 41 whole periods of x² and the average is unbiased.

**8. Record.** One record in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema
`frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09), written at registration: tier `M`, mechanism "lifecycle compression", bead
N31b-11, work package BD-423, the protocol's path and BLAKE3 hash with the constants, the falsifier of section 5,
ceiling "a ratio of simulated steps; nothing on wall time, field time or the wear laws", fallback `{ ships =
"aged model variants at hand-specified wear states, no time axis", kill_gate = "KILL-LIFECYCLE" }`, tool site
`host`, `integrated_gate = "GATE-FOUNDRY-12"`, status `registered`, an identifier of the register's form
`PILOT-<set>-<nn>`. The result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]`: reference and compressed step counts; the
compression; the error curve by hour; the control results; cost; verdict `falsifier-rejected` (the method held)
or `falsifier-holds` (it failed), or `inconclusive` when a control did not behave as stated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol and all constants are registered before the reference is integrated.
- [ ] The record holds the step counts (reference 1.8 × 10⁸), the compression achieved (at least 50) and the largest slow-state error against 5%.
- [ ] The micro average returns a/2 for the no-feedback case within 10⁻⁶; the linearized-feedback closed form is matched by the reference within 1%.
- [ ] The four controls behave as stated; in particular the planted collapse forces full resolution before z*.
- [ ] The verdict is `falsifier-rejected` only if the error is at most 5% at a compression of at least 50 and every control behaved as stated; otherwise the record states that the epic is not funded, that aged model variants at hand-specified wear states are the fallback, and that `frob-lifecycle` is not created.
- [ ] The record states that the ratio is in simulated steps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the pilot program: `mean_of_sin_squared_is_half`; `linear_feedback_closed_form` (c₀ = 1,
c₁ = 0.5, ε = 10⁻⁴, z₀ = 0, t = 10⁴: z = 2·(e^0.5 − 1) = 1.2974); `step_doubling_estimate_order` (halving H
reduces the estimate fourfold for the explicit macro rule of section 3, whose local error is of order H²); `step_count_includes_settling_and_rejections`;
`rate_average_two_temperatures` (2.5 against 2.0).

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_register.rs` (N38a-09's file, row-specific names):
`lifecycle_pilot_row_complete` (falsifier, ceiling, fallback with kill gate KILL-LIFECYCLE, a protocol whose file
hashes to the registered BLAKE3); `lifecycle_pilot_registered_before_result` (the result's date and ledger
sequence follow the registration, and its `protocol_blake3` equals the registered hash).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_lifecycle.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N31b-11 milestone=M6 labels=foundry,moonshot,pilot
gates=KILL-LIFECYCLE seed=0 needs=cpu plants=average-input,step-across-collapse,compression-500,window-20.125`):
registers the protocol; runs the reference, the compressed run and the controls, one step each (`register`,
`reference`, `compressed`, `controls`, `record`). It sources the STD-E2E logging helper: every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N31b-11`), `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; a control that does not behave as stated logs the
expected and observed values and the constants file to repeat it; it exits zero when the measurement is
complete, whatever the verdict. Log: `target/e2e-logs/pilot_foundry_lifecycle/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the protocol hash;
`steps_reference`, `steps_compressed`, `compression` (threshold 50), `z_error_max` (threshold 0.05), the
hourly error series, `returned_to_full_resolution_at_z` against z* for the planted variant, control results,
and a `summary` with the verdict. The pilot delivers no product code and nothing a `frob` command can reach (a
stand-alone program and a pilot entry), so it has no `scripts/e2e/` script: this registered pilot script is
its end-to-end script.

### Known answers and negative controls
Averaged growth (z(T) = ε·a·T/2 over whole periods). Wear feedback (the exponential closed form; its control:
a planted collapse at z* must force full resolution before z*). Rate averaging (2.5/τ_ref, not 2.0/τ_ref;
control: averaging the input is caught). Step arithmetic: 500 Hz × 100 h × 3,600 s/h = 1.8 × 10⁸. Negative
controls: section 7. Determinism: double precision, fixed step; identical results twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- KILL-LIFECYCLE (decided at M6): the product gate fails. Fallback: aged model variants at hand-specified wear states, with no time axis and no compression claim. A negative pilot ends the epic before that gate.
- A smooth oscillator is the easy case: a robot in contact has non-smooth fast dynamics and gait changes. A positive pilot funds the next step; it does not predict the gate.
- The pilot's wear law is a stand-in. Real coefficients do not exist with provenance yet (open question on wear, insulation and cell-fade data); the pilot neither needs nor supplies them.
