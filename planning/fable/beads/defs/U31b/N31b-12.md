## Background

A gate, in this program, passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold: a row of
`registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and that resolves in
the artifact graph. A gate with no record has the status `not run`, which is not a pass. Statuses are `not
run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩`. Each gate names exactly one of
the seven claims (Proof, Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality, Performance). Every
convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches. A kill gate names its trigger and its
fallback in advance, is decided at a named milestone by an ADR, and is never quietly re-scoped; a kill gate
that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan. Where a chapter and the gate
register differ, the register governs.

Foundry defines fifteen convergence gates and seven kill gates. Twelve gates are in the register's main
table; three were added by late subsections and are listed in the register's index of late gates. The chapter
and the register disagree on four milestones, and the register's ruling is recorded here so that no bead
follows the superseded date. The gate rows also drive the status table of the README and the milestone exits:
a milestone exits when every gate with a part due there has a pass or a kill decision with its fallback
adopted.

This bead creates the rows (all `not run`) with everything that can be known in advance. It measures nothing.

## Scope

In: twenty-two rows with their fields; the milestone rulings; the claim of each gate; the form check for the
two design-comparison rows; the map to measuring and deciding beads; the consumer row for the actuator
chapter's ageing kill gate.
Out: measurements (the beads named per row); the ADRs (the kill-gate beads); regenerating the printed
register.

## Technical approach

**Row fields** (DESIGN, following the registry's schema): `id`, `claim`, `threshold` (the full criterion of
the defining subsection, not the register's brief), `earliest` (per the register), `defined_in`,
`measured_by` (bead key and script), `controls` (identifiers in the negative-control registry), `kill` (the
kill gate it triggers, if any), `status`, `claim_ids`, `receipt`.

Consumer row: KILL-SINEW-AGE (owned by the actuator chapter; decided at M6 with the lifecycle epic): the
ageing law fails its pilot, or no card with ageing coefficients better than `assumed` exists when the
lifecycle work is funded; fallback: capacity fade and resistance growth are user-set constants per scenario.

**Milestone rulings recorded in the rows.** (1) The chapter tags GATE-FOUNDRY-01 to -03 and KILL-LOADS at M4;
Foundry lands at M5; the register sets M5 and allows the three gates to be recorded at M4. (2) The chapter
tags GATE-FOUNDRY-12 and KILL-LIFECYCLE at M8; lifecycle compression is an M6 item; the register sets M6. (3)
The late gates take the milestone of their defining rows: 13 and 14 at M5, 15 at M3. (4) KILL-PACKET is
unchanged by the packet-review subsection.

**Form check for design-comparison rows.** GATE-FOUNDRY-05 and the corpus comparison inside GATE-FOUNDRY-15
are method experiments: their rows are not admitted until the six parts are registered (ceiling; competing
arms; potency control and A/A arm; paired lower bound; pre-declared fallback; reachability pre-analysis). The
row stores the registration's hash. For GATE-FOUNDRY-05: ceiling 0.27 × 0.30 ≈ 0.08 of robot mass; arms are
the optimized link, the hand-sized tube and the beam rung; the fallback is KILL-TOPOPT's.

**Bets.** The generative-structure bet is read from GATE-FOUNDRY-05, -08 and -09 with KILL-TOPOPT,
KILL-TOLERANCE and KILL-PACKET at M5; the rows carry that tag so the bet's status can be generated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds fifteen Foundry gate rows and seven kill-gate rows, each `not run`, each with claim, full criterion, earliest milestone per the register, defining subsection and measuring or deciding bead.
- [ ] The registry check fails if a Foundry row lacks a claim, names two claims, lacks a control (convergence gates) or names a missing bead.
- [ ] The four milestone rulings are present as notes on their rows, and no row carries the superseded milestone.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-05 and the corpus part of GATE-FOUNDRY-15 cannot be set to `pass` without a registered six-part form (checked by the registry loader).
- [ ] The README status table generated from the registry shows the twenty-two rows.
- [ ] Every Foundry gate identifier appears in the `gates` field of at least one bead that measures or decides it (cross-check script over the bead definitions).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test gates_registry -- foundry`: `foundry_rows_present` (22);
`each_gate_has_one_claim`; `convergence_gates_have_controls`; `kill_gates_have_trigger_fallback_milestone`;
`milestones_follow_register` (01 to 03 and KILL-LOADS at M5; 12 and KILL-LIFECYCLE at M6; 13 and 14 at M5; 15
at M3; 11 and KILL-FIRMWARE at M8); `design_comparison_needs_form_hash`; `pass_requires_receipt_and_commit`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_gate_rows.sh`: loads the registry through `frob doctor --json` (or the registry loader's
command), lists the Foundry rows, and runs `cargo xtask check-contracts`, which fails on a release path whose
claims are missing from the registry. Logs per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/foundry_gate_rows/`: per row its
id, claim, earliest milestone and status; the summary counts rows by status.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the counts (15 and 7) and the milestone table. Negative controls: a row edited to `pass`
without a receipt (the loader must refuse); a row with claim "Known answer; Design comparison" (two claims;
refused); a kill row without a fallback; GATE-FOUNDRY-12 set to M8 (the milestone test fails). Determinism:
the registry is data; identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- GATE-FOUNDRY-15 was printed with two claims. The register's rule gives it the claim of its governing part; this bead reads the known-answer clauses as governing and treats the corpus share as a recorded figure that decides only a template default. The register owner may rule otherwise before M3.
- The register's table carries briefs; the defining rows carry clauses the brief leaves out, and a clause the brief leaves out is not waived. The rows here store the full criterion.
- A threshold, trigger or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs.
