## Background

A gate, in this program, passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold: a row of
`registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and that resolves in
the artifact graph. A gate with no record has the status `not run`, which is not a pass. Statuses are `not
run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩` (the plan's five; the record
stores them per part as the seven statuses of N40-01, listed under Technical approach). Each gate names exactly one of
the seven claims (Proof, Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Design comparison, Reality, Performance). Every
convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches. A kill gate names its trigger and its
fallback in advance, is decided at a named milestone by an ADR, and is never quietly re-scoped; a kill gate
that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan. Where a chapter and the gate
register differ, the register governs.

Foundry defines fifteen convergence gates and seven kill gates. Twelve gates are in the register's main
table; three were added by late subsections and are listed in the register's index of late gates. The chapter
and the register disagree on four milestones, and the register's ruling is recorded here so that no bead
follows the superseded date. The gate rows also drive the status table of the README and the milestone exits:
a milestone exits when every gate with a part due there has a pass or a kill decision with its fallback
adopted.

The record of each gate and kill gate is created once, by the register bead that owns it (Scope names each).
This bead supplies the chapter's side of every one, with everything that can be known in advance: the full
criterion, the controls, the measuring or deciding bead, the milestone rulings as the chapter must read them.
It measures nothing.

## Scope

This bead is the Foundry chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the fifteen gates: the full criterion of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads as
  the defining statement); the `controls` and `tier` fields of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the
  chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead and script; the owner's claim reading of
  GATE-FOUNDRY-15; the form check for the two design-comparison rows.
- For the seven kill gates: the map from each to its deciding bead, and the check that each has its ledger
  record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The milestone rulings, as notes of the declarations; the consumer declaration for the actuator chapter's
  ageing kill gate.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-FOUNDRY-01 to -12 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-FOUNDRY-13 to -15: N40-10.
- The kill records of the seven Foundry kill gates, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each
  trigger reads: the fallback ledger, N40-27. The record of KILL-SINEW-AGE is N40-23's.
- Measurements (the beads named per row); the ADRs (the kill-gate beads); regenerating the printed register.

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not** (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart). `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01. The records of GATE-FOUNDRY-01 to -12 are
created by N40-02 and those of GATE-FOUNDRY-13 to -15 by N40-10, with the fields the register governs: `id`,
`kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and, in `threshold`, the register's brief. The seven kill records are created by the fallback
ledger (N40-27) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no record and
writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the identifiers in the negative-control registry
   (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`), and the tiers the gate is measured on.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
   until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side
   data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full
   criterion of the defining subsection (the table below), of which the record's `threshold` is a brief;
   `measured_by`, the bead key and script; `kill`, the kill gate the gate triggers, if any; `notes`, for the
   milestone rulings; `bet`; and, for a design-comparison row, `form_hash`.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record. It keeps the map from each kill gate to its deciding bead as data of the
   registry check. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is
   written by the deciding bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30. For
   KILL-SINEW-AGE the Foundry declaration has `role = "consumer"`.

The Claim, Earliest, Trigger, Fallback and Decided-at columns below are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-27 and N40-23 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree; a note such as "may be recorded at M4" is a note and adds no part, and the tier marks "[M]" beside
two claims are the mechanism's tier and not part of the claim. If the two ever differ, the register governs
(rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record.
Statuses are the seven of N40-01, per part (`not-run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed`, `blocked`, `no-claim`,
`superseded`); a part's `claim_id` and `receipt` are fields of the record. A kill decision's outcome is
`not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred` (N40-03).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds fifteen Foundry gate records and seven kill records once each (created by N40-02, N40-10 and N40-27; none by this bead), every part `not-run`; each gate has an owner declaration with its full criterion and measuring bead, whose claim and milestone parts equal the record's; each kill gate has a deciding bead.
- [ ] A planted second record for a Foundry identifier fails the load with `duplicate-id`.
- [ ] The registry check fails if a Foundry row lacks a claim, names two claims, lacks a control (convergence gates) or names a missing bead.
- [ ] The four milestone rulings are present as notes of their declarations, and no record or declaration carries the superseded milestone.
- [ ] GATE-FOUNDRY-05 and the corpus part of GATE-FOUNDRY-15 cannot be set to `pass` without a registered six-part form (checked by the registry loader).
- [ ] The README status table generated from the registry shows the twenty-two rows.
- [ ] Every Foundry gate identifier appears in the `gates` field of at least one bead that measures or decides it (cross-check script over the bead definitions).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test gates_registry -- foundry`: `foundry_rows_present` (22 identifiers, one
record each); `declarations_agree_with_records`;
`each_gate_has_one_claim`; `convergence_gates_have_controls`; `kill_gates_have_trigger_fallback_milestone`;
`milestones_follow_register` (01 to 03 and KILL-LOADS at M5; 12 and KILL-LIFECYCLE at M6; 13 and 14 at M5; 15
at M3; 11 and KILL-FIRMWARE at M8); `design_comparison_needs_form_hash`; `pass_requires_receipt_and_commit`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_gate_rows.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): loads the registry through `frob doctor
--registries --json`, lists the Foundry rows, runs `cargo xtask check-contracts`, which fails on a release path
whose claims are missing from the registry, and then the four negative controls of the section below on scratch
copies of the registry, each of which must fail the load or the test. Logs to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_gate_rows/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N31b-12`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; per row its id, claim,
earliest milestone, status per part, measuring or deciding bead and whether its owner declaration agrees with
the record; on a disagreement the field, both values and the two file paths. The `summary` counts rows by
status (22 expected, all `not-run` at this bead's closure) and controls caught (4 of 4).

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the counts (15 and 7) and the milestone table. Negative controls: a row edited to `pass`
without a receipt (the loader must refuse); a row with claim "Known answer; Design comparison" (two claims;
refused); a kill row without a fallback; GATE-FOUNDRY-12 set to M8 (the milestone test fails). Determinism:
the registry is data; identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- Dependencies (DESIGN): besides the register (N40-01) and the beads that create the records (N40-02, N40-10, the
  fallback ledgers N40-27 and N40-23), this bead's tests run the agreement lint (N40-09) on its declarations and
  the ledger cross-check (N40-30) on the kill-gate map, and its script reads the registries section of
  `frob doctor` (N32ax-03) and runs `xtask check-contracts` (N34-08) through the STD-E2E helper; each is a
  dependency for that reason (convention 7), and all are M0 or M1.

- GATE-FOUNDRY-15 was printed with two claims. The register's rule gives it the claim of its governing part; this bead reads the known-answer clauses (the tube case and the planted under-built link) as governing and treats the corpus share as a recorded figure that decides only a template default. This is the owner's reading: N40-10 enters the row with claim `known-answer` on it, and the row is not held `blocked: claim reading`.
- The register's table carries briefs; the defining rows carry clauses the brief leaves out, and a clause the brief leaves out is not waived. The declarations here store the full criterion.
- A threshold, trigger or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs.
