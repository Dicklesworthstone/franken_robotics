## Background

Foundry sizes links on two rungs: closed-form beam sizing (milliseconds; the engineered baseline; the
structural-margin analysis) and three-dimensional topology optimization for finalists (CPU-hours for a whole
robot). The second rung is a Frontier mechanism behind the generative-structure bet: links optimized under
simulated load histories are lighter than engineered parts at equal margin. The published record puts the
saving at 7 to 30% of link mass, which is 2 to 8% of robot mass on the one quadruped with a published mass
breakdown, and it shortens no decision.

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; a trigger,
threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs; and a kill
gate that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan.

KILL-TOPOPT. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-05 fails: saving under 7%, no convergence, or over budget. Fallback:
beam-rung sizing supplies mass and inertia feedback; three-dimensional optimization becomes an offline option
or is dropped; the structural-margin analysis is unaffected. Decided at M5 and recorded as an ADR. This bead
is that decision.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt and the two pilot records; the decision rule; the choice between "offline
option" and "dropped"; the downstream changes on each branch; the ADR; the fail-closed posture until decided.
Out: running the comparison (N31b-13); the pilots (N31b-06, N31b-07); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** The GATE-FOUNDRY-05 row: the one-sided 95% lower bound on the mean saving over five
   pairs against 0.07; convergence within five iterations in five of five pairs; the largest coarse-solve
   time against 120 s per link class; the potency, A/A and independence controls. The GATE-FOUNDRY-04 row
   (two-bar and mesh audit), which step 3 reads, with the known-answer battery's receipt (N31b-05). And the pilot records
   (first readings): stage 1 against 5% and 7%; stage 2 against 5%, the 2% change between iterations four and
   five, and the window-halving clause.
2. **Decision rule** (outcomes in N40-03's names, the kill-gate form every decision bead uses).
   - A pilot negative before funding: `not-reached`. The topology rung was never funded and is not described;
     the beam rung, which exists in any case, is what `foundry.optimize` runs; GATE-FOUNDRY-05 stays `not-run`,
     and the ADR cites the pilot's `[[pilot.result]]`.
   - Gate measured, all three clauses pass and controls behave: `not-fired`, citing the gate receipt.
   - Any clause fails: `fired`. No repair cycle is granted by the gate's text. A measurement voided by a failed
     control (the harness could not see the planted effect, or the A/A arm was not null) is not a failure of
     the mechanism: the harness is repaired and the measurement repeated once, under the same registration.
   - The gate not measured by the M5 exit although the rung was funded: `deferred`, with the later exit and
     the reason in the ADR (N40-03 rule 3).
3. **Offline option or dropped** (the fallback leaves this open; DESIGN rule for the ADR). Offline option if
   the two-bar and meshing gate (GATE-FOUNDRY-04) passed and the failure was on budget or on a saving between
   0 and 7%: `foundry.optimize --offline` remains, labelled exploratory-grade, outside the loop and outside
   packets (the option does not exist before this decision; on this branch the change that adds it to the
   VERBS-foundry row and to BD-403's verb is an item of the ADR's downstream list, step 4, and no script
   calls it before that change). Dropped if GATE-FOUNDRY-04 failed, the loop did not converge, or the saving bound was at or below
   zero: the adapters to the sibling's optimization crates are removed from the airlock's structural group.
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.optimize` returns the beam rung's sized sections as its result, with `rung: "beam"` and the
     note `KILL-TOPOPT`; the loads–mass loop (inertia feedback, convergence test, refusal 260) runs on the
     beam rung unchanged.
   - The packet's made links are stock sections; their geometry export, tolerances and harness are
     unaffected; the manufacturability checks apply to stock sections trivially.
   - The scenario "one link re-optimized to be lighter at equal margin" is recorded as demonstrated under the
     fallback's name: sized, not optimized.
   - The open question on meshing arbitrary optimized surfaces loses its consumer and is closed as moot.
   - The bet on generative structure is recorded lost on its structure clause; the program's thesis is
     untouched, because Foundry is outside it.
   - Unaffected: load extraction, the independent check, fatigue utilization, member sizing in screening.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** No report states a mass saving from optimization; an optimized
   geometry can enter a packet only after the gate row is `pass`; before that, packets use beam-rung sections.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TOPOPT-M5.md` (the program's one place for decision records and the
   kill-gate naming `ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>`), with the required fields of the decision template of
   N40-03: the kill gate and milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied (none after the
   first campaign); the registration hash and each of the three clause values with its receipt hash beside
   its number; the controls; the pilots' readings; the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the
   capability carries from now on ("beam-rung sizing: sized, not optimized" if fired); the offline-or-dropped
   choice with its reason; the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this
   ADR"; the downstream list of step 4 (gates that become `killed` or `superseded`, wording withdrawn); and the
   session that recorded it, which did not implement the optimizer. The KILL-TOPOPT row of
   `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the fallback ledger N40-27) gains `decision = {outcome, adr, date,
   session, receipts}`; GATE-FOUNDRY-05 keeps the row its measuring session recorded from N31b-13's receipt, and
   on `fired` its failed part takes status `killed` through N40-03's propagation, not by an edit of this bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The KILL-TOPOPT row holds the decision with one of `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred`, at M5; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-TOPOPT.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_topopt.rs`: `decision_table` (bound 0.069 → `fired`; 0.070 with the other
clauses passing → `not-fired`; convergence in four of five pairs → `fired`; 121 s → `fired`; no gate receipt at
the exit with the rung funded → `deferred` naming a later exit); `voided_measurement_is_not_a_fire` (failed

`offline_or_dropped_rule` (the four input combinations of step 3).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_topopt.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the GATE-FOUNDRY-05 receipt, the
GATE-FOUNDRY-04 row (with the battery receipt of N31b-05) and the pilot records, evaluates the rule, prints the
decision, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`, and, on a scratch gate register with the
decision `fired`, runs `frob foundry optimize --fixture planted-gain
--loop --key "$(foundry_key kt-fallback)" --json` (`foundry_key <label>` is the cluster preamble's name,
N31a-51, for STD-E2E's `e2e_key <label>`; the run has its own state root, so the fired-switch call is never
answered from a stored receipt of an unfired one) to show the beam-rung branch (`rung: "beam"`, the note, the loop's iterates).
Logs to `target/e2e-logs/kill_topopt/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N31b-15`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the three clause values
with thresholds and units, the GATE-FOUNDRY-04 status, control results, pilot readings, the receipt hashes read,
the decision and the offline-or-dropped choice; on a rejected receipt the field and the hash mismatch. The
`summary` carries `gates: ["KILL-TOPOPT", "GATE-FOUNDRY-05"]`. Exit zero when the decision is computed, whatever
it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the rule on planted receipts (the table above). Negative controls: a receipt whose threshold
field was edited to 0.05 (the registration hash check must reject it); a receipt without the A/A result
(incomplete; no decision); an ADR that states a saving without a receipt (rejected by the claim-resolution
check).

## Considerations

- The pilot's falsifier (5%) is weaker than the gate (7%); a design that passes the pilot can still be killed here.
- A saving on one link class does not license a robot-level statement; the ADR prints the link's share of robot mass.
- If the sibling's three-dimensional lane changes upstream after a kill, reopening needs a new registration and a new measurement, not a re-reading of old data.
