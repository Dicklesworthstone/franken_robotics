## Background

Foundry sizes links on two rungs: closed-form beam sizing (milliseconds; the engineered baseline; the
structural-margin analysis) and three-dimensional topology optimization for finalists (CPU-hours for a whole
robot). The second rung is a Frontier mechanism behind the generative-structure bet: links optimized under
simulated load histories are lighter than engineered parts at equal margin. The published record puts the
saving at 7 to 30% of link mass, which is 2 to 8% of robot mass on the one quadruped with a published mass
breakdown, and it shortens no decision.

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; a trigger,
threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs; and a kill
gate that fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan.

KILL-TOPOPT. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-05 fails: saving under 7%, no convergence, or over budget. Fallback:
beam-rung sizing supplies mass and inertia feedback; three-dimensional optimization becomes an offline option
or is dropped; the structural-margin analysis is unaffected. Decided at M5 and recorded as an ADR. This bead
is that decision.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt and the two pilot records; the decision rule; the choice between "offline
option" and "dropped"; the downstream changes on each branch; the ADR; the fail-closed posture until decided.
Out: running the comparison (N31b-13); the pilots (N31b-06, N31b-07); the mechanisms.

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** The GATE-FOUNDRY-05 row: the one-sided 95% lower bound on the mean saving over five
   pairs against 0.07; convergence within five iterations in five of five pairs; the largest coarse-solve
   time against 120 s per link class; the potency, A/A and independence controls. And the pilot records
   (first readings): stage 1 against 5% and 7%; stage 2 against 5%, the 2% change between iterations four and
   five, and the window-halving clause.
2. **Decision rule.**
   - A pilot negative before funding: the topology rung was never funded; this decision records `killed:
     fallback adopted` on the pilot's record, and GATE-FOUNDRY-05 is recorded `not run: pilot negative`.
   - Gate measured, all three clauses pass and controls behave: KILL-TOPOPT does not fire.
   - Any clause fails: KILL-TOPOPT fires. No repair cycle is granted by the gate's text. A measurement voided
     by a failed control (the harness could not see the planted effect, or the A/A arm was not null) is not a
     failure of the mechanism: the harness is repaired and the measurement repeated once, under the same
     registration.
3. **Offline option or dropped** (the fallback leaves this open; DESIGN rule for the ADR). Offline option if
   the two-bar and meshing gate (GATE-FOUNDRY-04) passed and the failure was on budget or on a saving between
   0 and 7%: `foundry.optimize --offline` remains, labelled exploratory-grade, outside the loop and outside
   packets. Dropped if GATE-FOUNDRY-04 failed, the loop did not converge, or the saving bound was at or below
   zero: the adapters to the sibling's optimization crates are removed from the airlock's structural group.
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.optimize` returns the beam rung's sized sections as its result, with `rung: "beam"` and the
     note `KILL-TOPOPT`; the loads–mass loop (inertia feedback, convergence test, refusal 260) runs on the
     beam rung unchanged.
   - The packet's made links are stock sections; their geometry export, tolerances and harness are
     unaffected; the manufacturability checks apply to stock sections trivially.
   - The scenario "one link re-optimized to be lighter at equal margin" is recorded as demonstrated under the
     fallback's name: sized, not optimized.
   - The open question on meshing arbitrary optimized surfaces loses its consumer and is closed as moot.
   - The bet on generative structure is recorded lost on its structure clause; the program's thesis is
     untouched, because Foundry is outside it.
   - Unaffected: load extraction, the independent check, fatigue utilization, member sizing in screening.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** No report states a mass saving from optimization; an optimized
   geometry can enter a packet only after the gate row is `pass`; before that, packets use beam-rung sections.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-TOPOPT.md`: the registration hash, the three clause values, the controls, the
   pilots' readings, the decision, the offline-or-dropped choice with its reason, and the downstream list;
   the rows of `registries/gates.toml` for KILL-TOPOPT and GATE-FOUNDRY-05 with claim identifiers; written by
   a session that did not implement the optimizer.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at M5 with each trigger clause evaluated against its number (0.07; five iterations; 120 s).
- [ ] The KILL-TOPOPT row reads `pass` on GATE-FOUNDRY-05 with the kill not fired, or `killed: fallback adopted`.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry optimize --json` returns beam-rung sections with the note; the loop still converges on the planted-gain fixture; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes; beads that exist only on the surviving branch are closed with a typed reason; the offline-or-dropped choice is recorded.
- [ ] If not fired: the gate row is cited by the packet path that admits optimized geometry.
- [ ] No amendment to trigger, threshold or fallback was made after the first campaign of the measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_topopt.rs`: `decision_table` (bound 0.069 fires; 0.070 does not;
convergence in four of five pairs fires; 121 s fires); `voided_measurement_is_not_a_fire` (failed potency
control → `repeat`, at most once); `pilot_negative_records_not_run`; `offline_or_dropped_rule` (the four input
combinations of step 3).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_topopt.sh`: reads the GATE-FOUNDRY-05 receipt and the pilot records, evaluates the rule,
prints the decision, and, on a fixture registry with the kill fired, runs `frob foundry optimize --fixture
planted-gain --loop --json` to show the beam-rung branch. Logs per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/kill_topopt/`:
the three clause values with thresholds, control results, pilot readings, the decision, and a `summary` with
`gates: ["KILL-TOPOPT", "GATE-FOUNDRY-05"]`. Exit zero when the decision is computed, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the rule on planted receipts (the table above). Negative controls: a receipt whose threshold
field was edited to 0.05 (the registration hash check must reject it); a receipt without the A/A result
(incomplete; no decision); an ADR that states a saving without a receipt (rejected by the claim-resolution
check).

## Considerations

- The pilot's falsifier (5%) is weaker than the gate (7%); a design that passes the pilot can still be killed here.
- A saving on one link class does not license a robot-level statement; the ADR prints the link's share of robot mass.
- If the sibling's three-dimensional lane changes upstream after a kill, reopening needs a new registration and a new measurement, not a re-reading of old data.
