## Background

Foundry's structure loop starts from load cases extracted from simulation telemetry: for each link, real
instants that are extreme in some direction of load space (envelope cases, for sizing) and signed stress
histories at gauges (spectra, for fatigue). The extraction is checked by a free-body balance on every sample
and by the rule that a stress computed from unit loads equals a direct solve. It is a Frontier mechanism and
has a kill gate with a fallback named in advance, so that losing it costs a capability and never the
critical path.

KILL-LOADS. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-02 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback: load cases are declared by hand
from statics with a stated dynamic factor; spectra and fatigue read `no-claim`. The chapter printed the
decision at M4; Foundry lands at M5 and the gate register sets M5, allowing the gate to be recorded at M4.
The decision is recorded as an ADR. This bead is that decision.

GATE-FOUNDRY-02 (Known answer, TARGET): free-body residual at most 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee and 10⁻³ on
single-precision tiers; superposition equals direct solves (10³ random samples, relative difference at most
10⁻⁹ on the referee); the assembled-peak and equal-limb mutations refused.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt; the repair-cycle rule; the decision; downstream changes on each branch; the
ADR; the posture until decided.
Out: load extraction and its tests (BD-400); the proof-obligation tests (N31b-02).

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** The GATE-FOUNDRY-02 row: the largest free-body residual on the referee and on each
   single-precision tier with the dropped fraction; the largest superposition difference; whether each of the
   two mutations was refused (the known-answer battery's receipt, N31b-05, beside the loads script's, N31a-53);
   the controls' `caught` flags. Beside it, the ledger row of PO-FOUNDRY-01 (N31b-02), whose discharge is the
   superposition clause measured by the obligation's own test.
2. **One repair cycle** (N40-03 step 5's reading). A first failure opens exactly one repair cycle: a `note`
   line in the measuring bead's log with `cause = "repair-cycle: <failed receipt hash>: <hypothesis>"`, naming
   the failing clause; one fix to the mechanism; one re-measurement with the same threshold on the same
   fixtures. Changing a threshold, a fixture or the residual's scaling is not a repair. A second failure fires
   the kill gate; a second cycle would be a `deferred` decision with its own ADR.
3. **Decision** (outcomes in N40-03's names). Pass on the first or the second measurement: `not-fired`,
   citing the passing receipt. Fail twice: `fired`. The gate not measured by the M5 exit: `deferred`, with the
   later exit and the reason in the ADR. (`not-reached` does not arise: the extraction has no pilot of its own
   and is funded with the work package.)
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.loads` no longer extracts: with the KILL-LOADS switch set, `--campaign` refuses with
     FROB-REF-724 (exit 5) and offers `--declared statics` as its repair (N31a-13). Load sets are declared
     from statics: `foundry.loads <design> --declared statics [--dynamic-factor <k>] [--patterns <list>]`
     generates per link the port wrenches of each support pattern (stance, trot diagonals, single support,
     payload at rated value) under a stated dynamic factor printed with every result, in the file format
     `frob.foundry.declared-loads/1` of N31a-13, which also accepts hand-written cases; the consumers read it
     with `--loads <file>`. Each declared case is one consistent static instant and passes the free-body check
     at 10⁻⁹; the time-coherence rule still applies to how a declaration is written.
   - Spectra do not exist: every fatigue result reads `fatigue: no-claim`; the structural check reports
     stress, buckling and modes only.
   - The beam rung and the topology rung run on the declared cases; results carry the label `loads:
     declared` and class Estimated; the stage-2 comparison's fatigue-equality condition cannot be evaluated,
     so the optimization gate is read at equal stiffness and stress only and says so.
   - Referee re-extraction has nothing to re-extract: the promotion rule for load cases is not applicable,
     and a packet states `loads: declared` in place of re-extraction records. GATE-FOUNDRY-14 (N31b-37) is
     therefore in the ADR's downstream list: its parts take status `killed` with KILL-LOADS, the fallback and
     this ADR in `detail`.
   - Lifecycle compression loses its fatigue input from campaigns; its damage state is a threshold on
     declared duty only.
   - Unaffected: member sizing in screening (it already uses a bounded statics load set), tolerances, export,
     the packet's structure otherwise.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Envelope cases and spectra are labelled `provisional` and no
   packet is written from them; the beam rung may use the `statics` load set.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOADS-M5.md` (the program's one place for decision records and the
   kill-gate naming `ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>`), with the required fields of the decision template of
   N40-03: the kill gate and milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied (none); both
   measurements if two were made, each value with its receipt hash beside its number; the defect and fix of
   the repair cycle; the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries ("loads:
   declared"); the beads re-read, each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the downstream
   list of step 4; and the session that recorded it, which did not implement the extraction. The KILL-LOADS
   row of `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the fallback ledger N40-27) gains `decision = {outcome, adr, date,
   session, receipts}`; GATE-FOUNDRY-02 keeps the row its measuring session recorded, and on `fired` its failed
   part takes status `killed` through N40-03's propagation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOADS-M5.md` exists at M5 with N40-03's required fields and each clause of the gate evaluated against its number (10⁻⁶; 10⁻³; 10⁻⁹; two refusals).
- [ ] At most one repair cycle is recorded, with its defect report; no threshold or fixture changed between measurements.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry loads quad-ref --declared statics --key <k> --json` works and prints the dynamic factor, `--campaign` under the switch refuses with FROB-REF-724 and offers `--declared statics`, and every fatigue field reads `no-claim`; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes.
- [ ] If not fired: the gate row is `pass` and is cited by every consumer of envelope cases.
- [ ] The KILL-LOADS row holds the decision with one of `not-fired`, `fired` or `deferred`, at M5; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-LOADS.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_loads.rs`: `decision_table` (pass → `not-fired`; fail then pass →
`not-fired`, one repair-cycle line recorded; fail then fail → `fired`; a third measurement is refused; no
receipt at the exit → `deferred`);
`threshold_change_is_not_a_repair` (a second measurement under a different registration hash is rejected);
`declared_case_must_be_one_instant`; `fallback_sets_fatigue_no_claim`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_loads.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the gate receipt(s), including the battery's
mutation clauses (N31b-05), evaluates the rule, prints the decision, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance
--test kill_ledger`; on a scratch gate register with the decision `fired` runs `frob foundry loads quad-ref --declared statics --key $(foundry_key kl-declare) --json`, then `frob
foundry check quad-ref --link lower-leg --loads <the declared file> --key $(foundry_key kl-check) --json` on
the declared cases (keys from the cluster preamble of N31a-51), asserting the label `loads: declared`, the
printed dynamic factor and `fatigue: no-claim`; and `frob foundry loads quad-ref --campaign camp-small --key
$(foundry_key kl-campaign) --json` under the switch, asserting exit 5, FROB-REF-724 and the offered repair. Logs to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_loads/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N31b-16`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; clause values with thresholds
and units, the receipt hashes read, repair-cycle count, the decision, the evidence block of each verb call; on
a failed assertion the field, expected and observed values and the saved envelope path. The `summary` carries
`gates: ["KILL-LOADS", "GATE-FOUNDRY-02"]`. Exit zero when the decision is computed, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the decision table. The static case for the fallback: a 20 kg robot on four feet with dynamic
factor 3 declares 147.15 N per foot (20 × 9.81/4 × 3). Negative controls: a receipt with the residual scaling
changed; a declaration that sums two stance phases into one case (refused as not one instant); a fallback
build that still prints a fatigue utilization.

## Considerations

- The fallback is weaker in a specific way: a dynamic factor is a guess about impacts, and fatigue cannot be assessed at all. Reports must say so wherever a structural margin appears.
- A failure of the single-precision clause alone (10⁻³ on a fast tier) with a clean referee clause points at telemetry on devices, not at the method; the repair cycle should be spent there.
- The register's milestone (M5) governs over the chapter's (M4).
