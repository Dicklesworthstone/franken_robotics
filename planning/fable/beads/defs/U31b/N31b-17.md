## Background

Foundry checks each made part against its declared process (three- or five-axis milling, sheet, three
additive processes): minimum wall and member thickness, internal corner radius, tool access, overhang angle,
draft and undercut, enclosed voids, keep-in and keep-out. The sibling library supplies limits and two or
three of the measures; four geometric evaluators (corner radius from the curvature of the distance field,
tool access by visibility along setup directions, overhang from surface normals, voids from connected
components) are this project's own code. Those evaluators are the risk: they sample geometry, and a sampled
check can miss a small feature or flag a clean one.

A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is decided by an ADR at its milestone. KILL-FAB.
Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-07 fails. Fallback: manufacturability findings become advisory lints; a packet records
no manufacturability pass. Decided at M5. This bead is that decision.

GATE-FOUNDRY-07 (Known answer, TARGET): planted violations of wall, corner radius, tool access, overhang and
undercut are each detected with location and repair; clean parts pass; 20 of 20 fixtures.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt; the decision; downstream changes on each branch; the ADR; the posture until
decided.
Out: the checks (BD-405); packet pre-review and human review (N31b-32, N31b-33).

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** The GATE-FOUNDRY-07 row (the battery of N31a-30 run by the check script N31a-55): for each
   of the fifteen planted fixtures (three per violation kind: wall, corner radius, tool access, overhang,
   undercut), whether the feature was detected, whether the reported location lies inside the planted region,
   and whether a repair with target and difference was returned; for each of the five clean fixtures, whether
   it passed; the `caught` flags of the battery's nine evaluator mutants (N31a-30 step 3), which the gate
   recorder requires in the same run.
2. **Decision** (outcomes in N40-03's names). 20 of 20 with all controls caught: `not-fired`, citing the
   receipt. Anything less: `fired`. The gate's text grants no repair cycle; a run voided by a harness defect (a
   fixture file that did not load) is repeated once and is not a failure of the checks. The gate not measured
   by the M5 exit: `deferred`, with the later exit and the reason in the ADR. (`not-reached` does not arise:
   the checks have no pilot of their own.)
3. **Partial failure.** If one feature family fails and the others pass, the ADR may not keep the passing
   families as hard checks under the name of the gate: the trigger is the gate as a whole. It records which
   families failed, so that a later amendment, registered before a new measurement, could re-admit them.
4. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.check --fab` reports findings with severity `advisory`; it never returns a failing verdict on
     manufacturability and never triggers the tightened re-run of the optimizer.
   - `fab.json` in the packet has `status: "advisory"` and no `pass` field (mode `advisory` of N31a-26 step
     6); the manifest's evidence table reads `manufacturability: not assessed as pass`, the words of that
     step.
   - The mechanical pre-review of a packet does not count manufacturability; the human rubric item on
     producibility stands alone, and `review` stays `no-claim` until a record is imported.
   - Cost and embodied-carbon estimates are unaffected (they depend on process cards, not on the evaluators).
   - The upstream work item for geometric evaluators in the sibling becomes the route to reinstating the
     check: adopt the sibling's evaluators by differential test, then re-measure under a new registration.
   - Unaffected: structure, tolerances, geometry export, the packet's hashes and flags.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Findings are shown and a packet records no manufacturability
   pass; nothing describes a part as manufacturable.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FAB-M5.md` (the decision-record path of N40-03, under `docs/adr/`) with
   N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments; the 20
   fixture results and the controls, each with its receipt hash, beside the trigger's 20 of 20; the outcome;
   the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from then on; the beads re-read (BD-405, N31a-26
   to N31a-30, N31a-55, FD-FSIM-29), each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; the downstream
   list of step 4; and the recording session, which did not implement the evaluators. The KILL-FAB row of
   `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the fallback ledger N40-27) gains `decision = {outcome, adr, date,
   session, receipts}`. This bead records no gate: GATE-FOUNDRY-07 is recorded once, from the check script's
   receipt with the battery's beside it, by the session N31a-55 names (one recorder per gate, N38b-19), and on
   `fired` its failed part takes status `killed` through N40-03's propagation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at M5 with the fixture table (feature, detected, located, repair) and the count against 20 of 20.
- [ ] The KILL-FAB row holds the decision with one of `not-fired`, `fired` or `deferred`, at M5; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-FAB.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry check --fab --json` returns advisory findings with exit 2 at most and never a manufacturability verdict; a packet written afterwards has `status: "advisory"` in `fab.json`; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes.
- [ ] If not fired: the packet's fab report may carry `pass` per part, and the gate row is cited.
- [ ] No wording in any report or envelope describes a part as manufacturable or shop-accepted on either branch.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_fab.rs`: `decision_table` (20 of 20 → `not-fired`; 19 of 20 → `fired`; a
detected feature located outside the planted region counts as not detected; a detection without a repair
counts as not detected); `voided_run_repeats_once`; `fallback_report_has_no_pass_field`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_fab.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the receipt, evaluates the rule, prints the
decision, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`; on a scratch gate register with the
decision `fired` runs `frob foundry check --fab --fixture <row 1 of N31a-30's battery, the bracket with a 1.0 mm
web> --key "$(foundry_key kf-check)" --json` and `frob foundry export --fixture packet-min --packet
<scratch>/packet-min --key "$(foundry_key kf-export)" --json` (the packet's target is `--packet`, under the
session's export root; `foundry_key <label>` is the cluster preamble's name, N31a-51, for STD-E2E's `e2e_key
<label>`, and the run has its own state root, so the fired-switch calls are never answered from a stored
receipt of an unfired one),
asserting the advisory severity, exit 2 at most, and `status: "advisory"` with no `pass` field in the packet's
`fab.json`. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/kill_fab/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N31b-17`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time;
`planted_detected` (of 15), `clean_passed` (of 5), control results, the receipt hash, the decision, the
evidence block of each verb call; on a failed assertion the field, expected and observed values and the saved
output path. The `summary` carries `gates: ["KILL-FAB", "GATE-FOUNDRY-07"]`. Exit zero when the decision is
computed, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the decision table. Negative controls: a receipt in which a clean part's result was dropped
(19 results; incomplete, no decision); a fallback build that still emits `pass`; a report template containing
the word "manufacturable" (rejected by a wording check).

## Considerations

- Demotion to lints is a real loss of a guard: an optimized shape with an unreachable pocket can then enter a packet, visibly marked. That is why the packet says "no manufacturability pass" instead of saying nothing.
- A per-part pass was never an assembly pass; that limit holds on both branches.
- The evaluators sample; raising resolution to rescue a failing fixture after the fact is a change of method and needs a new registration.
