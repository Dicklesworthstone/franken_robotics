## Background

Foundry allocates tolerances by sensitivity: tolerances tᵢ ∝ (cᵢ/sᵢ²)^(1/3) meet a statistical variance budget
at least cost under an assumed 1/t cost law; shared setups go through correlated stacks; every allocation is
re-checked by evaluating the true quantity at the tolerance-band extremes, and features that fail are boxed
by interval evaluation. The allocation is first-order, so its risk is a mechanism whose response is too
curved for a linear stack to describe.

A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is decided by an ADR at its milestone.
KILL-TOLERANCE. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of features
in the pilot. Fallback: worst-case boxes per interface. Decided at M5. It is one of the three kill gates of
the generative-structure bet, which is lost if the build packet fails its tolerance closure. This bead is
that decision.

GATE-FOUNDRY-08 (Known answer, TARGET): the worked allocation to 10⁻⁹ (sensitivities 1 and 4, equal costs,
k = 3, √B = 10 µm: 15.990412 µm and 6.345799 µm); the zero-sensitivity and quadratic cases handled as
specified (refused, then boxed); a correlated stack equals its hand value.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt and the pilot record; the two-clause rule; the decision; downstream changes; the
ADR; the posture until decided.
Out: the allocation (BD-406); the pilot (N31b-08).

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** From the gate row: the two worked tolerances against the closed form; the handling of
   the zero-sensitivity feature and of q(x) = x² at nominal zero; the correlated-stack value against its hand
   value; the controls' `caught` flags. From the pilot record (a planar linkage with eight toleranced
   features): the share of features failing the band-extreme check, and the held-out Monte Carlo coverage.
2. **Decision rule.** KILL-TOLERANCE fires if any clause of the gate fails, or if the pilot's failing-feature
   share is above one fifth (with eight features: two or more). The pilot's own falsifier (coverage below
   target) decides funding and is recorded here as context; a negative pilot means the allocation was never
   funded and the decision records `killed: fallback adopted` on the pilot's record with the gate `not run:
   pilot negative`.
3. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.tolerance` returns worst-case boxes per interface: for each interface (a joint axis, a mount, a
     datum pair) the largest box of feature deviations for which interval evaluation of the quantity of
     interest stays inside the specification, split equally among the interface's features. Action field:
     `worst-case box`.
   - No cost comparison under the 1/t model is printed and no saving is claimed.
   - `tolerances.json` in the packet carries boxes with class Certified where the quantity is kinematic and
     evaluated in interval arithmetic, Estimated otherwise; sensitivities are still reported as information.
   - The as-built audit compares each measured feature with its box.
   - Correlated stacks and structured propagation for clearances remain available as analyses and are not
     used to loosen any tolerance.
   - The bet's tolerance clause is read on the boxes: the stack closes by construction, and the record says
     that the closure is worst-case, not statistical.
4. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Allocations are shown with their band-extreme results; a packet
   written before the decision carries worst-case boxes.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-TOLERANCE.md` with the gate values, the pilot's share and coverage, the
   decision and the downstream list; the gate rows for KILL-TOLERANCE and GATE-FOUNDRY-08; written by a
   session that did not implement the allocation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at M5 with both trigger clauses evaluated: the gate's clauses against their numbers, and the pilot's failing-feature share against one fifth.
- [ ] The KILL-TOLERANCE row records `pass` on the gate with the kill not fired, or `killed: fallback adopted`.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry tolerance --json` returns boxes with action `worst-case box`, prints no cost saving, and the packet's `tolerances.json` follows; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes.
- [ ] If not fired: the gate row is cited by the packet's tolerance section.
- [ ] On both branches the quadratic feature is boxed at t = √margin and never loosened.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_tolerance.rs`: `decision_table` (gate pass and share 1/8 → not fired;
share 2/8 → fired; gate clause off by 2 × 10⁻⁹ relative → fired; pilot negative → `not run` and fallback);
`box_split_equal_among_interface_features`; `fallback_prints_no_saving`.
Box known answer: sensitivities 1 and 4 with a worst-case limit of 10 µm on Σ|aᵢ|·tᵢ and an equal split of
the limit between the two features give 5 µm and 1.25 µm.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_tolerance.sh`: reads the receipt and the pilot record, evaluates the rule, prints the
decision; on a fixture registry with the kill fired runs `frob foundry tolerance --fixture
worked-two-feature --json` and asserts boxes and the absence of a cost ratio. Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_tolerance/`: the gate clause values, `failing_feature_share` (threshold 0.2),
`pilot_coverage`, the decision; `summary` with `gates: ["KILL-TOLERANCE", "GATE-FOUNDRY-08"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the decision table; the worked allocation; the box case above. Negative controls: a pilot
record whose share was recomputed after the fact with a different failure definition (rejected by the
registration hash); a fallback build that still prints "19.9% cheaper".

## Considerations

- Worst-case boxes over-tighten: they are safe and expensive. The fallback trades cost for certainty and the packet says which it carries.
- The one-fifth clause reads a pilot on one planar linkage; it is a deliberately blunt early signal.
- An interval box for a kinematic quantity can be class Certified, which is stronger than the allocation's class; that does not make the fallback better, only differently justified.
