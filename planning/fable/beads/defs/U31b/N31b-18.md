## Background

Foundry allocates tolerances by sensitivity: tolerances tᵢ ∝ (cᵢ/sᵢ²)^(1/3) meet a statistical variance budget
at least cost under an assumed 1/t cost law; shared setups go through correlated stacks; every allocation is
re-checked by evaluating the true quantity at the tolerance-band extremes, and features that fail are boxed
by interval evaluation. The allocation is first-order, so its risk is a mechanism whose response is too
curved for a linear stack to describe.

A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is decided by an ADR at its milestone.
KILL-TOLERANCE. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of features
in the pilot. Fallback: worst-case boxes per interface. Decided at M5. It is one of the three kill gates of
the generative-structure bet, which is lost if the build packet fails its tolerance closure. This bead is
that decision.

GATE-FOUNDRY-08 (Known answer, TARGET): the worked allocation to 10⁻⁹ (sensitivities 1 and 4, equal costs,
k = 3, √B = 10 µm: 15.990412 µm and 6.345799 µm); the zero-sensitivity and quadratic cases handled as
specified (the robust path of N31a-33: boxed by default with exit 2, and refused with FROB-REF-165 when the
statistical method is demanded with `--method statistical`; the quadratic fixture q = x²/L with L = 1 mm and a
10 µm margin is boxed at 100 µm); a correlated stack equals its hand value.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt and the pilot record; the two-clause rule; the decision; downstream changes; the
ADR; the posture until decided.
Out: the allocation (BD-406); the pilot (N31b-08).

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** From the gate row: the two worked tolerances against the closed form; the handling of
   the zero-sensitivity feature and of q(x) = x² at nominal zero; the correlated-stack value against its hand
   value; the controls' `caught` flags. From the pilot record (a planar linkage with eight toleranced
   features): the share of features failing the band-extreme check, and the held-out Monte Carlo coverage.
2. **Decision rule** (outcomes in N40-03's names). `fired` if any clause of the gate fails, or if the pilot's
   failing-feature share is above one fifth (with eight features: two or more); `not-fired`, citing both
   receipts, if every clause passes and the share is at most one fifth. The pilot's own falsifier (coverage
   below target) decides funding and is recorded here as context; a negative pilot means the allocation was
   never funded: `not-reached`, the ADR citing the pilot's `[[pilot.result]]`, GATE-FOUNDRY-08 staying
   `not-run`, and worst-case boxes per interface being what `foundry.tolerance` returns. The gate not measured
   by the M5 exit although the allocation was funded: `deferred`, with the later exit and the reason.
3. **What changes downstream if it fires.**
   - `foundry.tolerance` returns worst-case boxes per interface: for each interface (a joint axis, a mount, a
     datum pair) the largest box of feature deviations for which interval evaluation of the quantity of
     interest stays inside the specification, split equally among the interface's features. Each such
     feature carries `path: "worst-case-box"` in the report (the field and value of N31a-33 step 8; `action`
     keeps its own values `loosen`, `keep` or `tighten` relative to the process default).
   - No cost comparison under the 1/t model is printed and no saving is claimed.
   - `tolerances.json` in the packet carries the boxes with class Estimated: INV-FOUNDRY-06 caps every Foundry
     result at Estimated (the only exception is firmware's instance certificates), and the invariant battery
     N31b-01 checks it at the type level. Where the quantity is kinematic and the box comes from interval
     evaluation, the box records its `enclosure` (`product-epistemic`, or `local-bisection` if the admission
     of N33bx-06 failed: N31a-33's per-feature field) and the Certified class of the sensitivities it used, as
     inputs; sensitivities are still reported as information.
   - The as-built audit compares each measured feature with its box.
   - Correlated stacks and structured propagation for clearances remain available as analyses and are not
     used to loosen any tolerance.
   - The bet's tolerance clause is read on the boxes: the stack closes by construction, and the record says
     that the closure is worst-case, not statistical.
4. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Allocations are shown with their band-extreme results; a packet
   written before the decision carries worst-case boxes.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TOLERANCE-M5.md` (the decision-record path of N40-03, under `docs/adr/`) with
   N40-03's required fields: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments; each
   measured value (the gate clauses, the pilot's share and coverage) with its receipt hash beside the trigger's
   number; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability carries from then on; the beads
   re-read (BD-406, N31a-31 to N31a-33, N31a-56, BD-408), each "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by this
   ADR"; the downstream list of step 3; and the recording session, which did not implement the allocation. The
   KILL-TOLERANCE row of `registries/gates.toml` (entered by the fallback ledger N40-27) gains `decision =
   {outcome, adr, date, session, receipts}`. This bead records no gate: GATE-FOUNDRY-08 is recorded once, from
   the tolerance script's receipt, by the session N31a-56 names (one recorder per gate, N38b-19), and on
   `fired` its failed part takes status `killed` through N40-03's propagation.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at M5 with both trigger clauses evaluated: the gate's clauses against their numbers, and the pilot's failing-feature share against one fifth.
- [ ] The KILL-TOLERANCE row holds the decision with one of `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred`, at M5; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-TOLERANCE.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry tolerance --json` returns boxes with `path: "worst-case-box"` on every feature, prints no cost saving, and the packet's `tolerances.json` follows; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes.
- [ ] If not fired: the gate row is cited by the packet's tolerance section.
- [ ] On both branches the quadratic feature is boxed at t = √(margin·L) (100 µm for the fixture) and never loosened.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_tolerance.rs`: `decision_table` (gate pass and share 1/8 → `not-fired`;
share 2/8 → `fired`; gate clause off by 2 × 10⁻⁹ relative → `fired`; pilot negative → `not-reached`, the gate
`not-run`; no gate receipt at the exit with the allocation funded → `deferred`);
`box_split_equal_among_interface_features`; `fallback_prints_no_saving`.
Box known answer: sensitivities 1 and 4 with a worst-case limit of 10 µm on Σ|aᵢ|·tᵢ and an equal split of
the limit between the two features give 5 µm and 1.25 µm.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_tolerance.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): reads the receipts (the tolerance script's,
N31a-56, and the known-answer battery's, N31b-05) and the pilot record, evaluates the rule, prints the
decision, runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`; on a scratch gate register with the
decision `fired` runs `frob foundry tolerance --fixture worked-case
--json` (a `read` verb: no idempotency key; the fixture name is N31a-56's) and asserts boxes, field `path` =
`worst-case-box` on every feature, class Estimated and the
absence of a cost ratio. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/kill_tolerance/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript:
every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-18`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall
time; the gate clause values with thresholds and units, `failing_feature_share` (threshold 0.2),
`pilot_coverage`, the receipt hashes read, the decision; on a failed assertion the field, expected and observed
values and the saved envelope path. The `summary` carries `gates: ["KILL-TOLERANCE", "GATE-FOUNDRY-08"]`. Exit
zero when the decision is computed, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the decision table; the worked allocation; the box case above. Negative controls: a pilot
record whose share was recomputed after the fact with a different failure definition (rejected by the
registration hash); a fallback build that still prints "19.9% cheaper".

## Considerations

- Worst-case boxes over-tighten: they are safe and expensive. The fallback trades cost for certainty and the packet says which it carries.
- The one-fifth clause reads a pilot on one planar linkage; it is a deliberately blunt early signal.
- An interval box for a kinematic quantity rests on Certified inputs (exact sensitivities and an interval enclosure), which is a stronger justification than the statistical allocation's; the box is still reported as Estimated under INV-FOUNDRY-06, and that does not make the fallback better, only differently justified.
