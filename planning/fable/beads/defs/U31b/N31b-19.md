## Background

Foundry's build packet (`.fbuild`) is a directory addressed by its manifest hash: parts list, geometry in
four formats (STL, 3MF, GLB and a faceted STEP solid produced by a write, decode and bit-exact re-emit
chain), tolerances, mass properties, a wiring harness, a schedule, evidence, and four no-authorization flags
that are always false. Two gates guard it. GATE-FOUNDRY-09 (Known answer, TARGET): tetrahedron round trip
bit-exact; open shell and over-bound parts refused; mass properties from exported geometry within 10⁻⁶ of the
model. GATE-FOUNDRY-10 (Known answer, TARGET): packet schema-valid, every hash matching, flags present and
false, cross-consistency of parts, geometry, nets and schedule; a mutated flag rejected by the offline
auditor; a writer killed at any file boundary leaves no packet.

A kill gate names its trigger and fallback in advance and is decided by an ADR at its milestone. KILL-PACKET.
Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-09 or -10 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback: parts list, mass properties and STL
only; STEP and harness deferred. Decided at M5. The packet-review subsection leaves this kill gate unchanged.
The program's definition of done accepts either branch: "the foundry is real" with the full packet, or with
the reduced packet of this kill gate in force. This bead is that decision.

## Scope

In: reading the two gate receipts and the pilot record; the repair-cycle rule; the decision; the exact
contents of the reduced packet; downstream changes; the ADR; the posture until decided.
Out: export and packet mechanisms (BD-407, BD-408); the auditor's checker (N31b-44); packet review.

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** GATE-FOUNDRY-09's row: STEP instance count (18) and replay result; the two refusals; the
   largest relative difference of mass, centre of mass and inertia against 10⁻⁶. GATE-FOUNDRY-10's row:
   schema validity; hash recomputation; four flags; the cross-consistency checks; the auditor's rejection of
   a mutated flag; the kill-at-every-boundary result (zero packets left). The pilot record (one hand-built
   part and one purchased part through the whole export) as the first reading.
2. **One repair cycle** covers the two gates together: a first failure of either opens exactly one cycle (a
   defect report, one fix, one re-measurement of both gates under the same fixtures). A second failure of
   either fires the kill gate. A pilot negative before funding means the full packet was never funded: the
   decision records `killed: fallback adopted` on the pilot's record.
3. **Invariants that are not negotiable on either branch.** The flags (all four present and false), the
   whole-or-nothing write, the hash of every file, and the mass identity. If the *failure* is in one of these
   (a flag can be set, a partial packet survives a kill, hashes do not match), no packet of any kind is
   written until it is fixed: the reduced packet is not a way around a safety property. The reduced packet is
   the fallback for failures of the interchange and harness content.
4. **The reduced packet** (if fired): `manifest.json` (same schema, with `reduced: true`), `parts.json`,
   `mass.json` and `geometry/<part>.stl`, and nothing else. Absent: STEP, 3MF and GLB files, `harness.json`,
   `schedule.json`, `tolerances.json`, `structure/`, `fab.json`. Structural, tolerance and manufacturability
   results whose own gates passed stay available through their verbs and are cited in the manifest by
   evidence identity, not shipped as packet files. The mass identity is checked between the model,
   `mass.json` and the STL alone. (The fallback's wording is "parts list, mass properties and STL only; STEP
   and harness deferred"; this bead reads "only" strictly.)
5. **What else changes downstream if it fires.** `foundry.export` writes the reduced packet and says so in
   its envelope and in every view header; the mechanical pre-review's cross-export item compares the model,
   `mass.json`, the STL and the robot-description exports only; its referential-integrity item drops the
   net checks; the as-built lane loses datum and fiducial records only if the geometry record was the failing
   part (otherwise they travel beside the STL); the upstream work item for a mesh entry point to the
   sibling's STEP exporter becomes the route to reinstating STEP; the bet's packet clause is read on the
   reduced packet and recorded as such.
6. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** No packet leaves the tool as a build packet; `foundry.export`
   may write pilot-labelled artifacts for testing only.
7. **Record.** `docs/adr/KILL-PACKET.md`: both gates' values, the repair cycle if used, the decision, the
   reduced-packet content list and the downstream list; the gate rows for KILL-PACKET, GATE-FOUNDRY-09 and
   GATE-FOUNDRY-10; written by a session that did not implement the packet.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists at M5 with every clause of both gates evaluated against its number.
- [ ] At most one repair cycle is recorded for the pair of gates.
- [ ] The KILL-PACKET row records both gates `pass` with the kill not fired, or `killed: fallback adopted`.
- [ ] If fired: `frob foundry export --json` writes a packet with `reduced: true`, containing exactly the files of step 4, with four false flags, a matching manifest hash and a passing mass identity against the STL; the pipeline script's fallback variant passes.
- [ ] A failure in flags, atomicity, hashes or mass identity blocks every kind of packet until fixed; the test for this exists.
- [ ] No report calls a reduced packet a build packet without the word reduced.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_packet.rs`: `decision_table` (both pass → not fired; one fails then both
pass → not fired with one repair; a second failure → fired); `safety_property_failure_blocks_all_packets`
(a receipt with `flags_false: 3 of 4` yields `blocked`, not `reduced`); `reduced_packet_file_set` (exact
list); `reduced_packet_mass_identity_uses_stl`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_packet.sh`: reads the receipts and the pilot record, evaluates the rule, prints the
decision; on a fixture registry with the kill fired runs `frob foundry export <fixture> --json`, lists the
directory, recomputes hashes, checks flags and the STL mass identity, and runs the auditor. Logs per STD-E2E
to `target/e2e-logs/kill_packet/`: both gates' clause values, repair-cycle count, the decision, the file
list, `flags_false`, `mass_rel_diff_max`; `summary` with `gates: ["KILL-PACKET", "GATE-FOUNDRY-09",
"GATE-FOUNDRY-10"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the decision table; 18 instances; 10⁻⁶; four flags. Negative controls: a reduced packet with
a flag removed (rejected by reader and auditor); a reduced packet that still contains a STEP file (the file
set test fails); a third measurement after two failures (refused).

## Considerations

- The reduced packet is still useful: a parts list with card references, masses with uncertainty and an STL per part are enough for a quotation request, which is an import lane and not a gate.
- Deferring the harness removes the voltage-drop, current-density, bend-radius and slack checks from the packet; they remain available as an analysis outside it.
- If only the STEP chain fails and everything else passes, the fallback as written still removes 3MF, GLB, the harness and the other sections. Keeping more would be a change of fallback and needs a dated amendment before the measurement, not after.
