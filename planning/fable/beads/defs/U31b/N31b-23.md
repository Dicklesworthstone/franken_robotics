## Background

Lifecycle compression is a Moonshot of Foundry in its own crate, `frob-lifecycle`: slow wear, fatigue,
insulation and cell states are advanced by averaged macro steps so that months of duty can be estimated from
a few million simulated steps. It is off the critical path, and the build packet does not depend on it. Its
kill gate and fallback were named before any measurement.

KILL-LIFECYCLE. Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-12 fails. Fallback: aged model variants at hand-specified wear states,
with no time axis and no compression claim. The chapter printed the decision at M8; lifecycle compression is
an M6 item and the gate register sets M6. Decided by an ADR. The workspace rule for the crate is stricter
than for most: if the pilot fails, the crate's fallback is deletion, not absorption into another crate (§34).
This bead is that decision, recorded in the kill-gate form of N40-03 against the row that the fallback ledger
N40-27 entered.

GATE-FOUNDRY-12 (Fidelity, TARGET): a 100-hour reference (1.8 × 10⁸ steps) compressed at least 50-fold in
steps with at most 5% error on every slow state; the planted backlash-induced gait collapse caught.

## Scope

In: reading the gate receipt and the pilot record; the decision; the fallback's exact form; downstream
changes; the ADR; the posture until decided.
Out: the measurement (N31a-59); the pilot (N31b-11); the battery-ageing kill gate, which belongs to the
actuator chapter.

## Technical approach

1. **What is read.** From the pilot record: the error at a compression of 50 in steps against 5%, and whether
   the planted collapse forced full resolution. From the gate row: compression; largest error per slow-state
   family at every hourly mark; the collapse clause; the four known answers; the controls.
2. **Decision** (outcomes in N40-03's names).
   - Pilot negative (`falsifier-holds`) or the owner did not fund the epic: `not-reached`. The epic was never
     funded; the capability does not exist and is not described; the crate is not created, or is deleted if
     it was started (deletion, not absorption); GATE-FOUNDRY-12 stays `not-run`.
   - Gate measured and every clause passes: `not-fired`, citing the gate receipt.
   - Any clause fails (compression under 50, any slow state over 5% at any mark, the collapse not caught):
     `fired`. The gate grants no repair cycle; a run voided by a harness defect is repeated once.
   - The gate not measured by the M6 exit although the epic was funded: `deferred`, with the later exit and
     the reason in the ADR (N40-03 rule 3).
3. **The fallback** (`fired`). Aged model variants at hand-specified wear states: a caller names a wear
   state directly, for example backlash per joint, capacity fade and resistance growth, a friction change,
   each with its provenance (`hand-specified`). The variant is a new model identity; aged and new robots run
   as per-world variants in one batch, so "does the worn robot still pass?" is one run. There is no time
   axis: no "after N hours", no time to first threshold, no maintenance interval, no mass against service
   duration front, and no compression figure. DESIGN (one surface, chosen for users: one call gives a whole
   variant set, and the verb's name does not move): the fallback ships through the verb's own fallback mode,
   `frob foundry lifecycle <design> --mode hand-specified --wear-states <file> --key <k>` (N31a-50 step 8),
   whose variant set `eval.run` and `nemesis.search` take as `--variants`; the capability is renamed "aged
   variants at hand-specified wear states", and a request for a time axis is refused with `FROB-REF-724`,
   status `killed`, naming the fallback and this ADR, repair `--mode hand-specified`. On `not-reached` there
   is no such verb: aged variants are made by ordinary design edits (`design.edit` of the parameters) and
   evaluated design by design, without the variant-set input that only the funded epic adds.
4. **What else changes downstream if it fires.** The time-axis modules of `frob-lifecycle` (`micro`,
   `macro_step`, `resolve`, `rare`, the rate integration of the laws and the outputs in time) are removed by
   ADR; the crate keeps the hand-specified variant builder, its firewall rules and its class ceiling; the
   refusal code for lifecycle laws stays registered and unused; the open question on wear, insulation and
   cell-fade coefficients loses its Foundry consumer (the actuator chapter's ageing question remains its own);
   evaluation and adversarial search can still request aged variants, now by wear state. N31b-28 is re-read
   and its record notes the lost consumer. Unaffected: fatigue utilization inside one campaign (the structural
   check), the build packet.
5. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** No report states a service duration, a time to threshold or a
   maintenance interval; aged variants may be made by hand-specified state.
6. **Record.** `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIFECYCLE-M6.md` with the fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate, milestone
   M6, the trigger as registered and any amendments applied, the pilot's and the gate's measured values with
   their receipts, the outcome, the fallback and the capability's new name, the beads re-read (N31a-47 to
   N31a-50, N31a-59, N31b-28), the downstream list, a note citing the recorded state of KILL-SINEW-AGE without
   deciding it, and the recording session, which did not implement the mechanism. The KILL-LIFECYCLE row of
   `registries/gates.toml` (entered by N40-27) gains `decision = {outcome, adr, date, session}`; GATE-FOUNDRY-12
   keeps the row N31a-59 wrote; the owner declaration that N40-30 compares (`[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"`,
   the ledger's trigger, fallback, decided, mode and reads copied, the decision with each measured value citing
   its receipt) goes in `frob-lifecycle`'s `contract.toml`, or, on `not-reached` with no crate, in
   `frob-foundry`'s.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIFECYCLE-M6.md` exists with N40-03's fields and each clause of the gate, or the pilot's falsifier, evaluated against its number (50; 5%; collapse).
- [ ] The KILL-LIFECYCLE row holds the decision with one of `not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or `deferred`, at M6; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` passes for KILL-LIFECYCLE.
- [ ] If fired: an aged variant set made with `--mode hand-specified` runs beside the new robot in one batch; a time-axis request returns `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, status `killed`; no output carries a time axis or a compression figure; the time-axis modules are absent and the packet path still builds.
- [ ] If not reached: `frob-lifecycle` is absent from the workspace, the packet path still builds, and `frob capabilities --json` does not describe lifecycle compression.
- [ ] If not fired: every lifecycle output still prints class Estimated and the step-ratio wording.
- [ ] The ADR cites the recorded state of KILL-SINEW-AGE without deciding it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_lifecycle.rs`: `decision_table` (all clauses pass → `not-fired`;
compression 49 → `fired`; one slow state at 5.1% at one mark → `fired`; collapse stepped across → `fired`;
pilot `falsifier-holds` → `not-reached`, crate absent; no gate receipt at the exit with the epic funded →
`deferred` naming a later exit); `fallback_variant_has_no_time_axis` (schema check: no hours field);
`aged_variant_is_new_model_identity`; `time_axis_refused_724_when_fired` (with the decision `fired` in a
scratch register: status `killed`, the fallback and the ADR named, repair `--mode hand-specified`).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_lifecycle.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N31b-23 milestone=M6 labels=foundry,moonshot,kill-gate
gates=KILL-LIFECYCLE,GATE-FOUNDRY-12 seed=0 needs=cpu plants=after-1000-hours,settling-omitted`; sources the
STD-E2E helper). Steps: `read` reads the pilot record and the gate receipt; `decide` evaluates the rule and
prints the decision; `ledger` runs `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`; then, on a scratch gate
register with the decision `fired`: `refuse` runs `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob foundry lifecycle
quad-ref --duty trot --horizon-h 10 --key $(foundry_key klc-ta) --json` and asserts status `killed` and the
fallback named; `aged` creates an aged variant set (`frob foundry lifecycle quad-ref --mode hand-specified
--wear-states fixtures/lifecycle/knee-backlash-0p5mrad.toml --key $(foundry_key klc-age) --json`: knee backlash
0.5 × 10⁻³ rad, f_Q and f_R zero); `batch` runs new and aged in one batch (`frob eval run quad-ref --variants
<set> --key $(foundry_key klc-eval) --json`) and asserts two variant columns on equal world addresses and no
time field in either. Every key comes from the cluster preamble of N31a-51 (step and input digest), so a run
under the fired switch is not answered from a stored receipt of an earlier run with the same literal key. Logs to
`target/e2e-logs/kill_lifecycle/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every
line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-23`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time;
the clause values with thresholds, the receipt hashes read, the decision, both model identities, the batch's
per-variant verdicts and evidence blocks; on a failed assertion the field, expected and observed values and the
saved envelope path. The `summary` carries `gates: ["KILL-LIFECYCLE", "GATE-FOUNDRY-12"]`. Exit zero when the
decision is computed, whatever it is.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the decision table. Negative controls: a fallback report that prints "after 1,000 hours"
(rejected by a wording and schema check); a workspace in which the packet path fails to build once the crate
is removed (the firewall's deletion drill must have prevented this); a receipt whose step count omits
settling steps (rejected: the accounting must include every fast step).

## Considerations

- The fallback answers the most useful of the four questions ("does the worn design still pass?") and gives up the other three, which need a time axis.
- The register's milestone (M6) governs over the chapter's (M8); the verb registry's M8 date for `foundry.lifecycle` is the surface's date, not the decision's.
- Killing a Moonshot frees a slot under the cap on active Moonshots; the decision is therefore one step more urgent than the work.
