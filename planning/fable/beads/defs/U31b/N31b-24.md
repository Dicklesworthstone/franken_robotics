## Background

Foundry's rule is that the optimizer never checks its own result: stress, buckling, modes and fatigue of an
optimized link are evaluated on a separately generated, body-fitted tetrahedral mesh of the extracted
surface, a different discretization from the optimizer's cut cells. That rule needs a mesher that accepts
whatever surface the optimizer produces: thin members, holes, junctions, near-tangent sheets.

FACT (FrankenSim at commit `483d09c682`): `fs-mesh` has incremental Delaunay tetrahedralization on exact
predicates with quality refinement and audits, and constrained recovery and labelled volumetric meshing of
piecewise-linear complexes; its crate documentation still lists full boundary conformity as successor scope.
Whether an optimized link surface meshes cleanly is therefore unknown.

OPEN-FOUNDRY-1. Question: whether `fs-mesh` produces a conforming mesh of an arbitrary optimized surface.
Fail-closed default, in force until decided: if it does not, the independent check is a second cut-cell solve
at a different resolution and background offset, and results are labelled `check: same-family`. Owner: the
Foundry lead. It blocks GATE-FOUNDRY-04, whose second clause is "the extracted surface meshes and passes the
mesh audit on 20 of 20 fixtures".

This bead is the experiment that answers the question and the decision record that follows. An upstream work
item asks the sibling for a statement of boundary conformity; its interim posture is that conformity is
measured per mesh and recorded with the structural evidence, which is what this experiment sets up.

## Scope

In: the 20-surface corpus; the measurement protocol; the decision rule; the record; the consequences for the
gate's clause and for the check's label.
Out: fixing the mesher (upstream work, in the sibling's repository under its rules); the independent check
itself (BD-402).

## Technical approach

1. **Corpus** (registered before the run). This is the decision corpus: surfaces given straight to the
   mesher. It is not the gate's battery: the 20 fixtures of GATE-FOUNDRY-04's meshing clause are the density
   fields of N31a-18, which go through the whole extraction path of N31a-17 at M5 and are measured against the
   limits this record sets. Twenty extracted surfaces at level ½ of a projected density field: the pilot's lower-leg link at two resolutions; the two-bar optimum;
   eight synthetic optimized shapes generated by registered seeds from the sibling's three-dimensional lane
   (a cantilever bracket, a three-port knuckle, a plate with lightening holes, a torsion link, each at two
   length scales); and nine stress cases built to be hard: a member at exactly the minimum length scale, two
   sheets nearly touching, a junction of five members, a surface with a sliver triangle, a genus-6 lattice
   block, a surface after 1% smoothing, a mirrored pair, a part with frozen cylindrical bosses, and a part
   decimated under a Hausdorff tolerance.
2. **Measurement per surface.** (a) Does meshing complete? (b) Does the mesh pass the sibling's audit? (c)
   Boundary conformity: the largest distance from the mesh boundary to the input surface and back, divided by
   the minimum length scale (DESIGN acceptance: at most 0.05). (d) Quality: smallest dihedral angle and
   largest radius-edge ratio after refinement. (e) Element count and wall time. (f) Does the cantilever
   known answer hold on a mesh of comparable size (deflection within 2%, stress within 5%)?
3. **Decision rule.**
   - 20 of 20 complete, pass the audit and meet the conformity bound: the body-fitted check is the path;
     OPEN-FOUNDRY-1 closes as "yes, on the registered corpus"; per-mesh conformity stays in every check
     record.
   - Otherwise: the same-family fallback becomes the path for the failing class of surfaces, or for all, as
     the owner decides. The independent check is then a second cut-cell solve at a different resolution (a
     registered ratio, DESIGN: 1.5 times finer) and a background offset of half a cell in each axis, and every
     result carries `check: same-family`. The meshing clause of GATE-FOUNDRY-04 cannot be met as written; the
     owner either records the clause as failed (the gate then blocks optimized geometry from packets) or
     files a dated amendment, before the gate is measured, that reads the clause on the same-family check.
     This bead prepares both texts and does not choose.
4. **What `check: same-family` means downstream.** The label travels with the structural margin into the
   packet's evidence; the packet invariant "no geometry enters a packet without passing the independent check
   on a mesh the optimizer did not use" is satisfied only in the sense that the second grid is not the
   optimizer's grid, and the packet prints the weaker label; a result labelled same-family is never
   described as independently checked.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-FOUNDRY-1.md`: the corpus digest, the table of measurements, the decision, the
   owner's signature, and for a negative outcome the two alternative texts for the gate's clause; the
   register of open questions updated; the failing surfaces attached to the upstream work item.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The corpus of 20 surfaces is registered with its digest before meshing starts.
- [ ] The record holds, per surface, completion, audit result, conformity ratio, quality figures, element count and time.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by the Foundry lead, with the rule's inputs shown.
- [ ] If negative: the same-family check is specified with its resolution ratio and offset, the label is defined, and both texts for the gate's clause are on file for the owner.
- [ ] The failing surfaces, if any, are attached to the upstream request for a conformity statement.
- [ ] The record, and until it exists the register entry of OPEN-FOUNDRY-1, states the fail-closed default in the form its consumers apply it: a structural check that could not mesh carries `check: same-family` (applied by N31a-17 and BD-402, which depend on this bead), and GATE-FOUNDRY-04 reads `blocked: OPEN-FOUNDRY-1` until the record exists (the gate register's status, N31b-12). The record also states the conformity limit (0.05 of the minimum length scale) and the same-family grid (1.5 times finer, offset half a cell per axis) as the values those consumers use.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
Self-checks of the experiment's scripts: `conformity_metric_on_known_pair` (a unit cube surface against a
mesh whose boundary is offset by 0.01 gives 0.01); `sliver_fixture_has_a_sliver` (a triangle with an angle
under 1°); `corpus_digest_stable`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_mesh_conformity.sh`: registers the corpus, meshes each surface through the sibling,
runs the audit and the measurements, then the two negative controls. It sources the STD-E2E logging helper:
every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-24`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall
time; a surface that fails logs the first failing measure with its value and threshold and the path of the
surface file to repeat it; it exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the outcome. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/open_foundry_1/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with the
corpus digest, tool pin, and per surface `meshed`, `audit_pass`, `conformity_ratio` (threshold 0.05),
`min_dihedral_deg`, `elements`, `wall_s`; `summary` with the count meshed and passed out of 20 and the
outcome the rule gives.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the cantilever on a body-fitted mesh (1.159 mm within 2%; 30 MPa within 5%). Negative controls:
an open surface (must be refused by the mesher's input check, not meshed); a surface offset by a known
distance after meshing (the conformity metric must report it). Determinism: meshing on exact predicates is
deterministic; the same surface gives the same mesh hash twice.

## Considerations

- A positive result holds for the registered corpus, not for "arbitrary" surfaces; the per-mesh conformity measurement stays in every check so that a later failure is seen and labelled.
- The same-family fallback weakens the independence that the no-self-check rule was written for: both solves share a formulation. That is why the label exists and why the owner, not this bead, decides how the gate's clause is read.
- If the topology rung is killed for other reasons, this question loses its consumer and closes as moot.
