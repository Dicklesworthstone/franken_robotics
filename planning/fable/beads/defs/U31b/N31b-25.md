## Background

Foundry's build packet contains a parts list: made parts with geometry, material, process and mass, and
purchased parts by card reference and quantity. A bill of materials that people can act on usually has
prices. Prices are data with an owner and a licence, they change, and a price printed by a design tool reads
as a quotation. Decision ADR-FOUNDRY-10 therefore says: no priced bill of materials without a pinned,
licensed catalogue. A request for prices without one is refused (FROB-REF-818; repair: omit prices).

OPEN-FOUNDRY-2. Question: which parts catalogue and price data, under what licence. Fail-closed default, in
force until decided: card references and no prices. Owner: the project lead. It blocks nothing: the packet
gate and the scenario "a build packet" pass without prices. The program-level table of open data questions
groups it with the questions on dimensional norms, retrofit prices and actuator-card prices: one cited source
each is wanted; until then generic prices are labelled `assumed`, no cost claim appears in a published
report, and `--strict-data` refuses a cost verdict. The owner's input is wanted at M5.

This bead prepares the decision and records it. It also fixes what "pinned" and "licensed" mean in code, so
that the refusal has a precise trigger whichever way the owner decides.

## Scope

In: the options with their consequences; the definition of a pinned, licensed catalogue; the record; the
default's behaviour.
Out: acquiring any catalogue; the parts list itself (BD-408); the cost and embodied-carbon estimates of the
manufacturability checks, which use process-card unit rates and have their own rule.

## Technical approach

1. **What a pinned, licensed catalogue is** (DESIGN). A registered data artifact with: a content hash (the
   pin); a revision date; a source name; a licence identifier with the uses it permits (at least: display in
   a private packet; redistribution in an exported packet; publication in a report), each a yes or no; a
   currency and a price basis (unit, quantity break); and per-entry provenance. A price is printable only for
   a use the licence permits; the packet's manifest names the catalogue by hash.
2. **Options put to the owner.**
   - A. *No prices* (the default made permanent): parts lists carry card references, quantities and masses;
     FROB-REF-818 stays the answer to any priced request. Cost: scenario text that mentions "a bill of
     materials against a pinned catalogue" is read as a catalogue of *parts*, not prices.
   - B. *An owner-supplied catalogue*: the owner names one source and licence; it is ingested through the
     sibling's schema-checked catalogue ingestion, pinned, and its permitted uses recorded. Prices print with
     the catalogue's hash, date and the label `assumed` or `cited` according to provenance.
   - C. *User-supplied catalogues only*: the tool ships none; a user registers their own under their own
     licence; prices appear only in that user's private packets and never in a published report.
3. **Behaviour common to all options.** A priced field never appears without catalogue hash, revision date
   and currency. A packet with prices carries, like every packet, `fabrication_released: false`; a price is
   not a quotation, and the bill-of-materials view header says so. `--strict-data` refuses any cost verdict
   resting on a price labelled `assumed`. Mass, geometry and evidence never depend on the catalogue.
4. **Until decided (fail-closed default).** Option A's behaviour.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-FOUNDRY-2.md` with the option chosen, the source and licence if any, the
   permitted uses, and the owner's signature; the register of open questions updated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three options and their consequences are on file before the owner is asked.
- [ ] The definition of a pinned, licensed catalogue exists as a schema, and FROB-REF-818's trigger is expressed against it.
- [ ] The decision is recorded and signed by the project lead, or the default is recorded as still in force at the M5 exit.
- [ ] Under the default: `frob foundry export --prices --json` returns exit 3 and FROB-REF-818 with the repair "omit prices"; a packet has no price field.
- [ ] Under B or C: a price prints only with catalogue hash, date, currency and provenance label, and only for a permitted use.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry catalogue`: `priced_without_catalogue_refused_818`; `catalogue_requires_hash_and_licence`
(a catalogue file without a licence identifier is not admitted); `use_not_permitted_blocks_print` (a
catalogue that forbids redistribution yields no prices in an exported packet); `strict_data_refuses_assumed_price`;
`mass_and_geometry_independent_of_catalogue` (packet address unchanged except for price fields).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_prices.sh`: (1) a priced export with no catalogue, asserting exit 3 and FROB-REF-818;
(2) with a synthetic catalogue registered for the test (labelled `synthetic`), asserting prices with hash,
date and currency; (3) the same catalogue with redistribution forbidden, asserting no prices in the exported
packet. Logs per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/foundry_prices/`: `event: refusal` for step 1; for steps 2 and 3
the catalogue hash and the count of priced lines; the summary counts assertions.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the refusal's code and repair. Negative controls: a parts card that carries a bare price field
(must be refused at export, not printed); a report template that sums prices labelled `assumed` into a cost
verdict under `--strict-data` (must be refused).

## Considerations

- The question blocks nothing, which is why the default can stand indefinitely. The cost of leaving it open is only that packets carry no prices.
- Catalogue licences commonly forbid redistribution; option C exists so that a user's own data never leaves their workspace.
- A price is the one field in a packet that ages by itself; the revision date is mandatory for that reason.
