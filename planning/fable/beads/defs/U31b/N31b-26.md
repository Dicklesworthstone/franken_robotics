## Background

The build packet's wiring harness lists nets, connectors, conductor sizes, lengths with slack and checks. One
of the checks is whether a conductor can carry its current. In practice that is read from a current-capacity
table (an ampacity table), which depends on the standard, the insulation, the ambient temperature, bundling
and installation. The plan has not chosen a table, and a wrong choice silently over- or under-sizes every
wire.

OPEN-FOUNDRY-3. Question: which current-capacity table governs conductor sizing. Fail-closed default, in
force until decided: current density against a limit declared on the cable card, with no capacity claim.
Owner: the Foundry lead. The program-level table of open questions records what it blocks: any conductor
capacity claim. It does not block the packet gate.

What the default does and does not say. With the default the harness prints, per net: voltage drop
I·ρ·L/A against the bus budget; current density I/A against the card's declared limit; bend radius against
the card; slack across each joint. It does not say that the conductor's temperature rise is acceptable, and
the field `capacity` reads `no-claim`. Worked values under the default: copper, 1 m each way, 0.5 mm², 10 A
on a 24 V bus gives 0.0688 Ω, a drop of 0.688 V (2.9%), 6.9 W dissipated and 20 A/mm².

## Scope

In: the options; the decision record; the exact behaviour of the default; what changes in the harness section
when a table is adopted.
Out: the harness itself and its default checks (N31a-41, on which this bead depends, with the packet verb
BD-408 that writes `harness.json`: the tests below drive that code, and the table admission and the
`capacity_a` line of options B and C are added to it here in every option, because the unit test
`table_requires_conditions` and the script's second export run them on a `synthetic` table whichever way the
decision falls; under option A no table of record is registered and every net reads `capacity: no-claim`);
thermal modelling of bundles (not in this program's scope).

## Technical approach

1. **Options put to the owner.**
   - A. *Keep the default*: density against the card's limit; `capacity: no-claim`.
   - B. *Adopt one named table* (a standard's ampacity table for the conductor type, with its ambient and
     bundling corrections), entered as a registered data artifact with source, edition, licence and
     provenance per row. The harness then prints `capacity_a` for each net with the table's identity, and a
     net above capacity is a finding with the next conductor size as repair. Class Estimated; the statement
     is "per the named table", never "safe".
   - C. *Table per cable card*: each cable card carries its manufacturer's rating with conditions; no global
     table; a card without a rating reads `no-claim` for its nets.
2. **Rules common to B and C.** A capacity is printed only with its conditions (ambient temperature,
   bundling, insulation rating); a net whose conditions are not covered by the table reads `no-claim`; the
   density check stays as a second, independent line; nothing in the packet depends on the table except the
   capacity field and its finding.
3. **Default behaviour, precisely.** `harness.json` per net: `{ current_a, area_mm2, length_m, drop_v,
   drop_fraction, power_w, density_a_per_mm2, density_limit (from the card, with provenance), bend_radius,
   slack, capacity: "no-claim" }`. A density above the card's limit is a finding with the next stock area as
   repair. A card without a density limit yields `density: no-claim` and the finding "cable card lacks a
   limit".
4. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-FOUNDRY-3.md` with the option, the table's identity if any, and the owner's
   signature; the register of open questions updated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The options and their consequences are on file; the decision is recorded and signed by the Foundry lead, or the default is recorded as still in force at the M5 exit.
- [ ] Under the default, every net's `capacity` reads `no-claim` and no text in the packet or its views states or implies that a conductor's capacity was checked.
- [ ] The voltage-drop worked case reproduces 0.0688 Ω, 0.688 V (2.9%), 6.9 W and 20 A/mm²; halving the area doubles the drop.
- [ ] Under B or C, a capacity prints only with table identity and conditions, and an uncovered condition reads `no-claim`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry harness_capacity`: `default_capacity_is_no_claim`; `density_against_card_limit`
(20 A/mm² against a limit of 15 A/mm² is a finding whose repair is 0.75 mm², the next stock area that brings
density to 13.3 A/mm²); `card_without_limit_is_no_claim`; `voltage_drop_worked_case`;
`table_requires_conditions` (a table row without an ambient temperature is not admitted).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_harness_capacity.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): exports the packet of the harness
fixture that carries the worked case (`frob foundry export --fixture harness-24v --packet <scratch> --key
"$(e2e_key default)" --json`; `harness-24v` is N31a-41's fixture: copper, 1 m each way, 0.5 mm², 10 A, 24 V;
`packet-min` is the container writer's section set of N31b-03 and is not a design the verb can export), reads
`harness.json`, asserts the default fields and the worked case; with a synthetic table registered for the test
(labelled `synthetic`), exports again into a second directory (`--key "$(e2e_key with-table)"`, its own label:
`foundry.export` is a `run` verb and the two exports are compared) and asserts `capacity_a` with its identity
and conditions, and `no-claim` for a net outside the table's ambient range. Logs to `target/e2e-logs/foundry_harness_capacity/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-26`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line,
exit code and wall time; per net the fields of step 3 with units and the card limit; on a failed assertion the
net, the field, expected and observed values and the packet path. The summary counts nets with `no-claim` and
assertions passed.

### Known answers and negative controls
The voltage-drop known answer and its control (halving the area doubles the drop). Negative controls: a
harness view that prints "capacity OK" under the default (rejected by a wording check); a table applied
outside its ambient range (must read `no-claim`).

## Considerations

- Current density is a weak proxy for heating: it ignores bundling and ambient temperature. The default is honest about that by claiming nothing.
- A standard's table is usually licensed; adopting one meets the same licence questions as a parts catalogue.
- Sag and dynamic whip of cables need a rod model and stay `no-claim` whatever is decided here.
