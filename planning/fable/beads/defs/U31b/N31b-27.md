## Background

Foundry reports cumulative fatigue damage as *utilization* under a named empirical law: signed stress
histories at gauges are counted into cycles by rainflow, damage accumulates by the linear rule D = Σ nᵢ/N(Sᵢ)
with N(S) from the material card's stress-life curve, and utilization for a service duration is compared with
1 divided by the card's scatter factor. The rule that keeps this honest is that the curve must be valid for
the material state, the process and the surface finish; otherwise the result is `fatigue: no-claim`
(FROB-REF-163).

Printed parts are where that rule bites. Topology-optimized links are natural candidates for additive
processes, and stress-life behaviour of printed material depends strongly on process, build orientation,
porosity, surface finish and post-treatment. Curves for printed and cast material are model claims; the
sibling's fatigue module calls itself an empirical baseline, "not a validated component-life claim".

OPEN-FOUNDRY-4. Question: stress-life data for printed material by process and orientation. Fail-closed
default, in force until decided: `fatigue: no-claim` for printed parts. Owner: the Foundry lead. It blocks
the fatigue leg of GATE-FOUNDRY-05 for printed fixtures only: the gate's comparison of an optimized link with
an engineered tube at equal fatigue utilization cannot be made for a printed link, so the gate fixture is a
wrought alloy.

## Scope

In: the card fields that make a curve valid for a printed part; the admission rule; the options; the
decision record; what the default does to each consumer.
Out: generating fatigue data (no test programme exists in this project); the cycle counter (BD-402); wear,
insulation and cell-fade coefficients (the lifecycle data question).

## Technical approach

1. **Card fields for a stress-life curve** (DESIGN, additions to the material card): `process` (`fdm`, `sls`,
   `slm`, `wrought`, `cast`), `build_orientation` relative to the gauge plane (for example `0°`, `45°`,
   `90°`), `surface_finish` (`as-built`, `machined`, `polished`), `post_treatment` (stress relief, hot
   isostatic pressing, none), `stress_ratio` of the curve, the curve itself with its validity range in
   stress and cycles, `scatter_factor`, and per-field provenance (`cited` with source, `measured` with
   dataset identity, `assumed`).
2. **Admission rule for a fatigue result.** A gauge's fatigue utilization is computed only if the part's
   process, the orientation class of the gauge plane, the finish and the post-treatment all match the
   curve's fields, and every counted stress lies in the curve's validity range. Any mismatch gives `fatigue:
   no-claim` for that gauge with the missing or mismatched field as the repair. A curve with provenance
   `assumed` yields a result labelled `assumed` that `--strict-data` refuses.
3. **Options put to the owner.**
   - A. *Keep the default*: printed parts read `fatigue: no-claim`; structural margins for printed links rest
     on stress, buckling and modes only, and say so.
   - B. *Admit cited curves*: one cited source per (process, orientation, finish, post-treatment) class the
     reference robots use, entered with its licence; results are Estimated with the source named.
   - C. *Admit measured curves through the calibration lane*: curves from coupon tests imported as datasets
     with provenance; results Estimated, with the dataset's validity cell.
   Under B and C a knock-down for orientation may never be invented by interpolation between classes: a
   class without a curve stays `no-claim`.
4. **Consequences of the default for consumers.** The comparison gate runs on a wrought-alloy fixture; a
   printed fixture may run beside it with the fatigue leg `no-claim` and is not part of the gate. The build
   packet of a printed link lists `fatigue: no-claim` among its no-claim items. Lifecycle compression's
   fatigue state for a printed link is `no-claim`.
5. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-FOUNDRY-4.md` with the option, the sources if any, and the owner's signature;
   the register of open questions updated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The card fields exist in the material card schema and a curve without `process`, `build_orientation`, `surface_finish` or provenance is not admitted for a printed part.
- [ ] Under the default, every printed part's fatigue result is `fatigue: no-claim` with FROB-REF-163's repair naming the missing data, while stress, buckling and modes still answer.
- [ ] The decision is recorded and signed by the Foundry lead, or the default is recorded as still in force at the M5 exit.
- [ ] The comparison gate's registration states its fixture material (wrought) and that printed fixtures are outside the gate until this question closes.
- [ ] No result for a printed part uses a wrought curve, with or without a factor.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry fatigue_admission`: `printed_part_without_curve_is_no_claim`;
`orientation_mismatch_is_no_claim` (a curve for `0°` applied to a gauge plane classed `90°`);
`finish_mismatch_is_no_claim` (`machined` curve, `as-built` part); `stress_outside_validity_range_is_no_claim`;
`assumed_curve_refused_under_strict_data`; `wrought_curve_never_used_for_printed`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_fatigue_admission.sh`: `frob foundry check --structure <printed link> --json` with a
card lacking a printed curve, asserting `fatigue: no-claim`, FROB-REF-163 in the findings and complete
stress, buckling and modal results; then with a synthetic matching curve registered for the test (labelled
`synthetic`), asserting a utilization with the curve's identity; then with a mismatched orientation. Logs per
STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/foundry_fatigue_admission/`: per gauge the admission result and reason; the
summary counts gauges with and without a claim.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: the fatigue block (N(S) = 10⁶·(100 MPa/S)³; 2,000 cycles at 200 MPa, 50,000 at 100 MPa,
400,000 at 50 MPa: damage 0.116) computes only when the curve is admitted. Negative controls: a build that
falls back to the wrought curve when the printed one is missing (must be caught by the last unit test); a
card whose curve lacks a stress ratio.

## Considerations

- The default removes fatigue from exactly the parts that topology optimization favours; that is the honest position until data exists, and it is why the comparison gate is run on a wrought fixture.
- Published curves for printed alloys vary widely with machine and powder; a cited curve is still a model claim and stays Estimated.
- Orientation classes are coarse on purpose: a finer scheme would invite interpolation that the data cannot support.
