## Background

Firmware export (a Moonshot of Foundry) proves that no intermediate of an exported fixed-point controller
overflows on its declared input ranges by posing bit-vector queries: the question "is there an input in range
for which this intermediate overflows?" is bit-blasted and decided by the sibling proof tool's decision
procedure. Three outcomes exist: unsatisfiable with a replayed proof stream (an instance certificate, class
Certified); satisfiable with a counterexample; or no answer within the budget. The third must be treated as
*undecided* and therefore refused (FROB-REF-827: a fixed-point obligation refuted or undecided within budget;
repair: the counterexample input, or a wider format), because an unanswered overflow question is not a
certificate.

OPEN-FOUNDRY-6. Question: the time budget per bit-vector obligation. Fail-closed default, in force until
decided: 2 s (TARGET), with a timeout treated as undecided and therefore refused. Owner: the Lemma lead. It
blocks GATE-FOUNDRY-11.

Two things make the number matter. Too small, and honest controllers are refused for lack of time, which
would fire the kill gate for the wrong reason. And a wall-clock budget makes the *outcome* depend on the
machine: the same obligation could be certified on a fast host and refused on a slow one. The program's rule
is that a result never claims more than its inputs support and that exact results are the same everywhere; a
refusal that depends on host speed is a determinism defect in an otherwise exact pipeline.

## Scope

In: the measurement of query times and solver effort on the pilot's and the gate's obligations; the options
for the budget's unit and value; the decision record.
Out: the query builder (N31a-45) and the exporter (N31a-44); the pilot itself (N31b-10). This bead depends on
N31a-45: the measurement runs that builder's queries for the gate's fixtures, and the unit tests below drive its
budget handling; if option B or C is adopted, the effort cap, the watchdog and the recorded budget are added to
that code here.

## Technical approach

1. **Measurement** (registered before it runs). For every obligation of the pilot's controller and of the
   gate's fixtures (the clamped and unclamped proportional–integral loops, the proportional–derivative loop,
   the energy-budget monitor, the full-range product and negation), at 16-bit and 32-bit word lengths: wall
   time on both reference hosts (ten repetitions each); solver effort in machine-independent units (decisions,
   conflicts, propagations) from the solver's counters; proof-stream length; independent-checker replay
   time. Also a scaling probe: the unclamped integrator unrolled to 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128 ticks, to see how
   effort grows with the bound, since an unrolled query is the tempting alternative to an inductive one.
2. **Options put to the owner.**
   - A. *Keep 2 s of wall time*: simple; machine-dependent at the margin.
   - B. *A budget in solver effort* (DESIGN recommendation): a cap on conflicts, chosen from the
     measurement as a multiple of the largest effort any expected-certifiable obligation needed (for example
     ten times), with the 2 s wall time kept only as a watchdog whose firing is reported as an infrastructure
     fault and not as `undecided`. The outcome is then the same on every host.
   - C. *Tiered budgets*: a small default and a larger budget on request, each recorded in the certificate.
   In every option a timeout or cap hit is `undecided` and refused; the budget in force and the effort spent
   are recorded in each certificate and each refusal.
3. **Decision rule proposed.** If the largest wall time of any expected-certifiable obligation is under one
   tenth of the default on the slower host, the default's value is safe; the unit question (A against B) is
   then decided on determinism grounds. If any expected-certifiable obligation needs more than the default
   on either host, the owner chooses between a larger budget and declaring that controller kind not
   exportable.
4. **Record.** `docs/adr/OPEN-FOUNDRY-6.md` with the measurement table, the option, the value and unit, and
   the Lemma lead's signature; the register of open questions updated; the gate's registration cites the
   budget in force.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The measurement plan is registered before any timing run; the table holds wall time on both hosts, solver effort, proof length and replay time for every obligation, and the scaling probe's five points.
- [ ] The decision is recorded and signed by the Lemma lead, or the 2 s default is recorded as still in force when the gate is measured.
- [ ] Whatever the option, an obligation that exhausts its budget yields `undecided` and FROB-REF-827, never a certificate and never a silent pass.
- [ ] Every certificate and every refusal records the budget in force (value and unit) and the effort spent.
- [ ] If option B is adopted: the same obligation gives the same outcome on both reference hosts, shown on the gate fixtures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry bv_budget` (Cargo feature `moonshot-firmware-export`): `timeout_is_undecided_and_refused` (a
budget of zero effort on a non-trivial query → FROB-REF-827 with `undecided`);
`budget_recorded_in_certificate`; and, only if option B is adopted (the code they test is added here then),
`effort_cap_is_host_independent` (the outcome under a conflict cap equals a stored outcome) and
`watchdog_is_infrastructure_fault_not_undecided`; under option C, `tier_recorded_in_certificate` (the larger
budget, when requested, appears in the certificate and the refusal).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/foundry_bv_budget.sh`: registers the plan, runs every obligation ten times on the host,
collects counters, runs the scaling probe and the two negative controls. It sources the STD-E2E logging
helper: every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N31b-29`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and
wall time; a verdict that differs from the known answer logs the obligation, both verdicts and the path of the
formula to repeat it; it exits zero when the measurement is complete. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/open_foundry_6/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with
the host fingerprint, tool pin, and per obligation `verdict`, `wall_ms` (minimum, median, maximum),
`conflicts`, `decisions`, `propagations`, `proof_records`, `replay_ms`; the probe's series; `summary` with
the largest wall time against 2,000 ms and the largest effort.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the verdicts of the fixed-point cases (product of −1 and −1 satisfiable; negation of −32768
satisfiable; unclamped step satisfiable with a state from which 400 more counts overflow; clamped base and
step unsatisfiable). The scaling probe's verdicts are known too, on the 16-bit word (at 32 bits all five are
unsatisfiable, since 400 × 128 = 51,200 is far inside the range): from zero with at most 400 counts per tick,
|I| ≤ 400·k after k ticks, so the queries unrolled to 8, 16, 32 and 64 ticks are unsatisfiable (400 × 64 =
25,600) and the one unrolled to 128 is satisfiable with the first overflow at tick 82 (400 × 82 = 32,800); a
probe verdict that differs is a defect of the query builder, not a timing point. Negative controls: a build that maps a timeout to `unsat` (must be caught by the first
unit test); a budget read from the environment at run time without being recorded (rejected by the
certificate schema).

## Considerations

- A wall-clock budget is the one place in the export pipeline where the answer could differ between machines; option B removes that, at the cost of tying the budget to one solver's counters.
- Inductive queries (a base case and a step) keep effort flat as controllers run longer; the scaling probe exists to show why unrolling is not the default.
- If the firmware epic is killed at its pilot, this question closes as moot.
