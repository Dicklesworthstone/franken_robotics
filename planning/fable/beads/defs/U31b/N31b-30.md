## Background

A build packet can pass every mechanical check and still be a packet nobody could build from. The plan keeps
human review of packets as an import lane: no gate depends on a reviewer, and until a review record is
imported a packet's `review` field reads `no-claim`. What the plan adds is a protocol, so that an imported
review means the same thing for every packet and so that the packet pipeline is exercised on work it was not
developed against.

The first element of the protocol is a set of *hidden briefs*. A pipeline tuned on its own development
fixtures will pass them; the question is what it does with a brief it has never seen. DESIGN: a set of 12
written briefs (TARGET), authored by someone who has not seen the development fixtures of the packet
pipeline. Each states a mission, an approved parts catalogue, a budget, the interfaces and the deliverables.
The digest of the set is committed before the pipeline version under test is frozen, and the briefs are
opened only to the frozen pipeline. A brief is used once and then retired to the development corpus, by the
rule that governs confirmation clusters in the blinded qualification lane: no cluster serves two requests,
none has appeared in any discovery dataset, and once used it is retired into discovery and never serves as a
confirmation again.

Why the order matters: if the briefs could be read before the freeze, the pipeline could be adjusted to them
and the test would measure tuning. Committing the digest first and opening only to a frozen binary makes
that impossible to do without leaving a trace. Tier: Solid [S] (bookkeeping and sealing, no new physics).

## Scope

In: the brief format; the sealed store; the commit, freeze, open, use and retire protocol with its ledger
records; the independence attestation; refusals of reuse and of early opening.
Out: running briefs through the pipeline (N31b-31); checking the resulting packets (N31b-32); review records
(N31b-33); the gate measurement (N31b-34).

## Technical approach

**1. Brief format** (schema `frob.brief/1`, name DESIGN; canonical JSON):
`{ schema, id, mission (the mission statement in the mission language, plus a prose paragraph), catalogue
(the approved parts: card references the design may use, and nothing else), budget (mass, peak power, energy,
envelope, and a compute budget for the run), interfaces (mounts, payload interface, power and data ports,
each with frame and type), deliverables (the packet sections required), author: { independent: true,
attestation }, created }`. A brief contains no design, no hint of a solution and no reference to a
development fixture.

**2. The set.** Twelve briefs (TARGET), ordered; the set digest is BLAKE3 over the concatenation of the
briefs' canonical bytes in order, under the domain string of the schema. The briefs should span the
archetypes the pipeline claims (DESIGN: at least four legged, three wheeled or tracked, two manipulators on a
base, and three that are deliberately infeasible under their catalogue or budget, because a correct refusal is
a valid outcome and must be exercised).

**3. Protocol and ledger records.**
1. *Author.* The author writes the set outside the development workspace. Default when the owner names no
   person (DESIGN): a fresh-context session with a path jail that excludes the pipeline's fixtures, tests and
   their history; its attestation records the jail's configuration.
2. *Seal.* The set is stored encrypted under an owner-held key in the sealed store; the workspace sees only
   the digest.
3. *Commit.* A ledger record `brief-set-committed { digest, count, time }`.
4. *Freeze.* A ledger record `pipeline-frozen { commit, stack receipt, binary digest }` whose sequence number
   is greater than the commit record's. A freeze recorded before the commit is invalid for this set.
5. *Open.* The runner requests brief i with the owner scope; the store checks that the requesting binary's
   digest equals the frozen one and that brief i has no use record, writes `brief-opened { id, binary }`,
   and releases the brief to that process only.
6. *Use.* On completion the runner writes `brief-used { id, outcome digest }`.
7. *Retire.* The brief is copied to the development corpus with the label `retired-brief` and its outcome;
   from then on it may be read by anyone and may never be opened as a hidden brief again.

**4. Refusals** (typed; codes from the evidence-and-protocol block of the registry, allocated with the
registry owner; names DESIGN): `brief-set-not-committed` (opening before the commit record);
`pipeline-not-frozen-or-different` (the requesting binary is not the frozen one); `brief-already-used`;
`brief-in-development-corpus` (a brief whose digest already appears in the corpus is not admissible in a
set).

**5. What is never logged.** Brief contents before retirement. Logs carry identifiers and digests only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A set of twelve briefs exists in the sealed store with its digest committed, and the commit record precedes the freeze record in the ledger.
- [ ] Each brief validates against the schema and names a mission, a catalogue, a budget, interfaces and deliverables; at least three are infeasible by construction.
- [ ] Opening a brief with a binary other than the frozen one, before the commit, or a second time, is refused with its typed refusal.
- [ ] After use each brief is in the development corpus labelled `retired-brief` and can no longer be opened as hidden.
- [ ] The author's independence attestation is on file; where the default author was used, the jail configuration is recorded.
- [ ] No brief content appears in any log before retirement (checked by scanning the logs for the briefs' distinctive strings after the run).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry briefs`: `set_digest_is_order_sensitive`; `commit_must_precede_freeze` (a fixture
ledger with the two records swapped is rejected); `open_requires_frozen_binary`; `second_open_refused`;
`retired_brief_not_admissible_in_new_set`; `brief_schema_rejects_embedded_design` (a brief with a `design`
field is invalid); `logs_carry_no_brief_text`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_briefs.sh`, on a *synthetic* set of three briefs made for the test (never the real
set): commit, freeze, open brief 1 with the frozen binary (succeeds), with a rebuilt binary (refused), open
brief 1 again (refused), retire it and try to include it in a new set (refused). Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_briefs/`: ledger sequence numbers of commit and freeze, per attempt the brief
identifier, binary digest and refusal name; the summary counts refusals expected and seen. The real set is
exercised only by the gate's script.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: twelve briefs; one use each; the ordering commit < freeze < open < use < retire. Negative
controls: a freeze recorded before the commit; a binary rebuilt after the freeze; a brief opened twice; a
brief copied into the corpus before use (the admissibility check must catch it). Determinism: digests are
exact; the protocol's outcome does not depend on the machine.

## Considerations

- Twelve briefs are consumed by one measurement. A second measurement (after a repair, or for a later pipeline version) needs a new set; the cost of authoring is part of the cost of the gate.
- The default author is a session, not a person. It satisfies "has not seen the development fixtures" only as far as its jail holds; the owner may prefer a human author, and the attestation records which it was.
- Infeasible briefs are essential: a pipeline that always produces a packet has not been tested on the outcome "typed refusal naming the binding constraint".
- The approved catalogue in a brief is a list of card references; no prices are needed, so the brief protocol does not depend on the open question about price data.
