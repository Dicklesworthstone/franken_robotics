## Background

The packet-review protocol exercises the whole path from a written brief to a build packet on briefs the
pipeline was not developed against. Its second element is *unattended production*: each brief runs from
mission query to design, qualification verdict and packet with no human edit in between; attempts and
refusals stay in the record. A brief the pipeline cannot satisfy must end in a typed refusal that names the
binding constraint. A correct refusal is a valid outcome and is reviewed as one.

Two reasons for the rule. First, a packet that needed a person to nudge the design, relax a budget or
hand-fix a file is evidence about that person, not about the pipeline; the record must be able to show that
nobody intervened. Second, the alternative to a packet must be an honest stop. A pipeline that cannot meet a
brief and still emits something packet-shaped is worse than one that refuses, and a pipeline that fails with
an untyped error has told the reader nothing about which requirement to change.

Ceiling, stated before any time comparison: no time claim is made. No reference packet has been timed by
this project, so a figure such as hours for a scripted reference against minutes for the pipeline would be
illustrative. Time from brief to accepted packet is measured only inside the ten-fold protocol, in a stratum
that includes packets, with reviewer minutes inside the clock. This bead records wall time as a fact of the
run and compares it with nothing. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the runner; its fixed policy; the run record; the no-edit guarantee and how it is checked; the two valid
outcomes; what counts as naming the binding constraint.
Out: the briefs and their sealing (N31b-30); checking the packet (N31b-32); the human review (N31b-33); any
timing comparison.

## Technical approach

**1. The runner.** A fixed program in `frob-conformance` (DESIGN: `briefrun`), with no interactive input and
no decision of its own beyond the order below. It calls only released verbs through `frob --json`, as an
agent would, with a registered seed and the brief's compute budget.
1. Open the brief through the sealed store (the frozen binary only).
2. Translate the brief mechanically: mission statement → the mission query; catalogue → the card allow-list;
   budget and interfaces → design constraints. The translation is a table in the runner, fixed before the
   freeze.
3. `mission.synthesize` (and the cascade through `solve.run`) under the allow-list and budgets → a finalist
   design with its evidence, or an answer showing that no design in the allowed family satisfies the
   mission.
4. `eval.qualify` for the finalist → a qualification verdict for its `DesignId` and `ModelId`.
5. `foundry.loads` (with referee re-extraction), `foundry.size`, `foundry.optimize --loop` where the
   topology rung is in force, re-qualification of the edited design, `foundry.check`, `foundry.tolerance`,
   `foundry.export` → the packet.
6. Write the run record and the use record of the brief.

**2. Retries.** The runner may follow a refusal's first ranked repair automatically when the repair is a
typed edit or a documented flag change that stays inside the brief (DESIGN: at most three repair steps per
verb; each is an *attempt* in the record). It may never relax the brief: a repair that would exceed a budget,
leave the catalogue or change an interface ends the run.

**3. Outcomes.** Exactly one of:
- `packet`: a packet address, with the verdict it cites.
- `refusal`: the last refusal's code, message and pointer, plus the *binding constraint*: the item of the
  brief (a budget line, a catalogue restriction, an interface, the mission's requirement) that the refusal's
  evidence shows cannot be met together with the rest. Naming is valid when (a) the pointer resolves to an
  item of the brief, and (b) the record contains evidence for it: a mission answer with an exclusion or an
  outer set that excludes the allowed family, a budget refusal with observed and required values, or a
  qualification verdict that is a counted failure at the requested level.
Anything else (a crash, an untyped error, a budget stop of the runner itself, an exit code 9) is `invalid`
and counts against the gate.

**4. The run record** (schema `frob.briefrun/1`, name DESIGN): `{ brief: digest, pipeline: { commit, stack
receipt, binary digest }, seed, calls: [{ seq, verb, request digest, envelope digest, exit, wall_ms, attempt,
refusal: code | null }], outcome: { kind, packet | refusal + binding_constraint + evidence ids }, wall_s,
budget_spent, workspace_digest_before, workspace_digest_after_excluding_run_outputs }`.

**5. No human edit, and how it is shown.** The run executes in a fresh workspace created by the runner; the
record's two workspace digests must agree once the run's own declared outputs are excluded; every design
version in the lineage from the mission answer to the exported design must have been created by a verb call
listed in `calls` (each `DesignId` in the lineage maps to an envelope digest); any design version, card or
file not so accounted for marks the run `invalid: unaccounted input`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a synthetic feasible brief the runner ends in `packet` with a complete record: every design version in the lineage maps to a recorded verb call and the workspace digests agree.
- [ ] On a synthetic brief made infeasible by its budget, and one made infeasible by its catalogue, the runner ends in `refusal` with a binding constraint that resolves to that budget line or catalogue restriction, with evidence identifiers.
- [ ] A file edited in the workspace during a run, or a design version created outside the recorded calls, marks the run `invalid`.
- [ ] The runner never relaxes a brief: a planted repair that would exceed the mass budget ends the run instead of being applied.
- [ ] Attempts and refusals along the way are all in the record, including those later repaired.
- [ ] Wall time is recorded and no ratio or comparison of times is computed or printed.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance briefrun`: `translation_table_is_total` (every brief field maps to a
constraint or is rejected); `lineage_accounting_detects_foreign_version`; `repair_inside_brief_is_attempt`;
`repair_outside_brief_ends_run`; `binding_constraint_requires_pointer_and_evidence` (a refusal with a pointer
to an item not in the brief is `invalid`); `exit_9_is_invalid`; `no_time_ratio_in_record_schema`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_briefrun.sh`, on three *synthetic* briefs (feasible; over-tight mass budget; a catalogue
without any actuator able to hold the payload): runs `briefrun` for each, validates the record, recomputes
the lineage accounting, and for the feasible one runs the mechanical pre-review on the packet. Then a
tampered run (a file touched mid-run by a second process). Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_briefrun/`: per brief its digest (not its content), the sequence of verbs with exit
codes and wall times, attempts, the outcome, the binding constraint's pointer, the evidence block of the
final envelope; the summary counts `packet`, `refusal` and `invalid`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers (synthetic): the over-tight budget brief sets a mass budget below the mass of its own required
payload plus the lightest allowed actuator set, so no design exists and the binding constraint is the mass
line; the catalogue brief's heaviest allowed actuator has a continuous torque below the static holding
torque of the payload at the stated reach, so the binding constraint is the catalogue. Negative controls:
the tampered run; a runner build that swallows a refusal and continues with a default; a runner that reports
`refusal` with the pointer of an internal field. Determinism: with a fixed seed and frozen binary two runs of
one brief give the same outcome and the same packet address.

## Considerations

- The runner is deliberately not an agent: its policy is fixed before the freeze so that the result does not depend on a session's choices. Agent-driven production from briefs belongs to the ten-fold protocol.
- Re-qualification after the mass edit is part of the fixed order; a packet citing the pre-edit verdict is a defect the pre-review catches.
- A typed refusal with a correct binding constraint is as much a success of the pipeline as a packet, and the gate counts it so.
- Real hidden briefs are opened only by the gate's script; this bead's tests use synthetic briefs so that no hidden brief is spent on development.
