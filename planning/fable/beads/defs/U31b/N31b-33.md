## Background

Human review of a build packet is an import lane in this program. Decision ADR-FOUNDRY-11: no gate depends
on a reviewer, a machine shop or a board; those are import lanes that rest at `no-claim`, because a simulator
cannot gate on what it lacks. A human review of a packet, a quotation from a machine shop and a first-article
inspection are records that may be imported; until one is, the packet's `review` reads `no-claim`.

This bead gives the lane a record format and an import step, so that an imported review means the same thing
for every packet. It also fixes what is gated and what is only recorded. The gate covers the mechanical half
only (the pre-review of items M1 to M6 and the import mechanics). The human half is a recorded count with a
TARGET: at least 11 of the 12 hidden-brief packets receive `accept` or `accept-with-changes`. The count, the
reviewer minutes and the list of rubric failures are printed in the release rubric. While no record exists
the count reads `no-claim`, and no text may describe a packet as reviewed.

The rubric exists because of what a program cannot see. The standing example: a two-part bracket whose second
fastener sits behind the first part once it is mounted passes every per-part check and every mechanical item,
and cannot be assembled in the listed order. Rubric item R2 is for that case. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the record schema and its validation; the import step; the sidecar store; the manifest view; the refusal
for a wrong digest; the count over a brief set; the wording rule; the hook for the release rubric.
Out: the mechanical pre-review (N31b-32); recruiting or instructing reviewers; any timing of review inside a
speed comparison (that belongs to the ten-fold protocol, with reviewer minutes inside the clock).

## Technical approach

**1. The record** (schema `frob.review/1`), a sidecar addressed by the packet's manifest digest:

```json
{ "schema": "frob.review/1", "packet": "manifest digest", "brief": "brief digest or null",
  "reviewer": { "independent": true, "role": "mechanical designer" },
  "rubric": { "R1_producibility": "pass", "R2_assembly_and_tool_access": "fail",
              "R3_harness_and_service": "pass", "R4_quotable_from_packet_alone": "pass",
              "R5_evidence_legible_and_not_overstated": "pass" },
  "notes": [{ "item": "R2", "part": "bracket-b", "text": "second fastener is enclosed after step 3" }],
  "outcome": "accept-with-changes", "minutes": 55, "date": "YYYY-MM-DD" }
```

Field rules. `packet` is the manifest digest of the packet reviewed. `brief` is the digest of the hidden
brief the packet answers, or null. `independent` means the reviewer took no part in developing the pipeline
or the design. The five rubric items take `pass` or `fail` (DESIGN: also `not-assessed`, allowed only when
`role` is a machine-shop quotation or a first-article inspection, which use the same sidecar). `outcome` is
`accept`, `accept-with-changes` or `reject` (DESIGN: the third value; the plan names the first two as the
favourable ones). A `fail` on any item is incompatible with `accept`. Every `fail` needs at least one note
naming the item. `minutes` is the reviewer's time. Notes are imported text: they are marked untrusted data
and are never interpreted as instructions.

**2. Import.** `frob foundry check <packet> --review-record <file> --json`:
1. Run the mechanical pre-review; a packet that fails it cannot have a review imported (the reviewer's time
   is not spent on a packet whose files disagree).
2. Validate the record against the schema and the field rules.
3. Compare `packet` with the packet's manifest digest; a mismatch is refused
   (`review-record-packet-mismatch`; repair: re-issue the record against the right packet).
4. Store the record's canonical bytes as a sidecar keyed by (manifest digest, record digest). **Importing a
   record changes no byte of the packet**: the manifest still says `review: "no-claim"` and the address is
   unchanged.
5. The *manifest view* (what `frob foundry check <packet>` and reports render) then shows `review` as the
   record's outcome with the record digest in place of `no-claim`, the reviewer's role, and whether the
   reviewer was independent. With several records, all are listed; the view's headline is the latest
   independent review.

**3. The count over a brief set.** For a set of hidden briefs: the number of packets whose latest
independent review record has outcome `accept` or `accept-with-changes`, out of the number of packets
produced; plus total reviewer minutes and the list of rubric failures by item. TARGET: at least 11 of 12. A
refusal outcome of a brief run has no packet; its review, if any, is recorded against the run record's digest
in the same sidecar format (DESIGN) and is listed separately, not counted among packets. With no record the
count is `no-claim`.

**4. Wording rule.** A packet without an independent review record may not be described as reviewed in any
envelope, report, view header or release text; the strings are constants and a check scans generated text. A
mechanical pass is never rendered as `review: accept`. A record with `independent: false` renders as "review
(not independent)" and does not count.

**5. Release rubric hook.** A read-only query returns, for a named brief set, `{ packets, accepted,
accepted_with_changes, rejected, minutes_total, rubric_failures: [{ item, packet, part, text digest }] }` or
`no-claim`; the release rubric prints it as recorded, with the target beside it and without calling it a
gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Importing a valid record leaves the packet's bytes and manifest digest unchanged, and the manifest view shows the record's outcome with the record digest in place of `no-claim`.
- [ ] A record whose `packet` digest does not match is refused, and nothing is stored.
- [ ] A record with a `fail` item and outcome `accept`, a `fail` without a note, an unknown rubric key or a missing `independent` field is rejected by validation.
- [ ] With no record, the view shows `review: no-claim`, the count query returns `no-claim`, and the wording check finds no text describing the packet as reviewed.
- [ ] A non-independent record is stored and shown as such and is not counted.
- [ ] The count query returns the right numbers on a synthetic set of three packets with outcomes accept, accept-with-changes and reject (2 of 3; minutes summed; one rubric failure listed).
- [ ] Notes are carried with the untrusted mark through every verb that echoes them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry review`: `import_leaves_packet_digest_unchanged`; `wrong_packet_digest_refused`;
`fail_item_with_accept_rejected`; `fail_requires_note`; `not_assessed_only_for_non_review_roles`;
`view_shows_outcome_and_record_digest`; `latest_independent_record_is_headline`; `count_two_of_three`;
`no_record_is_no_claim`; `mechanical_pass_never_renders_accept`; `notes_marked_untrusted`;
`record_canonical_bytes_stable` (key order and whitespace do not change the record digest).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/foundry_review_record.sh`: (1) `frob foundry check <packet> --json`, asserting `review:
no-claim`; (2) import the example record (R2 fail; `accept-with-changes`; 55 minutes), asserting the view and
that BLAKE3 of every packet file and the address are unchanged; (3) import a record with a wrong digest,
asserting the refusal; (4) import a record for a packet that fails the pre-review, asserting the refusal to
import; (5) the count query on the synthetic set. Logs per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/foundry_review_record/`:
packet digest before and after, record digest, outcome, `independent`, the refusal name, the count; the
summary names GATE-FOUNDRY-13 (its two import clauses).

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the example record validates and renders `accept-with-changes`; the digest is unchanged by
import. Negative controls: a record edited after import (its digest no longer matches the sidecar key and it
is rejected on read); an importer that rewrites the manifest's `review` field (the digest-unchanged test must
fail); a report template containing "reviewed" for a packet with no record. Determinism: exact; identical on
every machine.

## Considerations

- The record is a person's statement. The tool stores and shows it; it does not verify that the reviewer is who the record says or that they were independent. The view therefore always prints the role and the independence flag as recorded.
- 11 of 12 is a recorded target, not a gate: a release may ship with the count at `no-claim`, and the rubric must then say exactly that.
- Reviewer minutes are recorded for the ten-fold protocol's packet stratum, where they sit inside the clock; here they are a plain record.
- The schema as printed has no field for a refusal outcome's review; recording it against the run record's digest is a DESIGN extension flagged for the owner.
