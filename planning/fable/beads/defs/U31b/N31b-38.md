## Background

A screening campaign compares many robot designs cheaply. Designs declare how each link is filled: `solid`,
a shell of some wall thickness, a lattice fraction. Mass and inertia follow from the fill. Nothing in that
arithmetic asks whether the declared wall could carry the robot. So a screening campaign rewards links that
could not be built: a limb segment whose shell is thinner than any section that survives its own loads has
less distal inertia, lower swing torque and a better score than its buildable version would have.

The remedy is to run Foundry's beam rung, which costs milliseconds, as a validity check on what a design
declares. This amends the decision that Foundry's work runs after the design decision: the topology rung,
the finite-element checks and the build packet stay with qualified finalists; the beam rung also runs at
preflight and after the first rollouts as a validity check that can block promotion.

The check is needed at M3, with screening. The full beam rung (envelope cases from load campaigns, the
finite-element form of the structural margin, fatigue at notches, the verb `foundry.size`) lands at M5 with
the rest of Foundry and needs the sibling's structural libraries. The closed forms need none of that. This
bead delivers them early, as a small kernel with no sibling dependency, so that the M3 check has something to
call and the M5 work extends the same code.

What it is not: a floor on mass, not a design. No fatigue, no joints, no local buckling beyond the card's
knock-down, and impact enters only through the load factor until telemetry exists. Tier: Solid [S] for the
check; the kernel is closed-form arithmetic.

## Scope

In: the beam idealization of a link between its ports; section properties; four criteria; the continuous
mass floor and the lightest passing stock section; inertia about the proximal joint; exact decisions; the
result type.
Out: where loads come from (N31b-39 for bounded statics, N31b-40 for measured peaks); the preflight wiring;
the `{shell: sized}` fill (N31b-41); everything of the M5 beam rung beyond these formulas (the seed bead BD-401
extends these modules with the verb `foundry.size`, its load-set reader, the value of the frequency floor, the
fatigue field of the result, analysis A-15 and the finite-element form, and restates none of these formulas;
N31a-60 extends the load model from a tip wrench to a full time-coherent link load; N31a-61 adds stock sizing
under campaign loads with notch fatigue and the baseline); the M5 crate scaffold (N31a-01, which extends the
crate this bead creates: features, module map, class ceiling, the decision record). This bead is the one
definition of the section properties, the four static criteria, the stock search and its tie rule, and the
exact decisions; BD-401, N31a-60 and N31a-61 depend on it.

## Technical approach

Crate `frob-foundry` (layer 5), modules `beam/{section,criteria,stock,size}.rs` (the layout the M5 bead
uses). No dependency on the airlock.

**1. Idealization.** A link between its proximal joint port and each distal port is a prismatic member of
length L along unit axis e, as a thin-walled tube or a rectangular section. A load case is a wrench (f, m) at
the distal port, one consistent instant. Internal loads at the root: axial N = f·e; torque T = m·e; bending
moment M = ‖m_⊥ + L·(e × f)‖, where m_⊥ is the part of m normal to e; shear V = ‖f − N·e‖.

**2. Section properties.** Tube of mean radius r and wall t: area A = 2π·r·t; second moment I = π·r³·t;
section modulus Z = π·r²·t; torsion constant J = 2π·r³·t. Rectangle b × h: A = b·h; I = b·h³/12; Z = b·h²/6.

**3. Criteria**, each a utilization u that passes when u ≤ 1:
- *Yield*: σ = |N|/A + M/Z; τ = T/(2π·r²·t) for the tube; u = √(σ² + 3·τ²)/(S_y/SF).
- *Euler buckling*: P_cr = π²·E·I/(K·L)², K = 2 for a member treated as a cantilever, 1 for pinned ends;
  u = SF_b·N_compressive/P_cr, SF_b the declared buckling safety factor (default 2, the load factor the
  independent buckling check of the M5 work requires, as N31a-60 uses it).
- *Local wall buckling* (tube): the card's knock-down factor times the classical thin-cylinder stress
  E·t/(r·√(3·(1 − ν²))) (DESIGN: the plan fixes "a card knock-down"; this is the value it scales);
  u = σ_compressive/σ_local.
- *First bending frequency* (when a floor is supplied): the beam formula with end masses; for a cantilever
  with tip mass m_tip and member mass m_b, f₁ = (1/2π)·√(3·E·I/((m_tip + 0.2357·m_b)·L³)) (DESIGN: the
  one-mode Rayleigh value; 0.2357 = 33/140); u = 1.015·f_floor/f₁. DESIGN: the factor 1.015 is in the
  criterion because the one-mode value is an upper bound on the true frequency (1.5% high with no end mass),
  so a test without it could pass a member whose frequency is below the floor. It is the same test as
  N31a-60's f₁/1.015 ≥ f_floor and BD-401's; the three beads were reconciled to this one form. The floor is an
  input: the callers supply it (BD-401 states the value `foundry.size` passes).

**4. Two answers.**
- *The floor*: the smallest wall t_req (tube at the link's declared mean radius) or depth (rectangle at
  declared width) for which every criterion passes in every load case; closed form where one criterion
  governs (yield in pure bending: t_req = M/((S_y/SF)·π·r²)), otherwise by monotone bisection on the wall.
  Mass floor m_floor = ρ·A(t_req)·L.
- *The stock section*: the lightest entry of the stock card that passes everything (ties: smaller outer
  dimension, then card order); `no-stock-passes` with the nearest entry and its utilization otherwise.
Both come with inertia about the proximal joint for a uniform member, m·L²/3 about a transverse axis through
the root (the tube's own radius term m·(r_o² + r_i²)/4 is added and reported separately).

**5. Exact decisions.** Card values and loads given as rationals are compared in exact arithmetic (class
D0) wherever the criterion is rational in its inputs (yield squared; buckling with π² bracketed by
outward-rounded rationals); otherwise with outward-rounded intervals. A decision inside the rounding interval
is `at-limit` and counts as not passing.

**6. Result** (DESIGN):
`MemberSizing { link, port, t_required, m_floor, stock: Section | NoStockPasses { nearest, utilization },
m_stock, inertia_proximal_floor, inertia_proximal_stock, governing: { case, criterion, utilization },
decision_class: D0, evidence_class: Estimated }`. The evidence class is Estimated because the material card
is.

**7. Performance.** No allocation in the per-case path; a link with ten load cases and a twenty-entry stock
card sizes in well under a millisecond (TARGET: 20 µs per link per case on the reference host), so that the
whole check fits its 50 ms budget on a robot of twenty links.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The tube known answer: a cantilevered tube 0.3 m long with 600 N across its tip, S_y = 276 MPa, safety factor 2, mean radius 15 mm, density 2,700 kg/m³ returns t_required = 1.85 mm and m_floor = 0.141 kg within 1%, and inertia about the root 4.2 × 10⁻³ kg·m² within 1%.
- [ ] From a stock card of walls 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 mm the same case picks 2.0 mm (mass 0.153 kg).
- [ ] Rational inputs at exactly the limit are reported `at-limit` and do not pass; one unit in the last place below passes.
- [ ] A material card without yield strength or without provenance yields a typed error naming the field (the M5 verb maps it to its refusal).
- [ ] The crate builds at M3 with no sibling dependency and passes the layer check; `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-foundry` exists with its ten sections, marking everything but this kernel as not yet present.
- [ ] Timing: at most 20 µs per link per case on the reference host, measured and recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-foundry beam`:
- `tube_section_properties`: r = 15 mm, t = 2 mm: A = 1.885 × 10⁻⁴ m², I = 2.121 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴, Z = 1.414 × 10⁻⁶ m³, J = 4.241 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴; and a second row (from the M5 seed BD-401, kept here so that the test has one definition) r = 14 mm, t = 2 mm: A = 1.7593 × 10⁻⁴ m², I = 1.7241 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴, Z = 1.2315 × 10⁻⁶ m³, J = 3.4482 × 10⁻⁸ m⁴.
- `frequency_criterion_carries_margin`: f₁ = 101 Hz against a floor of 100 Hz does not pass (u = 1.005); f₁ = 102 Hz passes (u = 0.995).
- `root_loads_from_tip_force`: f = 600 N normal to e, L = 0.3 m: M = 180 N·m, N = 0, T = 0, V = 600 N.
- `tube_floor_known_answer`: t_req = 180/(138 × 10⁶ × π × 0.015²) = 1.8453 mm; m_floor = 2π × 0.015 × 0.0018453 × 0.3 × 2,700 = 0.14087 kg; m·L²/3 = 4.226 × 10⁻³ kg·m².
- `stock_pick_two_millimetres`: mass 0.15268 kg; inertia 4.580 × 10⁻³ kg·m².
- `under_built_ratio`: declared 0.05 kg against the floor: 2.82.
- `euler_buckling_cantilever_and_pinned`: tube r = 10 mm, t = 1 mm, L = 0.5 m, E = 69 GPa: 8,558 N pinned; 2,139 N as a cantilever (K = 2).
- `von_mises_combination`: σ = 100 MPa, τ = 50 MPa → 132.29 MPa.
- `axial_plus_bending`: N adds |N|/A to the bending stress with the governing sign.
- `at_limit_is_not_pass`; `tie_break_is_deterministic`; `no_stock_passes_reports_nearest`.
- `monotone_bisection_matches_closed_form` (combined bending and torsion against a brute-force scan at 1 µm steps).
- `card_without_yield_is_typed_error`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `solid-rod-modulus` (π·r³/4 used for the tube), `ignore-torsion` (τ dropped from the yield check on a torsion-dominated case), `moment-arm-from-midspan` (L/2 in place of L). BD-401 reuses the first two under these names.

### End-to-end script and logging
This kernel has no verb of its own at M3; it is exercised from the user's side through `frob design check`
by the check's script (`scripts/e2e/design_check_member_sizing.sh`, in the preflight bead) and by the gate's
script. This bead contributes `scripts/e2e/foundry_beam_kernel.sh`, which runs the test target (`cargo test
-p frob-foundry beam`), a micro-benchmark and the three planted defects through the negative-control runner
(each must be caught). It sources the STD-E2E helper and logs to
`target/e2e-logs/foundry_beam_kernel/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`N31b-38`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; metrics `t_required_mm`
(1.85 within 1%), `m_floor_kg` (0.141 within 1%), `inertia_root_kgm2` (4.2 × 10⁻³ within 1%),
`ns_per_link_case` (threshold 20,000) with the host fingerprint; per planted defect its id and outcome; on a
miss the first differing value with its expected value and the test to repeat it. The summary names
GATE-FOUNDRY-15 (its first clause).

### Known answers and negative controls
The tube case (root moment 180 N·m; allowable 138 MPa; 1.85 mm; 0.141 kg; 4.2 × 10⁻³ kg·m²; a 0.05 kg
declaration under-built by 2.8). The cantilever of the chapter's known answers through the rectangular
section (30 MPa root stress for 50 N on a 0.20 m bar of 20 × 10 mm). Negative controls: the three planted
defects. Metamorphic: scaling all loads by k scales every stress utilization by k and t_req by k for a
yield-governed tube; rotating a transverse load about the tube axis changes nothing; swapping width and depth
of a rectangle under a single-axis bending load changes the result (a check that the axis is respected).
Determinism: class D0 for decisions; identical on every machine.

## Considerations

- The plan dates the full beam rung at M5 and this check at M3. This bead resolves the order by delivering the closed forms first; the M5 seed must build on these modules. If the lead prefers, this bead can be folded into an earlier start of that seed, but the M3 gate cannot wait for M5.
- The floor is a necessary condition only. A link that passes it can still fail fatigue, a joint interface or a real impact; the check's wording says "under-built", never "adequate".
- The reported mass floor uses the continuous wall; the stock section is heavier. Flagging uses the floor (it cannot be argued with); the sized fill uses the stock wall.
