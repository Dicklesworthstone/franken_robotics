## Background

The design preflight (`frob design check`) runs eight stages on a design before anything is simulated. Stage
6, statics and sizing, checks support margins, the mass spiral, actuator envelopes, thermal steady state, the
bus and budgets. It did not check structure: a design could declare a limb shell so thin that no section
could carry the robot, gain low distal inertia and low swing torque from it, and outscore its buildable
version. The plan's hypothesis register carries this as an active item: a closed-form check that a declared
link wall survives the design's own loads keeps screening from rewarding links that cannot be built; how
often it changes the winning actuator or gear ratio is unmeasured.

This bead adds the check to stage 6, *before any rollout*, beside the actuator checks: the beam rung of the
structural-margin analysis (A-15) under a bounded load set from statics, as a validity check on declared link
walls. The full form of that analysis stays a separate verb. The check does not make structure part of the
decision-time thesis; it removes one way for screening to mislead.

Ownership as the plan states it: `frob-foundry` sizes; `frob-analyze` calls it at stage 6; the verb is the
existing `foundry.size`, called by `design.check`. Two workspace rules shape how: a crate depends only on
lower crates (`frob-analyze` is layer 4, `frob-foundry` layer 5), and a lower crate reaches a service above
it only through a trait it defines itself. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the bounded statics load set; which links are checked; the comparison of declared mass and inertia with
the floor; the finding `under-built` with its typed repair; the trait through which stage 6 reaches the
sizing kernel; timing.
Out: the sizing formulas (N31b-38); the check under measured loads, the label and the promotion block
(N31b-40); the sized fill (N31b-41); the corpus comparison (N31b-42).

## Technical approach

**1. Which links.** Every link whose fill is a shell or a lattice. A `solid` link is reported `not checked:
solid fill` (its wall is its radius; the mass-plausibility advisory covers it); a `declared` fill (imported
mass properties) is reported `not checked: declared fill`; `massless` bodies are skipped.

**2. The bounded load set from statics.** Each foot, wheel or end-effector port carries the robot's weight
times a class load factor, in the worst direction the port admits. Defaults (DESIGN, on the archetype card,
overridable per design with provenance): 3 for legged feet, 2 for wheels, 1.5 for manipulator ports with the
rated payload added. Reading for manipulator ports (DESIGN, recorded for the owner): the reference load is
the rated payload plus the weight of the arm distal to the link, times 1.5; an end effector does not carry
the base's weight. For each checked link on the chain from an end port to the base, the load at the link is
that port force F transmitted through the chain, bounded over the declared joint ranges without simulating:
axial N ≤ F; bending moment at the link's proximal joint M ≤ F·R, where R is the largest distance from that
joint to the end port over the joint ranges (bounded above by the sum of the lengths of the link and the
links distal to it); torque about the link axis T ≤ F·R_⊥, with R_⊥ the largest lateral offset of the end
port from the link's axis (zero for a planar chain). "Worst direction the port admits" is taken over the
port's admissible force set (for a contact port, the friction cone about its normal; for an end effector,
any direction). The set is small: one case per end port per checked link. It is labelled `load set:
bounded-statics` with the factors used.

**3. Sizing and comparison.** For each checked link the sizing kernel returns the floor (smallest passing
wall at the link's declared mean dimensions, its mass m_floor and its inertia about the proximal joint) and
the lightest passing stock section. The check compares them with what the design's fill produces: declared
mass m_decl and declared inertia about the proximal joint.
- `under-built` when m_decl < m_floor (exact comparison; `at-limit` counts as under-built). The finding
  carries the ratio m_floor/m_decl, the governing case and criterion, the declared and floor inertias, and
  the load factor used.
- otherwise `sized-ok (bounded-statics)`, with the margin.

**4. The finding.** Code `A-15.<link>.under-built`, severity `high`, exit code 2 for `design.check`
(completed with findings; this is a finding, not a refusal). Ranked repairs as typed edits, each with its
predicted effect: (1) `set-fill` to `{shell: <stock wall>}` with the predicted mass and inertia change; (2)
`set-fill` to `{shell: sized}`; (3) a larger mean radius from the stock card, where that is lighter than a
thicker wall. The envelope's `next_actions` carries the first repair as a command.

**5. Reaching the kernel.** `frob-analyze` defines the trait (DESIGN):
`trait MemberSizer { fn size(&self, req: &MemberSizingRequest) -> MemberSizingAnswer; }` with plain request
and answer types of its own (link geometry between ports, material and stock card references, load cases,
safety factors; floor, stock, governing case). `frob-foundry` implements it over its kernel. `frob-verbs`
passes the implementation into stage 6 when it runs `design.check`. With no implementation present the stage
reports `structure: not checked (no sizer)`, which is never a pass. The public verb `foundry.size` (M5)
calls the same kernel; a test asserts both paths return the same answer for the same request.

**6. Evidence and timing.** Decisions are exact (class D0) for rational inputs; the evidence class is
Estimated because cards are. The stage-6 timing report gains a line `member sizing`. Budget: the check adds
at most 50 ms to `design.check` on the reference robots (TARGET), inside stage 6's 600 ms and the 2 s total
for stages 1 to 6.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A planted under-built link (the lower-leg segment of a reference quadruped declared at 0.05 kg where the floor is 0.141 kg for a 20 kg robot with factor 3) is flagged `under-built` by `frob design check` before any rollout, with ratio 2.8, the governing case, and the three ranked repairs.
- [ ] Applying the first repair and re-running the check clears the finding.
- [ ] The check reads only the design, the cards and the archetype factors: it runs with no simulation and no telemetry.
- [ ] Links with `solid` or `declared` fills are reported `not checked` with the reason, never as passing.
- [ ] Without a registered sizer the stage reports `structure: not checked (no sizer)`.
- [ ] The check adds at most 50 ms to `design.check` on each reference robot (measured median and 95th percentile recorded).
- [ ] The layer check passes: `frob-analyze` has no dependency on `frob-foundry`; the inversion is recorded as an architecture decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-analyze stage6_member_sizing`:
- `bounded_statics_foot_force`: 20 kg robot, legged foot, factor 3: F = 588.6 N.
- `moment_bound_is_force_times_reach`: lower leg 0.3 m, end port at its tip: M = 176.6 N·m; upper leg 0.3 m above it: reach bound 0.6 m, M ≤ 353.2 N·m.
- `under_built_ratio`: floor for 588.6 N on the 0.3 m tube (r = 15 mm, allowable 138 MPa): t_req = 1.810 mm, m_floor = 0.1382 kg; declared 0.05 kg → ratio 2.76 (the chapter's rounded case with 600 N gives 1.85 mm, 0.141 kg and 2.8).
- `at_limit_counts_as_under_built`; `solid_fill_not_checked`; `declared_fill_not_checked`.
- `wheel_factor_two`; `manipulator_port_reference_load` (payload 2 kg, distal arm 1.5 kg, factor 1.5: 51.5 N).
- `repairs_are_typed_edits_in_rank_order`; `finding_code_format`.
- `no_sizer_reports_not_checked`.
- `verb_and_stage_paths_agree` (in `frob-verbs` tests, once the M5 verb exists).
- Planted defects, each caught: `factor-one` (load factor ignored), `reach-of-link-only` (distal links dropped from the moment arm of a proximal link), `compare-with-stock-not-floor` reported as a behaviour change, not a defect, and pinned by a test of the stated rule.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_check_member_sizing.sh`: (1) `frob design check quad-thin-shank --json`, asserting exit
2, the finding code, ratio 2.8 within 2%, and the repairs; (2) `frob design edit quad-thin-shank set-fill …`
with the first repair, then `design check`, asserting the finding is gone; (3) `frob design check` on each
reference robot with and without the check enabled (a test switch), recording the added time; (4) a build
without the sizer, asserting `not checked`. Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/design_check_member_sizing/`: per link `m_declared_kg`, `m_floor_kg`, `ratio`,
`governing_case`, `criterion`; `added_ms_p50` and `added_ms_p95` (threshold 50); the evidence block (tier
`static`, determinism `exact`); the summary names GATE-FOUNDRY-15.

### Known answers and negative controls
The tube case (600 N across the tip of a 0.3 m tube: 180 N·m; 138 MPa allowable; 1.85 mm; 0.141 kg; 4.2 ×
10⁻³ kg·m²; a 0.05 kg declaration under-built by a factor of 2.8, with swing inertia understated by the same
factor). Negative controls: the planted under-built design must be flagged; its sized twin must pass; the
three planted defects. Metamorphic: scaling robot mass by k scales every port force by k; adding a distal
link never lowers a proximal link's moment bound; renaming links changes nothing. Determinism: exact
decisions, identical on every machine.

## Considerations

- The moment bound F·R over joint ranges is an outer bound: it can flag a link that a pose-aware analysis would pass. That is acceptable for a floor that asks for millimetres of wall; the measured-load check after rollouts replaces it with what actually happened.
- The load factors are DESIGN defaults on the archetype card and are the weakest input of the check; the finding prints the factor so a reader can argue with it.
- The manipulator-port reading is a DESIGN interpretation of a short sentence; the literal reading (the base's weight at the gripper) would flag every arm.
- The lattice fill is treated as an isotropic fraction; whether it needs anisotropy is an open question owned by the Foundry lead, and the assumption is printed in the check report.
- This is a sixth trait inversion in a workspace that lists five; the decision record should say why the kernel is not simply placed in `frob-analyze` (the plan assigns sizing to `frob-foundry`, and the M5 beam rung extends the same code).
