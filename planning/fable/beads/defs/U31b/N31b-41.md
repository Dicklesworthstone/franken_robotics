## Background

In this program's design language a body's mass and inertia are not typed in: they are computed from a
Region, a material and a *fill* that says how the Region is occupied: `solid`, `{shell: t}` (a wall of
thickness t), `{lattice: φ}`, `declared` or `massless`. There is no default fill. A wall thickness is
therefore something an agent must choose for every limb segment, and a screening campaign rewards choosing
it too thin: less distal inertia, lower swing torque, a better score, and a link that could not be built.

The member-sizing check catches a wall that is too thin. This bead offers the other half: a link may ask to
be sized instead of declaring a wall. The fill `{shell: sized}` takes the thinnest stock wall that passes
the beam-rung criteria, and the model's mass properties are set from it before the first rollout (DESIGN).
It is to become the default for archetype templates if the gate's corpus comparison supports it; otherwise it
stays an option and the check stays a promotion guard.

Why a fill and not a post-hoc repair: an agent that must choose a wall will choose one, check it, and
iterate; an agent that may write `sized` cannot get the wall wrong and cannot game it. The cost is that mass
now depends on loads, and loads on mass. Tier: Solid [S].

## Scope

In: the grammar; the resolution step and where it runs; the fixed-point iteration and its termination; the
resolved design version and its provenance; re-resolution under measured loads; the refusal when no stock
wall passes; the template-default switch.
Out: the sizing kernel (N31b-38); the checks (N31b-39, N31b-40); the corpus experiment that decides the
default (N31b-42).

## Technical approach

**1. Grammar.** In a design file a body's fill may be `{ "shell": "sized" }`, optionally with `"stock":
<stock card reference>` and `"safety_factor": <rational>` (defaults from the archetype card). It is valid
only for Regions the shell rule supports with a uniform wall (box, cylinder, sphere, capsule, and a mesh via
its inward offset). The S-expression view prints `(shell sized)`.

**2. Authored and resolved versions.** The authored design keeps `sized`. A *resolved* design version is the
authored one with every sized fill replaced by a concrete `{shell: t}` through typed edits (`set-fill`),
each carrying provenance `sized: { load_set, stock, wall, governing_case }`. Only a resolved version can be
compiled to a model; its `DesignId` and `ModelId` are computed from the concrete walls. The lineage edge
from authored to resolved is of kind `resolve-sized`.

**3. Where resolution runs.** In the verb layer, before the first mass computation, for every verb that
needs a model of a design containing sized fills (`design.check`, `sim.run`, the cascade): the verb layer
calls the member-sizing trait (defined in `frob-analyze`, implemented by `frob-foundry`), because neither
`frob-design` nor `frob-model` may depend on a higher crate. The result is memoized by the authored design's
identity and the load set's identity.

**4. The fixed point.** Port forces depend on the robot's weight, which depends on the walls being chosen.
Resolution is a monotone iteration on a finite set:
1. Set every sized link to the thinnest wall of its stock card.
2. Compute the mass roll-up of the whole robot.
3. Build the bounded statics load set (weight times the class load factor at each foot, wheel or
   end-effector port; moment bounds over joint ranges).
4. For each sized link, find the thinnest stock wall that passes under that load set; never choose a wall
   thinner than the current one.
5. If no wall changed, stop; otherwise go to 2.
Each pass raises at least one wall or stops, and walls come from finite lists, so the iteration ends after
at most Σ_links (stock entries − 1) passes. It reaches the least fixed point: the lightest assignment that
is consistent with its own weight (the general fact about monotone maps on finite chains that the plan's
co-design construction rests on). The number of passes and the final walls are recorded.

**5. No stock passes.** If a link needs a wall thicker than its stock card offers (or thicker than half the
Region's smallest dimension, where a shell stops being a shell), resolution refuses: interim code
FROB-REF-105 with the pointer at the fill, the governing case and utilization of the thickest entry, and
ranked repairs as typed edits: a larger mean radius; a stronger material card; `solid` fill.

**6. After rollouts.** If a resolved link is `under-built` under measured loads, the repair offered by the
promotion refusal is re-resolution with the measured load set: `design.edit … resolve-sized --loads
measured:<batch>` creates a new resolved version whose walls pass under the union of the bounded statics and
the measured cases. The old version keeps its label.

**7. Templates.** Archetype templates carry a switch `limb_fill_default` with values `declared` (each limb
segment has an explicit wall, as today) and `sized`. It ships as `declared`. The corpus comparison flips it
to `sized` only if its decision rule says so; the flip is one data change in the template registry and
creates new template versions.

**8. Evidence.** A resolved wall is class Estimated (cards). Its decision is exact for rational inputs. The
design check prints, per sized link, the wall, the mass, the governing case and the number of passes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A design with `{shell: sized}` on its lower-leg segments resolves to concrete walls before any rollout; the resolved version's model has the mass and inertia of those walls; the authored version still says `sized`.
- [ ] The tube case: a 0.3 m limb segment of mean radius 15 mm carrying 600 N across its tip with stock walls 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 mm resolves to 2.0 mm (0.153 kg); its stage-6 check passes with the floor shown as 1.85 mm and 0.141 kg.
- [ ] The fixed point terminates and is the least one: on a fixture where the first pass's weight forces a second raise, the result equals a brute-force search for the lightest self-consistent assignment.
- [ ] A link that no stock wall satisfies is refused with the pointer at the fill and three ranked repairs.
- [ ] Re-resolution under measured loads yields a new version that passes the measured check.
- [ ] `sim.run` on an authored design resolves first; it never simulates a model with an unresolved fill, and the resolved identities are echoed in the envelope's `bound` field.
- [ ] The template switch exists and ships as `declared`; changing it changes template identities and nothing else.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-design fill_sized` and `cargo test -p frob-verbs resolve_sized`:
- `grammar_roundtrip` (JSON and S-expression views); `sized_on_unsupported_region_refused`.
- `resolved_version_has_concrete_wall_and_provenance`; `authored_keeps_sized`; `lineage_edge_resolve_sized`.
- `tube_resolves_to_two_millimetres`: 0.15268 kg; inertia about the root 4.580 × 10⁻³ kg·m².
- `fixed_point_two_passes` (synthetic): two sized links; pass 1 picks 1.5 mm for both at an initial robot mass of 19 kg; the added mass raises the weight past the 1.5 mm limit of the proximal link, pass 2 picks 2.0 mm for it; pass 3 changes nothing; the brute-force lightest consistent assignment is (2.0, 1.5).
- `walls_never_decrease_between_passes`; `pass_count_bound` (two links with four stock entries each: at most six raising passes).
- `no_stock_passes_refused_with_repairs`.
- `reresolve_under_measured_loads` (900 N measured: 2.77 mm required → stock 3.0 mm if offered, else refusal).
- `model_identity_depends_on_resolved_wall`; `memoized_by_design_and_load_set`.
- `template_switch_default_declared`.
- Planted defects, each caught: `single-pass-resolution` (stops after pass 1 on the two-pass fixture), `resolve-in-place` (mutates the authored version), `thinner-on-later-pass`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_fill_sized.sh`: (1) `frob design check quad-sized --json`, asserting resolved walls per
sized link, the pass count, and no `under-built` finding; (2) `frob sim run quad-sized …`, asserting the
envelope's `bound` names the resolved design and model; (3) `frob design show` on the authored design,
asserting `sized` is still there; (4) a design whose stock card is too thin, asserting exit 3 and the
repairs; (5) after a push-recovery batch that overloads a link, `frob design edit … resolve-sized --loads
measured:<batch>` and a passing measured check. Logs per STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/design_fill_sized/`:
per sized link `wall_mm`, `mass_kg`, `governing_case`; `passes`; the authored and resolved identities; the
summary names GATE-FOUNDRY-15.

### Known answers and negative controls
The tube case (floor 1.85 mm and 0.141 kg; stock 2.0 mm and 0.153 kg). The two-pass fixture's answer (2.0,
1.5). Negative controls: the three planted defects; a resolution that reads the stock card in file order
instead of by wall thickness (a shuffled card must give the same result). Metamorphic: adding a thicker
entry to a stock card never changes a resolution that already passed; scaling the robot's non-structural
mass up never thins a wall. Determinism: exact; identical on every machine.

## Considerations

- A sized design's mass is an output of loads. Two tools that disagree on the load factor will disagree on the mass; the provenance on each resolved wall names the load set and factor for that reason.
- Resolution uses the bounded statics set, which is conservative; a design sized this way is rarely the lightest buildable one. That is intended: it is a floor that makes screening honest, not an optimum.
- The interaction with the actuator mass spiral is one-directional here: actuator cards are fixed during resolution. Changing an actuator re-runs resolution, since the weight changed.
- Whether `sized` becomes the template default is decided by measurement, not here; if the corpus shows it rarely changes a winner, the simpler default (declared walls plus the promotion guard) stays.
