## Background

The member-sizing check keeps a screening campaign from rewarding links that could not be built. Whether it
*matters* is an empirical question that the plan states as a HYPOTHESIS: enforcing sized members changes the
winning actuator card or gear ratio in a non-trivial share of campaigns. The mechanism by which it could: a
limb shell that is too thin has less distal inertia; swing torque demand falls; a smaller actuator or a
different gear ratio then looks sufficient; the design that wins is one whose buildable version would need
the larger actuator and might not win at all.

The plan names the test: 10 quadruped and 5 mobile-manipulator campaigns from the design corpus, each run
with declared walls and with sized members, reporting how often the winner or its actuator selection
changes. The outcome decides one thing, by a rule fixed in advance in the gate: if the winner or its actuator
selection changes in fewer than 1 campaign in 15, the check stays as a promotion guard and `{shell: sized}`
is not made the template default. Either way the check itself stays.

This is a method experiment and must have the six parts every such experiment has before a gate may cite it:
a ceiling, competing arms, a potency control with an A/A arm, pairing, a pre-declared fallback and a
reachability pre-analysis.

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; the fifteen campaigns in two arms; the change measure; the controls; the
recorded share; the decision on the template default and its record.
Out: the checks and the fill (N31b-39 to N31b-41); the other clauses of the gate (N31b-43).

## Technical approach

**1. Campaigns** (registered before any run). Fifteen screening campaigns drawn from the design corpus by a
registered seed: ten on quadruped families and five on mobile-manipulator families. A campaign is a candidate
set of designs of one family (DESIGN: 24 candidates) that differ in actuator cards, gear ratios and limb
dimensions, a task family available at M3 (for quadrupeds: velocity tracking and terrain traversal with
push recovery; for mobile manipulators: payload transport and reach), a fixed world-address set, a fixed
budget and the campaign's ordinary ranking rule. Campaigns whose candidates have no shell or lattice limbs
are not eligible and are redrawn.

**2. Arms.** (A) *Declared*: candidates as authored in the corpus, with their declared walls; the structural
label is recorded and does not affect the ranking. (B) *Sized*: every shell limb segment of every candidate
set to `{shell: sized}` and resolved before rollouts; everything else identical. Both arms use the same world
addresses, seeds, controller synthesis rule and budget, so each campaign is a pair.

**3. Measure.** For each campaign: the winner under A and under B (by lineage identity that ignores fills),
and the winner's actuator selection (the actuator card and gear ratio of every joint). A campaign *changes*
when the winner differs, or the winner is the same candidate lineage but its actuator selection differs
(possible where selection is a lever the campaign optimizes per candidate). Reported: the count of changed
campaigns out of 15, split into winner changes and selection-only changes and by robot class; the count of
arm-A winners that were `under-built` under the bounded statics set and under measured loads; the mass and
swing-inertia ratios (sized over declared) of each winner's limbs.

**4. Controls.**
- *Potency*: one extra, planted campaign in which the leading candidate wins only because its shank is
  declared at 0.05 kg where the floor is 0.141 kg (a factor 2.8 in mass and swing inertia), and its sized
  twin needs the next actuator size. Arm B must change the winner. If it does not, the experiment cannot see
  the effect it is about and its result is void.
- *A/A*: one campaign run twice under arm A with the same addresses must give the same winner and selection
  (zero changes); with different session seeds for any stochastic component of the ranking rule, the change
  count over three repetitions is recorded as the experiment's noise floor.
- *Null by construction*: a campaign whose candidates are all already at or above the floor must show no
  change.

**5. Ceiling and reachability, written before the run.** The share is a count out of 15, so its resolution
is 1/15 (6.7%). Zero changes in 15 does not show that the true share is zero: the one-sided 95% upper bound
is 1 − 0.05^(1/15) = 18.1%. One change in 15 gives an interval from 0.17% to 31.9% (95% two-sided, exact binomial). The
experiment can therefore support "the effect exists on this corpus" (at least one change, potency confirmed)
and cannot support a precise rate. The decision rule below is the plan's and is applied as written.

**6. Decision rule (pre-declared fallback).** Fewer than 1 changed campaign in 15 (that is, none): the check
stays as a promotion guard and `{shell: sized}` is not made the template default. One or more: the template
switch `limb_fill_default` is set to `sized` for the archetype templates of the robot classes in which a
change was observed (DESIGN: per class, so that a result on quadrupeds does not change manipulator
templates). In both cases the preflight check and the promotion block remain in force.

**7. Record.** The registration (six parts, hash, ledger sequence before the first campaign); per campaign
both winners, both selections, labels and the mass and inertia ratios; the controls; the share with its
interval; the decision; an architecture decision record `docs/adr/SIZED-TEMPLATE-DEFAULT.md`; the figure in
the gate row's notes for GATE-FOUNDRY-15.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The six parts are registered before the first campaign; the campaign list and address sets are fixed by a registered seed.
- [ ] Fifteen campaigns ran in both arms on identical addresses; the record holds both winners and selections for each.
- [ ] The potency control changed the winner, the A/A control showed zero changes on identical seeds, and the null campaign showed none; otherwise the result is recorded as void and the experiment is repaired and repeated once.
- [ ] The share is recorded as a count out of 15 with its exact binomial interval, split by robot class and by kind of change.
- [ ] The decision follows the pre-declared rule and is recorded in the architecture decision record; the template switch is changed only if the rule says so, and only for the classes concerned.
- [ ] The script exits zero when the measurement is complete, whatever the share.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/sized_members_experiment.rs`: `change_definition` (same lineage and selection
→ no change; same lineage, different gear ratio at one joint → selection change; different lineage → winner
change); `lineage_identity_ignores_fill`; `binomial_bounds` (0 of 15 → one-sided upper bound 0.181; 1 of 15 →
[0.0017, 0.319] at 95% two-sided); `decision_rule_table` (0 → not default;
1 → default for the class observed); `void_when_potency_missed`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sized_members_experiment.sh`: registers the protocol; for each campaign runs arm A and arm B
through the campaign runner (`frob` screening and comparison verbs) on the registered addresses; runs the
three controls; computes the share and applies the rule. Logs per STD-E2E to
`target/e2e-logs/sized_members_experiment/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: per campaign its identity, class, address
digest, `winner_declared`, `winner_sized`, `selection_declared`, `selection_sized`, `changed`, `kind`,
`winner_declared_under_built`, `mass_ratio`, `swing_inertia_ratio`; control results; `changed_campaigns`
(count of 15) with its interval; the decision; the evidence blocks (tiers and determinism classes of the
campaign results, which are provisional fast-tier results and are labelled so); the summary names
GATE-FOUNDRY-15 (its fourth clause).

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the planted campaign's factor 2.8; the binomial bounds above. Negative controls: the potency
campaign (must change); the A/A pair (must not); the null campaign (must not); a harness that compares
design identities including fills (it would count every campaign as changed, and the lineage test must
prevent it). Determinism: with registered seeds and addresses each arm of each campaign reproduces its winner
on the same backend.

## Considerations

- Fifteen campaigns can show that the effect exists; they cannot measure its rate. The record states the interval so that nobody reads 0 of 15 as "never" or 3 of 15 as "20% of the time".
- Winners here are screening winners on a fast tier: provisional results. The experiment is about whether enforcing buildable members changes which design screening prefers, which is a statement about screening, and is reported with that scope.
- The corpus has planted winners and planted defects by design; campaigns are drawn so that no planted structural defect is in the fifteen, and the planted one is the potency control.
- Making `sized` the default changes every template's mass; it is done by new template versions, never by editing existing ones, so earlier results stay reproducible.
