## Background

A **refusal** is the answer a verb gives when it will not compute what was asked: a stable code, a message,
the offending value beside what was required, and ranked repairs (the typed changes that would make the
request admissible, best first). The machine-readable registry is `registries/refusals.toml`, loaded by
`frob-core`; a code with no row does not compile, an unknown code in a serialized refusal is a load-time
error, and codes are never reused. Codes are grouped in families by their first digit (§35.11): `1xx` design
and model, `2xx` numerics and integrator admission, `3xx` scene, `4xx` control and estimation, `5xx` learning
and evaluation protocol, `6xx` backend and device, `7xx` budget and capability, `8xx` evidence and protocol,
and `9xx` internal faults, the one family whose rows exit with code 9.

Foundry (crates `frob-foundry` and `frob-lifecycle`: structure, manufacture, lifecycle) defines fourteen
codes, which the registry bead N31-REF enters. Its text also says, in words, that nine further situations are
refused, and gives none of them a number; a tenth was added by the bead that specifies the packet's
cross-consistency check, and an eleventh by the bead that specifies the firmware exporter's exportable set.
Three are in the late subsections on packet review, on referee re-extraction of load cases and on member sizing
in screening; four belong to the hidden-brief protocol of packet review; one was added by the bead that
specifies the sized fill; one by the bead that specifies the hardware side of firmware export; one by the
packet assembler; one by the firmware exporter's scope check.

Why this is a bead of its own. Without rows these refusals cannot be loaded, tested or documented, and the
drafting units chose stand-ins of three kinds: an existing code of another chapter with a pointer (FROB-REF-105
"a body without a fill" for row 1; FROB-REF-820 "a result that is not `established` is cited as established"
for row 3), a bare name with exit 3 (rows 2, 4, 9 and 10) or exit 5 (row 11, behind the named constant
`FIRMWARE_CONTROLLER_NOT_EXPORTABLE`), and a promise of codes "allocated with the registry owner" (rows 5 to 8). Rejected alternatives: inventing a number in each mechanism bead (drafters collide, and
a collision burns a number for ever); folding each case into an existing code for good (one meaning per code
is the registry's rule, and agents repair by code: "add a fill" is not a repair for a link that no stock wall
can carry). Numbers are no longer typed by a chapter at all: each refusal is a request in the program's
allocation ledger `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (the rule of N35-33, applied to the late requests of
every chapter by N35x-02), and the evidence-and-protocol family is under an owner decision because it is full
(N35x-01). This bead is the Foundry side of that: the requests, the row content, the interims while a number
is held, the verb lists and the binding tests. The work is tier Solid, data entry plus one owner confirmation,
and is due at M3 because rows 1 and 2 are raised by the member-sizing check that GATE-FOUNDRY-15 measures at M3.

The first draft of this bead proposed a continuation block `9xx` for rows 3 to 9 (FROB-REF-901 to -907) and
entered those numbers by default. That proposal is withdrawn: `9xx` is the internal family, whose rows exit
with code 9 (§35.11), so a refusal with exit 3 cannot live there, and the ledger's default under the hold
(N35x-01, step 3) says in words that 901 to 907 are not entered.

## Technical approach

1. **Decision requested** (owner: the keeper of the refusal registry in `frob-core`, with the Foundry lead).

   - *Numbers in the design-and-model family.* FACT (read from the plan's registry and every chapter table):
     in `1xx` the numbers 101 to 116, 118 to 126, 130 to 136 and 160 to 169 are taken (163 to 169 are
     Foundry's); other beads propose 127 and 128; 170 to 199 are free. Rows 1 and 2 are filed with the
     proposals 170 and 171, next to Foundry's own block, and the ledger assigns them (a free proposal is
     honoured: N35x-02). The ADR records the assignment.
   - *Where rows 3 to 10 go.* They are evidence-and-protocol refusals (a label cited beyond its standing, a
     record bound to the wrong artifact, a sealed-set protocol broken, a packet whose parts disagree). FACT: in
     `8xx` every number from 801 to 899 is cited by the plan except 836 to 839, and the surface chapter's beads
     already propose 836 to 838; the family has one free number for eight rows. The ruling belongs to the owner
     decision N35x-01 (which recommends reclassifying requests into their proper families first, then a
     four-digit continuation from 8100 for what remains). This bead files the eight requests in `8xx` with no
     proposed number, and they stay `held` until that ruling; the ledger then numbers them by a re-run. The
     Foundry lead may argue a reclassification for any row in the ADR (row 10, for instance, is about a
     design's packet sections and could be read as `1xx`); the ledger decides.
   - *Row 11.* A capability refusal, not an evidence one: the exporter has no lowering for a construct outside

     what is refused (N35-33 rule 2): `7xx`, decade `72x` (DESIGN: the decade of FROB-REF-724, not implemented

     from the build, which is not the case). It is filed with no proposal (a bead never invents a number), is
     not under the `8xx` hold, and is numbered by the ledger's next run, unless rule 1 merges it (below, under
     Considerations).

   and status, the numbers assigned, the interim in force for each held row, the date, and whether the `8xx`

   numbers in their named constants (N31b-33, N31a-43, N31a-44) and close their open point. Until then the interims are in force and are legal: each
   is a registered code and each refuses.
6. **Verb refusal lists.** The registry entries gain: the verbs that resolve a sized fill (`design.check`,

   assigned code, or the interim code with its `detail`, or `uncoded (open)`. Table 3 of N31-REF lists the
   eleven with the same status, and its
   count of refusals that are neither assigned nor on an interim equals the rows open in their raising beads.
7. **Binding.** The rule of the fourteen: every row has a test that provokes it through a public verb and
   asserts the code (or the interim code and the `detail`), the exit, the block keys and, where a repair
   exists, that the first repair applied makes the same request admissible. A row whose mechanism has not
   landed is `pending: <bead>` and is reported, not failed, until that bead's milestone (rows 1 and 2 at M3;
   rows 3 to 8 and 10 at M5; rows 9 and 11 at M8, and never if KILL-FIRMWARE fires, when they are withdrawn
   with the firmware lane and the record says so).
8. **Layout.** Rows 170 and 171 in `registries/refusals.toml`, which `frob-core` loads, so a crate of any layer
   can raise a registered code; a named constant in the crate that raises each row (the resolver's crate for
   row 1, `frob-arena` for row 2, `frob-foundry` for rows 3 to 11, row 11 under the feature
   `moonshot-firmware-export`); the held rows' interims are served in the
   interim list of `frob --json schema` from the ledger; tests in
   `crates/frob-conformance/tests/refusals_foundry_late.rs`.

## Considerations

- The `8xx` family being full is a fact about the whole registry, not about Foundry; it is decided once for all chapters by N35x-01, and this bead's held rows are numbered by the ledger's re-run after that ruling.
- An interim code shares its number with a refusal of another chapter (the qualification lane's 841, 843, 844 and 883; the trust ladder's 820; the stack receipt's 890). The `detail` value is what separates them for an agent and for the closure test, which is why a held row raised without it is a planted defect.
- Rows 4 and 10 have no fitting existing code; the fail-closed rule refuses them from day one and keeps their raising beads open on that point, which may hold GATE-FOUNDRY-13 (row 4) and GATE-FOUNDRY-10 (row 10) at M5 if the ruling has not come by then. The ADR says so, so that the owner sees what the hold costs.
