## Background

The verb registry of FrankenRobotics will hold about 243 verbs. An agent harness pays for every tool definition
in its context window on every turn, and a model choosing among hundreds of tools chooses badly. A registry
cluster is only a namespace and can be large (in the registry of 243 verbs `scene` has thirteen, `design` twelve
and `analyze` twenty-six). What an agent
sees is therefore a **task cluster**: at most seven tools chosen for one activity ("make and change a design",
"find out why it failed"), declared in the registry beside the verbs. A verb may sit in several task clusters, and
a lint requires every verb to sit in at least one. The agent moves between clusters with one tool,
`session_focus`.

This bead delivers that mechanism for the M1 verbs on the MCP server `frob-mcp` (the server built on the sibling
library `fastmcp_rust`, with one generic handler `VerbTool` per registry entry), together with the four `session`
verbs. It serves the program risk "the agent surface was too large and agents got lost", whose mitigations are
exactly: clusters of at most seven tools, `session.focus`, `next_actions` and the generated guide.

**Focus is about context size, not authority.** Authority is the session's capability token. Hiding a tool does
not protect anything, and a direct call to a hidden tool is refused only because the library refuses calls to
disabled tools, not because the caller lacks a right.

Design decision ADR-TETHER-01 allows exactly one piece of per-verb code in `frob-mcp`: the MCP half of
`session.focus`, which edits the visible tool set of the connection. Everything else is generated.

## Scope

This bead is the switching mechanism and its tests on the served surface. The assignment of verbs to task
clusters is registry data and is not written here.

In: serving the default cluster and the three base tools; `session_focus` on MCP (enable and disable per
connection, the notification, the listing in the result); the four `session` verbs on both surfaces in their M1
form; how a long `run` tool is served before the daemon exists; the tests of the rules of focus on the served
surface, for every task cluster whose verbs are registered at M1 (list contents, list size, list bytes against
the size target, focus is not authority); the re-run of the M1 parity script (`scripts/e2e/tether_parity.sh
--through M1`, N32a-11's) with the session handlers and the cluster mechanism in the build. Its receipt is
recorded for GATE-TETHER-02 with the `record_gate` example of the gate recorder, as BD-137's was; no script
writes a gate row.

Out, and which bead has it:

- The registry form of a task cluster (`[[task_cluster]]` tables and the `task_clusters` field of a verb), the
  assignment of every verb to its clusters (all 45 clusters, those served at M1 included), the `task:<cluster>`
  tags, the lints that keep the assignment valid (at most seven tools; every verb seated; no base tool inside a
  cluster) and the list-budget measurement on the generated definitions: NJL-05, on which this bead depends.
- The fallbacks for clients without session state or without re-listing, and the compatibility experiment:
  N32a-12.
- Authenticated capability tokens and the scope catalogue (N32b-01, M2; BD-258, M3); enforced device budgets
  (N32a-15).
- The M2 forms of `session.open` and `session.show` with scopes, budgets left, namespace and derived sessions:
  N32b-04, which depends on this bead.
- The bounded wait and cancellation semantics in full: N32a-16, N32a-17 (M2).
- The verbs `job.wait` and `job.cancel` behind the base tools `job_wait` and `job_cancel`: their rows are
  VERBS-job's, registered at M2, and their handlers are N29a-23's on the bounded wait of N32a-16 and the device
  protocol of BD-210. This bead builds no form of them (below).
- The same tests for the whole registry on the daemon at M3, and the record of GATE-TETHER-12: BD-298.

## Technical approach

**The principal task clusters** (quoted from the registry, whose assignment NJL-05 owns and whose table governs
if the two ever differ; at M1 a cluster lists only the tools whose verbs are registered).

| Task cluster | Activity | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| `orient` (default) | Find out what exists | `capabilities`, `doctor`, `robot_docs`, `schema`, `design_templates`, `scene_list`, `session_show` |
| `author` | Make and change a design | `design_new`, `design_show`, `design_edit`, `design_diff`, `design_check`, `design_import`, `control_synth` |
| `analyze-body`, `analyze-motion`, `analyze-budget` | Static questions | The static analyses, at most seven per cluster (NJL-05 lists them and adds a fourth cluster, `analyze-evidence`, because twenty-six analyses do not fit three clusters) |
| `simulate` | Estimate, run, look | `sim_estimate`, `sim_run`, `sim_replay`, `run_fork`, `telemetry_summary`, `telemetry_failures`, `telemetry_stills` |
| `diagnose` | Why it failed | `explain_run`, `autopsy_intervene`, `autopsy_attribute`, `autopsy_show`, `telemetry_query`, `telemetry_inspect_pixel`, `telemetry_energy` |
| `compare` | Paired studies | `study_plan`, `study_submit`, `study_status`, `study_compare`, `eval_promote`, `experiment_suggest`, `predict_register` |
| `mission` | Inverse design | `mission_new`, `mission_check`, `mission_synthesize`, `mission_refute`, `mission_distinguish`, `mission_show`, `result_revalidate` |
| `qualify` | The blinded lane and certificates | `eval_qualify`, `eval_scorecard`, `eval_ledger`, `prove_bundle`, `prove_verify`, `report_design`, `prove_list` |
| `operate` | Watch jobs, artifacts, devices, the session | `job_list`, `job_status`, `job_find`, `artifact_list`, `artifact_read`, `artifact_export`, `artifact_import` (the job, artifact, device and session verbs are eleven once the base tools are set aside; `spark_devices` sits in `devices` and `fleet`, `session_show` in `orient`, and `session_open` and `session_close` in the cluster `housekeeping`) |

The clusters `attack`, `learn`, `places`, `search`, `reality` and `make` are declared now and fill at later
milestones. M1 verbs that no principal row lists (`module.*`, `actuator.*`, `algebra.*`, `task.*`, `sense.*`,
`contact.*`, `evidence.trace`, `design.patch`, `design.blame`, `design.view`, `design.export`) have their seats
in further clusters of at most seven. DESIGN: there is one assignment for the whole registry, NJL-05's, because
the lint "every verb sits in at least one task cluster" can only be checked against one table; an earlier
starting assignment in this bead (an `algebra` cluster, `design_blame` in `actuators`, an `operate-2` cluster)
is withdrawn in its favour. In that assignment the M1 verbs sit as follows: `tasks` (seven `task` verbs),
`sensing` (seven `sense` verbs), `contact`, `modules` (`module_list`, `module_show`, `module_check`,
`module_substitute`, `design_patch`, `design_view`, `design_export`), `actuators` (the six `actuator` verbs with
`analyze_envelope`), `symmetry` (`algebra_irreps`, `algebra_orbits`, `analyze_symmetry` and four more tools,
`sense_symmetry`, `control_symmetry`, `phylum_quotient` and `gait_atlas`, each listed once its verb is
registered), `proofs` (which seats `evidence_trace`) and `search` (which seats `design_blame`).

**Base tools.** Three tools are always listed and are not counted: `session_focus`, `job_wait`, `job_cancel`.
They are the registry entries whose `task_clusters` is `["base"]`, and the server reads the set from the
registry. At M1 only `session.focus` is registered; `job.wait` and `job.cancel` enter with VERBS-job at M2 and
are then listed with no change to this bead's code. "Plus the base three" below is therefore an upper bound that
is met with one base tool at M1 and with three from M2.

**Switching, numbered.** `session_focus {cluster}`:
1. Validate `cluster` against the declared names (an unknown name is `FROB-REF-802` with the list of names as the
   repair).
2. Record the focus in the session (the generic half, in `frob-verbs`; the CLI records it too, where it only
   sets the default of `robot-docs --cluster`).
3. MCP half: for each tool of the previous cluster not in the target and not a base tool, call
   `McpContext::disable_tool`; for each tool of the target, `enable_tool`. FACT: the library then omits disabled
   tools from `tools/list`, refuses a direct call to one, and emits its catalog-changed notification.
4. Return an envelope whose result lists the names and one-line purposes of the now-visible tools, for clients
   that do not act on the notification.

A new connection starts focused on `orient`: at start-up every tool outside `orient` and the base three is
disabled for that connection. TARGET: the definitions of the base tools plus one task cluster occupy at most
12 kB of `tools/list`.

**The `session` verbs (cluster `session`; M1 form).**

| Verb | Kind | Input | Result | Refusals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `session.open` | mutate | `scopes` (list), `budget` (map of unit to amount), `namespace` (optional), `idempotency_key` | `session` identifier, `namespace`, the scopes and budgets recorded, the focus (`orient`) | 802, 805, 803 |
| `session.show` | read | none | scopes, budgets left per unit, namespace, focus, visible tools; from M2 also the daemon's overhead line | 802 |
| `session.focus` | mutate, `key_optional` | `cluster`; `idempotency_key` accepted, not required | the now-visible tools with one-line purposes | 802 |
| `session.close` | mutate | `idempotency_key`; no session argument: it closes the caller's own session | the reservations released and the final spend | 802, 805 |

The refusal column names the codes a caller meets most; the registry rows have no chapter refusals of their own
(VERBS-session, the rows of record, on which this bead depends), and the full list is the surface-wide codes of
the kind that the generator appends (NJL-01). No session verb needs a scope: all four rows write `capabilities =
[]` with `public = true`, because reading, focusing or closing one's own session must work for any session, and a
root `session.open` has no prior token to hold a scope (the plan's worked MCP session holds six scopes, none of
them `session:*`, and calls `session_focus` and `session_show`). `session.close` closes the session the call is
made in (on the CLI the session named by the process option `--session` or by `FROB_SESSION`, which carry the
session identifier `s:…` and never a token; over MCP the connection's session) and takes no session identifier
as an input, so it cannot close another caller's session. From M2 the session is the one of the caller's
token, which travels only in `FROB_SESSION_TOKEN` or in the file named by `--session-token-file` (N32b-01). `session.focus` is a `mutate` verb that needs no key (DESIGN, lint `RL-21` of N32a-02): it sets
the caller's focus to the named cluster, so a repeat changes nothing, and the plan's worked session calls
`session_focus {cluster}` with no key; without the exemption the kind rule would refuse every such call with
`FROB-REF-805`. Its appended list therefore omits 805.

At M1 a session is a local record: one principal, a namespace, a focus and declared budgets, of which the
CPU-seconds budget is enforced by the in-process job engine. Authenticated, attenuable tokens and device budgets
replace this record without changing the four schemas. Example (from the worked mission session):
`frob session open --scopes design:write,mission:run,nemesis:run,autopsy:run,sim:run,qual:run --budget
gpu_s=1800,cpu_core_s=7200 --key s-1 --json` returns exit 0 with a session identifier and the namespace
`agent-17`. The worked session as printed in the plan omits `--key`; `session.open` is a `mutate` verb without
`key_optional`, so the call without a key is `FROB-REF-805`, and the example carries one (as VERBS-session's does).

**A long `run` tool at M1 (DESIGN, following the rows of record).** No daemon and no bounded wait exist before
M2 (N32a-14, N32a-16). A `run` tool is therefore served by the in-process route of the dispatch interface
(N32a-04): the call is admitted through the local job ledger (N32a-08, N32a-09), so it has a job record and is
found by its key; it runs to completion in the server process; and it returns the result, with the warning
`executor: in-process`, never a handle (VERBS-job: before the daemon exists a CPU-tier verb never returns a
handle). `sync_wait_ms` is in the generated schema from M0 and is validated, and it does not shorten the call
until N32a-16 lands; `VerbTool::timeout()` returns none for a `run` entry until then, so that the library's
default request timeout of 30 s does not cut an in-process run. A caller that gives up first has a transport
failure: the run is not stopped by a dropped call (ADR-TETHER-05: a dropped connection is never a decision to
stop work), and a second call with the same key returns the stored result with `idempotent_replay: true`. If
the server process dies, the job stays in the ledger and is resumed from its last committed checkpoint by the
next process that opens the ledger. (An earlier form of this bead built in-process forms of `job_wait` and
`job_cancel` at M1. They are withdrawn: the verb appendix and VERBS-job date both verbs at M2, where N29a-23
builds them on N32a-16's wait, and at M1 the cancel path of an in-process job is the running process's own
interrupt, N32a-08. The bounded wait arrives on both surfaces at M2; until then a long call is recovered by its
key, as above.)

Determinism: `tools/list` for a given focus is a pure function of the registry. Evidence: session verbs return
class `Tested`, tier `static`, `claims: []`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The parity diff for the M1 verbs is empty and the served `tools/list` (all clusters listed) equals the registry under the name map.
- [ ] After `session_focus` to any declared cluster, `tools/list` holds at most seven tools plus the base three, and exactly the tools the registry assigns to that cluster.
- [ ] A new connection lists the registered tools of `orient` plus the registered base tools (at M1 `session_focus`) and nothing else.
- [ ] A direct call to a tool outside the focus is refused by the library; after focusing on its cluster the same call succeeds.
- [ ] The result of `session_focus` lists every now-visible tool with a purpose line.
- [ ] For every declared cluster whose verbs are registered, the served `tools/list` result of the base tools plus that cluster, serialized compactly and measured as UTF-8 length, is at most 12,000 bytes (TARGET; the measured maximum is recorded). The same budget on the generated definitions of all 45 clusters is NJL-05's.
- [ ] `session_show` returns identical scopes and budgets before and after each focus change (focus is not authority).
- [ ] The four session verbs have schemas, examples and refusal lists and answer identically on the CLI and over MCP.
- [ ] A `run` tool called at M1 runs to completion in the server process and returns its result with the warning `executor: in-process`; its job record is in the local ledger; a second call with the same key returns the stored result with `idempotent_replay: true` and creates no second job; no tool returns exit 8 in this build, and `job_wait` and `job_cancel` are not listed until their rows are registered.
- [ ] Stage 7 of NJL-01's `scripts/e2e/verbs_registry_schema.sh` passes against this build, which holds the admission transaction of N32a-09: `frob --json session focus mission` with no key succeeds (exit 0), and `frob --json session open --scopes design:read` with no key is `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-mcp focus`, `cargo test -p frob-verbs session`)
- `default_focus_is_orient`: in-memory transport, fresh connection → the names equal the registered tools of the `orient` row plus the registered base tools (on a `synthetic` registry with all seven `orient` rows and the three base rows: 7 + 3).
- `base_set_read_from_registry`: with the M1 registry the base set is {`session_focus`}; on a `synthetic` registry that adds `job.wait` and `job.cancel` with `task_clusters = ["base"]` the same server lists three, with no code change.
- `run_tool_completes_in_process`: a `run` tool on the CPU fixture returns exit 0 or 2 with `executor: in-process`, one job row in the ledger, and never a `job` handle; the same call repeated with its key is a replay.
- `focus_author_lists_exactly_author`: `design_new`, `design_show`, `design_edit`, `design_diff`, `design_check`, `design_import`, `control_synth` plus the base three.
- `focus_result_lists_tools`: the envelope's result has one entry per visible tool.
- `hidden_tool_call_refused`: `mission_synthesize` while focused on `author` → library refusal; after `session_focus {cluster: "mission"}` → accepted.
- `unknown_cluster_802`: `session_focus {cluster: "authr"}` → 802 with the declared names.
- `focus_needs_no_key`: `session_focus {cluster: "author"}` with no `idempotency_key` succeeds on both surfaces; the same call twice gives the same visible list and one focus record; with a key it also succeeds.
- `served_cluster_equals_registry_row`: for every declared cluster whose verbs are registered, the served list after focus equals the registry's `[[task_cluster]]` row plus the base three. (That no cluster exceeds seven tools and that every verb is seated are lints of the registry, NJL-05: `task-cluster-size`, `verb-without-task-cluster`.)
- `list_bytes_at_most_12k`: per cluster, the byte length of the served list, serialized compactly.
- `focus_is_not_authority`: with a synthetic token lacking `mission:run`, focusing on `mission` shows the tools and a call is refused with `FROB-REF-720`, exit 5.
- `session_open_show_close_roundtrip`: budgets declared are the budgets shown; close releases them.
- `session_verbs_need_no_scope`: a session opened with an empty `scopes` list calls `session_show`, `session_focus {cluster: "author"}` and `session_close`; none is refused `FROB-REF-720`.
- `close_is_own_session_only`: `session_close` with a `session` property is `FROB-REF-802` (unknown parameter); two sessions A and B, a close in A leaves B open with its budgets.
- `open_without_key_805`: `session_open` without `idempotency_key` → `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3.
- `two_connections_independent_focus`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/mcp_m1_clusters.sh`: starts the real `frob-mcp` over stdio and over loopback HTTP; lists tools;
focuses on each declared cluster in turn and lists again; runs the M1 studio sequence through tools
(`design_new`, `design_check`, `design_edit`, `design_check`, `control_synth`, `sim_run`, `explain_run`; every
`mutate` and `run` call with an `idempotency_key` written by `e2e_key`) with
focus changes between `author`, `simulate` and `diagnose`; calls `session_show` before the first and after the
last focus change and asserts that scopes and budgets are unchanged; with `mission` focused calls `design_check`
and asserts the library's disabled-tool error, then focuses `author` and asserts that the same call succeeds (or
answers `FROB-REF-724` for a verb registered as declared only); runs one `mission_synthesize` to completion
(warning `executor: in-process`, no handle) and repeats the call with the same key, asserting
`idempotent_replay: true` and one job record. It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/mcp_m1_clusters/<utc>.ndjson` plus a wire transcript: `cmd` lines per tool call with exit code
and wall time, the envelope `evidence` block, `metric` lines for tools listed per focus (threshold 7 + 3), list
bytes per cluster (threshold 12,000), `assert` lines for each list against the registry row; every line with
`ts`, `script`, `bead` (`BD-138`), `step`, `event` and the transport; on failure the first tool name present on
one side only (served list against registry row) and the path of the saved wire transcript; a `summary` naming
GATE-TETHER-02 and the focus clause of GATE-TETHER-12.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the `orient` and `author` rows above; the base set of three (one registered at M1, three from M2).
- Negative controls: a server build that skips `disable_tool` on focus (the list test must find more than ten tools); a server patched to list eight tools after a focus; a server patched to drop a scope on focus (the `session_show` comparison must fail). The planted registries (eight tools in one cluster; a verb in no cluster) are controls of the registry lints, NJL-05.
- Metamorphic relation: focusing clusters in any order leaves `session_show` and every later tool result unchanged (focus is not state that enters a result).
- Determinism lanes: list bytes per focus identical twice on one machine, on both transports, in debug and release.

## Considerations

- OPEN-TETHER-2 (do the agent harnesses in use speak one of the two protocol eras the library serves, and act on catalog-changed notifications; owner: Tether lead; blocks GATE-TETHER-12) is open. Fail-closed default: static clusters by launch flag plus the generic tool `verb_invoke`. Those fallbacks and the experiment are a separate bead; this bead's `session_focus` result already lists the visible tools for clients that ignore the notification.
- FACT: when a connection has no session state, `disable_tool` reports failure; the handler must surface that as a warning naming the fallback and must not pretend the list changed.
- The plan dates the session verbs at M0 in its verb appendix and builds them here at M1; they were registered as declared at M0.
- The assignment of unlisted verbs to further clusters is a starting assignment, kept in the registry by NJL-05; the fresh-agent gates decide whether it helps, and a regrouping there needs no change here, because this bead's tests read the cluster rows from the registry.
