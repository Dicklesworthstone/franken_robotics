## Background

Three properties of the FrankenRobotics surface exist so that an agent can trust it under failure and at scale,
and all three are due at milestone M2, when the daemon `frobd` and the GPU backends arrive:

1. **Idempotency.** One job and one budget charge per (principal, verb, idempotency key), under any interleaving
   and any crash point (INV-TETHER-05). Without it, a dropped connection plus a retry runs and charges work twice.
2. **Pagination pinned to a committed frontier.** The pages of one cursor sequence, concatenated, equal the
   query's rows at the pinned frontier: none skipped, none repeated (INV-TETHER-06), even while other worlds are
   still publishing.
3. **Integers above 2⁵³ as strings.** No integer travels as a JSON number unless its schema type is `int53` and
   its value is within ±(2⁵³ − 1) (INV-TETHER-07), because clients that decode numbers as doubles change larger
   values silently: 2⁵³ + 1 = 9,007,199,254,740,993 becomes 9,007,199,254,740,992.

Each mechanism was built in its in-process form at M1 (the admission transaction, the cursor, the number types).
This bead wires them into the daemon for every verb of the M2 surface, with real concurrency between client
processes and real device workers publishing while queries page, and closes the three gates there. It is the
seed bead whose done-condition is "GATE-TETHER-05; GATE-TETHER-06; GATE-TETHER-07".

## Scope

In: the admission transaction on the daemon's request path for every `mutate` and job-submitting `run` verb;
`design.patch` under key and expected parent; `job.find`; binding retention; cursors on every paginated verb of
the M2 surface with pins honoured by collection; the number types on every M2 schema, including job, cursor,
chunk and address fields; the fault hooks, race harness and campaigns; the three gate records. Out: the
mechanisms' internals and in-process proofs (N32a-09, N32a-10, N32a-07); the chunk-publishing transaction
(BD-091; its device workers, BD-210); budgets (N32a-15); tokens and namespaces (BD-258).

## Technical approach

**1. Idempotency on the daemon.** The daemon's dispatch pipeline runs, for a keyed verb, the four-step protocol:
(1) resolve names to immutable identifiers, fill defaults, canonically serialize (sorted keys, no insignificant
whitespace, NFC strings, numbers by the integer rules) and take the BLAKE3 digest; (2) begin an immediate
transaction and look up (principal, verb, key); (3) found with the same digest → return the stored receipt with
`idempotent_replay: true`; found with a different digest → refuse `FROB-REF-803`, exit 7, showing both
resolutions; not found → insert the binding, debit the reservation and insert the job row in state `admitted`,
all in this transaction; (4) commit, and only then enqueue and reply. Omitting the key is `FROB-REF-805`. On
restart the daemon enqueues every `admitted` job with no start record. Keys are scoped to (principal, verb) and
kept as long as the job record; for `mutate` verbs that create no job the retention is 24 h (TARGET).
- *Wiring.* A registry-driven guard: any entry with `needs_key()` goes through the transaction; there is no
  per-verb opt-in, so a new verb cannot forget it. `design.patch` passes `expected_parent`, checked against the
  lineage head inside the same transaction (`FROB-REF-804`, exit 7, when stale).
- *Fault hooks.* The daemon is built for tests with named crash points: before begin, after lookup, after insert,
  after commit and before enqueue, after enqueue and before reply. A test selects a point by environment variable
  and the daemon aborts there (a real process death, not a simulated error).
- *Race harness.* `frob-conformance::races` launches two real client processes (CLI and MCP in every pairing)
  released by a barrier, each submitting under the same key.

**2. Pagination on the daemon.** A first page fixes the query digest (run or object, filters, ordering), the
committed frontier of the store at that moment, the total-order key of the last row returned and the schema
version, in an opaque authenticated cursor (`frob.cursor/1`, tagged with the daemon key). Later pages read only
rows at or below the frontier and strictly after the key. The telemetry order key is (instant, world address,
channel, sample index). A cursor used with changed filters is `FROB-REF-806`; a cursor whose frontier has been
collected is `FROB-REF-807`; `follow` continues into rows committed later and states the new frontier.
- *Wiring.* Every registry entry with `paginated = true` (at M2: `telemetry.query`, `telemetry.histogram` pages,
  `mission.show`, `job.list`, `run.list`, `artifact.list`, `scene.list`, the catalogs) uses the one cursor
  implementation; a lint forbids a paginated verb with its own cursor type. Pins are registered in the ledger so
  that the collector (bounded, with tombstones) skips pinned frontiers.
- *Campaign.* Device workers (and the fake device under the lab runtime) publish chunks from worlds running at
  different speeds while a reader pages to the end; the reader's concatenation is compared with a snapshot read
  at the same frontier after the run.

**3. Integers on the daemon's surface.** Every schema served at M2 uses only `int53` (a JSON number within
±(2⁵³ − 1); an input outside it is `FROB-REF-808`) or `u64str` / `i64str` (a decimal string matching
`^(0|[1-9][0-9]{0,19})$`, with an optional leading minus for the signed type). New at M2 and typed here: job
admission sequence, commit sequence and frontier (`u64str`), chunk index and lease generation (`u64str`), world
addresses, seeds and stream counters (`u64str`), tick indices and nanosecond instants (`u64str`), trial and pair
counts and progress (`int53`). The golden envelope set is extended with one envelope per M2 verb and boundary
values on each of those fields.

Determinism: the campaigns are seed-explicit; the outcome sets are schedule-independent. Evidence class of the
three gates: Tested (known answer).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TETHER-05 (TARGET): 1,000 races of two identical submissions and 1,000 races with a changed payload each leave exactly one job and one charge; a crash injected at every commit point leaves one job, one charge and a replayed receipt. Negative control: the two-statement variant fails.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-06 (TARGET): over 10⁴ seeded schedules of concurrent appends, concatenated pages equal the frontier snapshot, including rows with equal timestamps; a changed filter with an old cursor is refused.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-07 (TARGET), re-read on the M2 surface: the golden envelope set passes through a double-decoding client with no changed value; the schema lint finds no bare 64-bit integer.
- [ ] Every keyed verb of the M2 registry is refused without a key (`FROB-REF-805`) and replays with one; no verb bypasses the guard (a registry-driven test enumerates them). The `key_optional` entries (`study.plan`, `session.focus`) are the one exception: without a key they are admitted and bind nothing.
- [ ] `frob job find --verb <verb> --key <k>` (a key is scoped to principal and verb; the job verbs are N29a-23's) returns the job after the daemon was killed between commit and reply, in 100 of 100 seeded trials.
- [ ] `design.patch` retried with the same key returns the original version; with a stale `expected_parent` it is refused with `FROB-REF-804`.
- [ ] Collection never removes rows under a live cursor pin; an expired pin yields `FROB-REF-807` and never a silent gap.
- [ ] Each gate is recorded from the script's receipt with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate <id> --receipt <hash>` (the script writes no gate row itself); the record carries measured counts (races, schedules, envelopes, crash points) and names the commit, the hardware fingerprint and the seeds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-cli -- daemon_idem daemon_cursor`, `cargo test -p frob-conformance races`)
- `every_keyed_verb_guarded`: for each registry entry with `needs_key()` and without `key_optional`, a call without a key → 805; each `key_optional` entry without a key is admitted.
- `two_process_race_identical`: 1,000 seeds; assertions: one job row, one `reserve` budget event, both clients hold the same job identifier.
- `two_process_race_changed_payload`: 1,000 seeds; one job, one charge, the other client holds 803 with both resolutions.
- `crash_points_all`: for each of the five named crash points, kill, restart, retry: one job, one charge, a replayed receipt (or, for the points before commit, a fresh admission and still exactly one job).
- `two_statement_variant_fails`: the test-only daemon build must produce two jobs in at least one of the 1,000 races.
- `patch_replay_and_stale_parent`.
- `paging_under_device_appends`: 10⁴ seeded schedules on the fake device; 100 on `batch-cpu`; a sample on each device family.
- `equal_timestamps_across_worlds`: two worlds with a sample at the same instant straddling a page boundary.
- `changed_filter_806`, `collected_frontier_807`, `pin_blocks_collection`.
- `offset_and_time_only_variants_fail`: the daemon built with N32a-10's test-only offset paginator returns {2, 3} as page two of the KA-764 fixture (row 2 repeated) and fails `paging_under_device_appends` on at least one schedule; built with the time-only cursor it skips the second sample at t = 2 in `equal_timestamps_across_worlds`. Each failure is reported with its seed.
- `m2_golden_set_survives_double_client`; `m2_schema_lint_zero`.
- `bare_u64_schema_caught`: a planted M2 schema that declares the commit sequence as a bare 64-bit integer makes the lint report exactly that field, and an envelope carrying 9,007,199,254,740,993 in it comes back from the double-decoding client as 9,007,199,254,740,992 (one changed value reported).
- `boundary_values_roundtrip`: frontier, chunk index, seed and world address at 2⁵³ − 1, 2⁵³, 2⁵³ + 1, 2⁶⁴ − 1.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tether_gates_05_06_07.sh` (seed-explicit, its own scratch state root): starts `frobd`; runs the race
campaign through real `frob` and `frob-mcp` clients; runs the crash-point campaign; runs a multi-world fixture
job and pages `frob telemetry query … --limit 200 --json` during and after it; reruns N32a-10's
`scripts/e2e/pagination_frontier_cpu.sh` with the verb parameter set to `telemetry.query` and then `job.list`
(that script pages a test-lane verb until the real paginated verbs exist; this is its run on them); runs the
double-decoding client over every M2 verb's example output. It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/tether_gates_05_06_07/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`BD-211`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block;
`metric` lines give races run (threshold 1,000 + 1,000), jobs per race (threshold exactly 1), charges per race
(exactly 1), crash points covered (5 of 5), schedules run (threshold 10⁴), duplicate and skipped rows (0),
envelopes checked, changed values (0), lint findings (0); on failure the seed, the schedule and the first
differing value or key; the final `summary` names the three gates with their measured values.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-763 (exact): requests A and B with the same key and payload, interleaved, leave one job and one charge; the retry returns the stored receipt with `idempotent_replay: true`; the same key with a different digest is refused (803, exit 7).
- KA-764 (exact): rows at t = 1, 2, 3, 4, page size 2; a row at t = 1.5 commits after page one; page two returns {3, 4}; nothing repeated or skipped; two samples at t = 2 from two worlds are both returned once.
- KA-756 (exact): 9,007,199,254,740,993 is refused as `int53` (808) and preserved exactly as `u64str`; as a bare number it decodes to 9,007,199,254,740,992.
- Negative controls (registered; each must be caught): CX-345, look up then insert in two statements (two jobs, two charges); CX-346, offset pagination (page two {2, 3}); CX-347, a time-only cursor (skips the second sample at t = 2); CX-348, a 64-bit integer sent as a number. Their control rows in `controls.toml` (one per catalogue entry, `seed` = the CX number, with the gates of the entry) are seeded by the tool of N38b-05 from N32-CX's entries, which also allocates their `NC-` numbers; no mechanism bead writes or allocates a row. The plants (`idem-two-statement`, `cursor-offset`, `cursor-time-only`, `schema-bare-int64`) were built and first caught at M1 by N32a-09, N32a-10 and N32a-07. This bead binds its daemon-level tests (`two_statement_variant_fails`, `offset_and_time_only_variants_fail`, `bare_u64_schema_caught`) to those same controls by the name prefix `nc_<nnnn>_` of each, and adds no row.
- Determinism lanes: campaigns twice on one machine with the same seeds give the same outcome sets; across worker counts; debug and release; on the Linux host and on the Mac.

## Considerations

- The M2 exit lists these three gates under "Tether and safety"; the gate register dates GATE-TETHER-07 at M1, where it was first recorded on the M1 surface; this bead's record is its re-read on the larger surface.
- Real two-process races are slower than simulated ones; 2,000 races are a few minutes on the CPU and run in the gate lane, with the 630 enumerated interleavings in the per-commit lane.
- The crash-point hooks exist only in test builds; a lint asserts that no release build contains them.
- When capability tokens arrive, the principal in the binding changes from the single local principal to the token's principal; bindings made before are scoped to the local principal and are not migrated.
