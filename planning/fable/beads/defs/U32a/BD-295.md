## Background

At M2 the daemon `frobd` of FrankenRobotics gives every job its own environment on a device. That wastes almost
the whole device on the work agents do most. The plan's arithmetic (DESIGN arithmetic on the reference batch of
B = 8,192 worlds; no measurement):

| Work | Slots | Share of the batch |
|---|---|---|
| A mission cell probe of 16 worlds | 16 | 0.2% |
| A fixed-n study of 30 pairs | 60 | 0.7% |
| An anytime-valid comparison at its cap of 96 pairs | 192 | 2.3% |
| A 2,048-world `sim.run` | 2,048 | 25% |

Decision ADR-TETHER-07: the daemon dispatches **episodes into slots of a persistent environment** per device and
shape class, not one environment per job. Reason: one paired comparison fills about 2.3% of a batch; without
sharing, the rest of the device idles. The unit of work becomes a world, not a job. Alternative kept as the
fallback: one environment per job, queued (`frobd --dispatch=per-job`).

Further arithmetic that sizes the gain. With 20 s episodes at a 2 ms step, one batch-episode is 8,192 × 10,000 =
81.92 M world-steps. Taking a round R = 2 × 10⁶ world-steps per second (TARGET, the order of the performance
targets, not measured) it lasts 40.96 s of device time, of which the 96-pair comparison is attributable
192 × 10,000 ÷ R = 0.96 s. With 5% of slots (410) held in reserve, 40 such comparisons fit in one batch-episode
with 102 slots to spare. **Ceiling, stated before any speed test:** the gain over one environment per job is at
most min(k, 1 ÷ fill) for k concurrent small jobs, so at most 42-fold for 192-slot jobs and nothing at all for a
single agent running one job at a time, who gains only the removal of environment creation from each call.
Against a right-sized environment per job the ceiling is the measured ratio of step time at 8,192 worlds to step
time at the job's own size, which is unknown until the M2 profile.

The correctness condition is INV-TETHER-08: a world's digest and results depend on its address and identities,
never on its slot, its co-tenants, dispatch order, preemption or resumption. Tier: Solid within a shape class;
across model variants it relies on the model-family machinery (variants inside padded capacities).

## Scope

In: the dispatcher's queue per shape class; the pure selection function `assign`, which decides at each reset
boundary which admitted episodes take the freed slots and in what order; the two decisions of budgeted mode
(an episode waits because of the job cap, or because only reserve slots are free for its class), with the one
configuration record that holds the cap and the reserve; environment lifecycle; result routing by world
address; the three dispatch modes and the shadow rollout; the measurements of GATE-TETHER-09.

Out, with the bead that has it (the first two are dependencies of this bead; the daemon calls them in this
order at every boundary: `assign` here, then `plan_resets`, then `refill`):
- The slot table of an environment (model slots and world lanes, with their states), the choice of the lane
  each chosen episode is written into (the pure function `plan_resets`, which keeps worlds of one model
  contiguous in runs), admission of a model table into a free model slot, and the table's own check of the cap
  and the reserve: N17a-09 (frob-phylum, M2). A lane of that table and a slot of this bead are the same index.
- The persistent environment object on the device, its `refill` call that validates and writes the world rows
  at the masked reset under a host-write lease, its hard limits on jobs and reserve slots, the refusal
  `FROB-REF-611` for a shape change, the per-job mode at environment level and the kernels' row isolation:
  N21b-21 (frob-kernels, M3).
- Size classes, ordering policy, preemption, and the admission guard that refuses a new submission with
  `FROB-REF-703` when utilization or the per-environment job cap is reached (BD-296, N32a-21); attributable
  charging (N32a-15); the kill decision (N32a-23).

## Technical approach

**Structure.** The daemon keeps one persistent environment per device and **shape class** (the size bucket of
the model-family subsystem: a capacity for bodies, joints, contacts). Model variant, scene, task and job are
per-world buffers. When a slot's episode ends, the dispatcher resets that slot with the next admitted episode
from any job of the same shape class. Results are keyed by world address, never by slot or arrival order. No
shape change enters a running environment, because the static launch graph of the device layer forbids it; a job
of another shape class waits for, or opens, another environment.

**Dispatch as a pure function (DESIGN).**

```rust
// Intent, not signatures. Called at every reset boundary of an environment.
pub fn assign(freed: &[SlotId], queue: &OrderedEpisodes, cfg: &SlotConfig, occ: &Occupancy) -> Vec<(SlotId, WorldAddress)>;
```

1. `freed` is the ascending list of slots whose episode ended at this boundary (from the masked-reset mask).
2. `queue` is the admitted episodes of this shape class in the scheduler's order.
3. Skip an episode whose job is not yet in the environment when 64 jobs already are (cap 64, TARGET); it stays
   queued and keeps its place.
4. Batch-class episodes may take free slots only down to the reserve (5% of slots, TARGET); interactive-class
   episodes may take reserve slots.
5. Hand the eligible episodes, in queue order and no more of them than there are freed slots they may take, to
   the reset planner of the slot table, `plan_resets(freed, assignments)` (N17a-09), as an ordered list of
   assignments (model variant index, scene variant, task, job, world address). The planner chooses the lane of
   each: the lowest-indexed freed lane in a run of the episode's model variant, so that worlds of one model
   stay contiguous in runs. `assign` chooses no lane itself; lane choice has this one owner, and both
   functions are pure, so their composition is. In an environment that holds one model variant every freed
   lane is eligible, and the result is then the first eligible episode in the lowest freed slot, the second in
   the next, and so on. The pairs `assign` returns are the planner's. The world rows (model variant index,
   scene variant, task, job, world address; seeds follow from the address) are written by the environment's
   `refill` at the masked reset (N21b-21), one row per assignment.
6. Record the assignment (boundary index, slot, address) in the operational log. The record is for replay of the
   dispatcher; nothing downstream reads the slot.

Because every kernel reads and writes only its own world's rows (the device layer's invariant) and random draws
depend on the address alone, the slot chosen cannot affect a world's trajectory.

**Proof obligation PO-TETHER-03: slot dispatch preserves digests.** Argument: dispatch is a pure function of
queue state; no kernel reads another world's rows; so a world's digest is a function of its address and
identities alone. Verification: the golden co-tenancy test of this gate, on top of the kernel-level
batch-composition test (GATE-SPARK-15), which is a precondition.

**Environment lifecycle.** Open on first demand for a (device, shape class) after a memory preflight; keep
resident between jobs (this is what removes environment creation from each call); close the least recently used
idle environment when the preflight for a new one fails (DESIGN); never resize in place.

**Budgeted mode.** At most 64 jobs per environment; 5% of slots reserved for the interactive class; beyond that,
queue (TARGET values, recalibrated from the M2 queue trace). Three layers hold these two limits, with one
source of values and one place of decision (DESIGN; the plan states the limits in the Phylum, device and daemon
chapters and gives the scheduling policy to the daemon):
- *Values.* The cap and the reserve fraction are fields of the daemon's `SlotConfig` (in the daemon's
  configuration digest). The daemon passes them to the slot table and to the environment when it opens them;
  neither keeps a value of its own that could differ. 5% of 8,192 slots is 410.
- *Decision, here.* `assign` decides which episode waits (steps 3 and 4). It never refuses: an admitted job
  beyond the cap stays in the queue and keeps its place. Whether a new submission is accepted into the queue
  at all while utilization or the cap is reached is decided earlier, by the admission guard (`FROB-REF-703`,
  `retry: backoff`; BD-296).
- *Checks below.* The slot table treats the two limits as invariants (`plan_resets` returns `LimitReached` and
  writes nothing for an assignment that would break one; N17a-09), and the environment enforces them as hard
  limits on `refill` (N21b-21). Because `assign` skips such episodes first, neither result is seen in normal
  operation; they exist so that a defect in the dispatcher cannot exceed a limit.
- *Names.* The "short jobs" for which the Phylum and device chapters reserve slots are the jobs of size class
  `interactive` (a point estimate of at most 30 s of device time; BD-296); every other job is batch class.

**Modes and rollout.** `frobd --dispatch=per-job` (the M2 behaviour and the rollback), `--dispatch=shadow`
(first on `batch-cpu`: run each job both ways and compare per-world digests; a difference raises an alarm and
keeps per-job results), `--dispatch=shared` (the target). Rollout order: shadow on `batch-cpu`, shadow on each
device family, shared.

**Measurements for the gate.**
- *Slot invariance:* 1,000 episodes are run solo and in 100 random co-tenancies (random other jobs, random slot
  permutations, random arrival orders, seeds recorded); per-world digests must be identical. The gate register
  (N40-08's `[[shared_fixture]]` record, plan §40.3) puts the digest part of GATE-TETHER-09 in the
  batch-independence group with GATE-KIN-05, GATE-PHYLUM-02, GATE-SPARK-15, GATE-CHRON-01 and GATE-AUTOPSY-01:
  the fixture is one (BD-181's frozen world set and seeded co-tenancies), it is run once per milestone, and the
  digest part cites that run's receipt for the device family, the same hash the other five cite, so that N40-08's
  `one_run` check holds at the exit. The daemon campaign of this bead's script (below) is the dispatcher's own
  check: it verifies PO-TETHER-03 through `frobd` and any digest difference in it trips KILL-SLOT, but it is not a
  second run of the shared fixture and is not cited for the digest part. The dispatch limits below govern the
  gate (§40.3: "the dispatch limits govern; digest equality is the precondition also tested by GATE-SPARK-15").
- *Throughput:* at 8,192 identical worlds, world-steps per second under shared dispatch against a dedicated
  environment, as a paired measurement on the same device and thermal state; loss at most 5%.
- *Step time:* 99th-percentile step time under sharing at most 1.10 of solo.
- *Admission to first step:* from the admission commit to the first executed step of the job's first episode,
  for jobs of up to 256 worlds: 0.1 s, 0.5 s and 2 s at the 50th, 95th and 99th percentiles.

Determinism: digests under the device family's bitwise class are the object of the test. Performance figures are
Performance claims with a disclosure block (hardware, commit, configuration digest).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TETHER-09 (TARGET): 1,000 episodes run solo and in 100 random co-tenancies have identical digests; at 8,192 identical worlds shared dispatch loses at most 5% throughput against a dedicated environment and 99th-percentile step time is at most 1.10 of solo; admission to first step for jobs of up to 256 worlds is at most 0.1, 0.5 and 2 s at the 50th, 95th and 99th percentiles. Measured values are recorded per device family by a session that did not implement the work, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-TETHER-09 --receipt <hash>` citing receipts `frob.gauntlet.receipt/1` (named by the BLAKE3 hash of their bytes, N38a-02, N38b-19): for the digest part the receipt of the milestone's one run of the batch-independence shared fixture (N40-08), for the dispatch limits the receipt of this bead's measurement. The e2e log's summary digest (`body_sha256`, a SHA-256 of the log) is not a receipt and the recorder refuses it.
- [ ] The record that decides KILL-SLOT (this gate alone) is complete and handed to the decision bead, which closes the seed's condition "KILL-SLOT decided".
- [ ] Shadow mode on `batch-cpu` shows zero digest differences over the M3 battery before `shared` is enabled on any device.
- [ ] `assign` is pure: replaying the operational log reproduces every assignment.
- [ ] A job of another shape class never enters a running environment; it waits or a second environment is opened after the preflight.
- [ ] The reserve and the cap hold: batch episodes never occupy reserve slots; a 65th job queues.
- [ ] Results are retrievable by world address and carry no slot identifier.
- [ ] `frobd --dispatch=per-job` restores the M2 behaviour with identical digests for the same jobs (the rollback is demonstrated).
- [ ] The pilot for slot dispatch (N21b-20, a dependency: a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu` with and without sharing, with its fill-fraction ceiling and falsifier) is on record with a verdict that funds this work before this bead is started, and is cited.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs slots`, `cargo test -p frob-cli dispatcher`)
- `assign_lowest_slot_first`: one model variant; freed {3, 7, 9}, queue [a, b] → (3, a), (7, b).
- `assign_delegates_lane_choice`: lanes 0 to 7 are a run of variant A and lanes 8 to 15 a run of variant B;
  freed {3, 7, 9}, queue [b₁ of variant B, a₁ of variant A] → (9, b₁), (3, a₁): the pairs are those of
  `plan_resets` (N17a-09) for the same list, and `assign` contains no lane rule of its own.
- `assign_respects_reserve`: 8,192 slots, 410 reserved, 7,782 occupied by batch work → a batch episode is not assigned; an interactive one is.
- `assign_respects_job_cap`: 64 jobs present → the 65th job's episodes are skipped, stay queued in place, and
  no refusal is produced; `plan_resets` is never called with them (its `LimitReached` result is not observed).
- `limits_have_one_source`: for an environment opened under a `SlotConfig` of (8 jobs, 10%) instead of the
  defaults (64, 5%), the cap and reserve that the slot table and the environment report are (8, 10%).
- `forty_comparisons_fit`: 40 jobs of 192 slots occupy 7,680; 102 slots remain outside the 410-slot reserve.
- `no_slot_in_results`: the result schema of an episode has no slot field (schema test).
- `shape_class_isolation`: an episode of class C2 is never assigned to an environment of class C1.
- `replay_assignments`: 10⁴ boundaries replayed from the log.
- `lifecycle_lru_eviction`: with memory for two environments and a third class arriving, the least recently used idle one closes.
- `shadow_alarm_on_planted_difference`: a kernel build that leaks a neighbour's row (test-only) makes shadow mode raise the alarm.
- `ka_761_slot_share_arithmetic` (the mechanism bead's claim of KA-761, N32-KA): 192 ÷ 8,192 = 3 ÷ 128 =
  0.0234375, computed from the dispatcher's own occupancy count of a 96-pair job.
- `po_tether_03_assign_pure_and_slot_blind` (property test, `testkit::prop`, 256 cases over random freed sets,
  queues of up to three model variants and `SlotConfig` values): `assign` called twice on the same inputs returns
  the same pairs; every returned address is an admitted episode of the queue, at most one per freed slot, and
  none of a job beyond the cap or of the batch class on a reserve slot.
- `inv_tether_08_digest_slot_independent` (`batch-cpu`, a 16-world `synthetic` fixture, 32 seeded cases): each
  case places the 16 worlds in random slots of a persistent environment beside random co-tenant worlds and a
  random arrival order; every per-address digest equals the world's solo digest.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/slot_dispatch.sh --lane batch-cpu|cuda|metal --seed <s>` (header: `bead=BD-295 milestone=M3
labels=tether,spark,dispatch gates=GATE-TETHER-09,KILL-SLOT seed=295 timeout_s=3600 needs=cpu,frobd` plus `cuda`
or `metal` for those lanes, `plants=slot-keyed-draw,reserve-leak,arrival-keyed-results`; the campaign is about
10⁹ world-steps, beyond the default 600 s): grants four principals with `e2e_session grant` before it starts
`frobd` and opens one session for each with `e2e_session open` once the daemon answers (from M2 a call without a
token is `FROB-REF-723`); starts `frobd --dispatch=shadow`, then `shared`; submits the co-tenancy campaign through real `frob` clients from the four sessions (1,000 reference
episodes, 100 random co-tenancies), each submission `frob sim run <design> --scene <scene> --worlds <n> --seconds
20 --tier <tier> --no-memo --key "$(e2e_key <label>)" --json`, where `<tier>` is the lane's `sim.run` tier (`batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda` or
`gpu-metal`; `cuda` and `metal` are lane names, and as `--tier` values they are 802) (world seeds follow from the world addresses; the script's seed drives only
the choice of co-tenants, slot permutations and arrival orders, and is logged on every line); runs the 8,192-world throughput pair; runs 500 small jobs of up to 256
worlds for the admission latency distribution; switches to `per-job` and repeats a sample. Keys: every
submission carries its own key (labels `slot-solo-<i>`, `slot-co-<t>-<i>`, `slot-perjob-<i>`); the solo and co-tenant
runs of one reference episode are two submissions under distinct keys, because a repeated key would return the
first run's stored receipt and compare it with itself. Every submission also carries `--no-memo` (`sim.run` sets
`memo = true`, NJL-01 variation 14), and each envelope's memo disposition is asserted `NoMemo`: the co-tenant run of
a reference episode has the solo run's request content, so without it the memo graph would serve the solo result,
the digests would agree whatever the dispatcher did, and the plant `slot-keyed-draw` could not be caught. Plants: `slot-keyed-draw` (a test-only kernel build that
keys a draw by slot) must make the digest-difference metric non-zero; `reserve-leak` (a dispatcher
configuration that lets batch episodes take reserve slots) must fail the reserve assertion; `arrival-keyed-results`
(a result store keyed by arrival order) must fail the per-address lookup. It sources the shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/slot_dispatch/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript. Every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead` (`BD-295`), `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block;
`metric` lines: digest differences (threshold 0 of 100,000 comparisons), throughput ratio shared ÷ dedicated
(threshold ≥ 0.95), p99 step-time ratio (≤ 1.10), admission-to-first-step p50, p95, p99 (0.1, 0.5, 2 s), slot
fill fraction, idle slot-seconds, with units and the disclosure block; digests logged where compared; on failure
the first differing world address, both digests and the co-tenancy seed. The `summary` names GATE-TETHER-09 and
KILL-SLOT.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-761 (exact): the slot share of a 96-pair comparison in 8,192 worlds is 192 ÷ 8,192 = 2.34%.
- Known arithmetic: 81.92 M world-steps per batch-episode; 40.96 s at R = 2 × 10⁶; 0.96 s attributable; 40 comparisons with 102 slots to spare; ceiling min(k, 1 ÷ fill) ≤ 42.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a draw keyed by slot instead of address (digests differ under permutation); a kernel that reads a neighbouring row; a result store keyed by arrival order (two jobs' results swap under reordering); a dispatcher that lets a batch job take reserve slots (the latency distribution degrades and the reserve test fails).
- Metamorphic relations: permuting slots, changing co-tenants and changing arrival order leave every per-world digest unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: the co-tenancy campaign twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release on `batch-cpu`; on each device family against its class.

## Considerations

- KILL-SLOT. Trigger: any digest difference under co-tenancy, or this gate's throughput or step-time limits fail after one optimization pass. Fallback: `--dispatch=per-job`, queued. The surface chapter's statement governs where the device chapter's differs (its limits contain the device chapter's and add the 5% throughput limit and the optimization pass); GATE-SPARK-15 is a precondition, not a second trigger.
- A single agent running one job at a time gains nothing but the removal of environment creation; the benefit is for swarms, and the gate does not claim otherwise.
- The throughput figure R is a target; if the M2 profile shows a right-sized environment steps much faster per world than a full one, the ceiling against "right-sized per job" may be below 1 for some sizes, and those sizes then stay on per-job dispatch by configuration.
- Sharing interacts with attribution (each job pays for its occupied slots) and with the dashboard's slot strip; both read the same occupancy record.
- A paired comparison's two arms may land in different boundaries; the study engine consumes outcomes in address order, so completion order is irrelevant.
