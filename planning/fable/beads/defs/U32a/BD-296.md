## Background

Once many agents share the devices of FrankenRobotics through the daemon `frobd`, the order in which work runs
decides how long each agent's turn blocks. Agents mostly ask short questions and sometimes start long ones. The
plan's queueing arithmetic (an M/G/1 model with Poisson arrivals; a model, not a measurement) shows why the
obvious remedy fails. One device, 90% of jobs 10 s and 10% of jobs 30 min (E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s²):

| Utilization ρ | First-come wait | Short-class wait, priority only | Short-class wait, preempt-resume | Slowdown of long jobs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 857 s | 439 s | 0.12 s + quantum | 2.4% |
| 0.7 | 2,001 s | 621 s | 0.17 s + quantum | 3.4% |
| 0.8 | 3,430 s | 713 s | 0.20 s + quantum | 4.0% |
| 0.9 | 7,716 s | 806 s | 0.22 s + quantum | 4.5% |

The wait under priority is the remainder of the long job already on the device; **only preemption removes it**
(counterexample CX-351: "priority without preemption protects interactive jobs"). The result flips if resume
costs several seconds or a device is reserved for interactive work: then run to completion.

This bead builds the scheduler that the arithmetic calls for, as the decision core DC-09 ("which job runs when on
a device"; tier Solid): size classes taken from the system's own estimate, fair ordering across agents, shortest
estimated work within an agent, ageing against starvation, preempt-resume at snapshot boundaries, and an
admission guard. Decision ADR-TETHER-08 bounds all of it: the scheduler decides when work runs and never what is
computed; no wall-clock quantity enters a result. Alternatives excluded by the plan: bandit allocation on
dispatch; mechanism design for the queue (agents would report their own job sizes; here estimates come from the
system); a device reserved for interactive work as the default (it idles the device and is kept only as the case
in which the conclusion flips).

## Scope

In: size-class assignment; the per-class queues; deficit round-robin across agents weighted by remaining budget;
shortest estimated remaining work within an agent with linear ageing; preempt-resume of batch jobs at snapshot
boundaries with its rate limit and batch share; the admission guard and `FROB-REF-703`; the `campaign` class and
`FROB-REF-704`; wiring the pure guard table to the real dispatcher; the published capacity table; the
measurements of GATE-TETHER-10. Out: the guard table itself and its model check (N32a-21); the queueing model
and simulator (N32a-20); parameter values (N32a-22); slot dispatch (BD-295); the kill decision (N32a-24); the
fleet scheduler across the two machines (its own seed bead).

## Technical approach

**Size classes** come from the system's own estimate, never from the agent (TARGET boundaries):

| Class | Rule | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `interactive` | Point estimate at most 30 s of device time | May use the slot reserve; may trigger preemption of batch work |
| `batch` | Above 30 s and inside the machine envelope | Runs in non-reserve slots; may be parked |
| `campaign` | Above the machine envelope (default 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device, until the owner approves a compute class) | Runs only under an approved compute envelope for its scope. At submission, without one, the classifier refuses it with `FROB-REF-704` (exit 4) before envelope admission; at dispatch and at resume, if the approval has lapsed, the scheduler refuses it with `FROB-REF-704` |

**Which of the two envelope codes fires where (convention 14; N32-REF row 704, N35-REF row 761, N36-20, plan
§32.11.2).** The plan gives two codes for a job above the envelope: `FROB-REF-761` (a job exceeds the compute
envelope in force) and `FROB-REF-704` (a campaign-class job without an approved compute envelope), both exit 4,
`retry: after-change`, divided by raising site so that one oversized job gets one code. The plan's rule for the
size classes is that the `campaign` class is "refused with `FROB-REF-704` until the owner approves" an envelope.
The scope of a demand is, in this order: the token of a launcher it runs under (`frob-launch`, the campaign
runner), the `envelope_scope` the request cites (the common `run` input `--envelope-scope
<gate:…|campaign:…|pretrain:…>`, NJL-01), a scope the verb derives from its own inputs (`train.family`), otherwise
`adhoc`, whose class in force is E0.
1. *At submission* (a job the daemon queues): this classifier runs after the device-fit check (602) and before the
   envelope admission (761) and the session-budget check (701). A job in the `campaign` class whose scope has no
   approved, unexpired request row (N21c-35's `class_in_force` gives E0 for it) is refused with 704; nothing is
   bound, reserved or queued.
2. *At dispatch and at resume:* before a queued job's first episode is placed, and again before a parked job is
   resumed, the dispatcher sizes it again with the same classifier; a job in the `campaign` class whose approval
   expired or was withdrawn while it waited (or that reached the queue by a path that did not pass submission,
   such as a re-enqueue of an `admitted` job after a daemon restart) is refused with 704, leaves the queue in state
   `refused` with its terminal receipt (every trial `unstarted`), and its reservation is released.
3. *Every other envelope case is 761*, raised by N36-20's `admit_run` before the job starts: a run executed in
   process (no size class exists there), a launch through `frob-launch`, a job under an approved scope that
   exceeds that class's per-run limit or the scope's cumulative device-hours, and a cited `envelope_scope` that
   contradicts the launcher's token. `admit_run` never builds 704 and this classifier never builds 761.
4. *A job held under a scratch approval is never re-sized.* Under a scratch registries root (N32ax-03) a job whose
   needed class exceeds the class in force of record is admitted with `hold: scratch-approval` and is never
   dispatched until it is cancelled; the dispatch pass skips held jobs, so it neither re-sizes them nor answers
   them 704, and withdrawing the scratch approval leaves the hold as it is. The dispatch-time 704 is therefore
   exercised against an in-memory registry view of kind `of-record` (unit tests below), never under a scratch
   root, and on the device lane only with an approval of record.
DESIGN reason for the envelope checks before the budget (convention 14): the envelope depends only on the job and
the ratified limits, so every caller gets the same answer, and an agent is never sent to raise a budget for a job
the envelope refuses anyway.

**Registration into the admission pipeline (N32ax-01).** The pipeline of a job-submitting `run` verb is an
ordered list of named stage slots, `preconditions`, `fit`, `size_class`, `envelope`, `budget`, `guard`,
`transaction`, with `size_class` and `guard` left empty until this bead. This bead registers two stages and
changes no position and no other stage's code:
1. `size_class`: the classifier above. At submission it assigns the class from the estimate's point value,
   records it on the job row and echoes it in the envelope, and refuses a `campaign`-class job whose scope has no
   approved envelope with 704 (rule 1 above). The class is not part of the request digest (it derives from the
   cost model, which may be recalibrated between a request and its retry, and a retry under the same key must not
   become FROB-REF-803), while the scope it reads is (a cited `envelope_scope` is an input of the request). The
   same function is called by the dispatcher at dispatch and at resume, where it raises 704 on a lapsed approval.
   A run executed in process has no size class: the slot passes it unchanged and `admit_run` decides (761).
2. `guard`: the admission guard below (`FROB-REF-703`, `retry: backoff`), the last stage before the
   transaction, so that no caller backs off and retries a request that a standing check, the envelope or the
   budget would refuse anyway.
A refusal in either stage binds nothing, reserves nothing and writes no job row (N32ax-01's rule), so the same
key may be retried after the cause is repaired.

A job whose actual work exceeds twice its estimate is reclassified at its next boundary and counted by the
estimate-reliability monitor (of the last 50 jobs, 20% over twice their estimate triggers the fallback).

**Order within a class.** Deficit round-robin across agents, weighted by remaining budget; within an agent,
shortest estimated remaining work first; a waiting job's effective work decays linearly to zero over 600 s
(TARGET), so no job waits behind an unbounded stream of shorter ones. Deficit round-robin (DESIGN detail): each
agent a with waiting work has a deficit counter D_a; each round D_a is increased by q₀·w_a, where q₀ is the
scheduling quantum in device-seconds (5 s by default, OPEN-TETHER-4; the same quantum N32a-21's dominant-agent
row measures the deficit against) and w_a is the
agent's remaining budget divided by the mean remaining budget of agents with waiting work, clamped to
[0.25, 4]; the agent may start episodes while their estimated work is at most D_a, and D_a is reduced by the
work started; an agent with nothing waiting has D_a reset to zero.

**Preempt-resume.** A batch-class job can be parked at a snapshot boundary (quantum at most 5 s, TARGET) and
resumed later with identical digests. Steps: (1) the guard table emits `Preempt(job)` (the batch job with the
most remaining work, when the reserve is full and an interactive job waits); (2) at the job's next snapshot
boundary its worlds are snapshotted: state, solver warm starts, packet queues and stream counters; (3) the job's
state becomes `preempted`, with a checkpoint event carrying the per-world digests; its slots are freed at the
boundary; (4) on resume the snapshot is restored into any free slots, the digests are recomputed and compared
with the checkpoint's, and the state returns to `running`; (5) a difference fails the job as indeterminate,
raises the alarm and trips the fallback. Limits: at most one preemption per batch job per 30 s, and the batch
class keeps at least 30% of device time over a sliding window (TARGET; window 600 s, DESIGN); when the share
would fall below it, interactive work waits for the reserve.

**Admission guard.** Utilization is held under 0.8 per device (TARGET): busy slot-seconds over capacity
slot-seconds on a sliding window (60 s, DESIGN). Beyond it, or when the per-environment job cap is reached, new
work is refused with `FROB-REF-703` (exit 4) and `retry: backoff`, with `poll_after_ms` from the capacity table.

**Wiring the decision model.** Each quantum the dispatcher reads integer state from the ledger, calls the pure
guard-table function, executes the returned actions (co-batch at the next reset boundary; use the reserve;
preempt; open a second environment after the memory preflight; serve other agents first; fall back), and appends
the decision record. The table and its parameters are in the daemon's configuration digest.

**The capacity table the daemon publishes.** Predicted wait by class against utilization, from the closed forms
scaled by Kingman's correction (c_a² + c_s²) ÷ (1 + c_s²), with the squared coefficients of variation of
inter-arrival and service times measured from the daemon's own trace; labelled `model`. Refreshed on a schedule,
never on the dispatch path.

**Measurements for the gate.**
- *The replayed mix.* 90% interactive jobs by count, offered load ρ = 0.7 on one device. Lane A: the real
  dispatcher run inside the queue simulator on the full mix in virtual time, including the model's case (10 s
  and 30 min jobs). Lane B (the gate's device lane): the mix replayed against `frobd` on a device within the
  machine envelope of one hour, with real small jobs as the interactive class and real batch jobs of a few
  minutes (DESIGN; a longer replay needs an approved compute class).
- *Thresholds.* Interactive wait at the 95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10% (time from first
  start to completion over the job's device time alone, minus one, averaged over batch jobs); no job beyond the
  ageing bound.
- *Evidence untouched.* The M3 comparison battery is run once undisturbed and once with random preemption and
  reordering injected by a test hook at every boundary; every per-world digest and every study verdict must be
  identical.

Determinism: scheduling uses the clock; results do not (INV-TETHER-09). Refusals: `FROB-REF-703` (submission,
stage `guard`; repairs: retry after `poll_after_ms`, or submit a smaller question), `-704` (submission, stage
`size_class`, and again at dispatch and resume; repairs in N32-REF's order: split the work below the envelope,
the refusal giving the envelope and the estimate; ask the owner to approve an envelope for the scope, a request
row of `registries/compute_envelope.toml`, and resubmit citing it with `--envelope-scope <scope>`, over MCP the
`envelope_scope` input, under a new key).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TETHER-10 (TARGET): on the replayed mix (90% interactive) at utilization 0.7: interactive wait at the 95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10%; no job beyond the ageing bound; random preemption and reordering leave every digest and verdict identical. Measured values are recorded per device family with the configuration digest (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-TETHER-10 --receipt <hash>`).
- [ ] The measurements that decide KILL-PREEMPT (resumed digests; measured resume cost against the quantum) are recorded and handed to the decision bead, which closes the seed's condition "KILL-PREEMPT decided".
- [ ] Size classes are assigned from the system's estimate; a caller-supplied hint cannot change a class (there is no such parameter).
- [ ] A campaign-class submission to the daemon whose scope has no approved envelope (uncited, or citing with `--envelope-scope` a scope no approved row admits) is refused with `FROB-REF-704`, exit 4, by the size classifier before envelope admission, binding and reserving nothing; a campaign-class job under an approved scope that exceeds that class's per-run limit is `FROB-REF-761`, never 704; a queued campaign-class job whose approval of record lapses before dispatch is refused with `FROB-REF-704` at dispatch, ends `refused` with every trial `unstarted`, and its reservation is released; a job held under `hold: scratch-approval` is never dispatched or re-sized; a request beyond the guard is refused with `FROB-REF-703` and `retry: backoff`.
- [ ] The classifier and the guard are registered into N32ax-01's `size_class` and `guard` slots; the slot list is unchanged; `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/tether/admission_stage_order.toml` holds nineteen rows (six conditions singly and in the thirteen possible pairs, table below) and its loader reports none `not yet due`.
- [ ] With two agents of equal budget submitting continuously, device time splits within 10% of evenly; with budgets 3:1 the split follows the weights within the clamp.
- [ ] A 30 min batch job that has waited 600 s starts before any job of its own class admitted after that moment (the ageing bound holds on the device, not only in the model).
- [ ] At most one preemption per batch job per 30 s; the batch class receives at least 30% of device time in every window of the replay.
- [ ] Run inside the simulator, the real dispatcher reproduces the preempt-resume column of the model within 5% plus the boundary delay (the known answer of the queueing model, now for the real policy).
- [ ] The DC-09 tables (guard table, capacity table) regenerate bit-identically, and each fallback is demonstrated by forcing its trigger.

## Considerations

- KILL-PREEMPT. Trigger: a resumed job's digest differs, or measured resume cost exceeds the quantum. Fallback: run to completion with the slot reserve only. With the fallback, interactive latency depends on the reserve and on batch jobs' natural boundaries; the gate's 6 s target is then re-read and may not hold, which the record must say.
- OPEN-TETHER-4 (reserve, job cap, quantum, ageing period; defaults 5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s) supplies the parameters; they are configuration.
- If KILL-SLOT fired, preemption parks whole environments instead of freeing slots, and the reserve has no meaning; this bead's ordering and guard still apply.
- The device lane's mix differs from the model's 30 min case because of the one-hour envelope; the model case is covered in the simulator lane. A full-scale replay is a named gate measurement needing the owner's compute approval.
- The 30 s interactive boundary interacts with the bounded wait's 30,000 ms maximum: an interactive job normally completes inside one maximum wait.
