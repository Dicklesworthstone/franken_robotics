## Background

Milestone M3 adds places, task families, adversarial search, failure autopsy, the blinded qualification lane and
reports to FrankenRobotics. This bead registers their verbs: every verb whose first milestone is M3 becomes an
entry of `registries/verbs.toml` (compiled into the static table of the crate `frob-verbs`, from which the CLI,
the MCP tools, the schemas and the agent guide are generated) with an input schema, an output schema, at least
one runnable example and a refusal list. The surface parity gate is recorded for the M3 set.

Two things distinguish M3 from the earlier verb sets.

*Authority is real.* Capability tokens exist, so every verb's `capabilities` list is enforced, and several M3
verbs sit on security boundaries: `eval.qualify` returns a verdict and a receipt and nothing per world;
`scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything derived from it; no verb may
read a confirmation world.

*The registry freezes.* After M3 the surface is what fresh agents are tested on (the studio gate at M3, the
partial ten-fold gate, the tournament later). From the freeze on, a verb's name, kind and schemas change only
through a schema version bump with a recorded reason. Open question OPEN-ATEL-4 is due at this freeze: whether
`result.revalidate` and `mission.revalidate` stay two verbs over one engine or become one verb. Default: two
verbs, one engine. Owner: the Tether lead.

GATE-TETHER-02 (Known answer, TARGET): the CLI and MCP surfaces are generated from the registry and their parity
diff is empty; every verb has a schema, an example and a refusal list; the served `tools/list` equals the
registry under the name map (`.` and `-` replaced by `_`).

## Scope

In: registry entries, schemas, examples, refusal lists, task-cluster assignments, bindings, golden envelopes and
status for the M3 set; scope enforcement per entry; the freeze procedure and its lint; the decision record for
OPEN-ATEL-4; the M3 parity record. Out: each verb's computation; the MCP surface integration (BD-298); the
failure-class gate (BD-299); the injection and flag gates of the safety chapter (their seeds).

## Technical approach

**The checklist per verb** (unchanged from M1 and M2): a complete entry; two schemas whose integers are only
`int53`, `u64str` or `i64str`, with units on quantities and the untrusted mark on fields that echo imported
text; a runnable example; a refusal list (the handler's codes plus the common codes of its kind: every verb
802, 724, 720, 723; `run` 701, 702, 703, 805, 803 and the `6xx` family; `mutate` 805, 803, 804, 725; `admin`
720; paginated 806, 807; integer inputs 808); a golden envelope; a binding with no computation; a truthful
`capabilities` row.

Verbs the verb appendix dates at M3 whose mechanism lands later (`shield.budget`, `shield.check`) are registered
as declared and answer `FROB-REF-724`; verbs the appendix dates at M4 whose reduced form lands now
(`mission.separate`, the revalidation pair) are registered with `first_milestone = "M3"`.

**Determinism-class arguments** [BD-269]. Verbs that run physics accept `--determinism` and `--precision`
profiles; a use that a class does not license (for example interventional attribution under an uncontrolled
class) is refused by the engine with its own code, which must be in the verb's refusal list.

**Estimates.** Every `run` verb of the set is estimable and returns the conformal interval of its stratum, or
`uncalibrated` [BD-297].

**Security-relevant entries (checked by table-driven tests).**
- `eval.qualify`: the output schema has no per-world field; the lint forbids any array of world addresses in it.
- Every verb that could expose a world (`telemetry.*`, `sim.replay`, `run.fork`, `run.show`, `autopsy.*`,
  `nemesis.*`, `explain.*`, stills, replays, `artifact.read`) lists the sealed-read refusal of the blinded lane
  in its refusal list.
- `scene.purge` is `admin`, `destructive = true`, and needs its scope; `scene.bank` and `nemesis.hardset`
  declare their two kinds with an `actions` table.
- Exporting verbs (`report.*`, `nemesis.shrink` writing a counterexample world, `prove.bundle`) carry the three
  no-authorization flags, all `false`.

**The freeze, numbered.**
1. Decide OPEN-ATEL-4 (default: two verbs, one engine) and record it in `docs/adr/tether/OPEN-ATEL-4.md`.
2. Write `registries/verbs.freeze` : for each entry its name, kinds, schema identifiers and the digests of both
   schemas.
3. A lint (`RL-FREEZE`) from then on fails the build if a frozen entry's name or kind changes, or if a schema's
   digest changes without its version number increasing and a line in `registries/verbs.changes.toml` (entry,
   old and new version, reason, date).
4. New verbs may be added after the freeze; they are frozen at the next milestone's verb-set bead.

**Gate run.** `scripts/e2e/tether_parity.sh --through M3`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-TETHER-02 for the M3 verbs: empty parity diff; a schema, an example and a refusal list for every M3 verb; `tools/list` equals the registry under the name map; measured counts recorded.
- [ ] Every M3 verb is refused without its scope (`FROB-REF-720`) and accepted with it; `admin` verbs name their scope.
- [ ] `eval.qualify`'s output schema passes the no-per-world lint; a discovery token cannot read a confirmation run through any of the listed verbs (each returns the sealed-read refusal).
- [ ] Exporting M3 verbs carry the three `false` flags; `scene.purge` is marked destructive.
- [ ] The freeze file exists; `RL-FREEZE` fails on a planted rename, a planted kind change and a planted schema change without a version bump.
- [ ] OPEN-ATEL-4 has a decision record; the registry matches it.
- [ ] Every example of every implemented M3 verb runs on the fixtures and returns a schema-valid envelope with a claim product; declared verbs answer 724.
- [ ] Every M3 verb sits in a task cluster of at most seven.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs handlers_m3`, `cargo test -p frob-conformance golden`)
- `every_m3_entry_complete` (table-driven checklist).
- `scope_table`: for each M3 verb, a token without the scope → 720; with it → not 720.
- `qualify_output_has_no_world_fields` (schema lint): a planted `failed_worlds` array fails.
- `sealed_read_listed`: each world-exposing verb has the sealed-read refusal in its list.
- `two_kind_entries`: `scene.bank` (show → read; evict → admin), `nemesis.hardset` (show → read; run → run).
- `purge_is_destructive_admin`.
- `freeze_lint_rename`, `freeze_lint_kind_change`, `freeze_lint_schema_without_bump`, `freeze_lint_allows_bump_with_change_record`.
- `revalidate_pair_one_engine`: `mission.revalidate` and `result.revalidate` on the same change return the same surviving set.
- `declared_verbs_724`: `shield.budget`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/m3_verbs.sh` (seed-explicit; `frobd` running with tokens): the adversary-and-diagnosis loop as
commands: `frob session open --scopes design:write,sim:run,nemesis:run,autopsy:run,qual:run --budget
gpu_s=600 --json`; `frob nemesis search <design> --mission <q> --budget gpu_s=240 --key nem-1 --json`;
`frob nemesis shrink <world> --key shr-1 --json`; `frob run fork <run> --key f-1 --json`;
`frob autopsy intervene <run> --key aut-1 --json`; `frob autopsy attribute <fork> --json`;
`frob eval qualify <design> --mission <q> --dry --json`; `frob report design <design> --json`; then the same
`nemesis search` under a session without `nemesis:run` (expect exit 5); then the parity script. It sources the
shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/m3_verbs/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code
and wall time; the envelope's `evidence` block per call; `assert` lines for expected exits, for the absence of
per-world data in the qualify answer and for the flags on exports; `refusal` lines; `metric` lines for estimate
against actual; a `summary` naming GATE-TETHER-02 (M3).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: empty diff with one exemption; registry, CLI and MCP counts equal; the freeze file's digest stable across two builds.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a qualify schema with a per-world array; a world-exposing verb without the sealed-read refusal in its list; an M3 `admin` verb with an empty capability list; a frozen verb renamed.
- Determinism lanes: generated documentation, the freeze file and golden envelopes identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, on both hosts.

## Considerations

- This bead has two dozen dependencies; entries are registered as declared early and flipped as handlers land. The gate is recorded when the last M3 handler lands.
- Several M3 verbs come from subsections added late to their chapters and have no seed bead (`module.characterize`, `sense.visibility`, `task.audit`, `eval.items`); their handlers are the owning units' new beads, and until they land the verbs stay declared.
- The freeze does not forbid change; it makes change visible, because the fresh-agent results are only comparable across a stable surface.
- Program risk: the surface is large even in clusters. The M3 set alone is about fifty verbs.
