## Background

Milestone M3 adds places, task families, adversarial search, failure autopsy, the blinded qualification lane and
reports to FrankenRobotics. This bead registers their verbs: every verb whose first milestone is M3 becomes an
entry of `registries/verbs.toml` (compiled into the static table of the crate `frob-verbs`, from which the CLI,
the MCP tools, the schemas and the agent guide are generated) with an input schema, an output schema, at least
one runnable example and a refusal list. The surface parity gate is recorded for the M3 set.

Two things distinguish M3 from the earlier verb sets.

*Authority is real.* Capability tokens exist, so every verb's `capabilities` list is enforced, and several M3
verbs sit on security boundaries: `eval.qualify` returns a verdict and a receipt and nothing per world;
`scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything derived from it; no verb may
read a confirmation world.

*The registry freezes.* After M3 the surface is what fresh agents are tested on (the studio gate at M3, the
partial ten-fold gate, the tournament later). From the freeze on, a verb's name, kind and schemas change only
through a schema version bump with a recorded reason. Open question OPEN-ATEL-4 is due at this freeze: whether
`result.revalidate` and `mission.revalidate` stay two verbs over one engine or become one verb. Default: two
verbs, one engine. Owner: the Tether lead. The decision and its record `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-4.md` are N28c-29's
(a dependency); this bead freezes the registry in the form that record states and checks that it does.

GATE-TETHER-02 (Known answer, TARGET): the CLI and MCP surfaces are generated from the registry and their parity
diff is empty; every verb has a schema, an example and a refusal list; the served `tools/list` equals the
registry under the name map (`.` and `-` replaced by `_`).

## Scope

In: registry entries (those no cluster registry bead enters), schemas, examples, refusal lists, bindings, golden
envelopes and status for the M3 set; the check that each M3 verb is seated in NJL-05's task-cluster assignment
(the assignment and its lints are NJL-05's, a dependency); scope enforcement per entry; the freeze procedure and its lint; the check that
the registry matches the OPEN-ATEL-4 record; the M3 parity record. Out: each verb's computation; the MCP surface
integration (BD-298); the failure-class gate (BD-299); the injection and flag gates of the safety chapter (their
seeds); the OPEN-ATEL-4 decision and its record (N28c-29).

This seed bead is the capstone of the M3 verb set and depends on the beads that build its parts. For the cluster
`autopsy` the parts are two: the five rows are entered as data, with every field, by the cluster registry bead
VERBS-autopsy, and they are checked against the handlers by N27b-16 (each example runs on the fixtures, each
refusal list covers what the handler raises, no `autopsy.fork` or `autopsy.divergence` exists). This bead
depends on both, enters no second copy of those rows, and applies to them what is its own: the checklist per
verb as a sweep over the whole M3 set, scope enforcement, the sealed-read listing (which is why each of the five
rows lists `FROB-REF-840`), the freeze and the M3 parity record.

## Technical approach

**The checklist per verb** (unchanged from M1 and M2): a complete entry; two schemas whose integers are only
`int53`, `u64str` or `i64str`, with units on quantities and the untrusted mark on fields that echo imported
text; a runnable example; a refusal list (the handler's chapter codes; the common codes below are appended by the
generator); a golden envelope; a binding with no computation; a truthful `capabilities` row.

The common codes are the appended sets of the registry's entry schema (NJL-01), one rule at every milestone:
`read` 720, 723, 724, 802 (paginated also 806, 807); `run` 701, 702, 703, 704, 720, 723, 724, 761, 802, 803,
805, 808 (761 is the compute-envelope refusal of N36-20's `admit_run`, appended by kind, NJL-01; 808 through
`sync_wait_ms`); `mutate` 720, 723, 724, 725, 802, 803, 804, 805 (805 omitted for a `key_optional` row, N32a-02
RL-21); `admin` 720, 723, 724, 802. Codes appended for a row's fields (NJL-01): 808 for an entry of any kind
with an `int53` input (a `read` or `mutate` with string inputs only does not carry it); 790 when `needs_groups`
is non-empty; 891 for a parameter of
kind `artifact-input<F>`, `artifact-ref<F>` or `artifact-identity<F>`, 892 also for `artifact-identity<F>`, 893
for `foreign-file`; 734 and 828 for a parameter that accepts a path to a file (`artifact-input<F>`,
`artifact-identity<F>`, `foreign-file`). The generator appends them; no row repeats them. The `6xx` codes of a
device-tier `run` verb are chapter codes in its own row. (Earlier drafts of this bead listed a shorter set by
kind, without 704, 761 and 808 on `run` and without 723 on `mutate` and `admin`.)

Verbs the verb appendix dates at M3 whose mechanism lands later (`shield.budget`, `shield.check`) are registered
as declared and answer `FROB-REF-724` (both are declared late rows of NJL-02's `LATE_ROWS`); verbs the appendix
dates at M4 whose form lands now (`mission.separate`, the revalidation pair) keep `first_milestone = "M4"`, the
appendix's value, answer from M3 and are listed by `frob capabilities` as implemented. This is the one rule of
BD-137, NJL-01 and the cluster registries (the field keeps the appendix's milestone; what a build serves is
decided by the handler); an earlier form of this sentence set the field to M3, which the rows of VERBS-mission
and VERBS-result do not do.

**Determinism-class arguments** [BD-269]. Verbs that run physics accept `--determinism` and `--precision`
profiles; a use that a class does not license (for example interventional attribution under an uncontrolled
class) is refused by the engine with its own code, which must be in the verb's refusal list.

**Estimates.** Every job-submitting `run` verb of the set is estimable (the lint `estimate-required` of N32a-15,
a dependency) and returns the conformal interval of its stratum, or `uncalibrated` [BD-297].

**Security-relevant entries (checked by table-driven tests).**
- `eval.qualify`: the output schema has no per-world field; the lint forbids any array of world addresses in it.
- Every verb that could expose a world (`telemetry.*`, `sim.replay`, `run.fork`, `run.show`, `autopsy.*`,
  `nemesis.*`, `explain.*`, stills, replays, `artifact.read`) lists the sealed-read refusal of the blinded lane,
  `FROB-REF-840` (N39-REF), in its refusal list.
- `scene.purge` is `admin`, `destructive = true`, and needs its scope; `scene.bank` and `nemesis.hardset`
  declare their two kinds with an `actions` table.
- Exporting verbs (`report.*`, `nemesis.shrink` writing a counterexample world, `prove.bundle`) carry the three
  no-authorization flags, all `false`.

**The freeze, numbered.**
1. Read the OPEN-ATEL-4 record `docs/adr/OPEN-ATEL-4.md` (N28c-29: two verbs, one engine) and check that the
   registry holds the two rows it names over the one engine; a mismatch stops the freeze.
2. Write `registries/verbs.freeze` : for each entry its name, kinds, schema identifiers and the digests of both
   schemas.
3. A lint (`RL-FREEZE`) from then on fails the build if a frozen entry's name or kind changes, or if a schema's
   digest changes without its version number increasing and a line in `registries/verbs.changes.toml` (entry,
   old and new version, reason, date).
4. New verbs may be added after the freeze; they are frozen at the next milestone's verb-set bead.

**Gate run.** `scripts/e2e/tether_parity.sh --through M3`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs handlers_m3`, `cargo test -p frob-conformance golden`)
- `every_m3_entry_complete` (table-driven checklist).
- `scope_table`: for each M3 verb, a token without the scope → 720; with it → not 720.
- `qualify_output_has_no_world_fields` (schema lint): a planted `failed_worlds` array fails.
- `sealed_read_listed`: each world-exposing verb has `FROB-REF-840` in its list.
- `appended_refusals_one_rule`: the appended list of every M3 verb equals the entry schema's set for its kind and fields (NJL-01's `appended_refusals_by_kind` and `field_appended_codes` over the M3 set; every M3 `run` verb carries 761).
- `two_kind_entries`: `scene.bank` (show → read; evict → admin), `nemesis.hardset` (show → read; run → run).
- `purge_is_destructive_admin`.
- `freeze_lint_rename`, `freeze_lint_kind_change`, `freeze_lint_schema_without_bump`, `freeze_lint_allows_bump_with_change_record`.
- `revalidate_pair_one_engine`: `mission.revalidate` and `result.revalidate` on the same change return the same surviving set.
- `declared_verbs_724`: `shield.budget` and `shield.check` answer 724, exit 5, and `capabilities` reports them not implemented.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/m3_verbs.sh` (header: `bead=BD-312 milestone=M3 labels=tether,registry,verbs gates=GATE-TETHER-02
seed=312 needs=cpu,frobd,mcp plants=qualify-world-array,sealed-read-missing,admin-no-scope,frozen-rename`, each
plant one negative control below; `frobd` running with tokens): the adversary-and-diagnosis loop as commands:
`frob session open --scopes design:write,sim:run,nemesis:run,run:run,autopsy:run,qual:run,report:read --budget
gpu_s=600 --key m3-s1 --json` (scopes named by cluster, BD-258: `run.fork` needs `run:run`, `report.design`
`report:read`; `session.open` is a `mutate` verb, so it carries a key);
a test campaign opened under a second, administrative session (`frob eval ledger --open --task <task>
--world-family <synthetic family> --key m3-open --json`, N24b-15); `frob nemesis search <design> --task <task>
--legality <profile> --budget gpu_s=240 --key nem-1 --json`; `frob nemesis shrink <world> --key shr-1 --json`;
`frob run fork <run> --world <world> --before failure --dt 1.0s --classify-only --key f-1 --json`;
`frob autopsy intervene <fork> --do '<intervention>' --key aut-1 --json`; `frob autopsy attribute <fork> --json`;
`frob eval qualify <design> --task <task> --claim '<claim>' --dry --key qual-dry-1 --json` (a `run` verb carries
a key in its dry form too); `frob report design <design> --json`;
then the same `nemesis search` under a session without `nemesis:run` and a new key (expect `FROB-REF-720`, exit
5); then the parity script; finally the administrative close of the test campaign. It sources the
shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/m3_verbs/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code
and wall time; the envelope's `evidence` block per call; `assert` lines for expected exits, for the absence of
per-world data in the qualify answer and for the flags on exports; `refusal` lines; `metric` lines for estimate
against actual; a `summary` naming GATE-TETHER-02 (M3).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: empty diff with one exemption; registry, CLI and MCP counts equal; the freeze file's digest stable across two builds.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a qualify schema with a per-world array; a world-exposing verb without the sealed-read refusal in its list; an M3 `admin` verb with an empty capability list; a frozen verb renamed.
- Determinism lanes: generated documentation, the freeze file and golden envelopes identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, on both hosts.

## Considerations

- This bead has about sixty dependencies (the beads that build the M3 handlers); entries are registered as declared early and flipped as handlers land. The gate is recorded when the last M3 handler lands.
- Several M3 verbs come from subsections added late to their chapters and have no seed bead (`module.characterize`, `sense.visibility`, `task.audit`, `eval.items`); their handlers are the owning units' new beads, and until they land the verbs stay declared.
- The freeze does not forbid change; it makes change visible, because the fresh-agent results are only comparable across a stable surface.
- Program risk: the surface is large even in clusters. The M3 set alone is about fifty verbs.
