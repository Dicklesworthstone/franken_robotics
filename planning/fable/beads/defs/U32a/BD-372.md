## Background

An agent designing a robot in FrankenRobotics normally walks a sequence of verbs: check the design, sweep it
under a baseline controller, race the survivors, promote the winners. Between the steps that require judgement
there are steps that do not: pass the identifier along, poll the job, call the obvious next verb. Each of those
is an agent turn, and agent turns are slow.

`solve.run` is the agent-facing form of the whole sequence: **one call from a task specification and a budget to
a ranked set with verdicts**. It is orchestration, not a mechanism: every number in its answer comes from the
verbs it calls. The co-design cascade itself (six stages: deduplicate and check; reuse and synthesize; screen;
race; rank robustness; qualify, with a control built into every stage) is run by the verb `opt.cascade`, which
`solve.run` calls. `solve.run` adds none of its own evidence. The registry holds one name per mechanism: the
cascade is `opt.cascade`, and `solve.run` is only its agent-facing caller.

Tier Frontier. HYPOTHESIS: about five-fold fewer serial agent turns per accepted design. The test is the second
arm of GATE-TETHER-13, which is the one-verb arm of the surface A/B (GATE-ATEL-03). There is one A/B, and the
kill gate KILL-SOLVE is decided on it. **Ceiling, stated before any test:** the fraction of turns in recorded
sessions that carry no decision; if that fraction is below 30% the ceiling is under 1.4-fold and the work is not
funded. The pilot (20 recorded sessions replayed on paper, plumbing turns counted) must therefore be on record
with the verdict `fund` before this bead starts. Fallback if the gate fires: the MCP workflow prompts, which
cost nothing to keep.

Why build it at all, given the cascade verb exists: `opt.cascade` is a campaign verb with a campaign's inputs (a
population, a manifest of rates and controls). `solve.run` takes what an agent has at the start, a task and a
budget, fills the rest with registered defaults, and returns something an agent can act on in one step. Whether
that convenience is worth a verb is exactly what the A/B decides.

## Scope

In: the registry entry, schemas, example and refusal list of `solve.run`; the compiler from (task, constraints,
budget) to a campaign manifest for `opt.cascade`; budget splitting; progress; the answer assembly with
provenance per number; the estimate; its arm in the A/B (the measurement the kill decision reads). Out: the
cascade and its stages (BD-352); the A/B's protocol, other arms and adjudication (the Atelier unit); the kill
decision (N32a-32); the prompts (N32a-26).

## Technical approach

**Entry.** `solve.run`, cluster `solve`, kind `run`, tier `F`, capabilities `["opt:run", "sim:run",
"study:submit"]` plus `qual:run` when qualification is requested, task cluster `search`, first milestone M4,
estimable, paginated result. Common properties of a `run` verb: `idempotency_key`, `budget`, `sync_wait_ms`,
`estimate_only`, `on_cancel`.

**Input (`frob.solve.run.in/1`).** `task` (a task or mission identifier), `candidates` (one of: a list of design
identifiers; a generator identifier with a count; a design family), `constraints` (requirement overrides, as
expressions of the requirement language; data, never code), `budget` (required; named units), `stop_at` (the
last stage to run: `check`, `sweep`, `race`, `promote`, `qualify`; default `promote`), `top_k` (default 3).

**Compilation, numbered.**
1. Resolve every name to an immutable identifier; echo the resolutions in `bound`.
2. Build the campaign manifest for `opt.cascade` from the registered defaults of the task's family: stage list
   up to `stop_at`, screening rates, audit rates, the pair cap of races, the study type (anytime-valid), the
   baseline controller per morphology class. The manifest is content-addressed; `solve.run --estimate` returns
   its identifier, so the same campaign can be inspected or run directly with `opt.cascade`.
3. Split the budget across stages by the registered fractions for the task family (DESIGN defaults; recorded in
   the manifest); the estimate of each stage must fit its share at the interval's upper end, otherwise the call
   is refused with `FROB-REF-701` and the repairs name a smaller candidate set or an earlier `stop_at`.
4. Submit one job that runs `opt.cascade` on the manifest. `solve.run` submits no other physics.
5. Stream progress with denominators per stage (designs checked of N; pairs raced of the cap; finalists
   promoted of k).
6. Assemble the answer from the cascade's result records.

**Output (`frob.solve.run.out/1`).** `ranked`: the top-k designs, each with its verdict per requirement
(`settled`, `tied`, `inconclusive`, and where it applies `model-robust` or `model-conditional`), the evidence
for each comparison, and for **every number a `source`** naming the verb and result record it came from
(`design.check`, `eval.compare`, `eval.promote`, …). `excluded`: typed exclusions with counts by reason.
`campaign`: the manifest identifier and the `report.campaign` handle. `controls`: each stage's control and
whether it ran (`not-run` is reported, never hidden). `next_actions`: the decisions left to the agent. The
envelope's claim product is the weakest over everything consumed; `solve.run` can never raise a class.

**Honest endings.** "Every admitted alternative fails", "tied within the margin" and "inconclusive at this
budget" are results (exit 2), not refusals. A budget stop inside the cascade follows the cascade's rule and the
surface's: nothing partial is published as a result; completed stages' results remain as their own records and
the answer says which stage stopped.

**Its arm in the A/B.** In the one-verb arm, agents have the ordinary surface plus `solve.run` in the `search`
cluster. This bead supplies the arm's configuration (a capability profile and launch flags), runs it inside the
A/B's harness and records, per brief, serial turns per accepted design from the session transcripts, beside the
A/B's own measure (restricted mean time to an accepted answer).

Determinism: the manifest is a pure function of the resolved request and the registered defaults; the cascade's
results are those of its verbs. Evidence: no new evidence; claim kind and class inherited through the claim
product.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's verdict is `fund` and is cited; otherwise this bead is closed as not funded with the kill decision's record.
- [ ] `solve.run` is registered with schemas, an example and a refusal list and passes the parity harness; `solve.run --estimate` returns a campaign manifest identifier that `opt.cascade` accepts.
- [ ] For a seeded brief, every number in the answer of `solve.run` equals the number returned by the named source verb for the same record (a mechanical provenance check over the whole answer); no number lacks a `source`.
- [ ] The answer of `solve.run` and the answer of `opt.cascade` run directly on the same manifest are the same set of ranked designs with the same verdicts.
- [ ] The envelope's evidence class is never stronger than the weakest consumed result (property test over planted mixtures of provisional and established inputs).
- [ ] An honest ending (all alternatives fail; tied; inconclusive) returns exit 2 with the typed reason; a budget stop publishes no partial ranked set.
- [ ] Second arm of GATE-TETHER-13 (the same A/B as GATE-ATEL-03): the one-verb arm is run and its serial turns per accepted design are recorded per brief with the ratio to the hand-driven arm and its interval, against the pre-registered factor.
- [ ] The measurement that decides KILL-SOLVE is recorded and handed to the decision bead; if the gate fires, the entry is removed and the generated documentation no longer mentions the verb.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs solve`)
- `manifest_is_content_addressed`: the same resolved request gives the same manifest identifier; a changed `top_k` changes the answer assembly, not the manifest; a changed `stop_at` changes the manifest.
- `budget_split_sums_to_budget`: the stage shares sum to the call's budget in every unit.
- `refused_701_when_a_stage_does_not_fit`: with a budget where the race stage's upper end exceeds its share → 701 with a repair naming an earlier `stop_at`.
- `every_number_has_source`: a schema lint (every numeric leaf of the output sits in an object with a `source`) and a run-time check.
- `provenance_matches_source_verbs`: for each numeric leaf, re-read the source record and compare.
- `equals_opt_cascade_on_same_manifest`.
- `claim_product_weakest_wins`: one provisional finalist among established ones → the ranked set's envelope is provisional.
- `honest_endings_exit_2`: all-fail, tied, inconclusive.
- `constraints_are_data`: a constraint string that is not an expression of the requirement language is refused with 802.
- `no_physics_outside_cascade`: the job tree of a `solve.run` call contains exactly one child campaign.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/solve_run.sh --seed <s>`: on the seeded cascade population fixture with a planted winner: `frob
solve run --task <task> --candidates <generator>:64 --budget gpu_s=600 --estimate --json`; the same with `--key
sol-1 --sync-wait-ms 0` (exit 8); `frob job wait`; then `frob opt cascade --manifest <id> --key cas-1` on a
fresh store and a comparison of the two answers; then the provenance check. It sources the shared helper and
writes `target/e2e-logs/solve_run/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code and wall time;
the envelope's `evidence` block; progress events per stage with denominators; `assert` lines for equality with
the cascade, for every `source`, for the planted winner appearing in the ranked set; `metric` lines for estimate
against actual per stage. A second script, `scripts/e2e/solve_arm.sh`, runs the one-verb arm inside the A/B
harness and logs, per brief, turns, accepted designs, turns per accepted design and the ratio; its `summary`
names GATE-TETHER-13 (second arm), GATE-ATEL-03 and KILL-SOLVE.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: on the seeded population the planted winner is in the top-k; `solve.run` and `opt.cascade` agree exactly.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a `solve.run` that computes a summary statistic itself (a number without a matching source record); one that labels a ranked set established when a finalist is provisional; one that hides a control that did not run; an arm configuration in which the one-verb agents also receive extra budget (the A/B's equalization check must fail).
- Determinism lanes: manifest identifiers and, on `batch-cpu`, the ranked set identical twice on one machine and across worker counts; debug and release.

## Considerations

- KILL-SOLVE. Trigger: the ceiling is under 1.4-fold, or agents with `solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs. Fallback: the prompts. If it fires, nothing else in the system changes.
- Program risk (structure helped agents only slightly): the earliest signal is the ratio of restricted mean times in the surface A/B, which needs an effect size and not merely a win. A convenience verb that does not show an effect loses its speed claim and its place.
- A one-call form hides intermediate decisions; the answer therefore lists the decisions it took by default (budget split, `stop_at`, rates) so that an agent can take them back by calling the stages itself.
- A full campaign may exceed the default compute envelope; the estimate says so and the call is refused as campaign-class until the owner approves a compute class.
