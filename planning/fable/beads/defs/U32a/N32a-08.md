## Background

Anything in FrankenRobotics that takes longer than an agent should wait is a **job**: a study of paired episodes,
a mission synthesis, a training run. A job must survive everything around it: the client that submitted it may be
killed, the connection may drop, the daemon may restart, a device may be lost. The crash-consistent job protocol
makes three promises: an admitted job is never lost and never duplicated; nothing partial is ever published as a
result; and every terminal state accounts for every planned trial.

This bead is the core of that protocol on the Tether side (tier Solid): the **job ledger** and the **job state
machine**, in the crate `frob-verbs`. Telemetry and results are published in chunks whose format and store belong
to Chronicle (the telemetry subsystem); Tether owns the leases and the transaction discipline those publishes run
under. The M1 form runs on the CPU with no daemon; the same code is hosted by the daemon `frobd` from M2.

Design decisions (DESIGN):
- *The ledger is a FrankenSQLite database under the state root, with exactly one writer.* FACT to recheck at the
  pin: the suite's mail server warns of a snapshot-drift defect in the engine's concurrent-writer mode and
  defaults to a single writer (`mcp_agent_mail_rust/crates/mcp-agent-mail-server/src/lib.rs`). From M2 the writer
  is the daemon. At M1, with no daemon, the `frob` process that runs a job takes an exclusive lock file under the
  state root for the duration of its writes; a second writer waits or is refused with `retry: backoff`.
  Alternatives excluded: replicated consensus, gossip membership and escrow counters (one daemon per host; nothing
  is replicated; they solve a problem this system does not have).
- *Job logs persist independently of any client connection.*
- *Every transition is identified by (job, logical world, episode, tick)*, never by wall-clock time, slot or
  device, so a replayed job produces the same transition records.

## Scope

In: the ledger tables for jobs, events, logs and leases, which are the only definition of those tables; the
state machine with all eight states and its legal transitions, each written as one transaction with its event
row; the storage of trial counts and terminal receipts on the job row; lease generations as a row the engine
increments; the engine's side of cancellation and of a budget stop; restart recovery of the ledger; the `job`
block of the envelope. The M1 form runs on the CPU with no daemon.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the types of the stop acknowledgement and of the terminal receipt (`StopCause`, `StopAck`,
  `TerminalReceipt`, `KindCounts`), the rule that maps each trial outcome to one of the four counts, the
  sub-count `interrupted`, the constructor that refuses a receipt whose counts do not sum to the planned
  number, and the discarding of unpublished state on the worker's side: N29a-05 (in `frob-chronicle`), which
  this bead depends on. This engine constructs those types and stores them; it defines none of its own;
- everything a device adds, at M2: device workers and how they publish, the two chunk scales, the lease
  manager's device triggers (a failed device chunk that moves worlds, a stall, a device loss), device-fault
  retries, draining and releasing device resources on cancel, unattached column files on restart, resource
  accounting, the device lanes of the fault battery, and the two columns `device` and `shape_class` it adds
  to the job row: BD-210, which depends on this bead and changes no table, state or transition defined here;
- the admission transaction and idempotency binding, with the unique index on (principal, verb, key)
  (N32a-09); the chunk-publishing transaction and the chunk tables (BD-091, BD-210); the job verbs `job.list`,
  `job.status`, `job.wait`, `job.find`, `job.cancel` as registry entries (N29a-23); budgets (N32a-15);
  dispatch and preemption policy (BD-295, BD-296).

## Technical approach

**States and transitions.**

| From | To | Cause | Recorded with |
|---|---|---|---|
| (submission) | `refused` | Admission refused (budget, guard, scope, schema). No job identifier, no binding, no charge | the refusal envelope only |
| (submission) | `admitted` | The admission transaction committed | job row, binding, reservation |
| `admitted` | `running` | First start record | (job, world, episode, tick 0) |
| `admitted` | `failed_to_start` | Environment or device could not be opened | the refusal |
| `admitted` | `cancelled` | Cancelled before starting | cancel receipt |
| `running` | `preempted` | Parked at a snapshot boundary by the scheduler | checkpoint event with its digest |
| `preempted` | `running` | Resumed | resume event; the digest must equal the checkpoint's |
| `running`, `preempted` | `cancelled` | `job.cancel`, drained | cancel receipt |
| `running` | `complete` | All planned trials accounted for, nothing failed the job | terminal receipt |
| `running`, `preempted` | `failed` | Budget stop, device fault beyond retry, chunk conflict (`FROB-REF-810`, indeterminate), a resume digest that differs | terminal receipt with the refusal |

Terminal states: `refused`, `failed_to_start`, `cancelled`, `complete`, `failed`. The states are eight
(`refused`, `admitted`, `running`, `failed_to_start`, `cancelled`, `preempted`, `complete`, `failed`); with the
pseudo-state `(submission)` the table has nine entries and twelve legal transitions (two from submission, three
from `admitted`, four from `running`, three from `preempted`). `preempted` exists from M1 although nothing
preempts before M3, so that the row format does not change later. Any other transition is a bug: it is a typed
internal error, rejected by the engine before it reaches the ledger, and never written.

**One transaction per transition.** Each transition is one ledger transaction that updates the job row and
appends its `job_event` row; a terminal transition writes the terminal receipt in the same transaction. A
budget stop ends in `failed`, not in `cancelled` (DESIGN: nobody asked for the job to stop and the caller
receives a refusal; `cancelled` is reserved for a requested cancel; the device bead BD-210 and the receipt
bead N29a-05 state the same).

**Trial accounting (INV-TETHER-04).** A job has `planned` trials (episodes, pairs, cells; the unit is in the
plan). Each trial ends in exactly one of four counts, by the counting rule of the receipt type (N29a-05; DESIGN,
since the source names only the counts; this bead first used another mapping, with a physical failure inside
`completed`, which is withdrawn): `completed` (it reached a terminal tick with a task outcome and no failure
class), `failed` (it reached a terminal tick with a failure class of kind `physical`: the simulated robot
failed), `refused` (the solver or integrator refused or faulted, kind `numerical`, which includes a non-finite
state; or the request for it was invalid, kind `protocol`; or its device was lost with none of the same family
to resume on), `unstarted` (it never started, or it was cut short by a cancellation or a budget stop; the
second case is also counted in the sub-count `interrupted`). Every terminal receipt carries the four counts and
they sum to `planned`; the receipt's constructor refuses any other. A numerical refusal, a budget stop and a
physical failure are three different counts and are never merged into one count or one message. A fault of the
job as a whole (a device fault beyond retry, a chunk conflict, a resume digest that differs) is not a trial
outcome: it moves the job to `failed`, and the trials it cut short are `unstarted` and `interrupted`.

**Tables (DESIGN; integer columns that can exceed 2⁵³ − 1 are strings on the wire).**

```
job(id PK, principal, namespace, verb, request_digest, state, planned, completed, failed, refused, unstarted,
    unit, run, size_class, reservation, admitted_seq, hold, receipt_json)          -- hold: NULL or 'scratch-approval'
job_event(job, seq, kind, world, episode, tick, payload_json)      -- PK (job, seq); kind: start, checkpoint,
                                                                   -- resume, preempt, trial_end, cancel_request,
                                                                   -- cancel_ack, terminal, recovery
                                                                   -- payload of an event that changes state:
                                                                   -- from, to, cause, commit_seq, trace_id,
                                                                   -- wall_time (the last two are operational
                                                                   -- metadata, outside the event's digest)
job_log(job, seq, line)                                            -- survives every client
run_lease(run PK, lease_gen)                                       -- the fencing token of the current writer
```

The four count columns mirror the stored receipt, which is the `TerminalReceipt` of N29a-05 serialized in
`receipt_json` (with its sub-count `interrupted`, its counts by kind and its stop cause). The idempotency key is
not a column of `job`: it lives in the binding table of the admission transaction (N32a-09), which holds the
unique index on (principal, verb, key). The last transition of a job is its newest `job_event` row. From M2 the
device bead adds the columns `device` and `shape_class` by an additive migration (BD-210).

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-verbs/src/jobs/{row.rs, states.rs, engine.rs, restart.rs}`; BD-210 extends
`engine.rs` and `restart.rs` for device workers and adds `lease_manager.rs`.

**Held jobs.** Under a scratch registries root (a `--registries-root` other than the one of record, N32ax-03) a
job whose needed compute class exceeds the class in force **of record** is admitted and held: the admission
transaction (N32a-09) writes `hold = 'scratch-approval'` on its row. A held job stays in `admitted`; no queue,
dispatcher or restart recovery ever starts it (recovery re-enqueues it still held); no device is allocated and
no world is stepped; the only legal transition out of it is `admitted → cancelled`. `hold` is never cleared: an
approval of record is a new submission. Reason: a scratch approval may show that a job would be admitted, never
run it (the hazard is an agent approving itself a larger envelope).

**Leases.** Each run has a lease generation. The engine increments it, in a ledger transaction, whenever worlds
are reassigned to workers: a retry, a shard split, a device loss, a resume after restart. The chunk-publishing
transaction (owned by the Chronicle beads, running in this same database) checks the writer's generation against
`run_lease`, the expected predecessor and the contiguous commit frontier, and publishes the checked payload
hashes, the index and the logical frontier together; an older writer is fenced out and writes nothing. Each
(world, episode) has a fixed logical chunk partition that does not depend on how the device was sharded: chunk k
covers ticks [k·C, (k + 1)·C). A duplicate identical chunk reuses its original receipt; different bytes for a
committed extent are a replay error: the publishing transaction refuses the publish with its own code
(`FROB-REF-878`, the store's refusal, and marks the run `determinism-violated`), and this engine then moves the
job to `failed` as indeterminate and reports the chunk conflict to the caller as `FROB-REF-810`, exit 9. The
receipt carries both codes.

**Cancellation.** At the documented safe boundary (the kernel-batch boundary of the device layer): (1) record
`cancel_request`; (2) stop dispatching new work for the job; (3) drain in-flight device work; (4) discard
unpublished state; (5) record `cancel_ack` with the last committed tick per (world, episode); (6) write the
receipt: outstanding device work at the moment of the request (worlds and kernel batches in flight), the observed
cancellation latency, the four trial counts. Steps 3 to 5 on the worker's side, and the types of the
acknowledgement (`StopAck`) and of the receipt, are N29a-05's; the engine records what the worker returns. A dropped handle is not a cancellation receipt: only step 6 is. The
latency is operational metadata and enters no digest.

**How a cancel request reaches a job that runs in process (DESIGN; M1 has no daemon and no `job.cancel`).**
The ledger admits one writer process, and with no daemon that process is the `frob` invocation that is running
the job, so no other process can record a request (`single_writer_lock` below). The request is therefore that
process's own interrupt. For the duration of an in-process job `frob` handles SIGINT and SIGTERM: the first
signal sets the job's stop word; the engine runs steps 1 to 6 and the CLI prints its one envelope with
`job.state: cancelled` and the receipt, exit 2 (the exit N32a-17 gives a CLI call that cancelled its own job).
A second signal exits 9 at once, without waiting for the drain, and leaves the job to step 4 of restart
recovery. The option `--on-cancel` (default `leave`, N32a-17) governs a wait on a daemon's job and is not
consulted here: an in-process job has no wait apart from the work, and exiting while leaving a `running` row
that no process executes would report work as running that nothing is doing. A process killed outright is the
transport class as before: the job is found by its key and resumed by restart recovery. From M2 a job under
`frobd` is cancelled by `job.cancel` from any process (N29a-23), and an interrupt of a call that only waits on
such a job follows N32a-17. The handler is `crates/frob-cli/src/interrupt.rs`; it does nothing but set the stop
word and count signals.

**Budget stop.** The same drain as cancellation, ending in `failed` with `FROB-REF-702`. Nothing partial is
published as a result (INV-TETHER-03): committed chunks remain as telemetry and the receipt says `budget-stop`.

**Restart recovery, numbered.** (1) Open the ledger and take the writer lock. (2) For every job in `admitted`
with no start record: enqueue it. (3) For every job in `running` or `preempted`: ignore chunks that are not
attached to a committed frontier, increment the run's lease generation, and rebuild from the last committed
checkpoint. (4) For every job with `cancel_request` and no `cancel_ack`: complete the drain bookkeeping and write
the receipt. (5) Record a `recovery` event per job touched. Garbage collection is bounded and respects retained
checkpoints and the frontiers pinned by open cursors.

**The `job` block of the envelope.** `id`, `state`, `progress {done, total, unit}` (always with a denominator),
`eta_s` with its interval, `poll_after_ms`, `last_committed`.

Determinism: the event log of a job is a pure function of the job's request and identities (class exact for the
log's content; wall-clock fields are excluded from its digest). Evidence class: Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every legal transition of the table is reachable in a test; every other pair is rejected before any write.
- [ ] Every transition is one transaction with its event row: a fault injected at each statement of a
      transition leaves either the old state with no event or the new state with its event, and a terminal
      state is never visible without its receipt.
- [ ] For 10⁴ seeded jobs with random trial outcomes, cancellations and budget stops, the four terminal counts sum to `planned` (KA-766).
- [ ] After a kill at each of the engine's write points and a restart, the ledger passes its consistency check, every `admitted` job is enqueued exactly once and every running job resumes from its last committed checkpoint with a new lease generation.
- [ ] A cancel returns a receipt naming the last committed tick per world, the outstanding device work and the observed latency; dropping the handle produces no receipt and leaves the job running.
- [ ] After a budget stop or a cancellation no result object exists for the job; committed telemetry chunks remain readable.
- [ ] With no daemon, an interrupt signal sent to a `frob` process running a job in process ends the job `cancelled` with its receipt and one envelope (exit 2); a second signal during the drain exits 9 and the next start completes the receipt by recovery step 4; no state other than `cancelled` or `running` is ever visible in between.
- [ ] A late writer (an older lease generation) is refused and writes nothing; a chunk conflict moves the job to `failed` with `FROB-REF-810`, exit 9.
- [ ] The plants of CX-349 (`chunk-key-tick-range`) and CX-350 (`chunk-key-no-episode`) are built under those plant identifiers and caught by this bead's tests, each bound by its name prefix `nc_<nnnn>_` to the control that the seeding tool of N38b-05 derives from N32-CX's catalogue entry (one control each, gate GATE-TETHER-08); this bead writes and allocates no row of `controls.toml`.
- [ ] A second writer process cannot open the ledger for writing.
- [ ] A held job (`hold = 'scratch-approval'`) is never started by the queue or by restart recovery, and cancelling it yields a receipt with every trial `unstarted`.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` documents the state machine, the receipt and the recovery steps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs jobs`)
- `transition_table_exhaustive`: all 9 × 9 pairs of the eight states and the pseudo-state `(submission)`; exactly the twelve legal ones succeed.
- `transition_writes_event_atomically` (a fault at each statement of the transaction).
- `counts_sum_to_planned` (property, 10⁴ cases): `completed + failed + refused + unstarted == planned` at every terminal state.
- `three_outcomes_not_merged`: a job with one numerical refusal, one physical failure and a budget stop reports 1 in `refused`, 1 in `failed`, the trials the stop cut short or never started in `unstarted` (those cut short also in `interrupted`), nothing of the three in `completed`, and three distinct messages; the job's state is `failed` with cause `budget-stop`.
- `cancel_receipt_fields`; `dropped_handle_is_not_cancel`.
- `in_process_interrupt_cancels`: a real `frob` child process running the 2,000-tick CPU fixture job is sent SIGINT after its second committed chunk: exit 2, exactly one envelope on standard output, `job.state: cancelled`, the receipt's last committed tick per world equal to the frontier rows, the four counts summing to `planned`; the same with SIGTERM. `second_interrupt_exits_9`: with the drain held by the test-only pause point, a second SIGINT exits 9, the job row is still `running` with a `cancel_request` and no `cancel_ack`, and the next process's recovery writes the receipt. `on_cancel_leave_not_consulted_in_process`: the first test with `--on-cancel leave` given explicitly has the same outcome.
- `budget_stop_publishes_no_result`.
- `restart_enqueues_admitted_once`: 50 admitted jobs, kill, restart twice: each has exactly one start record.
- `held_job_never_starts`: a job row with `hold = 'scratch-approval'` survives two restarts in `admitted` with zero start records and zero worlds stepped; `admitted → running` on it is rejected before any write; `job.cancel` moves it to `cancelled` with a receipt whose four counts are 0, 0, 0 and `planned` unstarted.
- `restart_resumes_from_checkpoint`: lease generation goes from g to g + 1; chunks beyond the committed frontier are ignored.
- `late_lease_writes_nothing`; `chunk_conflict_fails_job_810`.
- `cx_349_range_keys_caught` and `cx_350_episode_free_keys_caught`: with the test-only flag that keys chunks by
  tick range (CX-349), the retried shard's ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) both commit and the test fails; with the
  flag that drops the episode from the key (CX-350), episode 1's chunk 0 replaces episode 0's and the test fails;
  without the flags both pass (one duplicate or conflict; two keys). The two test functions carry the prefix
  `nc_<nnnn>_` of the controls seeded for CX-349 and CX-350 (nnnn is the `NC-` number the seeding tool issued).
- `progress_always_has_denominator`.
- `single_writer_lock`: a second process attempting a write is refused with `retry: backoff`.
- `event_identity`: every event row has (job, world, episode, tick) and no wall-clock column in its digest.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/job_state_machine_cpu.sh`: on the CPU with the built-in fixture, submits a job with `frob … --key
"$(e2e_key <label>)" --json` (one label per submission), kills the process at three points (after admission,
mid-run, during cancel drain), restarts, and reads the job back by identifier. No job verb exists before M2, so
at M1 the read is `cargo run -p frob-verbs --example job_show -- <job id>` (DESIGN: a read-only printer of the
job row, its events and its receipt from the ledger under `FROB_STATE`, an example target and not a verb); from
M2 the same step calls `frob job status <job id> --json` (N29a-23). Then the script runs a cancel (an interrupt signal sent to the in-process run, the M1 request path
above; the kill "during cancel drain" is a second process kill after that signal) and a budget stop (a run with
`--budget cpu_core_s=1`). NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/job_state_machine_cpu/`: `cmd` lines with exit codes, `assert` lines for the state after each
restart, the four counts and their sum, the last committed tick against the chunk frontier, ledger consistency;
`metric` lines for cancellation latency (recorded); on failure the job's event log path. The `summary` names
GATE-TETHER-08 (CPU rows).

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-766 (exact): the completed, failed, refused and unstarted counts of a terminal receipt sum to the planned number.
- KA-765 (exact), consumed here through the lease and conflict rules: with the fixed partition [k·C, (k + 1)·C) per (world, episode), a retried shard's second extent is a duplicate (original receipt) or a conflict (`FROB-REF-810`); a reset that reuses tick zero does not overwrite the first episode.
- Negative controls (CX-349, CX-350 seed them): a build that keys chunks by tick range, in which ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) both commit; a build that keys by (world, tick), in which the second episode overwrites the first; a build that enqueues `admitted` jobs on restart without checking for a start record (a job runs twice). Each must be caught. This bead first builds the plants of CX-349 and CX-350 under the plant identifiers of their catalogue entries in the counterexample registry N32-CX (`chunk-key-tick-range`, `chunk-key-no-episode`) and catches them. Their two control rows in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (`seed` = the CX number, `kind = "mutation"`, gate GATE-TETHER-08) are derived from those entries by the seeding tool of N38b-05, which also allocates their `NC-` numbers: no mechanism bead writes or allocates a control row, and this bead only binds its two tests to the seeded controls by name. BD-210 on the daemon and N32b-24 through the binaries bind their tests to the same controls.
- Determinism lanes: the event log digest identical twice on one machine, across worker counts, in debug and release.

## Considerations

- The chunk conflict has two codes, one per layer, and both are kept: the telemetry chapter's refusal table gives `FROB-REF-878` to the store's refusal of a publish with different bytes under a committed chunk key, and the surface chapter's table gives `FROB-REF-810` (exit 9) to the chunk conflict the caller of the job sees. This bead uses 810 for the job-level failure; the publisher's own refusal is the Chronicle beads' (BD-091, BD-210), and the receipt carries both.
- OPEN-CHRON-5 (lease granularity: one generation per run, or one per shard with a versioned world-to-shard map; default one per run; owner: Tether lead) is a parameter here.
- The lab-runtime exploration of this state machine (crash at every await point, torn writes, clock jumps) is the simulation-testing bead; this bead's kill tests are the deterministic subset that runs on every commit.
- The mapping of trial outcomes to the four counts is DESIGN where the plan names only the counts, and it has one owner: the receipt type of N29a-05.
