## Background

A long job in FrankenRobotics is run by the daemon, while the agent's tool call merely waits on it. Two things
flow along that wait: progress, towards the agent, and cancellation, towards the job. Both are easy to get subtly
wrong.

*Progress* must be honest: every event has a numerator, a denominator and a unit (episodes, pairs, rungs, cells,
worlds). A bare spinner or a numerator without a total tells an agent nothing about whether to keep waiting.

*Cancellation* has two meanings that must not be confused. A client may stop waiting (its own timeout fired, the
user pressed interrupt, the connection dropped). Or a principal may decide the work itself should stop. Decision
ADR-TETHER-05: **wire cancellation of a waiting call ends the wait, not the job; `job.cancel` cancels the job.**
Reason: a client timeout is not a decision to discard admitted, charged work; the job is recoverable by its key.
Alternative excluded: cancel the job whenever the caller goes away, which makes every flaky connection destroy
paid work. The invariant is INV-TETHER-11: a cancelled wait is not a cancelled job unless the call asked for
that; a cancelled job drains.

FACT about the MCP library (`fastmcp_rust`, to re-read at the pin): `McpContext` offers `checkpoint()`,
`is_cancelled()`, `report_progress_with_total`, `report_progress_exact` and `cx()`; when the client sent no
progress token a progress call is a no-op. Wire cancellation is only partly qualified: Unix stdio and native
WebSocket route a cancellation while a handler runs; non-Unix stdio, SSE and custom transports keep blocking
boundaries; a handler that does not cooperate can outlive the bounded process-exit drain. On cancellation the
library may deliver a request-cancelled error (−32004) or nothing.

Tier Solid. Measured by the second clause of GATE-TETHER-12 (a wire-cancelled wait leaves the job running) and
by the transport clause of GATE-TETHER-04 (a killed connection during a wait leaves the job recoverable by key
in 100 of 100 trials).

## Scope

In: mapping daemon progress frames to MCP progress and to CLI progress events; the checkpoint discipline of the
long-tool handler; what a cancelled wait does on each surface; the `on_cancel` property; how `job_cancel` reaches
the daemon and what its receipt says; the rule that device state is protected by the daemon's drain and never by
the library's process-exit drain. Out: the wait timer (N32a-16); the job engine's drain and receipt (N32a-08 and
the device job protocol); the Tasks extension (N32a-30).

## Technical approach

**Progress.**
1. The daemon emits a progress frame `{job, done, total, unit, state}` at most 4 times per second per job
   (DESIGN rate limit), always including the first and the last.
2. MCP: each frame becomes `ctx.report_progress_with_total(done, total, Some(message))`, where `message` is the
   unit and state; `report_progress_exact` is used when a count needs its exact spelling (a value that is a
   string-typed 64-bit quantity). No progress token from the client: the call is a no-op and nothing else
   changes.
3. CLI with `--json`: each frame is printed as one `frob.progress/1` line before the final envelope; without
   `--json`, a single updating line on standard error.
4. A frame without a `total` is a bug: the frame type cannot be constructed without one, and for work whose
   total can grow (an anytime-valid study) `total` is the declared cap.

**Cancellation of a wait.**
- *In-process verbs.* A verb that runs in the caller's process receives the call's `Cx` (`ctx.cx()`) and observes
  cancellation at its own checkpoints; a cancelled short verb returns nothing and has changed nothing (its
  mutation, if any, is one ledger transaction: committed or not).
- *Long tools.* The handler does no device work. It submits a job, waits on the job's frames and calls
  `ctx.checkpoint()` at each one, so it always cooperates with the library's cancellation, including on
  transports where cancellation is only checked at a checkpoint. On cancellation the handler returns nothing (the
  library sends its cancelled error or nothing) and **leaves the job running**.
- *`on_cancel`.* A `run` call may set `on_cancel: "cancel_job"` (CLI: `--on-cancel cancel_job`; default
  `"leave"`). Then, on cancellation of the wait, the handler first sends the daemon a cancel request for the job
  it submitted, and returns without waiting for the drain.
- *CLI interrupt.* On the first interrupt signal during a wait the CLI stops waiting and prints its one
  envelope: with `leave`, the deferred form (exit 8, the job handle, `next_actions` naming `frob job wait`); with
  `cancel_job`, it requests cancellation, waits for the receipt and prints the envelope with `job.state:
  cancelled` and the receipt (exit 2, DESIGN). A second interrupt exits 9 without waiting.

**Cancellation of a job.** `job_cancel {job}` (kind `admin`, scope `job:cancel`) asks the daemon to cancel. The
daemon's job engine, which owns the device, drains at the kernel-batch boundary: request, drain in-flight device
work, discard unpublished state, acknowledge with the last committed tick per world. The receipt reports the
outstanding device work at the moment of the request and the observed cancellation latency. A dropped handle is
not a cancellation receipt. Because the drain is done inside the daemon, the library's unqualified process-exit
drain is never the mechanism that protects device state (INV-TETHER-10): killing `frob-mcp` outright during a
call is safe by construction.

**A killed connection.** No result reaches the caller: this is the transport failure class. The caller must not
resubmit blindly; it reconnects and calls `job.find` with its idempotency key. One job and one charge exist, or
none do.

**Lint.** A source lint on `surface/frob-mcp`: no call into a backend or kernel API, and every `await` inside the
long-tool loop is followed by a checkpoint before the next frame is processed.

Determinism: progress frames are derived from committed counts and are monotone in `done`; their timing is
wall-clock and enters nothing. Cancelling and re-waiting never changes a job's digests.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every progress event on both surfaces has `done`, `total` and `unit`; `done` is non-decreasing; the last event before a successful envelope has `done = total` or the job's declared stop.
- [ ] With no progress token, the tool call's result is byte-identical to the result with one.
- [ ] A wire-cancelled wait (library client cancel, on stdio and loopback HTTP) leaves the job `running` and later `complete` with unchanged digests (second clause of GATE-TETHER-12).
- [ ] The same with `on_cancel: "cancel_job"` ends the job `cancelled` with a receipt naming the last committed tick per world.
- [ ] Killing the connection or the client process during a wait leaves the job recoverable by `job.find` with its key in 100 of 100 seeded trials (transport clause of GATE-TETHER-04); no second job and no second charge exist.
- [ ] `job_cancel` without the scope is refused with `FROB-REF-720`; with it, the receipt carries outstanding device work and observed latency.
- [ ] CLI: first interrupt prints exactly one envelope (exit 8 under `leave`); second interrupt exits 9.
- [ ] The lint finds no device call in `frob-mcp` and fails on a planted one.
- [ ] After 1,000 cancelled waits the daemon's resource counters are at baseline (no leaked waiter, timer or device resource).

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-mcp progress cancel`, `cargo test -p frob-cli interrupt`)
- `frame_requires_total` (compile-time: the constructor takes `total`).
- `progress_monotone`: a recorded frame sequence with a regression is rejected by the forwarder as an integrity fault.
- `no_token_is_noop`: results equal with and without a progress token.
- `exact_spelling_used_for_u64`: a 64-bit tick count is reported with `report_progress_exact`.
- `cancelled_wait_leaves_job` (in-memory transport, lab clock): cancel at each frame boundary of a 20-frame job; the job completes; digests equal the uncancelled run.
- `on_cancel_cancel_job`: the job ends `cancelled`; the receipt has a last committed tick for every world.
- `handler_checkpoints_every_frame`: an instrumented context counts one checkpoint per frame plus one before submission.
- `cli_first_interrupt_exit_8`, `cli_second_interrupt_exit_9`, `cli_cancel_job_prints_receipt`.
- `job_cancel_requires_scope`.
- `rate_limit_keeps_first_and_last`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/progress_cancel.sh`: with a real `frobd`, `frob-mcp` and `frob`: run a slow fixture job; record
progress events on both surfaces; cancel the wait on the wire; verify the job by `frob job status`; repeat with
`on_cancel`; kill the client process 100 times at seeded offsets and recover each by `frob job find --key`;
interrupt the CLI. It sources the shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/progress_cancel/<utc>.ndjson` plus a
transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code and wall time; `metric` lines for progress events (count, any missing
denominator: threshold 0), recovery by key (threshold 100 of 100), observed cancellation latency against the
250 ms target at the 99th percentile (recorded with its device), leaked resources (threshold 0); `assert` lines
for job states and digest equality; a `summary` naming GATE-TETHER-12 (cancel clause) and GATE-TETHER-04
(transport clause).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: a job whose wait was cancelled has the same per-world digests as one that was never interrupted.
- Negative controls: a handler build that cancels the job on every wait cancellation (the "leaves the job running" assertion must fail); a build with a blocking read in the loop and no checkpoint (the cooperation test must time out and fail); a build where the adapter performs the drain itself (killing the adapter mid-drain must be seen to leave device work outstanding, and the leak check must fail).
- Determinism lanes: digest equality after cancel-and-rewait on `batch-cpu`, twice, across worker counts, debug and release.

## Considerations

- On transports where the library cannot route a cancellation while a handler is blocked, cancellation takes effect at the next frame; the frame rate (at least one per second for a live job, by a heartbeat frame if there is no new count, DESIGN) bounds that delay.
- An agent that sets `on_cancel: "cancel_job"` and has a short client timeout will cancel its own work repeatedly; the generated guide explains the default and when to change it.
- The exit code for a CLI call that ends by cancelling its own job (2) is DESIGN; the plan fixes only that the acknowledgement names the last committed tick.
- OPEN-TETHER-2 covers whether real harnesses send wire cancellation at all; this mechanism is correct either way.
