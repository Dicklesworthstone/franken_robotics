## Background

The crash-consistent job protocol of FrankenRobotics promises that a job survives late writers, retries that
split a shard, resets, crashes and restarts without losing, duplicating or corrupting anything. The protocol's
parts are built by several beads: the job state machine and leases (Tether), the chunk-publishing transaction
(Chronicle), the device job protocol, the daemon. Unit and simulation tests exercise each part. This bead is the
end-to-end measurement of the whole on a running system, from the user's side, which is what the gate asks for.

GATE-TETHER-08 (Known answer; earliest M2; TARGET): *job protocol under seeded faults (late lease, overlapping
ranges, a reset reusing tick zero, a kill between chunk write and publish, a daemon restart): the frontier stays
contiguous, a duplicate chunk reuses its receipt, a conflicting chunk is flagged, terminal counts sum to
planned, a cancel names the last committed tick, and no device resource leaks.*

Terms. Telemetry and results are published in **chunks**: each (world, episode) has a fixed logical partition in
which chunk k covers ticks [k·C, (k + 1)·C), independent of how the device was sharded. The **frontier** of a
(world, episode, stream) is the highest committed chunk index; contiguous means the committed chunks are exactly
the indices 0 to the frontier. A **lease generation** is the fencing token of the current writer of a run; it is
incremented whenever worlds are reassigned to workers. One ledger transaction enforces the lease generation, the
expected predecessor and the contiguous frontier, and publishes the checked payload hashes, the index and the
frontier together.

Two counterexamples fix the design and become negative controls here. Range-keyed chunks (CX-349): a retry
splits a shard, and ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) both cover ticks 50 to 99; with range keys both commit. A key
of (world, tick) (CX-350): a reset reuses tick zero, and the second episode overwrites the first.

## Technical approach

**Fault menu and injection (test builds only; a lint asserts release builds contain no hook).** A fault is
selected by `FROB_FAULT=<name>@<job>:<world>:<episode>:<chunk>` plus a seed.

| Fault | How it is injected | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Late lease | A worker is paused after reading its lease; the engine reassigns its worlds (generation g → g + 1); the worker resumes and publishes with g | The publish is refused (the store's code to the worker is `FROB-REF-875`) and writes nothing; the new writer's chunk commits; the frontier advances once |
| Overlapping ranges | A shard is split mid-chunk and the retry re-emits ticks that the first writer already covered: with the run's chunk length C (256 unless the job set another), the first writer holds ticks [0, 2C) and the retry holds [C, 3C), the plan's ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) scaled to C = 50; the hook also makes the retry offer the mid-chunk remainder [C + C/2, 2C) as one publish | Only partition extents are accepted: the extent [C, 2C), already committed with identical bytes, returns its original receipt; [2C, 3C) commits once; the mid-chunk remainder is refused as not a partition extent (the store's code to the worker is `FROB-REF-885`); no tick is stored twice |
| Reset reusing tick zero | A world ends an episode and resets; the next episode starts at tick 0 | The second episode has a different key; both episodes' chunk 0 exist; neither overwrote the other |
| Kill between chunk write and publish | The worker writes the payload file, then the process is killed before the publishing transaction commits | After restart the unattached payload is ignored; the frontier is unchanged; the job resumes from the last committed checkpoint and republishes |
| Daemon restart | `kill -9` of `frobd` at a seeded point during a multi-world job | Recovery: every `admitted` job without a start record is enqueued once; running jobs resume with a new lease generation; clients recover by key |
| Conflicting duplicate (the control for "a conflicting chunk is flagged") | A committed extent is republished with one byte changed | The store refuses the worker's publish (`FROB-REF-878`); the run is marked as a determinism violation; the job fails as indeterminate and its caller sees `FROB-REF-810`, exit 9, with 878 named as its cause |

**The script, numbered (`scripts/e2e/job_protocol_faults.sh <lane> --seed <s>`).**
1. Start `frobd` on a scratch state root (lane `fake`: the daemon's worker is the address-pure fake device under
   the deterministic runtime; lane `batch-cpu`; lane `cuda`; lane `metal`). The script accepts all four lane
   names. This bead runs and reports `fake` and `batch-cpu`; the lanes `cuda` and `metal` are run by the
   script of N29a-24 (`scripts/e2e/job_protocol_devices.sh`, its shell-driven trials), which records them.
2. Submit the fixture job with `frob sim run … --worlds 64 --episodes 3 --key "$(e2e_key f-<s>-<fault>)" --sync-wait-ms 0
   --json` (the fault's name with `-` for `_`, as `e2e_key` labels allow) (192 planned trials; chunk length C fixed at admission), one key per fault and seed, so that no fault's
   job replays another's stored answer.
3. For each fault of the menu, with the seed choosing the injection point, run the job to a terminal state (or
   through the restart) and then run the assertions.
4. Separately: submit under the key `"$(e2e_key c-<s>)"`, then `frob job cancel <job> --json` at a seeded moment; read the
   receipt.
5. Stop the daemon gracefully and read its resource counters.

**The six assertions (each is one `assert` line with its measured value).**
- *A1 contiguous frontier:* the frontier audit of `frob-conformance::job_faults` (the function the unit test
  `frontier_audit_detects_hole` checks), run read-only over a copy of the ledger taken after the run (no verb
  exposes the raw chunk index), shows for every (world, episode, stream) committed indices exactly 0 … frontier;
  `frob telemetry query` over each stream up to its frontier then reads every committed tick once, which checks
  the same from the user's side.
- *A2 duplicate reuses its receipt:* the receipt returned to the retrying writer is byte-identical to the
  original (read from the job's event log).
- *A3 conflict flagged:* the conflicting publish yields `FROB-REF-810` at the job, with the store's
  `FROB-REF-878` as its cause in the receipt; `frob job status` shows `failed`, indeterminate, exit 9; no row of
  the committed extent changed (content hash unchanged).
- *A4 counts sum:* in every terminal receipt `completed + failed + refused + unstarted = planned` (192).
- *A5 cancel names the tick:* the cancel receipt lists a last committed tick for each of the 64 worlds, and each
  equals the end of that world's frontier chunk.
- *A6 no leak:* after the graceful stop, the six numbers of the leak set equal their values before the first
  job. The leak set is one list for every lane of the gate, the same in N29a-24 (DESIGN: the two drafts counted
  different resources): (1) the free-slot count; (2) the entries of the arena's lease table; (3) the pool's
  outstanding bytes (pooled buffers); (4) the device's allocated bytes as the backend reports them (device
  memory); (5) the open file handles of the daemon and its workers; (6) the number of registered waiters. On
  the lanes `fake` and `batch-cpu` number 4 has no device behind it and is recorded as not applicable, never as
  passed.

**The lane receipt.** The gate lane of this script writes the receipt (for each of the two lanes and each fault,
the seeds run, the count of runs in which A1 to A6 all held, the measured values of the six assertions for the
worst run, the daemon's configuration digest and the hardware fingerprint) under its content address,
`receipts/gates/<hash>.json`, where `<hash>` is the BLAKE3 hash of its bytes (N40-01); the named copy
`receipts/gates/GATE-TETHER-08/lanes-<commit>.json` (DESIGN path) is a convenience only and is never cited. N29a-24
records GATE-TETHER-08 from its own `g4_chaos` receipt (the recorder takes only tier receipts, and a tier receipt
has no field for another receipt), in which the entry `tether08:lanes@host` verifies this receipt: it loads the
file that `FROB_TETHER08_LANES` names, recomputes its BLAKE3 hash against its name, requires the run's commit and
reports per lane and fault the seeds run and the six assertions. This bead writes nothing in
`registries/gates.toml`.

Determinism: on the `fake` and `batch-cpu` lanes the final ledger digest is a function of (seed, fault, binary);
on device lanes (run by N29a-24) digests are compared per world against the lane's own class. Evidence: Tested
(known answer).

- [ ] The run is repeated for at least 100 seeds per fault on the `fake` lane and 10 on `batch-cpu` (TARGET lane sizes; the counts run are recorded).
- [ ] The script run with the lane `cuda` or `metal` uses the same hooks and the same six assertions and exits `not run` with the reason when the family has no backend; N29a-24's script calls it that way.
- [ ] Each negative control below is caught by the assertion named for it.
- [ ] The lane receipt names the commit, the hardware fingerprint, the seeds and the configuration digest of the daemon, and is stored as `receipts/gates/<hash>.json` with `<hash>` the BLAKE3 hash of its bytes (N40-01).

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance job_faults`)
- `fault_spec_parses`: `late_lease@j:1:7:0:3` and a malformed spec.
- `frontier_audit_detects_hole`: a synthetic chunk index with indices {0, 1, 3} is reported with the hole at 2.
- `receipt_equality_is_bytewise`.
- `count_sum_checker`: (150, 2, 0, 40, planned 192) passes; (150, 2, 0, 39, 192) fails.
- `leak_counter_diff`: a planted unreleased lease is reported by name.
- `no_fault_hooks_in_release` (lint).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/job_protocol_faults.sh` as above. It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/job_protocol_faults/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(`N32a-18`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time, and for verb calls the
envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed). `metric` lines per fault and
lane: holes in the frontier (threshold 0), duplicate receipts equal (must be true), conflicts flagged (must be
1 of 1), count sum against planned (exact), worlds with a named last committed tick (64 of 64), leaked resources
(0), recovery time and cancellation latency (recorded with their units). Ledger digests are logged where state
is compared. On failure: the first differing value, the fault spec, the seed and the paths of the ledger copy
and the job event log. The final `summary` gives assertions passed and failed, names GATE-TETHER-08 and the
lane, and gives the path and digest of the lane receipt.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-765 (exact): a retry that splits a shard into tick ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150), and a reset that reuses tick zero. With the fixed partition [k·C, (k + 1)·C) per (world, episode), the second extent is a duplicate (original receipt) or a conflict (`FROB-REF-810`); the second episode does not overwrite the first.
- KA-766 (exact): completed, failed, refused and unstarted trial counts of a terminal receipt sum to the planned number.
- Negative controls (each a test-only build flag; each must fail the named assertion): range-keyed chunks, so that both overlapping ranges commit (A1 or the no-duplicate-tick check); a (world, tick) key, so that the second episode overwrites the first (the both-episodes check); a publisher that advances the frontier before the row is durable (A1 after the kill fault); a worker that skips the lease check (the late-lease expectation); a cancel path that acknowledges before draining (A5 and A6).
- Metamorphic relation: for a fault-free run, the final ledger digest is invariant under the seed that orders worker scheduling.
- Determinism lanes: `fake` and `batch-cpu` twice on one machine with identical digests; across worker counts; debug and release; each device family against its class.

## Considerations

- The plan gives the chunk conflict two codes in two chapters (`FROB-REF-810` in the surface chapter, `FROB-REF-878` in the telemetry chapter). The two refusal registries divide them by layer and keep both: 878 is the store's refusal to the publishing worker and its record of the conflicting chunk key, and its registry row carries `surfaces_as = "FROB-REF-810"`; 810 is what the job and its caller see, with 878 as its cause; both exit 9 with `retry: never`. A3 therefore asserts 810 at the job and reads 878 from the cause field of the receipt, and N29a-24 states the same for its device trials.
- No device leak is asserted from the daemon's own counters (numbers 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 of the leak set); on real devices the backend's allocator statistics (number 4) are compared as well, which is why the device lanes belong to N29a-24.
- The gate's device entries depend on the backends that exist at M2; a device family without a backend is recorded `not run` with the reason, never `pass` (in N29a-24's row).
- The 100-trial "killed connection during a wait" measurement belongs to the failure-class gate and is not repeated here.
