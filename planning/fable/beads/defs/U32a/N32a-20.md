## Background

The daemon of FrankenRobotics shares a few GPUs between many agents. Agents mostly submit short, interactive
questions (a comparison of a few seconds) and occasionally long ones (a training run of half an hour). The
scheduling question that matters is: *how long does a ten-second job wait behind a training run?* An agent's turn
blocks on that wait, so it is paid in agent idle time.

The plan answers with arithmetic before any code (DESIGN arithmetic on an M/G/1 queue with Poisson arrivals; a
model, not a measurement), and the answer decides the design: **priority alone is not enough; only preemption
removes the wait**, because the wait under priority is the remainder of the long job already on the device. This
bead turns that arithmetic into three checked artifacts: the closed forms as code with exact known answers, a
discrete-event simulator against which the real dispatcher is later run, and the capacity table the daemon
publishes, which corrects the Poisson assumption for bursty arrivals. It builds no scheduler; the scheduler bead
plugs into the simulator built here. Tier Solid; it needs no device.

## Scope

In: the closed forms; the worked table; the simulator with a policy interface and three reference policies; the
sweep that shows when the conclusion flips; the capacity-table generator with Kingman's correction from a trace;
known answer KA-758 and counterexample CX-351. Out: the real dispatcher (BD-296) and its guard table (N32a-21);
the replayed-mix gate on devices (BD-296); parameter fitting from the M2 queue trace (N32a-22); the worked
card GB-39 (bead GB-39, `registries/cards/GB-39.toml`, which depends on this bead): the card holds the case at
utilization 0.7 as ten exact rationals (2,000.5556 s, 620.8621 s, 5/29 s, 30/29 and the rest) with its own
controls, and its simulated lane runs this bead's simulator and reference policies at this bead's tolerance
of 5%; it builds no second simulator and no second table.

## Technical approach

**The model.** One server (one device). Jobs arrive at rate λ; service time S is general. Utilization ρ = λ·E[S].
Two classes: short (class 1) with arrival rate λ₁ and service S₁, and long. Define W₀ = λ·E[S²] ÷ 2 (the mean
residual work seen by an arrival).

| Policy | Mean wait of a short job |
|---|---|
| First come, first served | W₀ ÷ (1 − ρ) |
| Non-preemptive priority for the short class | W₀ ÷ (1 − ρ₁), with ρ₁ = λ₁·E[S₁] |
| Preempt-resume (short class preempts long) | λ₁·E[S₁²] ÷ (2·(1 − ρ₁)): the short class sees only itself; plus at most one quantum, because preemption happens at snapshot boundaries |

Long jobs under preempt-resume are slowed by the factor 1 ÷ (1 − ρ₁), a slowdown of 1 ÷ (1 − ρ₁) − 1.

**The worked case.** One device; 90% of jobs take 10 s and 10% take 30 min (1,800 s). E[S] = 0.9·10 + 0.1·1,800 =
189 s. E[S²] = 0.9·100 + 0.1·3,240,000 = 324,090 s². For the short class λ₁ = 0.9·λ, E[S₁] = 10 s,
E[S₁²] = 100 s², so ρ₁ = 9·λ. With λ = ρ ÷ 189:

| Utilization ρ | First-come wait | Short-class wait, priority only | Short-class wait, preempt-resume | Slowdown of long jobs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 857 s | 439 s | 0.12 s + quantum | 2.4% |
| 0.7 | 2,001 s | 621 s | 0.17 s + quantum | 3.4% |
| 0.8 | 3,430 s | 713 s | 0.20 s + quantum | 4.0% |
| 0.9 | 7,716 s | 806 s | 0.22 s + quantum | 4.5% |

(Check at ρ = 0.7: λ = 1 ÷ 270 s⁻¹; W₀ = 324,090 ÷ 540 = 600.17 s; first come 600.17 ÷ 0.3 = 2,000.6 s; ρ₁ =
1 ÷ 30; priority 600.17 ÷ 0.96667 = 620.9 s; preempt-resume (0.9 ÷ 270 × 100) ÷ (2 × 0.96667) = 0.172 s;
slowdown 1 ÷ 0.96667 − 1 = 3.45%.) The quantum is at most 5 s (TARGET).

**When the result flips.** If resume costs several seconds, or a device is reserved for interactive work, running
to completion is right. The simulator sweeps resume cost r ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10} s and reports the r at
which preempt-resume stops beating priority-with-reserve on the loss the dispatcher uses.

**Kingman's correction.** A swarm's arrivals burst, so the capacity table the daemon publishes scales the
Poisson-model waits by (c_a² + c_s²) ÷ (1 + c_s²), where c_a² and c_s² are the squared coefficients of variation
of inter-arrival and service times measured from the daemon's own trace. For Poisson arrivals c_a² = 1 and the
factor is 1. In the worked case c_s² = (324,090 − 189²) ÷ 189² = 288,369 ÷ 35,721 = 8.07; arrivals with
c_a² = 4 would scale waits by (4 + 8.07) ÷ (1 + 8.07) = 1.33.

Which c_s² a cell uses (DESIGN, applying the plan's factor cell by cell): the moments of the work that the
cell's residual term W₀ is taken over. The first-come and priority-only cells wait behind the whole mix, so they
use the mix's c_a² and c_s² (1.33 above). The preempt-resume cell of the short class sees only its own class, so
it uses that class's measured c_a² and c_s²; in the worked case the short class's service is a constant 10 s
(c_s² = 0), and the factor is the class's own c_a², about four times its Poisson value rather than 1.33 times
under the burst above. Applying the mix's 1.33 there would understate the bursty interactive wait by a factor
of about three, which is the wait the interactive target is set on.

**Capacity table (schema `frob.capacity/1`, DESIGN).** Rows: size class × utilization in {0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} →
predicted mean wait, with the measured c_a², c_s², E[S], E[S²] per class, the trace window, and the label
`model` (never `measured`). Generated by a pure function of a trace summary; published as part of the decision
core DC-09's record and shown by the dashboard's queue pane.

**Simulator (`frob-conformance::queuesim`).**

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub trait Policy {                      // the real dispatcher implements this later
    fn on_arrival(&mut self, now: Time, job: SimJob);
    fn on_boundary(&mut self, now: Time, running: Option<&Running>) -> Decision;  // continue, preempt, start(job)
}
pub struct SimConfig { arrivals: ArrivalProcess, service: ServiceDist, quantum: Time, resume_cost: Time, seed: u64 }
pub fn simulate(cfg: &SimConfig, policy: &mut dyn Policy, jobs: u64) -> SimReport;  // waits by class, slowdowns
```

Virtual time only; arrivals by address-seeded draws (Poisson, or a two-state burst process with a chosen c_a²);
service from the two-point distribution or from a trace; preemption only at boundaries spaced one quantum apart.
Three reference policies live in the harness: first come first served, non-preemptive priority, preempt-resume.
The report gives per-class mean wait with a batch-means confidence interval (100 batches).

Determinism: the simulator is a pure function of (config, policy, seed); class exact. Evidence class of KA-758:
Tested; of the capacity table: Estimated (a model), labelled as such.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The closed-form functions reproduce all sixteen cells of the worked table to the printed digits.
- [ ] KA-758 (TARGET): for the reference policies, each cell of the table is within 5% of the simulated mean at 10⁶ simulated jobs, with the batch-means interval reported; the preempt-resume column is compared after subtracting the measured mean boundary delay (at most one quantum).
- [ ] CX-351 is registered as a negative control: under the priority-only policy at ρ = 0.7 the short-class wait is about 621 s, which fails an interactive-wait target of 6 s at the 95th percentile; the control must be caught by the same check the scheduler gate uses.
- [ ] The capacity-table generator reproduces the Poisson table for c_a² = 1; for c_a² = 4 in the worked case it scales the first-come and priority-only cells by 1.33 and the short class's preempt-resume cell by that class's own factor (its c_a², since its c_s² = 0); on a simulated burst trace each corrected cell is closer to the simulated mean than the uncorrected one.
- [ ] The flip sweep reports a resume cost above which run-to-completion has the lower loss, and the value is recorded for the kill gate KILL-PREEMPT.
- [ ] The `Policy` trait is documented so that the dispatcher can be run in the simulator unchanged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-758 (5%; at 10⁶ simulated jobs, TARGET): with 90% of jobs 10 s and 10% of jobs 30 min (E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s²): first-come waits 857, 2,001, 3,430 and 7,716 s at utilization 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9; short-class waits 439, 621, 713 and 806 s under priority only, and 0.12, 0.17, 0.20 and 0.22 s plus one quantum under preempt-resume; slowdown of long jobs 2.4%, 3.4%, 4.0% and 4.5%.
- CX-351 (negative control): priority without preemption. At utilization 0.7 a short job waits 2,001 s first come first served, 621 s under non-preemptive priority and 0.17 s plus one quantum under preempt-resume.
- Metamorphic relations: scaling all service times and inter-arrival times by k scales every wait by k; relabelling job identifiers changes nothing.
- Determinism lanes: reports identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The two-point service distribution has high variance; if a cell's batch-means interval at 10⁶ jobs is wider than 5%, the run length is increased and the count recorded. The tolerance is the plan's target, not a proven property of the estimator.
- The model has one server. Two devices in one host are two queues under the M3 design; a shared multi-server queue would change the formulas and is not assumed.
- The table is a model. The scheduler's gate is measured on a replayed mix on real devices; this bead supplies the expectation and the negative control, not the pass.
- The related decision-core known answer (waits of about 2,000 s, 621 s and 0.2 s in the worked queue) is the same arithmetic at ρ = 0.7.
