## Background

The daemon `frobd` of FrankenRobotics decides, every scheduling quantum, which episodes go into which free slots
of a device's environment, whether a waiting interactive job may use the reserve, whether a long job is parked,
whether a second environment is opened. That decision is a small controller sitting next to statistical
machinery (anytime-valid comparisons, promotions, qualification) that must not be disturbed by it. The plan's
position is strict (ADR-TETHER-08): **the scheduler decides when work runs and never what is computed; no
wall-clock quantity enters a result.** Reason: feedback from load into results breaks replay and makes verdicts
depend on who else was running. Alternatives excluded, each named in the plan: bandit allocation on dispatch (a
second controller on shared telemetry, and regret is the wrong objective); mechanism design for the queue
(estimates come from the system, not from agents); replicated consensus, gossip membership and escrow counters
(one daemon per host; nothing is replicated).

This bead builds the decision model as a **pure function**: a guard table with hysteresis that maps the integer
state read from the ledger to actions. It is the decision core the plan calls DC-09 ("which job runs when on a
device": queueing with size classes and preempt-resume, per quantum, tier Solid). Being pure, it can be replayed
on a recorded trace, run inside the queue simulator, model-checked, and shadowed against its fallback. The
scheduler bead wires it to the real dispatcher.

## Scope

In: the state record; the guard table and its hysteresis; the action set; the ordering key with linear ageing;
the loss function; the fallback and its triggers; decision records for replay; the model check of PO-TETHER-05;
the three non-interference rules as tests. Out: executing the actions on devices (BD-295, BD-296); size-class
assignment and the admission guard (BD-296); fitting the parameters and loss weights from the queue trace
(N32a-22).

## Technical approach

**State (all integers, read from the ledger once per quantum).** Per device and shape class: slot capacity B,
free slots, reserve size, episodes waiting by size class and agent, environments open. Per job: size class,
estimated remaining work (milliseconds of device time), admission sequence, milliseconds waited (quantized to the
quantum), preemptions in the last 30 s. Per device: the batch class's share of device time over the sliding
window (600 s, DESIGN; per mille, an integer from the ledger's busy slot-seconds by class). Per agent: deficit
counter, remaining budget. Global: of the last 50
finished jobs, how many ran over twice their estimate; whether any resume digest differed; the memory preflight
result and the environment creation cost for a waiting shape class.

**The guard table.**

| State | Signal | Action | Loss avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under-filled | Free slots beyond the reserve while episodes of this shape class wait | Co-batch at the next reset boundary | Idle slot-seconds |
| Saturated | No free slot and an interactive job waiting | Use the reserve; if it is full, preempt the batch job with the most remaining work at its next snapshot boundary, unless the batch share would fall below 30% (300 per mille, TARGET) over the window: then queue, and the interactive job waits for the reserve | Agent idle time |
| Mixed shapes | A waiting job's shape class has no environment | Open a second environment if the memory preflight passes and the job's estimated work exceeds the creation cost; otherwise queue | Recompilation and a re-captured launch graph |
| One agent dominant | An agent's deficit exceeds its quantum while others wait | Serve the others first | Unfairness |
| Estimates unreliable | Of the last 50 jobs, 20% ran over twice their estimate; or a resume digest differed | Fall back to round-robin per agent and run to completion; on a digest difference also fall back to per-job dispatch and raise an alarm | Misclassified long jobs; a broken invariant |

Rows are evaluated in the order: estimates unreliable, one agent dominant, saturated, mixed shapes, under-filled
(DESIGN order: safety first, then fairness, then latency, then throughput). **Hysteresis** (DESIGN parameters,
all in the configuration): the unreliable state is entered at 10 of the last 50 and left only at 5 or fewer of
the last 50 with no digest difference outstanding; a digest difference is cleared only by an operator; the
dominant-agent state is left when the deficit falls below half a quantum; a preemption of a given job is not
repeated within 30 s. The table and its parameters are part of the daemon's configuration digest, which every
job receipt records.

**Ordering key with linear ageing.** Within a size class: deficit round-robin across agents, weighted by
remaining budget; within an agent, shortest estimated remaining work first. A waiting job's effective work decays
linearly to zero over the ageing period A = 600 s (TARGET):

    effective_work(j, t) = estimated_remaining_work(j) × max(0, 1 − waited(j, t) ÷ A)

and ties break by admission sequence. After 600 s a job's key is 0, the minimum, so no job waits behind an
unbounded stream of shorter ones.

**Loss, in seconds (used for shadow evaluation and for fitting, never for results).**

    L = Σ over jobs j of c(j)·wait_j + (idle slot-seconds ÷ B) + Σ over agents a of max(0, deficit_a) + Σ preemption cost

with c = 1 for interactive and 0.1 for batch jobs, and the preemption cost equal to its measured snapshot and
restore time. These are DESIGN defaults; nothing here is measured, and the weights are refitted from the M2
queue trace.

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub fn decide(state: &DispatchState, cfg: &GuardConfig, prev: &Hysteresis) -> (Vec<Action>, Hysteresis);
pub enum Action { CoBatch{..}, UseReserve{..}, Preempt{job}, OpenEnvironment{shape}, Queue{job},
                  ServeOthersFirst{agent}, FallbackRoundRobin, FallbackPerJob, Alarm{reason} }
```

**Decision records.** Each quantum's (state digest, actions, configuration digest) is appended to the ledger's
operational log, so any decision can be replayed: `decide` on the recorded state must return the recorded
actions. A shadow lane evaluates the fallback policy on the same state and records both actions and the loss
difference.

**Running `decide` in the queue simulator (DESIGN).** N32a-20's simulator (`frob-conformance::queuesim`, a
dependency) takes any implementation of its `Policy` trait. This bead adds the adapter
`frob_conformance::queuesim::DecidePolicy`: at each boundary it builds a `DispatchState` from the simulator's
queue, with virtual time as the quantized clock reading, calls `decide`, and maps `Preempt` to the simulator's
preempt, `UseReserve` and `CoBatch` to start, and `Queue` to continue. The adapter lives in the conformance crate
so that `frob-verbs` gains no dependency on the simulator. The simulator, replay and fallback steps of the
end-to-end script are one harness, `crates/frob-conformance/examples/dispatch_model.rs`: `cargo run --release -p
frob-conformance --example dispatch_model -- <simulate | replay | force-fallback> --seed 21` (`simulate` takes
`--rho 0.7 --jobs 1000000`, `replay` takes `--trace <recorded trace>`, `force-fallback` takes `--trigger
<over-estimates | digest-difference>`).

**Three rules that keep the scheduler from interfering with evidence.**
1. It decides when, never what: e-processes consume episodes in address order, so random preemption and
   reordering leave digests and verdicts identical.
2. No effort profile of a solver reads the wall clock or changes inside a run.
3. No adaptive rule ships without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated
   fallback.

**Proof obligation PO-TETHER-05: with linear ageing no admitted job waits forever.** Argument: after 600 s a
job's key is the minimum, and ties break by admission order, so only jobs admitted earlier (finitely many) can be
ordered before it within its agent; deficit round-robin serves every agent with waiting work within one round.
Verification: a bounded explicit-state model check of `decide` (up to 3 agents, 6 jobs, time in quanta up to
A + 20 quanta, adversarial arrivals of short jobs): in every reachable state, a job that has waited A is started
before any job admitted after it in its class. The bound is per class: the interactive class preempts or uses the
reserve ahead of batch work by the saturated row, and the batch class is kept from starving across classes by
its 30% share of device time (BD-296), not by ageing.

Determinism: `decide` is a pure function of its arguments (class exact). Its inputs include a quantized clock
reading, which is recorded; nothing it outputs enters a result or an evidence record (INV-TETHER-09).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `decide` is pure: a property test replays 10⁵ random states twice and through serialization with identical actions.
- [ ] Each row of the guard table is exercised by a constructed state that triggers it and by a neighbouring state that does not.
- [ ] Hysteresis: a sequence oscillating between 9 and 10 over-estimates of 50 enters the fallback once and does not leave until 5 or fewer.
- [ ] PO-TETHER-05: the model check finds no state in which a job that has waited the ageing period is overtaken by a later admission; with ageing disabled (negative control) it finds a starvation trace.
- [ ] The fallback is demonstrated by forcing each trigger (20% over-estimates; a resume digest difference): actions become round-robin and run to completion, and for the digest case also per-job dispatch with an alarm.
- [ ] Decision records replay: for a recorded trace of 10⁴ quanta, `decide` reproduces every recorded action.
- [ ] The configuration digest changes when any table parameter changes.
- [ ] A source lint finds no read of the wall clock in any solver-effort or result-producing path reachable from the dispatcher.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs dispatch_model`)
- `row_under_filled`: 100 free slots beyond the reserve, 3 waiting episodes of the class → `CoBatch`.
- `row_saturated_reserve_then_preempt`: no free slot, interactive waiting, reserve free → `UseReserve`; reserve full → `Preempt` of the batch job with the most remaining work.
- `row_saturated_batch_share_floor`: reserve full, interactive waiting, batch share 320‰ and the preemption would take it to 290‰ → `Queue`, no `Preempt`; at 360‰ falling to 310‰ → `Preempt` (the 30% floor that BD-296 states and measures is decided here, since `decide` emits every `Preempt`).
- `row_mixed_shapes`: preflight passes and work 120 s > creation cost 20 s → `OpenEnvironment`; work 5 s → `Queue`.
- `row_dominant_agent`; `row_estimates_unreliable`; `row_digest_difference_alarm`.
- `ageing_key`: estimated work 900 s, waited 300 s, A = 600 s → effective 450 s; waited 600 s → 0; waited 700 s → 0.
- `aged_job_precedes_shorter_newcomer`: within the `batch` class, a 30 min job aged 600 s is ordered before a 2 min batch job admitted now (same agent, so the key decides). Ageing orders within a size class only: a 10 s `interactive` job admitted at the same moment still goes first, through the class order and the saturated row (a test asserts both, so that the ageing bound is not read as a cross-class promise; the device form of the bound, BD-296's, says "its own class").
- `tie_breaks_by_admission`.
- `preemption_rate_limit`: the same job is not preempted twice within 30 s.
- `hysteresis_enter_10_leave_5`: over-run counts 9, 10, 9, 10, 6, 5 of the last 50 → fallback entered at the first 10 and left only at 5; a digest difference keeps it until the operator clears it.
- `preempt_disabled_config` (the KILL-PREEMPT configuration): no free slot, interactive job waiting, reserve full → `Queue`, never `Preempt`; reserve free → `UseReserve`.
- `loss_example`: two interactive jobs waiting 3 s and 5 s, one batch waiting 100 s, 400 idle slot-seconds at B = 8,192, no deficit, one preemption of 0.5 s → L = 8 + 10 + 0.0488 + 0.5 = 18.55 s.
- `model_check_no_starvation`; `model_check_finds_starvation_without_ageing`.
- `replay_trace_10k`.
- `decide_policy_adapter` (`cargo test -p frob-conformance queuesim_decide`): on 10³ seeded simulator states the adapter's decision equals the mapping of `decide`'s actions on the state it built, and the state carries the simulator's virtual time, never the wall clock.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/dispatch_model.sh` (header: `bead=N32a-21 milestone=M3 labels=tether,dispatch,model
gates=GATE-TETHER-10,KILL-PREEMPT seed=21 needs=cpu plants=no-ageing,clock-in-decide,fairness-after-saturation`,
each plant one negative control below and each must be caught): runs the unit and model-check targets; runs `decide` inside the queue simulator
through `DecidePolicy` (the `dispatch_model` example's `simulate`) on the worked workload at ρ = 0.7 and compares
the interactive wait with the preempt-resume expectation; replays a recorded decision trace (`replay`); forces
both fallback triggers (`force-fallback`). It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/dispatch_model/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines; `metric` lines for states explored
by the model check, the longest wait of an aged job (quanta), replay mismatches (threshold 0), simulated
interactive wait against the model (recorded), loss of policy against fallback on the shadow lane; `assert` lines
for each fallback trigger; a `summary` naming GATE-TETHER-10 (ageing clause) and PO-TETHER-05.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the ageing values above; the loss example (18.55 s); at ρ = 0.7 in the simulator the short-class wait is about 0.17 s plus the boundary delay under this policy.
- Negative controls: ageing disabled (the model check must find starvation); a `decide` that reads a global clock (the purity test must fail); a table in which the dominant-agent row is evaluated after the saturated row (the constructed fairness state must fail).
- Metamorphic relation: relabelling agents and jobs consistently permutes the actions and changes nothing else.
- Determinism lanes: all tests twice on one machine, debug and release, both hosts.

## Considerations

- OPEN-TETHER-4 (the slot reserve, per-environment job cap, quantum and ageing period, from the M2 queue trace; owner: daemon lead; defaults 5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s) supplies this table's parameters; they are configuration, not constants.
- The kill gate KILL-PREEMPT (a resumed job's digest differs, or measured resume cost exceeds the quantum) removes the `Preempt` action; the table must behave sensibly with it disabled (saturated → use the reserve, otherwise queue), which is a tested configuration.
- The row order and the hysteresis thresholds are DESIGN; the plan gives the rows and the triggers.
- The loss is a tool for comparing policies offline. Using it online to adapt the table would be an adaptive rule and falls under rule 3.
