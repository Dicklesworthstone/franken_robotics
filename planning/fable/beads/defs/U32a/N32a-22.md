## Background

The scheduler of the FrankenRobotics daemon has a handful of numbers in it, and at planning time every one of
them is a guess. The plan says so (each is labelled TARGET or DESIGN and "recalibrated at M2") and records the
question as **OPEN-TETHER-4**: *the slot reserve, per-environment job cap, quantum and ageing period, from the M2
queue trace.* Owner: the daemon lead. It blocks GATE-TETHER-10, the scheduling gate. Fail-closed default until
decided: **5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s**.

What the four numbers mean:
- **Slot reserve** (default 5% of an environment's slots): slots kept free for the interactive class, so that a
  short job starts at the next reset boundary instead of waiting for a slot. On the reference batch of 8,192
  worlds 5% is 410 slots.
- **Per-environment job cap** (default 64): the most jobs that may share one persistent environment; beyond it,
  work queues or is refused by the admission guard.
- **Quantum** (default at most 5 s): the spacing of snapshot boundaries at which a batch job can be parked, that
  is, the longest a batch job runs before a boundary at which it may be preempted.
- **Ageing period** (default 600 s): the time over which a waiting job's effective work decays linearly to zero,
  which bounds starvation: no job waits behind an unbounded stream of shorter ones.

The same trace also refits the weights of the dispatcher's loss function (interactive wait weight 1, batch wait
weight 0.1; DESIGN defaults) and re-reads the other TARGET settings of the scheduler: the interactive size-class
boundary (a point estimate of at most 30 s of device time), the utilization guard (0.8 per device), at most one
preemption per batch job per 30 s, a batch share of at least 30% of device time, and the "estimates unreliable"
rule (of the last 50 jobs, 20% ran over twice their estimate). These are reported, and changed only if the
evidence is clear.

This is an experiment bead: it produces a recorded decision, not a mechanism. It uses only artifacts that already
exist: the M2 daemon's job events as the trace, the queue simulator, and the pure decision function.

## Scope

In: the queue-trace format, its capture and extraction and its sufficiency rule; validation of the simulator
against the recorded waits; the loss with every term defined; the pre-registered parameter grid, the robustness
arrival processes and the admissibility constraints; the fit and validation protocol; the decision rule; the
sensitivity report; the decision record; the configuration change, if any. Out: the scheduler itself (BD-296);
the decision function (N32a-21); the simulator (N32a-20); the gate (BD-296 measures it with the parameters
decided here); the kill decisions KILL-SLOT and KILL-PREEMPT (N32a-23, N32a-24), which read the constants this
bead records.

## Technical approach

**Trace (schema `frob.queuetrace/1`, DESIGN; newline-delimited JSON, captured by the job engine per device from
M2 on).** One row per job from the daemon's ledger events: agent namespace (pseudonymized by a hash), admission
time (daemon clock, nanoseconds as a decimal string), verb, size class as later assigned, shape class, worlds
and slots requested, planned episodes, estimated work with its interval, actual attributable device time, start
time, end time, terminal state. One row per device-state sample: occupied slots, free slots, queue lengths by
class. No design, scene or task content. Wall-clock instants are operational data and never enter a result.
Extracted by `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example queuetrace -- --from <state root>` (the conformance crate
ships examples, not a command of its own). The peer-link pilot (N32a-34) reads the same schema.

**Sufficiency rule.** At least 2,000 jobs, at least 100 of them batch class, from at least 3 agents, spanning at
least 7 calendar days of use (DESIGN thresholds). Below that the defaults hold and the record says
`insufficient trace`.

**Loss, in seconds** (the dispatcher's own, as in the decision model N32a-21):

    L = Σ_j c(j)·wait_j + (idle slot-seconds ÷ B) + Σ_a max(0, deficit_a) + Σ preemption cost

with c(j) = 1 for interactive jobs and 0.1 for batch jobs (DESIGN defaults); B the batch capacity; deficit_a the
round-robin deficit of agent a beyond its quantum while others wait; and the cost of a preemption equal to its
measured snapshot and restore time.

**Replay.** The queue simulator feeds the trace's arrivals (same instants, sizes, agents and estimates) to the
real decision code with a candidate constant set, using measured service rates (step time by world count, from
the M2 profile) and the measured snapshot-and-restore time for preemptions. It emits per-job waits, per-step idle
slots, per-agent deficits and preemption events. Two further arrival processes test robustness: the trace
time-compressed to utilization 0.7 and to 0.8, and a bursty resampling that preserves the trace's squared
coefficient of variation of inter-arrival times c_a².

**Protocol, numbered (registered in the repository before the trace is read).** The plan fixes the question, its
owner, the defaults and that the M2 queue trace decides; the grid, the split and the decision rule are DESIGN.
1. Commit the registration file `registries/experiments/OPEN-TETHER-4.toml`: the grid, the split, the arrival
   processes, the loss, the obligations, the decision rule below, and the commit of the simulator and decision
   function.
2. Validate the simulator before it is used to choose: the replay of the trace under the constants actually in
   force during capture must reproduce the recorded per-job waits within 5% at the median. If it does not, the
   experiment stops and the simulator is repaired first.
3. Split the trace in time: the first 70% of jobs by admission time for fitting, the last 30% for validation (a
   time split, because usage drifts).
4. Grid: reserve ∈ {0, 2, 5, 10, 15}% of slots; job cap ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}; quantum ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} s; ageing
   period ∈ {150, 300, 600, 1,200} s: 320 points, each replayed on the three arrival processes (the trace as
   recorded, the time-compressed one, the bursty one). The interactive boundary, the guard and the rate limits
   stay at their defaults in the grid and are re-read separately in step 8.
5. For each grid point, replay the fitting split through the queue simulator driving the real decision function,
   with the measured snapshot and restore time as the preemption cost. Record the loss L and the obligations:
   interactive wait at the 95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10%; utilization under 0.8; no job
   beyond the ageing bound of its setting; at most one preemption per batch job per 30 s; batch class at least
   30% of device time; quantum at least twice the measured snapshot-and-restore time (otherwise preemption costs
   more than it yields). A point is admissible only if every obligation holds on all three arrival processes.
6. Keep the grid points that are admissible on the fitting split. Evaluate them on the validation split, where
   the loss of a point is the mean of L over the three arrival processes.
7. **Decision rule.** Adopt the point with the lowest validation loss among those that meet every obligation on
   both splits, but only if a block-bootstrap 95% lower bound (1,000 resamples, blocks of one day) of its loss
   improvement over the defaults is above zero. Otherwise keep the defaults. Two further brakes on moving a
   constant without evidence: (a) the default is kept for any constant whose default value lies in an admissible
   setting within 2% of the minimum; (b) the search is repeated with the batch weight c ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}, and
   a constant is changed from its default only if its chosen value is the same under all three. If the defaults
   themselves fail an obligation and no grid point passes, record which obligation fails for the best setting
   and keep the defaults: it is evidence for the kill gates (the scheduling gate will show the same failure and
   KILL-PREEMPT reads it), not a reason to widen the grid after the data.
8. Loss weights: refit the batch weight c as the ratio of mean batch-class to mean interactive-class tolerated
   wait observed in the trace (cancellations and resubmissions as the signal), reported with its interval; adopt
   only with the owner's sign-off, since it changes what the table optimizes. Report Kingman's factor
   (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²) from the trace, which the daemon's published capacity table applies to the queueing
   model's waits.
9. Write the decision record `docs/adr/OPEN-TETHER-4.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`) with the
   trace summary and digest, the 320 × 3 results (stored as a
   content-addressed artifact), the chosen point, the bootstrap interval, the sensitivity table (each constant's
   choice under the three batch weights) and the resulting configuration digest; write the chosen constants as
   the parameters of the dispatcher's table with the trace's digest; update the daemon's default configuration.

**What changes downstream.** The four numbers are configuration fields of the daemon; a change alters the
configuration digest that every job receipt records. GATE-TETHER-10 is then measured with the decided values,
and the decision-core record of DC-09 cites this record and holds the constants with the trace digest. The
dashboard and `job.list` read the constants from the daemon, never from a source of their own.

Determinism: the replay is a pure function of (trace, grid point, seeds); results are reproducible from the
registration. Evidence class of the decision: Estimated (a model fitted to a trace), stated in the record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration file is committed before the trace is extracted (commit order checked by the script).
- [ ] The trace meets the sufficiency rule and is stored with its digest, or the record says `insufficient trace` and the defaults (5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s) are confirmed.
- [ ] The replay of the trace under the constants in force during capture reproduces the recorded per-job waits within 5% at the median.
- [ ] All 320 grid points have a fitting result on each of the three arrival processes; every admissible point has a validation result.
- [ ] The decision rule is tested code and its output equals the recorded constants; the sensitivity table shows each constant's choice under the three batch weights.
- [ ] The dispatcher's table holds the constants with the trace digest; `frobd`'s configuration digest changes when a constant changes; a job receipt names the digest; the scheduling gate's record cites the constants in force.
- [ ] The decision record states the chosen values, the validation loss against the defaults, the bootstrap lower bound, and which obligations each of the top five points met.
- [ ] A planted trace generated from the simulator with known best parameters (reserve 10%, quantum 2 s) is recovered by the protocol (the experiment's own known answer).
- [ ] The daemon's default configuration equals the decided values and its digest is recorded.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance open_tether_4`)
- `trace_schema_roundtrip`; `trace_has_no_content_fields` (a lint on the extractor's output columns).
- `sufficiency_rule`: 1,999 jobs over 9 days → insufficient; 2,000 jobs with 99 batch → insufficient; 2,000 jobs over 6 days → insufficient; 2,000 with 100 batch from 3 agents over 7 days → sufficient.
- `time_split_70_30`: split by admission time, not at random.
- `grid_is_320`.
- `loss_terms_on_hand_trace`: two interactive jobs waiting 2 s and 4 s, one batch job waiting 100 s, 4,096 idle slot-seconds at B = 8,192, no deficit, one preemption costing 0.8 s: L = (2 + 4) + 0.1·100 + 0.5 + 0 + 0.8 = 17.3 s.
- `reserve_slots_rounding`: 5% of 8,192 is 409.6, reserved as 410; 2% is 164; 0% is 0.
- `ageing_key`: effective work of an 1,800 s job at ages 0, 150, 300 and 600 s under a 600 s period: 1,800, 1,350, 900, 0.
- `constraint_filter`: a setting with interactive 95th-percentile wait 6.1 s is inadmissible.
- `rule_keeps_default_within_two_percent`; `rule_no_admissible_setting_keeps_defaults`; `rule_requires_stability_across_batch_weights`.
- `kingman_factor_from_trace`: c_a² = 4 and c_s² = 8.07 give 1.331.
- `decision_rule_keeps_defaults_without_significant_gain`: a synthetic result table in which the best point's bootstrap lower bound is −0.3 s → defaults.
- `decision_rule_rejects_obligation_failure`: the lowest-loss point with interactive p95 of 7 s is not chosen.
- `planted_optimum_recovered` (synthetic trace, labelled `synthetic`).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/open_tether_4.sh --state-root <path> --seed 1` (header: `bead=N32a-22 milestone=M3
labels=tether,experiment,dispatch gates=GATE-TETHER-10 seed=1 needs=cpu
plants=late-registration,widened-grid,ignore-preempt-cost,no-ageing,undigested-constants`, each plant one
negative control below and each must be caught): checks the registration commit precedes extraction;
extracts the trace; runs the grid on both splits; applies the rule; writes the record. It sources the shared
helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/open_tether_4/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code and
wall time; one `metric` line per grid point and split (loss in seconds, interactive p95 against 6 s, batch
slowdown against 10%, utilization against 0.8, jobs beyond the ageing bound against 0); `metric` lines for the
bootstrap bound and the trace summary; `assert` lines for sufficiency and for the planted-optimum check; a
`summary` naming OPEN-TETHER-4 and GATE-TETHER-10 (parameters). The trace is logged only as summary statistics
and its digest; agent namespaces appear only as hashes. The script also logs the simulator-validation error
against 5% and, on failure, the first violated constraint or the first job whose replayed wait departs from the
record; after the decision it shows the daemon picking the constants up: `frob doctor --json` reports the
configuration digest (a new one if any constant changed) and the four keys with their decided values (N37-02's
surfacing of every configuration key, reached through N32a-14), and that digest equals the one the decision
record states. Reading
the constants from `job.list`, which the dashboard and the KILL-TUI fallback need, waits for its row to carry
them (requested of VERBS-job); the script does not call `job.list` for them.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the planted optimum is recovered on the synthetic trace.
- Known answers: the hand trace's loss 17.3 s; the 410-slot reserve; with 5% reserved, 40 comparisons of 192 slots fit in 8,192 with 102 slots to spare.
- Negative control: a simulator that ignores preemption cost prefers a 1 s quantum; with the measured cost included and the "quantum at least twice the cost" obligation, that setting must be inadmissible when the cost is 0.8 s.
- Negative control: a replay with ageing disabled must show a job beyond the ageing bound on the bursty process.
- Negative control: constants edited in the dispatcher's table without a trace digest are rejected by the table loader as stale against their contract.
- Negative controls: a registration committed after extraction (the order check must fail); a grid widened after results (the registration digest must not match and the script must fail); a random instead of a time split on a trace with drift (the validation loss must be seen to be optimistic, which the planted-drift test demonstrates).
- Determinism lanes: the grid results identical twice on one machine and on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The trace at M2 comes from per-job dispatch, a different regime from the one being tuned; the simulator bridges that, and the first weeks of M3 use are a second trace on which the decision is re-read (the record carries a re-read date).
- An agent swarm's arrivals are bursty; the trace's squared coefficients of variation are reported so that the capacity table's correction uses measured values.
- Tuning to a small trace is the main risk; the bootstrap rule and the default-keeping bias are the guard.
- If the snapshot and restore time measured at M2 exceeds every quantum in the grid, preemption cannot pay; that is the trigger of the kill gate KILL-PREEMPT and is recorded here for it.
- The search optimizes the dispatcher's loss, whose weights are themselves judgments. The sensitivity rule (change a constant only when the choice is stable across weights) keeps the experiment from laundering a weight into a "measured" constant.
- Nothing here may feed back into results: the constants change when work runs, never what is computed, and the slot-invariance and resume-digest checks hold for every setting in the grid by construction (PO-TETHER-05).
- An M2 trace reflects M2 usage: few agents, development workloads. The constants are re-examined when the swarm grows; the experiment is designed to be re-run on any later trace.
