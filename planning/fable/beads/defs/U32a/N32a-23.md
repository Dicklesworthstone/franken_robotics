## Background

A kill gate in this project is a decision written before the work starts: a trigger with its measurement, the
milestone at which it is read, and a fallback named in advance. This bead is the decision for **KILL-SLOT**.

The mechanism at stake: the FrankenRobotics daemon keeps one persistent environment per device and shape class
and dispatches episodes from many jobs into its free slots, instead of opening one environment per job. The bet
is that small jobs (a 96-pair comparison fills 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.3% of a batch) can share a device without
changing any world's result and without slowing the device. Both halves can fail: sharing is only legitimate if a
world's digest is independent of its co-tenants, and only worthwhile if the shared environment is nearly as fast
as a dedicated one.

The idea in full (ADR-TETHER-07): when a slot's episode ends, the dispatcher resets that slot with the next
admitted episode from any job of the same shape class; results are keyed by world address, never by slot or
arrival order. The motive is arithmetic on the reference batch of B = 8,192 worlds: a mission cell probe of 16
worlds fills 0.2% of a batch, a fixed-n study of 30 pairs 0.7%, an anytime-valid comparison at its cap of 96
pairs 2.3%, a 2,048-world run 25%. Without sharing, the rest of the device idles while small jobs queue.

The ceiling, stated before any speed test: the gain over one environment per job is at most min(k, 1 ÷ fill) for
k concurrent small jobs, so at most 42-fold for 192-slot jobs (8,192 ÷ 192 = 42.7), and nothing at all for a
single agent running one job at a time, who gains only the removal of environment creation from each call.
Against a right-sized environment per job the ceiling is the measured ratio of step time at 8,192 worlds to step
time at the job's own size, unknown until the M2 profile.

Why this is a kill gate and not a tuning question: a world's digest and results may depend on its address and
identities and never on its slot, its co-tenants, dispatch order, preemption or resumption. One digest difference
under co-tenancy means evidence depends on who else was running, which no other benefit can pay for.

Two chapters of the plan state this gate. The surface chapter governs; the device chapter is a consumer. The
surface chapter's limits contain the device chapter's (99th-percentile step time more than 10% above solo) and
add the 5% throughput limit and the optimization pass. The fallback is the same in both.

## Scope

In: the gate's definition as registered; the decision function; reading GATE-TETHER-09, deciding, recording the
decision, stating the downstream consequences on each branch (daemon, budgets, scheduling, dashboard), and
demonstrating the rollback. Out: the mechanism and its measurement (BD-295); the kernel-level invariance test
(GATE-SPARK-15, a precondition measured by BD-181); the register row's format and its entry before any
measurement (N32b-25); preempt-resume and its own kill gate (N32a-24); the reserve, cap, quantum and ageing
constants (N32a-22), which do not enter this trigger.

## Technical approach

**The gate.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Any digest difference under co-tenancy; or the throughput or step-time limits of GATE-TETHER-09 fail after one optimization pass |
| Decided on | GATE-TETHER-09 alone. GATE-SPARK-15 tests the same digest equality at kernel level and is a precondition, not a second trigger |
| Milestone | M3 |
| Measurement | Slot invariance: 1,000 episodes solo and in 100 random co-tenancies, identical digests. At 8,192 identical worlds: throughput loss of shared dispatch against a dedicated environment at most 5%; 99th-percentile step time at most 1.10 of solo (TARGET; both limits inclusive). The gate's admission-to-first-step percentiles (0.1, 0.5 and 2 s at the 50th, 95th and 99th percentiles for jobs of up to 256 worlds) are part of the gate and are recorded, but are not part of this trigger. |
| Fallback | `frobd --dispatch=per-job`: one environment per job, queued |
| Fail-closed default until decided | `per-job` dispatch for any run whose result is cited as evidence; slot dispatch only in shadow |

**Decision function** (DESIGN; pure; tested code in `crates/frob-conformance`; applied per device family).

```
decide(precondition_current: bool,          // GATE-SPARK-15 current for this driver and fingerprint
       digest_mismatches: u64,              // over 1,000 episodes × 100 random co-tenancies
       throughput_loss: f64,                // 1 − shared ÷ dedicated, at 8,192 identical worlds
       p99_step_ratio: f64,                 // shared ÷ solo
       optimization_passes_used: u8)        // 0 or 1, derived from the record's history, never declared
  -> does-not-fire | fires | undecided
```

- `precondition_current` false: `undecided` (the measurement is not admissible; the family runs per-job until
  the precondition is re-established).
- `digest_mismatches > 0`: fires. No optimization pass applies to a digest difference. A root-caused defect in a
  kernel is fixed under the kernel gate, the precondition is re-established, and the measurement is repeated from
  the start as a new record.
- `throughput_loss ≤ 0.05` and `p99_step_ratio ≤ 1.10`: does not fire.
- Limits missed and `optimization_passes_used = 0`: `undecided`; one optimization pass is allowed, with its
  changes listed in the record.
- Limits missed and `optimization_passes_used = 1`: fires.

**Decision procedure, numbered.**
1. Confirm the precondition: GATE-SPARK-15 has a current PASS on each device family concerned. If it does not,
   the decision is deferred for that family and the family runs per-job. Confirm also that the shadow rollout on
   `batch-cpu` ran (each job both ways, digests compared), with its count of jobs and mismatches.
2. Read the GATE-TETHER-09 record per device family. Check that it names the commit, the hardware fingerprint
   and the seed, and that its negative control (a kernel stand-in that reads a neighbouring slot's row) was
   caught in the same run; a record without the caught control is inadmissible.
3. *Digest clause.* One differing digest in the co-tenancy campaign fires the gate for that family. There is no
   optimization pass for a digest difference: the daemon switches to per-job at once and an alarm is raised; the
   difference itself is a defect to diagnose (a kernel reading a neighbour's row, a draw keyed by slot).
4. *Performance clauses.* If throughput loss exceeds 5% or the step-time ratio exceeds 1.10: one optimization
   pass is allowed (profile, change one lever at a time, re-measure, disclose). Re-read. Still failing: fire.
5. Record the decision in the kill-gate ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SLOT-M3.md` (every decision record lives in
   `docs/adr/`, named by gate and milestone, with N40-03's required fields), one decision section per device
   family: the record's hash, the branch, the measured mismatch count, throughput loss and step ratio, each beside
   its threshold, the optimization pass if one was used, the configuration digest. The ADR and the register row are written by a session that did not implement slot
   dispatch.
6. Demonstrate the fallback either way: with `--dispatch=per-job` the same jobs give identical per-world digests
   and the daemon passes its lifecycle script.

**What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Not fired | Shared dispatch is the default (`--dispatch=shared`, the mode name of BD-295); `per-job` stays as the rollback switch and as the runtime response to any digest alarm; size classes, the reserve, the per-environment job cap and preempt-resume build on it; the dashboard's slot strip shows co-tenancy; jobs are charged attributable device time (occupied slots ÷ capacity × step time) |
| Fired (a family, or all) | That family runs one environment per job, queued (`--dispatch=per-job` becomes its default). The persistent-environment code stays behind the switch for later re-measurement but is not used for evidence. The reserve, the per-environment job cap and the co-batch row of the dispatcher's guard table have nothing to act on and are disabled by configuration; scheduling reduces to ordering whole jobs (size classes and ageing still apply; preemption parks whole environments, if the preemption gate allows). Small jobs pay environment creation on each call unless a warm environment of their shape class is kept. Charges become whole-environment device time (a job is charged the device's whole step time while it holds it) and the daemon's overhead line holds only environment creation. The ceiling claims about sharing (up to 42-fold for 192-slot jobs under k concurrent small jobs) are withdrawn from every report, and the queueing waits of the capacity table are recomputed for whole-device service. The dashboard's Devices pane shows one job per environment. The scheduling gate's thresholds (GATE-TETHER-10) are re-read against the fallback and recorded with it. Co-batching of design variants inside one job is unaffected: that is the variant mechanism, not the dispatcher. Mechanisms that rest on batch-composition invariance for other reasons (teleport, forks, the memo graph) are unaffected by a performance failure, but a digest failure puts them in question too and is escalated |
| Runtime, either branch | A digest difference detected in operation (a resume or a shadow comparison) makes the dispatcher fall back to per-job dispatch at once and raise an alarm; that is the guard table's own rule and does not wait for a decision |

- [ ] If a performance clause failed first, the record shows exactly one optimization pass with its lever, before and after figures and disclosure block.
- [ ] The kill-gate register row carries the decision, date and commit; the daemon's default `--dispatch` per family matches it.
- [ ] The rollback demonstration is recorded: identical digests under `per-job` for a sample of 100 jobs.
- [ ] If fired, the dependent beads' records state the reduced configuration (reserve and co-batch disabled; charging rule changed).
- [ ] The decision function is tested code; applied to the recorded GATE-TETHER-09 values it returns the recorded branch.
- [ ] On the firing branch, `frobd` starts with per-job dispatch by default for the family, `session.show` charges whole-device time, and `frob capabilities` reports slot dispatch `killed` with the fallback named.
- [ ] On the surviving branch, `frobd --dispatch=per-job` still passes the job-protocol tests (the rollback is alive).
- [ ] The register row is filled by a fresh session and cites the gate receipt.

### End-to-end script and logging

gates=KILL-SLOT,GATE-TETHER-09 seed=23 needs=cpu,frobd plants=mislabelled-not-fired,second-pass,seed-mismatch,uncaught-cross-slot`,
each plant one negative control below and each must be caught): reads the gate record, applies the decision

`ks-<mode>-<i>`, so that the second mode does not return the first mode's stored receipts, and every envelope
must show `idempotent_replay: false`), and writes the ADR skeleton `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SLOT-M3.md`. NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/kill_slot_decision/`: `cmd` lines; `metric`
lines repeating the gate's measured values against their thresholds (0 differences; ≥ 0.95; ≤ 1.10); `assert`
lines for the precondition, the branch and digest equality under rollback; a `summary` naming KILL-SLOT and
GATE-TETHER-09. The script reads the gate record through `frob capabilities --json`, and on `batch-cpu` also runs
a 64-episode job solo and in 8 random co-tenancies under both dispatch modes (`frob sim run <design> --scene
<scene> --worlds 64 --seconds 20 --tier batch-cpu --key kc-<mode>-<tenancy> --json`, distinct keys for the same reason) and
compares per-world digests (`frob telemetry divergence`); it then checks the consequence of the recorded branch from the user's side (the
daemon's default mode; the charge reported by `frob session show`).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the six rows of the decision table.
- KA-761: 192 ÷ 8,192 = 2.34%.
- Negative controls: a record with one digest difference labelled `not fired` (the consistency test must fail); a second optimization pass recorded (the procedure check must fail); a rollback demonstration run with a different seed set from the shared run (the comparison harness must refuse to compare).
- Negative control: a second optimization pass presented as the first (two change lists in the record) is rejected, because `optimization_passes_used` is derived from the record's history.
- Negative control: the planted cross-slot read must appear as caught in the gate record, or the record is inadmissible.

## Considerations

- The digest clause has zero tolerance because a single difference means a world's result depended on its neighbours, which would undermine every paired comparison run under sharing.
- The optimization pass exists so that a first, untuned implementation does not kill a sound idea; it is one pass, declared, with the levers disclosed.
- The pilot for slot dispatch (a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu` with and without sharing) is evidence about the ceiling, not about this gate; a pilot that showed no gain would already have stopped the work before this decision.
- Deciding per device family is DESIGN (the plan states one gate and one fallback): sharing may hold on one backend and not the other, and a single global decision would discard a sound result on one of them.
- The reference rate used in the motivating arithmetic (2 × 10⁶ world-steps per second) is a target, not a measurement; the decision uses only measured ratios.
- A single agent running one job at a time gains nothing from slots; if the workload at M3 is that, firing costs little and the record should say so.
- The reserve, cap, quantum and ageing constants are under the open question OPEN-TETHER-4, decided from the M2 queue trace (N32a-22); they do not enter this trigger.
