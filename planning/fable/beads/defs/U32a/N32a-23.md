## Background

A kill gate in this project is a decision written before the work starts: a trigger with its measurement, the
milestone at which it is read, and a fallback named in advance. This bead is the decision for **KILL-SLOT**.

The mechanism at stake: the FrankenRobotics daemon keeps one persistent environment per device and shape class
and dispatches episodes from many jobs into its free slots, instead of opening one environment per job. The bet
is that small jobs (a 96-pair comparison fills 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.3% of a batch) can share a device without
changing any world's result and without slowing the device. Both halves can fail: sharing is only legitimate if a
world's digest is independent of its co-tenants, and only worthwhile if the shared environment is nearly as fast
as a dedicated one.

Two chapters of the plan state this gate. The surface chapter governs; the device chapter is a consumer. The
surface chapter's limits contain the device chapter's (99th-percentile step time more than 10% above solo) and
add the 5% throughput limit and the optimization pass. The fallback is the same in both.

## Scope

In: reading GATE-TETHER-09, deciding, recording the decision, stating the downstream consequences on each branch,
and demonstrating the rollback. Out: the mechanism and its measurement (BD-295); the kernel-level invariance test
(GATE-SPARK-15, a precondition measured by its own bead).

## Technical approach

**The gate.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Any digest difference under co-tenancy; or the throughput or step-time limits of GATE-TETHER-09 fail after one optimization pass |
| Decided on | GATE-TETHER-09 alone. GATE-SPARK-15 tests the same digest equality at kernel level and is a precondition, not a second trigger |
| Milestone | M3 |
| Measurement | Slot invariance: 1,000 episodes solo and in 100 random co-tenancies, identical digests. At 8,192 identical worlds: throughput loss of shared dispatch against a dedicated environment at most 5%; 99th-percentile step time at most 1.10 of solo. (The gate's admission-to-first-step percentiles are recorded but are not part of this trigger.) |
| Fallback | `frobd --dispatch=per-job`: one environment per job, queued |

**Decision procedure, numbered.**
1. Confirm the precondition: GATE-SPARK-15 has a current PASS on each device family concerned. If it does not,
   the decision is deferred for that family and the family runs per-job.
2. Read the GATE-TETHER-09 record per device family.
3. *Digest clause.* One differing digest in the co-tenancy campaign fires the gate for that family. There is no
   optimization pass for a digest difference: the daemon switches to per-job at once and an alarm is raised; the
   difference itself is a defect to diagnose (a kernel reading a neighbour's row, a draw keyed by slot).
4. *Performance clauses.* If throughput loss exceeds 5% or the step-time ratio exceeds 1.10: one optimization
   pass is allowed (profile, change one lever at a time, re-measure, disclose). Re-read. Still failing: fire.
5. Record the decision per device family as an ADR (`docs/adr/tether/KILL-SLOT.md`): the record's hash, the
   branch, the optimization pass if one was used, the configuration digest.
6. Demonstrate the fallback either way: with `--dispatch=per-job` the same jobs give identical per-world digests
   and the daemon passes its lifecycle script.

**What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Not fired | Shared dispatch is the default; size classes, the reserve and preempt-resume build on it; the dashboard's slot strip shows co-tenancy; jobs are charged attributable device time (occupied slots ÷ capacity × step time) |
| Fired (a family, or all) | That family runs one environment per job, queued. The reserve and the co-batch row of the dispatcher's guard table have nothing to act on and are disabled by configuration; scheduling reduces to ordering whole jobs (size classes and ageing still apply; preemption parks whole environments, if the preemption gate allows). Small jobs pay environment creation on each call unless a warm environment of their shape class is kept. Charges become whole-environment device time. The ceiling claims about sharing (up to 42-fold for 192-slot jobs under k concurrent small jobs) are withdrawn from every report. Mechanisms that rest on batch-composition invariance for other reasons (teleport, forks, the memo graph) are unaffected by a performance failure, but a digest failure puts them in question too and is escalated |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR for KILL-SLOT exists per device family with status `not fired` or `fired`, citing the GATE-TETHER-09 record and the GATE-SPARK-15 status.
- [ ] If a performance clause failed first, the record shows exactly one optimization pass with its lever, before and after figures and disclosure block.
- [ ] The kill-gate register row carries the decision, date and commit; the daemon's default `--dispatch` per family matches it.
- [ ] The rollback demonstration is recorded: identical digests under `per-job` for a sample of 100 jobs.
- [ ] If fired, the dependent beads' records state the reduced configuration (reserve and co-batch disabled; charging rule changed).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `decision_table`: inputs (precondition current?, digest differences, throughput loss, step-time ratio, pass used?) → branch. Examples: (yes, 0, 3%, 1.05, no) → not fired; (yes, 1, 0%, 1.00, no) → fired at once; (yes, 0, 7%, 1.05, no) → optimization pass; (yes, 0, 7%, 1.05, yes) → fired; (yes, 0, 4%, 1.12, yes) → fired; (no, –, –, –, –) → deferred, per-job.
- `adr_status_consistent_with_record`: an ADR saying `not fired` while the cited record has a failing clause fails the consistency test.
- `default_dispatch_matches_decision`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_slot_decision.sh`: reads the gate record, applies the decision table, runs the rollback
demonstration (`frobd --dispatch=per-job` on a 100-job sample and a digest comparison against the shared-mode
results), and writes the ADR skeleton. NDJSON under `target/e2e-logs/kill_slot_decision/`: `cmd` lines; `metric`
lines repeating the gate's measured values against their thresholds (0 differences; ≥ 0.95; ≤ 1.10); `assert`
lines for the precondition, the branch and digest equality under rollback; a `summary` naming KILL-SLOT and
GATE-TETHER-09.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the six rows of the decision table.
- Negative controls: a record with one digest difference labelled `not fired` (the consistency test must fail); a second optimization pass recorded (the procedure check must fail); a rollback demonstration run with a different seed set from the shared run (the comparison harness must refuse to compare).

## Considerations

- The digest clause has zero tolerance because a single difference means a world's result depended on its neighbours, which would undermine every paired comparison run under sharing.
- The optimization pass exists so that a first, untuned implementation does not kill a sound idea; it is one pass, declared, with the levers disclosed.
- The pilot for slot dispatch (a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu` with and without sharing) is evidence about the ceiling, not about this gate; a pilot that showed no gain would already have stopped the work before this decision.
- Deciding per device family is DESIGN (the plan states one gate and one fallback): sharing may hold on one backend and not the other, and a single global decision would discard a sound result on one of them.
