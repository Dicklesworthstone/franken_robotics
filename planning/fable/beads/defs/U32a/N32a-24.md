## Background

The scheduler of the FrankenRobotics daemon parks a long (batch-class) job at a snapshot boundary so that a short
(interactive) job can run, and resumes the long job later. Jobs are sorted into size classes by the system's own
estimate (`interactive`: at most 30 s of device time; `batch`; `campaign`). The queueing arithmetic behind it (a
model, not a measurement): on one device at utilization 0.7 with 90% of jobs 10 s and 10% of jobs 30 min
(E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s²), a 10 s job waits about 2,001 s first come first
served, 621 s with non-preemptive priority, and 0.17 s plus one quantum (at most 5 s) with preempt-resume; long
jobs slow by about 3.4%. The wait under priority is the remainder of the long job already on the device, and only
preemption removes it.

The same analysis names the conditions under which the conclusion flips: if a resume costs several seconds, or a
device is reserved for interactive work, running to completion is right. And preemption is only legitimate if a
resumed job is the same job: its per-world digests after resume must equal those of an uninterrupted run. The
snapshot must therefore include everything that carries state across a step: solver warm starts, packet queues
and stream counters.

The two halves of the trigger follow from that. A resumed job whose digest differs means the scheduler changed
what was computed, which breaks the rule that the scheduler decides when work runs and never what (ADR-TETHER-08;
INV-TETHER-09). A resume that costs more than the quantum means the cure costs more device time than the wait it
removes.

**KILL-PREEMPT** is the decision, written in advance, on whether preempt-resume survives its measurement.

## Scope

In: the gate's definition as registered; the decision function; reading the measurement, deciding, recording,
stating the consequences (the dispatcher's guard table, the capacity table, the scheduling gate, the dashboard),
demonstrating the fallback. Out: the scheduler and the scheduling gate's measurement (BD-296); the decision model
(N32a-21); the queueing model (N32a-20); the register row's format and its entry before any measurement
(N32b-25); slot dispatch and KILL-SLOT (N32a-23), which is decided first because this gate's fallback names the
slot reserve.

## Technical approach

**The gate.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | A resumed job's digest differs; or measured resume cost exceeds the quantum |
| Milestone | M3, after KILL-SLOT |
| Measurement | (a) Digest equality: batch jobs parked and resumed at random snapshot boundaries, per-world digests compared with an uninterrupted run of the same job (part of the scheduling gate's "random preemption and reordering leave every digest and verdict identical"). (b) Resume cost: wall time of snapshot plus restore per preemption of a batch job at the reference batch, per device family and shape class, at the 50th, 95th and 99th percentiles over at least 200 preempt-resume cycles, against the quantum. DESIGN: the plan says only "measured resume cost"; the count of 200 and the 99th percentile as the deciding statistic are this bead's choices, and the 95th is recorded beside it |
| Quantum | At most 5 s (TARGET); a constant under OPEN-TETHER-4, read from the decision-core table DC-09 at decision time (the decided value comes from the queue-trace experiment N32a-22) |
| Fallback | Run to completion with the slot reserve only |
| Fail-closed default until decided | Run to completion; preemption only in the scheduling gate's own measurement runs |

**Decision function** (DESIGN; pure; tested code in `crates/frob-conformance`; applied per device family).

```
decide(resume_digest_mismatches: u64, cycles: u64, resume_cost_p99_s: f64, quantum_s: f64, flip_point_s: Option<f64>)
  -> does-not-fire | fires(digest) | fires(cost) | undecided
```

- `resume_digest_mismatches > 0`: fires (digest), whatever the cycle count.
- `cycles < 200`: `undecided` (not enough resumes to speak about cost).
- `resume_cost_p99_s > quantum_s`: fires (cost). A cost equal to the quantum does not fire by this clause.
- `flip_point_s` present and `resume_cost_p99_s > flip_point_s`: fires (cost), by step 3 of the procedure.
- Otherwise: does not fire.

**Decision procedure, numbered.**
1. Read the scheduling gate's record per device family (GATE-TETHER-10: on a replayed mix with 90% interactive
   jobs at utilization 0.7, interactive wait at the 95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10%; no
   job beyond the ageing bound; random preemption and reordering leave every digest and verdict identical) and
   the resume-cost samples. Check commit, hardware fingerprint, seed and the caught negative control (a resume
   that restores a stale warm start). Read the quantum in force from the decision-core table.
2. *Digest clause.* One differing digest fires the gate for that family, with no repair cycle inside the gate:
   preemption is disabled at once (the dispatcher's own fallback already does this when it observes a
   difference) and the difference is diagnosed as a defect of the snapshot's contents.
3. *Cost clause.* Compare the 99th-percentile resume cost with the quantum. If it exceeds the quantum, fire for
   that family and shape class. If it is below the quantum but above the flip point reported by the queueing
   model's sweep (the resume cost at which run-to-completion has the lower loss on the measured mix), fire as
   well and record that the cost, not the digest, decided (DESIGN reading of "the result flips if resume costs
   several seconds").
4. Write the kill-gate ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-PREEMPT-M3.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`, named
   by gate and milestone, with N40-03's required fields), one decision section per device family: the record's
   hash, both measurements
   (mismatch count, number of cycles, the three percentiles of resume cost, the quantum in force), the branch,
   the configuration digest. The ADR and the register row are written by a session that did not implement
   preemption. Amend the decision-core table DC-09 for the branch taken.
5. Demonstrate the fallback: with preemption disabled, the daemon passes the scheduling script's digest
   campaign, and the interactive wait distribution is recorded for the reserve-only configuration.

**What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Not fired | Preempt-resume stays in the guard table with its limits: when no slot is free and an interactive job waits, the dispatcher uses the reserve and, if it is full, preempts the batch job with the most remaining work at its next snapshot boundary; at most one preemption per batch job per 30 s; the batch class keeps at least 30% of device time (TARGET); the quantum as decided. The rate limit and the batch floor are enforced and reported |
| Fired | The `Preempt` action is removed from the dispatcher's guard table by configuration: a saturated environment uses the reserve and otherwise queues; batch jobs run to completion. Interactive protection is the 5% slot reserve and the size-class order within the queue. The job state `preempted` is reached only by a graceful daemon stop. The interactive-wait clause of the scheduling gate (95th percentile at most 6 s) is re-read under the fallback and recorded against it with its measured value; if it no longer holds, the gate is recorded `killed` with this fallback in the row's detail, not passed. The capacity table the daemon publishes switches to the priority-with-reserve column of the model (for the example mix: 439, 621, 713 and 806 s at utilization 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, for interactive work that does not fit the reserve). The decision core's record states that its fallback is in force. Snapshot code remains, because replay, forks and checkpoints need it; only its use by the dispatcher is withdrawn. The dashboard's preemption column shows zero and the Queue pane's mode line says "run to completion". Size classes, fair ordering, ageing and the admission guard are unaffected |
| If KILL-SLOT fired first | There is no slot reserve. The fallback then reads: run to completion, one environment per job, interactive jobs ordered first in the queue. The record states this combined state explicitly |
| Runtime, either branch | A resume whose digest differs in operation makes the dispatcher fall back at once to round-robin per agent, run to completion and per-job dispatch, and raise an alarm; that is the guard table's own rule and does not wait for a decision |

- [ ] The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-PREEMPT-M3.md` exists with one decision section per device family, each with status `not fired` or `fired` (and which clause), citing the scheduling gate's record.
- [ ] The measured resume cost (50th and 99th percentiles, per device family and shape class) and the quantum are in the record with their units.
- [ ] The kill-gate register row carries the decision, date and commit; the daemon's default configuration matches it.
- [ ] The fallback demonstration is recorded: zero digest differences and the interactive wait distribution under reserve-only.
- [ ] If fired, the capacity table and the decision-core record show the fallback as in force.
- [ ] The decision function is tested code; applied to the recorded values it returns the recorded branch.
- [ ] On the firing branch: the guard table in `registries/cores/` has no preempt action; a saturated device with a waiting interactive job and a full reserve queues it; `frob capabilities` reports preempt-resume `killed` with the fallback named; the capacity table is regenerated.
- [ ] On the surviving branch: the rate limit and the batch floor are observable in `job.list` over a replayed mix.
- [ ] If KILL-SLOT fired, the record states the combined fallback (run to completion, one environment per job, interactive first).
- [ ] The register row is filled by a fresh session.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the five rows of the decision table; the model's ordering at ρ = 0.7 (621 s under priority only against 0.17 s plus the quantum under preempt-resume) as the expectation the measurement is read against.
- KA-758: the M/G/1 table (first come first served 857, 2,001, 3,430, 7,716 s; priority only 439, 621, 713, 806 s; preempt-resume 0.12, 0.17, 0.20, 0.22 s plus one quantum; long-job slowdown 2.4%, 3.4%, 4.0%, 4.5%, at utilization 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9).
- Negative controls: a record with a differing digest labelled `not fired` (the consistency test must fail); a resume-cost figure taken from the model instead of a measurement (the record schema requires a measurement with its hardware fingerprint, and the check must fail).
- Negative control (CX-351): a dispatcher with preemption removed must show the priority-only waits in the simulation; if it showed the preempt-resume waits, the simulation is not measuring preemption.
- Negative control: resume cost computed as a mean when the samples have a long tail (mean 3 s, 95th percentile 7 s; quantum 5 s; no flip point, so that only the quantum clause can decide) must still fire, because the deciding statistic is the 99th percentile; the mean-based reading would not fire.

## Considerations

- A digest difference on resume is more serious than this gate: it means a snapshot is incomplete, which also threatens checkpoints used for crash recovery, forks and teleport. The ADR must name the missing state and the beads that share the snapshot.
- Preemption can be sound on one device family and too costly on another (snapshot copies are cheap in one memory model and not in another); the decision is per family (DESIGN).
- The fallback keeps interactive work responsive only through the reserve; under a saturated reserve an interactive job waits for a batch job's natural end, which is the 621 s regime of the model. The dashboard's queue pane must show which regime is in force.
- The gate can be re-read after a snapshot optimization; the fallback is a configuration, not a deletion.
- The model that motivates preemption assumes Poisson arrivals; a swarm's arrivals burst, and the daemon scales waits by Kingman's factor (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²) from its own trace. The decision uses measurements only.
- A resume cost just under the quantum passes the first cost clause but may still make preemption a poor trade; the loss function of the dispatcher (interactive wait at weight 1, batch wait at 0.1, plus preemption cost) is where that is weighed, its weights are refitted from the M2 queue trace, and the flip-point clause of step 3 is its reading here.
- No adaptive scheduling rule ships without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback; this gate is the demonstrated fallback for preemption.
