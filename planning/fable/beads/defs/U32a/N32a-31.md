## Background

`solve.run` is a proposed Frontier verb of FrankenRobotics: one call that compiles a task, constraints and a
budget into the sequence check, sweep under the baseline controller, race, promote, and returns a ranked set of
designs with verdicts. It is orchestration, not a mechanism: every number in its answer comes from the verbs it
calls, and the co-design cascade itself is run by another verb (`opt.cascade`), which `solve.run` calls. Its
value, if any, is that an agent spends fewer serial turns per accepted design, and an agent turn is slow (the
plan's working figure is 30 to 120 s of thinking per turn, a hypothesis).

The hypothesis to test later (in the one A/B of agent surfaces at M4) is "about five-fold fewer serial agent
turns per accepted design". The project's rule for Frontier work is that the **ceiling is stated before any
test**, from a pilot cheap enough to do on paper, with a falsifier.

- **Ceiling:** the fraction of turns in recorded sessions that carry no decision (pure plumbing). If a fraction p
  of turns is plumbing and a one-call form removed every one of them, turns per accepted design fall by at most
  1 ÷ (1 − p). At p = 0.30 that is 1 ÷ 0.70 = 1.43.
- **Falsifier:** if that fraction is below 30%, the ceiling is under 1.4-fold and the work package is not funded.
- **Pilot:** replay 20 recorded sessions on paper and count plumbing turns.
- **Kill gate:** KILL-SOLVE; fallback: the MCP workflow prompts, which cost nothing to keep.

For scale: the hoped-for five-fold reduction needs p = 0.80.

## Scope

In: the session sample; the operational definition of a plumbing turn; the counting protocol with two
independent raters and a mechanical classifier; the ceiling computation with its interval; the pilot record and
verdict. Out: building `solve.run` (BD-372, which depends on this pilot); the A/B that tests the hypothesis
(another unit's bead and the mission-session gate); the kill decision (N32a-32).

## Technical approach

**Sample.** 20 session transcripts in which a fresh agent reached an accepted design, drawn from the recorded
sessions of the studio gate and of the partial ten-fold gate's own arm. Selection rule, fixed before reading
them: the first 20 by recording time that (a) ended with a promoted or qualified design, (b) used at least 8
verb calls, and (c) cover at least 4 different task families, with at most 6 sessions from any one family
(DESIGN). If fewer than 20 qualify, the pilot waits; it is not run on a smaller sample.

**Unit.** A *turn* is one agent decision point: the agent reads an answer and issues the next call (or batch of
parallel calls, which count as one turn). Turns are read from the session transcript record (sequence number,
verb, resolved request digest, envelope digest, exit code).

**Definition (written before the transcripts are read).** A turn is **plumbing** if the next call is fully
determined by a fixed rule from the task specification and the previous envelopes, with no choice among
alternatives. Concretely, a turn is plumbing when all of these hold: (1) the verb called is the next step of the
registered workflow for the task; (2) every argument is either copied from an earlier envelope (`bound`
identifiers, a job handle, the top-ranked `next_actions` entry) or is the workflow's default; (3) the previous
answer offered no ranked alternatives among which the agent's call picked other than the first; (4) the previous
exit code was 0, 2 with no finding requiring a choice, or 8. A turn is a **decision** otherwise: choosing which
repair to apply when several are ranked, choosing a budget or world count, choosing to stop, choosing among
candidates, reacting to a refusal with a changed question. Polling turns (`job_wait` after exit 8) are plumbing.

**Protocol, numbered.**
1. Commit the registration `registries/pilots/solve-run.toml`: the ceiling formula, the falsifier (p < 0.30),
   the selection rule, the definition above and the classifier's commit.
2. Mechanical pass: a classifier in `frob-conformance` applies conditions (1) to (4) to each turn of each
   transcript and labels it.
3. Two independent raters (fresh-context sessions that did not write the classifier) label the same turns from
   the transcripts and the definition alone.
4. Agreement: Cohen's κ between the raters, and between each rater and the classifier. If any κ is below 0.7
   the definition is ambiguous: the disagreements are listed, the definition is tightened once, and step 3 is
   repeated with new raters; the first-round labels stay in the record.
5. Adjudicate remaining disagreements by majority of the three labels.
6. Compute, per session and pooled over all turns: p = plumbing turns ÷ turns; the ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − p); a 95%
   interval for the pooled p by bootstrap over sessions (10,000 resamples of the 20 sessions).
7. Verdict: `fund` if the pooled p is at least 0.30; `do not fund` if below. The record also states what
   reduction the pooled p would allow at best, next to the five-fold hypothesis, so that the later A/B is read
   against a stated ceiling.

**Record.** Per session: turns, plumbing turns, decision turns, polling turns; pooled p and its interval; the
ceiling; the κ values; the verdict.

Determinism: the mechanical pass is a pure function of the transcripts. Evidence class: `Estimated` (a count on
a sample of sessions), stated in the record; it is a ceiling, not a measured speedup.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration (ceiling, falsifier, selection rule, definition) is committed before any transcript is opened by a rater; the script checks commit order.
- [ ] Exactly 20 sessions meeting the selection rule are in the sample, with their transcript digests.
- [ ] Every turn of every session has three labels and an adjudicated label; κ values are reported; if a second round was needed, both rounds are in the record.
- [ ] The pooled plumbing fraction, its bootstrap interval and the ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − p) are recorded, and the verdict follows the rule (p < 0.30 → `do not fund`).
- [ ] The classifier is validated on synthetic transcripts with known labels (a pure-plumbing workflow → p = 1; a session where every call follows a refusal with a changed question → p = 0).
- [ ] The record is handed to the kill-gate decision and cited by the mechanism bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilot_solve`)
- `ceiling_formula`: p = 0.30 → 1.4286; p = 0.80 → 5.0; p = 0 → 1.0.
- `classifier_pure_plumbing`: a synthetic transcript that follows the studio workflow with default arguments and top-ranked actions → every turn plumbing.
- `classifier_decision_on_choice`: the agent applies the second-ranked repair → that turn is a decision.
- `classifier_decision_after_refusal`: a 701 refusal followed by a smaller question → decision.
- `polling_is_plumbing`: `job_wait` after exit 8.
- `parallel_calls_one_turn`.
- `kappa_computation`: a hand-worked 2 × 2 table (40 agree-plumbing, 30 agree-decision, 5 and 5 disagree → κ = 0.75).
- `selection_rule_applied`: a pool of 30 synthetic sessions yields the first 20 qualifying, with the per-family cap.
- `bootstrap_interval_reproducible` with a fixed seed.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_solve_run.sh --seed 1`: checks the registration's commit order; selects the sample; runs the
mechanical pass; ingests the two raters' label files; computes agreement, p, the interval and the ceiling;
writes the record. It sources the shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_solve_run/<utc>.ndjson` plus a
transcript: `cmd` lines; per session `metric` lines (turns, plumbing, decision, polling, p); pooled `metric`
lines (p with its interval, ceiling, κ values against the threshold 0.7); an `assert` line for the verdict
against the falsifier; a `summary` naming KILL-SOLVE (ceiling clause). Session content is referenced by digest.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the ceiling values above; κ = 0.75 for the hand-worked table.
- Negative controls: a definition edited after the raters started (the registration digest must not match and the script must fail); a classifier that counts every turn as plumbing (the synthetic decision cases must fail); a sample padded with sessions that did not end in an accepted design (the selection check must reject them).
- Determinism lanes: the mechanical pass and the bootstrap identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The ceiling is generous on purpose: it assumes the one-call form removes every plumbing turn and adds none. The real gain is lower, which is why a ceiling under 1.4-fold ends the work before it starts.
- Sessions recorded at M3 are from agents using the workflow prompts; if prompts already removed most plumbing, p will be low, and that is a true statement about what `solve.run` could still add.
- Turn counts are not wall time; the A/B measures time to an accepted answer. The pilot only bounds what turns could contribute.
- The 30 to 120 s per agent turn figure is a hypothesis measured elsewhere; this pilot does not depend on it.
