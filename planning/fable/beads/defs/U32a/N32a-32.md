## Background

A kill gate in this project is a decision written before the work starts: a trigger with its measurement, a
milestone, and a fallback named in advance. This bead is the decision for **KILL-SOLVE**.

`solve.run` is the agent-facing one-call pipeline of FrankenRobotics (tier Frontier): from a task specification
and a budget to a ranked set of designs with verdicts, by calling the co-design cascade verb `opt.cascade` and
the ordinary verbs. It adds no evidence of its own. The only reason to keep it is that agents reach an accepted
design in fewer serial turns with it than by driving the verbs themselves. The plan's hypothesis is about
five-fold fewer; its fallback, if the verb does not earn its place, is the set of MCP workflow prompts, which
describe the same sequence as guidance and cost nothing to keep (`studio_loop`, `compare_designs`,
`mission_query`).

What the verb does: it compiles a task, constraints and a budget into the sequence check, sweep under the
baseline controller, race, promote, and returns a ranked set of designs with verdicts. It is orchestration, not a
mechanism: every number in its answer comes from the verbs it calls. The reasoning behind the hypothesis
(HYPOTHESIS): an agent turn is believed to cost 30 to 120 s of thinking, so turns spent on plumbing (calling the
next obvious verb with the previous verb's output) dominate wall time and can be removed by one call.

The ceiling is stated before any test, as the project's rule on speed claims requires: the benefit is bounded by
the fraction of turns in recorded sessions that carry no decision. Removing all of them gives at most
1 ÷ (1 − p). Below p = 0.30 the ceiling is under 1.4-fold (1 ÷ 0.7 = 1.43) and the work is not funded; the
hoped-for five-fold needs p = 0.80.

There is **one A/B, not two**: the one-verb arm of the surface A/B on the design benchmark (the gate
GATE-ATEL-03, which tests the bet that agents design better with structure than with reward alone) is the second
arm of the mission-session gate GATE-TETHER-13. KILL-SOLVE is decided on it.

## Technical approach

**The gate.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The ceiling is under 1.4-fold; or agents with `solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs |
| Clause 1 (ceiling), read before the verb is built | The pilot's pooled fraction p of plumbing turns in 20 recorded sessions; the ceiling is 1 ÷ (1 − p); p below 0.30 gives a ceiling under 1 ÷ 0.70 = 1.43-fold (the plan's "1.4-fold", rounded), and p, not the rounded ceiling, is what is compared |
| Clause 2 (A/B), read after | In the surface A/B on the design benchmark: serial turns per accepted design in the arm with `solve.run` available, against the arm driving the verbs, at equal compute and tokens. The second arm of GATE-TETHER-13 states it: with `solve.run` available, serial turns per accepted design fall by the pre-registered factor, or KILL-SOLVE fires |
| Milestone | Clause 1 before `solve.run` is funded (the pilot); clause 2 at M4 (the A/B) |
| Fallback | The prompts |
| Fail-closed default until decided | No `solve.run`; agents use the verbs and the prompts |

**Decision function** (DESIGN; pure; tested code in `crates/frob-conformance`).

```
decide(stage1: proceed | fired(ceiling) | undecided | absent,     // N32ap1-01's clause-1 entry, as written
       stage2: {verdict: better | not-better | inconclusive, factor_registered: f64,
                ratio_point: f64, ratio_interval: (f64, f64)} | not-run)
  -> does-not-fire | fires(at stage 1 | at stage 2) | undecided
```

Stage 1 is not recomputed here: its input is the entry N32ap1-01 wrote with `decide_stage1` (the pilot's p,
the conservative recount and the ceiling are in that entry).
- Clause-1 entry absent or `undecided`: `undecided`; `solve.run` is not funded.
- Clause-1 entry `fired(ceiling)`: fires at stage 1, the outcome that entry wrote. The verb is not built.
- Clause-1 entry `proceed`: the verb is funded and stage 2 applies. Stage 2 not run: `undecided`.
- Stage 2 verdict `better` at the registered factor: does not fire.
- Stage 2 verdict `not-better` or `inconclusive` at the registered factor: fires at stage 2. An inconclusive A/B
  does not keep a Frontier verb alive: the burden is on the claim.
- Mapping to N40-03's four outcomes: does-not-fire is `not-fired`; fires is `fired`, with the clause (`ceiling` or
  `A/B`) named in the ADR and the fallback `prompts`; `undecided` is `not-reached` when a stage was not run and
  `deferred` when a record was inadmissible.

**Admissibility of the two records** (checked before the function is applied; an inadmissible record gives
`undecided`).
- Stage 1: checked by N32ap1-01 before the verb was funded (registration before counting; the registered
  definition used by the classifier and both raters; the selection rule applied, not sessions picked after
  reading them; p recomputed from the label files under the adjudicated and the conservative reading, the
  conservative one deciding when the two fall on different sides of 0.30; the search for plumbing-labelled turns
  whose reason names an alternative). This bead does not repeat them: it checks that the clause-1 entry exists,
  cites PILOT-SOLVE-01's result by digest and is consistent with it (N32ap1-01's `adr_status_consistent_with_record`,
  re-run with the whole file), and copies the entry's figures into the ADR's final status.
- Stage 2: the factor was registered before the A/B's first episode (ledger order); both arms had equal budgets
  and the same briefs; turns are counted by the session transcript capture, not by self-report.

**The pre-registered factor.** It is fixed in the A/B's registration before any session is run, and it may not
exceed the pilot's ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − p) (a factor above the ceiling could never be met and would decide the gate
by construction). If the registration names none, the fail-closed default is 1.5 (DESIGN: the same effect size
the A/B requires for its primary comparison of restricted mean time), and "no better" in the trigger means the
one-sided 95% lower bound of the turn ratio is below 1.

**Decision procedure, numbered.**
1. *Clause 1.* Read the clause-1 entry of N32ap1-01 (which applied the stage-1 function and the admissibility
   checks above to the pilot record). `fired: ceiling`: the verb was never built; keep the outcome `fired`
   (clause `ceiling`) that entry wrote; skip to step 5.
2. *Clause 2.* After the verb exists and the A/B has run: read, for the one-verb arm and the hand-driven arm,
   the per-brief serial turns per accepted design, the ratio and its interval, and restricted mean time to an
   accepted answer (reported beside it).
3. Fire if the lower bound of the turn ratio is below 1 (no better), or if the point estimate of the ratio is
   below the pre-registered factor. Otherwise do not fire.
4. Guard against a hollow win: if turns fell but the fraction of accepted designs that survive the A/B's
   adjudication fell too (agents accepted worse designs faster), the comparison is read on *adjudicated*
   accepted designs only; a verb that reaches fewer correct designs is "no better".
5. Write the ADR (`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SOLVE-M4.md`, with N40-03's required fields): which clause was read, the
   figures (sessions, turns, plumbing
   turns, rater agreement, p under both readings, the ceiling; at stage 2 the registered factor, the measured
   ratio with its interval and the A/B's verdict; the measured agent turn time from the OPEN-TETHER-3 record of
   N32a-33, with its rule-4 finding if it applied), the registration's digest, the branch. The ADR and the
   register row are written by a session that did not build `solve.run`.

**What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Not fired | `solve.run` stays registered (tier Frontier) in the `search` task cluster; the guide presents it as the one-call form with its measured turn ratio, its interval and the ceiling, in place of the five-fold hypothesis; it is never counted as evidence, only as orchestration |
| Fired on clause 1 | The verb is never built: the bead that would build it is closed as not built, with the fallback's name; its registry entry is removed before the M4 verb set is frozen and the name retired to `opt.cascade` (N32ap1-01's branch, VERBS-solve's removal rule); `opt_cascade` takes the vacated seventh seat of the `search` cluster (NJL-05); the workflow prompts are the documented route. The cascade verb `opt.cascade` is unaffected. The pilot's p and the ceiling stay on record |
| Fired on clause 2 | The verb is retired: its entry is removed at the next registry freeze (or kept only as a convenience outside every claim, if the owner prefers; the default is removal, with the name entering NJL-04's retired-name table with `use = ["opt.cascade"]`, so that the verb answers `FROB-REF-801` naming `opt.cascade`, and `opt_cascade` taking the vacated seat in `search`, as VERBS-solve's removal rule states), the MCP tool is gone, `frob capabilities` reports it `killed` with the fallback "prompts", the guide and prompts no longer mention it and `robot-docs` teaches the prompts, the cascade verb is unaffected, and the A/B's record states that the one-verb form "stays a convenience and is not counted as a lever". The second arm of the mission-session gate is recorded with its measured ratio and the gate's first arm stands on its own |

In every branch the prompts remain, and the co-design cascade (`opt.cascade`) is judged on its own gate.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the six rows of the decision table; ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = 1.43; a five-fold ceiling needs p = 0.80.
- Negative controls of stage 1, N32ap1-01's and re-run with its tests: a pilot whose sessions were selected after reading them (no selection rule on record) is inadmissible, so its entry is `undecided` and so is this decision; counting every turn that calls a `read` verb as plumbing inflates p, and the classifier examples include read turns that are decisions and must not be so counted.
- Negative controls: an ADR `not fired` citing a ratio whose lower bound is below 1 (the consistency test must fail); a factor registered after the first session (the registration's commit order check, owned by the A/B, must fail and this script must refuse to decide); a "win" in turns with fewer adjudicated-correct designs read as a pass (step 4's guard must catch it).

## Considerations

- Turns are the gate's quantity because the hypothesis is about serial agent turns; time to an accepted answer is what the A/B's primary comparison uses and is reported beside it so the two cannot be confused.
- The trigger's second clause is deliberately stricter than "statistically better": a small real gain that misses the pre-registered factor still retires the verb, because the surface must stay small (program risk: the agent surface is too large).
- If the A/B cannot be run at full size for want of an approved compute class, clause 2 is read on the campaign that was run, with its wider interval; an interval whose lower bound is below 1 fires the gate. A verb is not kept on an underpowered comparison.
- The hypothesis's own inputs (30 to 120 s per turn) are unmeasured until the wait-parameter experiment (N32a-33) and the agent gates run; if turns are cheap, even a real reduction in turns buys little time. The gate is about turns because that is what was claimed.
- An orchestration verb hides intermediate evidence from the agent. On the surviving branch its answer must still carry every called verb's evidence block and handles, and the budget it spent per stage.
- If the cascade it calls is itself narrowed at its own gates, `solve.run` inherits the narrower pipeline.
