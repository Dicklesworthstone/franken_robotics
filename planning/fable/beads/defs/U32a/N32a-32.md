## Background

A kill gate in this project is a decision written before the work starts: a trigger with its measurement, a
milestone, and a fallback named in advance. This bead is the decision for **KILL-SOLVE**.

`solve.run` is the agent-facing one-call pipeline of FrankenRobotics (tier Frontier): from a task specification
and a budget to a ranked set of designs with verdicts, by calling the co-design cascade verb `opt.cascade` and
the ordinary verbs. It adds no evidence of its own. The only reason to keep it is that agents reach an accepted
design in fewer serial turns with it than by driving the verbs themselves. The plan's hypothesis is about
five-fold fewer; its fallback, if the verb does not earn its place, is the set of MCP workflow prompts, which
describe the same sequence as guidance and cost nothing to keep.

There is **one A/B, not two**: the one-verb arm of the surface A/B on the design benchmark (the gate
GATE-ATEL-03, which tests the bet that agents design better with structure than with reward alone) is the second
arm of the mission-session gate GATE-TETHER-13. KILL-SOLVE is decided on it.

## Scope

In: reading the two measurements (the paper ceiling; the A/B's one-verb arm), deciding, recording, and the
consequences on each branch. Out: the pilot (N32a-31); the verb (BD-372); the A/B and its other arms (the
Atelier unit); the prompts (N32a-26).

## Technical approach

**The gate.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The ceiling is under 1.4-fold; or agents with `solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs |
| Clause 1 (ceiling), read before the verb is built | The pilot's pooled fraction p of plumbing turns in 20 recorded sessions; the ceiling is 1 ÷ (1 − p); p below 0.30 gives a ceiling under 1.4-fold |
| Clause 2 (A/B), read after | In the surface A/B on the design benchmark: serial turns per accepted design in the arm with `solve.run` available, against the arm driving the verbs, at equal compute and tokens. The second arm of GATE-TETHER-13 states it: with `solve.run` available, serial turns per accepted design fall by the pre-registered factor, or KILL-SOLVE fires |
| Milestone | M4 |
| Fallback | The prompts |

**The pre-registered factor.** It is fixed in the A/B's registration before any session is run, and it may not
exceed the pilot's ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − p) (a factor above the ceiling could never be met and would decide the gate
by construction). If the registration names none, the fail-closed default is 1.5 (DESIGN: the same effect size
the A/B requires for its primary comparison of restricted mean time), and "no better" in the trigger means the
one-sided 95% lower bound of the turn ratio is below 1.

**Decision procedure, numbered.**
1. *Clause 1.* Read the pilot record. Verdict `do not fund`: fire now; the verb is not built; skip to step 5.
2. *Clause 2.* After the verb exists and the A/B has run: read, for the one-verb arm and the hand-driven arm,
   the per-brief serial turns per accepted design, the ratio and its interval, and restricted mean time to an
   accepted answer (reported beside it).
3. Fire if the lower bound of the turn ratio is below 1 (no better), or if the point estimate of the ratio is
   below the pre-registered factor. Otherwise do not fire.
4. Guard against a hollow win: if turns fell but the fraction of accepted designs that survive the A/B's
   adjudication fell too (agents accepted worse designs faster), the comparison is read on *adjudicated*
   accepted designs only; a verb that reaches fewer correct designs is "no better".
5. Write the ADR (`docs/adr/tether/KILL-SOLVE.md`): which clause was read, the figures, the registration's
   digest, the branch.

**What changes downstream.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Not fired | `solve.run` stays registered (tier Frontier) in the `search` task cluster; the guide presents it as the one-call form with its measured turn ratio and ceiling; it is never counted as evidence, only as orchestration |
| Fired on clause 1 | The verb is never built; its registry entry is removed before the M4 verb set is frozen; the `search` cluster's seventh tool is reassigned; the workflow prompts are the documented route |
| Fired on clause 2 | The verb is retired: its entry is removed (or kept only as a convenience outside every claim, if the owner prefers; the default is removal), the guide and prompts no longer mention it, the cascade verb is unaffected, and the A/B's record states that the one-verb form "stays a convenience and is not counted as a lever" |

In every branch the prompts remain, and the co-design cascade (`opt.cascade`) is judged on its own gate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `decision_table`: (pilot verdict, ratio lower bound, ratio point, registered factor, adjudicated-accept ratio) → branch: (do not fund, –, –, –, –) → fired: ceiling; (fund, 0.9, 1.3, 1.5, 1.0) → fired: A/B (no better); (fund, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.0) → fired: A/B (below the factor); (fund, 1.6, 2.1, 1.5, 1.0) → not fired; (fund, 1.6, 2.1, 1.5, 0.6) → re-read on adjudicated designs.
- `factor_not_above_ceiling`: a registration with factor 3.0 and pilot ceiling 2.0 is rejected.
- `registry_matches_branch`: with the ADR fired, a registry that still lists `solve.run` fails the consistency test.
- `docs_free_of_solve_when_fired`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_solve_decision.sh`: reads the pilot and A/B records; applies the table; checks the registry,
the task cluster and the generated documentation against the branch. NDJSON under
`target/e2e-logs/kill_solve_decision/`: `cmd` lines; `metric` lines for p, the ceiling, the turn ratio with its
interval, the registered factor, restricted mean times; `assert` lines for the branch and the consistency
checks; a `summary` naming KILL-SOLVE, GATE-TETHER-13 (second arm) and GATE-ATEL-03.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: the five rows of the decision table; ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = 1.43.
- Negative controls: an ADR `not fired` citing a ratio whose lower bound is below 1 (the consistency test must fail); a factor registered after the first session (the registration's commit order check, owned by the A/B, must fail and this script must refuse to decide); a "win" in turns with fewer adjudicated-correct designs read as a pass (step 4's guard must catch it).

## Considerations

- Turns are the gate's quantity because the hypothesis is about serial agent turns; time to an accepted answer is what the A/B's primary comparison uses and is reported beside it so the two cannot be confused.
- The trigger's second clause is deliberately stricter than "statistically better": a small real gain that misses the pre-registered factor still retires the verb, because the surface must stay small (program risk: the agent surface is too large).
- If the A/B cannot be run at full size for want of an approved compute class, clause 2 is read on the campaign that was run, with its wider interval; an interval whose lower bound is below 1 fires the gate. A verb is not kept on an underpowered comparison.
