## Background

FrankenRobotics runs on two machines with different jobs: a Linux host with two RTX 4090 cards (the training
tier) and a Mac with a large unified memory (large scenes, the CPU referee, proofs, the viewer). Each runs its
own daemon `frobd`; there is one daemon per host and nothing is replicated. The plan proposes, as a Frontier
mechanism, a **peer link** between the two daemons that forwards whole jobs: only seeds, recipes, design and
module differences, receipts and policy weights cross it (never compiled scene fields, world state or rollout
buffers; a scene is regenerated on the receiving machine from its seed and must arrive at the same identifier).

A Frontier mechanism states its ceiling and runs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any work is funded.

- **Ceiling:** the device time one machine idles while the other queues. A link can recover at most that.
- **Pilot:** a week of both queue traces replayed on paper.
- **Falsifier:** under 10% overlap of idle and queued time (TARGET) the link is not built and the machines run
  standalone.

The same question is on the owner's list as **OPEN-TETHER-5**: *whether the peer link between the two machines
earns its upkeep.* Owner: the project owner. Due at M4. Fail-closed default: **standalone machines**; the fleet
scheduler then assigns jobs by the role table only, through the ordinary job verbs on each machine.

This bead runs the pilot and obtains and records the owner's decision. Without it the link's mechanism bead
stays blocked, which is the intended default.

## Scope

In: the two traces; the overlap metric with eligibility; the replay on paper; the pilot record and verdict; the
decision memo for the owner; the recorded decision; what changes on each branch. Out: the link itself
(N32a-35); the fleet scheduler and its own pilot and kill gate (the Spark unit); the scheduler inside one daemon
(BD-296).

## Technical approach

**Traces.** One week (7 consecutive days, both machines in ordinary use by agents) of each daemon's queue trace
in the shared trace schema (`frob.queuetrace/1`: per job the pseudonymized agent, admission time, size class,
shape class, device family, role tag, determinism class required, memory, estimated and actual device time,
start, end, terminal state), plus each device's occupancy timeline (busy slot-seconds per interval). No design,
scene or task content.

**Eligibility.** A queued job on machine X is *eligible* for machine Y at time t if, by the role table and the
job's resource vector, Y may run it: Y has a device of an acceptable family; the job's memory fits; the
determinism class the job requires is attainable on Y (a paired comparison whose arms would end up on different
device families is not eligible unless the cross-family bitwise class holds); the role table does not reserve
the work for X (training shards stay on the training cards; large-memory and world-bank work stays on the Mac).

**The overlap metric (DESIGN; the plan gives the idea and the 10% threshold).** Divide the week into intervals
of 10 s. For each interval and each direction X → Y, let q be the eligible queued work on X (device-seconds of
estimated work waiting) and f the idle capacity on Y (device-seconds free beyond Y's own reserve). The
recoverable device time in the interval is min(q, f, interval length × Y's free share). Then

    overlap = Σ over intervals and both directions of recoverable device time ÷ Σ over intervals of total device time available on both machines.

Also reported: the fraction of wall-clock time in which any recoverable time exists; the recoverable time by
direction and by role; the transfer volume the forwarded jobs would have needed (seeds, recipes, differences,
weights) against the target of at most 1 GB per campaign-hour; and the wait time the forwarded jobs would have
saved.

**Protocol, numbered.**
1. Commit the registration `registries/pilots/peer-link.toml` before extracting the traces: the ceiling, the
   falsifier (overlap < 10%), the metric, the eligibility rules, the interval length.
2. Extract both traces for the same 7 days; check clock alignment between the machines (offset recorded; a
   misalignment above one interval fails the pilot's precondition).
3. Compute the overlap and the secondary figures; bootstrap a 95% interval over days.
4. Verdict of the pilot: `build candidate` if overlap ≥ 10%; `do not build` if below. A week with either
   machine idle throughout, or with fewer than 500 jobs in total, is `insufficient` and the default stands.
5. Read the fleet scheduler's own gate: if KILL-FLEET fired (machines standalone, jobs assigned by the role
   table by hand), the link has no consumer and the recommendation is `do not build` regardless.
6. Write the decision memo for the owner (one page): the overlap with its interval, what a link could recover
   in device-hours per week, the upkeep it implies (a second authenticated service, cross-machine
   attenuated tokens, a transfer meter, re-queue rules when a machine drops out), and the recommendation.
7. Record the owner's decision as `docs/adr/tether/OPEN-TETHER-5.md`: `standalone` or `build`, with the date.

**What changes downstream.**

| Decision | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `standalone` (the default, and the result of a failed or insufficient pilot) | No link. Each daemon serves its own machine. An agent that wants work on the other machine opens a session there. The fleet scheduler, if funded, plans by the role table and submits through each machine's ordinary job verbs; the seeds-only rule for what may cross stays as a rule for people and scripts. The link's mechanism bead is closed as not needed |
| `build` | The mechanism bead is unblocked. The fleet scheduler may forward jobs over the link. The dashboard's device pane gains the peer's queue |

Evidence class of the pilot: `Estimated` (a replay on paper of one week), stated in the record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration (ceiling, falsifier, metric, eligibility, interval) is committed before the traces are extracted; the script checks commit order.
- [ ] Both traces cover the same 7 days with clocks aligned within one interval, or the pilot is recorded `insufficient`.
- [ ] The record reports the overlap with its bootstrap interval, the recoverable device-hours by direction and role, the transfer volume against 1 GB per campaign-hour, and the wait saved.
- [ ] The pilot verdict follows the rule (overlap < 10% → `do not build`).
- [ ] The metric is validated on synthetic traces: two machines never idle at the same time as the other queues → 0; one machine always idle while the other always has eligible queued work at full rate → the idle machine's share of total capacity.
- [ ] The owner's decision is recorded; absent a decision by the M4 exit review, the record states `standalone` by default.
- [ ] The mechanism bead's state matches the decision.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilot_peer_link`)
- `overlap_zero_when_no_coincidence` (synthetic).
- `overlap_full_coincidence`: machine A (1 device) always idle, machine B (2 devices) always queuing eligible work at a rate above A's capacity → overlap = 1 ÷ 3.
- `eligibility_role_table`: a training shard queued on the Linux host is not eligible for the Mac; a referee replay queued on the Linux host is.
- `eligibility_determinism`: one arm of a paired comparison is not eligible for another device family without the cross-family class.
- `recoverable_is_min_of_queue_and_idle`: q = 4 device-seconds, f = 10 → 4; q = 30, f = 10 → 10.
- `clock_misalignment_fails_precondition`: an offset of 25 s with 10 s intervals.
- `insufficient_week`: 499 jobs → `insufficient`.
- `verdict_rule`: 9.9% → `do not build`; 10.0% → `build candidate`.
- `kill_fleet_overrides`: overlap 30% with KILL-FLEET fired → recommendation `do not build`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/pilot_peer_link.sh --trace-a <path> --trace-b <path> --seed 1`: checks the registration's commit
order and the clock alignment; computes the metric; writes the record and the memo skeleton. It sources the
shared helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/pilot_peer_link/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines;
`metric` lines for jobs per machine, overlap with its interval against the threshold 10%, recoverable
device-hours by direction, transfer volume per campaign-hour against 1 GB, wait saved; `assert` lines for the
preconditions and the synthetic checks; a `summary` naming OPEN-TETHER-5 and the verdict. Traces are logged as
summary statistics only.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the synthetic cases above (0 and 1 ÷ 3); the min rule examples.
- Negative controls: an eligibility function that ignores the role table (the training-shard test must fail, and the overlap on a planted trace must be seen to be inflated); a registration edited after extraction (the digest check must fail); a week with one machine switched off counted as full idle capacity (the `insufficient` rule must catch it).
- Determinism lanes: the metric and the bootstrap identical twice on one machine with the same seed.

## Considerations

- The fleet scheduler's own ceiling argument already says the gain cannot come from training throughput (adding the Mac's GPU to training adds at most its share of the pair of cards); it can only come from moving non-training work off the training cards. The overlap measured here is the queueing side of that same question.
- A week is short; usage in the week before a gate is not typical. The record names the week and the milestone activities running in it, and the decision can be re-read on a later week.
- Upkeep is real: a second network-facing service is the only part of this system that leaves the local machine, and it would need its own threat review. A marginal overlap should not buy that.
- If the owner chooses `build` against a failed pilot, the ADR must say so and name the reason; the pilot's verdict stays on record.
