## Background

FrankenRobotics runs on two machines with different jobs: a Linux host with two RTX 4090 cards (the training
tier) and a Mac with a large unified memory (large scenes, the CPU referee, proofs, the viewer). Each runs its
own daemon `frobd`; there is one daemon per host and nothing is replicated (DESIGN: consensus protocols solve a
problem this system does not have). The plan proposes, as a Frontier mechanism, a **peer link** between the two
daemons that forwards whole jobs: when one machine queues work while the other idles, the busy one hands a job
across. Only seeds, recipes, design and module differences, receipts and policy weights cross it (never compiled
scene fields, world state or rollout buffers; a scene is regenerated on the receiving machine from its seed and
must arrive at the same identifier). Scene scans and other bulk or private data never cross; a job that needs a
private scan runs where the scan is.

The link is plumbing with upkeep: a second protocol, authentication between hosts, failure modes when the link
drops mid-job, and budgets that span daemons. That is why its ceiling is stated first.

A Frontier mechanism states its ceiling and runs a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier before any work is funded.

- **Ceiling:** the device time one machine idles while the other queues. A link can recover at most that.
- **Pilot:** a week of both queue traces replayed on paper.
- **Falsifier:** under 10% overlap of idle and queued time (TARGET) the link is not built and the machines run
  standalone.

The same question is on the owner's list as **OPEN-TETHER-5**: *whether the peer link between the two machines
earns its upkeep.* Owner: the project owner. Due at M4. Fail-closed default: **standalone machines**; the fleet
scheduler then assigns jobs by the role table only, through the ordinary job verbs on each machine.

This bead runs the pilot and writes the memo that is put to the owner. The decision itself (the owner's ruling,
its record, the configuration it sets and the checks that the default is in force) is the owner-decision bead
N41-12, which depends on this bead; the link's mechanism bead (N32a-35) depends on that decision and stays
blocked unless it reads `build`: staying blocked, with the machines standalone, is the intended default.

## Technical approach

**Traces.** One week (7 consecutive days, both machines in ordinary use by agents) of each daemon's queue trace
in the shared trace schema (`frob.queuetrace/1`: per job the pseudonymized agent, admission time, size class,
shape class, device family, role tag, determinism class required, memory, estimated and actual device time,
start, end, terminal state, the tier requested, worlds, attributable device time and the privacy mark), plus
each device's occupancy timeline (busy slot-seconds per interval; sampled occupied and free slots). No design,
scene or task content. The week is taken after the daemon with slot dispatch exists, and both traces must cover
the same calendar interval of at least 7 × 24 h: a 6-day trace, or traces from different weeks, are rejected.

**Eligibility.** A queued job on machine X is *eligible* for machine Y at time t if, by the role table and the
job's resource vector, Y may run it: Y has a device of an acceptable family; the job's memory fits; the
determinism class the job requires is attainable on Y (a paired comparison whose arms would end up on different
device families is not eligible unless the cross-family bitwise class holds); the role table does not reserve
the work for X (training shards stay on the training cards; large-memory and world-bank work stays on the Mac).
Four further conditions make a job *transferable* (all must hold with the ones above): none of its inputs is
private or otherwise barred from crossing machines (weights trained in a private scene inherit its mark); its
shape class is admitted on Y; a job that asked for bit-reproducibility against earlier runs on one device family
is not transferable to the other family; and its estimated remaining work exceeds a transfer overhead of 5 s
(DESIGN), so that forwarding could pay.

**The overlap metric (DESIGN; the plan gives the idea and the 10% threshold).** Divide the week into intervals
of 10 s. For each interval and each direction X → Y, let q be the eligible queued work on X (device-seconds of
estimated work waiting) and f the idle capacity on Y (device-seconds free beyond Y's own reserve). The
recoverable device time in the interval is min(q, f, interval length × Y's free share). Then

    overlap = Σ over intervals and both directions of recoverable device time ÷ Σ over intervals of total device time available on both machines.

The plan's phrase "overlap of idle and queued time" admits a second reading, and it is computed too:

    overlap_time O = the fraction of the week during which (X has idle capacity beyond its reserve and Y has transferable queued work) or the reverse.

Both are held against the 10% threshold; the falsifier fires if either is under it (DESIGN: the stricter reading
of an ambiguous sentence, because the default is not to build).

*Recoverable device time* R, in device-hours: ∫ min(idle device-seconds available on the idle machine,
device-seconds of transferable queued work on the busy one) dt over the week, computed with a greedy replay that
forwards the oldest transferable job first and charges each forward the transfer overhead. *Ceiling of the
benefit*: R divided by the total device-hours used on both machines that week, the largest share of throughput
the link could add.

Also reported: the recoverable time by direction and by role; the share of queued work that was not transferable,
broken down by reason (privacy, device family and determinism, shape class, role table, too short); the transfer
volume the forwarded jobs would have needed (seeds, recipes, differences, weights) against the target of at most
1 GB per campaign-hour; the wait time the forwarded jobs would have saved; and how much of R the alternative that
costs nothing would have captured (agents or the fleet scheduler submitting to the idle machine directly by the
role table), since that is the baseline the link must beat.

**Protocol, numbered.**
1. Commit the registration before extracting the traces: a row of the pilot register
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; id `PILOT-PEER-01`,
   DESIGN; tier `F`; `work_package = "WP-46"`; `gated_beads = ["N32a-35"]`, so that the record gates the link's
   mechanism bead and neither this bead nor the package's other work; `fallback = { ships = "standalone
   machines", kill_gate = "to-register" }` with the proposed wording `KILL-PEERLINK`, the trigger N32a-35 states:
   that kill row exists only after a `build` ruling, so it cannot be named when this pilot is registered, and
   N38a-09 accepts `to-register` at registration and withholds funding until the row exists; entering it, and
   setting this field to `KILL-PEERLINK`, belongs to the change that records a `build` ruling, requested of
   N41-12) whose frozen
   protocol `docs/pilots/PILOT-PEER-01.md` states the ceiling, the falsifier (overlap < 10%), the metric, the
   eligibility rules and the interval length. The result is appended as a `[[pilot.result]]` table: `do not
   build` is verdict `falsifier-holds`, `build candidate` is `falsifier-rejected`, `insufficient` is
   `inconclusive`.
2. Extract both traces for the same 7 days; check clock alignment between the machines (offset recorded; a
   misalignment above one interval fails the pilot's precondition).
3. Compute the overlap and the secondary figures; bootstrap a 95% interval over days.
4. Verdict of the pilot: `build candidate` if overlap ≥ 10% and overlap_time ≥ 10%; `do not build` if either is
   below. A week with either machine idle throughout, or with fewer than 500 jobs in total, is `insufficient`
   and the default stands. Below the threshold the rule alone decides: the recommendation is `standalone`
   without further argument and the pilot's numbers are recorded. (The decision bead N41-12 applies the rule:
   a ruling of `build` recorded there without the owner's explicit statement and reason is rejected by the
   record's check.)
5. Read the fleet scheduler's own gate: if KILL-FLEET fired (machines standalone, jobs assigned by the role
   table by hand), the link has no consumer and the recommendation is `do not build` regardless.
6. Write the decision memo for the owner (generated from the computation, at most two pages): both overlaps with
   their intervals and a day-by-day breakdown; R and the ceiling, that is, what a link could recover in
   device-hours per week; the share of queued work that was not transferable and why; the mean wait that
   forwarding would have removed in the greedy replay; the upkeep it implies (a second, host-to-host
   authenticated service; cross-machine attenuated tokens; job hand-off with idempotency keys spanning daemons;
   receipts and budgets charged on the executing daemon and reported to the submitting one; a transfer meter;
   re-queue rules when a machine drops out, under which the job is owned by exactly one daemon at every instant
   and is found by its key there); the no-cost alternative and what it captures; and the recommendation.
7. Hand the memo and the pilot record to the decision bead. The owner's decision is recorded there (N41-12), as
   `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-12-peer-link.md`: `standalone` or `build`, with the date and the owner's ruling verbatim;
   that record closes OPEN-TETHER-5 and names the consequences applied. One decision record exists for the
   question; this bead writes none.

**What changes downstream** (the memo states the two branches so that the owner sees what each ruling switches;
the table is applied and checked by N41-12, where it is repeated with the configuration key).

| Decision | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `standalone` (the default, and the result of a failed or insufficient pilot) | No link. Each daemon serves its own machine. An agent that wants work on the other machine opens a session there. The fleet scheduler, if funded, plans by the role table and submits through each machine's ordinary job verbs (`fleet.plan` forwards nothing); the seeds-only rule for what may cross stays as a rule for people and scripts. Shared views (archive, leaderboard) still merge between machines by exchanging receipts as files, because that needs no link. `frob capabilities` reports the peer link `not-in-build` with the fallback "standalone machines". The link's mechanism bead (N32a-35) is closed `withdrawn` citing the decision record (N41-17's typed close) |
| `build` | The mechanism bead is unblocked and funded as Frontier work with its own acceptance: every forwarded job has exactly one owner daemon; one job and one charge per key across both daemons; nothing private crosses (checked on the wire); a dropped link leaves both ledgers consistent. A kill gate is registered with it. The fleet scheduler may forward jobs over the link. The dashboard's device pane gains the peer's queue |

Fail-closed default until decided: standalone machines.

Evidence class of the pilot: `Estimated` (a replay on paper of one week), stated in the record.

**The harness.** The computation (clock alignment, eligibility, both overlaps, R, the ceiling, the breakdown, the
bootstrap, the verdict and the memo) is the example `crates/frob-conformance/examples/pilot_peer_link.rs` over a
module of the conformance crate that the unit tests also call; the crate ships examples, not a command of its
own. It is run as `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilot_peer_link -- <--synthetic | --trace-a <trace>
--trace-b <trace>> --seed <s> [--registration PILOT-PEER-01]`. The traces come from N32a-22's extractor, `cargo
run -p frob-conformance --example queuetrace -- --from <state root>`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registration (ceiling, falsifier, metric, eligibility, interval) is committed before the traces are extracted; the script checks commit order.
- [ ] Both traces cover the same 7 days with clocks aligned within one interval, or the pilot is recorded `insufficient`.
- [ ] The record reports the overlap with its bootstrap interval, the recoverable device-hours by direction and role, the transfer volume against 1 GB per campaign-hour, and the wait saved.
- [ ] The pilot verdict follows the rule (either overlap < 10% → `do not build`).
- [ ] Both overlaps, R and the ceiling are computed by tested code from the traces, which are stored with their digests; the non-transferable share is broken down by reason.
- [ ] The memo exists, is generated from the computation, states both branches and the recommendation, and is handed to N41-12 with the pilot record. (That the fleet scheduler's configuration reflects the ruling, with `fleet.plan` forwarding nothing under `standalone` and `frob capabilities` showing the link's status, is N41-12's criterion.)
- [ ] The metric is validated on synthetic traces: two machines never idle at the same time as the other queues → 0; one machine always idle while the other always has eligible queued work at full rate → the idle machine's share of total capacity.
- [ ] The pilot's recommendation follows the pre-stated rule (`standalone` below the threshold, on an `insufficient` week, or when KILL-FLEET has fired; otherwise the question goes to the owner with the memo). Recording the owner's decision, the default `standalone` absent a decision by the M4 exit review, and the state of the mechanism bead are N41-12's criteria.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance pilot_peer_link`)
- `overlap_zero_when_no_coincidence` (synthetic).
- `overlap_full_coincidence`: machine A (1 device) always idle, machine B (2 devices) always queuing eligible work at a rate above A's capacity → overlap = 1 ÷ 3.
- `eligibility_role_table`: a training shard queued on the Linux host is not eligible for the Mac; a referee replay queued on the Linux host is.
- `eligibility_determinism`: one arm of a paired comparison is not eligible for another device family without the cross-family class.
- `recoverable_is_min_of_queue_and_idle`: q = 4 device-seconds, f = 10 → 4; q = 30, f = 10 → 10.
- `clock_misalignment_fails_precondition`: an offset of 25 s with 10 s intervals.
- `insufficient_week`: 499 jobs → `insufficient`.
- `verdict_rule`: 9.9% → `do not build`; 10.0% → `build candidate`.
- `kill_fleet_overrides`: overlap 30% with KILL-FLEET fired → recommendation `do not build`.
- `overlap_time_on_hand_traces`: over a 100 h window, machine A idle during hours [0, 40) and machine B holding transferable queued work during [30, 50): overlap_time = 10 h ÷ 100 h = 10%, which does not meet "under 10%".
- `overlap_time_just_under`: B queued during [31, 50): 9%: `do not build`.
- `private_work_not_transferable`: the same traces with B's queued jobs marked private: overlap_time = 0.
- `device_family_not_transferable`: a job pinned to the CUDA family queued on the Linux host is not counted against the Mac's idle time.
- `recoverable_time_greedy`: 10 idle device-hours against 6 device-hours of transferable work recover 6 minus overhead; against 20, recover 10 minus overhead.
- `rule_under_ten_percent_is_standalone`; `rule_at_or_over_goes_to_owner`.
- `short_trace_rejected`: 6 days; `different_weeks_rejected`: no common interval.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the synthetic cases above (0 and 1 ÷ 3); the min rule examples; the hand traces (10%, 9%, 0%).
- Negative control: an overlap computation that counts all queued work as transferable reports 10% where the private-work case must report 0.
- Negative control: a recommendation of `build candidate` computed from an overlap under 10% is rejected by `verdict_rule`. (The control on the decision record, a ruling of `build` with an overlap under 10% and no owner statement, is N41-12's.)
- Negative controls: an eligibility function that ignores the role table (the training-shard test must fail, and the overlap on a planted trace must be seen to be inflated); a registration edited after extraction (the digest check must fail); a week with one machine switched off counted as full idle capacity (the `insufficient` rule must catch it).
- Determinism lanes: the metric and the bootstrap identical twice on one machine with the same seed.

## Considerations

- The fleet scheduler's own ceiling argument already says the gain cannot come from training throughput (adding the Mac's GPU to training adds at most its share of the pair of cards); it can only come from moving non-training work off the training cards. The overlap measured here is the queueing side of that same question.
- A week is short; usage in the week before a gate is not typical. The record names the week and the milestone activities running in it, and the decision can be re-read on a later week.
- Upkeep is real: a second network-facing service is the only part of this system that leaves the local machine, and it would need its own threat review. A marginal overlap should not buy that.
- If the owner chooses `build` against a failed pilot, the ADR must say so and name the reason; the pilot's verdict stays on record.
- The two machines are different device families. Much queued work may be non-transferable for determinism reasons alone, which lowers the ceiling before any measurement; the breakdown makes that visible.
- Privacy is a hard boundary: only seeds, recipes, differences, receipts and weights may cross, and weights trained in a private scene inherit its mark. A link that made crossing easy would need the same checks as export.
