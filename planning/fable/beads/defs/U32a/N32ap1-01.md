## Background

A kill gate in this project is a decision written before the work starts: a trigger with its measurement, the
milestone at which it is read, and a fallback named in advance. **KILL-SOLVE** governs `solve.run`, the proposed
one-call pipeline of FrankenRobotics (tier Frontier): from a task specification and a budget to a ranked set of
designs with verdicts, by calling the co-design cascade verb `opt.cascade` and the ordinary verbs. It adds no
evidence of its own; its only possible value is fewer serial agent turns per accepted design (the plan's
hypothesis: about five-fold). Its fallback is the MCP workflow prompts (`studio_loop`, `compare_designs`,
`mission_query`), which describe the same sequence as guidance and cost nothing to keep.

The gate has two clauses, read at two different times. Trigger: *the ceiling is under 1.4-fold; or agents with
`solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs.* The first clause is a ceiling counted on paper before the
verb exists: if a fraction p of the turns in recorded sessions carry no decision (pure plumbing), a one-call form
that removed all of them would cut turns per accepted design by at most 1 ÷ (1 − p). Below p = 0.30 the ceiling
is under 1.4-fold (1 ÷ 0.70 = 1.43) and the work package is not funded. The second clause is the A/B at M4,
after the verb is built.

This bead is the decision on the first clause, and it exists so that the decision sits between its measurement
and the branch it governs: the pilot that counts the turns (N32a-31) is the measurement, building the verb
(BD-372) is the branch, and BD-372 depends on this bead. (DESIGN: an earlier arrangement had one decision bead,
N32a-32, read both clauses after the verb was built and its A/B run; the verb bead was then gated only on the
pilot's own verdict, and a stricter reading of the same record by the decision, such as the conservative
recount below, could fire the gate after the verb had been built. N32a-32 keeps the second clause and completes
the same record.)

## Scope

In: the stage-1 half of the gate's decision function, as tested code; the admissibility checks on the pilot's
record; the conservative recount; the decision on clause 1; the first entry of the gate's ADR and register row;
the consequence of the branch for the verb bead, the registry row and the task cluster; the script that reads
it from the user's side.

Out, with the bead that has it: the pilot itself, its sample, its definition of a plumbing turn, its raters and

(N32b-18); the register row's format and its entry before any measurement (N32b-25).

## Technical approach

**The clause.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Trigger (clause 1) | The ceiling 1 ÷ (1 − p) is under 1.4-fold: p below 0.30, p being the pooled fraction of plumbing turns in 20 recorded sessions |
| Read | Before `solve.run` is funded: as soon as the pilot record exists (M4) |
| Fallback | The prompts |
| Fail-closed default until decided | No `solve.run`; agents use the verbs and the prompts |

**Decision function, stage 1** (DESIGN; pure; `crates/frob-conformance/src/kill_solve.rs`, the one implementation
of which N32a-32 adds stage 2).

```
decide_stage1(pilot: {sessions: u32, turns: u64, plumbing_adjudicated: u64, plumbing_conservative: u64,
                      registered_before_count: bool, selection_rule_recorded: bool,
                      alternatives_labelled_plumbing: u32} | not-run)
  -> proceed | fires(ceiling) | undecided
```

- `not-run`, fewer than 20 sessions, `registered_before_count = false` or `selection_rule_recorded = false`:
  `undecided` (an inadmissible record decides nothing; the verb stays unfunded).
- `alternatives_labelled_plumbing > 0`: `undecided`, and the record goes back to the pilot (a turn whose recorded
  reason names an alternative the agent considered is a decision turn, whatever its other marks).
- p_adj = plumbing_adjudicated ÷ turns; p_cons = plumbing_conservative ÷ turns, where the conservative recount
  treats every turn on which the two raters disagree as a decision turn. If the two fall on different sides of
  0.30 the conservative one decides (DESIGN); otherwise p_adj decides.
- p < 0.30: `fires(ceiling)`. Otherwise: `proceed`.

**Procedure, numbered.**
1. Read the pilot record, row `PILOT-SOLVE-01` of the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
   (N38a-09, reached through N32a-31; an earlier draft read a file of its own, `registries/pilots/solve-run.toml`)
   and its appended `[[pilot.result]]`, by the result's receipt digest. Check admissibility: the registration
   (ceiling formula, falsifier, selection rule, definition, classifier commit) precedes the first rater's label
   in commit order; the 20 sessions match the recorded selection rule; the classifier and both raters used the
   registered definition (the protocol digest in each label file equals the registered protocol's BLAKE3).
2. Search the stored sample for turns labelled plumbing whose recorded reason names an alternative; one found
   sends the record back.
3. Recompute p_adj, p_cons, the ceiling under each and the raters' agreement rate from the label files, not from
   the pilot's summary; a difference from the summary is a defect of the pilot and makes the record inadmissible.
4. Apply `decide_stage1`.
5. Write the first entry of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SOLVE-M4.md` (the one ADR of the gate, with N40-03's required
   fields): clause 1, the record's digest, sessions, turns, plumbing turns under both readings, the agreement
   rate, p and the ceiling under both readings, the branch. On `fires(ceiling)` the ADR's outcome is N40-03's
   `fired` (clause `ceiling`, fallback `prompts`); on `proceed` the outcome field is left to N32a-32.
   Write the stage-1 entry of the kill-gate register row (decision, date, commit). Both are written by a session
   that did not run the pilot's count and will not build `solve.run`.
6. Apply the branch (below) and check it from the user's side.

**What the branch changes.**

| Branch | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `proceed` | BD-372 is unblocked and cites this entry. The ADR's status stays open: the second clause decides (N32a-32). The pilot's p and ceiling go into the A/B's registration as the upper limit of its pre-registered factor (a factor above the ceiling could never be met) |
| `fires(ceiling)` | The verb is never built: BD-372 is closed as not built with this entry as its reason; the `solve.run` row is removed from the registry before the M4 verb set is frozen (a request to the row's cluster bead, VERBS-solve, in the same change); `solve_run` leaves the `search` task cluster, which then has six tools (NJL-05's rule); the prompts are the documented route; `frob capabilities` reports the capability `killed` with the fallback "prompts". `opt.cascade` is unaffected. N32a-32 then records the final status `fired: ceiling` without an A/B arm for the verb |
| `undecided` | Nothing is funded; the record goes back to the pilot with the failed check named |

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR's clause-1 entry exists with branch `proceed` or `fired: ceiling`, citing the pilot record by digest, with sessions, turns, plumbing turns, p and the ceiling under both readings and the raters' agreement rate.
- [ ] The decision function's stage 1 is tested code and, applied to the stored pilot record, returns the recorded branch.
- [ ] The recomputation of p from the label files equals the pilot's summary, or the record is refused as inadmissible with the difference named.
- [ ] The register row carries the stage-1 decision, date and commit, written by a fresh session.
- [ ] On `fires(ceiling)`: no served tool list contains `solve_run`, `frob capabilities --json` reports it `killed` with the fallback named, and `robot-docs`, the prompts and the skill file mention it only as killed (generated-files check). (BD-372 cites this entry on `proceed` and is closed as not built on `fires(ceiling)` by its own first acceptance line; it depends on this bead.)
- [ ] The ceiling under the deciding reading is recorded in the entry as a number, so that the A/B's pre-registered factor can be checked against it; that check (`factor_not_above_ceiling`) is N32a-32's.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_solve`)
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_solve.rs` is created here with the stage-1 rows; N32a-32 adds the stage-2
rows to the same file.
- `ceiling_formula`: p = 0.30 gives 1.4286; p = 0.29 gives 1.4085; p = 0.80 gives 5.0 (the five-fold hypothesis needs four turns in five to be plumbing); p = 0 gives 1.0.
- `below_thirty_percent_fires_stage_1`: 20 sessions, 1,000 turns, 299 plumbing (both readings) → `fires(ceiling)`.
- `at_thirty_percent_proceeds`: 300 of 1,000 under both readings → `proceed`.
- `conservative_reading_decides_when_straddling`: adjudicated 310, conservative 290 of 1,000 → `fires(ceiling)`; adjudicated 320, conservative 305 → `proceed`.
- `rater_disagreement_counts_as_decision`: a turn the raters disagree on is a decision turn in the conservative recount.
- `nineteen_sessions_undecided`; `pilot_not_run_undecided`.
- `registration_after_count_undecided`: a registration committed after the first label → `undecided`.
- `alternative_labelled_plumbing_sends_back`: one plumbing-labelled turn whose reason names an alternative → `undecided` with the turn named.
- `recount_differs_from_summary_inadmissible`: a summary of 300 plumbing against label files giving 290 → refused.
- `classifier_examples`: twelve stored turns (six of each kind, with envelopes) are classified as labelled; a turn that follows `next_actions[0]` but chooses a budget is a decision turn; read turns that are decisions are not counted as plumbing.
- `adr_status_consistent_with_record`: an entry `proceed` citing a record with p = 0.29 fails the consistency test.
- `registry_matches_branch`: with the entry `fired: ceiling`, a registry that still lists `solve.run` fails.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_solve_decision.sh --clause 1` (authored here; N32a-32 adds `--clause 2`): reads the pilot
record through the evidence handles in the gate register; re-reads the label files; recomputes both readings;
applies `decide_stage1`; writes the ADR entry skeleton; checks the branch's consequence from the user's side
(`frob capabilities --json` for `solve.run`; the served tool list of the `search` cluster on the in-memory
transport; the presence of the three fallback prompts in `prompts/list`). It sources the shared helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/kill_solve_decision/<utc>.ndjson` plus a transcript: `cmd` lines with exit code and wall time;
`metric` lines for sessions (against 20), turns, plumbing turns under both readings, p under both readings
against 0.30, the ceilings against 1.4, the raters' agreement rate; `assert` lines for each admissibility check,
the recomputation against the summary, the branch and its consequence; on failure the failed check, the record's
digest and the first differing session; a `summary` naming KILL-SOLVE (clause 1) and the recomputed branch
against the record. Session content is referenced by digest only.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the ceiling values above; 1 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = 1.43; a five-fold ceiling needs p = 0.80.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a pilot whose sessions were selected after reading them (no selection rule on record) → `undecided`; a classifier that counts every `read` call as plumbing (the classifier examples fail); an entry `proceed` from a record with p_cons = 0.28 and p_adj = 0.31 (the consistency test fails); a summary edited after the labels (the recount check fails).
- Determinism lanes: the recount and the branch identical twice on one machine and on both hosts.

## Considerations

- Splitting the gate's reading in two does not make it two gates: there is one KILL-SOLVE, one ADR and one register row, with a clause-1 entry here and the final status in N32a-32.
- The ceiling is generous on purpose: it assumes the one-call form removes every plumbing turn and adds none, so a ceiling under 1.4-fold ends the work before any of it is spent.
- If the workflow prompts already removed most plumbing from the recorded sessions, p is low and the gate fires; that is a true statement about what `solve.run` could still add, and the fallback is exactly those prompts.
- An `undecided` result leaves the verb unfunded, which is the fail-closed default; it is not a reason to read the record more leniently.
