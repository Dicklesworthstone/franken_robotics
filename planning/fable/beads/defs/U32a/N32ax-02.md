## Background

FrankenRobotics bets (BET-14) that its mechanisms need its own engine: that a short robust program over an
incumbent simulator cannot reach the same accepted answers, with the same checking scope, in comparable time. The
ten-fold test makes the bet losable. Its kill gate KILL-TENFOLD-TIE fires, per stratum of the sealed mission
suite, when the ablation arm (at most 2,000 lines over the rival's engine) reaches the same accepted scope with a
restricted mean time within a factor of 1.5 of the product's, or when the composed incumbent recovers at least
0.8 of the saving on elapsed time and on compute. It is decided at M3 and again at M7.

The fallback was written in advance: *say so; ship the layer over that engine for that stratum, behind the same
verbs, schemas and checker; keep engine work only for the gaps internal to an engine.* Two neighbouring outcomes
need the same thing: "engine advantage not established" (the layer is offered beside the engine for that
stratum) and "the rival is faster" (where licences allow, the rival's method is adopted behind the verbs). The
optional fourth arm of the test, arm D, is the same object measured: the product's own layer run over an
incumbent engine through the same verbs.

What "behind the same verbs" needs is a seam the product does not otherwise have. The dispatch interface has two
back ends, in process and a socket to the daemon, and both end in the product's own kernels. This bead adds the
seam: an **engine back end**, a worker process that runs the physics of a job on a pinned incumbent engine while
the daemon keeps everything that makes an answer checkable (admission, budgets, the job ledger, the chunk
publishing transaction, the evidence block, the certificate checker). It exists only on the firing branch of the
kill gate, or when the owner opens arm D; otherwise nothing here is built.

DESIGN decisions. (1) The engine runs **out of process, under the pin and the environment of the comparator
registry**; no incumbent code is linked, vendored or shipped. Reason: foreign tools are comparison instruments,
never dependencies (ADR-GAUNT-04); the fallback changes what the instrument is used for, not how it is held, and
the product's dependency firewall and licence stay as they are. Rejected: linking an engine as a library (it
would enter the build graph and its licence); a second set of verbs for the layer (two surfaces to keep in
parity, and "the same verbs" is the fallback's wording). (2) The seam is below the job engine, not in the
adapters: `frob` and `frob-mcp` do not know which engine ran. (3) A result always says which engine produced it,
and results of different engines are never pooled or paired.

## Scope

In: the engine registry; the registry field that says which verbs an engine may serve; selection and its echo;
the work-order protocol between daemon and worker; how chunks, cancellation, budgets and failures behave; the
evidence and identity rules; the capability matrix and its refusals; the reference worker over the product's own
CPU tier; acceptance of a layer on a stratum; tests. Out: the decision and its ADR (N39b-07); the tie statistics
(N39b-43); pins, environments, licences records and launch of an incumbent (BD-039, N39b-32); arm D's reporting
rule (N39a-21); the model exchange formats (N14a-25); the portable methods themselves (ladder, studies, mission
queries, memo graph), which run above the seam unchanged.

## Technical approach

**1. Engine registry** (`registries/engines.toml`, schema `frob.engine/1`; embedded at build time).

```toml
[[engine]]
name = "self-cpu"                       # the reference worker: the product's own batch-cpu tier behind the seam
kind = "reference"
[[engine]]
name = "<comparator row name>"          # must equal a row of the comparator registry; its pin is the identity
kind = "external"
state = "offered"                       # offered | stratum-default | withdrawn
strata = ["<stratum>"]                  # where the kill-gate record licenses it
adr = "docs/adr/<the ADR of the firing>"
verbs = ["sim.run", "eval.run", "study.submit"]         # what it may serve
claims = ["Design comparison", "Performance"]           # which of the seven claim kinds its results may be cited for
determinism = "unprobed"                # filled by the probe of point 6: D0 … D4
replay_single_world = "unverified"      # the two engine-internal gaps to be checked before use
solver_certificates = "unverified"
```

Lints: `engine-unknown-comparator`; `engine-without-adr` (an `external` row not in state `withdrawn` needs the
ADR of a recorded firing or of the arm D decision); `engine-verb-not-run` (only job-submitting `run` verbs);
`engine-state-stratum` (`stratum-default` needs exactly the strata the ADR names).

**2. Verb entries.** The closed entry schema gains `engines` (default `["native"]`); a verb served by an
external engine lists `"external"`, and gains the optional input property `engine` (a name of the engine
registry; CLI `--engine`). The surface parity check treats it like any other property. The entry table is closed
(N32a-02), so the field `engines` (values `native`, `reference`, `external`; default `["native"]`) enters it with
its lint, and the generated property is variation 13 of NJL-01's table (a dependency): an entry whose `engines`
names more than `native` gets the optional input `engine` and has appended the engine-selection codes of point 7
with `pointer: /engine`, today the interim 601 and 604 (724 is already in every list), replaced by the numbers
the allocation ledger issues for them. This bead writes that arm in `crates/frob-verbs/src/registry/derive.rs`,
with its test, in the change that adds the field, as NJL-01 requires of an arm whose field a later bead adds.
The rows that list `engines` are their cluster registries' (`sim.run` in VERBS-sim, `eval.run` in VERBS-eval,
`study.submit` in VERBS-study), entered in the same change as the first engine row; with only `self-cpu`
registered the three rows list `["native", "reference"]`. No script calls `--engine` before those rows carry it.

**3. Selection, resolved once.** In the resolve step of dispatch: (a) the call's `engine`; else (b) the session
default (`session.open --engine`); else (c) the registry's `stratum-default` for the stratum label of the bound
task, where the task carries one; else `native`. The result is an immutable engine identity
`e:<BLAKE3(name ‖ comparator pin digest ‖ environment digest ‖ worker version)>`, echoed in the envelope's
`bound.engine` and part of the request digest, so a retry cannot land on another engine under the same key.

**4. The seam** (DESIGN sketch).

```rust
pub trait EngineBackend: Send + Sync {                 // implemented by the daemon's worker supervisor
    fn identity(&self) -> EngineId;
    fn start<'a>(&'a self, cx: &'a Cx, order: &'a WorkOrder) -> BoxFuture<'a, Result<EngineStream, Refusal>>;
}
pub enum EngineFrame { Progress(Progress), Chunk(ChunkPayload), Outcome(EpisodeOutcome), Receipt(EngineReceipt) }
```

A work order (`frob.engine.order/1`, canonical JSON on the worker's standard input, then frames as
newline-delimited JSON on its standard output) holds: the exported model per design (the exchange file written by
`design.export`, with its loss report), the scene and task in the exchange forms the engine row names, the world
addresses and seeds, the horizon as a tick count and a rational step, the channel table requested, and the
budget caps. The worker is `frob-engine-worker --engine <name>`, started by the daemon inside the comparator's
pinned environment with the device, core and memory limits of the request.

**5. What stays with the daemon.** Admission (preconditions, budget, size class, compute envelope, guard) and the
idempotency binding happen before any worker starts. The worker publishes nothing: it returns chunk payloads, and
the daemon's publishing transaction applies its usual rules (lease generation, the fixed chunk partition chunk k
= ticks [k·C, (k + 1)·C) per (world, episode), a contiguous frontier, payload hashes, a duplicate reuses its
receipt, different bytes for a committed extent are `FROB-REF-810`). Results are keyed by world address.
Terminal counts sum to planned trials. Cancellation: the daemon sends a cancel frame; the worker stops at its own
batch boundary and acknowledges the last tick it returned; if no acknowledgement arrives within the drain
timeout (10 s, TARGET) the daemon ends the worker's process group, which also frees the engine's device memory;
the receipt names the last committed tick and says `drain: forced`. Charges: attributable device time is the
worker's occupancy of the device, from the meter; idle time is the daemon's overhead line.

**6. Evidence and identity.**
- `evidence.tier` is `external:<name>`; `model_id` is that of the exported model under the engine identity; a
  non-empty loss report on an element the task requires is a refusal (below), never a warning.
- The determinism class is measured, not declared: the probe runs the stratum's smoke battery twice on one
  machine and with two worker counts and records the class in the row; `unprobed` behaves as D4 (a D4 result is
  refused as evidence, `FROB-REF-825`; a paired statistic with a D4 arm is `FROB-REF-561`).
- No pooling across engines: a study binds one engine identity; a pair whose arms ran on different engines is
  refused with `FROB-REF-561`; memo keys include the engine identity, so a cached native result never answers an
  external request.
- `claims` in the envelope is the intersection of what the verb would claim and the row's `claims`. `sim.replay`
  of one world and any certificate that needs solver internals are served only when the row says `verified`.
- Certificates produced above the seam (study receipts, exact statistics, linear-program certificates) travel in
  the same `.frobcert` bundle and are checked by the same offline auditor `frob-verify`, which needs neither
  engine.

**7. Refusals.** Three conditions have no code of their own; each is requested through the refusal allocation
ledger under the slug below (DESIGN slugs; requested of N35x-02, which has no row for them yet) and raised, until
numbered, in the form rule 7 of N35-33 admits: its interim, an existing registered code, with `detail` equal to
the slug and the `pointer` named, behind one named constant per slug:

| Condition | Slug | Interim | Exit | Repairs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The engine's environment is absent, or its digest differs from the pinned one | `engine-environment-mismatch` | `FROB-REF-601`, `pointer: /engine` | 6 | Install the pinned environment (`frob doctor` prints the command); select `native` |
| The model, scene or task cannot be represented on the engine (loss report attached) | `engine-model-unrepresentable` | `FROB-REF-604`, `pointer: /engine/loss` | 6 | Remove or replace the named element; select `native` |
| The engine row does not serve this verb, stratum or claim | `engine-not-serving` | `FROB-REF-724`, `pointer: /engine` | 5 | `frob capabilities --json` lists what each engine serves |

A worker that returns a chunk for an address outside its order, or malformed, fails the job as indeterminate
through the publishing transaction's own checks.

**8. The reference worker.** `frob-engine-worker --engine self-cpu` wraps the product's `batch-cpu` tier behind
the same protocol. It is real physics, not a stand-in, and gives the seam an exact known answer: the same job
through `native` and through `self-cpu` yields bit-identical per-world digests. Every protocol test runs on it;
an incumbent is needed only for its own adapter's tests.

**9. Acceptance of a layer on a stratum.** For each fired stratum: the layer, driven by the common agent through
the product's verbs with the engine selected, reaches the accepted scope recorded for the tying arm on the
stratum's development missions; its phase ledger is recorded; the report's ratio for that stratum is the
layer's. Then the row's state becomes `stratum-default`. For "advantage not established" the state stays
`offered`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision record of KILL-TENFOLD-TIE (or of arm D) that opens this work is cited by the engine row's `adr`; with no such record the registry holds only `self-cpu` and no verb lists `external`.
- [ ] Through `self-cpu`, 1,000 worlds of the M2 standard battery have per-world digests bit-identical to `native` `batch-cpu`, solo and in mixed batches.
- [ ] The job-protocol fault battery (late lease, a kill between chunk return and publish, a worker killed mid-chunk, a daemon restart) holds through the seam: contiguous frontier, duplicate chunk reuses its receipt, conflicting chunk flagged, terminal counts sum to planned, no leaked process or device memory.
- [ ] A cooperative cancel names the last committed tick; an uncooperative worker is ended after the drain timeout and the receipt says `drain: forced`.
- [ ] `bound.engine` is present on every answer of an engine-served verb; the same idempotency key with another engine is `FROB-REF-803`.
- [ ] An absent or mismatched environment, an unrepresentable model and an unserved verb each give the refusal of the table, with one envelope, on the CLI and over MCP.
- [ ] A pair across two engines is refused (`FROB-REF-561`); an `unprobed` engine's result is refused as evidence (`FROB-REF-825`); a native memo entry is not returned for an external request.
- [ ] `cargo tree` of every `frob` binary shows no incumbent crate; the release contains no incumbent code; the dependency-firewall test passes unchanged.
- [ ] The surface parity diff is empty with an external engine registered; `frob-verify` accepts a bundle built from layer results and rejects its registered mutations.
- [ ] For each fired stratum the acceptance of point 9 is recorded with measured values, by a session that did not build the adapter.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs engine` and `cargo test -p frob-cli --test engine_backend` (the real daemon loop,
ledger and store; the worker is the real `self-cpu` reference worker; planted registries are labelled
`synthetic`):
- `selection_order`: call over session over stratum default over `native`; `selection_echoed_and_digested`.
- `self_cpu_bit_identical`: 64 worlds, three seeds, native against `self-cpu`: equal digests.
- `order_roundtrip`: a work order serializes canonically and parses back equal; 64-bit values are decimal strings.
- `chunk_rules_through_seam`: duplicate, out-of-order, overlapping and conflicting chunk payloads from a worker driven by a fault schedule (the reference worker under the deterministic runtime's fault injection).
- `worker_killed_mid_chunk_resumes_from_frontier`; `daemon_restart_reattaches_or_requeues`.
- `cancel_cooperative`; `cancel_forced_after_timeout` (a reference worker built with the test feature that ignores the cancel frame).
- `refusal_environment_absent`, `refusal_environment_digest_mismatch`, `refusal_unrepresentable` (a design with an element the exchange format drops), `refusal_unserved_verb`.
- `no_pairing_across_engines`; `unprobed_is_d4`; `memo_key_includes_engine`.
- `claims_intersection`: a row licensing only `Performance` yields no `Design comparison` claim.
- `lint_<name>` for the four registry lints.
- `derive_engine_variation_13`: an entry with `engines = ["native", "reference"]` gets the optional input `engine` and has 601 and 604 appended to its full refusal list (the interims, behind their constants); an entry with `["native"]`, or without the field, gets neither; each refusal of the table carries `detail` equal to its slug and the pointer named.
- `adapter_never_knows`: the CLI and MCP adapters' code paths are identical with and without an engine selected (the conformance suite of the dispatch interface passes for both).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/engine_backend.sh` (header: `bead=N32ax-02 milestone=M4 labels=tether,tenfold,engine
gates=KILL-TENFOLD-TIE seed=1 needs=cpu,frobd,mcp plants=wrong-address,relabel,linked-engine`). Part one, always:
with `frobd` running, (1) `frob sim run <fixture design> --scene <fixture scene> --worlds 64 --seconds 2 --tier
batch-cpu --key n1 --json` and the same with `--engine self-cpu --key s1` (distinct keys: one key with two
engines is `FROB-REF-803`, the acceptance criterion below); assert equal per-world digests through `frob telemetry divergence` (none); assert
`bound.engine` and `evidence.tier`; (2) kill the worker during the run of a second job, assert completion from
the committed frontier and equal digests; (3) `frob job cancel` on a third, assert the receipt; (4) the three
refusals of the table, each with `e2e_expect_refusal`; (5) the same run as the MCP tool `sim_run`. Part two, per
registered external engine: the stratum's smoke battery through the layer, the determinism probe (twice, and with
two worker counts), a paired study inside the engine and a refused pair across engines, a bundle checked by
`frob-verify`; logged `skipped: no external engine registered` when the registry holds none, never as passed.
Logs through the shared helper to `target/e2e-logs/engine_backend/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every `cmd`
with exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (tier, determinism, identities, seed); `assert`
lines with both digests and the first differing world on failure; `metric` lines for worker start time, frames,
chunks published, forced drains (threshold 0 in the cooperative steps) and attributable device seconds; refusals
with code, pointer and first repair; the `summary` naming KILL-TENFOLD-TIE.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answer (exact): `native` and `self-cpu` digests are equal for every world; the engine identity is a pure
  function of name, pin, environment digest and worker version.
- Negative controls: `wrong-address` (a worker that returns world 7's chunk under world 8's address: the digest
  comparison and the chunk-key check must catch it); `relabel` (a build that reports `evidence.tier` as a native
  tier for an external run: the tier assertion and the pairing refusal test must fail); `linked-engine` (a
  manifest that adds an incumbent crate to the graph: the firewall test must fail).
- Metamorphic: selecting `self-cpu` changes no result field other than `bound.engine`, `evidence.tier` and
  `cost`; worker count does not change digests.
- Determinism lanes: `self-cpu` twice on one machine, across worker counts, debug and release, both hosts (the
  class of `batch-cpu`); each external engine against the class its probe recorded.

## Considerations

- This is fallback work. If the gate does not fire at M3 it is not started, and it is re-read at the M7
  decision; a firing at M7 moves it to the milestone that follows. The seam itself (points 4, 5 and 8) is small;
  an incumbent's adapter is where the effort goes, one per engine.
- The two unverified engine-internal gaps (replay of one world outside its batch; solver-internal certificates)
  are checked on the installed incumbent before the row may say `verified`; until then the corresponding verbs
  are not served through that engine. The two documented gaps (a mixed-topology batch with run-to-run replay;
  the Metal tier) are why the product's engine stays for the strata that need them.
- Licences: an engine whose licence does not permit the use is not registered; the user installs the engine
  under its own terms and `frob doctor` reports what is present. The replication kit never carries an engine.
- Results of the layer carry weaker determinism than the product's tiers unless the probe shows otherwise, so
  some strata will lose paired comparisons through the layer. That is reported as a property of the layer.
- Security: a work order is data for a foreign process; it contains identifiers, exchange files and numbers,
  never a token, and text of untrusted origin stays in its marked fields. The worker has no access to the
  ledger or the store.
- The compute envelope applies to worker runs through the same meter as any other run.
