## Background

An agent often hands part of its work to a sub-agent: a search worker, a reviewer, a scripted loop. The sub-agent
should hold less authority than its parent, and whatever it may spend must come out of the parent's grant. Tether's
capability tokens (N32b-01: a session row in the daemon's ledger plus a daemon-authenticated bearer token listing
scopes and budgets) therefore support **attenuation**: a holder may derive a token with fewer scopes and a smaller
budget, never a wider one. An attempt to widen is refused with `FROB-REF-723` (token invalid or expired, or an
attenuation that would widen scopes or budget; exit 5). The budget of a derived token is debited from its parent.

The debit is the point. Smallest case that breaks the obvious alternative, a derived token that carries its own
copy of the requested budget: a parent holding 600 GPU-seconds derives three tokens of 600 GPU-seconds each, and
2,400 GPU-seconds are spendable under a 600-second grant. With the debit, the three requests are admitted only
while their sum stays within what the parent has left, so at most 600 is ever spendable.

Design decision (DESIGN): derivation is a daemon transaction on the ledger, requested through `session.open` with
the caller's own token as the parent. Rejected: a separate verb for derivation (the verb registry has exactly four
session verbs and one name per mechanism; an option of `session.open` keeps it so); offline derivation by the
holder (it cannot debit a budget).

## Scope

In: the derivation request and its checks; the single ledger transaction; the conservation law of budgets across a
session tree; return of unspent budget when a derived session closes or expires; cascade on parent close; depth
and fan-out limits; the widening refusal.
Out: token format and verification (N32b-01); the verb surface and schemas, including the `derive` field and the
`children` list (N32b-04, M2, which refuses `derive: true` with `FROB-REF-724` until this bead lands); admission and
job charging (N32a-15; the conformal interval it reads is BD-297); namespaces (N32b-03).

## Technical approach

**Closure first.** The child's requested scope set is closed under the implied-read rule before any check:
`Scopes::close_implied` (N32b-01, a dependency, which defines it in the scope type's module of `frob-verbs`; the
rule is BD-258's: `<cluster>:run` and `<cluster>:write` each imply `<cluster>:read`, and nothing else implies
anything). The parent's stored set is already closed (minting closes it), so the test below stays plain set
inclusion and reads no rule: a child asking `sim:run` alone is granted `sim:run` and `sim:read`, which is not a
widening because the parent's closed set holds both; a child asking `sim:read` under a parent that was granted
only `sim:run` is admitted for the same reason. The child token's `scopes` list is the closed set.

**Order on tokens.** Token C is no wider than token P when: scopes(C) minus budget scopes ⊆ scopes(P); for every
budget unit u, cap_C(u) ≤ left_P(u) at the moment of derivation, and a unit absent from P is absent from C;
expires(C) ≤ expires(P); namespace(C) = namespace(P); principal(C) = principal(P). A budget unit the derivation
request does not name is absent from C and nothing of it is debited from P (DESIGN: a derivation grants only what
it names; unlike a root open, which takes an omitted unit's amount from the grant, it never copies an amount). A derived session works in its
parent's namespace with less authority (DESIGN: a sub-agent is the same agent's helper, not a new tenant).

**The derivation transaction** (one immediate transaction of the job ledger; an idempotency key is required, as for
every `mutate` verb, so a retried derivation returns the stored session).

1. Verify the caller's token (N32b-01) and read the parent session row; it must be `open`. Close the child's
   requested scopes with `Scopes::close_implied`.
2. Check the order above on the closed set. Any violation: refuse with `FROB-REF-723`; `observed` lists each widening element (a
   scope the parent lacks, or a unit with requested and available amounts), `required` the parent's values.
3. For each budget unit u: `parent.left(u) −= cap_C(u)`; record `parent.delegated(u) += cap_C(u)`.
4. Insert the child row: `parent = P`, `depth = P.depth + 1`, scopes, caps, `left = cap`, expiry.
5. Commit, then mint and return the child token.

Limits (DESIGN defaults, in the daemon's configuration digest): depth at most 4; at most 64 open children per
session. Beyond either: `FROB-REF-723` with the limit as `required`.

**Conservation law.** For every session P and unit u, at every committed state:

  cap_P(u) = left_P(u) + reserved_P(u) + spent_P(u) + delegated_P(u),
  delegated_P(u) = Σ over children C of P of [cap_C(u) − returned_C(u)],

where `reserved` is the sum of P's own admitted jobs' outstanding reservations, `spent` P's own charged total, and
`returned_C` what child C has credited back so far (zero while it is open and idle; its full unspent remainder
once it and its jobs have finished). Summing over a tree: the total spent under a root never exceeds the root's
cap.

**Return.** When a derived session is closed, or found expired by the daemon's sweep, one transaction marks it
`closed` and credits `left_C(u)` back to `parent.left(u)`; reservations of its still-running jobs stay charged to
the child until each job reaches a terminal state, when the unused part is credited to the parent directly.
Closing a session never cancels its admitted jobs (a job is recovered by its idempotency key and cancelled only
by `job.cancel`).

**Cascade.** Closing or expiring a parent closes its open descendants in the same transaction, deepest first, each
returning its remainder upward. New admissions under a closed session are refused with `-723`.

**Reporting.** `session.show` of a parent lists each child: session identifier, scopes, caps, spent and left.
Idle reserve of a device is charged to the daemon's own overhead line and never to any session in the tree.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Deriving with a subset of scopes and budgets within what is left succeeds; the parent's `left` falls by the
      child's caps at once; the child's scopes are the request closed by `Scopes::close_implied`, and the subset
      test is applied to that closed set.
- [ ] Deriving with one extra scope, with a budget above what is left, with a later expiry, or with a unit the
      parent lacks is refused with `FROB-REF-723`, exit 5, naming the widening element; the ledger is unchanged.
- [ ] The conservation law holds after every transaction in a randomized run of opens, derivations, admissions,
      charges, closes, expiries and crashes.
- [ ] Closing a child returns its unspent budget; closing a parent closes its descendants and returns theirs.
- [ ] A retried derivation with the same key returns the same session (`idempotent_replay: true`) and debits once.
- [ ] Depth 5 and a 65th open child are refused.
- [ ] The plant `derived-token-copy` (built under `gauntlet-mutations` only) is caught by
      `three_children_cannot_exceed_parent`, bound to the seeded control of `tether-derived-budget-copy`; the
      caught status is in the evidence stream for the M3 exit's disposition of the finding.
- [ ] `session.show` of a parent lists open and closed children, paged, with each child's caps, spent, left and
      returned.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/tether_attenuation.sh` passes with the logging below.

for interleavings):

  admitted; parent left 400.
- `omitted_unit_absent`: parent {`sim:run`, `gpu:seconds=600`, `cpu:core_seconds=600`}; child asks `--budget
  cpu_core_s=200` alone: the child holds no `gpu` unit (a GPU-tier job under it is refused by admission), the
  parent's `gpu` left stays 600 and its `cpu` left is 400.
- `scope_widening_refused`: child asks `design:write`: `-723`, `observed` = [`design:write`].
- `closure_before_subset`: parent minted with {`sim:run`} (stored closed: `sim:run`, `sim:read`); a child asking
  {`sim:run`} is granted {`sim:run`, `sim:read`}; a child asking {`sim:read`} is admitted; a child asking
  {`eval:run`} is refused, `observed` = [`eval:read`, `eval:run`] (the closed request); the derivation path

- `three_children_cannot_exceed_parent`: three requests of 600 against 600: the first is admitted, the next two

- `expiry_cannot_exceed_parent`.
- `conservation_property`: 10⁴ seeded operation sequences with a crash injected at every commit point; the law is

- `return_on_close`, `return_on_expiry_sweep`, `cascade_close_returns_upward` (depth 3 tree: 600 → 300 → 100; the

- `depth_and_fanout_limits`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: 600 − 200 = 400 left after one derivation; 260 returned in `cascade_close_returns_upward`.
- Negative control (the counterexample of the background, catalogue entry `tether-derived-budget-copy` of N32-CX,
  whose plant this bead builds): the plant `derived-token-copy`, a derivation in which step 3 is skipped (the
  child carries a copy of its requested budget and the parent is not debited), built in `frob-cli` behind a
  test-only Cargo feature reachable only under `gauntlet-mutations` (BD-025) and absent from every release build.
  It must fail `three_children_cannot_exceed_parent`, with all three children admitted: 1,800 GPU-seconds
  spendable by the children and 2,400 in the tree, against a 600-second grant. The test is bound by name (prefix
  `nc_<nnnn>_`, STD-TEST) to the one control that N38b-05's seeding tool derives from the entry; this bead writes
  no row of `controls.toml`. The entry's rule has no gate in the register, so the seeding tool keeps it in
  `registry/seed_findings.toml`, and the M3 exit review (BD-319) must dispose of it: `gate-added` (naming a gate on
  token attenuation, with this bead's caught control as its first evidence) or `withdrawn` (with the reason). This
  bead's part is the plant and its caught status at M3; it adds no gate of its own.
- Negative control: a derivation that checks scopes with a prefix match (`design` admits `design:write`): the
  widening test must fail it.
- Metamorphic relation: deriving A then B from P leaves the same parent state as deriving B then A.
- Determinism: twice on one machine; across platforms; interleavings under seeded schedules.

## Considerations

- A hard partition (the child's cap leaves the parent at once) is simpler to reason about than a shared pool with
  per-child limits, and it makes the worst case of a runaway sub-agent exactly its cap. Cost: budget parked in an
  idle child is unavailable to the parent until the child closes; `session.show` makes that visible.
- Budgets across the two machines are per daemon (one daemon per host, nothing replicated); a session tree lives
  on one host.
- Interaction with the blinded qualification lane: `qual:run` can be delegated like any spend scope; the lane's
  error budget is per campaign and is not a session budget, so attenuation cannot reset it.
