## Background

FrankenRobotics is meant to be driven by many agents at once. Its surface, Tether, gives each agent a session with
a capability token (scopes for reading, spending and publishing, plus budgets), lets a holder derive narrower
tokens for sub-agents whose budgets are debited from the parent, gives each agent its own design namespace and
lineage, and offers a shared archive and leaderboard as read-only views. Each of those mechanisms has unit tests
in its own bead (N32b-01 to N32b-04). This bead is the end-to-end proof from the user's side: several real `frob`
and `frob-mcp` clients against one real `frobd`, doing what a careless or adversarial swarm would do, with the
ledger inspected after every step.

The property the seed work item for this area states is the heart of it: a session without the scope receives the
refusal and no partial data. "No partial data" is checked three ways here: the standard output holds exactly one
envelope with no `result`; no artifact, job, binding or reservation appears in the ledger; and no file appears
under any session root.

## Scope

In: `scripts/e2e/tether_capabilities.sh` with step groups `token-*`, `session-*`, `derive-*`, `ns-*`, `sealed-*`,
`kill-*`, `views-*` and `overhead-*`; the fixtures it needs; its log. Relation to the mechanism beads' own scripts
(`tether_tokens.sh` of N32b-01, `tether_session_verbs.sh` of N32b-04, `tether_attenuation.sh` of N32b-02,
`tether_namespaces.sh` of N32b-03): each of those proves its mechanism alone, at its own milestone, one agent at a
time. This script runs them first as a prerequisite step (their `summary` lines are folded into this log, with
their own bead keys) and then asserts only what needs several agents on one daemon at once: the three-way check of
"no partial data" after a refusal, parity of the CLI and MCP envelopes for every step, a derived token spending
through a real job while its parent and a second agent also run, sealed reads, a killed client, the shared views,
and the overhead line under shared slot dispatch. What this script proves, as distinct from the safety
chapter's script on the same mechanisms: depth on a small set of calls. One refused call is followed into the
ledger and the session roots (the three-way check of "no partial data"); token integrity and expiry; the budget
accounting of a derived token that really spends through a job; ownership of a namespace on a write, with forks
and job listings across namespaces; sealed reads; a killed client; shared views.

Out, with the bead that has it: breadth on the same mechanisms, in `scripts/e2e/safe_capabilities.sh` (N37-15):
the missing-scope refusal for every verb of the registry and every scope it requires, with the check that an
existing and a non-existing object are refused identically; the table of derivations that must be refused (an
extra scope, more budget than remains, a later expiry, a foreign namespace, the owner's qualification scope);
the cross-namespace export path; the resource-governance battery; the arguments that must not switch a check
off; the worker manifest. The two scripts share no assertion: where a refusal appears in both flows it is
asserted in the one named here and only used as a step in the other. The mechanisms (N32b-01 to -04); the
path-jail corpus (seed BD-258); the full refusal battery (N32b-21).

## Technical approach

The script is plain shell, idempotent and seed-explicit. It creates a temporary state root, starts `frobd` on it
with the `batch-cpu` backend only, and tears it down at exit. It sources the shared end-to-end helper. Every step
runs through the CLI and, where marked, again through `frob-mcp` over loopback HTTP, comparing the two envelopes.
Before the daemon starts the script writes the state root's `safety.toml` (mode 0600), because a root token is
minted only from a principal's grant (N32b-01): `principal.agent-a` and `principal.agent-b` with the scopes and
amounts their `session-*` opens request; `principal.agent-c` with `design:read`; `principal.agent-all` with
every catalogue scope that is not `owner_only` (the session of `sealed-*`); `principal.ops` with `session:read`
and `job:read` (the operator session of `overhead-*`). Each table names its principal's identifier as its
namespace.

| Group | Steps (commands abbreviated) | Asserts |
|---|---|---|
| `session-*` | Agents A and B each `frob session open --principal agent-a` (resp. `agent-b`) `--scopes design:read,design:write,sim:run,job:read,eval:read --budget gpu_s=0,cpu_core_s=600 --key sa-1` (resp. `sb-1`), each with no token in its environment (a root open presenting a token is `FROB-REF-723`, N32b-01; the grants are those of the setup above) (`job:read` for `job list` and `job find`, `eval:read` for `eval scorecard`; without them those steps would be scope refusals, not the assertions they are) | Exit 0; distinct namespaces; six budget fields consistent; token redacted in the transcript |
| `token-*` | A calls `design show` (admitted); A calls `train start … --key t-1` (no `train:run`); A repeats `design show` with one character of the token changed; C opens (`--principal agent-c --scopes design:read --ttl-s 1 --key sc-1`), waits 2 s, calls `design show` | Exit 0; exit 5 `FROB-REF-720` with `required: train:run`; exit 5 `FROB-REF-723`; exit 5 `FROB-REF-723`. For the three refusals, the three-way check: one envelope each with no `result`; ledger digest unchanged (no artifact, job, binding or reservation); no file under any session root. (That every verb refuses a missing scope is `safe_capabilities.sh`'s matrix; this group follows one refusal to the ledger and the disk. Among the many-agent scripts the altered-token and expired-token refusals are asserted only here; N32b-01's own script asserts them for one agent at M2.) |
| `derive-*` | A derives A1 (`session open --derive --scopes sim:run --budget cpu_core_s=200 --key d-1`); A1, holding its derived token, tries a root `session open --principal agent-a --scopes design:write --key d-2`; A1 runs a 64-world, 2 s `sim run --tier batch-cpu --key j1`; A shows; A1 is closed; A shows | Admitted, A left 400; the root open is exit 5 `FROB-REF-723` with nothing minted (a holder derives and cannot reopen its principal's grant, N32b-01); job completes; A's `children` row shows A1's spend; after close A's left = 600 − (A1's spend). (The derivations that must be refused, an extra scope and more budget than remains among them, are asserted in `safe_capabilities.sh`, step `attenuation`.) |
| `ns-*` | A and B each `design new quad-a --template quadruped-12dof --key n-1`; B runs `design edit agent-a/quad-a set-param foot.radius 0.030 m --expected-parent <A's head> --key e-1` with the correct expected parent, so that the refusal can only be for ownership; B runs `design new mine --from <A's address> --key n-2` (the fork source of the `design.new` row, landed by N32b-03); B runs `job list` | Both designs exist; exit 5 `FROB-REF-725`, A's head unchanged; fork succeeds and records provenance; A's job appears to B as a count by class only. (Among the many-agent scripts the write refusal across namespaces is asserted only here; N32b-03's own script asserts it for two agents without the MCP half.) |
| `sealed-*` | A session of `agent-all` holding every grantable scope (`--principal agent-all`, every scope of its grant named in `--scopes`, `--key sl-1`) calls `telemetry query`, `run show`, `sim replay`, `artifact read` on a planted confirmation run | Each refused with `FROB-REF-840`; no world address, outcome or trace returned |
| `kill-*` | 20 trials (A1 is closed by now, so the trials run under A): A submits a 64-world, 2 s `sim run --tier batch-cpu --key k-<trial>` (a fresh key per trial, so that each trial admits its own job), the `frob` client is killed with signal 9 at a seeded random instant, then `frob job find --verb sim.run --key k-<trial> --json` | The job is found in every trial (or, for a kill before admission, `found: false` with no charge); the ledger passes its consistency check; exactly one job and one charge exist for each admitted key |
| `views-*` | A and B each produce a scored result; the shared leaderboard view through `eval scorecard` is read by both (from M4 also the archive through `opt archive`; before M4 that step is logged `not yet due`, never `pass`) | Identical view digests for A and B; entries from both namespaces present, ordered by (metric, content address); private fixture entry absent |
| `overhead-*` | With shared slot dispatch on `batch-cpu` (environment of B = 512 slots, reserve 5% = 26 slots), A and B each run a small job while the reserve stays idle; `frob session show --all --json` under an operator session holding `session:read` (`--principal ops --scopes session:read --key so-1`) | For each step of the batch: the sum over sessions of `spent` plus the daemon's `daemon_overhead` line equals the device time of the step, within one tick of rounding; no session is charged for the idle reserve |

Fixtures: the built-in quadruped template; a flat-terrain scene; a planted confirmation run created by a helper
that holds the owner scope (the helper's token never reaches the agent sessions; it opens the owner's session
of BD-261 in the way N32b-01's open point on the owner's session provides, and while no owner session can be
opened the `sealed-*` group is logged `not yet due`, never `pass`); a private-marked synthetic scene
(labelled `synthetic`).

**Consistency check of the ledger** (used after every refusal and every kill): no job without a binding, no
binding without a reservation, the budget conservation law for every session tree, the commit frontier contiguous
for every (world, episode), and the sum of terminal counts equal to planned trials for finished jobs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 on a correct build and non-zero on each planted defect listed below.
- [ ] Every refusal step shows exactly one envelope, `ok: false`, no `result`, the expected code and exit, and an
      unchanged ledger digest.
- [ ] The derive group shows the parent's `left` falling at derivation and rising by the unspent remainder at
      close, to the unit.
- [ ] In 20 of 20 kills the job is recoverable by `job find --verb sim.run --key k-<trial>` with one job and one charge (or `found: false` and no charge when the kill came before admission).
- [ ] CLI and MCP envelopes agree for every step after removing declared per-surface defaults.
- [ ] The four mechanism scripts pass as the prerequisite step, and their summaries appear in this log.
- [ ] The overhead identity holds for every batch step of the `overhead-*` group, and no session is charged for
      the idle reserve.
- [ ] The log holds no token and no private-scene data.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The mechanisms' unit tests are in N32b-01 to N32b-04. This bead adds only helper tests:
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/ledger_consistency.rs`: `consistency_check_detects_orphan_job`,
`consistency_check_detects_double_charge`, `consistency_check_detects_conservation_break` (each on a ledger copy
with one row altered by hand).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tether_capabilities.sh` (header: `bead=N32b-05 milestone=M3 labels=tether,safety,many-agents seed=5
timeout_s=180 needs=cpu,frobd,mcp plants=scope-after-handler,derived-token-copy,unresolved-ownership-check,foreign-job-names,sealed-artifact-read`,
the five planted defects below in order). Log: `target/e2e-logs/tether_capabilities/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a
transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N32b-05`, or the mechanism bead for its group),
`step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact command line with secrets
redacted, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities,
seed), measured values with units and thresholds (budget triples; kill trials recovered against 20; view digests
equal), ledger digests before and after where state is compared, and on failure the first differing value and
the paths of the state root copy and both envelopes. The final `summary` line counts assertions passed and
failed and lists the invariants touched (INV-TETHER-05, INV-TETHER-10).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: budget arithmetic of the derive group (600 − 200 = 400 left at derivation; 600 − spend after
  close); the unscoped row of KA-755 (`FROB-REF-720`, exit 5).
- Planted defects the script must catch (each a test build of the mechanism's crate behind a test-only feature
  reachable only under `gauntlet-mutations`): (1) scope check after the handler: the refused `train start` leaves
  a job row; (2) `derived-token-copy` (N32b-02's plant for N32-CX's entry `tether-derived-budget-copy`; this run
  binds to the same seeded control and adds no row): A's `left` stays 600 after the derivation of A1 and does not
  fall with A1's spend; (3) ownership checked on the unresolved name: B's edit of A's design succeeds; (4) a `job list` that
  returns other principals' job names; (5) a confirmation run readable through `artifact read`.
- Determinism lanes: the script twice on one machine gives equal ledger digests at each step apart from
  wall-clock fields; on both platforms; kill instants come from a logged seed.

## Considerations

- The kill group uses the client, not the daemon; daemon restarts under seeded faults belong to the job-protocol
  gate (GATE-TETHER-08).
- Timing: the `ttl_s=1` step uses a real 2 s sleep; everything else is time-independent, so the script stays under
  about two minutes on the reference host (TARGET).
- If the MCP HTTP transport is not yet available in a build, the MCP half is skipped with a logged `metric` line
  `mcp_half: skipped` and the summary says so; a skipped half never counts as a pass of the parity assertion.
