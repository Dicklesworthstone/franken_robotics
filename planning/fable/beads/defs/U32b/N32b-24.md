## Background

A known answer is a case whose truth is fixed by a derivation, not by running the candidate: an exit-code table,
an identity that counts must satisfy, a closed-form queueing wait, an exact binomial bound. The known-answer
claim ("the implementation recovers an analytic or synthetic truth") is one of the seven kinds of claim this
project keeps apart, and it is the kind most of the surface's gates make. Tether, the surface subsystem (verb
registry, envelope, daemon, MCP server), states twelve known answers, numbered KA-755 to KA-766, and seven
counterexamples, CX-345 to CX-351 (registry bead N32-CX), that serve as their negative controls.

Each mechanism bead cites the answers it needs. This bead is the one place where the twelve are executable
together, on the host and through the real binaries, and where the counterexamples' plants are run through
those binaries, each test bound to the one control that the seeding tool derived from its catalogue entry. It runs on two
mechanisms of the conformance crate and on no capstone: the known-answer run per milestone with its receipts
(N38a-13) and the negative-control registry with its runner (N38b-05, which seeds the registry and brings the
control runner N38b-04). One answer and one counterexample belong
to a third-milestone bead and are not rebuilt here: KA-758 (the M/G/1 table against a discrete-event simulation
of the dispatcher) and CX-351 (priority without preemption) are executed by the queueing-model bead N32a-20 with
the dispatcher of BD-296; this battery, at the second milestone, lists them `not yet due` with that owner, and
from M3 runs those tests by name and records their results with the other eleven. The conformance registry requires that
join: every counterexample and every stated negative control maps to a control with at least one gate, with no
unmatched row.

## Scope

In: one executable test per known answer with its inputs and expected values (eleven built here; KA-758 run by
name from N32a-20 from M3); `scripts/e2e/tether_known_answers.sh`; the executed controls of CX-345 to CX-350 and of
the packet case (each plant run against the real mechanism through the real binaries, as N32-CX assigns them to
this bead); the plant `packet-binary-budget`, built here against the packet writer N24a-41; the binding of this
bead's tests to the controls that the seeding tool (N38b-05) derived from N32-CX's entries. This bead writes no
record of `controls.toml`.
Out: the registry text of the known answers and their derivation files (`N32-KA`, the other Tether unit, whose
`scripts/e2e/known_answers_tether.sh` checks the registry and collects claiming tests; this bead's script, with a
different name, exercises the binaries); the counterexample registry (N32-CX); KA-758, its simulator, the closed
forms and the capacity table, and the executed control of CX-351 (N32a-20 and BD-296, M3); the mechanisms;
gate-scale runs (1,000 races, 10⁴ schedules, 10⁶ simulated jobs) which the mechanism beads perform; this battery
runs each at known-answer scale.

**Formulas the tests recompute** (derivation notes hold the algebra).

- M/G/1 with Poisson arrivals of rate λ, utilization ρ = λ·E[S], W₀ = λ·E[S²]/2. First-come wait W₀/(1 − ρ).
  Short-class wait under non-preemptive priority W₀/(1 − ρ₁), ρ₁ = λ·0.9·10. Under preempt-resume the short class
  sees only itself: λ·0.9·10²/(2·(1 − ρ₁)), plus at most one quantum. Slowdown of long jobs: ρ₁/(1 − ρ₁) (2.4% at
  ρ = 0.5, where ρ₁ = 0.0238).
- Kingman's correction for bursty arrivals, used by the daemon's capacity table (N32a-20 builds the table and
  checks it on a trace; this battery checks only the arithmetic): multiply the wait by (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²),
  with c_a², c_s² the squared coefficients of variation of inter-arrival and service times.
- Batch arithmetic: with B = 8,192 worlds, 20 s episodes at a 2 ms step, one batch-episode is 8,192 × 10,000 =
  81.92 million world-steps; at R = 2 × 10⁶ world-steps per second (TARGET, not measured) it lasts 40.96 s; a
  96-pair comparison is attributable 192 × 10,000 ÷ R = 0.96 s; with 5% of slots (410) in reserve, 40 such
  comparisons fit with 102 slots to spare (8,192 − 410 − 40·192 = 102).
- Exact binomial upper bound: the p solving Σ_{j=0..k} C(n, j)·pʲ·(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ʲ = α; for k = 0, p = 1 − α^(1/n).

**Negative controls: one control per catalogue entry, written by the seeding tool alone (N32-CX, N38b-05).** A
control is a record of `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (`NC-…`, an append-only number; `seed` =
the entry; `kind = "mutation"`; the gates of the entry's rule; the defect; the expected failure). The seeding
tool derives exactly one from each entry of N32-CX and allocates its number. Neither this bead nor a mechanism
bead writes, allocates or edits a control (an earlier draft of this bead allocated the packet case's row and
appended tests and gates to the six others; a control has one writer, so that its identifier is allocated once
and its gates are those the tool resolved from the entry's rule). What this bead does:

1. CX-345 to CX-350. The mechanism beads of the first milestone built the plants and caught them in their own
   crates. This bead runs the same plants through the real `frob` and `frobd` and reports each of its tests to
   the same seeded control: the test reads the control's number from the registry by `seed` and calls
   `testkit::control(<that number>, …)` (STD-TEST). It adds no record and no gate. BD-211 binds its daemon-level
   tests of CX-345 to CX-348 to the same controls in the same way.
2. The packet case. This bead builds the plant `packet-binary-budget` (a writer that budgets by binary payload
   size) in the crate of the packet writer N24a-41, behind a test-only Cargo feature reachable only under
   `gauntlet-mutations` (BD-025) and absent from every release build, and its test is the first to report to the
   entry's seeded control. It appends that control's number to the `controls` list of the record of
   GATE-TETHER-16 (the rule of N32b-25 for a control that arrives after M0); the gate's own run at M3 uses the
   same plant (N32b-43).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/tether_known_answers.rs`: the eleven `ka_7NN_` tests built here (KA-758's are
N32a-20's), the six `cx_345_` to `cx_350_` control tests of the table, `packet_payload_budget`, and
- `kingman_factor`: with c_a² = 4 and the 90/10 mix's c_s² = E[S²]/E[S]² − 1 = 324,090/35,721 − 1 = 8.07 the
  factor is (4 + 8.07)/(1 + 8.07) = 1.331; with a second constructed c_s² = 15.1 it is (4 + 15.1)/(1 + 15.1) =
  1.186 (arithmetic only; the capacity table is N32a-20's).
- `batch_arithmetic`: 81,920,000 world-steps; 40.96 s; 0.96 s; 410 reserve slots; 40 comparisons; 102 spare.
- `alpha_sum_rational`: for n = 1..1,000, Σ equals α·n/(n + 1) as exact rationals.
- `binomial_closed_form_k0`: 1 − 0.025^(1/200) = 0.018275.
- `control_rows_join`: every chapter counterexample has a control and a gate.
- `binds_seeded_controls`: loading `controls.toml` through the STD-TEST loader, each of CX-345 to CX-350 and the
  packet case's entry is the `seed` of exactly one control, whose number this bead's test of the table reports
  to through `testkit::control`; the file is byte-identical before and after the suite; a registry overlay in
  which a second record carries `seed = "CX-345"` makes the lookup fail naming both control numbers (the
  uniqueness check on the registry itself is N32-CX's `one_control_row_per_mutant`).
- `packet_plant_absent_from_release`: `cargo tree -e features` on each product binary shows no
  `packet-binary-budget` feature.
- The numbers of the packet case's test (named above): 6,200,000 bytes of timeline rows are under the cap of 8 MiB
  (8,388,608 bytes) as binary, 8,266,667 bytes once Base64-encoded (× 4/3) and 8,866,667 bytes with the
  600,000-byte page, which is over; the real writer refuses before writing anything and the mutant writes a
  file larger than the cap.

## Considerations

- KA-758 compares a queueing model with a simulation of our dispatcher fed by the model's arrival process. It
  validates the dispatcher's implementation of preempt-resume against theory; it says nothing about real agent
  workloads, which the scheduling gate replays from a recorded mix.
- Tolerances are part of the answer. "To the printed digits" for KA-762 means four decimals; the test also keeps
  six-decimal values so that a change in rounding is visible.
- The rate R used in the batch arithmetic is a target, so KA-761 is stated as a ratio of slot counts, which does
  not depend on it.
