## Background

A known answer is a case whose truth is fixed by a derivation, not by running the candidate: an exit-code table,
an identity that counts must satisfy, a closed-form queueing wait, an exact binomial bound. The known-answer
claim ("the implementation recovers an analytic or synthetic truth") is one of the seven kinds of claim this
project keeps apart, and it is the kind most of the surface's gates make. Tether, the surface subsystem (verb
registry, envelope, daemon, MCP server), states twelve known answers, numbered KA-755 to KA-766, and seven
counterexamples, CX-345 to CX-351 (registry bead N32-CX), that serve as their negative controls.

Each mechanism bead cites the answers it needs. This bead is the one place where the twelve are executable
together, on the host and through the real binaries, and where the seven counterexamples are entered in the
negative-control registry and bound to the gates that must catch them. The conformance registry requires that
join: every counterexample and every stated negative control maps to a control with at least one gate, with no
unmatched row.

## Scope

In: one executable test per known answer with its inputs and expected values; `scripts/e2e/tether_known_answers.sh`;
the negative-control registry rows for the seven counterexamples and for the chapter's unnumbered cases; the
mapping from controls to gates; derivation notes under `docs/derivations/tether/`.
Out: the registry text of the known answers (`N32-KA`, the other Tether unit); the mechanisms; gate-scale runs
(1,000 races, 10⁴ schedules, 10⁶ simulated jobs) which the mechanism beads perform; this battery runs each at
known-answer scale.

**Formulas the tests recompute** (derivation notes hold the algebra).

- M/G/1 with Poisson arrivals of rate λ, utilization ρ = λ·E[S], W₀ = λ·E[S²]/2. First-come wait W₀/(1 − ρ).
  Short-class wait under non-preemptive priority W₀/(1 − ρ₁), ρ₁ = λ·0.9·10. Under preempt-resume the short class
  sees only itself: λ·0.9·10²/(2·(1 − ρ₁)), plus at most one quantum. Slowdown of long jobs: ρ₁/(1 − ρ₁) (2.4% at
  ρ = 0.5, where ρ₁ = 0.0238).
- Kingman's correction for bursty arrivals, used by the daemon's capacity table and checked on one constructed
  trace: multiply the wait by (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²), with c_a², c_s² the squared coefficients of variation of
  inter-arrival and service times.
- Batch arithmetic: with B = 8,192 worlds, 20 s episodes at a 2 ms step, one batch-episode is 8,192 × 10,000 =
  81.92 million world-steps; at R = 2 × 10⁶ world-steps per second (TARGET, not measured) it lasts 40.96 s; a
  96-pair comparison is attributable 192 × 10,000 ÷ R = 0.96 s; with 5% of slots (410) in reserve, 40 such
  comparisons fit with 102 slots to spare (8,192 − 410 − 40·192 = 102).
- Exact binomial upper bound: the p solving Σ_{j=0..k} C(n, j)·pʲ·(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ʲ = α; for k = 0, p = 1 − α^(1/n).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/tether_known_answers.rs`: the twelve `ka_7NN` tests, and
- `mg1_closed_forms`: W₀ at ρ = 0.7 is 600.2 s; the three waits 2,000.6, 620.9 and 0.172 s.
- `kingman_factor`: c_a² = 4, c_s² = 15.1 (the 90/10 mix: E[S²]/E[S]² − 1 = 324,090/35,721 − 1 = 8.07; a second
  constructed case at 15.1) gives factors (4 + 8.07)/(1 + 8.07) = 1.331 and (4 + 15.1)/(1 + 15.1) = 1.186.
- `batch_arithmetic`: 81,920,000 world-steps; 40.96 s; 0.96 s; 410 reserve slots; 40 comparisons; 102 spare.
- `alpha_sum_rational`: for n = 1..1,000, Σ equals α·n/(n + 1) as exact rationals.
- `binomial_closed_form_k0`: 1 − 0.025^(1/200) = 0.018275.
- `control_rows_join`: every chapter counterexample has a control and a gate.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tether_known_answers.sh`: on a temporary state root with `frobd` on `batch-cpu`: the KA-755 fixture
(five commands, exits compared); KA-756 through `frob` with a client-side double decoder; KA-763 as two racing
submissions with one key and a third with a changed payload; KA-764 as a paged `telemetry query` with an append
between pages; KA-765 through the test publisher; KA-766 on a job with planted failures and a cancellation; then
each mutant build is run on the same steps and must fail them. Log:
`target/e2e-logs/tether_known_answers/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`,
`bead`, `step` (the KA or CX number), `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's
`evidence` block, the measured value and the known answer with tolerance, `expected: pass | fail` for release
and mutant builds, digests where state is compared, and on failure the first differing value and artifact
paths. The `summary` line names the gates touched (GATE-TETHER-03, -05, -06, -07, -08).

### Known answers and negative controls
The battery is the known answers; its controls are the rows above. Determinism lanes: all host tests twice and
on both platforms; the simulated-daemon cases under fixed seeds; the M/G/1 simulation with a logged seed and a
confidence band reported beside each cell.

## Considerations

- KA-758 compares a queueing model with a simulation of our dispatcher fed by the model's arrival process. It
  validates the dispatcher's implementation of preempt-resume against theory; it says nothing about real agent
  workloads, which the scheduling gate replays from a recorded mix.
- Tolerances are part of the answer. "To the printed digits" for KA-762 means four decimals; the test also keeps
  six-decimal values so that a change in rounding is visible.
- The rate R used in the batch arithmetic is a target, so KA-761 is stated as a ratio of slot counts, which does
  not depend on it.
