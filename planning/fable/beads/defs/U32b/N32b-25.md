## Background

Every acceptance gate, kill gate and admission gate of FrankenRobotics has one record in `registries/gates.toml`.
A milestone exit is a query over that file; the README's status table, the `capabilities` verb and the
dashboard's gate view are generated from it and from nothing else. A gate names exactly one of the seven kinds
of claim (proof, known answer, parity, fidelity, design comparison, reality, performance), carries its threshold
and its milestone, lists the negative controls that must be caught in the same run as a pass, and records the
measured value, never the target.

Tether, the surface subsystem (verb registry, command line, daemon, MCP server, dashboard, viewer), defines
sixteen acceptance gates and seven kill gates; the unit adds an eighth kill gate, KILL-PEERLINK (DESIGN), for
the Frontier peer link of N32a-35. The record of each is created by the register bead that owns it (Scope names
each). This bead supplies the chapter's side of every one, so that from the first milestone on each
record has its controls, its tiers and its full threshold on file, and a pass without a caught control is
refused. It also fixes, for each gate, which work item measures it, because the chapter's gates are measured by
beads spread over several units and a gate nobody measures would stay `not-run` unnoticed. Where the chapter's
own table and the program's gate register disagree, the register governs; the reconciliations are recorded
below.

## Scope

This bead is the Tether chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record: each
is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the sixteen gates: the full threshold text of each (the gates table below, which a recording session reads
  as the defining statement); its tiers and controls, entered in the `tier` and `controls` fields of the records
  that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the chapter's owner declaration of each gate, with its measuring bead.
- For the eight kill gates: the map from each to its decision bead, and the check that each has its ledger
  record and exactly one owner declaration.
- The reconciliation notes; a lint that every Tether gate has a measuring bead and at least one control.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the records of GATE-TETHER-01 to -14 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim kind, section,
  milestone parts and the brief: N40-02. The same for GATE-TETHER-15 and -16: N40-10.
- The kill records, with trigger, fallback, decision point, mode and what each trigger reads: the fallback
  ledger, N40-27 for KILL-MCP, KILL-SLOT, KILL-PREEMPT, KILL-MCPTASKS, KILL-SOLVE and KILL-TUI, and N40-29 for
  KILL-REPLAYPACK. KILL-PEERLINK is a DESIGN kill gate that neither ledger holds (the plan names no kill gate for
  the link): its record is created by its mechanism bead N32a-35 (M4), in the fallback ledger's format, before
  the first forwarded job. That bead is built only under the owner's `build` ruling on the peer link (N41-12,
  recorded in `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-12-peer-link.md`). Under the ruling `standalone`, which is also what the record
  states when no decision is made by the M4 exit, N32a-35 is closed `withdrawn` and the record is never created:
  this bead's lint then accepts its absence and reports KILL-PEERLINK `not-reached` (the outcome of N40-03 for a
  mechanism that was not funded), never as a missing record.
- Measuring any gate; deciding any kill gate (the decision beads named in the table below); the register's
  schema and recorder (N38b-18, N40-01).

**Reconciliations (the register governs).**

- GATE-TETHER-09 is labelled "Parity; Performance" in the chapter and "Performance" in the register. A gate
  names exactly one claim: the row is `performance`. Its digest-identity clause is still part of the threshold.
- KILL-SLOT is stated by the surface chapter and by the device chapter. The surface chapter's statement governs:
  its limits contain the device chapter's (99th-percentile step time more than 10% above the same job run alone)
  and add the 5% throughput limit and the optimization pass. The kernel-level slot-invariance gate
  (GATE-SPARK-15) is a precondition, not a second trigger. The fallback is the same in both.
- KILL-MCP is stated once: the design of record is `frob-mcp` as a member of the core build graph running verbs
  in process; the socket form is the first fallback, not the default.
- GATE-TETHER-15, GATE-TETHER-16 and KILL-REPLAYPACK were defined after the register's main tables were compiled
  and are listed in its late-additions section, all at M3.
- KILL-PEERLINK has no plan text. A kill record has no pass, so the "recorded pass" that N41-12 requires before it
  accepts `fleet.peer_link = true` reads, in this register's vocabulary, as the record's first measurement (the
  campaign of N32a-35) entered with clauses (a) and (b) not triggered; the decision itself (`not-fired` or
  `fired`) is taken when clause (c) is read.
- Where a row's own milestone disagreed with the milestone skeleton, the register follows the skeleton; the
  milestones above are the register's.

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` of N40-01: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `claim_id`,
`section`, `consumers`, `threshold`, `milestone`, `recurring`, `tier`, `controls` and a `[[gate.part]]` list in
which each part has its `status` (`not-run` at entry), `measured`, `evidence` and `recorded`. The records of
GATE-TETHER-01 to -14 are created by N40-02 and those of GATE-TETHER-15 and -16 by N40-10, which fill the
fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the parts with their
scopes, `recurring`, `blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. The seven kill records of the chapter are
created by the fallback ledger (N40-27, N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`, and
KILL-PEERLINK's by N32a-35 in the same format. This bead creates
no record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. `controls` and `tier` in each gate's existing record: the `NC-…` numbers of the controls this chapter
   states (the last column of the table above) and the tiers the gate is measured on. Where a stated control is
   the plant of a catalogue entry or the control cell of a known-answer row (those cited by CX number for gates
   05 to 08 and 10, and whatever the rows of N32-KA state), its record and number are the seeding tool's
   (N38b-05, M0), which is the only writer of such a record; this bead lists the number. Where no catalogue
   entry and no known-answer row states it (for example the path that prints two envelopes, of GATE-TETHER-03),
   this bead registers the record under BD-025's rule for a control registered first for a gate: `seed =
   "GATE-TETHER-nn"`, the kind, target, defect and expected observation of the cell, and an `NC-` number
   appended in order, never chosen by hand. Its plant is built later by the measuring bead; until then the
   control runner reports it stale and the gate cannot pass, which is the intended pressure. No plant has two
   controls: before registering one, this bead looks the plant up among the seeded records and reuses what it
   finds. Numbers of controls that arrive after M0 (the packet case's, seeded when N32-CX's appended entries
   are numbered at M1 and listed on GATE-TETHER-16 by N32b-24; those a measuring bead registers for its own
   gate) are appended to the gate's `controls` list by the bead that brings them, under this bead's lint.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of the crate that implements the defining subsection (`registries/gate_declarations.toml`
   until that block is agreed with the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side
   data that the register record has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full
   threshold text of the table above, of which the record's `threshold` is a brief; `measured_by`, the bead
   keys above, so that the lint can check ownership; and `notes`, for the reconciliations.
3. Nothing in a kill gate's record (KILL-PEERLINK's included, which N32a-35 creates). It keeps the map from each
   kill gate to its decision bead as data of its lint. The owner declaration of a kill gate (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`) is written by
   the decision bead, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the ledger by N40-30.

The Claim, Milestone, Trigger, Fallback and Decided-at columns above are the chapter's statement of fields the
register governs. They were compared with N40-02, N40-10, N40-27 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and
they agree. Where a trigger above spells out what the ledger names by identifier (the two upstream work items
of KILL-MCP; the pilot of the Tasks adapter; the ceiling of the one-call pipeline), the ledger's text is the one
a declaration carries. If the two ever differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a
finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never settled by a second record.

## Considerations

- Decision beads. Five of the seven kill gates govern the build graph, the dispatcher, the Tasks adapter and the
  one-call pipeline, and are decided by the beads that sit beside those mechanisms (N32a-05, N32a-23, N32a-24,
  N32a-29, N32a-32); each depends on this bead and, through it, on the ledger group N40-27, so that its kill
  record exists before its measurement is read. KILL-TUI and KILL-REPLAYPACK are decided by N32b-31 and
  N32b-32. The ledger group that holds KILL-REPLAYPACK (N40-29) lands at M1, after this M0 bead; that gate is
  decided at M3, and the kill part of the lint is first required for it at the M1 exit. KILL-PEERLINK is decided
by its mechanism bead N32a-35 (M4), which creates its record; the lint requires it from the M4 exit under a
`build` ruling and reads its absence as `not-reached` under `standalone`.
- The chapter's open questions and where each is settled, so that none is orphaned: OPEN-TETHER-1 (can
  `frob-mcp` link handler crates in process; decided by the owner with the one-toolchain ruling: yes, as a
  member of the core graph, confirmed by INT-FMCP-01, with KILL-MCP's fallbacks otherwise) is recorded with the
  KILL-MCP decision; OPEN-TETHER-2 (protocol eras and catalog-changed notifications of the harnesses in use;
  Tether lead; blocks GATE-TETHER-12; default: static clusters by launch flag plus `verb_invoke`) is the
  experiment N32a-12; OPEN-TETHER-3 (default and maximum of `sync_wait_ms`; Tether
  lead; blocks GATE-TETHER-13; default 5,000 and 30,000) is the experiment N32a-33; OPEN-TETHER-4 (slot reserve,
  job cap, quantum, ageing period from the second milestone's queue trace; Daemon lead; blocks GATE-TETHER-10;
  default 5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s) is the experiment N32a-22; OPEN-TETHER-5 (whether the
  peer link between the two machines earns its upkeep; the owner; due at the fourth milestone; default:
  standalone machines) is the pilot N32a-34 and the owner decision N41-12, with the mechanism N32a-35 and its
  kill gate KILL-PEERLINK if the link is built.
- A gate measured by a bead of another unit is still this chapter's gate; when a measuring bead is split or
  renumbered, the `measured_by` field is what keeps the gate from being orphaned.
- Thresholds are targets set before measurement. Changing one after a measurement exists is a recorded amendment
  with its reason, never an edit of the row in place.
- Three of the sixteen compare agents, which need people-free scoring: their controls are scripted agents, so
  that the scorer itself can be shown to fail.
