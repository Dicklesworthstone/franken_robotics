## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that decides it: a trigger with numbers, a fallback named in
advance, and a statement of what changes downstream. KILL-SLOT governs the central scheduling idea of the daemon
`frobd`.

The idea (decision ADR-TETHER-07): the daemon keeps one persistent simulation environment per device and shape
class, and dispatches *episodes into slots* of it, instead of creating one environment per job. When a slot's
episode ends, the dispatcher resets that slot with the next admitted episode from any job of the same shape
class. Results are keyed by world address, never by slot or arrival order. The motive is arithmetic on the
reference batch of B = 8,192 worlds: a mission cell probe of 16 worlds fills 0.2% of a batch, a fixed-n study of
30 pairs 0.7%, an anytime-valid comparison at its cap of 96 pairs 2.3%, a 2,048-world run 25%. Without sharing,
the rest of the device idles while small jobs queue.

The ceiling, stated before any speed test: the gain over one environment per job is at most min(k, 1 ÷ fill) for
k concurrent small jobs, so at most 42-fold for 192-slot jobs (8,192 ÷ 192 = 42.7) and nothing at all for a
single agent running one job at a time, who gains only the removal of environment creation from each call.
Against a right-sized environment per job the ceiling is the measured ratio of step time at 8,192 worlds to step
time at the job's own size, unknown until the second milestone's profile.

The risk that makes this a kill gate: sharing a batch is only acceptable if a world's digest and results depend
on its address and identities and never on its slot, its co-tenants, dispatch order, preemption or resumption.
One digest difference under co-tenancy means evidence depends on who else was running, which no other benefit
can pay for.

## Technical approach

**The gate, as registered.** The surface chapter's statement governs over the device chapter's: its limits
contain the latter's and add the throughput limit and the optimization pass; the fallback is the same.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | KILL-SLOT |
| Decided at | The third milestone |
| Trigger | Any digest difference under co-tenancy; or GATE-TETHER-09's throughput or step-time limits fail after one optimization pass |
| Decided on | GATE-TETHER-09 alone. The kernel-level slot-invariance gate (GATE-SPARK-15) tests the same digest equality and is a precondition, not a second trigger |
| Limits (TARGET) | At 8,192 identical worlds, shared dispatch loses at most 5% throughput against a dedicated environment; 99th-percentile step time is at most 1.10 of solo |
| Fallback | `frobd --dispatch=per-job`: one environment per job, queued |

**Decision function.**

```
decide(precondition_current: bool,          // GATE-SPARK-15 current for this driver and fingerprint
       digest_mismatches: u64,              // over 1,000 episodes × 100 random co-tenancies
       throughput_loss: f64,                // 1 − shared ÷ dedicated, at 8,192 identical worlds
       p99_step_ratio: f64,                 // shared ÷ solo
       optimization_passes_used: u8)        // 0 or 1
  -> does-not-fire | fires | undecided
```

1. `precondition_current` false: undecided (the measurement is not admissible; run the precondition first).
2. `digest_mismatches > 0`: fires. No optimization pass applies to a digest difference; a root-caused defect in
   a kernel is fixed under the kernel gate, the precondition is re-established, and the measurement is repeated
   from the start as a new record.
3. `throughput_loss ≤ 0.05` and `p99_step_ratio ≤ 1.10`: does not fire.
4. Limits missed and `optimization_passes_used = 0`: undecided; one optimization pass is allowed, with its
   changes listed in the record.
5. Limits missed and `optimization_passes_used = 1`: fires.

The admission-to-first-step percentiles of GATE-TETHER-09 (0.1, 0.5 and 2 s at the 50th, 95th and 99th
percentiles for jobs of up to 256 worlds) are part of the gate and are recorded, but are not part of this
trigger.

**Procedure.** (1) Confirm the shadow rollout on `batch-cpu` ran: each job both ways, digests compared, with the
count of jobs and mismatches. (2) Read the gate record; check it names the commit, the hardware fingerprint and
the seed, and that its negative control (a kernel stand-in that reads a neighbouring slot's row) was caught in
the same run. (3) Apply the function. (4) Write the architecture decision record and the register row, from a
session that did not implement slot dispatch.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Does not fire | Slot dispatch is the default (`--dispatch=slots`); `per-job` stays as the rollback switch and as the runtime response to any digest alarm. Budgets charge attributable device time (occupied slots ÷ batch capacity × step wall time). The reserve, the per-environment job cap and preempt-resume proceed |
| Fires | The default becomes `--dispatch=per-job`: one environment per job, jobs queued per device. The persistent-environment code stays behind the switch for later re-measurement but is not used for evidence. A job is charged the device's whole step time while it holds it; the daemon's overhead line holds only environment creation. The slot reserve and the per-environment job cap have no meaning; interactive protection comes from queue order and, if it survives its own gate in a per-job form, from parking whole jobs. The 42-fold ceiling for small concurrent jobs is given up and the queueing waits of the capacity table are recomputed for whole-device service. The dashboard's Devices pane shows one job per environment. The scheduling gate's thresholds are re-read against the fallback and recorded with it. Co-batching of design variants inside one job is unaffected: that is the variant mechanism, not the dispatcher |
| Runtime, either branch | A digest difference detected in operation (a resume or a shadow comparison) makes the dispatcher fall back to per-job dispatch at once and raise an alarm; that is the guard table's own rule and does not wait for a decision |

**Fail-closed default until decided.** `per-job` dispatch for any run whose result is cited as evidence; slot
dispatch only in shadow.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision function is tested code; applied to the recorded GATE-TETHER-09 values it returns the recorded
      branch.
- [ ] The architecture decision record states the branch with the measured mismatch count, throughput loss, step
      ratio, passes used, and the gate record's hash.
- [ ] On the firing branch, `frobd` starts with per-job dispatch by default, `session.show` charges whole-device
      time, and `frob capabilities` reports slot dispatch `killed` with the fallback named.
- [ ] On the surviving branch, `frobd --dispatch=per-job` still passes the job-protocol tests (the rollback is
      alive).
- [ ] The register row is filled by a fresh session and cites the gate receipt.

- `within_limits_does_not_fire`: (true, 0, 0.05, 1.10, 0) does not fire (limits are inclusive).
- `over_limit_first_time_is_undecided`: (true, 0, 0.051, 1.00, 0).
- `over_limit_after_pass_fires`: (true, 0, 0.00, 1.11, 1).
- `stale_precondition_is_undecided`.
- `ceiling_arithmetic`: min(k, 1 ÷ fill) for 192-slot jobs is 8,192 ÷ 192 = 42.67 for k ≥ 43 (quoted as "at most
  42-fold") and k for smaller k; slot shares
  16, 60, 192 and 2,048 of 8,192 are 0.2%, 0.7%, 2.3% and 25%.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_slot_decision.sh`: reads the gate record through `frob capabilities --json`; recomputes the
decision; on `batch-cpu` runs a 64-episode job solo and in 8 random co-tenancies under both dispatch modes and
compares per-world digests (`frob telemetry divergence`); checks the consequence of the recorded branch from the
user's side (`frobd` default mode; the charge reported by `frob session show`). NDJSON lines per the shared
helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, digest
pairs compared and equal, the three measured values against their limits, the recomputed branch against the
record; the `summary` line names KILL-SLOT and GATE-TETHER-09.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-761: 192 ÷ 8,192 = 2.34%.
- Negative control: a decision recorded as "does not fire" with one digest mismatch in the gate record is
  rejected by the recomputation.
- Negative control: a second optimization pass presented as the first (two change lists in the record) is
  rejected: `optimization_passes_used` is derived from the record's history, not declared.
- Negative control: the planted cross-slot read must appear as caught in the gate record, or the record is
  inadmissible.

## Considerations

- The reference rate used in the motivating arithmetic (2 × 10⁶ world-steps per second) is a target, not a
  measurement; the decision uses only measured ratios.
- A single agent running one job at a time gains nothing from slots; if the workload at the third milestone is
  that, firing costs little and the record should say so.
- The reserve, cap, quantum and ageing constants are under an open question decided from the second
  milestone's queue trace (N32b-35); they do not enter this trigger.
