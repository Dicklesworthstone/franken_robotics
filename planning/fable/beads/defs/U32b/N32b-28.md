## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that decides it. KILL-PREEMPT governs whether the daemon
`frobd` may interrupt a running batch job to serve an interactive one.

The mechanism: jobs are sorted into size classes by the system's own estimate (`interactive`: at most 30 s of
device time; `batch`; `campaign`). A batch-class job can be parked at a snapshot boundary (quantum at most 5 s,
TARGET) and resumed later with identical digests; the snapshot includes solver warm starts, sensor packet
queues and random-stream counters. At most one preemption per batch job per 30 s, and the batch class keeps at
least 30% of device time (TARGET).

Why priority alone is not enough (a queueing model, not a measurement): take one device with 90% of jobs 10 s
and 10% of jobs 30 min (E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s²). At utilization 0.7 a short job waits 2,001 s
first-come-first-served, 621 s under non-preemptive priority, and 0.17 s plus one quantum under preempt-resume,
while long jobs slow by 3.4%. The wait under priority is the remainder of the long job already on the device;
only preemption removes it. The result flips if resume costs several seconds or a device is reserved for
interactive work: then running to completion is right.

Two things can make preemption unacceptable, and they are the trigger. A resumed job whose digest differs means
the scheduler changed what was computed, which breaks the rule that the scheduler decides when work runs and
never what (decision ADR-TETHER-08; invariant INV-TETHER-09). A resume that costs more than the quantum means
the cure costs more device time than the wait it removes.

## Technical approach

**The gate, as registered.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | KILL-PREEMPT |
| Decided at | The third milestone, after KILL-SLOT |
| Trigger | A resumed job's digest differs; or measured resume cost exceeds the quantum |
| Measurement | Digest and verdict identity across random preemption and reordering in the scheduling gate's replayed mix; resume cost = measured snapshot time + restore time of a batch job at the reference batch, at its 95th percentile over at least 200 preempt-resume cycles (DESIGN: the percentile and count are this bead's choice; the specification says "measured resume cost") |
| Quantum | 5 s (TARGET; a constant under the open question on dispatcher constants, read from the decision-core table at decision time) |
| Fallback | Run to completion with the slot reserve only |

**Decision function.**

```
decide(resume_digest_mismatches: u64, cycles: u64, resume_cost_p95_s: f64, quantum_s: f64)
  -> does-not-fire | fires | undecided
```

1. `cycles < 200`: undecided (not enough resumes to speak about cost).
2. `resume_digest_mismatches > 0`: fires.
3. `resume_cost_p95_s > quantum_s`: fires.
4. Otherwise: does not fire.

**Procedure.** (1) Read the scheduling gate's record (GATE-TETHER-10: on a replayed mix with 90% interactive
jobs at utilization 0.7, interactive wait at the 95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10%; no job
beyond the ageing bound; random preemption and reordering leave every digest and verdict identical) and the
resume-cost measurements; check commit, hardware fingerprint, seed and the caught negative control (a resume
that restores a stale warm start). (2) Read the quantum in force. (3) Apply the function. (4) Write the
architecture decision record and the register row from a session that did not implement preemption. (5) Amend
the decision-core table for the branch taken.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Does not fire | Preempt-resume stays in the guard table: when no slot is free and an interactive job waits, the dispatcher uses the reserve and, if it is full, preempts the batch job with the most remaining work at its next snapshot boundary. The rate limit (one preemption per batch job per 30 s) and the batch floor (30% of device time) are enforced and reported |
| Fires | The "preempt" action is removed from the guard table; the saturated state uses the reserve only and otherwise queues. Batch jobs run to completion. Interactive protection is the 5% slot reserve (and the size-class order within the queue). The capacity table the daemon publishes uses the priority-only column of the queueing model (for the example mix: 439, 621, 713 and 806 s at utilization 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9) for interactive work that does not fit the reserve. The scheduling gate's interactive-wait limit of 6 s is re-read: it is recorded against the fallback with its measured value, and if unmet the gate is recorded `killed` with this fallback, not passed. Snapshot code remains, because replay, forks and checkpoints need it; only its use by the dispatcher is withdrawn. The dashboard's preemption column shows zero and the Queue pane's mode line says "run to completion" |
| If KILL-SLOT fired first | There is no slot reserve. The fallback then reads: run to completion, one environment per job, interactive jobs ordered first in the queue. The record states this combined state explicitly |
| Runtime, either branch | A resume whose digest differs in operation makes the dispatcher fall back to round-robin per agent, run to completion and per-job dispatch, and raise an alarm, at once |

**Fail-closed default until decided.** Run to completion; preemption only in the scheduling gate's own
measurement runs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision function is tested code; applied to the recorded values it returns the recorded branch.
- [ ] The architecture decision record gives the mismatch count, the number of cycles, the 95th-percentile
      resume cost, the quantum in force and the gate record's hash.
- [ ] On the firing branch: the guard table in `registries/cores/` has no preempt action; a saturated device with
      a waiting interactive job and a full reserve queues it; `frob capabilities` reports preempt-resume `killed`
      with the fallback named; the capacity table is regenerated.
- [ ] On the surviving branch: the rate limit and batch floor are observable in `job.list` over a replayed mix.
- [ ] The register row is filled by a fresh session.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-758: the M/G/1 table (first-come 857, 2,001, 3,430, 7,716 s; priority only 439, 621, 713, 806 s;
  preempt-resume 0.12, 0.17, 0.20, 0.22 s plus one quantum; long-job slowdown 2.4%, 3.4%, 4.0%, 4.5%).
- Negative control (CX-351): a dispatcher with preemption removed must show the priority-only waits in the

- Negative control: resume cost computed as a mean when the samples have a long tail (mean 3 s, 95th percentile
  7 s) must still fire.

## Considerations

- The model that motivates preemption assumes Poisson arrivals; a swarm's arrivals burst, and the daemon scales
  waits by Kingman's factor (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²) from its own trace. The decision uses measurements only.
- A resume cost just under the quantum passes the trigger but may still make preemption a poor trade; the loss
  function of the dispatcher (interactive wait at weight 1, batch wait at 0.1, plus preemption cost) is where
  that is weighed, and its weights are refitted from the second milestone's queue trace.
- No adaptive scheduling rule ships without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a
  demonstrated fallback; this gate is the demonstrated fallback for preemption.
