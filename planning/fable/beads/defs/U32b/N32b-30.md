## Background

A kill gate is a decision written before the data that decides it. KILL-SOLVE governs a Frontier convenience of
the agent surface: the verb `solve.run`.

`solve.run` compiles a task, constraints and a budget into the sequence check, sweep under the baseline
controller, race, promote, and returns a ranked set of designs with verdicts. It is orchestration, not a
mechanism: every number in its answer comes from the verbs it calls, and it adds no evidence of its own. It is
the agent-facing form of the co-design cascade, which is run by `opt.cascade`; `solve.run` calls it.

The claim (HYPOTHESIS): about five-fold fewer serial agent turns per accepted design. The reasoning is that an
agent turn is believed to cost 30 to 120 s of thinking, so turns spent on plumbing (calling the next obvious
verb with the previous verb's output) dominate wall time and can be removed by one call.

The ceiling is stated before any test, as the project's rule on speed claims requires: the benefit is bounded by
the fraction f of turns in recorded sessions that carry no decision (pure plumbing). Removing all of them gives
at most 1 ÷ (1 − f). If f is below 30%, the ceiling is under 1.4-fold (1 ÷ 0.7 = 1.43) and the work package is
not funded. The pilot that measures f: replay 20 recorded sessions on paper and count plumbing turns.

There is one A/B, and the gate is decided on it: the second arm of the mission agent gate (GATE-TETHER-13: "with
`solve.run`, serial turns per accepted design fall by the pre-registered factor or KILL-SOLVE fires") is the
one-verb arm of the design benchmark's agent-surface A/B (GATE-ATEL-03). The fallback costs nothing to keep: the
MCP prompts, which lay out the same workflows as text (`studio_loop`, `compare_designs`, `mission_query`).

## Technical approach

**The gate, as registered.**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Kill gate | KILL-SOLVE |
| Decided at | Stage 1 before `solve.run` is funded (pilot); stage 2 at the fourth milestone (A/B) |
| Trigger | The ceiling is under 1.4-fold; or agents with `solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs |
| Stage 1 measurement | Over 20 recorded sessions, f = (turns classified as plumbing) ÷ (all turns); ceiling = 1 ÷ (1 − f) |
| Stage 2 measurement | The A/B's verdict on "serial turns per accepted design", one-verb arm against the verbs arm, against the factor registered before the A/B ran |
| Fallback | The prompts |

**What counts as a plumbing turn (DESIGN; fixed before counting, applied by two independent raters).** A turn is
*plumbing* when all three hold: (a) its tool call is the first entry of the previous envelope's `next_actions`,
or differs from it only by an idempotency key or a wait parameter; (b) its arguments are copied from prior
envelopes (identifiers, the scene, the task), with no value chosen by the agent; (c) the agent's stated reason,
where one is recorded, names no alternative considered. A turn that picks among repairs, chooses a budget,
edits a design, decides to stop, or interprets a failure is a *decision* turn. Disagreements between the raters
are counted as decision turns (conservative for the ceiling), and the agreement rate is reported.

**Decision function.**

```
decide(stage1: {sessions: u32, turns: u64, plumbing: u64} | not-run,
       stage2: {verdict: better | not-better | inconclusive, factor_registered: f64,
                ratio_point: f64, ratio_interval: (f64, f64)} | not-run)
  -> does-not-fire | fires(at stage 1 | at stage 2) | undecided
```

1. Stage 1 not run, or fewer than 20 sessions: undecided; `solve.run` is not funded.
2. f = plumbing ÷ turns; ceiling = 1 ÷ (1 − f). If f < 0.30: fires at stage 1. The verb is not built.
3. Otherwise the verb is funded and stage 2 applies. Stage 2 not run: undecided.
4. Stage 2 verdict `better` at the registered factor: does not fire.
5. Stage 2 verdict `not-better` or `inconclusive` at the registered factor: fires at stage 2. (An inconclusive
   A/B does not keep a Frontier verb alive: the burden is on the claim.)

**Procedure.** (1) Stage 1: confirm the pilot was registered before counting, that the classification rule above
is the one used, and that the 20 sessions were selected by a recorded rule (the first 20 complete studio or
mission sessions in the phase ledger after a fixed date), not picked. (2) Compute f and the ceiling; record.
(3) Stage 2: confirm the factor was registered before the A/B, that both arms had equal budgets and the same
briefs, and that turns are counted by the session transcript capture, not by self-report. (4) Apply the
function; write the architecture decision record and the register row from a session that did not build
`solve.run`.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Outcome | What changes |
|---|---|
| Fires at stage 1 | `solve.run` is not built; its registry entry is not added; the task cluster `search` lists another verb in its place; the bead that would build it is closed as not built with the fallback's name. The cascade verb `opt.cascade` is unaffected. The pilot's f and the ceiling stay on record |
| Fires at stage 2 | `solve.run` is withdrawn: registry entry removed at the next registry freeze, MCP tool gone, `frob capabilities` reports it `killed` with fallback "prompts"; `robot-docs` teaches the prompts. The second arm of the mission agent gate is recorded with its measured ratio and the gate's first arm stands on its own |
| Does not fire | `solve.run` stays, labelled Frontier, with the measured ratio and its interval in its documentation in place of the five-fold hypothesis |
| Either firing branch | The prompts remain, unchanged; they were never at risk |

**Fail-closed default until decided.** No `solve.run`; agents use the verbs and the prompts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The classification rule and the session selection rule are registered before the pilot's count.
- [ ] The decision function is tested code and returns the recorded branch on the recorded inputs.
- [ ] The architecture decision record gives sessions, turns, plumbing turns, rater agreement, f, the ceiling,
      and (at stage 2) the registered factor, the measured ratio with its interval and the A/B's verdict.
- [ ] On a firing branch, no served tool list contains `solve_run`, and `robot-docs` does not mention it except
      as killed.
- [ ] The mission agent gate's record shows which branch was in force.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_solve.rs`:
- `ceiling_formula`: f = 0.30 gives 1.4286; f = 0.29 gives 1.4085; f = 0.80 gives 5.0 (the hypothesis's
  five-fold needs four turns in five to be plumbing).
- `below_thirty_percent_fires_stage_1`: 20 sessions, 1,000 turns, 299 plumbing.
- `at_thirty_percent_proceeds`: 300 of 1,000.
- `nineteen_sessions_undecided`.
- `stage2_inconclusive_fires`; `stage2_better_does_not_fire`; `stage2_not_run_undecided`.
- `classifier_examples`: twelve stored turns (six of each kind, with envelopes) are classified as labelled; a
  turn that follows `next_actions[0]` but chooses a budget is a decision turn.
- `rater_disagreement_counts_as_decision`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/kill_solve_decision.sh`: reads the pilot's turn table and the A/B record through the evidence
handles in the gate register; recomputes f, the ceiling and the decision; checks the recorded branch's
consequence from the user's side (`frob capabilities --json` for `solve.run`; the served tool list of the
`search` cluster; the presence of the prompts). NDJSON lines per the shared helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block, f and the ceiling against 0.30 and 1.4,
the ratio and interval against the registered factor, the recomputed branch against the record; `summary` names
KILL-SOLVE, GATE-TETHER-13 and GATE-ATEL-03.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer: 1 ÷ (1 − 0.30) = 1.43; a five-fold ceiling needs f = 0.80.
- Negative control: a pilot whose sessions were selected after reading them (no selection rule on record) is
  inadmissible: undecided.
- Negative control: a stage-2 record whose factor was registered after the A/B's first episode (ledger order)
  is inadmissible.
- Negative control: counting every turn that calls a `read` verb as plumbing inflates f; the classifier examples
  include read turns that are decisions and must not be so counted.

## Considerations

- The hypothesis's own inputs (30 to 120 s per turn) are unmeasured until the wait-parameter experiment
  (N32b-34) and the agent gates run; if turns are cheap, even a real reduction in turns buys little time. The
  gate is about turns because that is what was claimed.
- An orchestration verb hides intermediate evidence from the agent. On the surviving branch its answer must
  still carry every called verb's evidence block and handles, and the budget it spent per stage.
- If the cascade it calls is itself narrowed at its own gates, `solve.run` inherits the narrower pipeline.
