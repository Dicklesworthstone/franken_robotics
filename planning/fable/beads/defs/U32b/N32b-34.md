## Background

Long work in FrankenRobotics is a job owned by the daemon. To spare an agent a round trip for work that
finishes quickly, every MCP tool whose registry kind is `run` accepts `sync_wait_ms`: the handler submits the
job under its idempotency key, then waits on the job's completion against a timer. If the job finishes, the
envelope carries the result (exit 0 or 2). If the timer wins, the envelope carries exit 8 and a job handle with
progress, an interval for the time remaining and `poll_after_ms`, and `next_actions` names `job_wait`, which
takes the same parameter. The values in force are design choices, not measurements: default 5,000 ms, maximum
30,000 ms, 0 for an immediate handle; out of range is refused as a schema failure (`FROB-REF-802`). The server's
request timeout is 40 s so that 10 s of margin cover admission and serialization. On the command line the
default is to wait to completion.

The reasoning behind the bounded wait (decision ADR-TETHER-04) rests on two unmeasured quantities. Most studio
verbs are meant to finish inside the wait (TARGET: `design check` under 2 s, `control synth` under 5 s), and an
agent turn is *believed* to cost 30 to 120 s of thinking (HYPOTHESIS), so an avoided poll saves more than the
compute it waits for. This is the open question OPEN-TETHER-3 (owner: Tether lead; it blocks the mission agent
gate; fail-closed default: 5,000 and 30,000). This bead measures the two quantities and sets the two numbers.

## Technical approach

**Measurements.**

1. *Agent turn time* T: for each consecutive pair of tool calls in a session transcript, the interval from the
   delivery of one call's result to the arrival of the next call, as recorded by the daemon's request log (wall
   clock; this is operational data and never enters a result). Source: the transcripts of the fresh-agent runs
   of the studio gate and of at least 20 further recorded sessions, per harness. Reported: the distribution
   (5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th percentiles) per harness and overall, and whether the 30 to 120 s hypothesis
   holds at the median.
2. *Verb latency* L: per `run` verb and stratum, the distribution of admission-to-terminal time of finished jobs
   from the ledger, on the workloads of the agent sessions (not synthetic). Reported per verb: the share
   finishing within 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and 30 s.
3. *Harness tool-call timeouts*, from the client probe table (N32b-33).

**Cost model of one call** (a simulation over the measured distributions, not a closed form). Policy (W, Wj):
first-call wait W, later `job_wait` wait Wj. For a call with latency L and independent turn times T₁, T₂, …:

- If L ≤ W: wall time = L; turns = 1.
- Else the handle returns at W; the agent thinks T₁ and calls `job_wait` at time W + T₁. If the job is already
  done the call returns at once; otherwise it waits up to Wj, and so on. Wall time is the instant the result is
  delivered; turns counts the calls.

Outputs per policy, averaged over the empirical (verb mix, L, T): mean wall time to result, mean turns per
call, and mean idle agent time (time the agent is blocked in a wait with nothing to decide). The first term
favours long waits when T is large; the third penalizes long waits when an agent could have done something
else, which matters only for harnesses that issue parallel tool calls (recorded per harness).

**Decision rule.**

1. Maximum: W_max = min(30,000 ms, 1,000 × (smallest harness tool-call timeout in the client table − 10 s),
   1,000 × (server request timeout − 10 s)), rounded down to a whole second, and never below 5,000 ms. If a
   harness's timeout forces a value below 30,000, its row in the client table carries its own cap and the global
   maximum stays at the largest value every listed harness accepts.
2. Default: over the grid W ∈ {0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30} s (capped by W_max), with Wj = W_max, choose the W that
   minimizes mean wall time to result; among values within 2% of the minimum choose the smallest (shorter waits
   keep the agent responsive at no measurable cost).
3. The choice is made per surface default only (one number for MCP); per-verb overrides are allowed only where a
   verb's median latency exceeds the default by a factor of four or more, and then its registry entry sets
   `sync_wait_ms` explicitly.
4. If the measured median T is below 5 s (turns are cheap), the hypothesis behind the bounded wait fails; the
   default is set to the 50th percentile of L across calls and the finding is reported to the one-call-pipeline
   decision (N32b-30), whose benefit depends on the same quantity.

**Recording.** The chosen default and maximum go into the registry's per-surface fields (so the parity gate
knows about them, and the schema bound in the `FROB-REF-802` check follows), with a decision record closing
OPEN-TETHER-3 that holds the distributions, the grid's results and the rule's output. Until then the values are
5,000 and 30,000.

**Confirmation run.** The studio and mission session scripts are re-run over MCP with the chosen values and
with the old ones; mean turns per session and wall time are compared. This is a smoke check that the change
does what the model predicted, not a new gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Turn-time and latency distributions are recorded from real sessions, with counts, per harness and per
      verb; the report states whether 30 to 120 s holds at the median.
- [ ] The simulation and the decision rule are tested code; the recorded default and maximum equal the rule's
      output on the recorded data.
- [ ] The registry's fields are updated through the generator; `frob schema --verb sim.run` shows the new bound;
      a `sync_wait_ms` above the new maximum is refused with `FROB-REF-802`.
- [ ] The server's request timeout remains at least the maximum plus 10 s.
- [ ] OPEN-TETHER-3 is closed by a decision record; the mission agent gate's record cites the values in force.
- [ ] If rule 4 applied, the finding is attached to the KILL-SOLVE decision's inputs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_tether_3.rs`:
- `cost_model_fast_job`: L = 2 s, W = 5 s: wall 2 s, 1 turn.
- `cost_model_slow_job_one_poll`: L = 20 s, W = 5 s, T₁ = 60 s, Wj = 30 s: the handle returns at 5 s, the poll at
  65 s finds the job done: wall 65 s, 2 turns.
- `cost_model_slow_job_long_wait`: the same job with W = 30 s: wall 20 s, 1 turn.
- `cost_model_very_slow_job`: L = 200 s, W = 5 s, T = 60 s each, Wj = 30 s: polls at 65 s (waits to 95 s), 155 s
  (waits to 185 s), 245 s (done): wall 245 s, 4 turns.
- `rule_picks_smallest_within_two_percent`.
- `max_respects_harness_timeout`: a harness with a 25 s tool timeout yields a per-harness cap of 15,000 ms.
- `cheap_turns_trigger_rule_4`: median T = 3 s.
- `turn_time_extraction`: on a stored request log, the interval is result-delivery to next-call arrival, not
  call to call.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sync_wait_calibration.sh`: extracts T from the daemon request logs of the recorded sessions and L
from the ledger; runs the simulation over the grid; prints the table (W, mean wall, mean turns, mean idle);
applies the rule; then runs the confirmation comparison over MCP with a scripted client whose think time is
drawn from the measured T. Log: `target/e2e-logs/sync_wait_calibration/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript;
every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block of
tool results in the confirmation run, the percentiles with units, the grid results, the chosen values against
the defaults, and the summary. Transcripts are read as records; no prompt or model output is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the four hand-computed cost-model cases above.
- Negative control: a turn-time extractor that measures call-to-call intervals (including the previous call's
  latency) inflates T; `turn_time_extraction` fails it.
- Negative control: a rule that picks the largest wait within tolerance; the tie-break test fails.
- Negative control: a registry default changed by hand without the decision record fails the generated-file
  check.

## Considerations

- T differs by harness and by model; the default is one number per surface, so it is set for the mix actually
  observed and revisited when the mix changes. The data and the rule are kept so that revisiting is a re-run.
- A wire-cancelled wait leaves the job running (the surface's rule), so a harness whose own timeout is shorter
  than the wait loses nothing but the result of that call, which `job_wait` retrieves; the per-harness cap
  exists to avoid that wasted turn, not to protect state.
- The model ignores queueing delay before a job starts; under load the interval estimate in the handle is what
  the agent should read, and the confirmation run is done on an idle daemon.
