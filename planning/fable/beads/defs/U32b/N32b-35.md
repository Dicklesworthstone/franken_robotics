## Background

The daemon `frobd` shares each GPU among many agents by dispatching episodes into the slots of a persistent
environment and by ordering jobs with size classes, fair sharing and preempt-resume. Four constants shape that
behaviour, and all four were chosen by reasoning, not measurement:

| Constant | Default (TARGET) | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Slot reserve | 5% of slots | Held for the interactive class (410 of 8,192) |
| Per-environment job cap | 64 jobs | Beyond it new work queues or is refused by the admission guard |
| Quantum | 5 s | The longest a batch job runs before a snapshot boundary at which it may be parked |
| Ageing period | 600 s | A waiting job's effective work decays linearly to zero over this time, so no job waits behind an unbounded stream of shorter ones |

This is the open question OPEN-TETHER-4 (owner: Daemon lead; it blocks the scheduling gate; fail-closed default:
5%, 64, 5 s, 600 s). The specification says where the answer comes from: the second milestone's queue trace, the
record of what agents actually submitted once the job engine existed. This bead turns that trace into the four
numbers, by replaying it through the real dispatcher under candidate settings and scoring each with the
dispatcher's own loss function.

Related constants that the same replay can recalibrate, and that are reported but changed only if the evidence
is clear: the interactive class boundary (point estimate at most 30 s of device time), the admission guard
(utilization held under 0.8 per device), the preemption rate limit (one per batch job per 30 s), the batch
floor (at least 30% of device time), and the "estimates unreliable" rule (of the last 50 jobs, 20% ran over
twice their estimate).

## Technical approach

**1. The queue trace** (`frob.queue-trace/1`, newline-delimited JSON; captured by the job engine from the second
milestone on, per device). One record per job: admission instant (daemon clock, nanoseconds as a decimal
string), agent namespace (hashed), verb, shape class, worlds, planned episodes, the estimate and its interval,
the actual attributable device time, the size class assigned, terminal state. One record per device-state
sample: occupied slots, free slots, queue lengths by class. Wall-clock instants are operational data and never
enter a result. Minimum admissible trace: 7 days or 2,000 jobs, whichever is later, from at least 3 agents
(DESIGN thresholds; below them the experiment reports `insufficient-trace` and the defaults stand).

**2. Replay.** The discrete-event simulator feeds the trace's arrivals (same instants, sizes, agents and
estimates) to the real decision code with a candidate constant set, using measured service rates (step time by
world count) and the measured snapshot-and-restore time for preemptions. It emits per-job waits, per-step idle
slots, per-agent deficits and preemption events. Two extra arrival processes test robustness: the trace
time-compressed to utilization 0.7 and 0.8, and a bursty resampling that preserves the trace's squared
coefficient of variation of inter-arrival times c_a².

**3. Loss, in seconds** (the dispatcher's own; DESIGN defaults for the weights, refitted here):

  L = Σ_j c(j)·wait_j + (idle slot-seconds ÷ B) + Σ_a max(0, deficit_a) + Σ preemption cost

with c(j) = 1 for interactive jobs and 0.1 for batch jobs; B the batch capacity; deficit_a the round-robin deficit
of agent a beyond its quantum while others wait; and the cost of a preemption equal to its measured snapshot and
restore time. The weights are refitted only in the sense of being reported with sensitivity: the search is
repeated with c(batch) ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}, and a constant is changed from its default only if the chosen value is
the same under all three.

**4. Search grid.** Reserve ∈ {0, 2, 5, 10, 15}% of slots; job cap ∈ {16, 32, 64, 128}; quantum ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} s;
ageing period ∈ {150, 300, 600, 1,200} s: 320 settings, each replayed on the three arrival processes.

**5. Constraints** (a setting is admissible only if all hold on all three processes): interactive wait at the
95th percentile at most 6 s; batch slowdown at most 10%; no job waits beyond the ageing bound of its setting;
at most one preemption per batch job per 30 s; batch class at least 30% of device time; quantum at least twice
the measured snapshot-and-restore time (otherwise preemption costs more than it yields).

**6. Decision rule.**

1. Among admissible settings take the minimum of mean L over the three processes.
2. Keep the default for any constant whose default lies in a setting within 2% of that minimum (do not move a
   constant without evidence).
3. If no setting is admissible, report which constraint fails for the best setting and keep the defaults; the
   scheduling gate will then show the same failure and the preemption kill gate reads it.
4. Report Kingman's factor (c_a² + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²) from the trace, which the daemon's published capacity table
   applies to the queueing model's waits.

**7. Recording.** The chosen constants are written to the decision-core table DC-09 with the trace's digest, the
grid results (as a content-addressed artifact), the sensitivity table and the rule's output; the table is part
of the daemon's configuration digest, so a change of constants is visible in every job receipt. A decision
record closes OPEN-TETHER-4. The dashboard and `job.list` read the constants from the daemon, never from their
own source.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A queue trace meeting the minimum is captured and stored with its digest; or the report says
      `insufficient-trace` and the defaults are recorded as still in force.
- [ ] The replay of the trace under the constants actually in force during capture reproduces the recorded
      per-job waits within 5% at the median (the simulator is validated against reality before it is used to
      choose).
- [ ] The 320 × 3 replays are stored; the decision rule is tested code and its output equals the recorded
      constants.
- [ ] The DC-09 table holds the constants with the trace digest; `frobd`'s configuration digest changes when a
      constant changes; a job receipt names the digest.
- [ ] The sensitivity table shows each constant's choice under the three batch weights.
- [ ] OPEN-TETHER-4 is closed by a decision record; the scheduling gate's record cites the constants in force.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_tether_4.rs`:
- `loss_terms_on_hand_trace`: two interactive jobs waiting 2 s and 4 s, one batch job waiting 100 s, 4,096 idle
  slot-seconds at B = 8,192, no deficit, one preemption costing 0.8 s: L = (2 + 4) + 0.1·100 + 0.5 + 0 + 0.8 =
  17.3 s.
- `reserve_slots_rounding`: 5% of 8,192 is 409.6, reserved as 410; 2% is 164; 0% is 0.
- `ageing_key`: effective work of an 1,800 s job at ages 0, 150, 300 and 600 s under a 600 s period: 1,800,
  1,350, 900, 0.
- `constraint_filter`: a setting with interactive 95th-percentile wait 6.1 s is inadmissible.
- `rule_keeps_default_within_two_percent`.
- `rule_no_admissible_setting_keeps_defaults`.
- `kingman_factor_from_trace`: c_a² = 4 and c_s² = 8.07 give 1.331.
- `insufficient_trace_detected`: 1,999 jobs over 9 days.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/dispatcher_calibration.sh <trace>`: validates the trace; replays it under the in-force constants
and compares with recorded waits; runs the grid; prints the admissible set, the loss table and the chosen
constants; writes the DC-09 update and shows `frobd` picking it up (`frob doctor --json` reports the new
configuration digest; `frob job list --json` reports the constants). Log:
`target/e2e-logs/dispatcher_calibration/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript; every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block of verb answers, measured
values with units and thresholds (validation error against 5%; per-setting percentiles against 6 s and 10%;
loss), the trace digest, and on failure the first violated constraint or the first job whose replayed wait
departs from the record. Agent namespaces appear only as hashes.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the hand trace's loss 17.3 s; the 410-slot reserve; with 5% reserved, 40 comparisons of 192
  slots fit in 8,192 with 102 slots to spare.
- Negative control: a simulator that ignores preemption cost prefers a 1 s quantum; with the measured cost
  included and the "quantum at least twice the cost" constraint, that setting must be inadmissible when the
  cost is 0.8 s.
- Negative control: a replay with ageing disabled must show a job beyond the ageing bound on the bursty process.
- Negative control: constants edited in the core table without a trace digest are rejected by the table loader
  as stale against their contract.

## Considerations

- A second-milestone trace reflects second-milestone usage: few agents, development workloads. The constants are
  therefore re-examined when the swarm grows; the experiment is designed to be re-run on any later trace.
- The search optimizes the dispatcher's loss, whose weights are themselves judgments. The sensitivity rule
  (change a constant only when the choice is stable across weights) keeps the experiment from laundering a
  weight into a "measured" constant.
- Nothing here may feed back into results: the constants change when work runs, never what is computed, and the
  slot-invariance and resume-digest checks hold for every setting in the grid by construction.
