## Background

FrankenRobotics runs on two machines: a Linux host with two CUDA cards and a Mac with a Metal device. Each runs
its own daemon `frobd`; there is one daemon per host and nothing is replicated (DESIGN: consensus protocols
solve a problem this system does not have). A proposed Frontier addition is a **peer link** between the two
daemons that forwards whole jobs: when one machine queues work while the other idles, the busy one hands a job
across. Only small, derived things would cross the link: seeds, recipes, design and module differences,
receipts and policy weights. Scene scans and other bulk or private data never cross; a job that needs a private
scan runs where the scan is.

The link is plumbing with upkeep: a second protocol, authentication between hosts, failure modes when the link
drops mid-job, and budgets that span daemons. So its ceiling is stated first, as the project's rule requires:
the benefit is at most the device time one machine idles while the other queues. The pilot is on paper: replay
a week of both machines' queue traces and measure that overlap. Under 10% overlap of idle and queued time
(TARGET), the link is not built and the machines run standalone.

This is the open question OPEN-TETHER-5. Its owner is the project owner; it is due at the fourth milestone; its
fail-closed default is **standalone machines**, under which the fleet scheduler assigns jobs by the role table
only (each machine has a declared role for each kind of work, and an agent or the scheduler submits to the
machine whose role fits). This bead prepares the decision: it defines the measurement precisely, applies the
pre-stated threshold, and records the owner's ruling with its consequences.

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** One week (at least 7 × 24 h of the same calendar interval) of `frob.queue-trace/1` from each
machine, after the daemon with slot dispatch exists. Each trace holds, per job, admission and start instants,
shape class, tier requested, worlds, attributable device time and privacy mark; and, per device, sampled
occupied and free slots.

**Definitions.**

- *Idle capacity* of machine X at time t: free slots beyond the reserve on any device of X, as a fraction of X's
  total slots.
- *Transferable queued work* on machine Y at time t: jobs admitted and not yet started on Y that could run on X
  without changing what they claim. A job is transferable when all hold: (a) none of its inputs is private or
  otherwise barred from crossing machines; (b) X has a backend that satisfies the job's requested tier and
  determinism class (a job that asked for bit-reproducibility against earlier runs on one device family is not
  transferable to the other family); (c) its shape class is admitted on X; (d) its estimated remaining work
  exceeds a transfer overhead of 5 s (DESIGN) so that forwarding could pay.
- *Overlap* O: the fraction of the week during which (X has idle capacity and Y has transferable queued work)
  or the reverse.
- *Recoverable device time* R: ∫ min(idle device-seconds available on the idle machine, device-seconds of
  transferable queued work on the busy one) dt over the week, in device-hours, computed with a greedy replay
  that forwards the oldest transferable job first and charges each forward the transfer overhead.
- *Ceiling of the benefit*: R divided by the total device-hours used on both machines that week: the largest
  share of throughput the link could add.

**The pre-stated rule.** If O < 10% (TARGET), the link is not built: the decision is "standalone" without
further argument, and the pilot's numbers are recorded. If O ≥ 10%, the question goes to the owner with the memo
below; the default remains standalone until the owner rules.

**The decision memo** (generated from the computation, at most two pages): O with a day-by-day breakdown; R and
the ceiling; the share of queued work that was not transferable and why (privacy, device family, shape class);
the mean wait that forwarding would have removed in the greedy replay; the upkeep list (a host-to-host
authenticated channel; job hand-off with idempotency keys spanning daemons; receipts and budgets charged on the
executing daemon and reported to the submitting one; behaviour when the link drops: the job is owned by exactly
one daemon at every instant and is found by its key there); and the alternative that costs nothing: agents or
the fleet scheduler submitting to the idle machine directly by the role table.

**Consequences by branch.**

| Ruling | What changes |
|---|---|
| Standalone (default, or O < 10%) | No peer link is built; its beads are closed as not built. The fleet scheduler assigns by the role table only. Shared views (archive, leaderboard) still merge between machines by exchanging receipts as files, because that needs no link. `frob capabilities` reports the peer link `not-in-build` with fallback "standalone machines" |
| Build the link | The link's mechanism is funded as Frontier work with its own acceptance: every forwarded job has exactly one owner daemon; one job and one charge per key across both daemons; nothing private crosses (checked on the wire); a dropped link leaves both ledgers consistent. A new kill gate is registered with it. The fleet scheduler may then plan forwards |

**Fail-closed default until decided.** Standalone machines.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Two week-long traces are captured with digests, or the bead records that they do not yet exist and the
      default stands.
- [ ] O, R and the ceiling are computed by tested code from the traces; the non-transferable share is broken
      down by reason.
- [ ] The rule is applied: O < 10% closes the question as standalone; otherwise the memo is delivered and the
      owner's ruling is recorded verbatim with its date.
- [ ] The decision record closes OPEN-TETHER-5 and names the consequences applied.
- [ ] The fleet scheduler's configuration reflects the ruling (`fleet.plan` forwards nothing under standalone).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_tether_5.rs`:
- `overlap_on_hand_traces`: over a 100 h window, machine A idle during hours [0, 40) and machine B holding
  transferable queued work during [30, 50): O = 10 h ÷ 100 h = 10%, which does not meet "under 10%".
- `overlap_just_under`: B queued during [31, 50): O = 9%: standalone.
- `private_work_not_transferable`: the same traces with B's queued jobs marked private: O = 0.
- `device_family_not_transferable`: a job pinned to the CUDA family queued on the Linux host is not counted
  against Mac idle time.
- `recoverable_time_greedy`: 10 idle device-hours against 6 device-hours of transferable work recover 6 minus
  overhead; against 20, recover 10 minus overhead.
- `rule_under_ten_percent_is_standalone`; `rule_at_or_over_goes_to_owner`.
- `short_trace_rejected`: 6 days.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/peer_link_pilot.sh <trace-A> <trace-B>`: validates both traces and their common interval; computes
O, R, the ceiling and the breakdown; applies the rule; writes the memo; then checks the recorded ruling from the
user's side (`frob fleet plan --json` shows no forwards under standalone; `frob capabilities --json` shows the
link's status). Log: `target/e2e-logs/peer_link_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript; every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the `evidence` block of verb answers, O
against 10%, R in device-hours, the ceiling, the trace digests, and on failure the first inadmissible record.
Agent namespaces appear as hashes; no scene or design name is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the hand traces above (10%, 9%, 0%).
- Negative control: an overlap computation that counts all queued work as transferable reports 10% where the
  private-work case must report 0.
- Negative control: a ruling recorded as "build" with O < 10% and no owner statement is rejected: below the
  threshold the rule alone decides, and it decides standalone.
- Negative control: traces from different weeks are rejected (no common interval).

## Considerations

- The two machines are different device families. Much queued work may be non-transferable for determinism
  reasons alone, which lowers the ceiling before any measurement; the breakdown makes that visible.
- Privacy is a hard boundary: only seeds, recipes, differences, receipts and weights may cross, and weights
  trained in a private scene inherit its mark. A link that made crossing easy would need the same checks as
  export.
- The cheap alternative, submitting to the idle machine by role, captures part of R with no link at all; the
  memo reports how much, since that is the baseline the link must beat.
