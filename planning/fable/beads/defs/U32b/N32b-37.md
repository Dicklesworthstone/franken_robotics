## Background

Two acceptance gates of the agent surface score whether a fresh agent can *complete tasks* with only the
generated documentation. Completion is not the whole of competence. An engineer's day is mostly setups that are
wrong in ordinary ways: a unit slip, a floor too slippery, a current limit set too low, a collision shape that
does not match what is drawn, an observation that arrives too late. A useful surface lets an agent find such a
cause quickly and cheaply, and, just as important, lets it say "nothing is wrong" or "the evidence cannot tell"
when that is the truth. The **agent diagnosis battery** is the acceptance test for that: thirty tasks, each a
setup with a seeded cause or with none, handed to a fresh agent with only the generated documentation and a
budget, and scored on diagnosis and on restraint (GATE-TETHER-15, Design comparison, third milestone).

This bead builds the battery itself: the thirty task packages, the rule by which each cause is seeded, the
minimal evidence that identifies it, and the answer key. Scoring and the gate run are N32b-39; capture of what
the agent did is N32b-38. Tier: Solid. Owner crate: `frob-conformance`.

The battery lives on the two registered fixtures. *Fixture 1* (`fixture:cart-sill-dock`): a front-wheel-drive
cart of 8.000 kg (design A, 60 mm front wheels) or 8.225 kg (design B, 75 mm) that must cross a 12 mm door sill
and dock; motor torque constant 0.2 N·m/A, current limit ±6 A per driven wheel, friction 0.8 nominal. *Fixture
2* (`fixture:quad-split-grip`): the reference 12-actuator quadruped climbing a 6° ramp onto a landing with a
low-grip strip (friction 0.25 against 0.8). Both have analytic layers whose rows are known answers, and several
of the seeded causes are identified by one of those rows without any simulation.

## Scope

In: the task package format; the seven classes and their seeding rules; per-class minimal evidence and minimal
simulation count; the answer key and how it is hidden; randomization of order; validation that each seeded task
is identifiable by its minimal evidence and each unseeded task is not misidentifiable; the no-defect and
inconclusive tasks.
Out: transcript capture and the clean-client reproduction (N32b-38); the scorer, the two arms and the gate
record (N32b-39); the fixtures and the verbs.

## Technical approach

**Composition.** Thirty tasks: four per seeded class (five classes, twenty tasks), five with no defect, five
inconclusive. Order is randomized per arm from a logged seed; the class is hidden from the agent. Within each
seeded class two tasks are on fixture 1 and two on fixture 2 where the class applies to both; otherwise all four
are on the fixture it applies to (noted below).

| Class | Seeded cause | The minimal evidence that identifies it | Minimal simulations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong units | A length entered in millimetres where metres are declared, inside the admissible range | The mass roll-up of `design.check` against the declared budget | 0 |
| Insufficient friction | Floor friction below the creep threshold of the lip | The friction-demand row of the analytic layer; no rollout is needed | 0 |
| Low current limit | A driver limit below the stall requirement | The tread-force cap row, and one `sim.run` to confirm the stall reason | 1 |
| Hidden visual and collision mismatch | A collision shape that differs from the visual by more than the pack threshold | The collision-to-visual deviation of `scene.accept` | 0 |
| Delayed observations | A packet latency beyond the decision deadline | `sense.causal-test` and the sample-age field | 0 (the causal test is its own evidence; no task rollout is needed) |
| No defect (5 tasks) | None | A passing verdict; the correct answer is that nothing is wrong | 1 |
| Inconclusive (5 tasks) | The evidence supplied cannot decide between two causes | The correct answer is `unknown`, with the experiment from `experiment.suggest` that would decide | 0 |

**Seeding rules, with the numbers that make each identifiable (DESIGN; each instance is validated before it
enters the battery).**

- *Wrong units.* One length field of the design holds the millimetre figure in a field declared in metres. The
  field is chosen so that the wrong value passes every schema and range check (no unit or dimension refusal
  fires), and so that the mass roll-up departs from the declared mass budget by at least 5%: a part whose mass
  is computed from density and dimensions. The declared budgets are the fixtures' known masses (8.000 kg and
  8.225 kg for the carts). The task statement gives the symptom only ("the cart no longer docks", "the quadruped
  tips on the ramp").
- *Insufficient friction.* Fixture 1 only (all four tasks). The floor friction is set below the creep threshold
  of the front lift-off at the lip, μ ≥ tan θ with tan θ = x/(r − h), x = √(2rh − h²), r the wheel radius and h the
  lip height: 0.7500 for design A and 0.6459 for design B at a sharp 12 mm lip (0.7332 and 0.6351 with 2 mm
  fillets). Seeded values are at least 0.05 below the threshold of the task's design and edge (for example 0.65
  for A, sharp), and the creep-speed rig is the stated operating mode, so that the analytic row decides.
- *Low current limit.* Fixture 1 (two tasks) and fixture 2 (two tasks). On fixture 1 the driver limit is lowered
  so that the tread-force cap 2·k_t·I_max/r falls below the traction the manoeuvre needs: at 5 A the cap for
  design A is 2 × 0.2 × 5 ÷ 0.060 = 33.3 N, below the 34.2 N the rear pull-over needs at zero payload offset (at
  the fixture's 6 A the cap is 40 N). One `sim.run` then shows the stall reason (command saturated and less than
  2 mm of advance over 0.5 s). On fixture 2 a hip or knee driver limit is set below the static holding torque
  on the 6° ramp, computed by the actuator-envelope analysis.
- *Hidden visual and collision mismatch.* The scene pack's collision field for one fixture element (the sill; a
  step of the ramp) is displaced or resized relative to its visual mesh by more than the pack threshold, with a
  waiver planted so that the pack loads. The deviation report of `scene.accept` lists the region and the
  deviation; stills, which are drawn from the collision field, also show it.
- *Delayed observations.* The link model of one sensor is given a latency such that the packet's arrival is
  after the decision instant it should inform (for the docking controller's wheel-rate loop at 100 Hz, a latency
  above 10 ms). `sense.causal-test` and the observation's age field (decision minus sample, in clock quanta)
  identify it.
- *No defect.* The unmodified fixture in a condition where it passes its requirement; the task statement
  reports a vague worry ("check this setup before we run the batch"). Correct answer: no defect, citing the
  passing verdict.
- *Inconclusive.* Two causes from the classes above are both consistent with the evidence the task supplies, and
  the discriminating evidence is withheld by construction (for example a run record with telemetry level too low
  to show sample ages, and a scene whose acceptance report is absent, with the design sealed against re-checks
  by the task's budget of zero simulations and no `scene.accept` scope). Correct answer: `unknown`, naming the
  two candidates and the experiment `experiment.suggest` returns to separate them.

**Task package** (`frob.diagnosis-task/1`, one directory per task under `fixtures/tether/diagnosis/`):
`task.json` (identifier, fixture, the statement shown to the agent, the budget, the scopes of the session,
the objects pre-loaded in the namespace); the seeded objects (design, scene, task, sensor configuration) as
content addresses; and, in a separate sealed store readable only by the scorer's owner-held scope, `key.json`:
class, seeded cause as a structured record (`{kind, object, field, nominal, seeded}`), the minimal evidence as a
list of verb calls with the field that shows the cause, the minimal simulation count, and for inconclusive
tasks the two candidates and the deciding experiment. The agent's session token cannot read the key store.

**Validation of every task before admission.** (1) *Identifiability:* a scripted reference client executes only
the minimal evidence and a rule-based reader recovers the seeded cause from the named fields. (2)
*Non-identifiability of the others:* on a no-defect task the same client finds a passing verdict and no row
that fails; on an inconclusive task the supplied evidence is consistent with both candidates (checked by
swapping which cause is actually seeded: the supplied evidence is byte-identical). (3) *No leak:* the task
statement, object names and identifiers contain no class word and no value that reveals the seed (a lint over
the statements). (4) *Stability:* the minimal evidence gives the same fields on the referee and on `batch-cpu`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Thirty validated task packages exist: 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 seeded, 5 no-defect, 5 inconclusive.
- [ ] For each seeded task the reference client recovers the cause from the minimal evidence alone, with the
      stated number of simulations.
- [ ] For each inconclusive task, swapping the true cause leaves the supplied evidence byte-identical.
- [ ] The key store is unreadable with any agent-grantable scope (attempt refused); the statement lint passes.
- [ ] Task order for an arm is a function of the logged seed.
- [ ] The analytic thresholds used by the seeds are recomputed in a test from fixture parameters.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/diagnosis_battery.rs`:
- `composition_counts`: 20 seeded in five classes of four, 5 and 5.
- `friction_thresholds`: x = √(2·60·12 − 12²) = 36.000 mm and tan θ = 36/48 = 0.7500 for r = 60 mm; x = 40.694 mm
  and tan θ = 40.694/63 = 0.6459 for r = 75 mm.
- `tread_force_caps`: 2 × 0.2 × 6 ÷ 0.060 = 40 N and ÷ 0.075 = 32 N; at 5 A, 33.3 N for design A.
- `rear_pull_over_demand`: W = 8.000 × 9.81 = 78.48 N; V = W × 32/55 = 45.66 N; demand V × 0.75 = 34.2 N.
- `units_seed_passes_range_checks`: `design.check` on each wrong-units task returns findings, not a refusal, and
  the mass roll-up differs from the budget by at least 5%.
- `reference_client_recovers_each_seed` (20 tasks).
- `no_defect_tasks_pass`; `inconclusive_swap_is_byte_identical` (5 tasks).
- `key_store_sealed`; `statements_do_not_leak`.
- `order_is_seeded`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/diagnosis_battery.sh` (owned by N32b-39) has a first phase `build-*` from this bead: it
materializes the thirty tasks in a fresh state root through `frob` verbs (`design new`, `design edit`,
`scene compile`, `task new`), runs the validation client on each, and writes a validation table. NDJSON lines
per the shared helper: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step` (task identifier), `event`, command, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's `evidence` block, simulations used against the minimal count, the recovered cause against
the key (logged only as match or mismatch: the key itself is never logged), and on failure the task and the
validation rule that failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the analytic rows above (0.7500, 0.6459, 0.7332, 0.6351; 40 N, 32 N; 34.2 N).
- Negative control: a wrong-units task whose seeded value is refused by a range check is rejected by validation
  (the cause would be handed to the agent by the refusal).
- Negative control: an "inconclusive" task in which one supplied field differs between the two causes is
  rejected by the swap test.
- Negative control: a statement containing the word "friction" on a friction task fails the lint.

## Considerations

- A battery can be memorized. The packages are parameterized (which field, which design, which offset below the
  threshold), and a fresh instantiation from a new seed is used for each recorded gate run; the answer key is
  sealed in the same way as confirmation worlds.
- "Inside the admissible range" for the unit slip is the hard part of seeding: a thousand-fold error passes
  range checks only in fields whose legal range is wide. Candidates are found by scanning the design schema for
  length fields whose range spans three decades; if a fixture has none whose error moves the mass roll-up, its
  two unit tasks move to the other fixture.
- The battery measures the surface, not the agent. A weak model failing it is uninformative; the comparison arm
  of the gate exists so that the surface's contribution is the difference between arms.
