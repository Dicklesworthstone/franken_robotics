## Background

A milestone of FrankenRobotics is a set of demonstrations with recorded values, not a date. **M0, "spine and
airlock"**, exists to retire the risks that could invalidate the architecture or the thesis with the smallest
possible amount of code. It answers two questions by measurement: can the sibling foundations be consumed from
outside their trees (bet BET-04), and is the rival's design loop what the plan composed from published figures
(the denominator of bet BET-13). No subsystem is built out at M0.

An exit is recorded by **a fresh-context session that did not implement the milestone**. The rule comes from
the sibling projects' experience of agent swarms producing the appearance of progress: no self-certification,
typed close reasons that cite a test and its output, and a negative control for every gate. This bead is that
session's work order for M0. It adds two decisions that must be on record at this exit: the kill gates
**KILL-FSIM** (is FrankenSim consumed by path, or through a pinned worker process, or lifted) and **KILL-MCP**
(is the MCP server a member of the core build graph, or its own workspace over a local socket, or absent with
the CLI alone), each recorded as an architecture decision record whether or not it fired; and the state of the
**two P0 upstream work items**, FD-FMCP-01 (fastmcp_rust brought onto the one toolchain T in its own
repository) and FD-ASUP-01 (FrankenSim's constellation, FrankenTorch and fastmcp_rust aligned on one published
asupersync version by changes in those repositories).

A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan, and from then on the
capability is described by the fallback's name.

## Scope

In: the review procedure; the list of gates due at M0; the re-run of demonstrations from a clean checkout; the
control sweep; the two kill-gate ADRs; the upstream-item record; the open questions and risks due; the stop
decisions; the exit record; the script that does the mechanical part.
Out: implementing or repairing anything (a failing gate is recorded as failing, and repair is new work for the
implementing beads); the decision beads' own analysis; later exits.

## Technical approach

**Who.** A session with a fresh context whose identity differs from `recorded.implemented_by` of every gate it
records. It reads this bead, the gate registry, the admission records and the code; it does not read the
implementers' transcripts as evidence.

**The exit rules (all must hold).**
1. Every gate of the milestone has a recorded value. A failing value is a record. A status of `not-run` blocks
   the exit. A kill gate that fired is recorded as an ADR with the fallback now in force.
2. Every demonstration runs from a clean checkout, through `frob --json` and, where the registry exposes the
   verb to agents, through `frob-mcp`.
3. GATE-GAUNT-01 holds: every gate that passed has a registered negative control that it catches, shown by a
   run over all controls in which each is caught by every gate it names.
4. The risk register has been read, and the open questions due have been answered or have taken their
   fail-closed defaults.
5. No PASS stands on a dirty sibling tree: each cited stack receipt has `dirty = false` for every sibling.

**Gates due at M0 (the gate register governs; thresholds are in each gate's own bead).**
- Admission: INT-TOOLCHAIN-01; INT-FSIM-01 to INT-FSIM-11 and INT-FSIM-18; INT-ASUP-01; INT-FSQL-01; INT-FT-01
  and INT-FT-02; INT-CUDA-01; INT-FSYM-01 and INT-FSYM-02; INT-FMCP-01 (its admission part); INT-FNP-01 and
  INT-FNP-02.
- Convergence: GATE-LEMMA-00 and GATE-LEMMA-06; GATE-MARROW-01 for SO(3) and SE(3); GATE-SPARK-00, -01, -07,
  -08, and the rows of GATE-SPARK-21 on `trj` that need no agent; GATE-TETHER-03; GATE-WS-01 and GATE-WS-04;
  the M0 parts of the later workspace gates indexed for M0 (GATE-WS-05, GATE-WS-06, GATE-WS-07, GATE-WS-09).
- Gauntlet: GATE-GAUNT-01, GATE-GAUNT-05 (oracle hygiene), GATE-GAUNT-08 (the plan-time pilots re-run and
  reproduce every count exactly) and GATE-GAUNT-10 (status accounting).
- Program: GATE-TENFOLD-01 (the parity baseline read into the first phase ledger).
- Kill gates decided: KILL-FSIM; KILL-MCP.

**Procedure (numbered; steps 2 to 6 and 10 are run by `scripts/e2e/exit_m0.sh`).**
1. Record the session identity and confirm it differs from every implementer of M0.
2. Clean checkout; run the six workspace checks; `xtask check-stack --json` must report `may_record_pass:
   true`. If a sibling is dirty or off-pin, stop: a gate run waits for a clean tree.
3. Query `registries/gates.toml` for every identifier above. Tabulate status, measured value against threshold,
   receipt hash, control identifiers and recorder. Any `not-run`, any `pass` without a caught control, any
   record whose recorder equals its implementer, any evidence that does not resolve in the artifact graph: a
   finding that blocks the exit.
4. Control sweep: run every registered control of the M0 gates; each must be caught by every gate it names. A
   control that has never failed its gate in any recorded run is listed as suspect.
5. Re-run the M0 demonstrations from their recorded commands and compare with the records:
   `frob doctor` lists the admitted foundations with their records and the stack receipt;
   `frob sim run g1 --tier referee --seconds 2` gives a state digest equal to FrankenSim's own, and the same
   job through `frob-fsim-worker` gives equal chunk digests; `frob spark probe` and `frob spark fingerprint`
   run on both device families; the Cayley step kernel runs from one source on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal;
   the exact and guarded predicate kernels give identical bytes on all three; the certificates for theorem
   cards T-01, T-03 and T-04 replay offline; the M0 verbs answer through `frob --json` and through `frob-mcp`;
   the incumbent phase ledger on `trj` exists with its disclosure blocks; the plan-time pilots re-run with
   their recorded counts.
6. Regenerate `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` and the README status table and confirm they equal the checked-in
   files.
7. **KILL-FSIM.** Read the trigger ledger's evaluation (bead N33a-10): hours of effort on INT-FSIM-01 against
   80; the cold check against one hour, 16 GiB and 8 cores; the largest count of breakage days in any 28-day
   window against 4. Write `docs/delivery/adr/ADR-KILL-FSIM-M0.md`: the three measured values, fired or not,
   and the posture in force: path consumption behind the airlock; or fallback (a), one pinned FrankenSim worker
   process per device exchanging whole jobs (already exercised by INT-FSIM-18); or fallback (b), a lift of the
   minimal referee set, only if (a) failed INT-FSIM-18 or the pin can no longer be built. State that the gate
   is re-read at every later exit.
8. **KILL-MCP and the P0 upstream items.** For each of FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01 record: the sibling
   repository and commit where the change landed, that it landed on the sibling's `main` through the sibling's
   own gates, the mirrored bead identifiers in both trackers, and the evidence (for FD-FMCP-01: fastmcp_rust's
   own test suite and MCP conformance checks green on T; for FD-ASUP-01: `cargo tree -d` showing one
   asupersync package at the one published version in each workspace). If either has been attempted and has
   not landed, say so. Write `ADR-KILL-MCP-M0.md`: INT-FMCP-01's record; fired only if the gate cannot pass
   within the M0 budget after both items were attempted; the posture in force: `frob-mcp` in the core graph on
   T running verbs in process; or fallback 1, `frob-mcp` as its own workspace talking to the daemon over a
   local socket in newline-delimited JSON; or fallback 2, agents use the CLI, which is complete on its own.
   Note that KILL-MCP is re-read at M1 with the M1 verbs.
9. Confirm that the choice of T and the decision on the units escape hatch exist as ADRs with the matrix they
   were read from.
10. Open questions due at M0, each answered or defaulted in the exit record: OPEN-SPARK-1 and OPEN-SPARK-2 (the
    compute envelope classes; default: 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device); OPEN-PROG-1 (before the first
    push); OPEN-SUITE-3 (dirty sibling checkouts: yes for development with the tree hash in the receipt, no
    PASS); OPEN-SUITE-5 (the cold-build budget: the machine envelope); OPEN-PERF-2 (the comparator revisions).
11. Risks read at this exit, with their leading indicators' current values: RISK-01 (FrankenSim API churn),
    RISK-02 (the toolchain train), RISK-03 (two asupersync versions in one build), RISK-23 (dirty trees).
12. **Stop decisions.** Re-plan before M1 if INT-FSIM-01 could not pass within two weeks and the worker also
    failed INT-FSIM-18 (the only route left is a lift from a closure of about 1.5 million lines). Rewrite the
    ten-fold thesis chapter before any work is funded against it if the measured phase ledger puts its
    ceilings under 3 for every mission class. Drop a device family from M2 if the probe shows it can hold no
    controlled determinism class. Only the first stops the CPU studio.
13. Write the exit record `docs/delivery/exits/M0.md`: the gate table, the control sweep, the two ADRs, the
    upstream record, defaults taken, stop decisions, and the verdict `exited`, `exited with fallbacks` or `not
    exited` with the blocking findings. Close this bead with a typed reason citing the script's log.

**The observable.** M0's exit line is: the admission records, one kernel on three backends, and the incumbent
phase ledger on `trj`. A milestone does not exit on a count of gates.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every gate of M0 has a recorded value and a caught negative control (seed done-condition; GATE-GAUNT-01): the query of step 3 returns no `not-run` and the sweep of step 4 shows every control caught.
- [ ] The recorder of every PASS differs from its implementer, and the reviewing session implemented none of M0.
- [ ] `ADR-KILL-FSIM-M0.md` and `ADR-KILL-MCP-M0.md` exist, each with the measured trigger values, the decision and the posture in force.
- [ ] The state of FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01 is recorded with sibling commits, gate evidence and mirrored bead identifiers, or as attempted and not landed.
- [ ] Every demonstration of step 5 reproduces from a clean checkout; each difference from the record is listed.
- [ ] No cited receipt has a dirty sibling; the generated admission file and status table equal the checked-in files.
- [ ] The exit record states the defaults taken for each open question due, the risks read, the stop decisions and the verdict.
- [ ] Gates the register dates at M0 whose records are missing (for example INT-FSIM-08, -09, -10 if their beads have not run) are listed as blocking, not waived.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance exit_review`)
- `exit_query_blocks_on_not_run`: a synthetic registry with one M0 gate `not-run` → verdict `not exited`, naming the gate.
- `exit_query_blocks_on_uncaught_control`, `exit_query_blocks_on_self_certification`, `exit_query_blocks_on_dirty_receipt`.
- `exit_query_accepts_fail_with_kill_adr`: a failed admission gate whose kill gate is `killed` with a fallback named → `exited with fallbacks`.
- `exit_gate_list_is_complete`: the M0 identifier list in the query equals the list above (a registry row dated M0 that the query omits fails the test).
- `suspect_controls_listed`: a control with no recorded catch appears in the suspect list.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/exit_m0.sh`, run by the reviewing session on the Mac and, for device steps, on `trj`. It runs the
six checks, `frob doctor --json`, the gate query, the control sweep, each demonstration command of step 5 and
the two regenerations. Logging per the shared helper of `STD-E2E`: every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `assert`
lines per gate with its measured value, unit, threshold, receipt hash, control identifiers and `caught`;
digests for the referee and worker rollouts with the first differing chunk on failure; a `metric` line for
each KILL-FSIM trigger value; a final `summary` with counts of gates passed, failed, killed and not run, and
the gates touched. Logs go to `target/e2e-logs/exit_m0/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it, and contain
no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers re-observed: the G1 digest equality; KA-767 (a receipt computed twice is byte-identical); KA-777 (element 3, not 12); KA-780 (worker digests equal in-process digests); the docking-grid counts of the plan-time pilot (344 admitted, 217 excluded, 9 on the boundary).
- Negative controls of the review itself (registered for GATE-GAUNT-10 and GATE-GAUNT-01): a planted self-certified PASS and a planted PASS with an uncaught control are each refused by the query; a planted gate dated M0 that the script does not check is caught by `exit_gate_list_is_complete`.
- Determinism lanes: the query over the same registry gives the same verdict and bytes on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The review must be allowed to end `not exited`. Its value is that it can.
- KILL-FSIM is "M0, then rolling": this ADR is the first of a series; the breakage-share trigger can only fire after weeks of data.
- If INT-FSIM-01 fails outright, M1 can still proceed on fallback (a) if INT-FSIM-18 passed; that combination is recorded as `exited with fallbacks`, and the README says "referee by pinned worker".
- The upstream items live in other repositories under their own rules; the review reads their state and never edits a sibling.
- Runs stay inside the machine envelope; device steps are scheduled one device at a time.
- The same procedure, with the M1 gate list and the schedule gate of bet BET-12, is the M1 exit review (bead BD-147).
