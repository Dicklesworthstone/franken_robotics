## Background

A milestone of FrankenRobotics is a set of demonstrations with recorded values, not a date. **M0, "spine and
airlock"**, exists to retire the risks that could invalidate the architecture or the thesis with the smallest
possible amount of code. It answers two questions by measurement: can the sibling foundations be consumed from
outside their trees (bet BET-04), and is the rival's design loop what the plan composed from published figures
(the denominator of bet BET-13). No subsystem is built out at M0.

An exit is recorded by **a fresh-context session that did not implement the milestone**. The rule comes from
the sibling projects' experience of agent swarms producing the appearance of progress: no self-certification,
typed close reasons that cite a test and its output, and a negative control for every gate. This bead is that
session's work order for M0. It adds two decisions that must be on record at this exit: the kill gates
**KILL-FSIM** (is FrankenSim consumed by path, or through a pinned worker process, or lifted) and **KILL-MCP**
(is the MCP server a member of the core build graph, or its own workspace over a local socket, or absent with
the CLI alone), each recorded as an architecture decision record whether or not it fired; and the state of the
**two P0 upstream work items**, FD-FMCP-01 (fastmcp_rust brought onto the one toolchain T in its own
repository) and FD-ASUP-01 (FrankenSim's constellation, FrankenTorch and fastmcp_rust aligned on one published
asupersync version by changes in those repositories).

A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan, and from then on the
capability is described by the fallback's name.

## Scope

In: the review procedure; the list of gates due at M0; the re-run of demonstrations from a clean checkout; the
control sweep; the check of the two kill-gate ADRs against their measurements; the upstream-item record; the
open questions and risks due; the stop decisions; the exit record; the script that does the mechanical part.
Out: implementing or repairing anything (a failing gate is recorded as failing, and repair is new work for the
implementing beads); the decision beads' own analysis and their ADRs (KILL-FSIM: N33b-05; KILL-MCP: N32a-05;
each writes its decision record at `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-M0.md` in the one form of N40-03, and this review
writes no second record); later exits.

## Technical approach

**Who.** A session with a fresh context whose identity differs from `recorded.implemented_by` of every gate it
records. It reads this bead, the gate registry, the admission records and the code; it does not read the
implementers' transcripts as evidence.

**How the review is run (one procedure, applied to M0).** This is the exit protocol of N40-05 with the M0 gate
list: the gate query is N40-04's, the runner is `scripts/exit/review.sh M0`, and the exit-time checks that
other beads attach to the runner (`scripts/exit/checks.toml`) are run by it, each landing as a section of the
exit record and each of its blocks making the verdict `not exited`. This bead writes no second query and no
second runner; `scripts/e2e/exit_m0.sh` wraps the runner and adds the M0 demonstrations. Before the runner, the
script runs `scripts/e2e/registers_exit.sh` (N41-04), which shows on a scratch root that this runner still
blocks on a fired risk signal with no outcome, on a question decided by silence and on a self-review. The
attached checks read at M0, with their owners: `firewall` (N40-07), `tracker-check` (N41-16: no cycle, no
orphan, every gate carried, every bead with a test plan), `principles` (N00-21: per principle, enforced, not yet
enforced or unproven; at M0 principles 4, 10, 11 and 12 must be enforced and proven, and an unproven one
blocks), `risk-review`, `risks-complete` and `risk-plants` (N41-01, N41-32), `premortem` (N41-PM: outcome
reasons 1 and 2 are readable at M0), `open-complete` and `owner-calendar` (N41-03, N40-35), `visibility-guard`
(N41-05; `unverified` is a warning), `moonshot-cap` (N10a-02) and `decision-sweep`, which runs
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin decision-sweep -- check --exit M0` over the M0 sweep record (the tool is
NAB-22's; the sweep itself is NAB-23, a dependency, made at the exit candidate commit before the runner).

**The exit rules (all must hold).**
1. Every gate of the milestone has a recorded value. A failing value is a record. A status of `not-run` blocks
   the exit. A kill gate that fired is recorded as an ADR with the fallback now in force.
2. Every demonstration runs from a clean checkout, through `frob --json` and, where the registry exposes the
   verb to agents, through `frob-mcp`.
3. GATE-GAUNT-01 holds: every gate that passed has a registered negative control that it catches, shown by a
   run over all controls in which each is caught by every gate it names.
4. The risk register has been read, and the open questions due have been answered or have taken their
   fail-closed defaults.
5. No PASS stands on a dirty sibling tree: each cited stack receipt has `dirty = false` for every sibling.

**Gates due at M0 (the gate register governs; thresholds are in each gate's own bead).**
- Admission: INT-TOOLCHAIN-01; INT-FSIM-01 to INT-FSIM-11 and INT-FSIM-18; INT-ASUP-01; INT-FSQL-01; INT-FT-01
  and INT-FT-02; INT-CUDA-01; INT-FSYM-01 and INT-FSYM-02; INT-FMCP-01 (its admission part); INT-FNP-01 and
  INT-FNP-02.
- Convergence: GATE-LEMMA-00 and GATE-LEMMA-06; GATE-MARROW-01 for SO(3) and SE(3); GATE-SPARK-00, -01, -07,
  -08, and the rows of GATE-SPARK-21 on `trj` that need no agent; GATE-TETHER-03; GATE-WS-01 and GATE-WS-04;
  the M0 parts of the later workspace gates indexed for M0 (GATE-WS-05, GATE-WS-06, GATE-WS-07, GATE-WS-09).
- Gauntlet: GATE-GAUNT-01, GATE-GAUNT-05 (oracle hygiene), GATE-GAUNT-08 (the plan-time pilots re-run and
  reproduce every count exactly) and GATE-GAUNT-10 (status accounting).
- Program: GATE-TENFOLD-01 (the parity baseline read into the first phase ledger).
- Kill gates decided: KILL-FSIM; KILL-MCP.

**Procedure (numbered; steps 2 to 6 and 10 are run by `scripts/e2e/exit_m0.sh`).**
1. Record the session identity and confirm it differs from every implementer of M0.
2. Clean checkout; run the six workspace checks; `xtask check-stack --json` must report `may_record_pass:
   true`. If a sibling is dirty or off-pin, stop: a gate run waits for a clean tree.
3. Query `registries/gates.toml` for every identifier above. Tabulate status, measured value against threshold,
   receipt hash, control identifiers and recorder. Any `not-run`, any `pass` without a caught control, any
   record whose recorder equals its implementer, any evidence that does not resolve in the artifact graph: a
   finding that blocks the exit.
4. Control sweep: run every registered control of the M0 gates; each must be caught by every gate it names. A
   control that has never failed its gate in any recorded run is listed as suspect.
5. Re-run the M0 demonstrations from their recorded commands and compare with the records:
   `frob doctor` lists the admitted foundations with their records and the stack receipt;
   `frob sim run g1 --tier referee --seconds 2 --key exit-m0-ref --json` gives a state digest equal to
   FrankenSim's own, and the same job through `frob-fsim-worker` (mode `remote`, `--key exit-m0-wkr`: two runs
   that are compared carry distinct idempotency keys, or the second returns the first one's stored receipt)
   gives equal chunk digests; `frob spark probe` and `frob spark fingerprint` run on both device families, each
   call with its own `--key`; the Cayley step kernel runs from one source on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal;
   the exact and guarded predicate kernels give identical bytes on all three; the certificates for theorem
   cards T-01, T-03 and T-04 replay offline; the M0 verbs answer through `frob --json` and through `frob-mcp`;
   the incumbent phase ledger on `trj` exists with its disclosure blocks; the plan-time pilots re-run with
   their recorded counts.
6. Regenerate `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` and the README status table and confirm they equal the checked-in
   files. Confirm that the foundation fact report of N00-16 (schema `frob.sibling-facts.report/1`, the
   sibling-code facts re-read at the pinned commits) is attached to the M0 admission record, and list every
   fact it reports `changed` or `unreadable`: such a fact is a finding against the beads that rely on it.
7. **KILL-FSIM.** Read the trigger ledger's evaluation (bead N33a-10): hours of effort on INT-FSIM-01 against
   80; the cold check against one hour, 16 GiB and 8 cores; the largest count of breakage days in any 28-day
   window against 4. Check `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FSIM-M0.md`, written by the decision bead N33b-05 (every
   decision record lives in `docs/adr/`; `docs/delivery/adr/` is retired): it carries N40-03's required fields
   (the trigger as registered and its amendments; each measured value with its receipt hash beside the
   trigger's number; the outcome; the fallback in force and the name the capability now carries; the beads
   re-read; what changes downstream; a recording session that implemented none of the mechanism); its three
   values equal the ledger's; and it states the posture in force: path consumption behind the airlock; or
   fallback (a), one pinned FrankenSim worker process per device exchanging whole jobs (already exercised by
   INT-FSIM-18); or fallback (b), a lift of the minimal referee set, only if (a) failed INT-FSIM-18 or the pin
   can no longer be built; and that the gate is re-read at every later exit. A missing record, a missing field
   or a value that differs from the ledger is a finding that blocks the exit.
8. **KILL-MCP and the P0 upstream items.** For each of FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01 record: the sibling
   repository and commit where the change landed, that it landed on the sibling's `main` through the sibling's
   own gates, the mirrored bead identifiers in both trackers, and the evidence (for FD-FMCP-01: fastmcp_rust's
   own test suite and MCP conformance checks green on T; for FD-ASUP-01: `cargo tree -d` showing one
   asupersync package at the one published version in each workspace). If either has been attempted and has
   not landed, say so. Check `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-MCP-M0.md`, written by the decision bead N32a-05, the same way:
   N40-03's required fields; INT-FMCP-01's record cited by hash; fired only if the gate cannot pass within the
   M0 budget after both items were attempted; the posture in force: `frob-mcp` in the core graph on T running
   verbs in process; or fallback 1, `frob-mcp` as its own workspace talking to the daemon over a local socket
   in newline-delimited JSON; or fallback 2, agents use the CLI, which is complete on its own; and the note
   that KILL-MCP is re-read at M1 with the M1 verbs. The exit record cites both ADRs by path and hash.
9. Confirm that the choice of T and the decision on the units escape hatch exist as ADRs with the matrix they
   were read from.
10. Open questions due at M0, each answered or defaulted in the exit record. The owner's: OPEN-SPARK-1 (GPU
    annexes; default: GPU results labelled exploratory) and OPEN-SPARK-2 (the compute envelope classes;
    default: 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device); OPEN-PROG-1 (before the first push); OPEN-SUITE-3 (dirty
    sibling checkouts: yes for development with the tree hash in the receipt, no PASS); OPEN-SUITE-5 (the
    cold-build budget: the machine envelope). Decided by the M0 measurements, and defaulted by ADR where the
    measurement did not decide: OPEN-PERF-2 (the comparator revisions), OPEN-SPARK-4, OPEN-SPARK-6,
    OPEN-SUITE-1, OPEN-SUITE-4, OPEN-SUITE-6, OPEN-WS-3, OPEN-GAUNT-4, OPEN-TENFOLD-7. Already decided and
    confirmed here by INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 and INT-FMCP-01: OPEN-SUITE-2 and OPEN-TETHER-1. That is the sixteen rows
    of the register whose deadline is M0; a `silent` one blocks (the `owner-calendar` check). The exit record
    also prints the questions asked now for M1 (OPEN-PROG-4, the reading of the M0 ledger, and the early
    notice of OPEN-TENFOLD-4).
11. Risks read at this exit: the 21 rows first readable at M0 (RISK-01 to -04, -07, -08, -10, -18, -20 to -25,
    -36, -48, -53 to -57), each with a reading and, where its signal fired, one of the three outcomes (the
    `risk-review` check). The four on which this exit's two kill decisions bear are read with their indicators'
    values in the ADRs: RISK-01 (FrankenSim API churn), RISK-02 (the toolchain train), RISK-03 (two asupersync
    versions in one build), RISK-23 (dirty trees). The 20 primary plants due at M0 are fired (`risk-plants`).
12. **Stop decisions.** Re-plan before M1 if INT-FSIM-01 could not pass within two weeks and the worker also
    failed INT-FSIM-18 (the only route left is a lift from a closure of about 1.5 million lines). Rewrite the
    ten-fold thesis chapter before any work is funded against it if the measured phase ledger puts its
    ceilings under 3 for every mission class. Drop a device family from M2 if the probe shows it can hold no
    controlled determinism class. Only the first stops the CPU studio.
13. Write the exit record `docs/delivery/exits/M0/EXIT.md` (the format of N40-05, schema `frob.exitrecord/1`,
    from the runner's skeleton): the gate table with a disposition per part, the control sweep, the two ADRs,
    the upstream record, the sections of the attached checks, defaults taken, stop decisions, and the verdict
    `exited` or `not exited` with the blocking findings; an exit that stands on a fallback is `exited` with the
    fallbacks named under "variants in force" and is announced as "exited with fallbacks". Close this bead with
    a typed reason citing the script's log.

**The observable.** M0's exit line is: the admission records, one kernel on three backends, and the incumbent
phase ledger on `trj`. A milestone does not exit on a count of gates.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance exit_m0`)
The query and the runner are tested in their own beads (N40-04, N40-05). These tests run them on the M0 gate
list, so that the M0 data is known to produce each verdict; they add no second implementation.
- `exit_query_blocks_on_not_run`: a synthetic registry with one M0 gate `not-run` → verdict `not exited`, naming the gate.
- `exit_query_blocks_on_uncaught_control`, `exit_query_blocks_on_self_certification`, `exit_query_blocks_on_dirty_receipt`.
- `exit_query_accepts_fail_with_kill_adr`: a failed admission gate whose kill gate is `killed` with a fallback named → `exited`, with the fallback under "variants in force" (announced as exited with fallbacks).
- `expected_failures_do_not_block`: INT-FSIM-05 and INT-FSIM-06 recorded `fail` with their interim posture verified and their upstream work items open → listed as failed with posture, verdict unaffected; the same two recorded `pass` with no fix landed upstream → a finding.
- `m0_attached_checks_all_run`: on a scratch root with the M0 table of attached checks, the exit record lists a section for each of the checks named above; with the `principles` record removed, the assertion of `exit_m0.sh` on that list fails and names it (the runner itself requires only its four core checks, so the M0 list is asserted here).
- `sixteen_questions_due_at_m0`: on the real register `due(M0)` lists the sixteen rows of step 10, two `answered`.
- `exit_gate_list_is_complete`: the M0 identifier list in the query equals the list above (a registry row dated M0 that the query omits fails the test).
- `suspect_controls_listed`: a control with no recorded catch appears in the suspect list.
- `m0_kill_adrs_checked`: on a scratch root, an `ADR-KILL-FSIM-M0.md` whose cold-check value differs from N33a-10's evaluation is a blocking finding naming the field; an ADR missing the recording session is a blocking finding; the same ADR placed under `docs/delivery/adr/` is refused by N40-03's loader (`decision-adr-path`).
- `m0_upstream_exempt_listed`: on the live export, NF-03, NF-04 and NF-05 appear in the tracker check's `upstream_exempt` section with their status and are not orphans; the five FDEF reports are ancestors of this bead.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/exit_m0.sh`, run by the reviewing session on the Mac and, for device steps, on `trj`. It runs
`scripts/e2e/registers_exit.sh` first, then the shared runner `scripts/exit/review.sh M0` (the six checks, the
gate query, the control sweep, the regenerations and the attached checks), `frob doctor --json`, and each
demonstration command of step 5; it asserts that the exit record holds a section for every attached check
listed under "How the review is run" and a disposition for every gate of the list above. Logging per the shared helper of `STD-E2E`: every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block; `assert`
lines per gate with its measured value, unit, threshold, receipt hash, control identifiers and `caught`;
digests for the referee and worker rollouts with the first differing chunk on failure; a `metric` line for
each KILL-FSIM trigger value; a final `summary` with counts of gates passed, failed, killed and not run, and
the gates touched. Logs go to `target/e2e-logs/exit_m0/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it, and contain
no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers re-observed: the G1 digest equality; KA-767 (a receipt computed twice is byte-identical); KA-777 (element 3, not 12); KA-780 (worker digests equal in-process digests); the docking-grid counts of the plan-time pilot (344 admitted, 217 excluded, 9 on the boundary).
- Negative controls of the review itself (registered for GATE-GAUNT-10 and GATE-GAUNT-01): a planted self-certified PASS and a planted PASS with an uncaught control are each refused by the query; a planted gate dated M0 that the script does not check is caught by `exit_gate_list_is_complete`.
- Determinism lanes: the query over the same registry gives the same verdict and bytes on both hosts.

## Considerations
- The review must be allowed to end `not exited`. Its value is that it can.
- KILL-FSIM is "M0, then rolling": this ADR is the first of a series; the breakage-share trigger can only fire after weeks of data.
- If INT-FSIM-01 fails outright, M1 can still proceed on fallback (a) if INT-FSIM-18 passed; that combination is recorded as `exited` with the fallback under "variants in force" (exited with fallbacks), and the README says "referee by pinned worker".
- Every bead of M0 is an ancestor of this review (the plan's graph rule, checked by `tracker-check`), which is why its dependency list is long: it names each M0 bead that no other M0 bead waits for. A bead discovered during M0 is added to it.
- No milestone waits on a sibling change landing (REV-1, rule 3; N41-16 check 3(d)). The only upstream items among this review's ancestors are the two P0 items FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01. The first toolchain train NF-05 waits on the frankensim and frankensympy legs NF-03 (FD-FSIM-40) and NF-04 (FD-FSYM-06), both P1, so it is in `related`, where check 3(b) exempts it and the two legs, and the exit record lists all three in `upstream_exempt` with their status. M0 needs T chosen and the graph building on it (INT-TOOLCHAIN-01, BD-031), not every sibling's own toolchain file moved; under candidate (b) the train to (a) is awaited by no exit. The register rows of NFx-01, otherwise reached only through those legs, are in-repository M0 work and stay a dependency. The five reports FDEF-01 to FDEF-05 also stay dependencies: each is in-repository work at M0 (the report under `tests/upstream/<item>/`, the standalone reproduction, the observed values copied from M0 admission records, and the guard this repository keeps) that no sibling change has to land for; none is a register item (their items FD-FSIM-10 to -13, FD-FNP-01 and FD-FSIM-33 are later beads no exit waits on), so check 3(d) does not list them, and in `related` they would be reported `tracker-orphan`, having no item among their ancestors.
- The upstream items live in other repositories under their own rules; the review reads their state and never edits a sibling.
- Runs stay inside the machine envelope; device steps are scheduled one device at a time.
- The same procedure, with the M1 gate list and the schedule gate of bet BET-12, is the M1 exit review (bead BD-147).
- Two M0 admission gates are expected to fail for the sibling at the first pin: INT-FSIM-05 (`planar_drive`) and
  INT-FSIM-06 (the PPO update). Neither routine is linked or used as an oracle. A `fail` on either, with its
  interim posture verified (the independent chord formula; PPO written here) and its upstream work item open
  (FD-FSIM-11, FD-FSIM-10), is the expected result and does not block this exit; the review lists both as failed
  with posture, never as passed (BD-033, BD-034 and the battery N33b-20 record them the same way).
- GATE-WS-06 and GATE-WS-09 were added to the gate register after the M0 acceptance list was written; the
  register governs, so their M0 rows (N33c-40, N33c-48) are read at this exit with the rest.
