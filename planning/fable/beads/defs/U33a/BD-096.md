## Background

The job state machine, the idempotency binding, the chunk publisher, pagination, the lease manager, the memo
store and the dispatcher of FrankenRobotics are concurrent code whose failures are rare interleavings: a crash
between a commit and its acknowledgement, a late writer after a lease moved, a retry that splits a shard. Such
failures are not found by waiting. They are found by **deterministic simulation testing**: run the real code on
a runtime whose clock, scheduler and faults are controlled by a seed, explore many schedules, and keep every
failing schedule as a regression that replays in under a second.

The harness is the **lab runtime of asupersync**, the suite's structured-concurrency library (foundation row
`FD-ASUP`: Consume; all async code; one package identity per build). FACT (read 2026-11-21 at head
`b32c5ba66315`; re-read at the pin): `asupersync/src/lab/` is about 127 thousand lines; its module header
lists virtual time, deterministic scheduling by seed, trace capture and replay, schedule exploration, test
oracles, cancellation injection at await points and chaos injection (`src/lab/mod.rs`); it is compiled under
the `full-runtime` feature and re-exports `LabConfig` and `LabRuntime` (`src/lib.rs:237, :502`); `chaos.rs`,
`injection.rs`, `ldfi.rs` (lineage-driven fault selection), `replay.rs`, `replay_minimization.rs`,
`schedule_minimizer.rs`, `explorer.rs`, `dual_run.rs` and `snapshot_restore.rs` exist. One caution:
`lab/conformal.rs` calibrates the lab's own oracle metrics and is not a general interval library; nothing here
uses its code for estimates.

Posture (DESIGN): Consume, as a **dev-dependency** of `frob-chronicle`, `frob-verbs` and `frob-conformance`,
with the `full-runtime` feature enabled for tests only; it is the same single asupersync package as the
production runtime, so the code under test is the code that ships. Admission gate **INT-ASUP-02**: the job
protocol runs under the lab runtime; 100 of 100 repeats of one seed give identical traces; a planted
lost-chunk bug is found within 10,000 explored schedules and replays from its minimized fixture. The seed
done-condition adds the CPU part of **GATE-CHRON-11**: full await-point coverage of the publish and submit
paths; every failure minimized to a regression that replays in under one second; dual-run agreement on 100
small jobs. Fallback if the lab runtime cannot be admitted: the chaos tests at kernel-batch boundaries with a
hand-written virtual clock, which keep fault injection and lose schedule exploration.

What is under test at M1 is the CPU form of the chunk protocol (bead BD-091). Publishing a chunk is one ledger
transaction that does five things or nothing: checks the writer's lease generation (a fencing token), checks
that the chunk covers exactly its fixed partition extent, checks that its index is the frontier plus one and
its `prev` equals the chain digest at the frontier, returns the original receipt for an identical duplicate
or refuses a conflict (`FROB-REF-878`, different bytes for a committed key), and commits, advancing the
frontier and the commit counter.

## Technical approach

**Harness (`crates/frob-conformance/src/sim/` with tests in `frob-chronicle` and `frob-verbs`).**
1. A scenario is a pure description: a run manifest (worlds, episodes, chunk length L), a set of client
   actions (submit with an idempotency key, cancel, disconnect, reconnect by key, paginated reads), and a fault
   plan. It is executed under `LabRuntime` with a recorded seed; all time is virtual.
2. **The fake device.** Episode outcomes and chunk contents are a pure function of the world address
   (run, world, episode, stream, chunk index) and the manifest, so a whole campaign simulates in milliseconds
   and identical work always has identical bytes. Its timing is controlled by the lab clock.
3. **Storage.** The real `Ledger` adapter over FrankenSQLite on a temporary directory, wrapped by a fault
   layer that can fail a write, tear a write (stop after a prefix of the bytes) or report a full disk at a
   scheduled point.
4. **Trace.** The harness records the schedule (the order of task polls), every fault fired and the final
   ledger state digest. A trace is a canonical byte string; "identical traces" means identical bytes.

**Fault menu (each fault is addressable by (await point, occurrence)).** A crash at every await point of the
publish and submit paths; a torn ledger write; duplicate and reordered delivery; delay; lease expiry and
takeover by a second writer; device loss; a full disk; a clock jump; cancellation at every boundary; a client
that disconnects with a job running and reconnects by submission key.

**Oracles (checked after every scenario, and at every quiescent point).**
- INV-CHRON-01: a committed chunk key maps to one content hash for the life of the run.
- INV-CHRON-02: for every (run, world, episode, stream) the committed chunks are exactly the indices 0 to the
  frontier, with no hole.
- INV-CHRON-03: one idempotency key yields one job, one budget charge and one receipt, under any crash or
  retry.
- INV-CHRON-04: a paginated query returns every row visible at its pin exactly once.
- INV-CHRON-12: collection never removes a pinned object, and every removal leaves a tombstone (exercised once
  the collector exists; until then the oracle is registered and reports `not exercised`).
- INV-ATEL-02: a study's statistic is a pure function of per-address outcomes in address order, independent of
  completion order, preemption, co-tenancy, shard count and memo hits. INV-ATEL-06: a decision core's action is
  a pure function of its table version, the ledger state at a pinned frontier and addresses, and reads no
  clock. (Both registered here and exercised as their subjects land.)
This discharges proof obligation PO-CHRON-01 (the publishing transaction preserves INV-CHRON-01 to -03 under
every fault of the menu) by simulation.

**Exploration.**
1. *Coverage first.* Enumerate the await points of the publish and submit paths (the lab runtime reports
   them); for each, at least one scenario fires a crash there. Coverage is the fraction hit; the gate needs 1.
2. *Lineage-driven selection.* From the lineage of a successful run, the faults that could have broken its
   outcome are tried first, before random ones.
3. *Seeded random schedules* up to the budget of the lane (TARGET for a release: 10⁵ seeded schedules; the
   per-commit lane runs 10³ inside the machine envelope).

**Minimization and the regression store.** A failing run is reduced to a seed, a fault list and a schedule of
minimal length with the lab runtime's minimizer, stored under `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/sim/<id>/`
(the canonical trace, the scenario, the violated oracle), and added to a test that replays every stored
fixture. A fixture must replay to the same violation in under one second.

**The planted lost-chunk bug (the control of INT-ASUP-02).** A test-only build flag makes the publisher
advance the frontier before the row insert is durable. Under a crash between the two, a chunk is lost while
the frontier claims it: INV-CHRON-02 fails. The explorer must find a violating schedule within 10,000 explored
schedules from a fixed seed, minimize it, and the fixture must replay.

**Dual runs (keeping the fake honest).** The same small job is executed against the fake device and against
the real CPU executor of the M1 job engine, and the two ledger states must be equal (same chunk keys, content
hashes, chain digests and frontier). 100 small jobs with seeds 0 to 99. At M2 the comparison target becomes
`batch-cpu` and then a device (the device part of the gate).

Determinism: every scenario is a pure function of (seed, scenario, binary); class D0 for traces. Evidence
class: `Tested`. The lab runtime never appears in a production dependency edge.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] INT-ASUP-02: one seed repeated 100 times gives 100 identical traces; the planted lost-chunk bug is found within 10,000 explored schedules and replays from its minimized fixture.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-11, CPU part: every await point on the publish and submit paths is hit by a crash fault at least once (coverage 1, with the list of points in the record); every failure found is minimized to a fixture that replays in under one second; dual-run agreement on 100 of 100 small jobs.
- [ ] Each planted protocol violation is refused by the real publisher in simulation: a late lease, an overlapping range that is not a partition extent, a conflicting duplicate (`FROB-REF-878`), and reuse of tick zero by a second episode (which must not collide).
- [ ] All oracles above are registered; those whose subject exists hold on every explored schedule of the lane.
- [ ] `cargo tree -d` shows one asupersync package with the lab runtime enabled for tests; no production target enables `full-runtime`.
- [ ] An admission record for INT-ASUP-02 exists with the seed, schedule counts, coverage, the fixture identifier of the planted bug and the asupersync pin.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance sim`, `cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test sim_publish`, `cargo test -p frob-verbs --test sim_submit`)
- `same_seed_same_trace_100`: 100 runs of seed 7 → one distinct trace digest.
- `different_seed_different_schedule`: seeds 7 and 8 give different schedules and equal final ledger digests (the outcome does not depend on the schedule).
- `crash_at_every_await_point`: coverage equals 1; the uncovered list is empty.
- `crash_after_commit_before_ack_is_harmless`: the retry finds the key committed with the same hash and receives the original receipt.
- `late_lease_refused`, `overlap_refused`, `conflict_refused_878`, `tick_zero_reuse_does_not_collide`.
- `idempotent_submit_under_crash`: requests A and B with the same key and payload, interleaved with a crash: one job, one budget charge, the retry returns the stored receipt marked as an idempotent replay.
- `pagination_pinned`: rows at t = 1, 2, 3, 4 with page size 2; a row at t = 1.5 commits after page one; page two returns {3, 4}, nothing repeated or skipped.
- `planted_lost_chunk_found_within_10000`: found, minimized, replayed.
- `fixture_replays_under_one_second`: every stored fixture.
- `dual_run_100`: fake against the real CPU executor, 100 seeds, equal ledger digests.
- `torn_write_recovers_to_prefix`, `full_disk_refuses_cleanly`, `clock_jump_does_not_reorder_commits`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/sim_ledger_submit.sh`: runs the three test targets with explicit seeds and the per-commit
schedule budget, then the planted-bug search, then the fixture replay, and writes the admission record.
Logging per `STD-E2E`: each line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall
time; `metric` lines for schedules explored, await points total and hit, failures found, minimized lengths,
replay time per fixture (threshold 1 s), identical traces (threshold 100 of 100), schedules until the planted
bug was found (threshold 10,000), dual-run agreements (threshold 100 of 100); on failure the seed, the fault
list, the violated oracle and the fixture path; a final `summary` naming INT-ASUP-02 and GATE-CHRON-11.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: idempotency (one job, one charge; the same key with a different digest is refused); pinned pagination returning {3, 4}; the fixed chunk partition (ranges [0, 100) and [50, 150) from a split retry cannot both commit; a reset that reuses tick zero does not overwrite the first episode).
- Negative controls (registered for INT-ASUP-02 and GATE-CHRON-11): the planted lost-chunk bug; a look-up-then-insert idempotency binding in two statements (two jobs run and the budget is charged twice); range-keyed chunks (both overlapping ranges commit); a fake device whose outcome depends on arrival order (the dual run must disagree).
- Metamorphic relation: the final ledger digest is invariant under the schedule seed for a fault-free scenario.
- Determinism lanes: traces identical twice on one machine, in debug and release, and on both hosts.

## Considerations

- The gate register dates INT-ASUP-02 at M2; this bead produces its record at M1 (a gate may pass before its milestone) and the device part of GATE-CHRON-11 at M2 belongs to the device job-protocol beads.
- The 10⁵-schedule release lane exceeds a few minutes; it runs under a timeout inside the machine envelope, and its count is recorded, not assumed.
- A simulation proves properties of the code paths it reaches; coverage of await points is necessary, not sufficient. The dual run is what ties the fake to reality, which is why a disagreement is a failure of the gate.
- If the asupersync pin moves, fixtures are replayed first: a fixture that no longer replays is re-minimized, never deleted.
- OPEN-CHRON-2 (the chunk length L; owner: the Chronicle lead) and OPEN-CHRON-5 (lease granularity; default one generation per run) are parameters of the scenarios, set to their defaults here.
