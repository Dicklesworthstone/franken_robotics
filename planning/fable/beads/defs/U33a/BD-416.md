## Background

A place changes because a robot is in it: a wheel drags oil out of a patch, a carried pot sloshes, a castor
pushes a rug into a fold, a cord wraps an axle. FrankenRobotics admits such "living" matter in three tiers and
keeps only the first on the critical path: Solid film-friction patches and rigid proxies (M3); Frontier
robot-advected films, slosh by an equivalent mechanical model and articulated cord and rug proxies (M6); and
this epic, the Moonshot tier: **coupled islands**. An island is a small region of resolved matter beside one
world on the CPU referee: a free-surface lattice fluid in a box of at most about one cubic metre, an implicit
cloth for one rug or curtain, or a Cosserat rod for one cable.

An island is used for exactly two purposes: as an oracle against which the Solid and Frontier models are
checked on finalists, and to verify a counterexample that the adversarial subsystem attributes to living
matter. It never runs in a training batch, and nothing on the critical path waits for it.

Why not resolved matter everywhere (DESIGN arithmetic, not measurement): a D3Q19 lattice needs 152 bytes per
cell, so a 10 m × 10 m × 3 m room at 1 cm is 45.6 GB for one world and about 374 TB at 8,192 worlds; a film
0.1 to 1 mm thick needs cells of 0.25 mm; and if coupled matter is active in 5% of worlds while the batch must
keep a tenth of its rigid-only throughput, a coupled world may cost at most c rigid worlds with
0.95 + 0.05·c ≤ 10, so c ≤ 181, a bound the existing CPU solvers miss by orders of magnitude. One cubic metre
at 5 mm holding the lattice Mach number at 0.1 for 1 m/s flow needs about 3,460 fluid steps of 8 × 10⁶ cells
per simulated second: an offline computation.

What exists in the siblings (FACT, read 2026-11-21): FrankenSim's `fs-lbm` has D3Q19 and D3Q27 lattices and a
mass-tracking free-surface module whose ledger is gated every step; `fs-tribo` has a quasistatic Reynolds-film
pressure solver; `fs-solid` has geometrically exact rods for statics; dynamic rods and a batched cloth are not
in the siblings (upstream work item FD-FSIM-22). `fs-lbm` and `fs-couple` are reached only through the
`multiphysics` feature of the airlock crate, off in every default build: their closure is 66 crates and about
4.1 million lines, of which 19 crates and about 2.3 million lines lie outside what M1 compiles. For cloth, the
sibling franken_textiles has `ftx-drape` (about 116 thousand lines; `cloth.rs`, `contact.rs`, `integrator.rs`),
which reports drapes that take tens of CPU seconds for a few thousand vertices and has no GPU path by design.

## Technical approach

**Funding rule.** One experiment decides whether this tier is ever built, and it needs no new solver: the
proxy-adequacy pilot on an incumbent engine that already ships cable and cloth (three scenes: a cord across
the path, a rug with a free edge, one square metre of oil; 200 randomized episodes each; one wheeled and one
legged robot; rerun with the proxies: a 20-link chain, a hinged thin plate, a friction patch, and the patch
with the dwell-time law). The measure is the share of full-model failures the proxy reproduces in the same
failure class within one second.

**KILL-LIVING (decided at M6, before any island work package).** Trigger: the proxies reproduce 90% or more of
the full-model failures, or an island costs more than 181 rigid worlds at 5% activity with no route to less.
Fallback, named now: the Solid tier and the Frontier proxies are the product; coupled islands are not built
beyond the oracle fixtures that already exist in the siblings; reports say that deformable matter is
represented by proxy. Because the second clause of the trigger is already met by the arithmetic above for
in-batch use, the epic can only ever fund islands as offline oracles, which is how it is scoped.

**The short plan this epic must hold before any child is funded (one page, in the delivery log).** For each
island kind: the question it answers that a proxy cannot; the sibling owner and its admission gate; the
exchange contract; the cost per simulated second inside the machine envelope; the falsifier.

**Island interface (DESIGN; the airlock's `multiphysics` group, traits `FluidIsland` and `CoupledIsland`).**
An island exchanges **whole fields at declared instants** with one referee world: at each coupling instant the
world sends the kinematic state of the bodies that touch the island (poses, twists, shapes), the island
advances to the next instant and returns the wrenches on those bodies and its own conserved-quantity ledger
(mass, momentum, energy with a residual line). No kernel of the batched step calls across the bridge. A result
that crossed it is labelled evaluation tier and cannot enter a throughput claim or a same-stack comparison.
With the feature off, or for a throughput-tier request, the bridge refuses (`FROB-REF-790`).

**The three island kinds and their owners.**
| Island | Owner reached | Admission | Interim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free-surface fluid, a box of at most about 1 m³ | `fs-lbm` through the bridge | INT-FSIM-17 (with BD-413) | The film-friction law stays the model if the gate fails |
| Implicit cloth, one rug or curtain | `ftx-drape`, as a **worker process in its own workspace** (its exact pin `serde_json = "=1.0.151"` cannot share a Cargo graph with FrankenSim's, which resolves 1.0.154) | INT-FTX-01 (bead N33a-26) | Rigid proxies; hinged plates |
| Cosserat rod, one cable | `fs-solid`'s rod module for statics; dynamics need FD-FSIM-22 | Admitted at first use by its own record | A chain of rigid links |

**INT-FTX-01**: a square cloth draped over a sphere matches `ftx-drape`'s settled shape to its own stated
tolerance, with `ftx-drape` run as a worker process. It is the pilot of the cloth item as well as its gate.

**Children to be opened when the plan is approved** (each with its own acceptance and tests; none exists as
work until then): the island exchange contract and its conservation ledger; the fluid island adapter; the
cloth island over the worker; the rod island; one end-to-end oracle comparison per island kind against its
proxy on a finalist.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The epic owns no code. Its children carry tests; the two checks that belong here are registry tests in
`frob-conformance`: `epic_living_plan_exists` (the plan file is present and names the five fields per island
kind) and `kill_living_recorded` (the gate registry row for KILL-LIVING is not `not-run` once the M6 exit
review runs).

### End-to-end script and logging
None of its own. The cloth oracle's script belongs to bead N33a-26; each island child will bring a script
`scripts/e2e/island_<kind>.sh` that runs one finalist with the island and with its proxy and logs, per the
shared `STD-E2E` format, the failure class of each run, the conserved-quantity residuals, the evaluation-tier
label on every envelope and the wall time against the machine envelope.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers inherited by the children: Poiseuille flow between plates (the parabola); film mass conserved to 10⁻¹² relative over 10⁴ steps (KA-772); the cloth drape against `ftx-drape`.
- Negative controls: the bridge with the feature off must refuse; a throughput-tier request must be refused; a result that crossed the bridge offered to a paired comparison must be rejected.
- Counterexample kept in view: living matter means a resolved fluid, cloth or cable solver in every training world (refuted by the memory and throughput arithmetic above).

## Considerations

- This is a Moonshot: priority backlog, off every critical path, and funded only if the pilot shows that proxies miss failures that matter. The expected outcome, given the arithmetic, is that KILL-LIVING fires and the fallback is the product.
- Compute: an island run can exceed the default envelope (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour); any such run needs the owner's written approval before it starts.
- Incumbent engines already ship cables, cloth and particle solvers coupled to rigid robots (FACT to be re-read from the primary source); nothing here is claimed as a first.
- Determinism: resolved-matter solvers are outside the determinism classes of the batched tiers; island results are evaluation-tier evidence only.
