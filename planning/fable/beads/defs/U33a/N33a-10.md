## Background

A **kill gate** names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; a kill gate that
fires is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan. **KILL-FSIM** guards the largest
dependency decision of FrankenRobotics: consuming FrankenSim (the sibling physics library) by path dependency
behind the airlock crate `frob-fsim` (ADR-SUITE-01), which is bet BET-04.

- **Trigger (any one of three):** (1) the admission gate INT-FSIM-01 cannot pass within two weeks of M0 effort;
  (2) its cold check exceeds the machine envelope (one hour of wall time, 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores); (3) BET-04's
  breakage threshold is crossed: upstream breakage above 20% of working days in any four-week window.
- **Fallback (a):** the volatile FrankenSim crates move behind a process boundary: one pinned FrankenSim worker
  process per device exchanges whole jobs with the core through the same airlock traits. The bedrock-direct
  crates that still pass their own admission stay path dependencies; those that do not are lifted first, since
  they are small.
- **Fallback (b)**, only if (a) fails its own gate INT-FSIM-18 or the pinned commit can no longer be built on
  any available toolchain: lift the minimal referee set with provenance headers and golden tests (`fs-ga`,
  `fs-math` deterministic functions, `fs-rand`, the `fs-mbd` articulated module, the `fs-contact` multicontact
  solve), record every lifted file in `registries/lifts.toml`, state "lift-only" in the README, and keep the
  upstream work items open.
- **Decided at:** M0, then rolling (re-read at every milestone exit).

The order matters. The lift set is small only on paper: the two modules it names sit on a closure of about 1.5
million lines (FACT, to be re-read), and cutting them free of the materials crate is engineering that fallback
(a) avoids entirely. A worker keeps FrankenSim's real code as the referee at an old pin for as long as needed.

A trigger is only as good as its measurement. Two of the three are easy to leave unmeasured: "two weeks of
effort" has no clock unless someone starts one, and "breakage above 20% of working days" has no numerator unless
breakage days are logged when they happen. FACT for context: 409 commits touched FrankenSim's multibody crate in
the thirty days before 2026-11-21, on 17 distinct days, so the rolling trigger is not hypothetical. This bead
creates the three measurements and the function that evaluates the trigger, so that the M0 exit review (bead
BD-044) and every later exit can record KILL-FSIM as an ADR from data.

## Scope

In: the ledger file, its three series, the evaluation function, the status row it yields for
`registries/gates.toml`, and the negative controls.
Out: the decision itself and its ADR (the exit-review beads and the decision bead of the upstream-work unit);
executing a fallback (the worker is BD-038; the lift machinery is N33a-01); the fallback-(b) trigger measured
through the worker.

## Technical approach

**Ledger (DESIGN, `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/ledger.toml`, schema `frob.kill-fsim-ledger/1`; append-only, small,
edited by the agent whose work the entry describes).**

```toml
[effort_clock]                      # trigger 1
started   = "<ISO date of the first INT-FSIM-01 attempt>"
entries   = [ { date = "…", session = "…", hours = 0.0, note = "what was tried" } ]
passed_on = ""                      # date of the first valid passing record of INT-FSIM-01, if any

[[cold_check]]                      # trigger 2; one row per INT-FSIM-01 admission record
record = "<admission record hash>"; wall_s = 0; peak_rss_bytes = 0; cores = 8; host = "trj | m5-ultra"

[[breakage_day]]                    # trigger 3
date    = "<ISO date>"
pin_from = "<40 hex>"; pin_to = "<40 hex or sibling head>"
kind    = "build | admission-gate | parity-gate | api-change"
located = "frankensim"              # classified by check-stack before attribution; never assumed
classified_by = "<BLAKE3 of the check-stack --json report that classified the failure>"
evidence = "<admission record hash, or commit of the adapter change>"
cost_hours = 0.0
```

**Definitions (DESIGN; fixed before any data).**
- *M0 effort on INT-FSIM-01*: the sum of `hours` in `effort_clock.entries`. Two weeks is 10 working days of one
  agent-equivalent, taken as 80 hours. Trigger 1 holds when the sum reaches 80 with `passed_on` empty.
  This is the one measure of trigger 1 (the same statement is in N33b-05, whose earlier draft counted ten
  working days between two dates): effort on the gate is what "two weeks of M0 effort" names, and the decision
  takes this value from `evaluate`.
- *Cold check over the envelope*: any `cold_check` row with `wall_s > 3600`, `peak_rss_bytes > 16·2³⁰` or
  `cores > 8`, on the newest admission record. One over-envelope row on an older record does not fire if a later
  record at a later pin is inside the envelope; the ledger keeps both.
- *Breakage day*: a working day (Monday to Friday) on which a build, an admission gate or a parity gate of this
  repository failed for a cause located in the sibling (established by running `xtask check-stack` first, and
  by the failing output), and recovering needed a change to the airlock or to pins. Several incidents on one day
  count once. A day spent on planned upstream work in the sibling's repository is not a breakage day.
- *Breakage share*: for every window of 28 consecutive calendar days, the number of distinct breakage days
  divided by the number of working days in the window (20). Trigger 3 holds when any window exceeds 0.20, that
  is, 5 or more breakage days in some window.

**Evaluation (`frob_conformance::kill::fsim::evaluate(ledger, records) -> KillStatus`).**
1. Validate the ledger (dates parse; every `evidence` hash resolves; `located` is a ledger foundation; every
   breakage day is a weekday; `classified_by` names a `check-stack --json` report, schema `frob.check-stack/1`
   of N33a-05, committed beside the ledger under `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/check-stack/<blake3>.json` and hashing
   to its name, whose `siblings` list has an entry for the `located` foundation with a drift class other than
   `on-pin`, or a `stack.sibling` finding for it; otherwise the attribution was assumed, not classified, and the
   entry is refused).
2. Compute the three trigger booleans with their measured values (hours against 80; the worst cold-check figure
   against each limit; the maximum window count against 4).
3. Return `{ fired: bool, triggers: [..], measured: {..}, recommended: "none | fallback-a | fallback-b" }`.
   `fallback-b` is recommended only if fallback (a)'s gate INT-FSIM-18 has a failing record or the pin has an
   admission record with status `fail` on every available toolchain; otherwise a firing recommends
   `fallback-a`.
4. Render the row for `registries/gates.toml`: `id = "KILL-FSIM"`, `status` one of `not-run` (no data), `pass`
   (evaluated, not fired), `killed` (fired, with the fallback named), `measured` holding the three values.

The function is pure: the same ledger and records give the same status (class D0). It holds no policy beyond
the trigger definitions above; changing a threshold is a dated amendment to the gate register made before the
measurement it governs.

**What changes downstream if it fires.** With fallback (a): `frob-fsim` builds in its `remote` mode; the referee
is called in rollouts and never inside a loop because each job has a fixed overhead; referee results carry the
worker's stack receipt; memoized referee keys change with the worker's pin; the capability is described as
"referee by pinned worker" in every report. With fallback (b): the README states "lift-only", lifted files are
in `registries/lifts.toml`, and the admission gates for the lifted crates are replaced by golden tests.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ledger exists with the effort clock started on the date of the first INT-FSIM-01 attempt, and a `cold_check` row for every INT-FSIM-01 admission record.
- [ ] `evaluate` returns `fired: false` on a ledger with 40 hours and a passing record, and names each trigger correctly on three constructed ledgers (80 hours with no pass; a 3,700 s cold check; 5 breakage days inside 28 days).
- [ ] 4 breakage days in a window do not fire (0.20 is not above 0.20); 5 do.
- [ ] A breakage entry whose `located` sibling was not established by a `check-stack` classification is refused by validation.
- [ ] The KILL-FSIM row of `registries/gates.toml` is generated from the evaluation and carries the three measured values; it is `not-run` when the ledger is empty, never `pass`.
- [ ] The M0 exit review can cite the evaluation's output hash in its ADR.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-conformance kill_fsim`)
- `not_fired_on_quiet_ledger`: 40 h, passed; cold 1,200 s, 9 GiB, 8 cores; 2 breakage days → `fired: false`, measured (40, 1200, 9·2³⁰, 2).
- `trigger1_effort`: 80 h, `passed_on` empty → fired, trigger 1; 79.5 h → not fired.
- `trigger2_wall`, `trigger2_memory`, `trigger2_cores`: 3,601 s; 16·2³⁰ + 1 bytes; 9 cores → fired, trigger 2 each.
- `trigger2_superseded`: an old record over the envelope and a newer one inside → not fired.
- `trigger3_window`: breakage days on 5 distinct weekdays within 28 days → fired; the same 5 days spread so that no 28-day window holds more than 4 → not fired.
- `trigger3_same_day_counts_once`: three entries on one date count as one.
- `trigger3_weekend_refused`: an entry dated on a Saturday is refused by validation.
- `breakage_without_classification_refused`: an entry with no `classified_by`, one whose report file is missing or does not hash to its name, and one whose report shows the `located` sibling `on-pin` with no `stack.sibling` finding are each refused; the same entry citing a report that classifies FrankenSim `fast-forward` is accepted.
- `recommends_b_only_when_a_failed`: fired with a passing INT-FSIM-18 record → `fallback-a`; with a failing one → `fallback-b`.
- `status_row_not_run_when_empty`.
- Property test `window_max_is_translation_invariant`: shifting every date by k days leaves the maximum window count unchanged (weekday pattern preserved by shifting in multiples of 7).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/admission_m0.sh` (bead N33a-28), step `kill_fsim_status`: after the INT-FSIM-01 record is written
it runs the evaluation through the conformance harness, asserts that the row is not `not-run`, and logs the
three measured values with their thresholds and units, the trigger booleans and the recommendation.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the constructed ledgers above, whose statuses are computed by hand.
- Negative controls (registered for KILL-FSIM): a ledger with the breakage series deleted must yield `not-run` for trigger 3, not "0% breakage"; a threshold edited after data exists must be refused unless the amendment is dated before the measurement.
- Determinism lanes: the evaluation's output bytes are identical on both platforms.

## Considerations
- "Process is never the product" applies: the ledger is one small file and one function. It exists because the alternative is deciding the program's largest dependency question from memory.
- The effort clock measures effort on the gate, not calendar time. If INT-FSIM-01 passes in the first session, trigger 1 is simply recorded as not fired with its hours.
- Risk-register rows that read this ledger: RISK-01 (FrankenSim API churn; leading indicator "adapter changes per pin move; BET-04's breakage share"), RISK-02 (the toolchain train) and pre-mortem row "FrankenSim moved weekly and adapter churn consumed the effort".
- Stop decision of M0 that depends on it: re-plan before M1 if INT-FSIM-01 cannot pass within two weeks and the worker process also fails INT-FSIM-18, because the only route left is a lift from a closure of about 1.5 million lines.
- The airlock stays whatever lands upstream, because FrankenSim's API moves daily; firing KILL-FSIM changes how the airlock is built, not whether it exists.
- This ledger holds the single definition of a breakage day and of the breakage share in this program. The
  compatibility train (N33b-04, which absorbed the second draft of the train, NF-01) appends a
  `[[breakage_day]]` entry for every working day one of its trains blocked (the dates it lists as
  `blocked_dates` in its log `receipts/train-log.ndjson`); the share it prints at each train,
  `breakage_share_28d`, and the worst window its `scripts/train/breakage_share.sh` reports are both obtained by
  calling `evaluate`. The KILL-FSIM decision (N33b-05) takes all three trigger values from `evaluate`. Neither
  recomputes the share by another rule.
