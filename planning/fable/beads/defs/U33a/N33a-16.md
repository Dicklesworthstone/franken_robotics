## Background

The airlock crate `frob-fsim` exposes FrankenSim to FrankenRobotics through traits grouped into Cargo features.
The `geometry` group (on by default) holds three traits: `GeometryKernel`, `SceneQuery` and `MeshExchange`,
over the FrankenSim crates `fs-query`, `fs-geom`, `fs-rep-sdf`, `fs-rep-mesh`, `fs-rep-frep`, `fs-scene`,
`fs-topo` and `fs-io`. This bead builds the first two, which the CPU referee needs at M1; the third, with the
representation crates, is bead N33a-30 at M2.

What they are for. FrankenSim's contact step takes its contact set from the caller and states that independent
endpoint gap checks are not continuous collision detection (FACT). Contact generation is therefore this
project's job: the contact crate builds contact sets from convex separation and signed-distance queries, and an
audit pass uses FrankenSim's certified continuous collision detection on sampled windows. `GeometryKernel` is
the door to those queries. `SceneQuery` is the door to referee scenes: role-tagged support and keep-out boxes
and half-spaces (FACT: `fs-scene/src/lib.rs`, 807 lines; `fs-query/src/convex.rs`, 616 lines).

Two limits of the sibling shape the trait and are recorded as data rather than hidden: (1) support maps exist
for the primitives FrankenSim has today, and capsules, convex hulls, heightfield pairs and distance-field pairs
are an upstream work item (FD-FSIM-03, P1; interim posture: this project's contact crate generates contact
sets and FrankenSim audits the path, with parity only where both apply); (2) FrankenSim's swept-sphere helper
encloses straight centre motion only (`fs-contact/src/broad_phase.rs:146–156`, a documented scope limit), so
bounds for rotating links are built by this project's contact crate and the helper is never used for an arc.

Admission: `fs-query` is covered by the INT-FSIM-01 record. `fs-geom` and `fs-scene` are first used here and
are admitted here by their own record at the pin of the day (an admission is never inherited). The named gate
INT-FSIM-08 (box-on-plane support equals the closed form; a mesh-backed interior is not admitted by that test)
is run by another bead through the trait defined here.

## Scope

In: the two traits and their in-process adapters; the capability record; the admission record for `fs-geom`
and `fs-scene`; closed-form known answers and controls for the queries exposed.
Out: contact-set generation, broad phase and arc-aware swept bounds (the contact subsystem); mesh, signed
distance field and implicit representations, topology checks and file exchange (bead N33a-30); scene packs
and the scene compiler (the scene subsystem).

## Technical approach

**Traits (DESIGN: intent, not signatures; module `frob_fsim::geometry`, feature `geometry`).**

```rust
pub trait GeometryKernel {
    fn capabilities(&self) -> GeometryCaps;      // which shape pairs have exact support, separation, distance
    fn support(&self, shape: &ConvexShape, pose: &Pose, dir: UnitVec3) -> Result<SupportPoint, RefereeRefusal>;
    fn separation(&self, a: (&ConvexShape, &Pose), b: (&ConvexShape, &Pose)) -> Result<Separation, RefereeRefusal>;
    fn certified_ccd(&self, a: MovingShape, b: MovingShape, window: TimeWindow) -> Result<CcdVerdict, RefereeRefusal>;
}
pub trait SceneQuery {
    fn build(&self, items: &[SceneItem]) -> Result<RefereeScene, RefereeRefusal>;   // boxes, half-spaces, roles
    fn supports_under(&self, scene: &RefereeScene, footprint: &ConvexShape, pose: &Pose) -> Result<Vec<SupportHit>, RefereeRefusal>;
    fn keep_out_hits(&self, scene: &RefereeScene, shape: &ConvexShape, pose: &Pose) -> Result<Vec<KeepOutHit>, RefereeRefusal>;
}
pub enum Role { Support, KeepOut }
pub struct Separation { pub gap: f64 /* metres; negative when overlapping */, pub witness_a: Point3, pub witness_b: Point3, pub normal: UnitVec3, pub kind: ResultKind }
pub enum ResultKind { Exact, Enclosure { lo: f64, hi: f64 } }
pub enum CcdVerdict { Clear { margin: f64 }, Hit { t_lo: Rational, t_hi: Rational }, Unresolved }
```

`ConvexShape` at M1: sphere, box, half-space, and whatever other convex primitives FrankenSim supports at the
pin, enumerated in `capabilities()`. Poses and vectors cross in bedrock geometric-algebra types or this
project's frame-typed wrappers; no `fs-query`, `fs-geom` or `fs-scene` type is public.

**Rules.**
1. *Capabilities are data.* `GeometryCaps` lists each shape pair as `exact`, `enclosure` or `unsupported`. A
   request for an unsupported pair is a typed refusal naming the pair; the adapter never substitutes a bounding
   shape.
2. *Results say what they are.* A distance returned by an iterative upstream routine is `Enclosure` with its
   bounds; a closed-form one is `Exact`. A caller that needs a certified gap uses the lower bound.
3. *The swept-sphere helper is not exposed for rotating motion.* `certified_ccd` accepts motions it can certify
   (the motion classes FrankenSim's certified routine states at the pin) and returns `Unresolved` otherwise;
   `Unresolved` is never mapped to `Clear`. FrankenSim's certified continuous collision detection lives in
   `fs-contact`, a crate of the `referee` group, so this one method exists only when the `referee` feature is
   enabled too; a geometry-only build refuses it with `FROB-REF-790` naming `referee`.
4. *Scenes are role-tagged.* A `SceneItem` without a role is refused. Half-spaces and boxes are the M1
   vocabulary; a mesh-backed interior is not admitted through `SceneQuery` (it needs scene cooking and swept
   cases, which are later work).
5. Every upstream error goes through the conversion of bead N33a-13.

**Admission record.** `covers.crates = ["fs-geom", "fs-scene"]`, in the form of INT-FSIM-01 applied to a
crate: compiled from this repository behind the `geometry` feature, the known answers below through the trait,
one negative control failing, citing the stack receipt of the day.

Determinism class: same platform, same binary. Evidence class: as FrankenSim's contracts state per query.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both traits compile under the `geometry` feature with no volatile type in their public face; `--no-default-features --features geometry` builds without `fs-mbd`.
- [ ] Box support: half-extents (0.3, 0.2, 0.1) m at the identity pose, direction (0, 0, −1) → support height −0.1 m; the same box rotated 45° about x → support value (0.2 + 0.1)·√2/2 = 0.2121320344 m below the centre, each to 10⁻¹² (TARGET).
- [ ] Sphere–half-space: centre height 0.75 m, radius 0.25 m over the plane z = 0 → gap 0.5 m, normal (0, 0, 1), `Exact`.
- [ ] Sphere–sphere: centres 1 m apart, radii 0.3 m and 0.2 m → gap 0.5 m; centres 0.4 m apart → gap −0.1 m.
- [ ] A scene with one support half-space and one keep-out box answers `supports_under` and `keep_out_hits` correctly for a footprint placed on the plane, inside the box and clear of it.
- [ ] An unsupported shape pair is refused by name; an item without a role is refused; a rotating-motion CCD request returns `Unresolved` or a certified verdict, never an uncertified `Clear`.
- [ ] The admission record for `fs-geom` and `fs-scene` exists and validates.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-fsim geometry`)
- `box_support_axis_aligned`: h(d) = a·|d_x| + b·|d_y| + c·|d_z| for half-extents (a, b, c); with (0.3, 0.2, 0.1) and d = (0, 0, −1): 0.1.
- `box_support_rotated_45_about_x`: 0.2·sin 45° + 0.1·cos 45° = 0.21213203435596426.
- `box_support_random_directions`: 10³ seeded unit directions against the closed form to 10⁻¹².
- `sphere_plane_gap`, `sphere_sphere_gap_separated`, `sphere_sphere_gap_overlapping`: the three values above.
- `separation_witnesses_consistent`: ‖witness_a − witness_b‖ equals |gap| and the normal is unit, to 10⁻¹².
- `scene_roles_required`, `scene_support_and_keep_out`.
- `unsupported_pair_refused`: a pair listed `unsupported` in the capabilities returns a refusal naming both shapes.
- `ccd_thin_plate_between_clear_endpoints`: a sphere of radius 0.05 m translating 1 m through a plate 1 mm thick at the midpoint: both endpoint poses are clear, and the verdict is `Hit` with a time bracket containing 1/2.
- `ccd_unresolved_never_clear`: a rotating link request returns `Unresolved` (or a certified verdict if the pin supports the class).
- Property test `support_is_positively_homogeneous_in_scale`: scaling a shape by s scales its support value by s.

### End-to-end script and logging
No script of its own; the capability is exercised by the contact known-answer battery of the contact subsystem.
The admission record is produced by the step `geometry_group_admission` of `scripts/e2e/airlock_groups.sh`
(bead N33a-34), which logs each closed-form value, its measured value, the tolerance and the control outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Closed forms: the box support function; sphere–plane and sphere–sphere gaps; the thin-plate sweep.
- Negative controls: an adapter build that answers the rotated-box support with the axis-aligned formula (0.1 instead of 0.2121) must fail; an endpoint-only gap check on the thin-plate sweep reports `Clear` and must be rejected by the test (the reason continuous detection exists); a swapped normal sign must fail the witness consistency test.
- Counterexample protected: "independent endpoint gap checks are continuous collision detection" is false; a thin step lying between two clear endpoint poses is the smallest case.
- Metamorphic relations: every query is invariant under a common rigid transform of both shapes (gap unchanged, witnesses and normal transformed), to 10⁻¹².
- Determinism lanes: bit-identical twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- When FrankenSim lands the support maps of FD-FSIM-03, `capabilities()` changes by itself; the switch-over rule is to re-run the contact generation parity gate.
- The 10⁻¹² tolerances are TARGETs for closed-form primitives; an iterative routine's stated tolerance replaces them where `ResultKind::Enclosure` applies.
- A scene's roles are the referee's only notion of semantics; richer scene packs live above the airlock and are lowered to this vocabulary for adjudication.
- If the worker-process fallback is active, geometry queries used inside a referee job run in the worker; queries used by the contact crate for its own contact generation need the bedrock or in-process path, which is one reason the fallback keeps bedrock crates as path dependencies.
