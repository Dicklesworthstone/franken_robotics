## Background

The Moonshot tier of living worlds in FrankenRobotics is a set of offline oracles: small regions of resolved
matter beside one referee world, used only to check cheaper proxy models on finalists (epic BD-416). For cloth
(a rug, a curtain) the reference is a sibling: **franken_textiles**, whose crate `ftx-drape` (FACT: about 116
thousand lines with `cloth.rs`, `contact.rs` and `integrator.rs`; read 2026-11-21) settles a cloth under
gravity against obstacles. Its foundation row is `FD-FTX`: posture Oracle and Subprocess, Moonshot; a worker
driven from `frob-conformance` behind a flag; **never a Cargo dependency**; admission gate INT-FTX-01; fallback
"no cloth tier; rigid proxies".

Why a worker process and not a dev-dependency. A Cargo graph holds one version of `serde_json` 1.x.
franken_textiles pins `serde_json = "=1.0.151"` at its workspace level and `ftx-drape` takes that pin;
FrankenSim's graph resolves 1.0.154 (through frankenscipy, which requires 1.0.154 or later; fastmcp_rust pins
`=1.0.154`). The two cannot share a graph. So the oracle is reached as a separate process in its own
workspace that exchanges whole jobs, the same posture the design defines for a pinned FrankenSim worker, until
the pin is relaxed in franken_textiles (upstream work item FD-FTX-01, P3; switch-over rule: re-run INT-FTX-01
with `ftx-drape` as a dev-dependency of `frob-conformance`, and keep the worker if `cargo metadata` still
reports a conflict).

**INT-FTX-01**: a square cloth draped over a sphere matches `ftx-drape`'s settled shape to its own stated
tolerance, with `ftx-drape` run as a worker process in its own workspace. The design says each sibling gate of
this kind is the pilot of its item as well as its gate. A Moonshot mechanism is not funded before its pilot
has run, so this bead does the part that needs no cloth solver of ours: it proves the oracle can be driven as
a worker, reproducibly, inside the machine envelope, and records the reference shape and its invariants.

**Ceiling, computed before the test:** the cloth oracle touches no critical-path claim. Its value is bounded
by the value of the cloth island, which is funded only if the proxy-adequacy pilot shows that proxies miss
failures (kill gate KILL-LIVING: proxies reproducing 90% or more of full-model failures end the tier). The
sibling itself reports drapes that take tens of CPU seconds for a few thousand vertices and no GPU path by
design, so the oracle is strictly an offline reference.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the dependency-conflict observation; a worker in its own workspace; the drape job and

## Technical approach

**Pilot record (written to `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before execution).** Mechanism: the
cloth evaluation tier's oracle, Moonshot. Tool and pin: franken_textiles at its revision of the day (head
`ecb13277aa02` on the day of reading), on its own toolchain file. Cost: at most one agent-day; CPU only; each
run under a timeout inside 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour. Fallback: no cloth tier; rigid proxies and hinged
plates. Kill gate: KILL-LIVING.

**Protocol (frozen before execution).**
1. *The conflict, observed.* In a scratch crate that depends by path on one FrankenSim crate and on
   `ftx-drape`, run `cargo metadata`. Expected: resolution fails, naming the two `serde_json` requirements.
   Record the message. (If it resolves, the pin has been relaxed upstream: record that, and the switch-over
   rule applies.)
2. *The worker workspace.* A directory `worker-ftx/` outside the core workspace with its own `[workspace]`
   table, lockfile and the sibling's toolchain channel, containing one small binary that depends on
   `ftx-drape` by path and on nothing of this project except a copied message schema. It reads a job on
   standard input and writes a result on standard output, both canonical JSON with binary columns
   base64-encoded; it has no network and no other side effect.
3. *The job.* `{ schema: "frob.ftx-job/1", cloth: { side_m: 1.0, grid: 65 × 65, areal_density_kg_m2,
   stretch and bend parameters by name }, obstacle: { sphere: { radius_m: 0.25, centre: (0, 0, 0.25) } },
   initial: flat, centred over the sphere at height 0.55 m, gravity_m_s2: 9.81, tolerance: the sibling's own
   default convergence tolerance, read at the pin, seed }`. Material parameters are the sibling's documented
   defaults at the pin, recorded by name and value.
4. *The result.* `{ job_hash, vertices (65 × 65 × 3 little-endian f64), iterations, residual, energy,
   wall_s, digest }`, with `job_hash` the BLAKE3 of the job's canonical bytes; resubmission of the same job is
   idempotent.
5. *Reproducibility.* Run the job three times. Pass: identical digests, or, if the sibling does not claim bit
   reproducibility, a maximum vertex difference below its stated tolerance; which of the two holds is
   recorded as a fact about the oracle.
6. *Invariants of the settled shape* (properties any correct drape of a centred square over a centred sphere
   must have, used to catch a mis-driven oracle):
   a. symmetry: the shape is invariant under the eight symmetries of the square (rotations by 90° and
      reflections) to within twice the stated tolerance;
   b. contact: no vertex is inside the sphere by more than the sibling's contact tolerance, and the centre
      vertex rests on the sphere's top, z = 0.50 m within that tolerance plus the cloth's thickness;
   c. inextensibility: the mean edge strain is below the bound implied by the stretch stiffness and the
      cloth's weight, as the sibling's own report states it (recorded);
   d. corners hang lower than edge midpoints, which hang lower than the centre.
7. *Cost.* Wall time and peak memory per run.
8. *Refusal path.* A job with a negative radius is answered by a `refuse` message carrying the sibling's
   reason; the worker never substitutes a default.

**Falsifier (any one).** The worker cannot be built without adding a dependency to the core graph; runs are
not reproducible within the oracle's own tolerance; an invariant of step 6 fails; a run exceeds the machine
envelope. If the falsifier holds, the cloth island is not funded and the row reads "no cloth tier".

**The gate proper.** INT-FTX-01 compares a candidate cloth model of this project with the reference. Until a
candidate exists, the admission record holds the reference half (`status: not-run`, reason "no candidate; the
tier is not funded") with the reference digest; when a candidate exists, the comparison is: maximum vertex
distance between candidate and reference settled shapes, after aligning the eight-fold symmetry, at most the
sibling's stated tolerance.

Evidence class: `Tested` for the oracle's reproducibility; any result that rests on it is labelled
`development-oracle` and evaluation tier.

- [ ] The pilot record exists with protocol, falsifier, ceiling, fallback, cost, tool and pin written before the run, and the result after it.
- [ ] `cargo metadata` on the shared-graph scratch crate is recorded (expected: a `serde_json` conflict naming 1.0.151 and 1.0.154).
- [ ] The worker builds in its own workspace and runs the job three times inside the envelope; reproducibility is recorded as bit-identical or within tolerance.
- [ ] The four invariants of the settled shape hold and their measured values are recorded.
- [ ] The malformed job is refused with the sibling's reason.
- [ ] `xtask check-deps` refuses a planted manifest that adds `ftx-drape` to the core workspace, and no manifest of this repository names it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (the worker's own tests in `worker-ftx/`, and the harness in `frob-conformance` behind the flag `oracle-ftx`)
- `job_hash_is_canonical`: two key orders of the same job give the same hash.
- `result_roundtrip`: the vertex block decodes to 65 × 65 × 3 finite numbers.
- `drape_reproducible_three_runs`.
- `drape_symmetry_eightfold`: maximum deviation under each of the eight symmetries ≤ twice the tolerance.
- `drape_contact_and_apex`: centre vertex height 0.50 m within tolerance plus thickness; minimum signed distance to the sphere ≥ −tolerance.
- `drape_ordering`: corner height < edge-midpoint height < centre height.
- `malformed_job_refused`.
- `harness_without_flag_reports_not_run`: the conformance suite built without the flag lists the oracle as absent, not as passed.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/pilots/ftx_drape_pilot.sh` (kept with the pilot record): builds the worker, runs the conflict probe,
the three drapes and the refusal case. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time and peak memory
(thresholds: one hour, 16 GiB), the job hash, the three result digests, the largest symmetry deviation, the
apex height, the minimum sphere distance, the mean edge strain, and the `cargo metadata` message; a final line
with the recommendation.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the apex height equals the top of the sphere (0.50 m); eight-fold symmetry; the height ordering.
- Negative controls: a job with the sphere shifted by 5 cm must break the symmetry check (showing the check can fail); a worker that returns the initial flat sheet (planted) must fail contact and ordering; the planted core-workspace edge to `ftx-drape` must be refused.
- Determinism lanes: the three runs on one machine; a fourth on the second host, recorded and compared within tolerance.

## Considerations

- The oracle's material model is the sibling's; a drape "matching" it says the candidate agrees with one implicit-cloth implementation, not with a physical rug. Reality stays a separate claim.
- If FD-FTX-01 lands, the worker may be replaced by a dev-dependency after `cargo metadata` shows a clean resolution; the gate is rerun at that pin.
- franken_textiles may hold private pattern data in its own repository; the worker reads only the job on standard input.
- This pilot is at backlog priority and is run only when epic BD-416 comes up for a funding decision.
