## Background

FrankenRobotics derives kernel arithmetic symbolically with FrankenSymPy (the owner's Rust computer-algebra
project; foundation row `FD-FSYM`, build-time posture, confined to the annex `derive/frob-derive`). The first
admission gate, INT-FSYM-01, shows the annex builds and derives one kernel. The second, **INT-FSYM-02**, admits
the specific files the kinematic analyses lean on: FrankenSymPy's sparse analytic Jacobians generated offline,
with assumptions preserved (FACT: `frankensympy/crates/fsym-calculus/src/sparse_jacobian.rs`, 1,705 lines, and
`compile.rs`, 2,198 lines; `fsym-formal/src/checker.rs`, 248 lines, and `envelope.rs`, 89 lines; read
2026-11-21). The rule is that a crate that compiles is not yet admitted: for each source file the project
leans on, a first test must pass before any code above calls it. Its test: **compile the planar two-link
Jacobian and evaluate it at a regular and a singular configuration against closed forms.**

Why this fixture. Kinematics is algebraic geometry (construction C-11): forward kinematics is a product of
exponentials, a polynomial map in (cᵢ, sᵢ) with cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, so Jacobians and singular loci are polynomial
objects. The planar two-link arm is the smallest mechanism with a nontrivial singular locus, and its Jacobian
and determinant are known by hand. A Jacobian route that is wrong at a singularity (dividing by something that
vanishes there, or simplifying under an assumption that fails there) is exactly the defect that would later
corrupt the workspace and singularity analyses, so the singular evaluation is the point of the test, not a
corner of it.

Why a separate bead. The gate register dates INT-FSYM-02 at M0 and lists it among the admission records the
M0 exit must hold, while the seed bead that uses the route for a real kernel (the derived leg
inverse-kinematics kernel with the workspace and singularity analyses, BD-105) is an M1 bead. This bead
produces the M0 record on the smallest fixture; BD-105 re-runs the gate at the pin of its own day on the leg.

## Scope

In: the derivation of the two-link forward map and its Jacobian through the sparse-Jacobian route; lowering to
the kernel representation; the evaluation at two configurations; the preserved assumptions; the admission
record.
Out: any spatial chain, inverse kinematics, workspace or singular-locus analysis (BD-105); certificates for
the Jacobian (later proof work).

## Technical approach

**The mechanism.** Link lengths l₁, l₂ (metres). Joint coordinates enter as unit complex numbers (c₁, s₁) and
(c₂, s₂) with cᵢ = cos qᵢ, sᵢ = sin qᵢ, because kernels use no trigonometric functions: a revolute coordinate
is a unit complex number, and angle sums are products. With c₁₂ = c₁·c₂ − s₁·s₂ and s₁₂ = s₁·c₂ + c₁·s₂:

- position: x = l₁·c₁ + l₂·c₁₂, y = l₁·s₁ + l₂·s₁₂;
- Jacobian with respect to (q₁, q₂): J = [[−l₁·s₁ − l₂·s₁₂, −l₂·s₁₂], [l₁·c₁ + l₂·c₁₂, l₂·c₁₂]];
- determinant: det J = l₁·l₂·s₂ (after reduction modulo cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1).

The derivative with respect to an angle is taken through the unit-complex representation by the rule
dcᵢ/dqᵢ = −sᵢ, dsᵢ/dqᵢ = cᵢ, which the derivation states as an assumption of the program.

**Steps.**
1. In `derive/frob-derive`, declare l₁, l₂ (dimension length) and cᵢ, sᵢ (dimensionless) with the assumptions
   cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1, l₁ > 0, l₂ > 0.
2. Build (x, y); obtain J from `fsym-calculus`'s sparse-Jacobian generation; record the sparsity pattern it
   reports (four structural nonzeros).
3. Compile through `fsym-calculus`'s compile route and lower to a `frob-kir` program `K-test-jac2r` with
   inputs (l₁, l₂, c₁, s₁, c₂, s₂) and outputs the four entries of J and det J; the dimension checker must
   accept it (entries of J in metres; det J in square metres).
4. Check that the assumptions survive: the emitted program's metadata (the formal envelope) lists cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1
   and the positivity of the lengths; an output that was simplified using an assumption cites it.
5. Emit the Rust body; evaluate in `f64` at the two configurations below; compare with the closed forms.
6. Ask FrankenSymPy's checker to confirm the polynomial identity det J − l₁·l₂·s₂ ≡ 0 modulo the ideal
   generated by c₁² + s₁² − 1 and c₂² + s₂² − 1.
7. Write the admission record for INT-FSYM-02 with `covers.crates` naming `fsym-calculus` and `fsym-formal`.

**Configurations and closed-form values (l₁ = 0.3 m, l₂ = 0.2 m).**

| Case | (q₁, q₂) rad | J (metres) | det J (m²) | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regular | (0.4, 0.9) | [[−0.30953713977603375, −0.1927116370834386], [0.329818063925783, 0.05349976572491747]] | 0.3·0.2·sin 0.9 = 0.046999614577649 | 2 |
| Singular (arm straight) | (0.4, 0) | [[−0.19470917115432526, −0.07788366846173012], [0.46053049700144255, 0.18421219880057704]] | 0 | 1: the first column is (l₁ + l₂)/l₂ = 2.5 times the second |

Tolerance: 10⁻¹² relative per entry for the Jacobian (TARGET); for the singular determinant, the absolute value
is at most 10⁻¹⁶ m² when evaluated from the reduced form l₁·l₂·s₂ with s₂ = 0 (it is exactly zero), and the
unreduced 2×2 determinant of the evaluated entries is at most 10⁻¹⁶ m² as well (recorded).

Determinism class: D0 for the derivation; the evaluation is plain `f64` arithmetic. Evidence class: `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The two-link Jacobian compiles through the sparse-Jacobian and compile routes of `fsym-calculus` in the annex, on T, and lowers to a dimensionally homogeneous `frob-kir` program.
- [ ] At the regular configuration all four entries and the determinant match the closed forms to 10⁻¹² relative.
- [ ] At the singular configuration all four entries match to 10⁻¹² relative, the determinant is zero within the stated bound, the result is finite (no division by a vanishing quantity) and the reported rank is 1.
- [ ] The identity det J ≡ l₁·l₂·s₂ modulo the two circle relations is accepted by the checker; the false twin det J ≡ l₁·l₂·c₂ is rejected.
- [ ] The emitted program's envelope lists the assumptions cᵢ² + sᵢ² = 1 and positive lengths.
- [ ] The negative control is caught: a Jacobian with one entry's sign flipped fails the regular comparison.
- [ ] An admission record for INT-FSYM-02 exists at M0 with the pin, the command, the measured residuals and the receipt hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test` in `derive/frob-derive`, target `jac2r`)
- `jac2r_regular_matches_closed_form`: inputs c₁ = cos 0.4, s₁ = sin 0.4, c₂ = cos 0.9, s₂ = sin 0.9; expected entries and determinant as in the table.
- `jac2r_singular_matches_closed_form`: c₂ = 1, s₂ = 0; expected entries as in the table; determinant 0; columns proportional with factor 2.5 to 10⁻¹².
- `jac2r_outputs_finite_on_singular_locus`: for 10³ values of q₁ with q₂ ∈ {0, π}, every output is finite.
- `jac2r_central_difference_agreement`: at 10³ seeded regular configurations, each entry agrees with a central difference of (x, y) with step 10⁻⁶ rad to within 10⁻⁷ times the largest absolute entry of J at that configuration (the difference's rounding error is about 10⁻¹⁰ m, so a bound relative to each entry would fail wherever an entry crosses zero, for example −l₂·s₁₂ near q₁ + q₂ = 0).
- `jac2r_det_identity_accepted` and `jac2r_det_false_twin_rejected`.
- `jac2r_dimensions`: entries have dimension length and the determinant length squared; a planted program adding an entry of J to det J is refused by the dimension checker.
- `jac2r_assumptions_in_envelope`.
- `jac2r_sign_flip_caught`: the planted program with +l₂·s₁₂ in the top-right entry fails `jac2r_regular_matches_closed_form`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/admission_m0.sh` (bead N33a-28), step `int_fsym_02`: builds the annex, runs the `jac2r` tests
through the offload shim and writes the record. NDJSON lines carry the command, exit code, wall time, per
configuration the eight measured numbers (four entries, the determinant, the largest relative residual, the
rank, the column ratio) against their closed forms and tolerances, the identity verdicts and the control's
`caught` flag.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Closed forms: the table above; det J = l₁·l₂·sin q₂.
- Negative controls (registered for INT-FSYM-02): the sign-flipped entry; the false determinant identity; a route that returns the determinant as a quotient with s₂ in a denominator (planted) must fail the finiteness test at the singular configuration.
- Metamorphic relations: rotating the base by an angle φ (replacing q₁ by q₁ + φ) rotates both columns of J by φ and leaves det J unchanged; scaling both lengths by k scales J by k and det J by k².
- Determinism lanes: regeneration of the emitted program is bit-identical twice and across hosts; the evaluated numbers are identical in debug and release.

## Considerations

- The fixture uses `f64` on the host; the kernel representation refuses `f64` on GPU targets, which is irrelevant here because this is an admission test, not a shipped kernel.
- FrankenSymPy's limits that bound later use and are not exercised here (FACT): Buchberger with a cap of 1,024 basis polynomials, univariate resultants only, a real solver capped at 8 variables and zero-dimensional ideals, so general six-joint inverse kinematics is not promised.
- If the sparse-Jacobian route fails admission, the fallback is differentiation inside `frob-kir` (its own derivative program by the same transformation), and the analyses of BD-105 use that; the record then says which route produced the Jacobian.
- Admission is never inherited: BD-105 re-runs INT-FSYM-02 at its own pin.
