## Background

FrankenRobotics memoizes results in one content-addressed **memo graph**: compiled subterms, analyses,
controller syntheses, scene compiles, episodes and comparisons, each keyed by a hash derived from its declared
inputs. The store has a byte budget (64 GB by default, DESIGN) and on exhaustion it evicts and never blocks.
What to evict matters more here than in a page cache: a scan over many designs touches each key exactly once,
so a recency policy fills the store with entries that will never be asked for again and pushes out the hot
set of the design being edited.

FrankenSQLite, the owner's SQLite rewrite, already has a policy built for that pattern. FACT (read 2026-11-21;
re-read at the pin): `frankensqlite/crates/fsqlite-pager/src/s3_fifo.rs` (3,058 lines) implements a
**three-queue policy**: a small admission queue, a main queue with bounded reinsertion, and a ghost queue of
metadata, with an adaptive small share between 5% and 30% and a rollout gate. Sibling files `arc_cache.rs` and
`evalue_eviction.rs` hold two further policies. The state machine is keyed on `fsqlite_types::PageNumber`, so
it cannot be instantiated over content hashes.

Posture (DESIGN): **Pattern** for the policy, written with the memo graph in `frob-verbs`; **Oracle** for the
sibling module, as a dev-dependency of `frob-conformance` only. Upstream work item FD-FSQL-01 (P3) asks
FrankenSQLite to make the state machine generic over its key type; the switch-over rule is to consume it and
delete the local copy. Fallback if the gate fails: LRU with admission weighted by recompute cost; never an
unbounded store.

**INT-FSQL-02**: under a bijection from keys to page numbers, our eviction sequence equals the sibling's on 10
recorded traces of at least 10⁵ accesses, and on the edit-loop trace the hit ratio is not below LRU's.

One deliberate departure from the sibling must not hide inside that comparison: memo entries differ in
recompute cost by orders of magnitude and in size, so the store accounts in bytes and its admission rule may
refuse a cheap entry. That departure is tested separately.

Why at M1: the gate register dates INT-FSQL-02 at M1 (the memo store first serves analyses then), while the
seed bead that names the gate (memoized episodes, BD-220) is an M2 bead. This bead produces the M1 record on
analysis-level traces; BD-220 re-runs the gate with episode traces.

## Scope

In: the policy state machine in unit-size mode; the byte-accounted, cost-weighted wrapper; the differential
harness against the sibling module; the trace format and the ten traces; the LRU comparison; the M1 admission
record.
Out: the memo graph, key derivation and shadow recomputation (BD-111); episode nodes and fingerprint scoping
(BD-220); retention and collection of evidence (pinned nodes are honoured here, defined elsewhere).

## Technical approach

**The policy, as a pattern (general shape; the exact thresholds, the reinsertion bound and the adaptation rule
are those of the sibling file at the pin, and the differential test is the arbiter).** Three FIFO queues:
*small* (new keys enter here), *main* (keys that proved themselves) and *ghost* (keys recently evicted from
small, metadata only). Each resident key has a small saturating access counter.
1. *Hit*: increment the key's counter (saturating); no queue movement.
2. *Miss*: if the key is in ghost, insert it at the head of main; otherwise at the head of small.
3. *Evict* while over capacity: if small is at or above its target share, pop its tail: a key accessed since
   insertion moves to main, otherwise it is evicted and its identity enters ghost. Else pop the tail of main: a
   key with a positive counter is reinserted at the head with the counter decremented (bounded reinsertion),
   otherwise it is evicted.
4. *Adaptation*: the small queue's share moves between 5% and 30% by the sibling's rule.
The local implementation (`frob_verbs::memo::evict`) is a pure state machine: `fn access(&mut self, key: K,
size: u64, cost: Cost) -> Vec<Evicted<K>>`, generic over the key type, with no clock and no randomness, so that
its eviction sequence is a pure function of the access sequence (the decision reads no clock; it is replayable).

**Two modes.** *Unit mode*: every entry has size 1 and admission weighting is off; this is the mode compared
with the sibling. *Store mode*: capacity in bytes; an entry larger than the capacity is refused; an entry
whose recompute cost is below the admission threshold may be refused when the store is full (it is cheaper to
recompute than to hold); pinned keys (evidence cited by a live claim) are skipped by eviction and are counted
against the budget.

**Differential harness (`crates/frob-conformance/src/oracle/s3fifo.rs`).**
1. Read a trace: a sequence of keys (32-byte hashes) with sizes and costs.
2. Build the bijection: distinct keys in first-appearance order map to page numbers 1, 2, 3, ….
3. Drive the sibling state machine with the page-number sequence and the local one (unit mode) with the keys,
   with equal capacities and equal configuration read from the sibling's defaults at the pin.
4. After every access compare the evicted set mapped through the bijection, in order. The first difference
   stops the run and reports the access index, both eviction lists and both queue occupancies.
5. At the end compare hit counts.

**Traces (`crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/memo_traces/`, format `frob.memo-trace/1`: a header with the
source and the seed, then fixed-width records; each at least 10⁵ accesses).** Ten traces: (1) *edit-loop*,
recorded from a session of repeated one-parameter edits and `design.check` on a template design; (2) *sweep*,
recorded from an analysis sweep over the first archetypes; (3) *compile*, recorded from incremental
compilation of a modular design; and seven generated traces labelled `synthetic` with fixed seeds: (4) a pure
scan (each key once), (5) a loop over a set slightly larger than capacity, (6) a Zipf-distributed stream with
exponent 0.8, (7) a scan interleaved with a hot set of 5% of capacity, (8) ghost churn (keys return just after
eviction), (9) a phase change (one hot set replaced by another), (10) a mix of the three recorded traces.
Recorded traces contain key hashes, sizes and costs only, never scene data.

**LRU comparison.** On trace 1 at capacities of 1%, 5% and 20% of the distinct keys, the local policy's hit
ratio is not below LRU's (compared exactly as integers: hits ≥ LRU hits).

**Admission record.** Gate INT-FSQL-02, `covers.crates` naming the pager package as it appears in the tracked
lockfile of the test workspace, the ten trace digests, per trace the accesses, evictions compared and hit
counts, the LRU comparison, and the control outcomes.

Determinism class: D0. Evidence class: `Tested`. The sibling module never appears in a production edge.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On each of the ten traces (each ≥ 10⁵ accesses) the local unit-mode eviction sequence equals the sibling's under the bijection, access by access; zero differences.
- [ ] On the edit-loop trace the hit count is not below LRU's at the three capacities.
- [ ] Store mode: the byte budget is never exceeded after an access returns; an entry larger than the budget is refused; a pinned key is never evicted in 10⁶ randomized accesses; a cheap entry is refused when the store is full and admitted when it is not.
- [ ] The negative control is caught: a local policy with the ghost queue disabled diverges from the sibling on the ghost-churn trace and the harness reports the first differing access.
- [ ] The eviction sequence is a pure function of the access sequence: two runs give identical sequences, and no clock is read (a source scan finds no time call in the module).
- [ ] An M1 admission record for INT-FSQL-02 exists and validates; the pager crate is a dev-dependency of `frob-conformance` only (`check-deps` refuses a normal edge).

## Test plan

### Unit tests (`cargo test -p frob-verbs memo::evict`, `cargo test -p frob-conformance oracle::s3fifo`)
- `miss_enters_small`, `ghost_hit_enters_main`, `small_tail_promotes_when_accessed`, `main_tail_reinserts_with_decrement`: four hand traces of at most ten accesses at capacity 4, whose expected queue contents are written out in the test and were derived by stepping the rules above by hand.
- `scan_does_not_evict_hot_set`: capacity 100; a hot set of 10 keys accessed between every 5 scan keys over 10⁴ scan keys; all 10 hot keys are resident at the end (LRU with the same capacity keeps them too; a FIFO control loses them).
- `differential_trace_01` … `differential_trace_10`: zero differences each.
- `lru_not_better_on_edit_loop`: three capacities.
- `store_mode_budget_never_exceeded` (property test, 10⁶ accesses with random sizes from 1 B to 1 GiB against a 64 GiB budget).
- `store_mode_pins_survive`, `store_mode_oversize_refused`, `store_mode_cheap_refused_when_full`.
- `no_clock_in_policy` (source scan); `sequence_is_deterministic`.
- `bijection_is_first_appearance_order`: the mapping is reproducible from the trace alone.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/memo_eviction_oracle.sh`: builds the oracle harness, runs the ten differential comparisons and
the LRU comparison through the offload shim, and writes the admission record. Logging per `STD-E2E`: each line
has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code and wall time; per trace a `metric` line
with accesses (threshold ≥ 100,000), evictions compared, differences (threshold 0), local hits, sibling hits
and LRU hits; on a difference, the access index, both eviction lists and the trace digest; a `summary` with
the gate INT-FSQL-02.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: equality of eviction sequences on ten traces; the four hand traces.
- Negative controls (registered for INT-FSQL-02): the ghost queue disabled; a fixed small share of 50% (outside the sibling's range) must diverge on the phase-change trace; a harness that compares only final hit counts (planted) must be shown insufficient by a policy that differs in order and not in count on a constructed trace.
- Counterexample kept in view: recency is a good eviction signal for a memo store (false for a scan that touches each key once).
- Determinism lanes: identical sequences twice, in debug and release, on both hosts.

## Considerations

- A differential test against a moving sibling breaks at pin moves when the sibling tunes its policy. That is intended: the local copy then follows, or FD-FSQL-01 lands and the copy is deleted.
- The policy is an adaptive controller in the sense of the workspace's controller gate; its replay scenario is the trace set, its evidence records are the eviction logs, and its demonstrated fallback is LRU with cost-weighted admission.
- A stale result served is costed at 100 units against the device-seconds of a needless recomputation (DESIGN ratio of the memo policy); eviction never creates staleness, only misses, so this policy can cost time and cannot cost correctness.
- The 64 GB default and the admission threshold are store parameters owned by the memo-graph bead; this bead tests the mechanism at whatever values are configured.
