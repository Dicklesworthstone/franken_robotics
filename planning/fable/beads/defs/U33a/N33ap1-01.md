## Background

The airlock crate `frob-fsim` is the only crate of FrankenRobotics that names FrankenSim's volatile types, and
two of its three binding rules are about refusals. It converts and never reinterprets: a FrankenSim refusal
becomes a typed referee refusal carrying the upstream reason verbatim, and is never retried with weakened
inputs (bead N33a-13). It exposes capacities as data: FrankenSim's sustained contact solver admits at most 21
contacts in one solve (FACT, read 2026-11-21 at `fs-contact/src/sustained/solve.rs`, `MAX_CONTACTS = 21`), and
the airlock carries that limit as a field read from the sibling, checks it when a model is compiled and again
before every upstream call, and refuses an over-capacity contact set by name with `FROB-REF-721` (exit code 5),
`observed` the count and `required` the capacity, instead of solving a truncated subset (bead N33a-14). A
truncated set would bias exactly the comparison the studio exists to make (a tracked design against a
point-foot design), and it would do so silently.

The standing rules of the numerics chapter are: refuse, never truncate; a budget stop publishes nothing
partial; a misspelt parameter is a refusal, not a default. The M0 part of the end-to-end script
`scripts/e2e/airlock_refusals.sh` (bead N33a-29) shows the absent-group refusal, the capacity as published by
`frob doctor` and the misspelt tier. It cannot show the contact refusals: at M0 the verb `sim.run` takes only
the catalog fixture `g1` on a contact-free step (BD-024), so no contact set reaches the referee through `frob`.
That becomes possible at M1, when the referee contact step of BD-080 lands, with `frob sim run --tier referee`
on stand and drop fixtures. This bead adds the M1 steps to the same script: the user-side proof that a contact
set one above the capacity is refused by name, that nothing truncated is published, and that a refusal raised by
FrankenSim itself arrives with its reason verbatim after exactly one upstream call.

It is the second half of one user-visible capability: "the referee tells you by name why it will not answer".
Split from N33a-29 in polishing round 1, because those steps cannot run before M1.

## Scope

In: the M1 steps of `scripts/e2e/airlock_refusals.sh` (capacity fits, capacity refused at admission, capacity
refused at run time with no truncation, upstream refusal verbatim with one upstream call); their synthetic
fixtures and the fixture generator; the test builds with planted defects that the script must catch; the M1
lines of the script's log.
Out: the script's form, its helper, its M0 steps and its logging frame (bead N33a-29); the mechanisms: the
conversion table (N33a-13), the capacities record, the two checks and the `referee` doctor section (N33a-14),
the contact step and its propagation of the refusal (BD-080), the transactional step (BD-081), the terminal
receipt (N29a-05) and the `run.show` and `run.list` verbs (BD-097); the traction subsystem's own capacity step,
which asserts the code and the two numbers on its quadruped (N18a-35, step `capacity_refusal`); the worker-
mismatch refusal `FROB-REF-791` and the stack-mismatch refusal `FROB-REF-890`.

## Technical approach

**Where the steps live.** In `scripts/e2e/airlock_refusals.sh`, tagged `M1`. Under `--milestone M1` (the
default once M1 is reached) the script runs its M0 steps and then these; before M1 they are logged `not-run`
with this bead's key. The capacity C used by every step below is read in the M0 step `capacity_is_data` from
`frob doctor --json --section referee`; nothing in the script is a literal 21.

**Fixtures (synthetic, under `tests/fixtures/airlock/`).** A generator, `gen_rest.sh <n>`, writes a referee
fixture of a rigid slab (0.6 m × 0.3 m × 0.05 m, 2 kg) resting on a plane through n sample spheres, placed in
row-major order on a grid of three rows of ⌈n/3⌉ columns (n = 21 gives a full 7 × 3 grid; n = 22 adds one
sample in an eighth column), in the fixture form that the M1 verb accepts for its stand and drop fixtures
(BD-080). `rest21` and `rest22` are checked in for the capacity read on the day of writing. A third fixture,
`tip22`, is a box standing on a 4 × 4 grid of 16 samples on one face, with 22 samples on an adjacent face and an
initial angular velocity that tips it onto that face within 0.2 s: its nominal stance (16) fits and its
exceptional stance `side: 22` overflows. Before the capacity steps run, the script checks that the checked-in
fixtures have C and C + 1 samples; if C has changed upstream, it regenerates them in scratch and logs the
regeneration, and it never runs a boundary test on a stale fixture.

**Steps (M1).** Every `frob sim run` below passes `--key airlock-<step>-<fixture>`: `sim.run` is a run verb, and
without an idempotency key it is refused with `FROB-REF-805`, exit 3 (N32a-09), so a step without one would
assert the wrong refusal.
5. `capacity_fits`: `frob sim run --tier referee --seconds 0.05 --json` on `rest<C>`. Assert: exit code 0;
   the episode reports C contacts in every step after settling; no refusal; mechanical energy never increases
   from step to step (the sign rule of KA-392, E(k+1) ≤ E(k) + 10⁻⁹ J); the slab's height drifts by less than
   1 mm after 0.02 s of settling.
6. `capacity_refused_at_admission`: the same command on `rest<C+1>`, whose nominal stance overflows. Assert:
   exit code 5; `ok: false`; `refusal.code = "FROB-REF-721"`; `observed = C + 1`; `required = C`; unit
   `contacts`; `retry = "after-change"`; the repairs equal the registry row's in its order (a documented patch
   reduction with its qualification fixture, then `--profile strict`); the refusal cites the source fact row of
   the capacity and the stack-lock digest; and `frob run list --json` before and after shows no new run (the
   refusal came from `referee_admission` before any job was planned, so no step ran and no budget was charged).
7. `capacity_refused_at_run_time`: the same command on `tip22` with `--seconds 0.5`. Assert: the run is
   admitted with `side: 22` listed among the overflow sets of its referee coverage (N33a-14, policy 3); it then
   ends with `FROB-REF-721`, `observed = C + 1` (the side face's sample count: 22 at C = 21, the fixture being
   regenerated as `tip<C+1>` when C moves), `required = C`, exit code 5; `frob run show <run> --json` shows the
   terminal receipt with one refused trial, zero completed, zero failed and zero unstarted; every published
   chunk ends at or before the last chunk boundary preceding the refused step, no published step has fewer than
   the true number of contacts (the episode's per-step contact counts equal the counts of the generator's
   reference trajectory up to the refusal), and the `.frun` episode records the refusal at that step (BD-080
   propagates it unchanged).
8. `upstream_refusal`: a referee request that FrankenSim's own solver rejects at the pin: the fixed-witness gate
   on the flat-to-edge transition fixture of N33a-14's `fixed_witness_refusal_is_typed` (a witness that
   separates tangentially within one call). It runs on a test build `frob-counters` with the airlock's
   upstream-call counter enabled (the counter N33a-14's unit tests use), which prints the count to the log on
   exit. Assert: `ok: false`; `refusal.code = "FROB-REF-721"` with payload `kind = "fixed-witness"` (N16-REF's
   row; the airlock's variant `FixedWitness`); `retry = "after-change"` (N33a-13 presents its `NumericalOnly`
   class that way) with the fixed-witness repairs in the registry's order, a shorter interval so that witnesses
   are fixed inside it (witness migration between calls), then `--profile strict`; an
   `upstream` object with crate `fs-contact`, the upstream variant and the upstream message, non-empty, equal
   byte for byte to the message the same input produces in the in-process unit fixture (or, above 2 KiB, its
   first 2 KiB and the explicit truncation marker), and `pin` equal to the binary's lock digest; and exactly one
   upstream call for the refused step (no second attempt with relaxed inputs).

**Negative controls (test builds, each with one planted defect, each of which the script must report as a
failure).** (1) Truncate to C contacts and continue: step 6 completes instead of refusing, and step 7 publishes a
step with C contacts where the reference has 22. (2) The run-time check removed: step 7's refusal arrives from
upstream without `observed` and `required`, and the field assertions fail. (3) The upstream message replaced by a
generic string: step 8's byte comparison fails. (4) A retry with a relaxed tolerance after the refusal: step 8
counts two upstream calls. The script's `--controls` mode builds the four test builds through the offload shim
into a separate target directory and runs the affected steps against each; the summary lists each control as
`caught` or `missed`, and a missed control fails the script.

**Logging (per the shared standard, in the script's log of N33a-29).** Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`
(this key for these steps), `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time, and the envelope's
`evidence` block; `refusal` events carry the full refusal object (code, message, pointer, observed, required,
unit, retry, repairs, upstream); `metric` lines carry contact counts against the capacity C, the energy
increment maximum against 10⁻⁹ J, the height drift against 1 mm, the upstream-call count against 1, and the
terminal receipt's four counts; digests of the completed runs and of each fixture (with a `regenerated` flag);
for each control its build, the step it targets and `caught`; on failure the first differing field of the
expected and actual refusal, or the first step whose contact count differs from the reference, with the run
identifier and scratch paths needed to reproduce. The `summary` line adds the controls caught and missed and
KA-778.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At M1 the script passes from a clean checkout on the Mac and on the Linux host, running its M0 steps and the four steps above; before M1 the four steps are logged `not-run` with this key.
- [ ] `rest<C>` runs with C contacts per step, no refusal, energy non-increasing and drift below 1 mm.
- [ ] `rest<C+1>` is refused with `FROB-REF-721`, exit code 5, `observed = C + 1`, `required = C`, the registry's repairs in order, and no new run in `run.list`.
- [ ] `tip22` is admitted with its overflow set listed, then refused at run time with the terminal receipt counting one refused trial and no published step past the last chunk boundary before the refusal; no published step has a truncated contact set.
- [ ] A FrankenSim-raised refusal arrives with crate, variant and message verbatim, the lock digest, and exactly one upstream call.
- [ ] The four planted controls are each caught; a missed control fails the script.
- [ ] The capacity C used throughout is the value `frob doctor` reported, and the checked-in fixtures are regenerated, never reused, when C differs from their sample counts.
- [ ] The log validates against the shared schema and its `summary` lists the controls and KA-778.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The steps are the test. The script's `--self-test` mode (N33a-29) gains matchers for these steps, each run on a
matching and a non-matching fixture and required to tell them apart: a 721 envelope with and without `upstream`;
a terminal receipt with one refused trial and one with a failed trial; a chunk list ending before and after the
refused step; a log with one and with two upstream calls. The fixture generator has its own check, `gen_rest.sh
--check`, which asserts that n = 21 and n = 22 produce exactly 21 and 22 samples on the documented grid and that
the output is byte-identical on a rerun. A shell lint runs in the formatting lane.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead extends `scripts/e2e/airlock_refusals.sh`; the logging is described above. It is run at the M1 exit
review through the shared exit runner, and after every pin move: an upstream change of the capacity constant or
of a refusal variant shows up here first.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-778 (control half): C contacts fit and C + 1 refuse, by name, with the two numbers.
- KA-392 (sign rule): the resting slab never gains mechanical energy from one step to the next.
- Negative controls: the four planted test builds above.
- Counterexamples protected: extra contacts can be dropped when a world exceeds its capacity (false); the referee can adjudicate every scenario the fast tiers run (false); a refused step may be retried with weakened inputs (false).
- Determinism lanes: the refusal envelopes are byte-identical on a rerun except for `ts` and `cost.wall_ms`; the completed runs give identical digests twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The test builds cost several links of the M1 closure; `--controls` is a separate mode, run at the M1 exit and at every pin move, while the default run uses only the release binary and `frob-counters`.
- The run-time fixture depends on the referee's contact generation placing all 22 samples of the side face in one step; if the tipping lands on an edge first, the episode may pass through counts between 16 and 22. The reference trajectory is generated once at the pin and checked in with its per-step counts; a pin move that changes it regenerates it and logs the change.
- When FrankenSim raises its contact capacity upstream (FD-FSIM-13), C changes by itself; `tip22` is then regenerated as `tip<C+1>`.
- The fixed-witness case depends on which refusals the pin can be provoked into; the chosen fixture is recorded in the log so a reviewer can see what was exercised.
- BD-080's step 5 carries the upstream reason in the `upstream` object of N33a-13 (crate, variant verbatim, message verbatim bounded to 2 KiB, pin), with `observed` and `required` numeric or absent (the request recorded here in round 1, applied to BD-080 in round 2); step 8 asserts exactly that object.
- No private data: all fixtures are synthetic.
