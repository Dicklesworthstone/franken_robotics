## Background

The **referee** is the most trusted simulation tier: FrankenSim's articulated dynamics and contact solve, reached
only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`. Two limits of the sibling's contact code decide how often the referee
can answer at all, and both were found by reading, not by running (FACT at head `483d09c682f2`):

- **A contact capacity.** The sustained contact solver admits at most 21 contacts in one solve
  (`frankensim/crates/fs-contact/src/sustained/solve.rs:26`, `pub const MAX_CONTACTS: usize = 21`, also the default
  budget of the frictional multi-contact solve). A single robot is one island, because every foot couples through
  the floating body. A sole with eight samples per foot fits when standing (16) and overflows in a fall or a
  crawl; six feet down with four samples each need 24; a tracked robot with patch samples along two belts
  overflows in normal driving.
- **Fixed material witnesses.** The sustained articulated step
  (`fs-mbd/src/articulated_contacts/step/sustained.rs`, module header lines 1–10 and lines 333–339) states that
  body pairs retain their original material witnesses and a fixed contact frame, and it refuses tangentially
  separating witnesses. A **witness pair** is the pair of points, one on each body in body coordinates, at which
  a contact acts. A wheel rolling over a sharp lip changes both the witnesses and the frame. This is a documented
  scope limit enforced by typed refusals, which is the behaviour this project wants from a referee.

A third fact concerns detection: the sibling's contact step takes the contact set from the caller and states that
"independent endpoint gap checks are not continuous collision detection"; its spacetime broad phase
(`fs-contact/src/broad_phase.rs`, 792 lines) is what finds contacts that both endpoint poses miss.

Three rules bind the airlock adapter (DESIGN) and this bead is their first test. It **converts and never
reinterprets**: a FrankenSim refusal becomes a typed referee refusal carrying the upstream reason, and is never
retried with weakened inputs. It **does not cache across pins**. And it **exposes capacities as data**: the
21-contact limit is a field of `RefereeContact`, checked when a model is compiled and again at run time, so that
an over-capacity contact set is a refusal by name and is never silently truncated. The reason for the last rule:
the contact set of a tracked robot overflows where a point-foot robot's does not, and dropping contacts would bias
exactly the comparison the design studio exists to make.

This bead is the M0 half of the story: it records, through the airlock at the pinned commit, that the sibling
detects the thin step, refuses 22 contacts and refuses the flat-to-edge-to-top transition. The M1 half (the same
transition completing under witness migration with the energy ledger closed) is BD-093. Upstream work items done
in FrankenSim's repository: FD-FSIM-12 (a moving-surface owner for the sustained step) and FD-FSIM-13 (a
sustained solve above 21 contacts, an island-wise solve, or a documented patch model).

## Technical approach

Files: `crates/frob-fsim/src/referee/capacity.rs` and `refusal.rs` (types), and tests in
`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission/int_fsim_04.rs` and `int_fsim_07_refusal.rs`.

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub struct RefereeCapacity {
    pub max_contacts_per_solve: u32,     // read from fs_contact's MAX_CONTACTS at build; 21 at the pin
    pub stack_lock_digest: Blake3,       // a capacity is a fact of one pin
}
pub enum RefereeRefusal {
    Capacity     { cap: u32, requested: u32, island: IslandId },          // -> FROB-REF-721
    FixedWitness { pair: ContactPairId, upstream_reason: String },        // -> FROB-REF-721
    Upstream     { crate_name: &'static str, upstream_reason: String },   // any other typed sibling refusal
}
```

`FROB-REF-721` (name: referee capacity; raised when an island exceeds the referee's contact cap or FrankenSim's
fixed-witness gate refuses; exit code 5, "capability not admitted"). Ranked repairs: (1) a documented patch
reduction with its qualification fixture, which turns the samples of a small patch into one four-dimensional
contact with the patch's torsional capacity; (2) `--profile strict`, the event-located profile on this project's
own kernels, as named adjudicator. The refusal's `observed` and `required` fields carry `requested` and `cap`, or
the upstream reason verbatim.

**Clause 1, the thin step (INT-FSIM-04).**
1. Canonical hand case: a sample sphere of radius 2 mm moves along +x at 5 m/s with step h = 2 ms, so it travels
   10 mm. A plate 2 mm thick lies 1 mm ahead. Clearing it takes 1 + 4 + 2 = 7 mm, so the end pose is 3 mm beyond
   the far face. Endpoint gaps are +1 mm and +3 mm: both clear, and the step encloses a collision.
2. Seeded family, 1,000 sweeps, addressed by `(seed, index)`: radius r ∈ [1, 10] mm; travel L = v·h with v ∈
   [0.5, 10] m/s and h = 2 ms; a thin box of thickness w ∈ [0.5, 5] mm whose near face is at d ∈ (0, L − 2r − w)
   from the sphere surface, so that the far endpoint is clear by construction; incidence up to 60° from the plate
   normal; straight centre motion only (the scope the sibling's swept helper documents). Draws that cannot satisfy
   the constraints are discarded and counted.
3. The airlock asks the sibling's spacetime broad phase for candidate pairs over each sweep. Pass: the
   sphere–plate pair is reported on 1,000 of 1,000.
4. Negative control `endpoint-only`: the same 1,000 sweeps queried only at the two endpoint poses report zero
   contacts; the test asserts that this detector fails the clause (and that it misses the canonical case).

**Clause 2, capacity (INT-FSIM-04).**
1. A rigid slab resting on a plane with n supplied point contacts in one island, for n = 21 and n = 22.
2. n = 21: the airlock admits the step and returns a result. n = 22: it returns
   `RefereeRefusal::Capacity { cap: 21, requested: 22, .. }` and no state.
3. Compile-time check: a model whose static maximum of simultaneously active contact samples is 22 produces the
   capacity finding at model compile, before any step, with the same numbers.
4. The cap is read from the sibling constant, not written as a literal: the test asserts
   `capacity.max_contacts_per_solve == fs_contact::…::MAX_CONTACTS` and records the value.
5. Negative control `truncate-contacts`: a test-only adapter that passes the first 21 contacts and steps. The
   test asserts that a returned state for n = 22 is a failure, so the mutant is caught.

**Clause 3, the fixed-witness refusal (INT-FSIM-07, refusal half).**
1. Fixture `wheel-over-lip`: a wheel of radius 0.10 m and mass 5 kg rolls under constant axle torque from a flat
   onto the sharp 90° edge of a riser 0.03 m high and onto its top. The contact feature sequence is flat, edge,
   top.
2. The sustained step is called through the airlock across the flat-to-edge transition with the witnesses and the
   frame that were valid on the flat (no migration).
3. Pass: the call returns `RefereeRefusal::FixedWitness` with the sibling's reason carried verbatim; the sibling is
   called exactly once (no retry with weakened inputs: the call counter is asserted); no state is published.
4. The same fixture restricted to the flat (no transition) steps without refusal, which shows the refusal is
   caused by the witness change and not by the fixture.
5. Negative control `weaken-and-retry`: a test-only adapter that, on refusal, shortens the interval or relaxes the
   tangential tolerance and retries until the sibling accepts. The call-counter assertion fails, so it is caught.
6. If the sibling does not refuse at the pin (FD-FSIM-12 may have landed), the record says `no-refusal-at-pin`,
   the interim posture is reviewed, and the clause is recorded as `changed`, not as a pass.

**Records.** Two entries in `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` (INT-FSIM-04, and the refusal half of INT-FSIM-07 marked
`part 1 of 2`), each with commit, command, output hash, receipt hash, the detection count, the cap read from the
sibling, the upstream refusal text with file and line, and the sibling facts re-read (caller-supplied contact
set; endpoint checks are not continuous detection). Claim: Known answer; class `Tested`; tier `referee`;
determinism D2 on one platform.

## Test plan

`cargo test -p frob-fsim --test admission`:
- `fsim04_thin_step_canonical`: the hand case; expects the pair reported although both endpoint gaps (+1 mm, +3 mm) are positive.
- `fsim04_thin_step_1000_of_1000`: expects `detected == 1000`; reports seed and index of the first miss.
- `fsim04_endpoint_only_mutant_misses`: expects 0 detections from the mutant and a failed clause.
- `fsim04_21_contacts_step`, `fsim04_22_contacts_refused` (expects `Capacity { cap: 21, requested: 22 }` and no state).
- `fsim04_capacity_read_from_sibling_constant`; `fsim04_compile_time_capacity_finding`.
- `fsim04_truncating_mutant_caught`.
- `fsim07_lip_transition_refused_typed` (expects `FixedWitness`, a non-empty upstream reason, call count 1).
- `fsim07_flat_only_steps`; `fsim07_weaken_and_retry_mutant_caught`.
- `refusal_maps_to_ref_721`: both variants map to code 721 with two repairs in the stated order.
- `capacity_carries_stack_digest`: a capacity built under one stack-lock digest is rejected under another.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/suite_admission_m0.sh` (bead N33b-20), steps `int_fsim_04` and `int_fsim_07_refusal`: runs the
admission lane, then `frob sim run --tier referee --fixture wheel-over-lip --no-migration --json` and asserts exit
code 5 with `refusal.code == "FROB-REF-721"`, the upstream reason present and the two repairs listed. Logged per
STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, the `refusal` event with code and upstream reason, metrics
`thin_step_detected` (threshold 1000 of 1000), `discarded_draws`, `contact_cap` (21 at the pin),
`sibling_calls_on_refusal` (threshold 1), and on failure the seed and index or the unexpected state digest.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-778, first half: the sustained step returns its typed refusal through the airlock at the pin; its stated
negative control is that an over-capacity set refuses. The hand case of the thin step (10 mm of travel, 7 mm to
clear, 3 mm beyond). Counterexamples protected: "the referee can adjudicate every scenario the fast tiers run"
(CX-151) and "when a world exceeds its contact capacity the extra contacts can be dropped" (CX-180). Metamorphic:
translating or rotating a sweep leaves detection unchanged; permuting the order of the 22 contacts leaves the
refusal unchanged. Determinism: one seed gives the same detection list twice on one machine and in debug and
release.

## Considerations

- RISK-31 (High): the referee cannot complete the scenes where decisions are made. Its signal is referee coverage per robot class under 80% at the fidelity coverage gate; mitigations are the transactional step with witness migration, the `strict` profile as named adjudicator, and upstream items FD-FSIM-12 and -13. This bead supplies the two facts that risk rests on, measured at the pin.
- What M1 may claim follows from clause 3: referee rollouts over planes and primitive obstacles are admitted; a wheel climbing a riser is admitted only through the event-located transactional step, which localizes new witness pairs at a consistent world contact point and transports or resets tangential memory.
- Switch-over rules: FD-FSIM-12 landing means the fidelity ledger is re-baselined once; FD-FSIM-13 landing means the capacity fixtures are re-run, and the cap is whatever the sibling constant then says, because the field is read and not written.
- Three responses to an overflow are allowed and removing contacts is not one of them: a documented patch reduction, a larger admitted solve, or a named refusal.
- The sibling's swept helper encloses straight centre motion only; rotating links need this project's own arc-aware bounds, which is why the seeded family uses straight sweeps and makes no claim about arcs.
