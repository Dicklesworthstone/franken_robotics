## Background

The plan's largest architectural bet about its foundations is ADR-SUITE-01: FrankenRobotics consumes FrankenSim
by **path dependency**, confined behind the airlock crate `frob-fsim` and pinned by commit. The owner's brief is
to use FrankenSim for the physics to the greatest extent possible, and the referee tier needs its multibody and
contact engines whole (about 438 thousand lines between `fs-mbd` and `fs-contact` with their tests); copying that
would be a fork. Two sibling products chose otherwise for their own reasons (a second asupersync version, a
different nightly for one crate, failing targets, out-of-repository relative paths), and each of those objections
has a mitigation here that is a gate.

A bet needs a stop rule written before the evidence arrives. This is it: **kill gate KILL-FSIM**, owned by the
suite chapter and decided at M0, then rolling. A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is
never quietly re-scoped; its decision is recorded as an ADR; a kill gate that fires is not a failure of the
program, because the fallback is the plan. This bead is the decision: it assembles the measurements, evaluates
the trigger, records the outcome, and says what changes downstream.

The hazards that make the trigger plausible (FACT on the day of writing): 409 commits touched `fs-mbd` in thirty
days; the composition contract version is 0; FrankenSim's own constellation lock is stale; one crate enables an
incomplete nightly feature; a build script fails closed outside the tree unless a build-identity variable is
set; a path dependency on `fs-mbd` compiles 42 other FrankenSim crates and about 1.57 million lines, and the M1
set 65 crates and about 2.06 million; FrankenSim's own checkout was dirty.

## Scope

In: the evaluation of the three trigger conditions from records; the decision record at the M0 exit and its
re-evaluation at every compatibility train and milestone exit; the statement of both fallbacks, their order and
the numeric condition for the second; the list of downstream changes on each branch.

Out: doing the fallbacks (N33b-06 for the worker posture, N33b-07 for the lift; both depend on this decision);
building and exercising the worker (BD-038); the train and the breakage log (N33b-04); the M0 exit review that
files the ADR (BD-044).

## Technical approach

**1. The trigger (any one fires the gate).**

| Condition | Threshold | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | INT-FSIM-01 cannot pass within two weeks of M0 effort | The admission record: the date effort began and the date of the first pass; ten working days |
| T2 | Its cold check exceeds the machine envelope | The same record: cold `cargo check --all-targets` wall time at most one hour, peak memory at most 16 GiB, at most 8 cores |
| T3 | BET-04's breakage threshold is crossed | The train log: upstream breakage above 20% of working days in any four-week window (more than 4 of 20 days) |

INT-FSIM-01, for reference: from this repository, a crate depending by path on `fs-ga`, `fs-mbd`, `fs-contact`,
`fs-time` and `fs-query` passes `cargo check --all-targets` with the root patch, profile and build-identity
settings, and reproduces the digest of a G1 forward-dynamics state computed inside FrankenSim at the pinned
commit.

**2. The fallbacks, in order.**
- **(a) A worker process.** The volatile crates move behind a process boundary: one pinned FrankenSim worker
  process per device exchanges whole jobs with the core through the same airlock traits. `frob-fsim` has two
  build modes behind those traits: `inproc` (path dependencies) and `remote` (every volatile `fs-*` dependency
  compiled out; the adapters speak a job protocol to the binary `frob-fsim-worker`, built from the small
  workspace `worker/` on FrankenSim's toolchain at the pinned commit). The bedrock-direct crates that still pass
  their own admission stay path dependencies; those that do not are lifted first, since they are small.
- **(b) A lift**, only if (a) fails INT-FSIM-18 or the pinned commit can no longer be built on any available
  toolchain: lift the minimal referee set with provenance headers and golden tests (`fs-ga`, the deterministic
  functions of `fs-math`, `fs-rand`, the `fs-mbd` articulated module, the `fs-contact` multicontact solve),
  record every lifted file in `registries/lifts.toml`, state "lift-only" in the README, and keep the upstream
  work items open.
- A further numeric condition for considering (b) once (a) is in force: if shadowing through the worker falls
  below 0.1% of device world-seconds at the M2 batch size, the worker cannot do the referee's job.

**Why this order.** The lift set is small only on paper: the two modules it names sit on a closure of about 1.5
million lines, and cutting them free of the material crate is engineering that fallback (a) avoids entirely. A
worker keeps FrankenSim's real code as the referee at an old pin for as long as needed. The lift is therefore
priced against the closure and the measured build numbers, not against the size of two crates.

**3. The decision procedure** (`scripts/decide/kill_fsim.sh`, a reader of records; it changes nothing).
1. Read the INT-FSIM-01 rows of `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` and `registries/gates.toml`; compute T1 and T2.
2. Read `registries/trains.toml`; compute the worst four-week breakage share; compute T3.
3. Read the INT-FSIM-18 row (worker fallback): passed, failed or not run; and the toolchain matrix row for the
   M1 closure.
4. Print one of: `hold` (no condition true); `fire: adopt (a)` (a condition true and INT-FSIM-18 passed);
   `fire: adopt (b)` (a condition true and INT-FSIM-18 failed, or the pin builds on no toolchain in play);
   `blocked` (a condition true and INT-FSIM-18 not run: run it first). It prints the numbers beside each
   threshold.
5. The outcome is written as an ADR with the measured values, the receipt hash, the date, and the next
   re-evaluation point. The gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` becomes `pass` (the bet holds; status of the
   kill gate recorded as not fired) or `killed: fallback adopted` naming (a) or (b).

**4. What changes downstream if it fires.**
- *Branch (a).* `frob-fsim`'s default mode becomes `remote`. Every referee call is a job with a fixed overhead
  (target at most 50 ms per job and at most 5% of wall time on jobs of one second or more), so the referee is
  called in rollouts and never inside a loop. The daemon starts, supervises and stops one worker per device or
  process slot. A second build exists (the worker), rebuilt only at pin moves, and the worker may stay on an old
  pin while the core moves. Group gates that need large closures run through the worker. What is preserved: the
  referee is FrankenSim's own code; teleport from a device tier to the referee; shadow sampling; promotion.
  What it does not solve: an API change still costs the adapter.
- *Branch (b).* The lift bead executes; the README states "lift-only"; lifted files are re-owned here with
  golden tests; the upstream work items stay open; the fidelity ledger is re-baselined once against the lifted
  referee.
- *Unchanged on every branch.* The airlock traits, and therefore every bead above the airlock; the admission
  tests (they run against whatever sits behind the traits); the stack lock and receipts.
- *Stop decision for the program.* Re-plan before M1 if INT-FSIM-01 cannot pass within two weeks and the worker
  process also fails INT-FSIM-18, because the only route left is the lift.

**5. Rolling.** The procedure is re-run by every compatibility train (the script prints the trailing share) and
at every milestone exit. A change of trigger, threshold or fallback is made only by a dated amendment to the
gate register, with its reason, before the measurement it governs.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At the M0 exit an ADR records the KILL-FSIM decision with: the two dates of T1, the cold-check wall time, peak memory and cores of T2, the worst four-week breakage share of T3, the INT-FSIM-18 status, the receipt hash, and the outcome (`hold`, `adopt (a)` or `adopt (b)`).
- [ ] The gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` carries one of the two admissible statuses and resolves to that ADR.
- [ ] The procedure returns `blocked` when a condition is true and the worker gate has no record; it never returns `adopt (a)` on an unexercised fallback.
- [ ] If the outcome is `hold`, the next re-evaluation point is named, and the beads of both fallback branches stay unfunded with this decision as their stated reason.
- [ ] If the gate fires, the beads of the chosen branch are opened, and the M1 plan is annotated with the changes of item 4.
- [ ] The decision is taken by the owner or by a fresh-context reviewer who did not implement the admission work; the implementer supplies the numbers.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`scripts/decide/tests/kill_fsim_cases.sh`, on synthetic record sets (labelled `synthetic`):
- `hold_when_all_within`: pass on day 6, cold check 41 minutes at 11 GiB on 8 cores, worst share 10%: `hold`.
- `fires_on_two_weeks`: first pass on working day 11: fires T1.
- `fires_on_envelope`: cold check 73 minutes, or peak 18 GiB: fires T2.
- `fires_on_breakage`: 4.5 blocked days in one four-week window (22.5%): fires T3; 4.0 days (exactly 20%) does not, because the trigger is "above".
- `adopt_a_requires_worker_pass`; `adopt_b_when_worker_failed`; `adopt_b_when_pin_unbuildable`; `blocked_when_worker_not_run`.
- `amendment_must_predate_measurement`: a threshold edited after the record's date is rejected by the reader.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/suite_kill_fsim_decision.sh`: runs the decision reader on the real records, checks that its outcome
equals the status in `registries/gates.toml` and that the ADR exists and cites the same numbers, then runs the
synthetic cases. Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, metrics `days_to_first_pass` (threshold 10),
`cold_check_minutes` (60), `peak_rss_gib` (16), `breakage_share_worst_4w` (0.20), `worker_gate_status`, the
outcome string, the receipt hash, and on a mismatch the field that differs between the reader, the gate file and
the ADR. Summary names KILL-FSIM.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the synthetic cases above, each with its expected outcome. Negative controls: a reader that
ignores T3 (the breakage case then returns `hold` and the test fails); an outcome of `adopt (a)` with no worker
record; a threshold changed after the fact. The control BET-04 itself names: the admission tests for the five
reported defects or limits must fail on the pinned commit if the defects are real and pass after the upstream
fix; that is evidence the admission lane measures something and is cited in the ADR.

## Considerations

- This is the cheapest decisive evidence in the program and is tested first, with the schedule bet. M0 exists largely to produce it.
- OPEN-SUITE-5: the budget for the cold build of the M1 closure on the build workers; default the machine envelope (one hour, 16 GiB, 8 cores); owner input wanted at M0. If the owner approves a larger budget in writing before the measurement, T2's threshold is amended by a dated entry.
- OPEN-SUITE-3: a dirty asupersync checkout is acceptable for development builds between receipts; no gate records a PASS on a dirty tree, so T1's "pass" needs clean trees.
- If only the units crate blocks the build, the remedy is not this kill gate: quantities reach every crate through one re-export module and the escape hatch for units is decided by the toolchain gate.
- Hazard 1 is why the airlock stays whatever is decided, and why the worker fallback was exercised at M0 although inactive: a fallback that has never run is a hope (counterexample CX-356).
