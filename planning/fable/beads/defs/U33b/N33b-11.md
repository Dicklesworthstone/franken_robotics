## Background

A periodic motion (a gait, a wear cycle) is stable when small perturbations shrink from one period to the next.
The linear map that carries a perturbation across one period is the **monodromy matrix** M, and its eigenvalues
are the **Floquet multipliers**: the motion is locally stable when every multiplier lies inside the unit circle,
apart from those that a symmetry or a free phase pins at 1. The gait subsystem (`frob-gait`, M4) builds M itself
and needs a trustworthy spectral analysis of it; lifecycle compression (`frob-lifecycle`, a Moonshot) needs the
same later.

FrankenSim has one (FACT, head `483d09c682f2`): crate `fs-spectral`, 14,329 library lines (24,566 with tests),
whose non-optional closure is 13 other FrankenSim crates and which depends on `fs-qty` directly. Its manifest
describes spectral problem admission and health monitoring; `floquet::analyze_monodromy`
(`fs-spectral/src/floquet.rs`) analyses a **supplied** monodromy matrix and also reports non-normal transient
growth. It is reached through the airlock's `spectral` feature group, off by default. Enabling the group adds
nothing to compile time, because `fs-spectral` is already inside the M1 closure; the group still exists because
it bounds what a crate above the airlock may name.

The group gate is INT-FSIM-13 (first needed at M3; thresholds are TARGETs):

| Known answer through the airlock | Negative control |
|---|---|
| The Floquet multipliers of the monodromy of ẍ + x = 0 over one period are both 1, to 10⁻¹² | A monodromy with a multiplier of modulus 1.01 is reported unstable |

Rejected alternative: computing eigenvalues of M with a general dense routine written here. The sibling's
analysis is the referee's, it reports transient growth that a bare eigenvalue list hides, and a second eigenvalue
code path would need its own admission. What stays here regardless is the rule that the monodromy is computed
over the full reduced state by `frob-gait`; the sibling only analyses what it is given.

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-fsim/src/spectral/floquet.rs` (feature `spectral`).

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
pub struct FloquetReport {
    pub multipliers: Vec<Complex64>,          // eigenvalues of the supplied monodromy, in a canonical order
    pub spectral_radius: f64,
    pub transient_growth: f64,                // the largest amplification over one or more periods, as the sibling reports it
    pub verdict: FloquetVerdict,              // Stable | Marginal { on_unit_circle: usize } | Unstable { outside: usize }
    pub unit_tolerance: f64,                  // the tolerance used to call a multiplier "on the circle"
    pub stack_lock_digest: Blake3,
}
pub trait Spectral {
    fn analyze_monodromy(&self, m: &DMatrixView<f64>, opts: &FloquetOptions) -> Result<FloquetReport, RefereeRefusal>;
}
```

Rules: the canonical order of `multipliers` is by decreasing modulus, then by argument in (−π, π], so that two
runs print the same list; a non-square or non-finite matrix is refused before the sibling is called; a sibling
refusal (for example a problem its admission rejects) is converted with its reason and never retried on a
perturbed matrix; the report names the tolerance, because "equal to 1" is a statement at a tolerance; nothing is
cached across pins.

**INT-FSIM-13, the test** (`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission/int_fsim_13.rs`, lane `--features spectral`).
1. *The harmonic oscillator.* For ẍ + x = 0 with state (x, ẋ) the flow over time t is
   Φ(t) = [[cos t, sin t], [−sin t, cos t]]. Over one period T = 2π the monodromy is the identity, so both
   multipliers equal 1. The test supplies Φ(2π) built two ways: the exact identity, and the product of 1,000
   rotations by 2π/1000 (which carries rounding of order 10⁻¹³). Pass: both multipliers within 10⁻¹² of 1 and
   verdict `Marginal { on_unit_circle: 2 }`.
2. *Half a period.* Φ(π) = −I: both multipliers −1 to 10⁻¹² (the signature a period-doubling test looks for).
3. *A damped oscillator.* ẍ + 2·ζ·ẋ + x = 0 with ζ = 0.1 over T = 2π: both multipliers have modulus
   e^(−ζ·T) = e^(−0.628319) = 0.533488, to 10⁻⁹; verdict `Stable`.
4. *Negative control from the gate.* The monodromy diag(1.01, 1/1.01) has a multiplier of modulus 1.01; the
   verdict must be `Unstable { outside: 1 }`. The same matrix conjugated by a seeded rotation gives the same
   verdict (the instability does not depend on the basis).
5. *Transient growth is reported.* M = [[0.9, 100], [0, 0.9]] has both multipliers 0.9 and is `Stable`, yet it
   amplifies the vector (0, 1) about a hundredfold in one period; `transient_growth` must exceed 50. A report
   that printed only the spectral radius would hide it.
6. *Planted adapters.* `planted::determinant-only` calls a matrix stable whenever the modulus of its determinant
   is at most 1; the matrix of case 4 has determinant exactly 1, so it reports `Stable` and must fail case 4.
   `planted::drop-unit-multipliers` removes multipliers near 1 before judging and must fail case 1 (it would
   report `Stable` with an empty list).
7. *Recorded.* The entry points used; what the sibling's admission refuses; whether its tolerance for the unit
   circle is configurable.

**Record.** `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` entry for INT-FSIM-13 at the M3 pin: commit, command, output hash, receipt
hash, the measured multiplier errors, the verdicts, the transient-growth value of case 5, and the statement that
enabling the group added no crate to the build. Claim: Known answer; class `Tested`; tier host; determinism D2 on
one platform.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-768: both multipliers equal 1 to 10⁻¹²; negative control: a multiplier of modulus 1.01 is reported unstable.
Metamorphic: a similarity transform of M leaves the multipliers unchanged; replacing M by M² squares them;
transposing M leaves them unchanged. Determinism: identical reports twice on one machine and in debug and
release.

## Considerations

- The gate register dates this gate at M3 while its first production consumer (`frob-gait`) lands at M4. The gate is run when the register says; the consumer re-runs it at its own pin, because an admission is never inherited.
- A multiplier exactly at 1 is expected for an autonomous orbit (the free phase) and for removed symmetry coordinates; deciding which unit multipliers are trivial belongs to the gait analysis, which removes and lists them before judging. The adapter reports all of them.
- `fs-spectral` depends on `fs-qty` directly; a toolchain train that breaks `fs-qty` breaks this group. The fallback for the gait analysis is a small symmetric-free eigenvalue routine against the same known answers, labelled in the result.
- For matrices of the sizes in scope (40 to about 130 reduced states) the analysis costs milliseconds; no performance claim is attached.
