## Background

A robot that cannot see where its next foot will land, or the threshold it is about to cross, fails for a reason
no controller can repair. Analysis **A-14, sensor coverage** asks, before any simulation: *does the sensor suite
see the footholds and the route ahead?* Its mathematics is ray geometry. It is a Solid analysis, runs in stages 7
and 8 of the design preflight when a scene is bound, and is the advisory fallback of the alias stage if that
stage's kill gate fires.

The seed work item listed A-14 beside two other analyses; it is split out here because it shares code with the
sensor subsystem and none with curvature or sensitivity.

The question, the method and the place in the preflight are fixed by the analysis catalogue. This bead fixes the
definitions (DESIGN, each with its reason):

- **Targets** are the things the body will touch or cross: the footholds of the nominal gait for the next steps
  (or the wheel paths), the route corridor ahead out to the lookahead distance, and, when a scene is bound, its
  thresholds, steps and hazard regions. Coverage of empty floor far from the route is not a target, because it
  changes no decision.
- **Lookahead distance** is the distance travelled during the sense-decide-actuate latency plus the stopping
  distance at the task's nominal speed: d = v·t_latency + v²/(2·a_brake). A sensor that sees the floor only inside
  that distance cannot inform a stop.
- **Own-body occlusion counts.** Occlusion by the robot's own links is easy to leave out of a coverage
  analysis that tests rays against the scene only; it is included here at the reference pose, because a mount
  that looks into the robot's own leg sees nothing however wide its field of view. Occlusion that changes along a motion (a swinging
  leg, a carried tote) is the dynamic extension, verb `sense.visibility`.

Rejected: a coverage score computed from field-of-view cones alone, with no range, incidence or occlusion test
(it rewards a camera pointed at the robot's own chest); and a single scalar "percent covered" with no list of
what is missed (an agent cannot repair a number).

## Scope

In: target generation from a gait or drive reference and a bound scene; per-sensor ray tests (field of view,
range, incidence, scene occlusion, own-link occlusion at the reference pose); the result with reasons and ranked
repairs; the verb `analyze.coverage`; the preflight entry points; known answers.

Out: ray kernels on batched tiers and sensor noise (BD-187); sensor cards and their failure modes (BD-238);
visibility along a motion and mounts as optimizer variables (the Sensorium beads for §20.24); the observability
Gramian and alias witnesses (A-17; BD-120, BD-240); optical failure modes such as mirrors and glass (BD-237),
which this static analysis reports only as "target lies behind a non-ranging surface" when the scene declares one.

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-analyze/src/coverage/{targets,cast,report}.rs`. The analysis is a memo node with field mask:
sensor mounts and cards, collision primitives and their poses at the reference configuration, the gait or drive
reference, and (when bound) the scene identity.

1. **Reference pose and motion.** The settled stance of the design, and the nominal gait from the baseline
   controller's footstep pattern (legged) or the wheel paths under the nominal body twist (wheeled), at the
   task's nominal speed v.
2. **Targets.** (a) The next k footholds per leg (DESIGN default k = 2) as discs of the foot's sample radius, or
   wheel-path strips out to d; (b) the route corridor: the swept footprint width out to the lookahead distance d,
   sampled on a grid of side 5 cm; (c) with a scene: each threshold, step edge and hazard region that intersects
   the corridor.
3. **Per-sensor test.** For each ranging or imaging sensor, from its mount at the reference pose, a target point
   P is **covered** when all hold: P is inside the sensor's angular field (pattern taken from the card: frustum
   half-angles, or scan elevations and azimuth span); its range is inside [r_min, r_max]; the incidence angle to
   the surface normal is below the card's limit; the segment from the sensor to P meets no scene surface first;
   and it meets none of the robot's own collision primitives first. A target is covered when at least the card's
   minimum number of valid returns land on it.
4. **Closed form used as the oracle on flat ground.** A sensor at height H, pitched down by θ from horizontal,
   with vertical half-field φ, sees the floor from x_near = H/tan(θ + φ) to x_far = H/tan(θ − φ) ahead of its
   mount (x_far is unbounded when θ ≤ φ; x_near is negative, behind the mount, when θ + φ > 90°), further cut by
   range: x ≤ √(r_max² − H²).
5. **Result.** Per target class: covered count and total; per uncovered target: the reason (`outside-field`,
   `too-near`, `too-far`, `grazing-incidence`, `occluded-by-scene`, `occluded-by-link:<name>`) and the sensor
   that came closest. `covers_lookahead: bool` with the nearest and farthest covered corridor distance against d.
   Ranked repairs as typed edits: tilt or raise the named mount by the smallest change that covers the missed
   targets (found by a one-dimensional search over pitch, then height, within the mount's declared lever
   bounds); or reduce the nominal speed to the value at which d falls inside the covered span.
6. **Preflight.** Stage 7 reports coverage of footholds and route for the bound scene; an uncovered foothold on
   the only admissible route contributes to the stage's "no admissible route" finding (`FROB-REF-119`) with the
   repair attached. Stage 8 lists the result beside the alias search. A stage that exhausts its budget returns
   `undecided` with what it had established; `undecided` is never a pass and never a refusal.
7. **Verb.** `analyze.coverage` (kind `read`): input a design, optional scene and task; output the result
   above. Evidence class `Tested` for the geometry at the reference pose, determinism D0; the result states that
   it is a static analysis at one pose.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Flat-floor closed form: for H = 0.5 m, θ = 45°, φ = 30° the covered span is 0.134 m to 1.866 m; a foothold 0.10 m ahead is `too-near` and one 0.20 m ahead is covered; with r_max = 1.5 m the far limit becomes √(1.5² − 0.5²) = 1.414 m.
- [ ] Lookahead: at v = 1 m/s, latency 0.2 s and braking 2 m/s², d = 0.2 + 0.25 = 0.45 m; `covers_lookahead` is true for the camera above and false when the camera is pitched to θ = 80° (the span then runs from 0.182 m behind the mount to 0.420 m ahead, short of 0.45 m).
- [ ] Own-body occlusion: a camera mounted behind a leg link reports the hidden footholds as `occluded-by-link:<name>`; moving the mount by the offered repair covers them.
- [ ] Scene occlusion: a target behind a wall is `occluded-by-scene`; removing the wall covers it.
- [ ] Every uncovered target has a reason and every finding has at least one ranked repair as a typed edit that, applied, makes the analysis pass.
- [ ] The analysis is a memo node: an edit to an actuator card recomputes nothing; an edit to a sensor mount recomputes it.
- [ ] `analyze.coverage` answers through `frob --json` with schema and example; stages 7 and 8 report its timing; the known answers are recorded for GATE-ATEL-02.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-analyze coverage`:
- `flat_floor_span_closed_form`: expects (0.134, 1.866) m to 10⁻³; `range_cut_far_limit` (1.414 m).
- `foothold_too_near_and_covered` (0.10 m and 0.20 m).
- `lookahead_distance_formula` (0.45 m); `steep_pitch_misses_lookahead` (span −0.182 to 0.420 m, so 0.45 m is uncovered).
- `own_link_occludes`; `repair_move_sensor_fixes_occlusion`.
- `scene_wall_occludes`; `incidence_limit_rejects_grazing` (a target at 85° incidence with a card limit of 80°).
- `min_returns_rule`: a target hit by fewer rays than the card's minimum is uncovered.
- `reasons_are_exhaustive`: property test: every uncovered target carries exactly one primary reason.
- `mask_excludes_actuator_cards` (memo live-dependency test).
- Planted defects, each caught: `cone-only` (no range or occlusion test: reports the behind-the-leg camera as covering), `ignore-own-links`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/analyze_coverage.sh`: on archetype `quad-mammal` with one forward depth camera, run
`frob analyze coverage <design> --json`; assert the covered span against the closed form computed in the script
from the mount's height and pitch; apply the first ranked repair with `frob design edit` and assert the finding
clears; bind a doorway scene and run `frob design check --stages 7,8 --json`, asserting A-14 is listed with its
stage timing. Logged per STD-E2E: command, exit code, wall time, evidence block, metrics `x_near_m`, `x_far_m`
(each with its closed-form expectation and a tolerance of 1 cm for grid sampling), `footholds_covered`,
`lookahead_m`, `covers_lookahead`, `repairs_offered`, and on failure the first target whose status differs from
the oracle.

### Known answers and negative controls
The flat-floor span and the lookahead case above. The occluded-payload hand case of the dynamic extension is
quoted as a consistency check on the shared ray code: a camera at the body's front edge at height H, a box whose
top front corner is 0.4 m ahead and 0.3 m above the floor; floor nearer than x = 0.4·H/(H − 0.3) is hidden: 1.0 m
at H = 0.5 m and 0.6 m at H = 0.9 m. Negative controls: the two planted defects. Metamorphic: yawing the whole
robot leaves coverage unchanged; scaling the robot, the mount heights and the ranges by one factor scales the
spans by that factor; widening the field never uncovers a target. Determinism: identical results twice on one
machine and across platforms inside the deterministic-function budget.

## Considerations

- The price of a repair is in another analysis: raising a 1 kg mast head by 0.4 m on a 20 kg robot lifts the centre of mass by 0.02 m, and with a half-track of 0.2 m the static tip-over angle falls from atan(0.2/0.30) = 33.7° to atan(0.2/0.32) = 32.0°. The repair list therefore names static stability (A-08) as an analysis to re-run.
- KILL-SKEL-ALIAS: if the alias stage returns witnesses a hand-built policy defeats, or decides fewer than half of the planted cases within budget, stage 8 becomes an advisory report built on this analysis and refuses nothing. This bead's result format is what that report prints.
- Coverage is necessary and not sufficient: it says a target is geometrically visible, not that the estimator can use the measurement. Observability and aliasing are A-17.
- Any move of a mount changes the observation-preserving symmetry subgroup; the repair list flags that the symmetry analysis must be re-run.
- Stages 7 and 8 depend on scene size and carry no total budget; the corridor grid is bounded by the lookahead distance, so the cost does not grow with the scene.
