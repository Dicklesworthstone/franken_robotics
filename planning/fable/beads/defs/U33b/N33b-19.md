## Background

Agents drive the studio through verbs: named operations of the `frob` command line and the MCP server, each with
a schema, a JSON envelope and a fixed exit code. The study engines of bead BD-124 need a surface with one
property above all: **the agent sees the cost and the reachable verdicts before any budget is spent, and the
object that runs cannot drift from the object that was planned.** That is why a study is two-phase (DESIGN):

- `study.plan` binds a trial manifest to immutable designs, scene, task, seed protocol, budget, backend and one
  declared study type (anytime-valid or fixed-n exact, never both). It returns a plan identifier that is the
  content address of the resolved manifest, the cost estimate with its interval, and, for a fixed-n study, the
  pre-analysis of which verdicts are reachable at that n. Planning runs no physics and is idempotent by
  construction.
- `study.submit` takes the plan identifier and an idempotency key and returns a job.
- `study.status` reports the progress of a submitted study.
- `eval.preanalyze` returns the reachable verdicts and the power of a fixed-n rule at a given n and level;
  `study.plan` calls it.
- `eval.compare` remains as the one-call form: it plans and submits an anytime-valid paired study with defaults.

Rejected: a single `compare` call that both decides the sample size and runs (the agent learns the cost after
paying it, and a request that names an unreachable verdict is discovered only at the end); letting `submit` take
a manifest (the submitted object could differ from the reviewed plan).

Registry kinds and milestones as registered: `study.plan` is `mutate`, `study.submit` and `eval.compare` are
`run`, `study.status` and `eval.preanalyze` are `read`. The registry dates `eval.preanalyze` at M1, the `study.*`
verbs at M2 and `eval.compare` at M3; the seed work item delivers all five on the CPU at M1. The rows keep the
appendix's milestones and the registry tier `S`; what lands early is the handler, listed by `frob capabilities
--json` as implemented with its form named. This bead enters the rows of its three study verbs (`study.plan`,
`study.submit`, `study.status`) at M1, in the form VERBS-study gives them (that bead, at M2, completes them and
enters `study.compare` and `study.cancel`), because they are needed at M1 and nothing that lands before this bead
enters them; `study.submit`'s row carries `memo = true`, the field of BD-111 (a dependency through BD-123). Their
handlers run with backend `referee` only (form `cpu`), the device backends following at their milestones. The
row of `eval.compare` is VERBS-eval's (a dependency), entered with the cluster at M1; this bead implements its
CPU form (`form: "cpu-referee"`) and enters no second row. The fifth verb, `eval.preanalyze`, is registered by
N24b-06, which defines it.

## Technical approach

Executors live in `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/{study,eval}.rs`; schemas are generated from
`registries/verbs.toml` and never edited by hand.

**`eval.preanalyze` (read): defined in N24b-06, used here.** The verb's input schema
`frob.eval.preanalyze.in/1`, its output record `frob.preanalysis/1` (`outcomes`, `counts`, `reachable`,
`first_reachable`, `min_b_for_prefer`, `concordance_interval`, `power`, `hash`), its verdict tokens (`promote`,
`inferior`, `practical-tie`, `inconclusive`) and its refusal `FROB-REF-532` with its three ranked repairs are
N24b-06's. What is here is the use. Step 3 of `study.plan` calls the enumerator with the fixed-n block's `rule`,
`n`, `level`, `margin` and `declared_verdicts`, and embeds the returned record in the plan unchanged, under
`preanalysis`. DESIGN: the plan's sample study plan spells that block with other field names and says its
spellings are proposals; embedding the verb's record unchanged keeps one schema and makes `preanalysis.hash` in
a plan equal to the `hash` that `eval.preanalyze` prints for the same arguments, which is the hash a study and a
qualification request cite. On the command line the fixed-n block is given as `--fixed-n <n> --rule … --level …
--margin … --declared-verdicts <tokens>`; `--declared-verdicts` is the same flag as on `eval.preanalyze`.

**`study.plan` (mutate).** Input: `designs` (identifiers resolved to immutable `DesignId`s), `scene`, `task`,
`controller_arm` (`shared` | `retuned-equal-resources` with the resources per arm | `inherits-weights` with its
warm start and transfer baseline), `seed_protocol` (pairing by (world, replicate); `unit: world | cluster`, with
the cluster map `all | mean { span }` for clusters), `budget`, `backend` (at M1: `referee`), and exactly one of
`anytime { null, margin, span, betting_rule, level, planning_effect? }` or `fixed_n { n, rule, level, margin,
declared_verdicts }`. `planning_effect` (optional, DESIGN) is a standardized paired effect δ > 0 (mean paired
difference over its standard deviation) that the caller assumes for sizing only; it is never estimated from
data, never enters the e-process and is not part of the stopping rule. The idempotency key is optional for this
verb (`key_optional = true`, lint RL-21 of N32a-02, allowed because the output declares
`x-frob-identity: "request-digest"`): a keyless plan is admitted and is not `FROB-REF-805`; with a key it
follows the protocol of N32a-09. The input is parsed into BD-124's `StudyPlan` (Appendix I.9).

*The manifest is a registered format (DESIGN).* On the command line the input is a manifest file (the registry
example is `frob study plan manifest.json --json`), with the fixed-n flags above as an alternative spelling of
its `fixed_n` block. Its schema is `frob.study.manifest/1`, owned by `frob-arena` (the reader is the parser into
`StudyPlan`), canonical JSON with no view. This bead enters its row in `registries/formats.toml` through the
format registry of N34-19 (identifier `FMT-FSTUDY`, extension `.fstudy`; a `.json` file whose top-level `schema`
member names the schema is routed as the same format, N34-24's rule), so that the verb's row can declare
`params = { manifest = "artifact-input<FMT-FSTUDY>" }`. The generator then appends 891, 734 and 828 (NJL-01's
variations 7 and 9: 891 for an unsupported schema version, 734 and 828 for the path jail and the content sniff),
and they are not in the row's chapter list. A file of another registered format given as the manifest is
`FROB-REF-802` with `observed` naming both formats (N34-24's routing); 893 belongs to a `foreign-file`
parameter only, and this row has none.

*Per-arm options.* The manifest may carry `arms`, a list parallel to `designs` with one table of options per
arm. Its member at M1 is `capacity = { contacts = <C>, rows = <R> }` (`rows` optional): the manifest form of
the step-plan capacity override that `sim.run` spells `--capacity contacts=<C>[,rows=<R>]` (N16a-14, M2). It
enters that arm's `StepPlanId` and is printed per arm in the plan. On an arm whose backend is the referee it is
refused with `FROB-REF-802` (pointer `/arms/<i>/capacity`), as `sim.run --tier referee --capacity` is: the
referee's contact cap is the sibling's constant, read and never set. At M1 the only backend is the referee, so
at M1 the field is parsed, validated for shape (positive integers) and always refused; the batched tiers that
accept it arrive at M2, where N16a-14's capacity registry validates the values and N18b-22's fixture
`fixture:capacity-bias` runs both arms at a cap of 8 through it. N16c-38 adds the member `solve_path` at M2.
An unknown member of an arm's table is `FROB-REF-802`.

*Not accepted: per-arm parameter settings (DESIGN, closing the question N33b-18 left with this bead).* A
manifest names immutable `DesignId`s and one task, and has no per-arm lever, card-field or task-parameter
settings. Reasons: resolving design settings inside `study.plan` would make planning write design versions,
and planning is pure (the same request, the same `plan_id`, nothing new written); and arms that carried
different task parameters would meet different tasks, while a pair compares designs on one task instance (the
pairing rules of N28a-18). A screening over design fields makes one design version per point with
`design.patch` (the commands N33b-18's plan call returns) and lists the `DesignId`s; a sensitivity to a task
parameter is one study per task value, or the static mode. N33b-18's refusal of a task parameter in a Morris
set (`FROB-REF-802`) therefore stands.

Steps:
1. resolve every mutable name to an immutable identifier and echo them in the envelope's `bound` block;
2. for fixed-n, call the pre-analysis and embed its record (below), so that admission sees it;
3. admit the plan through `study::admit` of N24b-14, the one Arena entry point, and validate nothing here a
   second time. Its checks run in its order: one type (`FROB-REF-552`); declarations present, with a missing
   pre-analysis or an unreachable declared verdict refused `FROB-REF-532` (reachable set and first reachable n
   attached); the determinism class of the backend (`FROB-REF-533`); the pairing rules of N28a-18
   (`FROB-REF-543` for an anytime metric without a declared span, `FROB-REF-556` for a pairing keyed by event
   order or a training counter); then the unit of inference, the race level α/(N − 1) and the controller arm
   (`FROB-REF-802` with the pointers `/seed_protocol/unit`, the pairwise study's `level`, `/controller_arm`).
   A defect that has two codes gets the Arena code on this path (the rule of N24-REF). The verb converts the
   `Refusal` into the envelope unchanged, with its ranked repairs;
4. compute the cost estimate with its interval; for an anytime plan that declares `planning_effect` δ, the
   estimate also carries `expected_pairs` = ⌈2·ln(1/α)/δ²⌉ (card GB-06: a tuned betting e-process grows at
   about δ²/2 nats per pair, so it crosses 1/α after about that many pairs; at α = 0.05: 6, 24 and 150 for
   δ = 1, 0.5 and 0.2), labelled `approximation: gaussian-growth` with the caveats GB-06 states (heavy-tailed
   differences stop later, and when the declared span caps the bet, for δ above about 0.27 at a span of 4σ, the
   mean stopping time exceeds the figure), and the episode estimate uses two episodes per expected pair. Without
   `planning_effect` the field is absent, never a default;
5. write the resolved manifest to the plan store and return `plan_id` = BLAKE3 of its canonical bytes, the
   estimate, and the pre-analysis (fixed-n) or the stopping thresholds (anytime).
The same request always yields the same `plan_id`; planning twice writes nothing new.

**`study.submit` (run).** Input: `plan_id`, `idempotency_key` (required: a call without one is `FROB-REF-805`,
exit 3). It loads the manifest by content address; an identifier the plan store does not hold is refused with
`FROB-REF-809` (plan not found, exit 7; N32-REF, due at M1 for this case; the superseded-identity case is
N32a-13's at M3), with the repairs "run `study.plan` again and submit the new identifier" and "`study.status`
lists live plans". Otherwise it creates one job, and returns within the bounded synchronous wait or exits 8
with a job handle (`id`, `state`, `progress {done, total, unit}`, `eta_s`, `poll_after_ms`, `last_committed`).
A retry with the same key and plan returns the stored receipt with `idempotent_replay: true`; the same key with
another plan is refused as a conflict (exit 7). Episodes run on the referee through the chunk protocol; the
engine consumes outcomes in address order.

**`study.status` (read).** Input: a study or job identifier. Output: `state`, pair counts so far with the
accounting identity (a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned), for anytime the current e-value and
confidence sequence, for fixed-n **progress only** (counts and whether exact curtailment has fired; no interim
verdict), and the stopping reason once terminal.

**`eval.compare` (run).** Input: two designs, a task, optional scene, budget and metric list, and optionally one
study-type block, `anytime { … }` or `fixed_n { … }` with the fields of `study.plan` (a comparison declares its
type before it draws an episode, ADR-ATEL-02; on the command line the same `--fixed-n`, `--margin`, `--level`,
`--declared-verdicts` flags). Without a block it builds an anytime plan with defaults (level 0.05, the task's
declared margin and span per metric, the referee backend at M1, a shared controller arm unless declared). A
request with both blocks is the 553 case at M1 (re-reading a stored fixed-n study as sequential is
`study.compare`'s 553 case, N28a-21); a `fixed_n` block whose declared verdicts include an unreachable one is
the 555 case. It runs N28a-18's `pairing::admit` on it first, so that a defect with
two codes gets the comparison code on this path (553, 555, 542 where `study.plan` answers 552, 532, 533), then
calls the planning and submission paths, and returns the comparison result or a job handle. Its refusals at
comparison time are `FROB-REF-553`, `-555`, `-542`, `-543`, `-556`. The
result carries: the verdict (`settled`, `tied`, `inconclusive`, `incomparable` or `infeasible`); the e-value and
confidence sequence; pairs used with the accounting identity; the tier; the first divergent event between the
arms when one exists; each design's absolute hard-limit verdict; `rank_robustness: unchecked`; and the label
`provisional` where the tier calls for promotion.

**Refusal lists (chapter codes; the generator appends the surface-wide ones).** Under the layer rule of N24-REF
and N28-REF (one situation, one code, one verb), the three study rows this bead enters list: `study.plan` 532,
533, 543, 552, 556, and the numbers the allocation ledger assigned to the N16-REF slugs `solve-path-mixed-study`
and `refinement-study-changes-clock`, read from `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` by slug and never typed
(VERBS-study's rule; their raising beads, N16c-38 and N16c-43, come at M2); `study.submit` 809; `study.status`
none. The codes that VERBS-study adds to these rows later enter with the beads that raise them (726 by N19b-03,
the study-admission slugs by N24c-37 and N24c-40). `eval.compare`'s row is VERBS-eval's, whose chapter list is
464, 502, 542, 543, 551, 553, 555, 556, 561, 843 and 890; of these this bead raises 542, 543, 553, 555 and 556 at
M1, and the others are raised by the beads VERBS-eval names. No row lists both codes of a pair (552/553,
532/555, 533/542).

**Envelope and exit codes (shared rules).** Exactly one JSON envelope per invocation. `evidence` is the claim
product of the inputs: claim Design comparison, class `Tested`, tier `referee`, determinism D2, identities and
seed. Exit 0 completed; 2 completed with findings; 3 a refused input or question (all the `5xx` codes above, and
805); 7 a conflict (a reused key with another plan, and 809); 8 accepted and still running. A refusal carries `code`, `message`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`,
`retry` and ranked `repairs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob study plan … --fixed-n 64 --margin 0.05 --json` returns a plan whose `preanalysis` has 2,145 outcomes split 521 / 521 / 0 / 1,103, `reachable` without `practical-tie`, and `first_reachable` 86 for it; with `--fixed-n 86` it has 3,828 split 1,031 / 1,031 / 1 / 1,765 (GATE-ATEL-06: the fixed-n planner reproduces the enumeration at n = 64 and n = 86 exactly).
- [ ] In both plans `preanalysis.hash` equals the `hash` printed by `frob eval preanalyze` for the same arguments, and the embedded record validates against `frob.preanalysis/1` of N24b-06. (The verb's own tables and GATE-ARENA-05 are asserted in N24b-06 and by the script of N24b-21, not here.)
- [ ] `study.plan` with both type blocks exits 3 with `FROB-REF-552`; with a fixed-n block that declares a tie at n = 64 it exits 3 with `FROB-REF-532` and the repair names n = 86.
- [ ] `study.plan` runs no physics (no episode is created; asserted from the ledger) and returns the same `plan_id` for the same request.
- [ ] `study.submit` under one idempotency key creates one job however often it is retried; the retry returns `idempotent_replay: true`; the key reused with another plan exits 7; an unknown `plan_id` exits 7 with `FROB-REF-809` and its two repairs; a submit without a key exits 3 with `FROB-REF-805`, while `study.plan` without a key succeeds and returns the same `plan_id` as with one.
- [ ] An anytime plan with `planning_effect` 1, 0.5 and 0.2 at α = 0.05 reports `expected_pairs` 6, 24 and 150 with the label `approximation: gaussian-growth` (GB-06); without `planning_effect` the field is absent.
- [ ] The three study rows carry the refusal lists stated above; `eval.compare`'s row contains 542, 543, 553, 555 and 556 and none of 552, 532, 533; no row lists both codes of one pair.
- [ ] The study manifest is a registered format: `registries/formats.toml` has the row for `frob.study.manifest/1`, the `study.plan` row declares `params = { manifest = "artifact-input<FMT-FSTUDY>" }`, the generator then appends 891, 734 and 828 from that entry, none of the three is in its chapter list, and no 893 is appended (the row has no `foreign-file` parameter); `frob study plan manifest.json --json` runs as printed; a file of another registered format given as the manifest is `FROB-REF-802`, exit 3, with `observed` naming both formats.
- [ ] A manifest may state `arms[i].capacity = { contacts, rows }`; it enters that arm's `StepPlanId` on a backend that accepts it, and on a referee arm (every arm at M1) it is refused with `FROB-REF-802` and the pointer `/arms/<i>/capacity`; an unknown member of an arm's table is 802; a manifest with per-arm lever, card-field or task-parameter settings is 802 (not accepted, by the decision above).
- [ ] `study.status` of a fixed-n study never contains a verdict before n complete pairs or exact curtailment.
- [ ] `eval.compare` on the two cart designs returns a verdict with the accounting identity, the tier and both absolute hard-limit verdicts, or an `inconclusive` with its stopping reason.
- [ ] Each of `FROB-REF-553`, `-555`, `-542`, `-543`, `-556` is returned for its planted request from the command line.
- [ ] The four verbs of this bead are in `registries/verbs.toml` with generated schemas, at least one example each and their refusal lists (the three study rows entered by this bead with `first_milestone = "M2"` and tier `S`; `eval.compare`'s row VERBS-eval's; `eval.preanalyze`'s N24b-06's); `frob capabilities --json` lists the three study verbs implemented with `form: "cpu"` and `eval.compare` with `form: "cpu-referee"`; `study.submit`'s row has `memo = true` and its generated schema `no_memo`; `frob schema <verb>` prints them; cancellation of a submitted study leaves committed chunks only.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/study_two_types.sh` on fixture `fixture:cart-sill-dock` (two cart designs A and B), seed-explicit and
idempotent (a scratch store per run):
1. `frob study plan … --fixed-n 64 --margin 0.05 --declared-verdicts promote,inferior,inconclusive --json`;
   assert a `plan_id`, a cost estimate, zero episodes in the ledger, and the embedded pre-analysis: 2,145
   outcomes split 521 / 521 / 0 / 1,103, no `practical-tie` in `reachable`. The same with `--fixed-n 86`: 3,828
   split 1,031 / 1,031 / 1 / 1,765.
2. `frob eval preanalyze --rule paired-clopper-pearson/1 --n 64 --margin 0.05 --level 0.05 --json` (the
   registry's form: `--rule` is required) and the same at 86; assert only that each `hash` equals
   the `preanalysis.hash` of the corresponding plan of step 1 (the tables of the verb are asserted cell by cell
   in `arena_study_preanalysis.sh` of N24b-21).
3. step 1's first request with `--declared-verdicts promote,inferior,inconclusive,practical-tie`; assert exit 3,
   `FROB-REF-532`, repair n = 86.
4. `frob study submit <plan_id> --key k1 --json` (the CLI spelling of the idempotency key is `--key`); poll
   `frob study status` until terminal; assert that
   no status before the end carries a verdict; assert the final counts satisfy the identity and the pre-analysis
   hash matches step 1.
5. repeat step 4's submit with `k1`; assert `idempotent_replay: true` and one job in total; then
   `frob study submit plan:b3:0000 --key k2 --json` (an identifier no plan has); assert exit 7 and
   `FROB-REF-809`; then step 1's plan without a key; assert exit 0 and the same `plan_id`.
5a. `frob study plan` on three anytime manifests at α = 0.05 with `planning_effect` 1, 0.5 and 0.2; assert

## Considerations

- The registry's milestones for these verbs (M2 and M3) describe when they exist on devices and through the full surface; nothing here blocks that. The coverage file records the difference with the seed's M1.
- A plan identifier names bytes and carries no verdict. An edited design is a different `DesignId`, so an old plan can never run against a new design by accident.
- Estimates at M1 come from measured referee episode times and have wide intervals; the estimate is attached to a budget refusal when its upper end exceeds the remaining budget.
- Seven verbs per cluster is the surface limit; these four, with `eval.preanalyze` of N24b-06, sit in the `study` and `eval` clusters and add no new cluster.
- Reports of a comparison as a claim go through the qualification lane; `eval.compare` on discovery worlds is a development result, and its envelope's `claims` list stays empty.
