## Background

Agents drive the studio through verbs: named operations of the `frob` command line and the MCP server, each with
a schema, a JSON envelope and a fixed exit code. The study engines of bead BD-124 need a surface with one
property above all: **the agent sees the cost and the reachable verdicts before any budget is spent, and the
object that runs cannot drift from the object that was planned.** That is why a study is two-phase (DESIGN):

- `study.plan` binds a trial manifest to immutable designs, scene, task, seed protocol, budget, backend and one
  declared study type (anytime-valid or fixed-n exact, never both). It returns a plan identifier that is the
  content address of the resolved manifest, the cost estimate with its interval, and, for a fixed-n study, the
  pre-analysis of which verdicts are reachable at that n. Planning runs no physics and is idempotent by
  construction.
- `study.submit` takes the plan identifier and an idempotency key and returns a job.
- `study.status` reports the progress of a submitted study.
- `eval.preanalyze` returns the reachable verdicts and the power of a fixed-n rule at a given n and level;
  `study.plan` calls it.
- `eval.compare` remains as the one-call form: it plans and submits an anytime-valid paired study with defaults.

Rejected: a single `compare` call that both decides the sample size and runs (the agent learns the cost after
paying it, and a request that names an unreachable verdict is discovered only at the end); letting `submit` take
a manifest (the submitted object could differ from the reviewed plan).

Registry kinds and milestones as registered: `study.plan` is `mutate`, `study.submit` and `eval.compare` are
`run`, `study.status` and `eval.preanalyze` are `read`. The registry dates `eval.preanalyze` at M1, the `study.*`
verbs at M2 and `eval.compare` at M3; the seed work item delivers all five on the CPU at M1, and this bead
registers them at M1 with tier `referee` only, the device rows following at their milestones.

## Scope

In: the five verbs' request and response schemas, executors in `frob-verbs`, refusal lists and examples; the plan
store (content-addressed manifests); the job wiring for `study.submit` on the CPU; the end-to-end script that
exercises both study types and their refusals from the user's side.

Out: the engines (BD-124); the rule enumerator (N24b-06); `study.compare`, `study.cancel` and `explain.compare`
(M2 and M3 surface, BD-262 and the Tether beads); MCP tool generation (BD-138); the qualification verbs
(`eval.qualify`, BD-259).

## Technical approach

Executors live in `crates/frob-verbs/src/verbs/{study,eval}.rs`; schemas are generated from
`registries/verbs.toml` and never edited by hand.

**`eval.preanalyze` (read).** Input: `rule` (identifier of a frozen rule, default the paired Clopper–Pearson
rule), `n`, `level`, `margin`, optional `alternatives` (pairs of discordance probabilities), optional
`declared_verdicts`. Output: `outcomes` (number of count pairs), `counts` per verdict, `reachable` (set),
`first_n_reachable` for each unreachable verdict (searched up to a stated cap), `power` per alternative, and
`hash` (BLAKE3 of the canonical pre-analysis record). If `declared_verdicts` contains an unreachable verdict the
answer is the refusal `FROB-REF-532` with the repair "raise n to `first_n_reachable`". Determinism D0; no physics.

**`study.plan` (mutate).** Input: `designs` (identifiers resolved to immutable `DesignId`s), `scene`, `task`,
`controller_arm` (`shared` | `retuned-equal-resources` | `inherits-weights` with its warm start),
`seed_protocol` (pairing by (world, replicate); clusters), `budget`, `backend` (at M1: `referee`), and exactly
one of `anytime { null, margin, span, betting_rule, level }` or `fixed_n { n, rule, level, margin,
declared_verdicts }`. Steps:
1. resolve every mutable name to an immutable identifier and echo them in the envelope's `bound` block;
2. validate pairing rules and the determinism class of the backend; refuse `FROB-REF-552` if both type blocks are
   present, `FROB-REF-533` under an uncontrolled class, `FROB-REF-543` if an anytime metric has no declared span;
3. for fixed-n, call the pre-analysis and refuse `FROB-REF-532` on an unreachable declared verdict or a missing
   pre-analysis;
4. compute the cost estimate with its interval;
5. write the resolved manifest to the plan store and return `plan_id` = BLAKE3 of its canonical bytes, the
   estimate, and the pre-analysis (fixed-n) or the stopping thresholds (anytime).
The same request always yields the same `plan_id`; planning twice writes nothing new.

**`study.submit` (run).** Input: `plan_id`, `idempotency_key`. It loads the manifest by content address (an
unknown identifier is a refusal), creates one job, and returns within the bounded synchronous wait or exits 8
with a job handle (`id`, `state`, `progress {done, total, unit}`, `eta_s`, `poll_after_ms`, `last_committed`).
A retry with the same key and plan returns the stored receipt with `idempotent_replay: true`; the same key with
another plan is refused as a conflict (exit 7). Episodes run on the referee through the chunk protocol; the
engine consumes outcomes in address order.

**`study.status` (read).** Input: a study or job identifier. Output: `state`, pair counts so far with the
accounting identity (a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned), for anytime the current e-value and
confidence sequence, for fixed-n **progress only** (counts and whether exact curtailment has fired; no interim
verdict), and the stopping reason once terminal.

**`eval.compare` (run).** Input: two designs, a task, optional scene, budget and metric list. It builds an
anytime plan with defaults (level 0.05, the task's declared margin and span per metric, the referee backend at
M1, a shared controller arm unless declared), calls the planning and submission paths, and returns the comparison
result or a job handle. Its refusals at comparison time are `FROB-REF-553`, `-555`, `-542`, `-543`, `-556`. The
result carries: the verdict (`settled`, `tied`, `inconclusive`, `incomparable` or `infeasible`); the e-value and
confidence sequence; pairs used with the accounting identity; the tier; the first divergent event between the
arms when one exists; each design's absolute hard-limit verdict; `rank_robustness: unchecked`; and the label
`provisional` where the tier calls for promotion.

**Envelope and exit codes (shared rules).** Exactly one JSON envelope per invocation. `evidence` is the claim
product of the inputs: claim Design comparison, class `Tested`, tier `referee`, determinism D2, identities and
seed. Exit 0 completed; 2 completed with findings; 3 a refused input or question (all the `5xx` codes above); 7 a
conflict; 8 accepted and still running. A refusal carries `code`, `message`, `pointer`, `observed`, `required`,
`retry` and ranked `repairs`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob eval preanalyze --n 64 --margin 0.05 --json` returns 2,145 outcomes split 521 / 521 / 0 / 1,103, `reachable` without the tie, and `first_n_reachable` 86 for the tie (GATE-ARENA-05 from the user's side); `--n 86` returns 3,828 split 1,031 / 1,031 / 1 / 1,765 (GATE-ATEL-06).
- [ ] `study.plan` with both type blocks exits 3 with `FROB-REF-552`; with a fixed-n block that declares a tie at n = 64 it exits 3 with `FROB-REF-532` and the repair names n = 86.
- [ ] `study.plan` runs no physics (no episode is created; asserted from the ledger) and returns the same `plan_id` for the same request.
- [ ] `study.submit` under one idempotency key creates one job however often it is retried; the retry returns `idempotent_replay: true`; the key reused with another plan exits 7.
- [ ] `study.status` of a fixed-n study never contains a verdict before n complete pairs or exact curtailment.
- [ ] `eval.compare` on the two cart designs returns a verdict with the accounting identity, the tier and both absolute hard-limit verdicts, or an `inconclusive` with its stopping reason.
- [ ] Each of `FROB-REF-553`, `-555`, `-542`, `-543`, `-556` is returned for its planted request from the command line.
- [ ] All five verbs are in `registries/verbs.toml` with generated schemas, at least one example each and their refusal lists; `frob schema <verb>` prints them; cancellation of a submitted study leaves committed chunks only.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-verbs verbs::study verbs::eval`:
- `preanalyze_schema_round_trip`; `preanalyze_64_and_86_counts`; `preanalyze_first_reachable_tie_is_86`.
- `plan_is_pure_and_content_addressed`: two calls, one `plan_id`, zero episodes.
- `plan_refuses_mixed_types`, `plan_refuses_unreachable_verdict`, `plan_refuses_missing_span`, `plan_refuses_uncontrolled_class`.
- `submit_unknown_plan_refused`; `submit_idempotent_replay`; `submit_key_conflict_exit_7`.
- `status_fixed_n_progress_only`; `status_identity_holds`.
- `compare_defaults_are_anytime`: the plan built by `eval.compare` is of type `Anytime` at level 0.05.
- `compare_result_has_absolute_verdicts_and_tier`.
- `one_envelope_per_invocation`: malformed input still yields exactly one envelope.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/study_two_types.sh` on fixture `fixture:cart-sill-dock` (two cart designs A and B), seed-explicit and
idempotent (a scratch store per run):
1. `frob eval preanalyze --n 64 --margin 0.05 --json`; assert the table and the missing tie.
2. `frob study plan … --fixed-n 64 --declare promote,inferior,inconclusive --json`; assert a `plan_id`, a cost
   estimate and zero episodes in the ledger.
3. the same with `--declare …,tie`; assert exit 3, `FROB-REF-532`, repair n = 86.
4. `frob study plan … --anytime … --fixed-n 64`; assert exit 3 and `FROB-REF-552`.
5. `frob study submit <plan_id> --idempotency-key k1 --json`; poll `frob study status` until terminal; assert that
   no status before the end carries a verdict; assert the final counts satisfy the identity and the pre-analysis
   hash matches step 2.
6. repeat step 5's submit with `k1`; assert `idempotent_replay: true` and one job in total.
7. `frob eval compare A B --task dock --budget … --json`; assert a legitimate disposition, the e-value, the pairs
   used and both absolute verdicts.
8. re-run step 7 with `--workers 1` and `--workers 8`; assert identical e-values (address-order invariance).
Every step is logged per STD-E2E: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code,
wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed), metrics
(`outcomes`, per-verdict counts, `pairs_used`, `e_value`, `episodes_created_by_plan` with threshold 0,
`jobs_for_key` with threshold 1), the plan and pre-analysis digests, and on failure the first differing value and
the run's artifact paths. The final `summary` line counts assertions and names GATE-ARENA-05 and GATE-ATEL-06.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-612 (the enumeration tables) and KA-090 (no tie at n = 64) through the verbs. Negative controls from the user's
side: the mixed-type request; the unreachable verdict; a plan whose pairing is keyed by a training counter
(`FROB-REF-556`); a fixed-n status polled for a verdict. Metamorphic: exchanging the design arguments of
`eval.compare` exchanges the sign of the effect and leaves the pair counts mirrored. Determinism: steps 1 to 4
are byte-identical across runs; steps 5 to 8 give identical statistics twice on one machine and across worker
counts.

## Considerations

- The registry's milestones for these verbs (M2 and M3) describe when they exist on devices and through the full surface; nothing here blocks that. The coverage file records the difference with the seed's M1.
- A plan identifier names bytes and carries no verdict. An edited design is a different `DesignId`, so an old plan can never run against a new design by accident.
- Estimates at M1 come from measured referee episode times and have wide intervals; the estimate is attached to a budget refusal when its upper end exceeds the remaining budget.
- Seven verbs per cluster is the surface limit; these five sit in the `study` and `eval` clusters and add no new cluster.
- Reports of a comparison as a claim go through the qualification lane; `eval.compare` on discovery worlds is a development result, and its envelope's `claims` list stays empty.
