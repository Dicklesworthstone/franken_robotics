## Background

FrankenRobotics consumes FrankenSim, the owner's physics library, through one airlock crate, `frob-fsim`, and
under one standing rule: **an admission is never inherited.** A crate, or a module of a crate, that is first used
at a milestone is admitted at that milestone by its own record, at the pin of that day, in the form of the
file-by-file admission tests: the code compiles from this repository behind its feature, one known answer passes
through the airlock, and one negative control fails. A record names the commit, the command, its output and the
resolution receipt it ran under.

The archetype `tri-wheel-stair` (two three-wheel clusters on a chassis, archetype 14 of the eighteen) arrives at
M3. Its stepping kinematics, a cluster rolling over the nose of a stair, are checked against an oracle, and the
design names one: FACT (to be re-read here at the pin): the FrankenSim crate `fs-mbd` has a module
`tri_wheel_cluster` that holds the cluster geometry against riser and tread. `fs-mbd` is already in the airlock's
`referee` group and its articulated-dynamics module was admitted at M0 by INT-FSIM-03, but that record says
nothing about this module. The archetype bead may use the module as an oracle only after its own admission test,
and no bead carried that test. This bead is that test.

The gate register has no row for this admission; like every first-use admission of a module inside an admitted
crate it is recorded in `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` and listed by `frob doctor`, and it blocks only its consumer.
Rejected alternatives: using the module on the strength of INT-FSIM-03 (inherited admission); skipping the oracle
and trusting the archetype's own closed forms alone (then nothing independent checks the stepping kinematics).
Tier: Solid. The probe uses no physics step, only geometry.

## Scope

In: the re-read of the module's public surface and conventions at the pin; a test-only adapter in the airlock; an
independent closed-form oracle for a three-wheel cluster on flat ground and seated on two consecutive treads; the
hand cases and a seeded family; negative controls; the admission record; the fallback if the module fails.
Out: the archetype itself, its nested symmetry group and its design heuristic for cluster spacing (N14b-04, which
depends on this record); the dynamics of a cluster on stairs (the referee step and the contact subsystem); any
change to the sibling module (if the probe finds a defect, it is an upstream work item in FrankenSim's
repository, registered through the upstream register BD-037, with this probe's failing case as its fixture).

## Technical approach

**1. Facts re-read at the pin (recorded in the admission record, never copied from here).** The module's path and
public functions; the meaning and units of each argument (cluster radius, wheel radius, riser, tread, cluster
angle); the zero and the sign of the cluster angle; the order in which the three wheels are indexed; the frame of
the returned points; what the module refuses and how (a typed error or a panic); whether results are `f64` only.

**2. Test-only adapter (DESIGN).** `crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission/tri_wheel_cluster.rs`, compiled only for the
admission lane (`frob-conformance` is the one test consumer on the allow-list of the `referee` group). It
converts this project's quantity types to the sibling's arguments and the sibling's result to
`[Point2; 3]` wheel centres in a stated frame: x along the direction of travel, z up, origin at the floor under
the first lower wheel. It converts and never reinterprets: a sibling refusal becomes a typed referee refusal
carrying the upstream reason. No production adapter is added; the archetype reads the oracle through
`frob-conformance`.

**3. Independent closed forms (`frob_conformance::oracle::tri_wheel`, exact where inputs are rational).** With ρ
the distance from the cluster axis to a wheel centre, r the wheel radius, h the riser and t the tread:
1. *Centres.* For cluster angle θ the centres are ρ·(cos(θ + 2πk/3), sin(θ + 2πk/3)), k = 0, 1, 2, about the
   axis. Any two centres are √3·ρ apart.
2. *Flat ground, two wheels down.* The axis is at height r + ρ/2 and the two contact points are √3·ρ apart.
3. *Seated on two consecutive treads.* The lower wheel centre is c₀ = (0, r); the upper is
   c₁ = (√(3ρ² − h²), h + r); the third is the apex of the equilateral triangle on the free side,
   c₂ = c₀ + R(+60°)·(c₁ − c₀); the axis is the centroid. The seat exists only if 3ρ² ≥ h² and
   r ≤ √(3ρ² − h²) ≤ 2t − r: there is then a position of the lower wheel on its tread for which both wheels stand
   on their treads and neither enters a riser. Otherwise the configuration is refused, not clamped.

**4. Hand case** (the archetype's fixture: h = 0.175 m, t = 0.260 m, ρ = 0.181 m, r = 0.100 m). Spacing
√3·ρ = 0.313501 m against the stair diagonal √(h² + t²) = 0.313409 m. Flat ground: axis height 0.1905 m. Seated:
c₁ = (0.260112, 0.275000) m, so the upper wheel advances 0.112 mm more than one tread per step;
c₂ = (−0.021499, 0.412763) m; axis (0.079538, 0.262588) m, at 0.181000 m from each centre.

**5. Seeded family.** 1,000 cases addressed by `(seed, index)`: ρ ∈ [0.10, 0.30] m, r ∈ [0.05, 0.15] m,
h ∈ [0.10, 0.20] m, t ∈ [0.22, 0.32] m, θ uniform. For each, the adapter's centres equal the closed forms to
10⁻¹² m (TARGET), and cases outside the seat's existence condition are refused by both sides.

**6. Record.** `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` entry `fs-mbd::tri_wheel_cluster (first use, M3)`: commit, command, output
hash, receipt hash, the facts of step 1, the hand case with measured values, the worst error over the family, the
controls caught, and the outcome `admitted as oracle` or `local closed form in force`. Claim: Known answer; class
`Tested`; tier host; determinism D0.

**7. If it fails.** The archetype's stepping kinematics are checked against the closed forms of step 3 alone, the
record says so, and the defect goes to the upstream register with the failing case. No milestone waits.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The module's public surface, units, angle convention, wheel order and refusal behaviour are recorded at the pin.
- [ ] The hand case passes through the adapter: spacing 0.313501 m; flat-ground axis height 0.1905 m; c₁ = (0.260112, 0.275000) m; c₂ = (−0.021499, 0.412763) m; each to 10⁻⁶ m as printed and to 10⁻¹² m against the closed form.
- [ ] 1,000 of 1,000 seeded cases agree to 10⁻¹² m, and every case outside the existence condition is refused by the adapter and by the oracle.
- [ ] Rotating θ by 120° returns the same three centres with indices rotated; the mirrored cluster returns the mirrored centres.
- [ ] Each negative control below is caught and registered in the negative-control registry.
- [ ] The admission record exists, validates and cites a receipt hash; `frob doctor` lists it; no production crate gains an edge to the module because of this bead (`xtask check-deps`).
- [ ] If the outcome is `local closed form in force`, an upstream work item with the failing fixture is recorded in `registries/upstream.toml`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission/tri_wheel_cluster.rs` and `cargo test -p frob-conformance oracle::tri_wheel`:
- `centres_are_sqrt3_rho_apart`: ρ = 0.181 m → 0.313501 m for every pair, at ten cluster angles.
- `flat_ground_axis_height`: r = 0.100 m, ρ = 0.181 m → 0.1905 m; contact spacing 0.313501 m.
- `seated_hand_case`: the four values of the hand case.
- `seat_refused_when_impossible`: h = 0.40 m with ρ = 0.181 m (3ρ² < h²) is refused by both sides.
- `upper_wheel_off_tread_refused`: t = 0.17 m with the hand-case ρ, h and r (the advance 0.260112 m exceeds 2t − r = 0.24 m).
- `c3_index_rotation` and `mirror_symmetry`.
- `seeded_family_1000`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the step `tri_wheel_oracle_admission` (due at M3) to `scripts/e2e/airlock_groups.sh`, the script
of N33a-34 that covers every first-use admission of a group or oracle and to which later beads add their steps
(N33b-21's script is the two-binary group matrix and has no oracle steps). The step runs the admission lane,
writes the record under `--record`, then calls `frob doctor --json` and asserts that the record is listed with its
receipt hash; the planted adapters of the known-answer section are declared as plants of the script header and
each must turn the step red under `run-all.sh --verify-plants`. It logs through the STD-E2E helper: `cmd` events with exit code and wall time, `metric` events
`spacing_m`, `axis_height_m`, `advance_m` and `worst_error_m` (threshold 10⁻¹² m), one `assert` event per control
with its `caught` flag, and on a mismatch the case address `(seed, index)`, both sets of centres and the first
differing coordinate. Before M3 the step logs `skipped: not yet due`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the hand case and the flat-ground case above. Negative controls (planted adapters, each must fail
at least one test): the cluster angle passed in degrees where radians are expected; wheel radius and cluster
radius swapped; the apex taken on the stair side (c₂ inside the riser); a seat that clamps an impossible riser
instead of refusing. Metamorphic relation: scaling ρ, r, h and t by one factor scales every centre by that factor.
Determinism lanes: identical results twice on one machine, in debug and release, on both platforms.

## Considerations

- The cluster-spacing relation of the archetype (spacing close to the stair diagonal) is a design heuristic of
  N14b-04, labelled DESIGN there; this bead admits the oracle for the kinematics and takes no position on it.
- The frame and angle conventions are the usual place for a silent error: the adapter states its frame, and the
  degrees-for-radians and wrong-apex controls exist because both mistakes still return plausible triangles.
- A pin move invalidates the record; the compatibility train re-runs this lane with the other admissions.
- Cost: no device; seconds of host time; no new crate enters any graph (the module is in `fs-mbd`, already in the
  closure of the `referee` group).
