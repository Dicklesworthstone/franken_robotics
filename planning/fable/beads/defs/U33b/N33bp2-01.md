## Background

FrankenRobotics consumes FrankenSim, a sibling project, by path at an exact pin (`git_rev` of its
`[[foundation]]` row in `franken-stack.lock`), behind one airlock crate `frob-fsim`. The plan's rule (DESIGN):
pins advance through a **compatibility train**: move the pin, write a new resolution receipt
(`frob.stack-receipt/1`), rerun the admission and parity gates, relock. Bead N33b-04 specifies that procedure,
implements it as `scripts/train/compat_train.sh`, defines the train log `receipts/train-log.ndjson`
(`frob.train-log/1`), and proves its guards on synthetic sibling repositories. A procedure proved only on
stand-ins is still a hope: this bead runs it once for real, on FrankenSim, at M0.

The real run matters for two measurements that exist nowhere else. Risk RISK-01 (FrankenSim API churn) is
signalled by "adapter changes per pin move". Bet BET-04 (FrankenSim can be consumed by path from outside its
tree, stably, on one toolchain) is falsified, and kill gate KILL-FSIM fires, when upstream breakage costs more
than 20% of working days in any four-week window, or when INT-FSIM-01's cold check exceeds the machine envelope
(one hour, 16 GiB, 8 cores). FrankenSim moves fast (FACT: 409 commits touched `crates/fs-mbd` in the thirty days
to 2026-11-21, on 17 distinct days; its composition contract is at version 0), so the first pin move after
INT-FSIM-01 is the first real reading of what consumption by path costs. The KILL-FSIM decision recorded at the
M0 exit (N33b-05) cites this train.

An admission is never inherited: a record is valid only for the commit it cites. The train therefore re-runs
INT-FSIM-01 at the new pin, with the G1 forward-dynamics digest and the two-second rollout digest recaptured
inside FrankenSim's own tree at that commit (BD-007's capture script), and does not carry the old fixture across.

The plan states the outcome rule itself: if the newer head breaks admission, the run ends as a recorded
`abandoned` train, which is an equally valid rehearsal. Either outcome closes this bead; no outcome is hidden.

This bead was split from N33b-04 in polishing round 2, so that the procedure, its script and its guard tests
(on which every upstream work item depends) are finished without waiting for a real upstream head, while the
real run, which waits on INT-FSIM-01 and on a clean newer head of FrankenSim, is its own unit of work. Nothing of
the first real train was dropped: its section, its acceptance lines and its real-run logging moved here.

## Scope

In: one real train of FrankenSim (`--sibling FD-FSIM`) from the admission pin (the `git_rev` cited by
INT-FSIM-01's passing record) to a newer clean head of FrankenSim's `main`, run by `scripts/train/compat_train.sh`
without `--dry-run`; the end-to-end wrapper `scripts/e2e/first_train_fsim.sh` that runs it with full NDJSON
logging and verifies the result; the four measurements the plan names (wall time, gates re-run with results,
adapter lines changed, whether the three root settings needed a change) and the build measurements at the new
pin; the checked-in evidence under `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/first-train/`; the record-consistency test of that
evidence.

Out, with the bead that has it:

- The procedure, its script, its preflight refusals and abandon path, the train log and its schema, the guard
  tests on synthetic siblings (N33b-04). This bead adds no step to the procedure and no rule to the script.
- The definition of a breakage day and the evaluation of the KILL-FSIM trigger (N33a-10); the decision and its
  ADR (N33b-05, which depends on this bead); the M0 exit review (BD-044).
- The rehearsal of both trains on a scratch stack with every guard tripped (NF-06); the first toolchain train
  (NF-05).
- INT-FSIM-01 itself and its cold/warm check (BD-006); the G1 digest probe, capture script and fixture (BD-007);
  INT-ASUP-01 (BD-008). This bead re-runs them through the train at the new pin, and an adopted train must
  show all three passing there, so all three are dependencies.
- Any change inside FrankenSim. If the train needs one, it is an upstream work item (`FD-FSIM-…`), opened or
  updated with the failing fixture; this bead never edits, fetches into or resets the sibling checkout.

## Technical approach

1. **Choose the target (no judgment call left).** C is the head of FrankenSim's `main` as checked out by
   whoever works in that sibling, on the day of the run, provided it is a descendant of the admission pin and
   the checkout is clean. The wrapper reads the head and `git status --porcelain` of the sibling and records
   both; it never moves the checkout. If the procedure's preflight refuses (`drift`: dirty, retreated or
   diverged; `not-a-descendant`; `frozen`), the refusal is logged with its classification and the run stops
   with nothing changed; it is retried on a later day, again without touching the sibling. Refusals are not
   trains and write no train-log line.
2. **Dry run.** `scripts/train/compat_train.sh --sibling FD-FSIM --to <C> --dry-run`. The wrapper asserts that
   no tracked file of this repository changed (`git status --porcelain` identical before and after) and logs
   the plan the script prints: the coupled siblings named by FrankenSim's own `constellation.lock` at C
   (asupersync, FrankenSQLite, FrankenTorch, franken_numpy, frankenscipy) with their classes, and the gate list
   step 7 will run.
3. **Real run.** The same command without `--dry-run`, under the offload shim for the builds. The procedure's
   ten steps run as N33b-04 specifies them: preconditions; coupled siblings (one that must move rides the same
   train, for example asupersync if FrankenSim at C needs another version, with INT-ASUP-01 re-run; one that
   disagrees and cannot move stops the train); move the pin and regenerate the lockfiles of the core and
   `worker/` workspaces; re-read the three root settings (the `[patch.crates-io] asupersync` entry, the
   FrankenSQLite profile overrides, the build-identity environment of the linear-algebra crate) and repeat any
   change at this repository's root; write the receipt; build on T with cold and warm times and peak memory;
   adapt inside `frob-fsim` only, counting `adapter_lines_changed`; re-run the gates; invalidate; relock in one
   commit, or abandon.
4. **Gates re-run at the new pin (what M0 has).** INT-FSIM-01 (BD-006's cold/warm check and the three root
   settings; BD-007's G1 digests recaptured inside FrankenSim at C, with `fd_digest` and `rollout_2s_digest` in
   `tests/fixtures/oracle/fsim/g1_fd_digest.toml` and a new capture record, then the candidate compared with
   both); every other FrankenSim admission gate with a record at M0 (the file-by-file tests among INT-FSIM-02 to
   -11 that exist, the group gates of every enabled group); INT-ASUP-01; the foundation-fact probes (GATE-WS-06,
   GATE-WS-09) and the fact sheet re-read (N00-16's runner reports each fact `unchanged`, `moved`, `changed` or
   `unreadable`, with the beads of a changed row's `consequence`); every fixture
   under `tests/upstream/` and the switch-over rule of every upstream item that landed in the span (read from
   `registries/upstream.toml`); the six `xtask` checks. A gate not yet built at M0 is listed in the train-log
   line as `not-run` with its key, never as passed. The parity gates of the tiers (GATE-KIN-02 and others) do
   not exist at M0 and are listed the same way.
5. **The four measurements and the envelope.** The admission record written at the new pin carries:
   `wall_seconds` of the whole train; the gates re-run with their results; `adapter_lines_changed` (from the
   diff of `crates/frob-fsim`; bedrock-direct users counted separately); `root_settings_changed`, a list naming
   which of the three root settings needed a change (empty if none); and the cold check (`cargo check
   --workspace --all-targets --locked` from an empty target directory) wall time, peak memory and cores, with
   the warm check time, against the envelope 3,600 s, 16 GiB, 8 cores. The cold-check row reaches the trigger
   ledger (N33a-10) through that admission record, as every INT-FSIM-01 record does; the ledger's `evaluate`
   then reports T2 from it.
6. **Outcome, either way recorded.** *Adopted:* one commit carries the lock row (new `git_rev`, `toolchain`,
   `receipt`), the lockfiles, the receipt file, the admission records (each citing, as `harness_receipt`, the
   tier run that re-ran its gate) and the train-log line. The train writes no gate row (N33b-04's step 9,
   N38b-19): the wrapper logs each re-run gate's harness receipt hash, and the part's recording bead named in
   the gate's register row (BD-008 for INT-ASUP-01) records it again from that receipt.
   *Abandoned:* this repository's lock, lockfiles and `receipts/` are restored byte for byte, the train-log
   line is written with `outcome = "abandoned"` and `failing_gate`; the remedy is named by key in the evidence
   record (an adapter change followed by a new train; or an upstream item opened or updated with the failing
   fixture); the working days the failure blocked are entered honestly in `blocked_dates`, and the script
   appends them to the trigger ledger as breakage days and prints `breakage_share_28d`.
7. **Evidence.** The wrapper copies its NDJSON log and transcript to
   `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/first-train/<train-id>.ndjson` and `.transcript.txt`, and writes
   `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/first-train/RECORD.toml` (DESIGN; a record of this bead, not a registry):
   `train_id`, `outcome`, `from_rev`, `to_rev`, `receipt_before`, `receipt_after`, `adopting_commit` (empty if
   abandoned), `log_blake3`, `transcript_blake3`, `remedy` (empty if adopted), `root_settings_changed`. These go
   in the adopting commit, or in a follow-up commit for an abandoned train. The KILL-FSIM ADR (N33b-05) cites
   `train_id` and `receipt_after`.

Determinism: the receipt and the train-log line are pure functions of the trees (KA-767); the build times are
measurements, logged with the host fingerprint and never compared across hosts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `receipts/train-log.ndjson` holds one line of `kind` `pin` whose `moves` include FD-FSIM with `from_rev` equal to the pin cited by INT-FSIM-01's passing record and `to_rev` a later commit of FrankenSim's `main`, with `outcome` `adopted` or `abandoned`, `wall_seconds`, `adapter_lines_changed`, every gate re-run with its status (`not-run` with a key for gates that do not exist at M0), and `failing_gate` set if abandoned; the line validates against `frob.train-log/1`.
- [ ] If adopted: the adopting commit contains the lock row, lockfiles, receipt, admission records (INT-FSIM-01 with `fd_digest` and `rollout_2s_digest` recaptured inside FrankenSim at `to_rev` and a capture record that passes BD-007's check, and INT-ASUP-01, each citing its tier run as `harness_receipt`) and the log line together, and no change to `registries/gates.toml` (the rows are re-entered by the gates' recording beads from the logged receipts); at that commit `check-stack` passes and reports FrankenSim `on-pin`; every re-run gate's record cites `receipt_after`; no record cites `receipt_before` as current for FrankenSim; no record cites a receipt with `dirty: true` (INV-SUITE-03).
- [ ] If abandoned: `franken-stack.lock`, the lockfiles and `receipts/` are byte-identical to their state before the run apart from the one `abandoned` log line; the failing gate and the remedy are named by key in `RECORD.toml`; the blocked working days are in the trigger ledger and the printed `breakage_share_28d` equals the ledger's evaluation.
- [ ] The admission record at the new pin (or, if abandoned, the evidence record) states the four measurements of the plan: wall time, gates re-run with results, adapter lines changed, and `root_settings_changed`; and the cold and warm check times, peak memory and cores against 3,600 s, 16 GiB and 8 cores.
- [ ] The sibling checkout is untouched: its head and the BLAKE3 of its `git status --porcelain` output are identical before and after the run (logged as `sibling_writes = 0`); the dry run changed no tracked file of this repository.
- [ ] `docs/delivery/kill-fsim/first-train/` holds the run's NDJSON log, its transcript and `RECORD.toml`, whose hashes match; the log validates against `frob.e2elog/1` and ends with a `summary`.
- [ ] The test `first_train_evidence_intact` passes and its five planted copies are each refused.
- [ ] The KILL-FSIM decision (N33b-05) cites this train's `train_id` and `receipt_after` (checked when that ADR is written; this bead supplies the two values in `RECORD.toml`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p xtask --test train_guards first_train` (the workspace's one test runner, as in N33b-04):
- `first_train_evidence_intact`: reads `RECORD.toml` and checks, durably (none of it depends on the current lock
  row, which later trains move): the train-log line with `train_id` exists and agrees with the record's
  `outcome`, `from_rev`, `to_rev`, `receipt_before` and `receipt_after`; both receipts exist under `receipts/`
  and hash to their names; for an adopted train, `git show <adopting_commit>:franken-stack.lock` has FrankenSim's
  `git_rev = to_rev` and `receipt = receipt_after`, the INT-FSIM-01 admission record of that commit cites
  `receipt_after`, and the gate list contains INT-FSIM-01 and INT-ASUP-01 with status `pass`; for an abandoned
  train, `receipt_after = receipt_before`, `failing_gate` is non-empty and `remedy` names a bead key that exists;
  the checked-in log and transcript hash to `log_blake3` and `transcript_blake3`.
- Five planted copies, made in a temporary directory from the real evidence, each refused with a named reason:
  (1) the adopting commit's lock row with `receipt` left at `receipt_before` (the stale-stack-lock plant of
  GATE-WS-01, seen through the record); (2) an admission record citing `receipt_before`; (3) the log edited by
  one byte; (4) a line with outcome `adopted` and one gate `fail`; (5) a receipt with FrankenSim `dirty: true`
  cited by an admission record (INV-SUITE-03).
- `first_train_record_schema`: `RECORD.toml` has exactly the fields of step 7; an unknown field or a missing one
  is refused.
- Before the real train exists the test fails with "first train not recorded", never passes vacuously; this bead
  is not closed until it passes.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/first_train_fsim.sh` (header: `bead=N33bp2-01 milestone=M0 labels=suite,upstream,e2e
gates=KILL-FSIM,INT-FSIM-01,INT-ASUP-01 timeout_s=3600`), sourcing the shared helper of STD-E2E; logs to
`target/e2e-logs/first_train_fsim/<utc>.ndjson` with the transcript beside it. Steps:
1. `preflight`: record this repository's commit and `dirty`, the sibling's head and porcelain hash, the
   `check-stack` classes of every foundation; assert FrankenSim is `on-pin` or `stale-lock` and the target is a
   descendant of the pin, else log the procedure's refusal (`refusal` event with the class) and end. The
   `summary` of a refused run gives `refusal_days`, the number of distinct days with a refusal in the logs
   under `target/e2e-logs/first_train_fsim/`, and from the fifth day `owner_request: clean-sibling-head`.
2. `dry_run`: the dry run; assert no tracked file changed; log the coupled-sibling classes and the gate plan.
3. `train`: the real run; one `cmd` line per procedure step with command, exit code, wall time and output hash;
   `metric` lines `cold_check_s` (threshold ≤ 3600), `warm_check_s`, `peak_rss_gib` (≤ 16), `cores` (≤ 8),
   `adapter_lines_changed`, `gates_rerun`, `gates_passed`, `gates_not_run`, `root_settings_changed` (a count,
   with the names in the line), `wall_seconds`, `breakage_share_28d` (threshold 0.20, `op` `le`).
4. `verify_adopted` (adopted only): `check-stack` passes and reports FrankenSim `on-pin`; the receipt computed
   twice at the adopted state is byte-identical (KA-767, `assert` kind `determinism`); BD-007's capture record
   check and the tests `g1_fd_digest_matches_oracle` and `g1_rollout_2s_digest_matches_oracle` pass at the new
   pin; `frob doctor --json` shows the new receipt hash.
5. `verify_abandoned` (abandoned only): the lock, lockfiles and `receipts/` hashes equal the preflight's; the
   train-log line names the failing gate; the trigger ledger gained exactly the `blocked_dates` entries.
6. `sibling_untouched`: the sibling's head and porcelain hash equal the preflight's; `metric` `sibling_writes`
   (threshold 0).
7. `evidence`: copy the log and transcript, write `RECORD.toml`, run `first_train_evidence_intact`.
On failure each `assert` line carries the first differing value and the `seq` of the command to reproduce it;
the `summary` lists asserts passed and failed, the gates touched with `measured_ok`, and the outcome. The
header's `timeout_s=3600` is the machine envelope, so the runner stops a train that runs longer (the cold check
alone may approach it); the procedure's step 10 then abandons the train, which is recorded as such. A budget
above one hour exists only as the owner's written approval, the decision of OPEN-SUITE-5 (N33c-34's option B):
a run under it is made from a commit whose header declares that budget as `timeout_s`, with `--approved-envelope
<id>` naming the approval, recorded on the `start` line, as STD-E2E requires for any `timeout_s` above 3600.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: KA-767 (a receipt computed twice on an unchanged tree is byte-identical; the wrapper computes it
  twice at the adopted state); the G1 digests recaptured inside FrankenSim equal the candidate's at the new pin
  (INT-FSIM-01's clause, and the M0 artifact for the rollout digest); the breakage share printed equals the
  trigger ledger's evaluation of the same days.
- Negative controls: the five planted copies of `first_train_evidence_intact`; the dry-run assertion (a planted
  script variant that writes the lock during `--dry-run` fails step 2, run once against a scratch copy of this
  repository); the sibling assertion (the comparison of step 6 run on a scratch sibling copy whose porcelain
  output was changed between the two readings fails).
- Counterexamples protected: "a procedure that has run on stand-ins has run" (it has not, until this line
  exists); "an admission carries over to the next pin" (the digests are recaptured at C); "an abandoned train is
  a failure to hide" (it is a recorded outcome and a breakage reading).
- Determinism lanes: the receipt and the train-log line are identical when computed twice from the same trees;
  the timings are measurements and carry the host fingerprint.

## Considerations

- RISK-01 (High): this is the first reading of adapter lines changed per pin move. A large number here is a
  finding for the KILL-FSIM decision, not a reason to pick an easier target head; the target is the clean head
  of the day, by rule.
- If FrankenSim's checkout stays dirty or diverged through the M0 window, no train runs and no line is written;
  the bead stays open, and no synthetic train is offered in its place. The KILL-FSIM decision (N33b-05) depends
  on this bead, and the M0 exit review (BD-044) depends on that decision, so M0 does not exit on a procedure
  that has never run for real: that is the fail-closed default, and it is deliberate. The logged refusals
  (date and class each) are the record of the wait. DESIGN, so that the wait is not silent: after refusals on
  five distinct working days the wrapper's `summary` carries `owner_request: clean-sibling-head`, asking the
  owner for a moment at which FrankenSim's `main` checkout has no uncommitted work, which only the owner can
  arrange. The sibling's state is never "fixed" by this project: other agents work there under its own rules.
- A train is the moment an upstream item is noticed: if FD-ASUP-01's FrankenSim leg (one asupersync version) or
  another FD item landed between the pins, its fixture flips and its switch-over rule runs inside this train
  (procedure step 7 (iv)), and `upstream_items` lists it.
- Cost: a cold build of the M1 closure (65 crates, about 2.06 million lines, FACT) plus the admission lanes, on
  the build workers through the offload shim; OPEN-SUITE-5 (N33c-34) sets the budget.
- Honesty of `blocked_dates`: the script prompts for them when the train is abandoned; a day spent on planned
  upstream work is not a breakage day (N33a-10's definition).
- If a freeze is in force (`frozen`), the train waits; no freeze is expected at M0.
