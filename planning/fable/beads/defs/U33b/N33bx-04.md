## Background

Foundry, the structure subsystem (crate `frob-foundry`, tier Frontier), optimizes the topology of a link on cut
cells and then checks the result under one architecture rule: **the optimizer never checks its own result.**
Stress, buckling, modes and fatigue are evaluated on a separately generated, body-fitted tetrahedral mesh of
the extracted surface, a different discretization from the optimizer's. The design names the mesher:
FrankenSim's `fs-mesh`. It also says what is uncertain about it: the crate's documentation still lists full
boundary conformity as successor scope, so whether an optimized surface meshes cleanly is an open question
(OPEN-FOUNDRY-1) answered by an experiment on 20 surfaces at M4, and until a sibling statement exists
conformity is measured per mesh and recorded with the structural evidence (the interim posture of upstream
work item FD-FSIM-30).

FrankenSim is reached only through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, group by group, and `fs-mesh` was on no
group's crate list: no adapter exposed it, and no admission record covered it. ADR-SUITE-03 (N33bx-02) adds it
to the `structure` group, whose consumer is `frob-foundry`. This bead builds the entry and admits it. The rule
is that **an admission is never inherited and never late**: a crate first used at M4 is admitted at M4, by its
own record, at the pin of that day; the first use from this repository is the conformity experiment, which
calls the mesher through this entry in the conformance lane so that its 20 surfaces travel the path M5 will use.

FACT (read 2026-11-22 at FrankenSim head `95a7fba8e8a7`; re-read at the pin by this bead's lane):
`frankensim/crates/fs-mesh` is layer L2, 17,772 lines of library source (26,016 with tests), and depends on
`fs-exec`, `fs-geom`, `fs-ivl`, `fs-math`, `fs-obs` and `fs-rep-mesh`. `fs-io` and `fs-solid` both depend on
it, so it is already compiled by the default build and by the `structure` group; admitting it adds a nameable
crate and no compile cost. It provides `delaunay` (incremental Delaunay tetrahedralization on exact predicates)
returning a `Tetrahedralization` with `audit(full_insphere) -> AuditReport { violations }`; `recover_segments`
and `recover_facets`; `refine` and `exude` for quality; and in `volumetric` a staged pipeline over a
piecewise-linear complex: `UnverifiedPlc::new(vertices, regions)`, `admit`, `recover`, `refine_constrained`,
`carve_and_label`, ending in a `LabeledTetComplex` (`positions`, `tets`, `region_of_tet`) with a
`VolumeConservationWitness`, a `QualityCensus` and recovery and refinement evidence. Long steps take the
executor context `Cx`. A hexahedral lane sits behind the feature `frontier-hexmesh`, which stays off.

Rejected: letting `frob-foundry` depend on `fs-mesh` directly (rule L-2); placing the entry in the default-on
`geometry` group because the crate is already in its closure (Foundry is Frontier work, and features that
select a tier of ambition are off by default); running the experiment inside the sibling's tree (its fixtures
would then not exercise the adapter that M5 relies on).

## Technical approach

**1. Trait (DESIGN: intent, not signatures; module `frob_fsim::structure::mesh`, feature `structure`).**

```rust
pub trait VolumeMesher {
    fn limits(&self) -> MesherLimits;                 // largest input and output sizes admitted; data
    fn tetrahedralize(&self, surface: &MeshHandle, regions: &[RegionSeed], opts: &TetOptions, cx: &Cx)
        -> Result<TetMeshHandle, RefereeRefusal>;
    fn audit(&self, mesh: &TetMeshHandle) -> TetAudit;    // the sibling's audits, verbatim
    fn facts(&self, mesh: &TetMeshHandle) -> TetFacts;    // counts, volume per region, smallest dihedral angle, largest radius-edge ratio
    fn export(&self, mesh: &TetMeshHandle) -> TetArrays;  // positions, tetrahedra, region per tetrahedron, boundary faces
}
pub struct TetOptions { pub max_radius_edge: Option<f64>, pub max_volume_m3: Option<f64>, pub recover_boundary: bool }
pub struct TetAudit { pub clean: bool, pub violations: Vec<String>, pub volume_witness: Option<VolumeWitness>, pub recovered: RecoveryReport }
```

`MeshHandle` is the surface type of `MeshExchange`. No FrankenSim type appears in the signatures; `Cx` is from
a bedrock-direct crate.

**2. Rules.**
1. *No repair.* A surface that is not closed, consistently oriented and manifold is refused before meshing,
   with the topology report of the mesh-exchange adapter (for an open box: one boundary loop of four edges).
2. *Audits are verbatim.* Every violation string of the sibling is returned. A mesh with violations is a mesh
   with `clean = false`; it is never presented as success, and the caller decides (Foundry refuses it).
3. *Conformity is measured, not assumed.* `export` gives the boundary faces so that the consumer measures the
   two-sided distance between the mesh boundary and the input surface. The adapter makes no conformity claim.
4. *Capacities are data.* An input above `limits()` or a request that would exceed the output cap is refused
   by name; nothing is decimated in silence.
5. *Cancellation.* A cancelled `Cx` yields a typed cancellation and no partial mesh.
6. *Every sibling error passes through the refusal conversion*: typed, carrying the upstream reason, never
   retried with weakened options.

**3. Independent audit of the arrays** (`frob_conformance::oracle::tetmesh`, shares no code with the sibling):
every tetrahedron has positive signed volume; every interior face is shared by exactly two tetrahedra and
every boundary face by one; the regions on the two sides of a labelled interface differ and elsewhere agree;
Σ volumes per region equals the region's enclosed volume computed from its surface by the divergence theorem.

**4. Admission lane** (`crates/frob-fsim/tests/admission/fs_mesh.rs`, lane `--features structure`).
1. Facts re-read at the pin and recorded: the pipeline's stage names, the audit's signature, the dependency
   list, the state of the conformity statement in the crate documentation, the feature list.
2. *Unit cube* (8 vertices, 12 triangles), no refinement: 5 or 6 tetrahedra (a cube has both decompositions
   and the eight corners are cospherical, so the count depends on tie-breaking and is recorded, not asserted);
   Σ volumes = 1 to 10⁻¹²; both audits clean; every boundary face lies in a face of the cube (distance 0 to
   10⁻¹²); Euler characteristic of the complex V − E + F − T = 1.
3. *Two regions.* A 2 × 1 × 1 box with an internal facet at x = 1 and one seed on each side: each region has
   volume 1 to 10⁻¹²; no tetrahedron crosses the facet; the interface is covered by faces whose two sides
   carry different regions.
4. *A cavity.* A unit cube with a centred cubic void of side ½: volume 1 − 1/8 = 7/8; Euler characteristic 2.
5. *Quality.* The cube refined with `max_radius_edge = 2` and `max_volume_m3 = 10⁻³`: the census meets both
   bounds or the audit names the elements that do not; at least 1,000 tetrahedra; Σ volumes still 1 to 10⁻¹².
6. *Refusals.* The open box is refused with its four-edge loop; two interpenetrating cubes given as one shell
   are refused with the sibling's reason; a request above the configured cap is refused by name.
7. *Determinism.* The content hash of `export` is identical twice and across worker counts 1 and 8; the class
   observed is recorded.

**5. Record.** `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` entry `fs-mesh (first use, M4)` under `FD-FSIM`:
`covers.crates = ["fs-mesh"]`, commit, command, output hash, receipt hash, the facts, the measured counts,
volumes, angles and hashes, the controls caught, status. No row in the gate register; `frob doctor` lists it.
This record is the admission of `fs-mesh` inside the `structure` group: INT-FSIM-15 (N33b-14, M5) admits the
group's other crates and does not repeat this lane, and the compatibility train re-runs this lane at every pin
move, so the M5 consumers read a record at their own pin. Claim: Known answer; class
`Tested`; tier host.

**6. If admission fails.** The independent check takes the fail-closed default of OPEN-FOUNDRY-1: a second
cut-cell solve at another resolution and offset, every result labelled `check: same-family`; the failing case
goes to the upstream register through BD-037's procedure. No milestone waits.

- `facts_at_pin`; `cube_volume_and_audits`; `cube_boundary_on_faces`; `cube_euler_is_one`.
- `two_region_box_volumes_and_interface`; `cavity_volume_seven_eighths_euler_two`.
- `refined_cube_meets_bounds_or_names_elements`.
- `open_box_refused_with_loop_of_four`; `self_intersection_refused_with_upstream_reason`; `over_cap_refused_by_name`.
- `cancelled_context_returns_no_mesh`.
- `export_hash_stable_across_runs_and_workers`.
- Property test `volume_is_preserved_under_refinement`: for 100 seeded boxes with sides in [0.05, 0.5] m the refined volume equals the product of the sides to 10⁻¹² relative.
`cargo test -p frob-conformance oracle::tetmesh`: `audit_accepts_kuhn_cube` (six tetrahedra of volume 1/6);
`audit_rejects_inverted_tet`; `audit_rejects_unpaired_interior_face`; `audit_rejects_wrong_region_volume`.

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead adds the step `fs_mesh_admission` (due at M4) to `scripts/e2e/airlock_groups.sh`, the script of
N33a-34 that covers every first-use admission and to which later beads add their steps (N33b-21's script is the
two-binary group matrix). The step runs the lane, writes the record under `--record`, asserts through
`frob doctor --json` that it is listed with its receipt hash. The four `planted::` adapters below are plants of
the script header, each turning the step red under `run-all.sh --verify-plants`. Logged with
the shared helper: `cmd` events with exit code and wall time; `metric` events per fixture (`tets`, `volume_m3`
against its expected value, `min_dihedral_deg`, `max_radius_edge`, `boundary_distance_m`, `export_hash`); one
`assert` event per refusal and per control with its `caught` flag; on a mismatch the fixture name, the first
offending tetrahedron or face and the path of the exported arrays. Before M4 the step logs `skipped: not yet
due`. From the user's side the entry is observed at M5 through `frob foundry optimize`, in Foundry's scripts.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: cube volume 1 with five tetrahedra (1/3 + 4 × 1/6) or six (6 × 1/6); two regions of volume 1;
cavity volume 7/8; Euler characteristics 1 and 2. Negative controls (planted adapters, each must fail a test):
`planted::heal-open-surface` closes the open box and meshes it; `planted::drop-audit-violations` reports
`clean` whatever the sibling says (caught on the self-intersecting shell and by the independent audit);
`planted::merge-regions` ignores the internal facet (region volumes 2 and 0); `planted::partial-on-cancel`
returns the tetrahedra built so far. Control of the oracle itself: an export with one tetrahedron's vertex
order swapped fails the independent audit. Metamorphic: translating, rotating by 90° or uniformly scaling the
surface changes volumes by the expected factor and leaves counts and audit verdicts unchanged. Lanes: twice
on one machine, worker counts 1 and 8, debug and release, both platforms, each against the recorded class.

## Considerations

- A clean audit says the mesh is a valid complex. It does not say the mesh follows the surface; that is the
  measured conformity of rule 3, and the reason FD-FSIM-30 exists.
- Sliver-prone inputs (two sheets nearly touching, a member at exactly the minimum length scale) belong to
  the 20-surface experiment, not to this lane; this lane admits the mechanism on shapes with exact answers.
- Meshes of optimized links may be private design data: hashes may be logged, arrays may not.
- A pin move invalidates the record; the compatibility train re-runs this lane with the other admissions.
- Memory: a finalist link at the M5 budget is of order 10⁵ to 10⁶ tetrahedra; `limits()` carries the cap, and
  Foundry jobs that exceed the build envelope use the worker-process mode of the airlock.
