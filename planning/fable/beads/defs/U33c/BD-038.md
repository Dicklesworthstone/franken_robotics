## Background

FrankenRobotics consumes FrankenSim, its referee physics, by path dependency behind the airlock crate
`frob-fsim`. The kill gate KILL-FSIM names what happens if that stops working. Trigger: the path-consumption gate
INT-FSIM-01 cannot pass within two weeks of M0 effort, or its cold check exceeds the machine envelope (one hour,
16 GiB, 8 cores), or upstream breakage exceeds 20% of working days in any four-week window (BET-04's threshold).
Fallback (a): the volatile crates move behind a process boundary: one pinned FrankenSim worker process per device
exchanges whole jobs with the core through the same airlock traits. Fallback (b), only if (a) fails this gate or
the pinned commit can no longer be built on any available toolchain: lift the minimal referee set (`fs-ga`,
`fs-math` deterministic functions, `fs-rand`, the `fs-mbd` articulated module, the `fs-contact` multicontact
solve) with provenance headers and golden tests, record every lifted file in `registries/lifts.toml`, state
"lift-only" in the README, and keep the upstream work items open.

The order matters. The lift set is small only on paper: its two modules sit on a closure of about 1.5 million
lines, and cutting them free of `fs-material` is engineering that fallback (a) avoids entirely.

INT-FSIM-18 runs at M0 even though the fallback is not active, because a fallback that has never run is a hope
(counterexample CX-356: "a fallback that exists on paper is a fallback"). This bead is the gate: it runs the same
referee job in process and through the worker and requires the same bits.

## Scope

In: the end-to-end measurement, its fixtures, its record, and the statement of what the result means for
KILL-FSIM. Out: building the pieces (N33c-10 protocol, N33c-11 client, N33c-12 worker, N33c-13 supervisor); the
KILL-FSIM decision itself, recorded as an ADR at the M0 exit review (BD-044); the numeric trigger for fallback
(b), measured at M2 (N33c-14).

## Technical approach

1. **The job.** A 2,000-step referee rollout of the built-in G1 humanoid model (the catalog builder reached
   through `frob-fsim`'s `RefereeDynamics`, as in BD-007), from its reference state under a fixed seeded control
   schedule, step 1 ms (two seconds of simulated time), `chunk_steps` = 250, outputs: state columns, events,
   per-chunk digests and energy-ledger rows. The job's canonical bytes are stored as a fixture
   (`tests/fixtures/suite/g1_rollout_2000.job`) with its `job_hash`.
2. **Two builds from one tree.** `frob` in mode `inproc`; `frob` in mode `remote` together with
   `frob-fsim-worker` built in the `worker/` workspace. Both under the same resolution receipt, on clean sibling
   trees (a dirty tree downgrades the result to a development record and no PASS is written).
3. **Measurements (TARGET thresholds).**
   1. *Digest equality.* The eight chunk digests through the worker equal, bit for bit, those of the same job
      run in process.
   2. *Kill and resubmit.* The worker is killed with SIGKILL after chunk k (k drawn from a seeded list covering
      early, middle and late: 0, 3, 6) and the job resubmitted; the digests are the same.
   3. *Overhead.* Fixed overhead per job (submission to first step, plus teardown), measured on a zero-step job
      over 100 repetitions: at most 50 ms per job (median and 95th percentile recorded). On jobs of one second
      of wall time or more: (wall time through the worker − wall time in process) / wall time in process ≤ 5%,
      measured as a paired difference over 20 alternating repetitions.
   4. *Negative control.* A worker built at a different commit refuses the job (`FROB-REF-791`).
4. **Record.** An admission record in `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`: gate, date, repository commit, receipt hash, the
   commands with their outputs or output hashes, the eight digests, the kill points, the overhead figures with
   units, the control's refusal envelope, and the verdict. A row in `registries/gates.toml` with the gate's
   `claim_id`. The record states the Cargo form the `worker/` workspace used (manifest-only binary path or the
   one-line `main.rs`).
5. **What a failure means.** If digest equality or kill-and-resubmit fails after one repair cycle, fallback (a)
   is not available; the M0 exit review must then record that KILL-FSIM's only remaining fallback is the lift,
   and if INT-FSIM-01 has also failed the program re-plans before M1 (the stop decision of M0). If only the
   overhead targets fail, the fallback works and is slow: the record says so, and the M2 trigger measurement
   (N33c-14) decides whether it can do the referee's job.

Evidence class of this gate: a known answer of tier G5 (digest equality on the referee). Determinism: the referee
claims same-platform determinism; the comparison is made on one machine at a time, on both machines.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The G1 digest through the worker equals the in-process digest: all eight chunk digests of the 2,000-step rollout, bit for bit. (Seed done-condition, first half; KA-780.)
- [ ] After killing the worker mid-job at each of the three kill points and resubmitting, the digests are unchanged.
- [ ] A worker at a different pin is refused with `FROB-REF-791`, exit code 5, before any step runs. (Seed done-condition, second half.)
- [ ] Fixed overhead per job is at most 50 ms and at most 5% of wall time on jobs of one second or more, or the measured values are recorded as failing those targets.
- [ ] The run leaves no orphan worker process, and the worker's lockfile hash is in the receipt.
- [ ] The ledger of the remote runs records every worker start, death, restart, stop and refusal with pid and receipt (one entry per event the supervisor emitted).
- [ ] The admission record and the gate row exist, cite the receipt, and name the negative control that was caught.
- [ ] The same script passes on `trj` and on the Mac (each compared with its own in-process run).
- [ ] A perturbed job (one seed changed) changes at least one chunk digest in both modes, and the two modes still agree with each other.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The unit tests of N33c-10 to N33c-13 are prerequisites and are re-run by the script. This bead adds, in
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/suite_worker.rs`:
- `g1_job_fixture_hash`: the stored job fixture hashes to its recorded `job_hash`.
- `inproc_digests_stable`: two in-process runs of the fixture give the same eight digests (otherwise the comparison with the worker means nothing).
- `metamorphic_mr37`: for 20 seeded variations of the control schedule, in-process and worker digests agree.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_worker_fallback.sh`:
1. `cargo xtask check-stack` (receipt verified; drift classes logged).
2. Build, through the offload shim, `frob` in mode `inproc` (`cargo build --release -p frob`), `frob` in mode
   `remote` (`cargo build --release -p frob --no-default-features --features referee-remote`, the switch of
   N33c-11, into a separate target directory) and `frob-fsim-worker` (`scripts/build/worker.sh`, N33c-12).
3. `frob sim run g1 --tier referee --seconds 2 --json` in process (the verb of BD-024); store chunk digests.
4. The same command with the `remote` binary and `--referee-worker <path>`; compare digests.
5. Three kill-and-resubmit runs.
6. The overhead measurements.
7. Rebuild the worker under a different receipt; expect exit 5 and `FROB-REF-791`.
8. Orphan check; then read the ledger of the remote runs and assert that the supervisor's worker events (start,
   death, restart, stop, refusal; N33c-13) are recorded there with pid and receipt.
The script sources the shared helper of STD-E2E and writes `target/e2e-logs/suite_worker_fallback/<utc>.ndjson`
plus a transcript. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead: BD-038`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` lines carry the exact
command, exit code and wall time; the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier `referee`, determinism,
identities, seed) is copied from each `frob --json` answer; `metric` lines carry `overhead_fixed_ms` (threshold
50), `overhead_fraction` (threshold 0.05) and each chunk digest; `refusal` lines carry code, pointer, observed and
required; on a digest mismatch the first differing chunk index, the first differing column and row, and the paths
of both chunk files. The `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and `gates: [INT-FSIM-18]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-780: chunk digests equal, bit for bit, to the same job run in process; the same after killing the worker
  mid-job and resubmitting; fixed overhead at most 50 ms per job and at most 5% of wall time on jobs of one
  second or more. Tolerance: exact for digests. Tier G5, referee. Negative control: a worker built at a
  different commit refuses the job.
- CX-356: the fallback is exercised although inactive.
- MR-37 with its registered mutation (a worker built at a different commit).
- Chaos row "worker fallback": the FrankenSim worker killed mid-job and resubmitted; oracle: bit-equal chunk
  digests.
- Planted defects the gate must catch, each run once as a self-test of the script: a worker patched to add one
  unit in the last place to one state column of chunk 4 (digest comparison must fail and name chunk 4); a client
  that skips the receipt check (the different-commit control must then fail the script).
- Lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; `trj` and the Mac, each against itself.

## Considerations

- The measured overhead prices fallback (a) for RISK-22 (the FrankenSim closure makes builds slow and the lift
  expensive) and feeds the M2 trigger: if shadowing through the worker falls below 0.1% of device world-seconds
  at the M2 batch size, the worker cannot do the referee's job and the lift is considered.
- This gate needs a working in-process build to compare against. If INT-FSIM-01 itself fails, the comparison
  reference is a run inside FrankenSim's own tree at the pinned commit, and the record says so.
- No pass is recorded on a dirty sibling tree (OPEN-SUITE-3); with 32 modified paths in FrankenSim's checkout on
  the day of planning, a clean export of the pinned commit may be needed for the gate run.
- The run stays inside the machine envelope; the cold build of the worker is the expensive part and is recorded.
