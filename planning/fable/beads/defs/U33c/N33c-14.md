## Background

KILL-FSIM is the kill gate for consuming FrankenSim, the referee physics, by path. Its fallbacks are ordered:
(a) a pinned FrankenSim worker process exchanging whole jobs through the airlock traits; (b) only if (a) fails,
a lift of the minimal referee set with provenance headers and golden tests. The gate is decided at M0 and then
rolling. Fallback (a) is proven to function by the gate INT-FSIM-18 at M0. Whether it is good enough to do the
referee's job is a different question, and the design gives it a number too.

**The trigger for fallback (b) (DESIGN).** If shadowing through the worker falls below 0.1% of device
world-seconds at the M2 batch size, the worker cannot do the referee's job and the lift is considered. The figure
0.1% is the bottom of the range the performance arithmetic gives for a CPU referee: a referee at 0.2 to 1.0 times
real time per core on 24 cores can shadow about 0.1 to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds across device rates from 1,000
world-seconds per second upward (HYPOTHESIS, measured at M2). The referee is a sampler by necessity; a worker
that cannot reach even the bottom of that range adds nothing to trust.

The terms: a *world-second* is one second of simulated time in one world. *Shadowing* is the sampled background
referee: a fraction of worlds of a device run, chosen by address hash, is re-run on the referee from snapshots and
compared. *Teleport* is a referee job whose initial state is a snapshot taken on a device tier. *Promotion* is the
rule by which a result earns a higher trust level after referee agreement. The worker fallback is designed to
preserve all three, because each is a whole job.

## Scope

In: the M2 measurement of the shadowing share through the worker; tests that teleport, shadow sampling and a
promotion-shaped job run in mode `remote` with results equal to mode `inproc`; the decision record. Out: the
shadow sampler (BD-214), teleport (BD-204) and promotion (BD-263) themselves; the lift (it has no bead until this
decision asks for one).

## Technical approach

1. **What is measured.** On each machine, for workloads Q (quadruped, 8,192 worlds) and H (humanoid, 4,096
   worlds) at the M2 batch sizes, for a device run of at least 60 s of wall time:
   - D = device world-seconds produced per wall second (worlds × step × steps per second);
   - W = referee world-seconds per wall second completed through workers (one worker per process slot, slots up
     to the core limit of the envelope in force, default 8 cores);
   - the share s = W / D, as a paired measurement over 5 repetitions, with the same quantity measured in mode
     `inproc` (s_in) for comparison;
   - the per-job fixed overhead and the bytes per snapshot crossing the boundary.
2. **Decision rule.**
   - s ≥ 0.1% on both workloads: fallback (a) can do the referee's job. Recorded; nothing changes.
   - s < 0.1% on either workload while s_in ≥ 0.1%: the process boundary is the cause. One repair cycle is
     allowed (batch more worlds per job; larger chunks; binary snapshot blocks). If still below: the lift is
     considered, which means a decision bead for fallback (b) is opened with this record as its evidence, priced
     against a closure of about 1.5 million lines.
   - s < 0.1% and s_in < 0.1%: the referee itself is too slow at this batch size; the boundary is not the cause
     and the lift would not help. Recorded as a referee-speed finding for the trust-ladder owners.
   - If fallback (a) is not active (the usual case), the outcome is recorded as readiness: the number that would
     apply if KILL-FSIM fired tomorrow.
3. **Capabilities preserved, as tests.**
   1. *Teleport.* A snapshot taken at the commit fence of a `batch-cpu` run is submitted as the `initial_states`
      block of a referee job in mode `remote`; chunk digests equal those of the same teleport in mode `inproc`.
   2. *Shadow sampling.* The background referee, configured to use mode `remote`, reports the same one-step
      defects for the same sampled addresses as in mode `inproc`, and the coverage figure on the receipt is the
      share actually completed.
   3. *Promotion-shaped job.* A batch of referee rollouts keyed by world address (the evidence a promotion
      reads) returns identical per-world digests in both modes.
4. **Labels.** Results obtained through a worker carry the worker's receipt. When the worker is at a different
   receipt than the core (an old pin), they are valid referee results and are refused as same-stack evidence
   without revalidation (`FROB-REF-890`); the shadow comparison itself is a cross-tier comparison and is
   unaffected.
5. **Record.** The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FSIM-M2.md`, entered at the M2 exit review with the fields every
   kill-gate decision carries (the trigger as registered, each measured value with its receipt hash beside the
   trigger's number, the outcome, the fallback in force, the beads re-read, the recording session): the measured shares with intervals, the envelope class under which
   they were measured, the decision, and for a "lift considered" outcome the list of files the lift would need
   (`fs-ga`, `fs-math` deterministic functions, `fs-rand`, the `fs-mbd` articulated module, the `fs-contact`
   multicontact solve) with their closure sizes re-counted at the pin.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] s and s_in are recorded for workloads Q and H on each machine that has the device tier, with units, repetition count and the envelope in force.
- [ ] The decision rule's outcome is written in `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-FSIM-M2.md` and referenced from the KILL-FSIM row of the gate register.
- [ ] Teleport through the worker yields chunk digests equal to the in-process teleport.
- [ ] Shadow sampling through the worker reports the same defects for the same addresses as in process, on a run with a planted one-step defect.
- [ ] A batch of 256 address-keyed referee rollouts returns identical per-world digests in both modes.
- [ ] A measurement taken with a worker at a different receipt is labelled with that receipt and is refused as same-stack evidence.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/suite_worker_m2.rs` (real worker, real `batch-cpu` snapshots):
- `teleport_remote_equals_inproc`: one G1 snapshot, 500 steps.
- `shadow_remote_same_defects`: 64 sampled worlds; one world carries a planted perturbation of 10⁻⁶ in one joint velocity at the snapshot; both modes flag exactly that address.
- `batch_rollouts_address_keyed`: 256 worlds; per-world digests equal across modes and independent of the order in which jobs are submitted.
- `share_arithmetic`: with `synthetic` rates D = 4,096 world-seconds per second and W = 8 world-seconds per second, s = 0.195%; with W = 2, s = 0.049% and the rule returns "below".

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_worker_share.sh` uses the two builds of BD-038, each with the background referee's feature
added: `frob` in mode `inproc` (`cargo build --release -p frob --features frontier-shadow`) and `frob` in mode
`remote` (`cargo build --release -p frob --no-default-features --features referee-remote,frontier-shadow`) with
`frob-fsim-worker`. At M2 `fidelity.shadow` is delivered only behind `frontier-shadow` (its verb row's first
milestone is M3; a build without the feature answers `FROB-REF-724`), so the script first asserts that
`frob capabilities --json` of each build lists `fidelity.shadow`, and stops with that assertion failed otherwise. Per workload and repetition (five alternating repetitions of each mode, each call under
a key of its own) it starts the device run (`frob sim run --workload Q --worlds 8192 --seconds 60 --tier gpu-cuda
--key ws-<mode>-Q-<rep> --json`; H with 4,096 worlds; `--tier batch-cpu` where no device is present), attaches
the background referee (`frob fidelity shadow start --job <job> --p <p> --cpu-cores 8 --tier referee --json`,
with the `fidelity:admin` scope), reads the share actually completed (`frob fidelity shadow status --job <job>
--json`, field `referee_coverage`) and computes s and s_in. The three capability tests run in both builds: teleport (`frob sim replay --run
<run> --world <w> --tier referee --key ws-tp-<mode> --json`), shadow sampling on a run with the planted one-step
defect, and the 256 address-keyed referee rollouts (`frob sim run <design> --task <t> --worlds 256 --tier referee
--key ws-roll-<mode> --json`). Logging through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/suite_worker_share/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it: every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step` and `event`; `cmd` lines the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's
evidence block; `metric` lines `device_world_seconds_per_s`, `referee_world_seconds_per_s` and `shadow_share`
(threshold 0.001) with their units, the mode, the envelope class and core count, and the worker's receipt;
`assert` lines for the three equalities with both digests; on failure the first world address whose digests
differ between the modes and the paths of both runs. The `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed,
`gates: [KILL-FSIM]` and the rule's outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the share arithmetic above; digest equality of teleport and batch rollouts across modes (MR-37
extended to snapshot starts). Negative controls: a worker throttled to one tenth of its speed (a test flag that
sleeps per step) must drive s below 0.1% and the rule must report it; the planted joint-velocity perturbation
must be flagged by the shadow comparison in both modes; a result from a worker at another receipt offered as
same-stack must be refused. Lanes: twice on one machine; both machines.

## Considerations

- The measurement needs the device tiers of M2; on a machine without one, `batch-cpu` stands in and the record
  says that D is a CPU rate.
- The background referee is a Frontier mechanism (BD-214, feature `frontier-shadow`, pilot N16b-16). If its pilot's
  falsifier held, BD-214 is closed `withdrawn` and this decision still closes (it never waits on a branch that
  may not be taken): W is then measured as the referee world-seconds per wall second completed by the 256
  address-keyed snapshot-start rollouts through the workers at the same slot count, the shadow-sampling
  capability test is recorded `not-run` with BD-214's close cited, and the ADR states which measurement of W it
  used.
- A share of at least 1% on workload H needs the referee on all 36 cores of the Mac, which is above the default
  envelope; under the default envelope of 8 cores the expected share is near the bottom of the range, so this
  trigger may fire for reasons of budget, not of design. The record must name the core count.
- RISK-01 and row 2 of the pre-mortem (FrankenSim moved weekly and adapter churn consumed the effort): the lift
  is priced against a closure of about 1.5 million lines, not against two crates.
- If the lift is ever taken, the README states "lift-only", every lifted file is recorded in
  `registries/lifts.toml` with a provenance header and a golden test, and the upstream work items stay open.
