## Background

asupersync's lab runtime gives FrankenRobotics deterministic simulation testing of its daemon: virtual time,
scheduling by seed, faults injected by schedule, failing schedules minimized to replay fixtures. Before that
harness is trusted as the test of the crash-consistent job protocol, it is admitted by a gate with two halves.
One half shows the harness is deterministic. The other shows it can find a bug of the kind it exists to find. A
harness that passes everything validates nothing.

**INT-ASUP-02** (first needed at M2). Pass criterion (TARGET): the job protocol runs under asupersync's lab
runtime; 100 of 100 repeats of one seed give identical traces; and a planted lost-chunk bug is found within
10,000 explored schedules and replays from its minimized fixture.

An admission is never inherited: the ledger and submit-path simulation tests of M1 use the harness after its own
probe; this gate admits the harness for the device job protocol of M2, by its own record, at the pin of that day.

## Scope
In: the record of INT-ASUP-02 that the gate register dates at M2: the gate's two measurements on the device job
protocol (100 of 100 identical traces from one seed; the planted lost-chunk bug found within 10,000 explored
schedules and replayed from its minimized fixture), and the record. The seed BD-096 holds the M1 record on the
CPU protocol; this one is measured again and does not inherit it.
Out: the harness (N33c-15); the stub and dual run (N33c-16); the M1 simulation tests of the ledger and submit
path with their oracles (BD-096); the protocol itself (BD-210); the measurement made with the admitted
harness on the device job protocol, which is a different thing from its admission: 10⁵ seeded schedules
against the invariants INV-CHRON-01 to -04, await-point coverage of the publish and submit paths, the four
protocol mutants, the dual runs against the real CUDA and Metal workers, and the device rows of GATE-CHRON-04
and GATE-CHRON-11 with the row of GATE-TETHER-08 (N29a-24, which depends on this bead: those rows are
evidence only on a tree where this gate is recorded).

## Technical approach

1. **System under test.** The device job protocol as built: submission with an idempotency key, admission,
   leases, chunk publish against the fixed partition, the committed frontier, cancel, restart. The device is the
   lab-clock stub (64 worlds, 8 chunks, two device slots, so that completions can interleave).
2. **Half one: determinism.** One seed S₀ (recorded), one fault list (a crash inside the publish of chunk 3 and a
   restart). Run 100 times in fresh processes. Capture the lab runtime's trace each time and hash it. Pass: one
   distinct hash among 100. Also recorded: the trace hash under S₀ on the other machine.
3. **Half two: the planted lost-chunk bug.** A test-only build flag `plant-lost-chunk` changes the publisher so
   that when two chunk publishes of one world complete in reversed order, the later-indexed chunk overwrites the
   frontier pointer and the earlier one is never committed. The correct publisher admits a chunk only if its
   predecessor is the frontier. Without reordering the planted build behaves correctly, so no fault-free run
   shows it.
   - Exploration: seeds S₁, S₂, … in a fixed order, each with schedule exploration enabled and the fault menu
     restricted to reorder, delay and crash. Oracle: the frontier is contiguous and every planned extent has
     exactly one row and one receipt at job end.
   - Pass: the oracle fails on the planted build within 10,000 explored schedules. Recorded: the number explored
     when it was found, with the seed.
   - The failing schedule is minimized and stored as a replay fixture; replaying the fixture on the planted build
     fails the same oracle; replaying it on the correct build passes.
4. **Control for half two.** The same 10,000 schedules on the unplanted build produce zero oracle failures. A
   failure here is a real bug and blocks the gate until fixed and re-run.
5. **Record.** Admission record with: asupersync revision and version, the receipt hash, S₀ and its trace hash,
   the count 100 of 100, the schedules explored before detection, the fixture's hash and replay time, the
   control's count, and the dual-run result of N33c-16. The script never writes the gate register: the row of
   INT-ASUP-02 in `registries/gates.toml` is entered from the receipt of the gate's tier run
   (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<S₀> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test <tier> --features gauntlet-mutations --
   INT-ASUP-02`, `<tier>` being the test target the gate record's `tier` field names; the plant
   `plant-lost-chunk` runs as the gate's registered control in that run) with `cargo run -p frob-conformance
   --example record_gate -- --gate INT-ASUP-02 --receipt <hash>`, by a session that did not implement the
   protocol (one recorder per gate; this bead is the one that measures INT-ASUP-02 at M2). No PASS on a dirty
   asupersync tree.
6. **If it fails.** After one repair cycle: the fallback of N33c-15 (a hand-written virtual clock with chaos
   tests at kernel-batch boundaries) comes into force; fault injection is kept and schedule exploration is lost;
   the chaos target for the daemon (every await point on the publish and submit paths hit by a crash fault) is
   then met by enumerated injection points instead of exploration. DESIGN, stated identically in N29a-24,
   whose device row of GATE-CHRON-11 carries the coverage figure: in the fallback a crash fault is injected at
   each point of the committed await-point list (`crates/frob-conformance/chaos/await_points.toml`) in turn,
   and coverage is still measured as (points hit, points listed) with the threshold of equality; it is not
   reported as unmeasured. What the fallback records as reduced is the exploration: the number of enumerated
   injection schedules in place of 10⁵ explored ones. Reason: a crash at a named await point is fault
   injection, which the fallback keeps, and an unmeasured coverage figure would let a new await point through
   with no crash test.

Budget: 10,000 stub schedules run in seconds to minutes; the whole gate stays far inside the machine envelope.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 100 of 100 runs of seed S₀ give one trace hash.
- [ ] The planted lost-chunk bug is detected within 10,000 explored schedules; the count and seed are recorded.
- [ ] The minimized fixture replays to the same oracle failure on the planted build in under one second, and passes on the correct build.
- [ ] The control (unplanted build, same schedules) has zero oracle failures.
- [ ] The dual-run scenario of N33c-16 passed on this tree, and its result is cited.
- [ ] The admission record exists and the gate row was entered with `record_gate` from the tier run's receipt; both cite the receipt and name the negative control caught.
- [ ] `cargo tree -d` shows one asupersync package in every workspace at the commit of the record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/int_asup_02.rs`:
- `seed_repeat_10` (a fast subset of half one inside `cargo test`): 10 runs, one trace hash.
- `half_one_run`: one run of the fault list of step 2 under the test's seed; prints the trace hash on one line (the end-to-end script runs it in 100 fresh processes).
- `planted_bug_needs_reorder`: on the planted build, a fault-free schedule passes the oracle (so the plant is not trivially visible).
- `planted_bug_found_small_budget`: found within the full budget; the test records the count.
- `fixture_replays`: the stored fixture fails on the planted build and passes on the correct one.
- `oracle_contiguous_frontier`: a `synthetic` ledger with extents {0, 1, 3} fails the oracle and names extent 2.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_lab_runtime.sh`:
1. The admission probe of N33c-15 and the one-package check, by calling that bead's script
   `scripts/e2e/lab_runtime_admission.sh` and folding its summary in.
2. The dual-run scenario of N33c-16 on the machine's real tiers.
3. Half one: 100 fresh-process runs of S₀, each `FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
   int_asup_02 -- half_one_run --exact`, collecting the trace hash each prints.
4. Half two on the planted build; minimization; fixture replay on both builds.
5. The control on the unplanted build.
6. The gate's tier run, `e2e_tier <tier> INT-ASUP-02 --mutations` (the tier the gate row's `tier` field names),
   logging the receipt hash; then the admission record of step 5 written with N33a-07's writer, its
   `harness_receipt` that hash, and validated. The script records no gate value: the `record_gate` call is made
   from the logged receipt by a session that did not implement the protocol.
The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/suite_lab_runtime/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript. `cmd` events carry each command, exit code and wall time; `metric` events carry
`identical_traces` (value 100, threshold 100, unit runs), `schedules_to_detection` (threshold 10,000, unit
schedules), `fixture_replay_ms` (threshold 1,000) and `control_failures` (threshold 0); `assert` events carry the
trace hash and the fixture hash; on failure, the first run index whose trace hash differs and the two trace file
paths. The `summary` names `gates: [INT-ASUP-02]`. Stub-driven lines carry `fixture: synthetic`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: one trace hash for one seed. The planted lost-chunk bug is the gate's negative control and is
registered in the negative-control registry. A second control tests the first half: a build with a deliberate
source of nondeterminism (a task that yields according to a hash of its heap address) must produce more than one
trace hash in 100 runs, or the determinism check is not looking at the right thing. Lanes: twice on one machine;
debug and release; both machines, with the cross-machine trace hash recorded.

## Considerations

- The plan's own registers place this gate at M2 in the gate register and wire the lab runtime at M1 for the
  ledger tests; this bead follows the register for the gate and leaves the M1 tests to their bead.
- Exploration order is fixed so that "within 10,000" is a reproducible number, not luck; a change to the
  explorer's order is a change to the gate and is recorded.
- Chaos target of the verification program: 10⁵ seeded schedules per release, every await point on the publish
  and submit paths hit by a crash fault at least once, every failing schedule minimized to a regression that
  replays in under a second. This gate admits the instrument; those targets are met by the daemon's own beads.
- RISK-03 (two asupersync versions) would invalidate the admission; the one-package check is part of the record.
