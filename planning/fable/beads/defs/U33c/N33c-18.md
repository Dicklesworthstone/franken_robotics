## Background

The memo graph stores results by content identity in front of every verb executor: compiled subterms, analyses,
controller syntheses, scene compiles, episodes. Its store has a byte budget (64 GB by default, DESIGN) and on
exhaustion it evicts and never blocks. It must evict by something better than recency, because a scan over many
designs touches each key once: under least-recently-used eviction such a scan flushes the hot entries of the
edit loop (the analyses of the design a person or agent is iterating on) and replaces them with entries that
will never be asked for again.

FrankenSQLite's pager solves the same problem for database pages. FACT (read 2026-11-21 at frankensqlite head
`79b735cb265a`; re-read at the pin by the differential oracle): `frankensqlite/crates/fsqlite-pager/src/
s3_fifo.rs` (3,058 lines) implements a three-queue policy: a small admission queue, a main queue with bounded
reinsertion, and a ghost queue of metadata, with an adaptive small share between 5% and 30% and a rollout gate.
Sibling files `arc_cache.rs` and `evalue_eviction.rs` hold two further policies. The state machine is keyed on
`fsqlite_types::PageNumber`, so it cannot be instantiated over content hashes.

**Posture: Pattern** for the policy, written here with the memo graph in `frob-verbs`; **Oracle** for the sibling
module in `frob-conformance` (N33c-20). The upstream work item FD-FSQL-01 (priority P3) would make the sibling's
state machine generic over its key type; when it lands the switch-over rule is: consume it and delete the local
copy. Until then the design is copied, not the code.

Rejected alternatives: plain LRU (scan pollution, above); linking the sibling with a key bijection in production
(a 32-byte hash does not fit a page number, and a lossy mapping in a production path is a correctness hazard);
an unbounded store (never).

## Scope

In: the policy state machine keyed on `Hash` (32-byte BLAKE3), in unit-size mode, with the adaptive small share;
its trace record and replay; its decision log. Out: byte accounting, cost-weighted admission, pins and the LRU
fallback (N33c-19); the differential oracle and the gate (N33c-20); the memo graph's keys (N28b-37), its shadow
recomputation, serving rules and budget clause (N28b-38), and its wiring in front of analyses (BD-111).

## Technical approach

Module `frob_verbs::memo::evict::s3fifo`. Pure state machine: no clock, no I/O, no randomness; given the same
access sequence it makes the same decisions.

1. **State (DESIGN).** Capacity N entries (unit-size mode). Three FIFO queues:
   - S, the small admission queue, target size s·N where s is the small share;
   - M, the main queue;
   - G, the ghost queue: keys only, no values, capacity equal to M's capacity in entries.
   Per entry in S or M: a saturating access counter `freq` in {0, 1, 2, 3}.
2. **Access(key).**
   1. Hit in S or M: `freq ← min(freq + 1, 3)`. Nothing moves.
   2. Miss, key in G: remove from G; insert at the head of M with `freq = 0`.
   3. Miss, key not in G: insert at the head of S with `freq = 0`.
   4. After an insert, while |S| + |M| > N: evict (step 3).
3. **Evict.**
   1. If |S| ≥ s·N: take the tail t of S. If `t.freq` ≥ the promotion threshold p, move t to the head of M
      (clearing `freq`); if M is now over its share, evict from M (step 3.2). Otherwise evict t from the store
      and push its key to the head of G (dropping G's tail if G is full).
   2. Else: take the tail t of M. If `t.freq` > 0 and t has been reinserted fewer than r times, decrement
      `freq`, increment its reinsertion count, and move it to the head of M; repeat with the new tail. Otherwise
      evict t from the store (its key does not enter G).
4. **Adaptive small share.** s is adjusted inside [0.05, 0.30]. The adaptation signal, step and window, the
   promotion threshold p and the reinsertion bound r are taken from the sibling's module **as read at the pin**
   and written into this crate's `contract.toml` with the sibling revision. They are not copied from this bead,
   because the differential oracle requires the eviction sequence to equal the sibling's exactly and the sibling
   is the authority. The canonical published S3-FIFO uses p = 2 and a fixed s = 0.10; those values are the
   starting point if a parameter cannot be identified, and the oracle then shows where the sequences diverge.
5. **Rollout gate.** The sibling gates its policy behind a rollout switch. Here the equivalent is the suite's
   controller rule: no adaptive controller (a cache policy is one) ships without a replay scenario, evidence
   records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback. The policy therefore starts in shadow: LRU acts,
   S3-FIFO runs beside it on the same accesses, both hit ratios are recorded, and the policy is promoted by one
   configuration flag (`memo.policy`, N33c-19) after INT-FSQL-02 passes and demoted by the same flag.
6. **Trace record.** `frob.memo-trace/1` (this bead owns the format; the oracle of N33c-20 and the byte and cost
   layer of N33c-19 read it): a canonical JSON header (`source`: `recorded` with the verb and its arguments'
   hash, or `synthetic` with the generator name; `seed`; `capacity`; the policy parameters; the sibling
   revision), then fixed-width little-endian records of 64 bytes, one per access: the 32-byte key, a 4-byte
   node-kind tag, 4 bytes reserved (zero), the entry size in bytes as u64, and the recorded cost as two f64
   (CPU core-seconds, GPU seconds). The unit-size state machine reads only the key; N33c-19 replays size and
   cost. An eviction log is the sequence of evicted keys with the access index at which each left. Recording is
   off by default and enabled by `--memo-trace <file>` on any verb, so that real edit loops can be captured for
   the oracle. It is a process option of the `frob` skeleton (BD-022), in no verb's schema and not offered over
   MCP: this bead adds `memo-trace` to `PROCESS_OPTIONS` of NJL-01, the list of options every invocation accepts
   whatever the verb. Keys are content hashes and reveal no private-scene
   data by themselves; traces of private scenes are still stored outside the repository.
7. **API.** `fn access(&mut self, key: Hash) -> Access { Hit | Miss { evicted: SmallVec<Hash> } }`,
   `fn contains(&self, key)`, `fn len()`, `fn stats() -> { hits, misses, promotions, reinsertions, ghost_hits,
   small_share }`.

Determinism class: exact. Layer rule: the policy sits in `frob-verbs` (decisions about what to keep live at layer
4 and above), its bytes in `frob-chronicle`; no kernel or step code depends on it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The state machine is deterministic: replaying a recorded trace reproduces the eviction log byte for byte.
- [ ] Invariants hold after every access on 10⁶ random accesses: |S| + |M| ≤ N; a key is in at most one of S, M, G; `freq` ≤ 3; |G| ≤ capacity of M; s ∈ [0.05, 0.30].
- [ ] On the one-pass scan trace (10⁵ distinct keys once each, interleaved with a 100-key hot set accessed every tenth access, N = 1,000), the hot set's hit ratio is at least that under LRU, and no hot key is evicted after its second access.
- [ ] The parameters p, r and the adaptation rule are recorded in `contract.toml` with the sibling revision they were read at.
- [ ] On one access stream the state machine and an LRU reference of the same capacity report both hit ratios side by side; the store's policy switch `memo.policy` and its `shadow` setting, which run this pair in the store, are N33c-19's.
- [ ] `--memo-trace` records a trace that replays offline to the same eviction log.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs memo::evict::s3fifo`:
- `hand_trace_small` (N = 4, S target 1, M share 3, G capacity 3, p = 2, r = 1, fixed share; queues written head first): the thirteen accesses A, B, C, D, E, A, B, D, D, F, F, F, H. Expected evictions: A at access 5 (from S, into G); B at access 6 (A returns from G into M; B leaves S into G); C at access 7 (B returns into M); E at access 10 (D, with `freq` 2, is promoted to M first); A at access 13 (F is promoted, M exceeds its share, A leaves M and does not enter G). Final state: S = [H], M = [F, D, B], G = [E, C].
- `hit_only_bumps_freq`: a hit moves nothing and saturates at 3.
- `ghost_hit_goes_to_main`.
- `main_reinsertion_bounded`: an entry with `freq` = 3 and r = 1 is reinserted once and evicted on the next pass.
- `invariants_random` (property test, 10⁶ seeded accesses over a Zipf-like key distribution).
- `scan_resistance_vs_lru`: the scan trace above; hot-set hit ratio S3-FIFO ≥ LRU.
- `replay_reproduces_eviction_log`.
- `trace_format_roundtrip`: a recorded trace of 1,000 accesses re-reads to the same keys, kinds, sizes and cost bits; every record is 64 bytes; a header with an unknown `source` is rejected.
- `small_share_stays_in_bounds`: under an adversarial alternation of scans and loops, s stays inside [0.05, 0.30].

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_memo_eviction.sh` (N33c-20) records a real trace with `frob design check --memo-trace` over an
edit loop and replays it; this bead's part logs `metric` events `hit_ratio_s3fifo`, `hit_ratio_lru`,
`small_share_final` and `evictions`, with the trace hash.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the thirteen-access hand trace, exact. Negative controls: a variant that moves an entry to the head
on every hit (that is LRU; the scan-resistance test must then show hot-set evictions and fail); a variant that
pushes main-queue evictions into the ghost queue (the hand trace then ends with G = [A, E, C]); a
variant with unbounded reinsertion (the bounded-reinsertion test loops and is cut by its step bound). Lanes:
twice on one machine; debug and release; identical eviction logs on both machines.

## Considerations

- The exact agreement required by the oracle makes this a faithful re-implementation of the sibling's decision
  rule; where the sibling's rule and the published algorithm differ, the sibling wins and the difference is
  written in the contract.
- Entries here differ in size and in recompute cost by orders of magnitude; unit-size mode exists for the
  differential test and for analysis, and production uses the byte and cost rules of N33c-19.
- Two controllers that read the same telemetry need an interference test before both are enabled; this policy
  reads only the access stream.
- If FD-FSQL-01 lands, this module is deleted and the sibling's generic state machine is consumed, after the
  oracle confirms the two agree on the recorded traces.
