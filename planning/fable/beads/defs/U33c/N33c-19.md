## Background

The memo store keeps results by content identity under a byte budget (64 GB by default, DESIGN) and evicts with
the three-queue S3-FIFO policy patterned on FrankenSQLite's pager. A database pager evicts pages: every entry has
the same size and costs about the same to read back. The memo store differs in two ways that matter. Entries
differ in size by orders of magnitude (a mass roll-up is bytes; an episode set is megabytes). And entries differ
in recompute cost by orders of magnitude (an analysis takes milliseconds of CPU; a 2,048-world episode set takes
GPU-seconds). A miss is not equally expensive.

DESIGN: the size accounting is in bytes and the admission rule may refuse a cheap entry. That is a departure from
the sibling's policy, and it is tested separately, not hidden inside the differential test (which runs the bare
state machine in unit-size mode against the sibling). The fallback, if the policy fails its admission gate
INT-FSQL-02: LRU with admission weighted by recompute cost; never an unbounded store.

Three further rules come from the store policy of the memo graph: on exhaustion the store evicts and never
blocks; nodes in the evidence closure of a live claim are pinned; and the store can be deleted at any time
without losing a result that a claim cites, because cited evidence is pinned in the Chronicle ledger, not in the
cache.

## Scope

In: the byte-weighted wrapper around the S3-FIFO state machine; the cost-aware admission rule; pin handling; the
LRU fallback policy with the same admission rule; the policy switch; statistics; the implementation of the
never-waiting interface `admit(node) → Admitted | NotCached(reason)` on which the memo graph's serve path relies
(N28b-38, section 4). Out: the unit-size state machine (N33c-18); the differential oracle (N33c-20); the memo
graph's keys (N28b-37), its serving rules, shadow recomputation and budget clause (N28b-38), and its wiring in
front of analyses (BD-111); the cost estimates themselves (the verb cost model).

## Technical approach

1. **Entry metadata (DESIGN).** `MemoMeta { key: Hash, bytes: u64, cost: Cost { cpu_core_seconds: f64,
   gpu_seconds: f64 }, kind: NodeKind, pinned: bool }`. `cost` is the measured cost of the computation that
   produced the entry, recorded at insertion, in the two units the project keeps apart (CPU core-seconds and GPU
   seconds are never summed; a fixed exchange weight w_gpu, default 20 CPU core-seconds per GPU second, DESIGN
   and recorded in the policy contract, is used only to order entries).
2. **Byte-weighted queues.** The capacities of S, M and the store are in bytes: budget B, small target s·B. An
   insert of an entry of b bytes evicts until used + b ≤ B. The ghost queue stays keyed in entries, with capacity
   equal to the number of entries M held at its last eviction.
3. **Oversize rule.** An entry with b > B/8 (DESIGN) is never admitted to the store; it is served to the caller
   and not cached, and the refusal to cache is counted. One entry must not be able to flush an eighth of the
   store.
4. **Cost-aware admission.** Let ρ(e) = (cost.cpu_core_seconds + w_gpu · cost.gpu_seconds) / bytes, the scalar
   cost saved per byte if the entry is hit once. On insertion of a new entry e that requires evicting victims
   v₁ … v_k chosen by the policy: admit e only if ρ(e) ≥ θ · max over the victims with `freq` > 0 of ρ(v); if no
   victim has `freq` > 0 the entry is admitted. θ defaults to 0.1 (DESIGN). A refused entry's key still enters
   the ghost queue, so a second request for it within the ghost window admits it to M regardless of ρ (it has
   shown reuse). "Cheap" therefore means cheap relative to what it would displace, and a cheap entry that is
   asked for twice is kept.
5. **Pins.** A pinned entry is skipped by eviction: when the tail of a queue is pinned, it is moved to the head
   without changing `freq` and the next tail is considered, at most |queue| times per eviction. Pinned bytes are
   accounted separately (`pinned_bytes`); if pinned bytes alone exceed the budget, the store reports
   `over-budget-by-pins` in its statistics and admits nothing new until pins are released. It never blocks and
   never evicts a pinned entry.
6. **LRU fallback.** `Policy::Lru`: a single queue ordered by last access, byte-weighted, with the same oversize
   rule, the same admission rule (with victims taken from the cold end) and the same pin handling. Selected by
   `memo.policy = "lru" | "s3fifo" | "shadow"` in the store's configuration: at M1, where verbs run in process,
   the `[memo]` table of the `frob` configuration file and the override `--memo-policy <lru|s3fifo|shadow>`, a
   process option of the `frob` skeleton in no verb's schema (this bead adds `memo-policy` to `PROCESS_OPTIONS`
   of NJL-01, as N33c-18 adds `memo-trace`); from M3 the daemon's
   configuration, read by `frobd` (default `shadow` until INT-FSQL-02 passes); `shadow` runs LRU as the acting
   policy and S3-FIFO beside it on the same access stream, recording both hit ratios and byte-weighted cost
   saved.
7. **Never unbounded.** There is no policy value that disables eviction. `--no-memo` disables serving, not the
   bound.
8. **The interface.** Steps 3 to 6 are what `admit(node)` evaluates, without waiting: `Admitted` after the
   victims are evicted, or `NotCached(reason)` with `reason` one of `size` (step 3), `cost` (step 4) or `pins`
   (step 5, `over-budget-by-pins`); the caller returns its computed result either way. This replaces the interim
   store of N28b-38, which refuses admission once the budget is reached and evicts nothing.
9. **Statistics (read by `store.status`).** Per policy: hits, misses, bytes used, pinned bytes, evictions,
   admissions refused by cost, admissions refused by size, ghost admissions, cost saved by hits in both units,
   and the current small share.
10. **Loss model, restated for this layer.** A stale result served costs 100 units against the GPU-seconds of a
   needless recomputation (the ratio of the memo policy's contract); eviction can only cause recomputation,
   never staleness, so an eviction error is always the cheap kind. That is why the admission rule may be
   aggressive.

Determinism: with costs taken from recorded values, the policy is a pure function of the access stream and the
metadata. Serving remains bit-identical or not at all; eviction changes time, never results.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Used bytes never exceed the budget after any insertion, on 10⁶ random inserts with sizes spanning six orders of magnitude; the store never blocks.
- [ ] An entry larger than B/8 is served and not cached, and counted.
- [ ] A cheap, large, never-reused entry is refused admission when it would displace reused expensive entries; the same key requested a second time within the ghost window is admitted.
- [ ] No pinned entry is ever evicted in a 10⁶-access test with 5% of entries pinned; with pins exceeding the budget the store reports `over-budget-by-pins` and keeps serving.
- [ ] Switching `memo.policy` between `lru`, `s3fifo` and `shadow` takes effect without restarting a job and without changing any served result (digests equal).
- [ ] In unit-size mode with admission disabled (θ = 0, no pins) the wrapper's eviction sequence equals the bare state machine's, so the differential test of INT-FSQL-02 is not affected by this layer.
- [ ] The departure from the sibling's policy (bytes, cost admission) has its own tests, listed below, and is described in the crate's contract.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs memo::evict::policy`:
- `bytes_never_exceed_budget` (property, 10⁶ inserts, sizes 10¹ to 10⁷ bytes, B = 10⁸).
- `oversize_not_cached`: b = B/8 + 1.
- `cost_admission_hand_case`: B = 1,000 bytes; residents: ten entries of 100 bytes each with cost 10 core-seconds and `freq` 1 (ρ = 0.1); newcomer of 100 bytes with cost 0.05 core-seconds (ρ = 0.0005 < θ·0.1 = 0.01) is refused; newcomer with cost 2 core-seconds (ρ = 0.02) is admitted.
- `second_request_admits_from_ghost`.
- `gpu_weight_orders_entries`: 1 GPU second outranks 19 CPU core-seconds per byte and not 21, at w_gpu = 20.
- `pinned_never_evicted`, `over_budget_by_pins_reports_and_serves`.
- `unit_mode_equals_bare_state_machine` (the same trace through both; identical eviction logs).
- `lru_fallback_same_rules`: oversize, admission and pins behave identically under `Policy::Lru`.
- `policy_switch_keeps_results`: served bytes before and after a switch are identical.
- `admit_reasons`: through `admit(node)`, the oversize entry is `NotCached(size)`, the refused hand-case newcomer
  `NotCached(cost)`, a newcomer while pins exceed the budget `NotCached(pins)`, the admitted newcomer `Admitted`;
  every call returns without waiting.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_memo_eviction.sh` (N33c-20): runs an edit loop with a store budget small enough to force
eviction under each policy and logs `metric` events `bytes_used`, `pinned_bytes`, `refused_by_cost`,
`refused_by_size`, `cost_saved_cpu_core_seconds`, `cost_saved_gpu_seconds`, and an `assert` event that the
verbs' result digests are identical across the three policy settings.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the cost-admission hand case; the w_gpu ordering case. Negative controls (planted defects): a
wrapper that counts entries instead of bytes (the budget property fails on the first large entry); an admission
rule with the inequality reversed (the hand case flips); an eviction that ignores the pin flag (the pin test
fails within the first thousand accesses); a policy value `none` (must be rejected by the configuration reader).
Lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- θ, w_gpu and the oversize fraction are policy parameters with a calibration duty: the controller rule requires
  a replay scenario and a calibration report before the policy acts. The recorded traces of the oracle bead are
  the replay scenarios; the calibration report compares cost saved under several θ.
- Cost estimates can be wrong (a cold cache makes a cheap analysis look expensive). The rule uses measured cost
  at insertion and the ghost path corrects under-admission after one repeat.
- Two controllers that read the same telemetry need an interference test before both are enabled: the job
  scheduler reads estimates, this policy reads access streams; if either starts to read the other's signal, the
  interference test becomes due.
- Private-scene results are cached like any other; the store lives outside the repository and the cache can be
  purged with the scene.
