## Background

Cloth is a Moonshot [M] evaluation tier of FrankenRobotics: a rug or a curtain simulated as cloth on the CPU
referee, for finalist designs only, never on a critical path. Before any cloth code of this project is trusted it
needs a reference. A sibling has one. FACT (read 2026-11-21 at franken_textiles head `ecb13277aa02`):
`crates/ftx-drape` exists, about 116 thousand lines with `cloth.rs`, `contact.rs`, `integrator.rs`; it takes its
workspace's exact pin `serde_json = "=1.0.151"`.

That pin decides the posture. A Cargo graph holds one version of `serde_json` 1.x. FrankenSim's graph resolves
1.0.154 (through FrankenSciPy, which requires 1.0.154 or later; fastmcp_rust pins `=1.0.154`). The cloth oracle
therefore cannot be a dependency of a crate that also links FrankenSim. **Posture: Oracle; Subprocess [M].**
`ftx-drape` is reached as a worker process that exchanges whole jobs, in its own workspace, driven from
`frob-conformance` behind a flag; never a Cargo dependency. This is the posture already defined for FrankenSim's
own worker fallback, reused. The upstream work item FD-FTX-01 (priority P3) relaxes the pin in franken_textiles;
its switch-over rule: re-run this gate with `ftx-drape` as a dev-dependency of `frob-conformance`, and keep the
worker if `cargo metadata` still reports a conflict.

**Gate INT-FTX-01** (TARGET; first needed at M6), which is also the pilot of the item: a square cloth draped over
a sphere matches `ftx-drape`'s settled shape to its own stated tolerance, with `ftx-drape` run as a worker
process in its own workspace. **Fallback:** no cloth tier; rigid proxies (a rug as a free slab, a cord as a fixed
ridge or a free rod).

## Scope
In: the pilot record, written to the pilot register before the run; the oracle worker (a small workspace outside
every graph of this project that links `ftx-drape`); the job it accepts and its refusal path; the drape fixture;
the invariants of the settled shape that show the oracle was driven correctly; cost; the comparison metric; the
gate record of INT-FTX-01 (the reference half, and the comparison half when a candidate exists) and the funding
recommendation.
Out: this project's own cloth tier (a child of the epic BD-416, opened only if the tier is funded; the island
bead is N19b-21); the relaxation of the pin (FD-FTX-01, upstream, in franken_textiles' repository); the
`serde_json` single-version check and conflict probe (N33c-38), which this bead relies on and does not restate.
This bead replaces the duplicate draft N33a-26; its protocol, invariants and falsifier are folded in below.

## Technical approach

1. **Oracle workspace (DESIGN).** `tools/oracle/ftx-drape-worker/`: its own `[workspace]`, its own `Cargo.lock`,
   its own toolchain as pinned by franken_textiles (`nightly-2026-08-31` at the starting state; it links nothing
   of this project's core, so the one-toolchain rule of the build graph does not bind it, exactly as for other
   development-time oracles). Dependencies: `ftx-drape` by path at the pinned revision, with that workspace's
   own `serde_json = "=1.0.151"`. It is excluded from every workspace that builds a `frob` binary, and
   `xtask check-deps` confirms `ftx-drape` is in none of them.
2. **Wire format.** The framing and canonical encoding of the referee job protocol (length-prefixed frames,
   canonical JSON header written by each side's own writer, binary blocks referenced by length and BLAKE3,
   integers above 2⁵³ as strings). Because the oracle worker cannot link this project's crates (it would import
   the conflicting graph), it carries its own minimal encoder and decoder for three frames: `hello { protocol:
   "frob.oracle-job/1", oracle: "ftx-drape", revision }`, `job { job_hash, cloth, obstacle, material, solver,
   outputs }`, `result { job_hash, vertices (binary, f64 triples), settled: bool, residual, stated_tolerance,
   iterations }`, and `refuse { job_hash, reason }`.
3. **The fixture.** A square cloth of side 1 m, a regular grid of 33 × 33 vertices, held flat and horizontal,
   centred 0.01 m above the top of a fixed rigid sphere of radius 0.25 m whose centre is at the origin, then
   released under gravity 9.81 m/s² along −z with the material and friction values of `ftx-drape`'s own drape
   example at the pinned revision (read from the sibling and recorded; none is invented here). No floor: the
   cloth hangs from the sphere. The run ends when the oracle reports the cloth settled by its own criterion.
4. **The comparison.** Our cloth tier runs the same fixture on the CPU referee to its own settled state. Both
   vertex sets are compared after removing the symmetry freedom of the problem (the fixture is symmetric under
   the dihedral group of the square; a rotation of the settled wrinkle pattern by a multiple of 90° about z or a
   reflection is the same answer): the distance is the minimum over the eight symmetry images of the root mean
   square vertex distance, and the maximum vertex distance under that best image. Pass: both within the tolerance
   `ftx-drape` states for its own settled shapes at the pin (`stated_tolerance` from the `result` frame; the gate
   takes the oracle's number, not one chosen here). Also recorded: the vertical extent of the draped cloth, the
   lowest vertex height, and whether both sides agree that no vertex penetrates the sphere by more than the
   stated contact tolerance.
5. **Order of the pilot.** (a) Build and run the oracle worker alone and store its result as a pinned oracle
   capture (`tests/fixtures/oracle/ftx_drape_square_sphere.result`, with revision, date and capture script;
   never regenerated from the candidate), registered as a row of the oracle register under `tools/oracle`
   (N38a-04), whose hygiene check refuses a capture produced by the candidate. (b) Self-consistency of the oracle: two runs give the same vertices
   within its own tolerance; the fixture at half the grid resolution settles to a shape within twice the
   tolerance after interpolation (recorded, not gating). (c) Only then compare the candidate tier.
6. **Record and decision.** Admission record (franken_textiles revision, the oracle workspace's lockfile hash,
   the `cargo metadata` conflict report from N33c-38, the distances and the tolerance); gate row, entered from the
   receipt of the run with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate INT-FTX-01 --receipt
   <hash>` by this bead, the one that measures the gate (the script never writes the gate register); funding ADR for
   the cloth part of the epic in `docs/adr/` (KNOWN_DRIFTS 4; DESIGN name `docs/adr/ADR-INT-FTX-01-M6.md`), which
   N19b-20 cites in KILL-LIVING's `kind:cloth` entry: PASS → the cloth tier may be offered as an evaluation tier
   labelled by its claim class; FAIL after one repair cycle → no cloth tier, rigid proxies.

**Pilot record and frozen protocol (DESIGN).** Before the run a record is written to
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, owner N38a-09) with status
`registered`: an identifier of the set `CLOTH` (`PILOT-CLOTH-<nn>`, the register's next free number,
append-only; not `PILOT-C-<nn>`, which N38a-09's grammar reserves for the pilot of construction C-nn); mechanism,
the cloth evaluation tier's oracle; tier `M`; `owner = "§33.15"`; bead N33c-28, work package WP-73; `protocol` {path `docs/pilots/cloth-oracle.md`,
BLAKE3, frozen date, ledger sequence}; the falsifier below; the ceiling (an evaluation tier for finalists only);
`tool` { name = "ftx-drape", pin = franken_textiles at its revision of the day on its own toolchain file, site =
"host" } (the worker runs on the host out of process; `development-oracle` is N38a-06's lane of a pinned
incumbent simulator, whose results fund provisionally, are queued for a re-run on the CPU referee and support no
gate, none of which fits a sibling oracle that is the reference half of INT-FTX-01); `cost` { cpu_core_h, gpu_h = "0", wall_h, envelope = "default" }: at most one agent-day,
CPU only, each run under a timeout inside E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores and one hour); `fallback` { ships =
"no cloth tier; rigid proxies and hinged plates", kill_gate = "KILL-LIVING" }. The result is appended as a
`[[pilot.result]]` (the reference digest, the reproducibility finding, the four invariants' values, cost, and the
comparison distances when a candidate exists; verdict; receipt). Folded into the protocol:
- *Reproducibility.* The job is run three times. Pass: identical digests, or, if the sibling does not claim bit
  reproducibility, a maximum vertex difference below its stated tolerance; which of the two holds is recorded as
  a fact about the oracle.
- *Invariants of the settled shape*, properties any correct drape of a centred square over a centred sphere has,
  used to catch a mis-driven oracle: (a) the shape is invariant under the eight symmetries of the square to
  within twice the stated tolerance; (b) no vertex is inside the sphere by more than the contact tolerance, and
  the centre vertex rests on the sphere's top within that tolerance plus the cloth's thickness; (c) the mean
  edge strain is below the bound implied by the stretch stiffness and the cloth's weight, as the sibling's own
  report states it (recorded); (d) corners hang lower than edge midpoints, which hang lower than the centre.
- *Cost.* Wall time and peak memory per run.
- *Refusal path.* A job with a negative radius is answered by a `refuse` frame carrying the sibling's reason;
  the worker never substitutes a default.
- *Idempotence.* `job_hash` is the BLAKE3 of the job's canonical bytes; resubmitting the same job returns the
  same result.

**Falsifier (any one).** The worker cannot be built without adding a dependency to the core graph; runs are not
reproducible within the oracle's own tolerance; an invariant fails; a run exceeds the machine envelope. If it
holds, the cloth island is not funded and the row reads "no cloth tier".

**The gate without a candidate (DESIGN).** INT-FTX-01 compares a candidate cloth model of this project with the
reference, and a candidate exists only if the Moonshot tier is funded. Until then the admission record holds the
reference half with the reference digest and `status: not-run`, reason "no candidate; the tier is not funded";
steps 4 and 5(c) run when a candidate exists.

Evidence: an oracle comparison of tier G2 (against a development-time reference); nothing here is a Reality
claim.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance cloth_oracle` (and the oracle workspace's own tests):
- `frame_codec_interop`: frames written by the oracle worker's encoder decode with this project's decoder and the reverse, byte-identical after re-encoding.
- `symmetry_min_distance`: a `synthetic` vertex set and its 90° rotation have distance 0 under the symmetry minimum and a positive distance without it.
- `rms_and_max_distance_hand_case`: two 2 × 2 grids differing by 0.01 m at one vertex give maximum 0.01 m and root mean square 0.005 m.
- `oracle_capture_hash`: the stored capture hashes to its manifest row.
- `penetration_check`: a vertex at radius 0.24 m is flagged at a contact tolerance of 1 mm.
- `job_hash_is_canonical`: two key orders of the same job give the same hash.
- `drape_reproducible_three_runs`; `drape_symmetry_eightfold` (maximum deviation under each of the eight symmetries at most twice the tolerance); `drape_ordering` (corner height < edge-midpoint height < centre height).
- `malformed_job_refused`.
- `harness_without_flag_reports_not_run`: the conformance suite built without the flag `oracle-ftx` lists the oracle as absent, not as passed.

In `crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_register.rs` (N38a-09), reading the real `pilots.toml`:
- `suite_cloth_oracle_pilot_row_complete`: falsifier, ceiling, fallback with kill gate KILL-LIVING, tool and pin,
  a frozen protocol whose file hashes to the registered BLAKE3, and (once run) a result.
- `suite_cloth_oracle_pilot_registered_before_result`: the result's date and ledger sequence follow the
  registration, and its protocol hash equals the registered one.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_cloth_oracle.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N33c-28 milestone=M6 labels=suite,pilot,moonshot
gates=INT-FTX-01 seed=0 needs=cpu plants=flat-sheet-worker,sphere-shift-5cm`; sources the STD-E2E helper; writes
`target/e2e-logs/suite_cloth_oracle/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript): builds the oracle worker through the
offload shim in its own target directory; runs the fixture job three times; compares against the stored
capture; checks the four invariants, the refusal of the negative-radius job and the two planted cases (a worker
returning the flat sheet; the sphere shifted by 5 cm), each of which must fail; runs `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test pilot_register -- suite_cloth_oracle`; then, only when a candidate cloth tier exists, runs
it through `frob --json sim run <the candidate's drape design> --scene <the drape fixture of step 3, as the
candidate tier's scene> --tier referee --worlds 1 --seconds <settle time> --seed 1 --key cloth-cand-1` and compares (without a
candidate the step is logged `not-run` with the reason "no candidate; the tier is not funded", and the summary
outcome is `reference-only`). Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code
and wall time. It logs `cmd` events, the `hello` frame's
revision, `metric` events `rms_distance_m` and `max_distance_m` with the oracle's `stated_tolerance` as
threshold, `settled` flags and iteration counts from both sides, wall time and peak memory per run, the
invariant values as `metric` events, and on failure the index and both positions of the worst vertex with the
symmetry image used. The `summary` names `gates: [INT-FTX-01]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the hand cases of the distance helper; by symmetry the settled centre vertex lies on the z axis
at height 0.25 m plus half the cloth thickness within the contact tolerance, on both sides. Negative controls: a
candidate run with gravity reversed must fail by a large margin; a candidate with the sphere radius changed to
0.26 m must fail (the comparison is sensitive at the centimetre scale); a comparison that skips the symmetry
minimum must report a spurious failure on a rotated-but-equal shape, which the unit test demonstrates. Lanes:
oracle twice on one machine; both machines recorded.

## Considerations
- Priority 4: a Moonshot, whatever its milestone. At most four Moonshot items are active at once in any
  milestone.
- Cloth draping has many wrinkle patterns of nearly equal energy; exact agreement between two solvers is not
  expected, which is why the tolerance is the oracle's own and why the symmetry minimum is taken. If the oracle's
  stated tolerance is tighter than its own run-to-run repeatability, the pilot reports that as its finding.
- Risk from the pre-mortem's late rows: an exact version pin in a sibling blocks an oracle. Mitigation here: the
  oracle runs as a worker process in its own workspace; the pin move is an upstream work item.
- The oracle worker is a foreign-graph development tool; it appears in no production dependency graph.
- Negative controls folded from the duplicate draft: a job with the sphere shifted by 5 cm must break the symmetry
  check; a worker that returns the initial flat sheet (planted) must fail the contact and ordering invariants.
- The oracle's material model is the sibling's: a drape that matches it says the candidate agrees with one
  implicit-cloth implementation, not with a physical rug. Reality stays a separate claim.
- franken_textiles may hold private pattern data in its own repository; the worker reads only the job on its
  input.
- This pilot is at backlog priority and is run only when the epic BD-416 comes up for a funding decision.
