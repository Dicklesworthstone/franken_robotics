## Background

FrankenRobotics records, with every result, what it is evidence for. One part of that record is the stack it was
computed on: which sibling revisions, which toolchain, which lockfiles. The resolution receipt captures those in
one canonical file, identified by its BLAKE3 hash. A result in the Chronicle ledger carries the receipt hash of
the binary that produced it, which is what lets a later reader decide whether two results may be compared as
results of the same stack.

Two kinds of result need special care, and the suite chapter makes an invariant of them:

- **INV-SUITE-05** A result that crossed the multiphysics bridge or came from a worker at a different receipt is
  never merged into a throughput-tier or same-stack claim (enforced by the evidence types, tested in the
  verification program).

The multiphysics bridge is the gated airlock feature that reaches FrankenSim's lattice-Boltzmann and coupled
multiphysics crates; a result that crossed it is labelled evaluation tier and cannot enter a throughput claim.
A worker at a different receipt is the referee worker process kept on an older FrankenSim pin while the core
moves: its results are real referee results, computed by different code than the core's own stack.

The refusal that guards the boundary:

| Code | Name | When |
|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-890` | `stack-mismatch` | Evidence recorded under another stack receipt is offered as same-stack evidence without revalidation |

The tempting shortcut is to treat a receipt as bookkeeping and pool whatever results share a design and a
scene. A paired comparison whose two arms ran on different FrankenSim commits measures the commits as much as
the designs; a ratio of times across a pin move or a toolchain train is not one measurement.

## Scope

In: the `StackId` field of the evidence block; the same-stack rule in the operations that pool or pair results;
the bridge and worker labels as types; the refusal with payload and repairs; the tests of INV-SUITE-05. Out: the
receipt itself (BD-001, BD-004); revalidation (BD-292); the bridge (BD-413); the worker (N33c-12).

## Technical approach

1. **Stack identity (DESIGN, `frob_core::evidence`).** `StackId { receipt: Hash, referee: RefereeStack }` with
   `RefereeStack = InProc | Remote { worker_receipt: Hash } | None`. Every envelope's `evidence` block and every
   ledger row carries `stack`. A binary's own receipt hash is compiled in (the same fail-closed build variable
   the worker uses). `referee` is `Remote` for any result with a referee contribution computed by a worker.
2. **Bridge label.** `crossed_bridge: bool` in the evidence block, set by the airlock when any input passed
   through the `multiphysics` feature group. It is sticky under composition: a result derived from one with the
   label has the label.
3. **Same-stack predicate.** `same_stack(a, b)` holds iff `a.receipt == b.receipt` and the referee fields are
   equal, where `InProc` and `Remote { worker_receipt }` are equal only if `worker_receipt` equals the core
   receipt. Results with `RefereeStack::None` (no referee contribution) compare on `receipt` alone.
4. **Where the predicate is enforced (each a typed operation that cannot be called with mixed inputs without an
   explicit conversion).**
   - Pooling episodes into one estimate; pairing two arms of a comparison; serving a memoized result as a hit
     (the memo key already carries the stack-lock digest); promotion evidence; any performance ratio.
   - A *throughput-tier claim* (a Performance or Parity figure) additionally refuses any input with
     `crossed_bridge` or with `RefereeStack::Remote` at a different receipt.
   - Cross-tier comparisons that are by design between different code (a device tier against the referee; the
     shadow sampler) are not same-stack claims and are typed as such; they record both stack identities.
5. **The claim product.** The stack identity joins the factors of the claim product as an equality constraint:
   the product of two evidence items is defined only when `same_stack` holds or when the operation is typed
   cross-stack. A cross-stack product yields the class `cross-stack`, which no same-stack or throughput claim
   accepts.
6. **The refusal.** `refusal { code: "FROB-REF-890", message: "evidence recorded under another stack receipt
   offered as same-stack evidence", pointer: "evidence/<index>/stack", observed: the offered receipt (and worker
   receipt), required: the current receipt, unit: "", affected_claims: the claims of the request, retry:
   "after-change", repairs: [ 1. revalidate the result under the current stack (`frob result revalidate <id>`),
   which recomputes what the pin move invalidated; 2. recompute; 3. present the result as cross-stack evidence
   with both receipts shown (allowed for display and export, not for pooling or pairing) ] }`. Exit code 3
   (refused input or question: the `8xx` family other than conflicts).
7. **Revalidation.** A revalidated result carries a new evidence item under the current receipt and a
   `derived-from` edge to the old one; the old result is never rewritten.
8. **Ledger.** Every `FROB-REF-890` is logged with both receipts. Reports print the receipt (short hash) beside
   every pooled number.

Determinism: the rule is exact. Evidence class: this mechanism constrains classes; it produces none.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every envelope and ledger row produced by the M1 verbs carries a `stack` field; serialization of a result without it fails.
- [ ] Pooling, pairing, memo serving and promotion refuse mixed-receipt inputs with `FROB-REF-890`, exit 3, naming the offending evidence item and both receipts.
- [ ] A throughput-tier claim refuses an input with `crossed_bridge` and an input from a worker at a different receipt.
- [ ] A device-versus-referee comparison across receipts is accepted as a cross-tier comparison and records both stack identities.
- [ ] The label `crossed_bridge` survives every composition in the test corpus; no operation clears it.
- [ ] The refusal row for `FROB-REF-890` exists in `registries/refusals.toml` with its three ranked repairs and exit code 3.
- [ ] Converting a cross-stack product into a same-stack claim fails to compile.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-core evidence::stack` and `cargo test -p frob-chronicle stack`:
- `same_stack_table`: nine pairs over {InProc, Remote(same), Remote(other), None} × two receipts with the expected truth table.
- `pool_mixed_receipts_refuses_890`: 100 episodes under receipt R₁ and one under R₂; the refusal's `pointer` names the odd one.
- `pair_mixed_receipts_refuses_890`.
- `memo_hit_other_stack_not_served`: a hit recorded under R₁ is a miss under R₂.
- `throughput_refuses_bridge_and_foreign_worker`.
- `cross_tier_comparison_allowed_and_labelled`.
- `bridge_label_sticky` (property test, INV-SUITE-05): for 10⁴ random composition trees over evidence items with random labels, the root has `crossed_bridge` iff some leaf has it, and the root is same-stack iff all leaves are pairwise same-stack.
- `compile_fail/cross_stack_into_same_stack.rs`.
- `revalidated_result_has_new_evidence_and_edge`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_refusals.sh` (N33-REF): produces episodes with the real `frob` binary; rebuilds the binary
under a second receipt (a scratch tree with one sibling commit moved, or the build variable set to a second
verified receipt) and produces more; requests a pooled estimate over both through `frob eval run`-style pooling
or the M1 study verb; expects exit 3 and `FROB-REF-890`. It logs a `refusal` event with code, pointer, observed,
required and the repairs offered, the two receipt hashes, and an `assert` event that the same request restricted
to one receipt succeeds with identical numbers on a rerun.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the truth table of `same_stack`. Negative controls (planted defects the tests must catch): a
pooling function that compares only design and scene identities (the mixed-receipt test fails it); a composition
that drops `crossed_bridge` when merging with an unlabelled item (the property test fails within a few cases); a
memo store keyed without the stack digest (the other-stack hit is served and the test fails). These are
registered in the negative-control registry under INV-SUITE-05. Lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release.

## Considerations

- The pin moves through a compatibility train (move the pin, write a new receipt, rerun the admission and parity
  gates, relock); after a train, everything recorded before is other-stack evidence until revalidated. That cost
  is intended, and it is why trains are rare and never happen during the ten-fold tournament.
- A development build on a dirty sibling tree has a receipt with a tree hash; two such builds almost never share
  a receipt, so pooled development results are refused across rebuilds. That is the fail-closed reading of
  OPEN-SUITE-3.
- Interaction with the hardware fingerprint: a result is also bound to the device fingerprint; stack identity
  and fingerprint are separate equalities and both must hold for a same-backend claim.
- Until the revalidation engine exists (M3), repair 1 is offered as "recompute".
