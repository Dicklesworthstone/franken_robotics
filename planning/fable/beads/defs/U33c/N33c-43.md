## Background

Actuator and transmission models in FrankenRobotics are composed from ports that exchange effort and flow, and on
the referee tier the composition uses FrankenSim's port-Hamiltonian crate `fs-phs` through the airlock. A
transformer (a gear, a belt, a screw) couples two ports with a modulus. A lossless transformer conserves power
under either sign of its modulus, so a power balance cannot detect a reversed gear. That makes a sign convention
between two entry points of one crate a real hazard.

FACT (confirmed by the source comment, read on 2026-11-21 at FrankenSim head `483d09c682f2`): in
`fs-phs/src/interconnection.rs:91` the legacy `transformer` carries the comment "The legacy transformer has the
opposite sign to the canonical map", the canonical map being `interconnect_mapped`. FACT (reported, not re-run):
with two unit scalar stores, efforts (2, 3) and coefficient 4, one entry point gives rates (12, −8) and the other
(−12, 8); both satisfy 2·12 + 3·(−8) = 0, and a coefficient of −4 in one convention reproduces the other.

DESIGN (this project's rule for direction): every transformer, gear and coupling joins two labelled ports with an
authored direction: positive rotation of the driving port corresponds to a stated sign of rotation at the driven
port. The **signed motion witness** checks it: a unit positive effort on the driving port, with the driven port
free and at rest, must produce a rate of the authored sign at the driven port. A reversed witness is refused
under the port-sign refusal. Interim posture toward the sibling: `frob-fsim` reaches only `interconnect_mapped`
and passes the authored sign explicitly. Upstream work item (described, not yet numbered): a documented or
removed legacy transformer. This is row 11 of the foundation-fact table, a convention hazard, and a probe of
GATE-WS-09: the signed motion witness for both transformer entry points.

## Scope

In: the probe of both sibling entry points at the pin; the airlock rule that only the canonical map is reached
and the sign is an explicit argument; a test that the legacy entry point is unreachable from the airlock's public
surface; the record. Out: the actuator cards, `actuator.check` and the port-sign refusal (the actuator chapter's
beads); the admission of `fs-phs` as a whole (INT-FSIM-09); the upstream change.

## Technical approach

1. **The two-store fixture.** Two scalar energy stores with unit storage coefficients (energy ½x² each, so effort
   equals state), states x = (2, 3) so that efforts are e = (2, 3), coupled by one transformer with coefficient
   n = 4. Read the state rates ẋ = (ẋ₁, ẋ₂) from each entry point:
   - through `interconnect_mapped` with the mapping the airlock builds from an authored direction;
   - through the legacy `transformer`.
   Expected (reported): one returns (12, −8) and the other (−12, 8). The probe records which is which; that
   assignment is the fact being pinned. Both must satisfy e·ẋ = 2·ẋ₁ + 3·ẋ₂ = 0 to 10⁻¹² (power conservation),
   and the two results must be exact negatives of each other.
2. **The signed motion witness through the airlock.** A driving port P with authored direction "positive
   rotation of P corresponds to positive rotation of Q" and ratio n = 4, driven port Q free and at rest, both
   with unit inertia. Apply a unit positive effort at P for one step. The witness reads the sign of the rate at
   Q:
   - through the airlock's adapter over `interconnect_mapped` with the authored sign passed explicitly: the sign
     at Q is positive (the authored one), and with the authored direction negated it is negative;
   - through the legacy `transformer` called with the same numeric coefficient: the sign at Q is the opposite of
     the canonical result. This is the recorded hazard: the same number means the opposite direction.
3. **The airlock rule.** The adapter's signature takes the direction as a typed argument, `Direction::Same |
   Direction::Opposite`, separate from the positive ratio magnitude; there is no entry that accepts a signed
   coefficient and forwards it. The adapter calls `interconnect_mapped` only. A test over `frob-fsim`'s source
   and symbol table asserts that the identifier of the legacy `transformer` does not occur outside the probe
   target, which is compiled only for conformance.
4. **Why the witness and not a power check.** The probe includes the demonstration that both entry points pass a
   power balance to 10⁻¹² with opposite directions, so that no reader replaces the witness with an energy audit.
5. **Record.** For GATE-WS-09: the rates from both entry points, which entry point gave which sign, the witness
   signs for both, the FrankenSim revision and receipt hash. If the legacy entry point has been removed or its
   sign documented at the type upstream, the row is `fixed upstream at <revision>`; the airlock rule stays, since
   an explicit direction costs nothing.

Determinism: exact small arithmetic; tier G1 known answer on the host.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At the pin, the two entry points return rates that are exact negatives, one of them (12, −8) and the other (−12, 8), each with e·ẋ = 0 to 10⁻¹²; the assignment is recorded.
- [ ] The signed motion witness through the airlock returns the authored sign, and the negated authored direction returns the opposite sign.
- [ ] The witness applied to the legacy entry point with the same coefficient returns the sign opposite to the canonical one.
- [ ] `frob-fsim` reaches only `interconnect_mapped`; the legacy identifier appears nowhere outside the conformance probe target.
- [ ] The adapter has no entry that takes a signed coefficient; direction is a separate typed argument.
- [ ] The results are in the GATE-WS-09 record with revision and receipt.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-fsim --features actuation probe_transformer_sign` (the group feature is enabled for the
probe build only):
- `two_store_rates_canonical` and `two_store_rates_legacy`: the two vectors; their sum is exactly (0, 0).
- `both_conserve_power`: 2·ẋ₁ + 3·ẋ₂ = 0 for each, to 10⁻¹².
- `witness_same_direction_positive`, `witness_opposite_direction_negative`.
- `witness_on_legacy_is_reversed`.
- `adapter_signature_has_typed_direction` (a compile test: passing a negative ratio magnitude is a refusal at construction; there is no signed-coefficient entry).
- `legacy_symbol_not_reachable`.
- Property test: for 10³ seeded ratios n ∈ [0.1, 100] and both directions, the witness sign equals the authored direction and the power balance closes to 10⁻¹² relative.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/suite_ws09.sh` (N33c-48): `frob-conformance probe transformer-sign --json`; logs `metric` events
with both rate vectors and the power residuals (threshold 10⁻¹²), and `assert` events `witness_canonical_sign`
and `witness_legacy_reversed` with the observed signs.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: rates (12, −8) and (−12, 8) for efforts (2, 3) and coefficient 4; 2·12 + 3·(−8) = 0. Negative
controls: an adapter that forwards a signed coefficient to the legacy entry point must fail the witness (this is
the reversed-gear mutant); a "witness" implemented as a power balance must pass on the reversed gear, which the
test demonstrates and thereby shows that it is not a witness. The actuator chapter's gate has the matching
catalog control (the witness passes on every card and fails on each card with its ratio negated). Lanes: twice
on one machine; debug and release; both machines.

## Considerations

- `fs-phs`'s constrained interconnections are deferred upstream, so closed-loop mechanisms do not rely on it;
  this probe concerns the open two-port coupling only.
- The reported rates were not re-run when the plan was written; if the probe finds different numbers at the pin,
  the measured ones replace them in the record and the plan's are marked superseded.
- A reversed gear in a real design shows as a controller that pushes the wrong way; on a legged robot it is a
  fall in the first step, on a wheeled base it is a drive that reverses. The witness catches it at card binding,
  before any rollout.
