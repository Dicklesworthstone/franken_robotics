## Background

FrankenSim's `fs-sos` crate offers polynomial models, sum-of-squares programs and interval verification,
including a routine that returns a "certified" lower bound of a polynomial on an interval. The mission-query and
shield designs name it as an owner worth probing, and one design cites its region-of-attraction routine. A late
reading found two cases in which the value it returns is not a bound.

FACT (both mechanisms confirmed by reading on 2026-11-21 at FrankenSim head `483d09c682f2`,
`fs-sos/src/lib.rs:80–84` and `:289–308`; the two returned values are reported and were not re-run):

1. **A trimmed coefficient.** `Poly::new` trims trailing coefficients below 10⁻¹⁵ in magnitude. The polynomial
   p(x) = 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·x has leading coefficient 2⁻⁵⁰ ≈ 8.88·10⁻¹⁶ < 10⁻¹⁵, so it becomes the constant 1 and is
   certified as bounded below by 1 on abs(x) ≤ 2²⁸, where the true minimum is p(2²⁸) = 1 − 2⁻²² ≈ 0.99999976.
2. **A final subtraction rounded to nearest.** `certified_bound_on` forms its result by one subtraction rounded
   to nearest, and returns 1 for p(x) = 1 − x/2 on abs(x) ≤ 2⁻⁵⁵, where the true minimum is 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶. In double
   precision 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶ rounds to 1 (the spacing below 1 is 2⁻⁵³), so the returned "lower bound" exceeds the
   minimum.

Why it matters although no certificate depends on it: a value from a floating-point routine is a proposal to
check. Two rules follow for any bound this project accepts from one: (1) the bound is evaluated on the authored
coefficients, or the error of translating them is bounded on the actual domain; (2) the last operation is rounded
outward.

Admission test: both probes through the airlock, each compared with exact rational evaluation of the authored
polynomial at the interval's ends. Interim posture: no `fs-sos` value enters a certificate; the `sos-gram` proof
kind is checked in exact rationals; a bound that is used as evidence is recomputed with the authored coefficients
and an outward-rounded last step. Upstream work item (added to the plan's register by description; numbered
`FD-FSIM-38` by N33c-47 and carried by NF-11): an authored-coefficient check and an outward-rounded result in
`fs-sos`. This is row 13 of the foundation-fact table and supplies two probes
of the workspace gate GATE-WS-09.

## Scope

In: the two probes at the pin; the exact comparison; the recompute path that turns a proposed bound into an
acceptable one for univariate polynomials on an interval; the rule's enforcement point; the record. Out: the
sum-of-squares certificates themselves and their checker (the Lemma and Augur beads); admission of `fs-sos` for
mission work (BD-127); the upstream change (NF-11).

## Technical approach

1. **Probe 1 (trimmed coefficient).** Author p(x) = 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·x as exact rationals (coefficients 1 and −1/2⁵⁰).
   Build it in `fs-sos` from the `f64` values and ask for the lower bound on [−2²⁸, 2²⁸]. Record the returned
   value b₁ (reported: 1) and whether the constructed polynomial retained its linear term. Exact evaluation at
   the ends: p(−2²⁸) = 1 + 2⁻²², p(2²⁸) = 1 − 2⁻²². The true minimum m₁ = 1 − 2⁻²² = 4194303/4194304. The probe's
   verdict is `not-a-bound` iff b₁ > m₁, decided in exact rationals (b₁ converted exactly from its bits).
2. **Probe 2 (rounded subtraction).** Author p(x) = 1 − x/2. Ask `certified_bound_on` for [−2⁻⁵⁵, 2⁻⁵⁵]. Record
   b₂ (reported: 1). Exact: m₂ = p(2⁻⁵⁵) = 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶. Verdict `not-a-bound` iff b₂ > m₂. The outward-rounded
   answer in double precision is the predecessor of 1, namely 1 − 2⁻⁵³, which is below m₂ and therefore a valid
   lower bound.
3. **Expected at the pin.** Both probes report `not-a-bound` (the facts hold). That is the recorded result; the
   gate requires that the two probes are recorded and compared with exact evaluation, not that the sibling is
   correct. If either probe reports a valid bound, the row is `fixed upstream at <revision>` for that case.
4. **The recompute path (DESIGN; `frob_fsim::bounds::recheck_univariate`).** It uses no exact rational
   arithmetic and nothing of the auditor `frob-cert`, so that no proposer depends on the auditor. Input: the
   authored coefficients, each as a pair of `f64` enclosing the authored value (a point when the value is
   representable, as the coefficients of both probes are: 1, −2⁻⁵⁰ and −1/2); the interval ends; the proposed
   bound b as an `f64`. The routine encloses the range of p on the interval by outward-rounded interval
   evaluation (directed rounding in software, as below): for degree ≤ 1 at the two ends, where the minimum lies;
   for higher degree in Horner form on a subdivision of the interval (at most 2¹⁰ pieces, DESIGN). Output:
   `Accepted { bound: b }` when b ≤ the enclosure's lower end, or `Rejected { proposed: b, enclosure_lower }`
   when b exceeds what can be shown; `enclosure_lower` is a valid bound, with the last operation rounded toward
   −∞, and is what the caller may use instead. A proposed bound is never passed through unchecked. The exact
   rational evaluation that decides the probes' verdicts and checks this routine (items 1 and 2; the property
   test below) runs in `frob-conformance` with `frob-cert`'s rationals, in test code only.
5. **Enforcement point.** In the airlock, every function that returns a bound obtained from `fs-sos` has the
   return type `Proposed<f64>`, which cannot be converted to an evidence value except through the recompute path
   (or through an exact certificate checker). A compile-fail test covers the direct conversion.
6. **Record.** For GATE-WS-09: b₁ and b₂ as hexadecimal bit patterns, m₁ and m₂ as rationals, the two verdicts,
   whether the linear term was trimmed, the outward-rounded bounds the recompute path returns (for probe 1: the
   largest double not above 1 − 2⁻²², which is that number itself since it is representable; for probe 2:
   1 − 2⁻⁵³), the FrankenSim revision and receipt hash.
7. **Re-run receipt of the Suite catalogue's FACT entry `suite-float-bound`** (N33-CX, a dependency; entry in
   `crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml`). N33-CX labels the entry FACT (the two values `fs-sos`
   returns, 1 for both polynomials, reported and not run here) with `rerun = false`, so the seeding tool writes
   its control as an `obligation`, runnable for no gate, and names this bead's probe record for GATE-WS-09 at the
   pin as the re-run. The receipt hash that the record of item 6 carries is that re-run receipt:
   1. When the record reproduces both values (b₁ and b₂ each exactly 1.0, bits `0x3FF0000000000000`, both
      verdicts `not-a-bound`) and the entry carries `rerun = false`, the recording session (which did not
      implement the probe) sets `rerun = true` and `rerun_receipt = "<receipt hash>"` on that entry, changes no
      other field, and re-runs N38b-05's seeding tool (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test seed_controls --
      --ignored seed`), which makes the control runnable under its existing `NC-` number.
   2. A record in which either value differs (the case fixed upstream, or another value) leaves `rerun = false`,
      flags the fact `changed` in the record with the measured bits, and the entry's case is revised by its
      catalogue owner (N33-CX).

Determinism: exact (rational arithmetic and directed rounding). Tier G0/G1 known answers on the host.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Probe 1 is recorded at the pin with the returned value, the exact minimum 1 − 2⁻²² and the verdict; at the starting state the verdict is `not-a-bound` with returned value 1.
- [ ] Probe 2 is recorded with the returned value, the exact minimum 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶ and the verdict; at the starting state `not-a-bound` with returned value 1.
- [ ] The recompute path rejects the proposed value 1 in both probes, with `enclosure_lower` 1 − 2⁻²² for probe 1 and 1 − 2⁻⁵³ for probe 2, each not above the exact minimum (checked in exact rationals in the conformance harness); `frob-fsim` has no dependency on `frob-cert`.
- [ ] A value of type `Proposed<f64>` cannot become evidence without the recompute path or an exact checker (compile-fail test).
- [ ] No `fs-sos` value appears in any certificate bundle written by the test corpus (a search of bundle contents for the proposer tag).
- [ ] The results (both returned values as bits, both exact minima, both verdicts, both rechecked bounds, whether the linear term was trimmed) are written with the FrankenSim revision and the receipt hash in the form N33c-48's GATE-WS-09 record cites (the gate row is N33c-48's, which depends on this bead); `cargo run -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --example foundation_probe -- sos-bounds --json` prints them.
- [ ] When the record reproduces both returned values (1 and 1, both `not-a-bound`), `suite-float-bound` carries `rerun = true` with `rerun_receipt` equal to the record's receipt hash and no other field changed, and its control is runnable after the seeding tool's re-run; a record in which either value differs leaves the entry byte-identical and flags the fact `changed`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-fsim --features optimize probe_sos_bounds` and `cargo test -p frob-conformance bounds_recheck`:
- `trim_threshold_arithmetic`: 2⁻⁵⁰ < 10⁻¹⁵ < 2⁻⁴⁹ (the coefficient is below the trim threshold; one binade up it is not).
- `probe1_exact_minimum`: p(2²⁸) = 4194303/4194304 in rationals.
- `probe1_verdict_at_pin`.
- `probe2_rounding_arithmetic`: in `f64`, 1.0 − 2⁻⁵⁶ == 1.0; the predecessor of 1.0 is 1 − 2⁻⁵³.
- `probe2_verdict_at_pin`.
- `recheck_degree1_exact`: both probes; outputs as stated.
- `recheck_rejects_overstated_bound`: proposed 1 for either probe is rejected with the enclosure's lower end.
- `recheck_outward_last_step`: for 10⁴ seeded univariate polynomials of degree ≤ 6 with rational coefficients on rational intervals, the returned bound is ≤ the exact minimum over a 10⁴-point rational grid including the ends.
- `compile_fail/proposed_into_evidence.rs`.
- `float_bound_rerun_sets_flag_on_scratch_catalogue` (in `frob-conformance`): a scratch `counterexamples.toml` with a `synthetic` FACT entry `suite-float-bound` (`rerun = false`) and a record with b₁ = b₂ = 1.0: the entry gains `rerun = true` and `rerun_receipt` equal to the record's receipt hash and no other field changes; a record with b₂ = 1 − 2⁻⁵³ (probe 2 fixed upstream) leaves the entry byte-identical; a second seeding run changes no byte.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: minima 1 − 2⁻²² and 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶; reported returned values 1 and 1; outward-rounded bounds 1 − 2⁻²²
and 1 − 2⁻⁵³. Negative controls: a recheck that evaluates the trimmed polynomial instead of the authored one
must accept 1 for probe 1 and is caught by `recheck_rejects_overstated_bound`; a recheck whose last subtraction
rounds to nearest must return 1 for probe 2 and is caught by the same test; a conversion that lets
`Proposed<f64>` through is caught by the compile-fail test. Lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; both
machines (directed rounding must give identical bits).

## Considerations

- Sum-of-squares certificates are checked by a different route entirely: a rational Gram matrix and multipliers,
  the polynomial identity exactly, and positive semidefiniteness by LDLᵀ over the rationals. That route never
  trusted a floating-point bound, which is why no existing certificate depends on this fact.
- The rule generalizes beyond `fs-sos`: a number never claims more than its certificate. Any floating-point
  routine that proposes a bound gets the same `Proposed` wrapper.
- Subdivision at 2¹⁰ pieces bounds the cost of the recheck; a polynomial whose enclosure is still too wide is
  rejected with the enclosure, never accepted on trust.
- Directed rounding in Rust is implemented in software (next-below after a round-to-nearest operation with an
  error-free transformation); no global rounding mode is changed.
