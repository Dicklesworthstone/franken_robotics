## Background

Scenes in FrankenRobotics reuse shapes as scaled instances, and several components cast rays through them: the
depth and lidar kernels, the viewer's picking, and any adapter over a sibling's ray primitive. A ray's hit
parameter means "distance along the ray" only if the ray's direction has unit length in the frame where the
distance is read. A late reading found that a sibling's ray transform changes that silently.

FACT (confirmed by reading on 2026-11-21 at franken_threed head `bf7abe16367e`,
`franken_threed/crates/f3d-math/src/ray.rs:487–490` and `vector3.rs:708–719`): `Ray::apply_matrix4` transforms
its direction with `Vector3::transform_direction`, which normalizes the result, as the scene-graph method it
mirrors does. The transformed direction is renormalized, so hit parameters under a scaled instance are in local
units.

The rule this project applies (rule 3 for scaled instances): let A be the linear part of the instance transform,
local to world. For a unit world direction d the local direction is A⁻¹·d. If the primitive renormalizes it, the
hit parameter is in local units and the world parameter is s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖. The conversion happens before hits
are sorted, clipped to a range gate, or used as optical depth.

The counterexample (the two-plane fixture): two planes facing one ray. An opaque foreground plane at 2 m belongs
to an instance of scale 1/2 and reports a local parameter of 4. A background plane at 3 m belongs to an instance
of scale 3 and reports 1. Sorting by the local parameter returns the occluded plane, and a range gate of 2.5 m
applied to the local values discards the foreground and keeps the background.

Admission test: the two-plane fixture through the adapter. Interim posture: the adapter converts by
s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖. Upstream work item (described, not yet numbered): a ray transform that returns the scale of
its parameter. This is row 14 of the foundation-fact table, a convention hazard; in GATE-WS-09 its row is due at
M2.

## Scope

In: the probe of the sibling's transform at the pin, in an admission-probe workspace outside every graph that
builds a `frob` binary; the conversion function used by every adapter over a renormalizing ray primitive (in
`frob-scene` and, in JavaScript, in the viewer's picking); the two-plane fixture through both; the record. Out:
the ray kernels and the scene compiler (which bakes scales so that compiled packs are in world metres); the
renderer route (N33c-24); the upstream change.

## Technical approach

1. **Probe workspace (DESIGN).** `tests/admission/f3d_ray/`: its own `[workspace]` and lockfile, a path
   dependency on `f3d-math` at the pinned revision with its `serde` feature off (so that the sibling's exact
   `serde_json` pin is not reached), and nothing from this project's core. franken_threed is never a Cargo
   dependency of a workspace that builds a `frob` binary; this probe is the one place its math crate is compiled,
   for admission only.
2. **The sibling's behaviour at the pin.** Build a ray with origin (0, 0, 0) and unit direction d = (0, 0, 1).
   Apply the inverse of an instance transform with uniform scale s (matrix A⁻¹ = (1/s)·I as a 4 × 4): the
   returned direction has length 1 for s = 1/2 and for s = 3 (it was renormalized), where the unnormalized local
   direction A⁻¹·d would have length 2 and 1/3. Record the lengths. For an anisotropic A = diag(2, 0.5, 1) and
   d = (1, 1, 0)/√2, the unnormalized local direction is (0.5, 2, 0)/√2 with length √(4.25/2) = 1.4577380; the
   sibling returns a unit vector along it.
3. **The conversion (`frob_scene::instance::ray_param_world`).** `fn ray_param_world(s_local: f64, a_inv: &Mat3,
   d_world_unit: Vec3) -> f64 { s_local / norm(a_inv · d_world_unit) }`, with a refusal if d is not unit to
   10⁻¹² or if A is singular. The same three lines exist in the viewer's JavaScript picking module with the same
   name, and both are tested on the same fixture file (`tests/fixtures/scene/two_planes.json`).
4. **The two-plane fixture, with its numbers.** Ray from the origin along +z.
   - Foreground: a plane at world z = 2 m, in an instance with A = (1/2)·I. Local direction A⁻¹·d = (0, 0, 2),
     norm 2; the renormalizing primitive reports s_L = 4 (the local plane is at local z = 4); s_W = 4/2 = 2 m.
   - Background: a plane at world z = 3 m, in an instance with A = 3·I. A⁻¹·d = (0, 0, 1/3), norm 1/3; the
     primitive reports s_L = 1; s_W = 1/(1/3) = 3 m.
   - Correct nearest hit: the foreground at 2 m. A range gate of 2.5 m keeps the foreground and discards the
     background.
   - The mutant that sorts local parameters returns the background (1 < 4), and the range gate of 2.5 m applied
     to local values discards the foreground (4 > 2.5) and keeps the background (1 < 2.5).
5. **Through the adapter.** The adapter over the sibling's ray primitive (in the probe workspace) casts the ray
   against both planes using `apply_matrix4` to move the ray into each instance's local frame, reads s_L, applies
   the conversion, sorts, and applies the gate. Expected: the foreground at 2 m. The viewer's pick on the same
   two-plane scene (rendered headless, one pick at the screen centre) returns the foreground object.
6. **Record.** For GATE-WS-09 (row 14, M2): the lengths of the returned directions, the two local parameters,
   the two world parameters, the nearest hit through the adapter and through the viewer's pick, the mutant's
   result, the franken_threed revision. If the sibling's transform now returns the scale of its parameter, the
   row is `fixed upstream at <revision>` and the conversion is fed from that value.

Determinism: exact small arithmetic in `f64` (the fixture's numbers are exactly representable or checked to
10⁻¹²). Tier G1 known answer on the host.

## Considerations

- Compiled scene packs are in world metres because the scene compiler bakes scales before it computes a field;
  the rule binds wherever a scaled transform survives to query time: an imported pack inspected before
  compilation, a distance brick shared by instances of different size, the viewer's picking rays, and adapters
  over a sibling's ray primitive.
- The sibling's behaviour mirrors a widely used scene-graph method, so it is a convention and not a defect; the
  hazard is at this project's boundary.
- An exact `serde_json` pin in franken_threed is why the probe turns the `serde` feature off and why the crate
  never joins the core graph.
- Optical depth and range gates are safety-relevant for lidar-based stopping; a parameter in local units would
  shorten or lengthen a stopping distance by the instance scale.
