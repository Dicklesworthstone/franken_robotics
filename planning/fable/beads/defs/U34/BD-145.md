## Background

FrankenRobotics claims that a design studio can decide among many robot designs without training a controller
for each. The mechanism is a ladder of evaluation rungs: a cheap rung (a static check, a synthesized baseline
controller, a shared family policy, a short horizon, a fast physics tier) scores every design, and only the
survivors go up to the expensive rung (a trained specialist, promoted on the referee tier). The ladder is only
as good as the agreement between adjacent rungs about the **order** of designs, and that agreement has not been
published for the case that matters. BET-17 is the bet that cheap rungs order designs as the trained,
referee-promoted rung does, well enough to eliminate. The rank-agreement experiment is the decisive early
experiment of the program: it decides one of the two largest levers of the ten-fold thesis.

What published neighbours report (FACT, to be re-read from the sources before a gate cites them): the value
predictions of a shared policy correlate with rollouts of the same policy at a rank correlation of 0.40 overall
and 0.50, 0.60 and 0.83 on three robots, over 17 designs, which is an easier comparison than a shared policy
against specialists; weight sharing in architecture search gave Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three
benchmarks. Agreement of 0.7 or better near the top is therefore not supported in advance, and the default,
until a stratum is measured, is that a cheap rung neither selects nor discards.

An experiment whose thresholds can move after the data arrive decides nothing. DESIGN: the experiment is
**pre-registered at M1**, long before it can run (the pilot on incumbent tools is due at M3, the run on this
project's own tiers at M4): the design set's draw, the strata, the statistics, the thresholds 0.7 and 0.5 and
the consequences of every outcome are frozen in a document whose identity is bound into the study record, so
that a threshold edited after the first result is refused. The same bead files the compute request, because the
experiment exceeds the default machine envelope and cannot start without the owner's approval. Tier: Frontier
(the rank-agreement ledger and its modes). This bead runs no training.

Rejected alternatives: registering at M3 when the pilot is ready (by then partial results from other work would
have been seen); thresholds per class chosen after a look at the scatter; a single correlation over all designs
(the bias of a cheap rung is not uniform, and a high correlation does not protect the winner).

## Scope

In: the registration document and its schema; its binding to the study plan and the refusal of an edited
registration; the pilot-register row; the compute request and the record of the owner's answer or the default.

## Technical approach

**The registration** (canonical JSON written by the project's canonical writer, schema name
`frob.registration/1`; the field spellings are DESIGN proposals confined to the content below). It has the six
parts every method experiment must have before its first run, plus the experiment's own parameters:

1. **Question and falsifier.** Do cheap rungs order designs as the trained, referee-promoted rung does? BET-17
   is falsified for a morphology class and task family if Kendall τ on the top band is below 0.7 at the lower
   bound; below 0.5 the cheap rung's verdicts are advisory for that class and the class leaves the speed claim;
   or top-k recall measured by the audit lane is below its declared floor; or a cut sized from τ alone discards
   the higher rung's best design in the calibration set.
2. **Population.** One morphology class per row. Row 1: quadruped-like legged, velocity-tracking task family,
   the class with a measured incumbent training time. Rows for wheeled bases at M3 and for tracked and hybrid
   classes at M4 are added as new registration versions before their data; classes expected to be hard (stairs,
   rubble, bipeds) are named as controls.
3. **Design set.** n = 40 designs in the class, drawn by a keyed counter-based stream whose key is the
   registration's identity and the label `design-set`, from the class generator (the class templates with limb
   lengths and masses perturbed within ±20 to ±30%, actuator cards within one family, payloads within the task
   range), **before any score exists**. The draw is a function of the key alone, so the set is fixed by this
   document although the corpus is built later.
4. **Strata.** Two strata of 20 designs: the half of the parameter box nearer the class template and the far
   half (DESIGN). Reason for two and not four: the conformal calibration that a selecting rung needs is valid
   only at n ≥ 19 per stratum, so a stratum of 10 could never reach the `selective` mode, and a verdict that
   cannot be reached must not be promised.
5. **Rungs and pairs.** Each design is scored three ways: by the shared policy with no adaptation on the fast
   tier; after a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps; by a specialist promoted on the referee. Until a family
   policy exists, the pilot's low rung is the synthesized baseline controller, which is a scorer like any other
   and has no exemption; the tier pair (fast tier against referee under one fixed controller) is a second pair
   with the same record type. All scores use common world addresses and seeds.
6. **Statistics.** Kendall τ = (concordant − discordant pairs)/C(n, 2), computed on the **top band**: the
   designs in the top m of either rung, with m = max(10, 3k) and k the number of finalists wanted (k = 3, so
   m = 10). Top-10 recall of the low rung against the high rung. Spearman ρ over the stratum. Mean signed
   residual per region (the bias of the cheap rung). The per-episode cross-tier correlation ρ_ep on 256 paired
   episodes per design. The containment rank k_c(m): the smallest k such that the high rung's top m all lie
   inside the low rung's top k; and the regret of a cut: the high-rung score of the best design the cut discards
   minus that of the best design it keeps. Intervals: percentile bootstrap over designs, 10,000 resamples,
   level 95%; every condition is read on the **lower** bound.
7. **Thresholds, fixed before data.** 0.7 and 0.5 on the lower bound of top-band τ; 0.7 on the lower bound of
   ρ_ep; for a tier pair, top-10 recall at least 0.8 beside τ ≥ 0.7. The alternative pair (0.6, 0.3) that was
   considered for the baseline rung is rejected here: the stricter pair is registered.
8. **Consequences**, per rung pair and stratum:

   | Mode | Condition | What the lower rung may do |
   |---|---|---|
   | `selective` | τ ≥ 0.7 with the winner check passed (retention at least the containment rank); calibration valid (n ≥ 19, exchangeability martingale below 20); audited miss rate at most α | Select finalists by the conformal survivor rule |
   | `discard-only` | 0.5 ≤ τ < 0.7 | The fixed table: the top third advances, so the top quarter always does; a design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best is never eliminated; the audit lane keeps running |
   | `uninformative` | τ < 0.5; or the stratum is unmeasured; or the planted winner was lost | Nothing: the rung is skipped in this stratum and its budget moves up one rung |

   Where ρ_ep is below 0.7 on at least 200 paired addresses, the tier-correction estimator is disabled for that
   stratum. A rung without a record is refused (FROB-REF-537).
9. **Kill gate and fallback, named in advance.** KILL-RANK: the rank-agreement gate fails for every morphology
   class tested (decided at M4; for tier pairs, top-weighted τ below 0.5 in more than half the strata of the
   reference classes). Fallback: the low rung is `uninformative` everywhere, every candidate that survives
   static analysis is scored on the higher rung, the ladder's claim shrinks to stopping and elimination among
   trained candidates, and the ten-fold report records which factor was lost.
10. **Ceiling**, computed before any run from the incumbent phase ledger: a rung that only discards saves at
    most the fraction it discards. If training is a share f of the loop's wall time and the rung discards a
    fraction d of designs at overhead h, the loop gains at most 1/(1 − f·d + h). The registration records f
    from the measured ledger and the d each mode implies (2/3 for the fixed table).
11. **Competing arms.** Successive halving on a fixed schedule; training every design (the incumbent
    workflow).
12. **Potency controls.** Planted populations with known top-weighted rank correlation on each side of each
    threshold (0.7 and 0.5), which the pipeline must classify correctly; a planted winner that the cheap rung
    represents poorly; an A/A pair (one rung scored twice), which must give τ near 1 and must not be reported
    as a discovery.
13. **Reachability pre-analysis.** A simulation, stored with its hash, of which modes the rule can return at
    n = 20 per stratum under true top-band τ of 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 0.95, with the probability of each mode.

**Binding and refusal.** `study.plan` takes the registration as a field of its manifest; the plan identifier is
the content address of the resolved manifest and therefore covers every threshold. `study.submit` takes the plan
identifier. Each result row of the study record carries the plan identifier and the registration's identity. A
result offered under a registration whose identity differs from the one the plan is bound to, or a verdict
requested against an edited registration, is refused as a conflict (exit 7; FROB-REF-809: plan not found, or
bound to an identity that has since been superseded). A new registration version is a new document with a link
to the old one; results recorded under the old version stay scored under it.

**Pilot register.** One row in the conformance crate's pilot register: mechanism (the rank-agreement ledger and
its modes), tier Frontier, the registration's identity as the protocol frozen before execution, the falsifier,
the ceiling, the fallback and kill gate, the cost, the tool and its pin (the incumbent harness revision for the
M3 pilot), and an empty result.

**Compute request** (OPEN-PROG-4; owner: the project owner; due at M1). About forty per-design trainings in one
morphology class: about 45 device-hours, or 5 to 7.5 where specialists trained for the family-policy experiment
are reused (TARGET). Requested class: E2, a design campaign of at most 48 device-hours. The default envelope E0
(16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) cannot run it. The request states the per-run ceiling (one specialist
training, with the incumbent's measured time as its planning value), the stop rule (the campaign stops when the
budget is spent; unscored designs are reported as unscored, never dropped), and what happens if it is not
approved: nothing runs above E0, every cheap rung is `uninformative`, so it neither selects nor discards, and
results are labelled as unranked. The owner's answer is recorded as an ADR; with no answer the default holds.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition: the registration is committed before any paired training runs (its commit precedes
      the first ledger row of a paired training for this study, and the pilot bead depends on this one).
- [ ] Seed done-condition: a threshold edited after the first result is refused by the study record (a copy of
      the registration with 0.7 changed to 0.6 has a different identity; appending a result or requesting a
      verdict under it returns exit 7).
- [ ] The registration has all six parts of a method experiment (ceiling, competing arms, potency control,
      paired lower bound, pre-declared fallback, reachability pre-analysis); a copy with one part removed is not
      admitted.
- [ ] The design-set draw is reproducible: the keyed stream yields the same 40 draw indices on two machines.
- [ ] The pre-analysis shows every promised mode is reachable at n = 20 per stratum, and a variant with four
      strata of 10 is rejected because `selective` is unreachable.
- [ ] The pilot-register row exists with an empty result.
- [ ] The compute request is filed with class E2 and its numbers; the owner's ruling or the fail-closed default
      is recorded as an ADR; no job above E0 has been submitted for this study.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

In the conformance crate (`cargo test -p frob-conformance registration`), with the real study types on the CPU:
- `registration_identity_covers_thresholds`: changing 0.7, 0.5, n, m, the bootstrap level or the strata changes
  the identity.
- `plan_binds_registration`; `result_under_edited_registration_is_conflict`;
  `new_version_links_to_old_and_old_results_stay`.
- `six_parts_required` (six cases).
- `design_draw_is_a_function_of_the_key`.
- `kendall_tau_winner_case`: forty designs, the cheap rung reproduces the true order except that the true best
  is ranked third; among the true top ten, 2 of 45 pairs are inverted, τ = (43 − 2)/45 = 41/45 ≈ 0.911; the
  containment rank for m = 1 is 3; a cut that keeps two designs has positive regret.
- `mode_table`: lower bounds 0.72, 0.55 and 0.45 map to `selective` (given the other conditions),
  `discard-only` and `uninformative`; an unmeasured stratum is `uninformative`.
- `preanalysis_rejects_strata_of_ten`.
- `ceiling_formula`: f = 0.6, d = 2/3, h = 0.02 gives 1/(1 − 0.4 + 0.02) ≈ 1.613.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/rank_agreement_registration.sh`: validates the registration file; runs `frob eval preanalyze` on
its rule; runs `frob study plan` with the registration and records the plan identifier; then, on `synthetic`
scores for the 40 draw indices (labelled `synthetic` in their evidence), submits a first result, edits a copy of
the registration's threshold, and asserts that `frob study plan` on the copy returns a different identifier and
that a result or verdict under the original plan with the edited registration is refused with exit 7. It logs
through the shared helper of STD-E2E to `target/e2e-logs/rank_agreement_registration/<utc>.ndjson`: each command
line, exit code and wall time; the two registration identities and plan identifiers; the envelope's `evidence`
block; the refusal's code; a `summary` line naming KILL-RANK as the kill gate this registration serves. It runs
inside the default machine envelope; it submits no training.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: τ = 41/45 with containment rank 3 on the winner fixture; the ceiling 1.613 for the stated
inputs; the mode table. Negative controls: the edited threshold; the registration missing its potency control;
the four-strata variant; a `synthetic` planted population with true top-band τ of 0.45 that a pipeline with a
loosened threshold would call `discard-only`. Determinism: the registration's identity and the draw are equal
twice on one machine and across platforms (class D0).

## Considerations

- RISK-27 (rank agreement between rungs is too low for cheap rungs to eliminate designs; severity High; signal:
  τ under 0.5 at the lower bound in the pilot, or verdict flips after training). RISK-10 (no compute envelope
  is approved; severity High). Pre-mortem scenario: rank agreement was low, so most designs were trained anyway.
- OPEN-PROG-4 blocks the pilot's ability to run above the default envelope; OPEN-SPARK-2 (the envelope classes)
  is due at M0. Fail-closed default for both: class E0; cheap rungs `uninformative`.
- OPEN-REFLEX-2 (the pair (0.7, 0.5) or (0.6, 0.3) for the baseline rung): this registration fixes the stricter
  pair before data, which is that question's default.
- A high correlation does not protect the winner: the winner check and the planted winner are in the
  registration for that reason.
- The registration cannot foresee the family policy's architecture; rows for it are added as a new version
  before any of its data exist, never by editing version 1.
- No dedicated refusal code exists for "registration changed after the first result"; the conflict code above
  is the fail-closed mapping until the registry's owner decides otherwise.
