## Background

FrankenRobotics identifies every artifact by the hash of its canonical bytes, and keeps one registry of its
artifact formats: thirteen rows, each with one identifier, one schema name with a major version, one owner crate
and one canonical encoding. Seven formats land at the first milestone and are measured there. The other six
arrive with their subsystems: the scene pack at M2, the clearance complex and the minimal counterexample world
at M3, the skill and the energy budget at M4, the build packet at M5. Each reader must pass the gate before its
format's first release, so the scene pack is measured at M2 (N34p1-01), the two derived formats at M3
(N34p1-02) and the skill and energy budget at M4 (N34p1-03). This seed bead closes the registry: it measures the
build packet, the last format, and at M5 records the gate for all thirteen on one commit.

**GATE-WS-02** (TARGET; claim Known answer; M1 for the formats that exist, then at each new format; complete at
M5): for each of the thirteen formats, 10,000 seeded round trips canonical → value → canonical with zero byte
differences; where a view exists, 10,000 round trips view → canonical → view with zero differences; every one of
10,000 single-byte mutations either fails to parse or changes the identity; and each reader survives 10⁷ fuzz
executions with zero panics before the format's first release. Negative control: a reader built with key-order
canonicalization disabled must fail the first clause.

Why completion is its own bead: "thirteen of thirteen at one commit" is a different statement from thirteen
separate measurements taken over four milestones on different pins. A gate's pass cites one resolution receipt.
Tier: Solid (the formats' subsystems are partly Frontier; the registry rule is not).

## Scope

In: the `.fbuild` row in final form with its harness wiring, blob, export-view and four-flag clauses and its
gate lane; the final check that the five earlier rows below are still as their beads recorded them; the
version-rule clause for every released format; the final thirteen-format gate run and record.
Out: the formats themselves (BD-162 scene pack, BD-233 clearance complex, BD-274 counterexample world, BD-336
skill, BD-337 energy budget, BD-408 build packet); the per-format gate lanes of the five formats that land at
M2 to M4 (N34p1-01, N34p1-02, N34p1-03); the harness (N34-22); the script (N34-23); the M1 formats (BD-141).

## Technical approach

**The six rows** (the registry's content for these formats):

| Identifier | Ext. | Schema | Owner | Produced by | Consumed by | Canonical form and views |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `FMT-FSCENE` | `.fscene` | `frob.scene/1` | `frob-scene` | Generators; importers and the scan adapter; authored | The scene compiler, then `frob-contact` and `frob-sense`; `frob-nemesis`; the viewer | Canonical: the manifest. Cooked bricks and meshes are derived blobs addressed by hash |
| `FMT-FCLEAR` | `.fclear` | `frob.clearance/1` | `frob-scene` | `frob-scene`, from a scene and a footprint family | `frob-analyze`, `frob-mission`, `frob-lemma` | Canonical and derived: reproducible from the identities of its inputs |
| `FMT-FMCW` | `.fmcw` | `frob.mcw/1` | `frob-nemesis` | The shrinker in `frob-nemesis` | `frob-arena` (regression batteries), `frob-autopsy`, `frob-mission`, the viewer | Canonical and derived |
| `FMT-FSKILL` | `.fskill` | `frob.skill/1` | `frob-control` | Controller synthesis; `frob-learn`, with weights by reference | `frob-control`, `frob-learn`, `frob-arena`, `frob-shield` | Canonical. Weights are blobs addressed by hash |
| `FMT-FENERGY` | `.fenergy` | `frob.energy/1` | `frob-shield` | `frob-shield` | `frob-shield`, `frob-mission`, `frob-lemma` | Canonical |
| `FMT-FBUILD` | `.fbuild` | `frob.build/1` | `frob-foundry` | `frob-foundry` | People and downstream tools; `frob-ground` for as-built comparison | Canonical: the manifest. Views: STEP and mesh files, bills of materials |

**Clauses beyond the gate's four, by kind of format** (each is a test in the owner's conformance test, named by
the same convention as the four):

1. **Derived formats** (`.fclear`, `.fmcw`): "canonical and derived" means reproducible from the identities of
   its inputs. `fmt_<ext>_rederive`: for 100 seeded inputs, regenerate the artifact from the input identities
   alone and compare bytes and identity with the stored artifact; zero differences. A derived artifact also
   lists its input identities, and changing one input identity changes the artifact's identity (a
   live-dependency proof).
2. **Formats with blobs** (`.fscene` bricks and meshes, `.fskill` weights, `.fbuild` geometry): the manifest
   references each blob by hash and length; no manifest embeds a path on the machine that wrote it
   (`assert_no_machine_path`); `fmt_<ext>_blob_mutation`: a single-byte change in any blob makes the read
   refuse, naming the blob; a missing blob refuses and is never replaced by a default.
3. **Export views** (the build packet's STEP and mesh files and bills of materials): one-way renderings.
   `fmt_fbuild_export_view_has_no_identity`: the exporting verb's envelope carries no identity for a view, and
   a view offered where the packet's identity is required is refused (FROB-REF-892, `view-is-not-identity`).
4. **Flags on export.** Every exported artifact carries the three no-authorization fields
   (`execution_authorized`, `hardware_safety_signoff`, `sim_to_real_certified`), all false; the build packet
   carries a fourth, `fabrication_released`, also always false, at the manifest root and in each generated
   drawing and bill-of-materials header; a skill carries the flags in its file header; a counterexample world
   spells the object `no_authorization`. `fmt_<ext>_flags`: the canonical form contains the fields, and a file
   edited to `true` or with a field removed is refused on read (absence is not read as false).
5. **Version rule after release.** Once a format has been released, a change that is not an added optional
   field needs a new major version and a migration edit that produces a new artifact linked to the old identity.
   `fmt_<ext>_old_identity_still_resolves`: an artifact written before a migration is still read by identity
   for citing old evidence.

**Work, as each format lands** (at its owner's milestone, before its first release): confirm the row, write the
generator and the named tests, run the commit lane, then the gate lane for that format (10⁷ fuzz executions and
the negative-control build), and add its record. The registry's gate row stays partial with a count. For
`.fscene`, `.fclear`, `.fmcw`, `.fskill` and `.fenergy` this is done at M2, M3 and M4 by N34p1-01, N34p1-02 and
N34p1-03, whose records this bead reads; this bead does it for `.fbuild` (script row: `.fbuild | M5 | frob
foundry check on the packet | frob foundry export on a built-in design`; the packet mode of `foundry.check` is
N31b-32's, hence the dependency, and both verbs are `run`, so each call in the row carries `--key`).

**Completion at M5:**
1. `cargo xtask check-contracts` reports thirteen landed rows, zero pending, and no second parser.
2. `scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh --lane gate` runs all thirteen formats on one commit and one resolution
   receipt, on clean sibling trees at their pins.
3. Thirteen records, each with the four clause results, the extra clauses that apply, the control's outcome,
   the seed, the commit, the receipt and the host fingerprint.
4. Release identities (CX-359). This bead appends the three fixed-seed `.fbuild` fixtures and their rows (first
   release `M5`) to `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/formats/release_identities.toml`, the file BD-141 creates
   and N34p1-01 to N34p1-03 extend. On the M5 commit `fmt_release_identities_hold` recomputes the identity of
   every fixture of all thirteen formats from its canonical bytes and compares it with the row recorded at the
   format's first release, whatever toolchain and sibling pins have moved since (each row names the toolchain,
   commit and receipt it was recorded under, and the log prints how many pin moves separate it from the M5
   commit): zero differences. The planted row `debug-render-identity` (N34-CX's control for CX-359, built by
   BD-141) must fail the comparison and be named.
5. The recording session, which implemented none of the formats, writes the gate's row as a pass only if all
   thirteen are clean and every control was caught, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate
   -- --gate GATE-WS-02 --receipt <hash>` (N38b-19); the known-answer row runs as `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo
   test -p frob-conformance --test g3_metamorphic -- KA-782`, and its key-order control with `--features
   gauntlet-mutations`. A format that fell back at a kill gate (for example the
   build packet reduced to its fallback form, or a deleted crate) is recorded under the fallback's name, and
   the registry row is tombstoned by ADR instead of being counted as passed.

Determinism class D0 for every clause. Evidence class of the records: Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition, GATE-WS-02: for each of the thirteen formats, 10,000 round trips with 0 byte
      differences; view round trips with 0 differences where a lossless view exists; 10,000 mutations with 0
      accepted-unchanged; 10⁷ fuzz executions with 0 panics; the key-order negative control caught; all on one
      commit and one receipt.
- [ ] `.fclear` and `.fmcw` re-derive from their input identities with zero byte differences over 100 seeded
      cases each.
- [ ] A single-byte change in a scene brick, a skill's weight blob or a packet's geometry blob is refused on
      read with the blob named.
- [ ] No manifest of any format contains a machine path.
- [ ] `release_identities.toml` holds three rows for each of the thirteen formats; on the M5 commit
      `fmt_release_identities_hold` finds zero differences from the identities recorded at each first release,
      and the planted `debug-render-identity` row is caught and named.
- [ ] Every exported artifact of the six formats carries its flags, all false; the build packet carries four.
- [ ] The registry has zero pending rows, and `check-contracts` finds no second parser for any of the thirteen
      extensions.
- [ ] The status table prints GATE-WS-02 as passed with "13 of 13" and the record's receipt; before that it
      prints the partial count.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Per owner (`cargo test -p <owner> --test conformance fmt_`): the tests of the five earlier formats are written by
N34p1-01 to -03 and rerun here on the M5 commit (`fmt_fscene_*`, `fmt_fclear_*`, `fmt_fmcw_*`, `fmt_fskill_*`,
`fmt_fenergy_*`); this bead adds `fmt_<ext>_old_identity_still_resolves` for each of the thirteen, and for the
build packet `fmt_fbuild_roundtrip`, `fmt_fbuild_refusals`, `fmt_fbuild_blob_mutation`,
`fmt_fbuild_export_view_has_no_identity`, `fmt_fbuild_flags` (four fields; `fabrication_released: true` is
refused). Fuzz targets `fmt_<ext>_read` for each. Each refusal test covers an unknown major version, an unknown
field at two depths and a duplicate member.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh --lane gate` (bead N34-23), with all thirteen rows active. It logs per format
and clause the counts against their thresholds, the control's outcome, the seeds, the commit and receipt, and
the final gate line "13 of 13", in the structured format of STD-E2E.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-782 (for each of the thirteen formats: zero byte differences in both directions; every mutation either fails
to parse or changes the identity; each reader survives 10⁷ fuzz executions with zero panics; tolerance exact;
tier G3 on the host; negative control: key-order canonicalization disabled). KA-781 on every format (respelling
keeps identity). Counterexamples: CX-358, CX-359 (a pin move must not change any stored identity: the thirteen
fixtures' identities recorded at their first release are compared at M5), CX-360, CX-361. Negative controls,
by N34-CX's plant identifiers where it has them: the `writer-no-key-sort` builds; the planted row
`debug-render-identity`; a derived artifact regenerated with one input identity changed (must differ); a mutated
blob; a flag set to `true`. Determinism lanes: the commit lane twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux
and macOS; and the fixtures' identities equal across every pin move since their first release.

## Considerations

- Four of the six formats belong to Frontier subsystems with kill gates. A format whose subsystem fell back is
  not a failure of this gate; its row is recorded under the fallback or tombstoned by ADR, and the count of
  "thirteen" changes only by that ADR.
- The M5 run is thirteen times 10⁷ fuzz executions plus control builds. It fits the default machine envelope
  when run in sequence under the envelope guard; it is not a campaign.
- Identities recorded at a format's first release are the long-lived known answers of this registry. If one
  changes at a later pin, the defect is in canonicalization (counterexample CX-359), never in the fixture.
- The M5 exit review depends on this bead; it reads the record and does not rerun the formats.
- Schema-named files beyond the thirteen (a replication kit's manifest, the bench protocol, the review record
  and others) get a row only by the decision bead N34-27. A row it adds is measured by the same four clauses
  and printed as an added format beside "13 of 13"; it never changes the count of plan formats.
- Privacy: scene and build-packet fixtures in the gate run are generated or licensed for redistribution; a
  private place is never a gate fixture.
