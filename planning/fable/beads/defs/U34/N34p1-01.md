## Background

FrankenRobotics identifies every artifact by the BLAKE3 hash of its canonical bytes under a domain string equal
to its schema name, and keeps one registry of its thirteen artifact formats (`registries/formats.toml`): each
has one identifier, one schema name with a major version, one owner crate and one canonical encoding; everything
else is a view, which is never hashed, signed or cited. Seven formats land at M1 and are measured by BD-141. The
scene pack is the first of the remaining six: it lands at M2 with `frob-scene` (layer 2, tiers Solid and
Frontier) and is released with the M2 terrain studio.

**GATE-WS-02** (TARGET; claim Known answer; M1 for the formats that exist, then at each new format; complete at
M5): for each format, 10,000 seeded round trips canonical → value → canonical with zero byte differences; where a
view exists, 10,000 round trips view → canonical → view with zero differences; every one of 10,000 single-byte
mutations either fails to parse or changes the identity; and each reader survives 10⁷ fuzz executions with zero
panics **before the format's first release**. Negative control: a reader built with key-order canonicalization
disabled must fail the first clause.

The last clause is why this bead exists at M2 and not inside the M5 completion bead: the scene pack's first
release is at M2, and a release may not precede its reader's fuzz clause. The owner bead BD-162 builds the
format and a commit-lane fuzz target; this bead measures the gate for it. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the `FMT-FSCENE` row in final form; the owner's `Subject` and named conformance tests; the blob clause and
the provenance clause for this format; the gate-lane run for `.fscene` and its record; the format's row in the
verb table of `scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh`. Out: the format itself (BD-162: manifest, `SceneId`, blob
references, ranged fields refused); the harness (N34-22); the script (N34-23); the M3 and M4 formats
(N34p1-02, N34p1-03); the build packet and the thirteen-of-thirteen record (BD-410).

## Technical approach

**The row** (registry content): `FMT-FSCENE`, extension `.fscene`, schema `frob.scene/1`, owner `frob-scene`.
Produced by generators, importers and the scan adapter, and by authors. Consumed by the scene compiler, then
`frob-contact` and `frob-sense`; by `frob-nemesis`; by the viewer. Canonical form: the manifest, as canonical
JSON. Cooked bricks and meshes are derived blobs addressed by hash. Number rule (registry rule 4, as BD-162
states it): every floating-point field that bears identity is written as the shortest decimal that round-trips
the binary64 value, produced by the project's writer and never by a library formatter; not-a-number and infinity
are not representable; integers above 2⁵³ are strings. View kind: none (the viewer's rendering is an `export`
view with no identity).

**Work:**
1. Confirm the row against the landed crate; correct `registries/formats.toml` if the owner's contract decided
   otherwise; `cargo xtask check-generated --write`, then `check-generated` clean.
2. In `crates/frob-scene/tests/conformance.rs`: the harness `Subject` (a seeded generator of valid manifests that
   reaches every schema field: terrain families, hazard lists, optical and fixture properties, the feasibility
   block, blob references of each kind), and the tests below.
3. **Blob clause** (formats with blobs): the manifest references each blob by hash and length; a single-byte
   change in any identity-bearing blob (bricks, directory, overlays, maps, tables) makes the read of the pack
   refuse with the blob named: the integrity refusal `scene-pack-identity-mismatch` (exit 9; row 4 of N19c-42); a
   missing blob refuses (`FROB-REF-303`) and is never replaced by a default. The visual mesh (the GLB under
   `presentation`) is the one blob outside the identity (ADR-HAB-04, as BD-162 and N19cp1-01 state it): a changed
   GLB byte leaves the pack readable with the same `SceneId`, and the mesh blob itself is refused by its own hash
   when it is opened through its blob reference (the lazy open of BD-162's `BlobRef`, which is what the viewer
   uses). Both halves are tested, so that neither rule can swallow the other.
4. **No machine path** (registry rule 5): `assert_no_machine_path` on every generated manifest; an importer
   that would record a source path records the file's hash and a portable name instead.
5. **Provenance on export** (registry rule 6): an exported pack carries the three no-authorization fields
   (`execution_authorized`, `hardware_safety_signoff`, `sim_to_real_certified`), all `false`, the schema name and
   the stack receipt of the binary that wrote it; a pack edited to `true`, or with a field removed, is refused on
   read (absence is not read as false).
6. Add the row `.fscene | M2 | frob scene show | frob scene gen (a terrain family at a fixed seed)` to the verb
   table of `scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh`.
7. Release identities (CX-359): three `.fscene` fixtures generated from fixed seeds are committed under
   `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/formats/fscene/`, and their rows (format identifier, fixture path,
   identity, first release `M2`, with the toolchain, commit and resolution receipt) are appended to
   `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/formats/release_identities.toml`, the file BD-141 creates; its test
   `fmt_release_identities_hold` recomputes them, and BD-410 compares them again at M5.
8. Gate lane: `scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh --formats fscene --lane gate`, which runs the four clauses at full
   size (10⁷ fuzz executions) and the negative control, the canonical writer rebuilt as the `writer-no-key-sort`
   mutant of N34-18 by the suite's one mutation mechanism (`--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-core/nc-<nnnn>`,
   the `NC-` record seeded from N34-CX's plant 1 of CX-358, which the control runner of N38b-04 executes; an
   earlier text used a compiler flag `--cfg frob_nc="no-key-sort"`, which is withdrawn: no such flag exists in
   the tree), and logs the record:
   format, clause counts, differences, executions, panics, control outcome, seed, commit, resolution receipt,
   host fingerprint. The gate's part for M2 is entered from the receipts of those tier runs by a session that
   did not implement the format, with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-WS-02
   --receipt <hash> --part M2`; the script records nothing itself. The registry's gate row prints the count of
   passed formats of thirteen ("8 of 13" after the seven of M1), still partial.

Determinism class D0 for every clause. Evidence class of the record: Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-WS-02 for `.fscene`: 10,000 round trips with 0 byte differences; 10,000 single-byte mutations with 0
      accepted-unchanged; 10⁷ fuzz executions with 0 panics; the key-order negative control caught (the build
      with `--features gauntlet-mutations,frob-core/nc-<nnnn>` fails clause 1); recorded before the M2 release
      that first ships the format.
- [ ] A single-byte change in an identity-bearing blob is refused on read (exit 9) with the blob named; a missing
      blob is refused (`FROB-REF-303`); a single-byte change in the visual mesh leaves the pack's `SceneId` and
      its read unchanged, and the mesh blob is refused by its hash when it is opened through its reference.
- [ ] No generated manifest contains a machine path; an export carries its three flags, all `false`, the schema
      name and a stack receipt hash.
- [ ] The generator reaches every field of the generated schema of `frob.scene/1` under `schemas/` (field
      coverage).
- [ ] `release_identities.toml` holds three `.fscene` rows with first release `M2`, and
      `fmt_release_identities_hold` passes with them.
- [ ] The script's gate line prints the true count of passed formats of thirteen and does not print a pass for
      the gate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-scene --test conformance fmt_`: `fmt_fscene_roundtrip` (10,000, seed 34);
`fmt_fscene_refusals` (an unknown major version `frob.scene/2` → FROB-REF-891 unless the owner's chapter states
its own version code; an unknown member at top level and one nested in a hazard entry; a duplicate member;
a viewer export offered on the identity path → FROB-REF-892); `fmt_fscene_mutations` (10,000);
`fmt_fscene_blob_mutation` (one flipped byte in a brick blob, refused with exit 9 by the integrity refusal
`scene-pack-identity-mismatch`, row 4 of N19c-42, with the blob's reference and both hashes named; one flipped
byte in the mesh blob of `presentation`, which is outside the identity: the read of the pack succeeds with the
same `SceneId`, and opening the mesh through its blob reference refuses it by its hash; a deleted
identity-bearing blob refused with `FROB-REF-303`); `fmt_fscene_flags` (canonical export contains the three `false` fields;
`true` refused; a removed field refused); `fmt_fscene_no_machine_path`. Fuzz target `fmt_fscene_read` (10⁵ in
the commit lane, 10⁷ in the gate lane).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/format_registry.sh --formats fscene` (commit lane) and `--lane gate`: `frob schema frob.scene/1`
digest against `schemas/`; `frob scene gen --family terrain/stairs --difficulty 0.5 --seed 34 --key fmt-fscene-gen`
writes a fixture; `frob artifact export <scene_id> --out <dir> --key fmt-fscene-export` (both are `run` verbs and
carry their idempotency keys); `frob scene show` binds the same identity from the exported bytes and from three
respellings; 200 seeded mutations through the CLI; the
registry refusals on the CLI and through `frob-mcp`. NDJSON lines through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/format_registry/<utc>.ndjson`: `ts`, `script`, `bead: "N34p1-01"`, `step`, `event`, exact
command line, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the scene's identity, `metric` lines for each
clause (differences against 0, executions against 10⁷, panics against 0), the control's outcome, the digest of
every exported file, and on failure the first failing index, the seed and the saved mutant's path; a `summary`
line with the count of passed formats of thirteen.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-781 (respellings bind one identity) on a real scene. KA-782 for this format (exact; tier G3 on the host).
Counterexamples: CX-358 (hash the file as written), CX-359 (the identity recorded at this first release is the
long-lived known answer that every later pin move is compared against at M5), CX-361 (tolerate unknown fields).
Negative controls: the `writer-no-key-sort` build; a mutated blob; a flag set to `true`; a reader patched to ignore one
unknown member (must fail clause 3). Determinism lanes: commit lane twice on one machine; debug and release;
Linux and macOS: identical identities.

## Considerations

- Privacy: gate fixtures are generated terrain or packs licensed for redistribution; a private place is never a
  gate fixture, and logs carry identities, never scene content.
- 10⁷ executions on scene manifests is minutes of CPU and fits the default machine envelope under the envelope
  guard; blob files are generated once per seed and reused by the fuzzer.
- If the owner's contract names its own version-refusal code for scenes, the refusal test and the script's table
  use it (the rule: an owner with its own code uses it; every other owner uses FROB-REF-891).
- The identity of the fixed-seed fixture recorded here is compared again at M5 by BD-410; a change at a pin move
  is a canonicalization defect, never a fixture change.
