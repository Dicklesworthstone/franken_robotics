## Background

When two placements of the same worlds (CPU against a GPU, one GPU family against another, a fast tier against
a slower one) cannot be shown bit-identical, the program compares them under determinism class D3: agreement
inside a recorded envelope, and agreement of task decisions. The second half exists because a state tolerance
and a decision are different things (decision ADR-NUM-03). A success predicate x ≥ 1 m with one placement
ending at 1.0000001 m and the other at 0.9999999 m has a state error of 2·10⁻⁷ m and two different verdicts;
two placements that end a metre apart may still agree that the robot fell.

Class D3 is not a corner case. Bit equality between a device and the CPU reference (class D1) is a HYPOTHESIS
under kill gate KILL-D1: if a device family cannot hold it, that family is class D2 for itself and D3 against
others, with this test as the standard of agreement; a teleported world is then a tolerance replay and
cross-family comparisons are unpaired. Promotion of any fast-tier result to `established` needs class D3 with a
ledger envelope and this test. The test is therefore what stands between "the GPU result looks close" and "the
GPU result may be cited".

**GATE-SPARK-16** (claim: Fidelity; TARGET; earliest M2). For every class D3 pair of placements used as
evidence, on a frozen ensemble of at least 4,096 worlds per reference workload: upper bound on discordant
decisions at most 1%; bound on net one-sided discordance at most 0.5%; continuous outcomes equivalent inside
ledger margins; first divergences recorded. A planted bias of one decision in fifty must fail.

This seed bead is the test as a running capability and the gate: the frozen ensemble, the command, the
integration with the check and with promotion, the planted bias and the gate record. The comparator and the
statistics are their own beads (N35-09, N35-10) and the thresholds are frozen by N35-11. Tier: Solid. It
serves BET-02 (one source, three backends, inside declared determinism classes) and RISK-04 (bit equality
across backends unattainable on a device family).

## Scope

In: the frozen ensemble per reference workload; `frob spark check --decisions`; the registry of placement
pairs "used as evidence"; writing the decision evidence the classifier and the licences read; the promotion
entry point; the planted-bias mutant and the A/A control; the gate record. Out: per-world comparison (N35-09);
statistics (N35-10); thresholds (N35-11); the check's kernel-level comparison and ledger (BD-176); the
end-to-end script (N35-12); the promotion rule itself.

## Technical approach

**Frozen ensemble (one per reference workload; content-hashed before the first run; never edited, only
superseded).** Stored under `frob-conformance/battery/equiv/<workload>/`.

| Field | Workload Q (reference quadruped: 12 joints, 18 degrees of freedom, contact capacity 32, row capacity 160) | Workload H (reference humanoid: 29 joints, 35 degrees of freedom, contact capacity 64, row capacity 320) |
|---|---|---|
| Tasks | Flat velocity tracking; rough-terrain traversal | Stand and recover; flat walking |
| Designs | The reference design and three parameter variants (leg length ±5%, payload +10%) | The reference design and three parameter variants |
| Worlds | 4 designs × 1,024 addresses = 4,096 (run seed fixed, episode 0, world indices 0 to 1,023 per design) | The same |
| Policies (frozen, by content hash) | The synthesized baseline controller; one trained policy checkpoint from the reference training | The baseline controller |
| Episode | 1,000 control intervals at decimation 10 and step 2 ms (20 s) | The same |
| Decisions | Termination cause; success predicate per task; each requirement verdict of the task file; the winner of each of three design comparisons (variant against reference, frozen fixed-n rule); the pass or fail of two gate metrics (success rate floor; energy per metre ceiling) | The same |
| Continuous outcomes | Return; energy per metre; time to failure | The same |

At least 4,096 worlds per reference workload is the gate's floor. The ensemble is larger than the smallest
size at which anything can be shown: with zero discordant worlds the bound is about 3/n, so 299 worlds are the
least that can show 1%, and 4,096 worlds admit thirty discordant worlds.

**Pairs "used as evidence".** A registry (`registries/placement_pairs.toml`, DESIGN) lists every pair of
placements that some result relies on: each device family against `batch-cpu` in `f32`; `gpu-cuda` against
`gpu-metal`; `batch-cpu` `f32` against `batch-cpu` `f64`; `batch-cpu` against the referee on the worlds the
referee admits. A pair whose class is D1 (bit equality attested) is exempt and recorded as such. A pair not in
the registry has no decision record, and the licence check then gives it no D3 rights.

**Command.** `frob spark check --decisions --pair <A>:<B> --workload <Q|H> [--ensemble <hash>]`:
1. Verify the ensemble hash and the threshold record (N35-11). With no frozen thresholds the run proceeds and
   every output is labelled `provisional-thresholds`.
2. Run the ensemble on A and on B under profile `cross-backend`, with identical world addresses, designs,
   scenes, tasks and policy identities, recording checkpoints every 50 control intervals, the energy ledger
   and decision records with margins.
3. Per world, build the comparison record (N35-09): scaled state error at checkpoints until the first
   divergence beyond the envelope; impulse and work residuals; event times; decisions and margins;
   `boundary-sensitive` flags; for each discordant world the first divergence (step, kernel, quantity), which
   is appended to the divergence ledger.
4. Compute the verdict (N35-10): n, k, the upper bound U; b, c, U_net; the two one-sided tests per continuous
   outcome; top-band rank agreement of the four designs; `equivalent`, `not-equivalent` or
   `insufficient-ensemble`.
5. Write the verdict record, link it to both runs, and write the `decisions[pair]` evidence field of the
   determinism record (N35-02) with currency keys (fingerprints, kernel hashes, step plan, profile).
6. Exit 0 on `equivalent`; exit 2 (completed with findings) on `not-equivalent`, with the discordant worlds
   listed; a refusal if the two runs are not a pair.

**Promotion entry point.** `equiv::for_promotion(sample_p, sample_q)` returns the per-world records for the
episodes a promotion re-evaluates on the higher tier, and the standing verdict of the tier pair for the task.
Promotion requires that standing verdict to be `equivalent` and current, and reports any discordant or
`boundary-sensitive` world of its own sample beside the promoted number.

**Controls (run in the same campaign as the gate).**
- *Planted bias, one decision in fifty.* A conformance mutant on placement B inverts the success decision of
  every world whose index within its design is divisible by 50 (about 2% of the 4,096 worlds: 84 with 1,024
  addresses per design; 82 when taken over a flat index 0 to 4,095). The upper bound is then about 2.4%
  (2.4007% at k = 82) and the pair must be `not-equivalent`.
- *A/A.* Placement A against itself: zero discordant worlds, U = 0.0731%, `equivalent`.
- *States only.* The comparator variant with the decision steps removed must report the conformance
  boundary world (the predicate x ≥ 1 m at ±10⁻⁷ m) as agreeing, and the gate test must reject that variant.

**Gate record.** One row of `registries/gates.toml` for GATE-SPARK-16 per pair and workload: commit, command,
ensemble hash, threshold record hash, n, k, U, b, c, U_net, each continuous outcome's bounds against its
margin, the count of first divergences recorded, the verdict, the planted-bias result, both fingerprint
digests, the receipt hash. Entered by a session that did not implement the work.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ensembles for Q and H exist with content hashes; a changed address, design, policy or task changes the hash and the command refuses a mismatching hash.
- [ ] `frob spark check --decisions` runs for every pair in the registry on each machine where both placements exist, and writes a verdict record and a determinism evidence row for each.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-16 per D3 pair and workload: U ≤ 1%; U_net ≤ 0.5%; every continuous outcome equivalent inside its ledger margin; a first-divergence record exists for every discordant world. Measured values are recorded; a pair that fails is recorded as failed and loses its D3 rights.
- [ ] The planted bias of one decision in fifty fails the test on every pair (seed done-condition), with U above 2%.
- [ ] The A/A control is `equivalent` with zero discordant worlds; the states-only variant is rejected by the gate test.
- [ ] With fewer than 4,096 worlds the command returns `insufficient-ensemble` and no gate row is written.
- [ ] Verdicts computed before the threshold freeze are labelled `provisional-thresholds` and are not accepted by the gate recorder.
- [ ] A pair of class D1 is recorded as exempt with its bit-equality receipt; no D3 verdict is fabricated for it.
- [ ] The capability is reachable through `frob --json`; cancellation of a running check leaves no partial verdict record and no leaked device resources.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `ensemble_hash_frozen`: the registered hash equals the directory's content hash; one changed world index changes it.
- `pairs_registry_complete`: every pair named by a promotion rule, a teleport path or a `paired-by-address` licence in the test fixtures is in the registry.
- `planted_bias_selects_one_in_fifty`: over indices 0 to 4,095 the mutant selects 82 worlds; per design (0 to 1,023) it selects 21, so 84 over four designs.
- `aa_pair_is_equivalent`: a run compared with itself.
- `states_only_variant_rejected`.
- `insufficient_ensemble_no_gate_row`: 2,048 worlds.
- `provisional_label_blocks_gate_record`.
- `d1_pair_exempt`.
- `verdict_evidence_row_currency`: changing a fingerprint digest after the run makes the evidence row stale for the classifier.
- `short_pair_cpu`: `batch-cpu` `f32` against `f64` on 512 worlds of Q for 100 control intervals: the pipeline runs end to end and returns `insufficient-ensemble` with all per-world records present.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_decision_equivalence.sh` is bead N35-12. It runs this command for each pair present, the
three controls, and the gate test `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g5 -- GATE-SPARK-16`, logging through
the STD-E2E helper every `cmd` with exact command line, exit code and wall time, the evidence block (claim
fidelity, class, tier pair, determinism D3, identities with both fingerprint digests, seed), `metric` lines
for n, k, U (threshold 1%), b, c, U_net (threshold 0.5%), each continuous outcome's bounds (thresholds ±Δ),
first divergences recorded, and on failure the first discordant world address with its decision, margins and
the paths of both episode records.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-788 (the boundary predicate is reported discordant and `boundary-sensitive`); KA-789 (299 worlds are the
  least that can show 1%; 4,096 admit thirty; one in fifty fails).
- CX-364 (a state tolerance does not imply agreement on the verdict); CX-365 (zero discordances on a small
  ensemble show nothing).
- Negative controls: the planted bias; the states-only variant. A third plant, an inversion of one decision
  in two hundred (about 20 worlds of 4,096), is run for the record and not as a pass condition: criterion 1
  admits 20 discordances, and criterion 2 catches the plant only if the inversions fall mostly in one
  direction (12 or more net). Its outcome is recorded so that the test's resolution is on file.
- Metamorphic: swapping A and B swaps b and c and leaves the verdict unchanged; permuting world slots on
  either placement leaves every record unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: each placement of the ensemble twice (identical digests, class D2 for itself); the
  verdict computation twice and across worker counts (identical records).

## Considerations

- Kill gate KILL-D1 (decided at M2 per device family). Trigger: GATE-SPARK-01 or GATE-SPARK-02 shows that a
  family cannot hold bit equality with `batch-cpu` for the floating kernels of the step. Fallback: the family
  is class D2 for itself and D3 against others with this test; a teleported world is labelled a tolerance
  replay; a comparison whose arms ran on different families is unpaired. This bead must therefore be ready
  whether or not the kill gate fires.
- OPEN-NUM-2 (thresholds and ensemble size; default as stated; owner Spark lead) blocks the gate and is
  decided by N35-11 before any real pair is compared.
- The M2 no-GPU variant: the gate then runs on the CPU pairs only (`batch-cpu` `f32` against `f64`;
  `batch-cpu` against the referee) and the device pairs are recorded `not run` with the reason.
- Compute: 4,096 worlds × 10⁴ world-steps is 4·10⁷ world-steps per placement and workload; seconds on a GPU
  and minutes on `batch-cpu`, inside the default envelope. The referee pair is limited to the worlds the
  referee admits and may need the gate-measurement class; without approval that row is `not run`.
- A result that is class D3 is statistical: the failure-mode review lists "class D3 comparisons are
  statistical" as the residual risk of this whole mechanism.
