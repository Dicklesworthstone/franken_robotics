## Background

The `fast-training` determinism profile is the one sanctioned use of class D4 (no reproducibility guarantee).
It leaves the physics step at class D2 and relaxes only three things in the learner: (a) the learner's
reduction order and matrix-product implementation (the matrix-multiply kernel `K-gemm` may use a device matrix
path); (b) minibatch composition by arrival; (c) the order in which shards deliver collected segments and
gradient sums, that is, asynchronous collection across two devices with a declared bound on version lag. Its
only output is a set of weights tagged `trained_under: D4`.

The program's rule for every Frontier mechanism is that a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier runs before the
work is funded, and that a speed experiment states its computed ceiling first: an experiment whose ceiling is
below its goal is refused (`FROB-REF-866`), because it cannot confirm anything. The asynchronous variant is
Frontier, under kill gate KILL-SHARD. The chapter itself states the ceiling of relaxations (a) and (b) before
any test: inference and learning together are about 13% of training wall-clock on one incumbent stack (FACT,
source register), so removing both entirely gains at most 1/(1 − 0.13) ≈ 1.15 on one device. The profile's
real use is therefore (c), and (c) lives or dies with two-device scaling.

FACT (source register): an incumbent's own two-card training run takes about 325 s against about 410 s on one
card, a gain of 1.26; about 45 s of the 410 s is compilation, the learner runs on one stream, and sample
efficiency falls as the batch grows. KILL-SHARD: after one optimization pass, if the paired lower bound of
strong scaling at equal task quality is below 1.6, no further sharding work is funded (asynchronous collection
and the `fast-training` profile's use of it included); the two cards then run as two independent job lanes.

This bead is the pilot: arithmetic on measured phase shares plus one small lag study, recorded before
N35-06 admits any relaxation knob. It serves decision ADR-NUM-05 and BET-02 by making sure that giving up
reproducibility in the learner is done only where it can pay.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the ceiling formulas and their evaluation on our own measured shares; the lag study;
the falsifier; the pilot record with a verdict per knob. Out: the profile's label, tag and rules (N35-06,
Solid, not gated by this pilot); the asynchronous update itself and the device matrix path (the learning and
performance units' beads, built only if this pilot funds them); KILL-SHARD's measurement (BD-199).

## Technical approach

**Inputs (all measured before this pilot; none measured by it on devices).**
- The phase ledger of our own reference training (BD-198, through the performance harness BD-216): per
  iteration, device-timed shares of environment step s_env, policy inference s_inf, update s_learn and other
  s_other, with s_env + s_inf + s_learn + s_other = 1.
- The incumbent phase ledger on host `trj` (BD-040), for context only.
- The synchronous two-shard result and the KILL-SHARD record (BD-199): one-device time T₁, two-device time
  T₂, compile and fixed time T_fix, transfer and synchronization time per iteration t_x.

**Ceilings (computed first, written into the record before the lag study runs).**
1. *Learner relaxation on one device.* If the relaxed learner and inference run g times faster, training
   speeds up by 1/(1 − (s_inf + s_learn)·(1 − 1/g)), which is at most C_a = 1/(1 − s_inf − s_learn) as g → ∞.
   With the incumbent's 13% the ceiling is 1.149.
2. *Asynchronous collection on two devices.* Let t_c be the collection time and t_l the update time of one
   iteration on one device (t_c + t_l = T_iter). Synchronous two-shard: T_sync = t_c/2 + t_l + t_x.
   Asynchronous, collectors never waiting for the learner: T_async ≥ max(t_c/2, t_l). The ceiling of the
   asynchronous variant over the synchronous one is C_c = T_sync / max(t_c/2, t_l), before any loss of sample
   efficiency. Illustration with t_l/T_iter = 0.13 and t_x = 0: T_sync = 0.565·T_iter, T_async ≥ 0.435·T_iter,
   C_c = 1.30. Fixed time lowers it: with a fixed share F of the job, the job-level ceiling is
   1/(F + (1 − F)/C_c).
3. *Goal (DESIGN, declared before data).* A knob is worth building if the paired lower bound of its gain in
   time to threshold at equal task quality can reach 1.10 for (a) and (b) together and 1.15 for (c). A ceiling
   below its goal ends that knob here with `FROB-REF-866` recorded as the reason.

**The lag study (paper-scale; existing tools; runs only for a knob whose ceiling clears its goal).**
1. Task: a small CPU control task with the suite's CPU trainer (a point-mass reach or a cart-pole), PPO with
   the program's update rules, 16 seeds.
2. Arms: collection with weights that are ℓ ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4} updates stale (ℓ = 0 is the synchronous arm);
   arms are paired by seed and world address.
3. Measure r(ℓ) = (updates to reach the threshold return at lag ℓ) / (updates at lag 0), as a paired ratio
   with its one-sided 95% upper bound exp(ℓ̄ + t·s/√n) on the log ratios (t = 1.753 for n = 16).
4. The lag the asynchronous schedule would produce is ℓ* = ⌈t_l / (t_c/2)⌉ (how many collection rounds
   complete during one update). The declared bound on version lag for the profile would be ℓ*.
5. Net gain bound: G = C_c / r_upper(ℓ*).

**Potency and A/A controls.** A planted slowdown (every update at lag 0 repeated twice, a true ratio of 2.0)
must be measured as r ≥ 1.8; an A/A comparison (lag 0 against lag 0 with different pairing labels) must not
give a lower bound above 1.02.

**Falsifier (any one closes the corresponding knob as `not funded`).**
- KILL-SHARD fired: knob (c) is closed without further work.
- C_a from our measured shares is below 1.10: knobs (a) and (b) are closed.
- C_c (job level) is below 1.15: knob (c) is closed.
- G is below 1.15 at ℓ*: knob (c) is closed (the stale policy costs more samples than the schedule saves).

**Fallback, named in advance.** The profile keeps its label, its tag and its refusals (they are needed for any
D4 training, including training with analytic gradients, and for the gates that prove D4 results are refused),
with only the knobs this pilot funded. If none is funded, `--determinism fast-training` is accepted only for
jobs that declare another registered D4 reason, and otherwise returns a capability refusal that names this
record.

**Record.** One entry in the pilot register: mechanism (`fast-training` relaxations), tier (Frontier for (c);
Solid for the label), protocol hash (frozen before step 1 of the lag study), the measured shares, C_a, C_c,
r(ℓ) with bounds, ℓ*, G, the verdict per knob (`funded` or `not funded: <reason>`), cost, tool pins.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol file is committed with its content hash before any lag-study run; the record cites that hash.
- [ ] C_a and C_c are computed from our own phase ledger and the BD-199 record, with the formulas above, and written to the record before the lag study starts (timestamps in the record show the order).
- [ ] A knob whose ceiling is below its goal is closed with the reason `ceiling-below-goal` and the refusal code `FROB-REF-866`; no lag study is run for it.
- [ ] If KILL-SHARD fired, the record closes knob (c) citing the kill decision, and nothing else is run for it.
- [ ] The lag study reports r(ℓ) for ℓ ∈ {1, 2, 4} as paired ratios with one-sided 95% bounds over 16 seeds; the planted slowdown is detected (r ≥ 1.8) and the A/A arm stays at or below 1.02.
- [ ] The record states a verdict for each of the three knobs, and N35-06's knob registry reads those verdicts.
- [ ] Cost stays paper-scale: no device-hours beyond the measurements already recorded by BD-198 and BD-199; the lag study fits the default envelope (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `ceiling_learner_known_answer`: s_inf + s_learn = 0.13 → C_a = 1.1494 (to four places); g = 2 gives 1.0695.
- `ceiling_async_known_answer`: t_l = 0.13, t_c = 0.87, t_x = 0 → T_sync = 0.565, max(t_c/2, t_l) = 0.435, C_c = 1.2989; with F = 45/410 the job-level ceiling is 1.2576.
- `ceiling_async_learner_bound`: t_l = 0.6, t_c = 0.4 → max = 0.6, C_c = (0.2 + 0.6)/0.6 = 1.3333 (the learner is the bottleneck).
- `lag_star_formula`: t_l = 0.13, t_c = 0.87 → ℓ* = ⌈0.13/0.435⌉ = 1; t_l = 0.6, t_c = 0.4 → ℓ* = 3.
- `paired_upper_bound_constant_ratio`: sixteen pairs each with ratio exactly 1.5 give s = 0 and a bound of 1.5.
- `falsifier_table`: four synthetic records (labelled `synthetic`), one per falsifier clause, each produce `not funded` with the right reason; a record clearing all gives `funded`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_pilot_fast_training.sh [--ledger <id>] [--seed N]`: (1) `frob --json fidelity ledger` and the
performance harness's report verb to read the phase shares and the two-shard record; (2) compute and print the
ceilings; (3) if a knob survives, run the lag study through `frob --json train start` on the small CPU task
with the lag option of the conformance build, for the three lags and the two controls; (4) write the pilot
record and re-read it. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/num_pilot_fast_training/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit
code and wall time; the evidence block of each envelope (class, tier, determinism: the lag arms are D4 and are
logged as such, never as evidence for a policy); `metric` lines for s_inf, s_learn, C_a, C_c, ℓ*, r(ℓ) with
bounds and G against their goals; `assert` lines for the controls; a `summary` with the verdict per knob.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The Amdahl figures of the performance chapter: 13% → 1.15; an environment step of 87% → 7.7; physics at 29%
  of the environment step → 1.4. The incumbent two-card gain 1.26 (325 s against 410 s).
- Negative controls: the A/A arm; the planted slowdown; a planted record with a ceiling of 1.05 and a goal of
  1.15 must be refused with `FROB-REF-866`.
- Determinism: the arithmetic is exact rational where the inputs are; the lag study's arms are D4 by
  construction and its only output is the ratio table, which is cited as a pilot result (class Estimated) and
  never as a Performance claim.

## Considerations

- The lag study is a proxy on a small task. It can falsify (a large efficiency loss at lag 1 on a toy task is
  bad news) more reliably than it can confirm; a `funded` verdict funds a measured experiment on the reference
  workload under the performance harness, not the mechanism's adoption.
- OPEN-PERF questions on the reference step and decimation move the shares; the pilot is re-run if the M2
  profile is re-measured.
- Analytic-gradient training is another declared user of the labelled D4 profile; it needs no knob from this
  pilot and is unaffected by its verdict.
- RISK-46 (scaling across two cards vanishes under transfer cost) is the risk this pilot prices before code is
  written.
