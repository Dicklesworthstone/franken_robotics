## Background

Two placements of the same worlds that are not bit-identical are compared under determinism class D3 by the
decision-level equivalence test: per world, do the task decisions (termination cause, success predicates,
requirement verdicts, comparison winners, gate metrics) agree? The pair is decision-equivalent when four
criteria hold: the one-sided 95% exact binomial upper bound on the rate of discordant worlds is at most 1%; the
bound on the net one-sided rate is at most 0.5%; continuous outcomes are equivalent by two one-sided tests
inside the fidelity ledger's margins; and the top of the design ranking agrees. The gate that uses the test,
GATE-SPARK-16, runs it for every class D3 pair of placements used as evidence, on a frozen ensemble of at least
4,096 worlds per reference workload, and requires that a planted bias of one decision in fifty fails.

The numbers 1%, 0.5% and 4,096 are TARGETs. OPEN-NUM-2 is the open question that owns them: "the
decision-equivalence thresholds (1% and 0.5%) and the ensemble size. Default: as stated, frozen once at M2 with
a written rationale. Blocks GATE-SPARK-16." Owner: the Spark lead. This bead is that decision. It must be made
**before** the gate is run on real placement pairs: thresholds chosen after seeing the discordance of our own
backends would be fitted to the result (the program's rule for every method experiment: the threshold and its
ceiling are written down before the first run).

What is at stake in each number:

- A looser discordance threshold admits backends whose results disagree with the CPU reference on more worlds,
  and every promoted or paired result inherits that disagreement. A tighter one may be unattainable for
  contact-rich tasks, where a world near a predicate boundary legitimately falls either way.
- The ensemble size sets what can be shown at all. With zero discordant worlds the bound is about 3/n: 299
  worlds are the least that can show 1%, and 598 the least that can show 0.5%.
- The net-rate estimator is a design choice left open by the criterion (N35-10 implements one: opposite
  endpoints of two one-sided 97.5% exact bounds on the two directional rates).

## Scope

In: the evidence to assemble; the candidate set; the decision procedure; the record (registry row and ADR);
the fail-closed default in force until the record exists; what must be re-run if the record ever changes.
Out: the statistics (N35-10); the comparator (N35-09); the gate run (BD-177); the ledger's margins for
continuous outcomes (BD-212), which this decision cites and does not set.

## Technical approach

**Fail-closed default (in force until this bead closes).** The thresholds as stated (1%, 0.5%, at least 4,096
worlds, 95% one-sided, the estimators of N35-10, rank criterion τ ≥ 0.7 on the lower bootstrap bound with
top-design agreement). Until the record exists, the verdict function labels every result
`provisional-thresholds`, and a provisional verdict cannot license promotion or `paired-by-address` pairing,
and cannot be recorded as a pass of GATE-SPARK-16.

**Evidence to assemble (no real placement pair is inspected for discordance before the freeze).**
1. *Operating characteristics of criterion 1*, exact, from the helper of N35-10. Reference values:

   | n | Largest admitted k | Pass probability at true rate 0.25% | 0.5% | 0.75% | 1% | 1.5% |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1,024 | 4 | 0.883 | 0.419 | 0.119 | 0.025 | 0.001 |
   | 4,096 | 30 | 1.000 | 0.982 | 0.496 | 0.045 | 0.000 |
   | 8,192 | 66 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.745 | 0.040 | 0.000 |
   | 16,384 | 142 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.960 | 0.045 | 0.000 |

   Reading: at 4,096 worlds a pair whose true discordance is 0.5% passes 98% of the time and one at 1% passes
   under 5% of the time; at 1,024 worlds a pair at 0.5% fails more often than it passes.
2. *Operating characteristics of criterion 2* for one-sided and balanced splits (at n = 4,096 a one-sided
   count of at most 11 passes and 12 fails; a balanced 15 against 15 passes; 20 against 10 fails).
3. *Boundary density of the reference tasks*, measured on one placement only (`batch-cpu` in double
   precision): the fraction of worlds in which some decision has a margin smaller than the divergence envelope
   of its deciding quantity (the envelope N35-09's boundary flag uses: the ledger's frozen one where BD-212,
   not a dependency of this bead, has frozen it, else the provisional one, and the bundle records which). This
   is an upper estimate of how many worlds could legitimately flip between two
   placements that agree inside the envelope. It is computed from margins alone and needs no second placement.
4. *Cost*: the wall time of 4,096 and 8,192 worlds per reference workload per placement pair on each machine,
   against the compute envelope (its classes and the device-hour meter are the M0 enforcement bead N36-20).
   The figure is the measured wall time of item 3's own run on `batch-cpu`, scaled by world count, and for a
   device placement the wall time of a timing run of 256 worlds on that device alone, scaled by world count
   (one placement at a time; no comparison is computed from it); each figure is labelled `measured` or
   `scaled from <n> worlds`. The cost verb `sim.estimate` (BD-219) is not a prerequisite: it is
   built on the throughput rows, which wait on the reference training, which waits on the gate this decision
   blocks, so a dependency on it would close a cycle. Where `sim.estimate` already exists its interval is
   quoted beside the measured figure.
5. *The planted control at each candidate*: the probability that a one-in-fifty one-sided bias fails (it must
   be 1 to within rounding at every candidate retained: at n = 4,096 the expected count is 82 and the bound is
   2.40%).

**Candidates.** (A) the default; (B) the default with n = 8,192; (C) 0.5% and 0.25% at n = 8,192 (for
workloads whose boundary density is below 0.1%); (D) 2% and 1% at n = 4,096 (only if the boundary density of a
reference task exceeds 0.5%, with the reason written).

**Procedure.**
1. Compute items 1, 2, 4 and 5 for each candidate; measure item 3 for workloads Q (reference quadruped) and H
   (reference humanoid) on the frozen ensemble's addresses. The ensemble is frozen by BD-177, which depends on
   this decision; its addresses are therefore generated here from BD-177's stated construction (four designs
   × 1,024 world indices, episode 0, one fixed run seed, recorded with the evidence), so that the density
   measured is the density of the ensemble BD-177 will freeze.
2. Reject a candidate whose planted control can pass with probability above 10⁻⁶, whose ensemble cannot run
   inside an approvable compute class, or whose discordance threshold is below twice the measured boundary
   density of a reference task (it would fail honest pairs by construction).
3. Among the rest prefer the default unless a rejection in step 2 removes it; a stricter candidate is chosen
   only if it costs under twice the default's wall time.
4. The Spark lead decides; the owner is asked only if the choice departs from the default.
5. Freeze: write the threshold record and the ADR; from that commit on, the verdict function reads the record
   and drops the `provisional-thresholds` label.

**Record.** A row in `registries/decisions.toml` (`id = "OPEN-NUM-2"`, `status = "decided"`, `p_max`,
`net_max`, `n_min`, `confidence`, `net_estimator`, `rank_tau_min`, `rank_requires_top1`, `evidence` (receipt
hashes of the five items), `decided_by`, `date`, `commit`) and `docs/adr/ADR-NUM-OPEN2.md` with the rationale,
the alternatives rejected and the re-run list. The record is content-hashed; every decision-equivalence verdict
names the hash.

**If the record is ever changed** (it is "frozen once"; a change needs new evidence and a new ADR): every
stored class D3 verdict is recomputed from its per-world records under the new thresholds, GATE-SPARK-16 is
re-recorded, and every promotion or `paired-by-address` licence that cited an old verdict is revalidated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Before the freeze commit, no verdict in the ledger carries a label other than `provisional-thresholds`, and GATE-SPARK-16 has no pass record.
- [ ] The evidence bundle contains the five items with receipt hashes; the operating-characteristic table is regenerated by a test and equals the reference values above to three decimals.
- [ ] Item 3 (boundary density) is measured on one placement only; the bundle's command log shows no decision-level comparison of a real placement pair before the freeze commit.
- [ ] `registries/decisions.toml` has the OPEN-NUM-2 row with every field, and `docs/adr/ADR-NUM-OPEN2.md` states the decision, the reasons, the rejected candidates and the re-run list.
- [ ] After the freeze, the verdict function returns unlabelled verdicts that name the record's hash; changing any threshold field changes the hash and re-labels stored verdicts as stale.
- [ ] The planted one-in-fifty bias fails under the frozen thresholds (asserted by BD-177).
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement N35-09 or N35-10.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `oc_table_reference_values`: the table above (16 pass probabilities and four admitted counts).
- `n_min_for_threshold`: 149, 299 and 598 worlds for 2%, 1% and 0.5%.
- `planted_control_fails_at_every_candidate`: at (1%, 4,096), (1%, 8,192), (0.5%, 8,192) and (2%, 4,096) a one-sided 2% bias has pass probability below 10⁻⁶ under criteria 1 and 2 together.
- `provisional_label_until_record`: with no decision row, verdicts are labelled; with a row, they are not and they carry its hash.
- `threshold_record_hash_binds_fields`: flipping each field changes the hash.
- `boundary_density_one_placement`: the density function takes one run and an envelope table; calling it with two placements is a type error.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_open2_evidence.sh [--seed N]`: (1) `cargo test -p frob-chronicle oc_` to regenerate the
operating-characteristic tables; (2) `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --precision f64 --workload <Q|H>
--designs <the four designs> --worlds-per-design 1024 --seed <run seed> --key o2-density-<Q|H>-<run-id>
--json` (4,096 worlds; `--worlds-per-design` is the row's spelling with `--designs`) on the
frozen ensemble's addresses (four designs × 1,024 world indices, episode 0) for workloads Q and H, then the
boundary-density report from the stored margins (`cargo run -p frob-chronicle --release --example
boundary_density -- --run <run>`); (3) the cost table for the candidate ensemble sizes on each placement pair,
from the wall time step (2) logged and a 256-world timing run on each device present (`frob sim run --tier
strict:<backend> --workload <Q|H> --worlds 256 --seconds 5 --key o2-timing-<backend>-<run-id> --json`), with
`frob sim estimate --json` beside it where that verb exists (a `skip` event `capability absent` otherwise, never
counted as passed); (4) assemble the evidence bundle and print its hash.
Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/num_open2_evidence/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines
with exact command line, exit code and wall time; the evidence block; `metric` lines for each pass
probability, each boundary density with its envelope, each cost estimate with its compute class; `assert`
lines that no pair comparison was invoked; a `summary` line with the bundle hash. The script never calls
`spark check --decisions` on a real pair.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-789 (the bound arithmetic: 299; 30 at 4,096; one in fifty fails).
- Negative controls: a planted attempt to freeze thresholds after a real pair comparison exists in the ledger
  (the freeze check refuses and names the comparison); a planted record with `p_max` above its ADR's stated
  value (hash mismatch).
- Determinism: the tables are exact computations and identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- If the decision is late, GATE-SPARK-16 stays unrecorded and every class D3 pair stays provisional, which
  blocks promotion of device results on a family that lacks class D1. That is the intended pressure.
- Under kill gate KILL-D1's fallback (a device family is class D2 for itself and D3 against others) this
  decision governs every cross-family statement the program makes about that family.
- The thresholds apply per (task, placement pair); a task with a deliberately knife-edge success predicate
  will show a high boundary density, and the right repair is in the task (a margin in the requirement), not a
  looser threshold.
- OPEN-NUM-2's row in the open-question register is closed by this bead's record.
