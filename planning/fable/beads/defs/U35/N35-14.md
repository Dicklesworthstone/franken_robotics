## Background

**Statement (INV-NUM-03).** Given a policy (weights and normalizers), a world's trajectory is a function of
its address, design, scene and task alone. It is the same bits when the world runs alone, in any batch, at any
slot, beside any co-tenants, in any bucket whose capacities it fits, and on any number of shards of one device
family. (A world address is `(run_seed, world, episode)`; a slot is a world's row position in a batch; a
bucket is a set of padded capacities for which one step plan is compiled; a shard is a part of a job on one
device.)

FACT (source register): an incumbent engine that documents bitwise replay of a batch holds a world equal to
its isolated run only to 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ in its own tests. The property is therefore worth having only if it is
exact. Decision ADR-NUM-02 makes it part of class D2 because the studio forks and teleports single worlds:
slot dispatch in the daemon, teleport of one world to the CPU, counterfactual forks and the memo graph all
rest on it.

The property is not obtained by testing alone. It follows from eight design conditions, each a rule somewhere
in the system:

1. No kernel reads or writes another world's rows (INV-SPARK-04).
2. A per-world reduction is a pairwise tree anchored at index 0 over power-of-two blocks, so which two active
   elements are added first does not depend on capacity.
3. Padding neutrality (INV-NUM-02): padding trails the active lanes and holds the neutral element of its
   consumer.
4. A mask is a select.
5. The number of solver iterations launched is a constant of the shape class; a world whose `done` mask is
   set is copied forward, not recomputed.
6. Randomness, resets and task assignment are functions of the world address.
7. Sort keys are total orders within a world.
8. Statistics across worlds never feed state. Observation normalizers are frozen within a collection segment
   and are constants of the policy in evaluation.

The dynamic gate (GATE-SPARK-15, BD-181) shows that digests are equal on the cases it runs. This bead is the
other half: an audit that checks each condition where it can be checked structurally, and a smallest-case
fixture per condition that fails when the condition is broken. An invariance test that passes while a
condition is violated on an unexercised path is the failure this guards against. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the audit over manifests and step plans; the eight fixtures; the audit report and where it is stored; the
normalizer rule as a tested contract. Out: the mechanisms that satisfy the conditions (reductions N35-13;
select lint BD-053; addressed draws BD-180; the environment's normalizer BD-192; the step plan's iteration
constants in the dynamics unit); the mutant kernels (N35-15); the draw-table test (N35-16); the gate (BD-181).

## Technical approach

**Audit (`frob_conformance::batch_conditions::audit(step_plan) -> Report`; schema `frob.batchcond/1`).**
For a step plan and its kernel manifests, one row per condition: `static` (pass, fail, or not-checkable),
`fixture` (pass or fail), evidence identifiers.

| # | Static check | Fixture (smallest case; must fail when the condition is broken) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every load and store in every state kernel indexes the kernel's own world lane; in the step plan's data-flow graph (built from the per-launch read and write slab sets of the step-plan record `frob.stepplan/1`, N21ap1-01) no output slab of a `stat` kernel is an input of a state kernel | *Poisoned neighbours*: run world w with every other world's rows filled with NaN patterns and with 10³⁰; w's digests equal its solo digests |
| 2 | Every padded-axis reduction in a manifest is declared `pairwise-anchored` | The three-element case a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1 at capacities 4 and 8: result 1 at both |
| 3 | Every reduced buffer declares its neutral element, and the initializer writes that element | The single active element −0.0 summed at two capacities: 0x80000000 both times |
| 4 | The select lint: no kernel forms m·a + (1 − m)·b from a mask | A masked lane whose unselected arm holds +∞: the selected value is returned unchanged (an arithmetic blend gives NaN, since 0·∞ is NaN) |
| 5 | Every launch count in the step plan is a literal or a shape-class capacity; no launch depends on a readback | A batch of two worlds in which one terminates at interval 10: the other's digests equal its solo digests for 100 intervals, and the terminated world's state slab is bit-identical from interval 10 until its reset |
| 6 | Every kernel input of kind `random` is produced by the addressed-draw kernel with the world's address words; reset and task-assignment kernels take no slot index | A world reset while in slot 0 of a batch of 4 and while in slot 3 of a batch of 64: identical reset pose bits and identical task assignment |
| 7 | Every sort declares a key of (value, then stable index within the world) | Four contacts with equal depth keys in one world, at two capacities and two slots: identical order (by index) |
| 8 | Normalizer statistics are produced by a `stat` kernel; the buffer the policy reads is versioned and is written only at a segment boundary; in evaluation the normalizer is covered by the policy's content hash | *Two worlds observing 1 and 3*: under a frozen normalizer each world's normalized observation and action equal those of its isolated run |

**The normalizer rule in full (condition 8).** During collection, per-world observation statistics accumulate
in a `stat` buffer as integer counts and floating sums. The normalizer the policy applies is the copy frozen
at the start of the collection segment (the rollout between two learner updates). At the segment boundary the
host merges the statistics in address order (a sum of sums, divided by the global count), produces the next
frozen copy, and that copy becomes part of the behaviour policy for the next segment. In evaluation the
normalizer is a constant of the policy: `PolicyId` covers weights and normalizers, so "given a policy" in the
statement above includes them. Worked case: two worlds observing 1 and 3 and normalized by their batch mean 2
see −1 and +1; the first world alone has mean 1 and sees 0, takes a different action and follows a different
trajectory. With a frozen normalizer (μ₀, σ₀) the first world sees (1 − μ₀)/σ₀ in both runs.

**Why "copied forward, not recomputed" (condition 5).** In lockstep every world of a shape class runs the same
number of solver iterations, so a world never gets more or fewer iterations because of its neighbours. A
world whose episode has ended is not stepped again with stale inputs: its committed state is copied forward
under a select until its reset, so its bits cannot depend on how long its co-tenants keep running.

**Report and storage.** The report is stored beside the step plan's check rows in the divergence ledger and is
one of the inputs the determinism classifier requires before it treats a `composition` record as complete
(a passing sweep with a failing or missing audit row is recorded `composition: pass (audit incomplete)` and
does not attest class D2).

**Surface.** `frob spark check --conditions [--plan <id>]` prints the eight rows. No new verb.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For the step plans of workloads Q (reference quadruped) and H (reference humanoid) on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal, the audit reports all eight static checks `pass` (none `not-checkable`) and all eight fixtures `pass`.
- [ ] Each fixture fails when its condition is broken by the plant of its unit test below, and the audit row names the kernel at fault. (That each of N35-15's ten mutants is caught by these fixtures is N35-15's acceptance; N35-15 depends on this bead.)
- [ ] The poisoned-neighbours fixture runs on every backend and leaves the tested world's digest chain equal to its solo chain over 1,000 control intervals.
- [ ] In the data-flow graph of each step plan there is no path from a `stat` output to a state input; a planted edge is reported with both kernel identifiers.
- [ ] The two-worlds fixture (observations 1 and 3) gives each world actions bit-identical to its isolated run, in collection and in evaluation; the normalizer buffer's version changes only at segment boundaries (asserted over three segments).
- [ ] `PolicyId` changes when a normalizer constant changes (the normalizer is inside the hashed content).
- [ ] The audit report is written per step plan with evidence identifiers and is read by the classifier.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `audit_rows_are_eight`: the report has exactly the eight rows above.
- `static_footprint_own_lane`: a manifest whose load uses `lane + 1` fails row 1.
- `static_stat_never_feeds_state`: a step plan with a planted edge from the normalizer statistics to the policy input within a segment fails rows 1 and 8.
- `static_launch_counts_literal`: a plan whose solve launch count is read from a device buffer fails row 5.
- `static_sort_key_total`: a sort declared with key (value) only fails row 7.
- `fixture_poisoned_neighbours`, `fixture_grouping_1e8`, `fixture_minus_zero_two_capacities`, `fixture_select_with_infinity`, `fixture_done_world_copied_forward`, `fixture_reset_by_address`, `fixture_equal_keys_ordered_by_index`, `fixture_two_worlds_one_and_three` (one per row; each asserts bit patterns).
- `batch_mean_normalizer_breaks_isolation`: the same two worlds under the mutant normalizer: world 1 sees −1 in the pair and 0 alone, and the fixture reports the mismatch.
- `policy_id_covers_normalizer`.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead's own lane is `scripts/e2e/batch_conditions.sh [--backend batch-cpu|gpu-cuda|gpu-metal] --seed <s>`:
for workloads Q and H on each backend present, `frob spark check --conditions --workload <Q|H> --backend <b>
--seed <s> --key "$(e2e_key bc-<q|h>-<b>)" --json` (the eight rows: static result, fixture result, evidence
identifiers; none `not-checkable`); then `frob artifact read <report>` for each stored `frob.batchcond/1` report,
asserting it equals the envelope; then, in the conformance build, the planted stat-to-state edge and the
`lane + 1` footprint of the unit tests through `cargo test -p frob-conformance --release batch_conditions::`,
each row failing with its kernel named. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/batch_conditions/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`,
`cmd` with exit code and wall time, the evidence block, one `assert` line per condition and backend (static
result, fixture result, evidence identifier), and on failure the kernel identifier, the slab and lane, and both
bit patterns; a `summary` line. The later lane is `scripts/e2e/num_batch_invariance.sh` (N35-17), step
`conditions`, beside the mutants of N35-15 and the dynamic gate.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-787: two worlds observing 1 and 3; each world's actions equal those of its isolated run. Negative
  control: a normalizer updated inside the segment.
- KA-786 (row 3) and the mask known answer of the kernel dialect (`select(pred, a, b)` returns the selected
  arm unchanged; the blend returns NaN).
- CX-368 (a batch-wide observation normalizer is harmless: it is not).
- Negative controls: the mutants of N35-15, one per row.
- Metamorphic: permuting world indices permutes results and changes nothing else; adding inactive worlds or
  unused capacity changes no active world's digest.
- Determinism lanes: the audit is a pure function of manifests and plans (identical output twice); the
  fixtures run twice and across worker counts on `batch-cpu`.

## Considerations

- Static checks depend on manifests being truthful. The kernel toolchain derives footprints from the kernel
  program, not from a hand-written declaration; a hand-written kernel without a derived footprint is
  `not-checkable` and blocks the row.
- A learner's reward normalization or advantage normalization across the batch is not state of a world and
  is allowed; it changes weights, not a world's trajectory given the weights. What is forbidden is any
  cross-world statistic entering physics, observations, resets or actions within a segment.
- Under the labelled `fast-training` profile the learner may reorder its own reductions; conditions 1 to 8
  still hold for the physics and for observations.
- Family batches (many designs in one batch) add a ninth concern, that a variant's rows are read only by its
  own world; it is condition 1 applied to the variant tables and is tested by the family unit's gate.
