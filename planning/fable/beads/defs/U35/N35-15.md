## Background

The batch-composition invariance gate (GATE-SPARK-15) asserts that a world's per-world digests are identical
when it runs alone, in 100 random co-tenancies, under slot permutation, at two bucket capacities and on one
and two shards. The gate's own text requires that "both negative controls" of the chapter's known-answer table
are caught, and the program's general rule is that a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing: a negative
control is a planted defect the test must catch.

The known-answer table gives three controls that concern this gate: *a kernel with a batch-wide floating
reduction feeding state* (against "one world alone, in 100 random co-tenancies, at two capacities, on two
shards gives identical digests"); *padding with +0.0* (against "the sum of the one active element −0.0 at two
capacities is −0.0 both times"); and *a normalizer updated inside the segment* (against "two worlds observing
1 and 3 each act as in their isolated run"). The metamorphic-relation register adds *a reduction tree shaped
by capacity* and *a batch-wide normalizer* as the mutations that must break batch-composition invariance, and
BET-02 (one source, three backends, inside declared determinism classes) names *a planted read of another
world's rows* as a control that must be detected.

A sweep over five arrangement axes can pass for the wrong reason: for example a slot-permutation lane that
never actually permutes. So each axis needs at least one mutant that only that axis can catch, and each mutant
must be shown to fail where it should and pass where it should (specificity). This bead builds those mutants.
Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: ten mutants (dialect variants with Rust twins for `batch-cpu`, or host seams), their registry rows, the
worked values the tests assert, the specificity matrix, the two fixture worlds some mutants need. Out: the
sweep and the gate (BD-181); the run-to-run mutants of the device lanes (the Spark unit) and of the CPU lanes
(N35-03); the draw-table test (N35-16).

## Technical approach

**Loading.** In a conformance build, `--mutant <name>` substitutes one variant kernel in the step plan or
flips one host seam. A variant kernel has its own manifest, its own kernel identity and its own cache key, so
it can never be served from, or pollute, a production cache entry. Every receipt and envelope of a mutant run
carries `conformance_mutant: <name>`, and the ledger stores the run as non-citable. A release build has
neither the option nor the variants.

**Axes.** `alone` (the world by itself against any batch), `cotenancy` (the same slot and capacity, different
neighbours), `slot` (the same worlds, permuted rows), `capacity` (the same worlds, a larger bucket), `shard`
(the same worlds on one device or split in two).

| Mutant | What is planted | Axis that must catch it | Expected first divergence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `batch-wide-reduction` | A kernel inserted after integration computes the floating mean of the base forward velocity over all worlds of the batch and adds 2⁻¹⁰ times it to each world's base forward velocity | `alone`, `cotenancy` | Control interval 1, the planted kernel, base velocity component 0 |
| `pad-plus-zero` | The initializer of one summed buffer writes +0.0 into padding instead of −0.0 | `capacity` (on the fixture world `neg-zero-sum`) | The reduction kernel; the summed channel; 0x80000000 against 0x00000000 |
| `normalizer-in-segment` | The observation normalizer is updated from the batch's running statistics at every control interval instead of at segment boundaries | `alone`, `cotenancy` | The first policy action after interval 1 |
| `capacity-shaped-tree` | One per-world sum is reduced by a midpoint-split recursion over the buffer's capacity (split at ⌈len/2⌉), which coincides with the anchored tree only when the capacity is a power of two | `capacity` (on the fixture world `mixed-magnitude-sum`, at capacities 12 and 18) | The reduction kernel; the summed channel of the fixture world |
| `neighbour-row-read` | One contact-row kernel reads the friction coefficient of lane (w + 1) mod batch for world w | `cotenancy`, `slot` | The contact-row kernel; the first contact |
| `slot-keyed-draws` | Reset poses are drawn from a stream keyed by slot index instead of world address | `slot` | The first reset |
| `arrival-placement` | The compaction that writes active contacts takes output positions from an integer atomic counter in completion order across worlds instead of from count, exclusive scan, write | `slot`, `shard` (racy on devices; see admission below) | The compaction kernel |
| `shard-local-normalizer` | In evaluation, each shard standardizes observations with its own shard's mean | `shard` | The first policy action |
| `iterations-by-slowest` | The host sets the solver iteration count of the next interval to the count the slowest-converging world of the batch needed | `cotenancy` | The solve stage; the first interval after a hard contact in a neighbour |
| `ties-by-capacity` | Contacts with equal sort keys are ordered by their position counted from the end of the buffer | `capacity` (on the fixture world `equal-keys`) | The sort kernel |

**Fixture worlds (added to the invariance test's world set so that data-dependent mutants are always
exercised).**
- `neg-zero-sum`: a world one of whose digested, summed channels has exactly one active addend equal to −0.0
  (a body at rest whose accumulated lateral impulse channel is −0.0 by construction).
- `equal-keys`: a flat box resting on a plane with four contacts of identical depth.
- `two-observers`: two worlds whose single observation is 1 and 3 with a one-unit linear policy, for the
  normalizer mutants.
- `mixed-magnitude-sum`: a world one of whose digested, summed channels has the seven active addends
  (0, 0, 0, 10⁸, 1, −10⁸, 1) in lanes 0 to 6, run in buckets of capacity 12 and 18.

**Worked values the tests assert.**
- `batch-wide-reduction` with two worlds at forward velocities 1 and 3 m/s: the batch mean is 2, the plant
  adds 2⁻⁹ m/s to each; world 1 alone has mean 1 and receives 2⁻¹⁰. The two digests of world 1 differ at
  interval 1, and the difference of the velocity component is exactly 2⁻¹⁰ m/s (bit patterns 0x3F804000 against
  0x3F802000 for velocity 1 + 2⁻⁹ against 1 + 2⁻¹⁰).
- `pad-plus-zero` on `neg-zero-sum`: at the world's own capacity the buffer has no padding lane beside the
  addend when the capacity is 1, and one +0.0 lane at capacity 2: sums 0x80000000 and 0x00000000.
- `normalizer-in-segment` on `two-observers`: normalized observations −1 and +1 in the pair; 0 for world 1
  alone.
- `capacity-shaped-tree` on `mixed-magnitude-sum`, in `f32` (the spacing near 10⁸ is 8, so 10⁸ + 1 = 10⁸).
  The anchored tree groups ((0 + 0) + (0 + 10⁸)) + ((1 − 10⁸) + (1 + pad)) = 10⁸ + (−10⁸) = 0 at every
  capacity. The midpoint-split recursion at capacity 12 groups [((0 + 0) + 0) + ((10⁸ + 1) − 10⁸)] + 1 = 1,
  and at capacity 18 groups [((0 + 0) + 0) + (10⁸ + 1)] + (−10⁸ + 1) = 10⁸ − 10⁸ = 0. So the mutant's sum is 1
  at capacity 12 and 0 at capacity 18, and the digests differ.

**Specificity matrix.** Each mutant is run through every axis. It must fail every axis listed for it, pass
every other axis, and pass `repeat` (the same arrangement twice), since all but `arrival-placement` are
deterministic. A mutant that fails an axis it is not keyed on is itself defective and fails its own test. The
matrix is stored with the registry rows, so a later change to the sweep that stops an axis from catching its
mutant is visible.

**Admission of the racy mutant, per device family.** `arrival-placement` depends on scheduling. Run 20 trial
pairs; if at least 19 differ, register it for the family with the observed rate; otherwise record it
`not-discriminating` on that family and do not count it for or against any axis there. Re-run admission after
any fingerprint change. On `batch-cpu` its Rust twin orders by a per-process nonce and is deterministic.

**Registry rows.** `registries/negative_controls.toml`: `id`, `plant`, `axes`, `fixture` (optional),
`expected_kernel`, `expected_quantity`, `tier = "G5"`, `backends`, `owner_test`, `known_answer` (KA-785, -786
or -787 where one applies), `counterexample` (CX-367, CX-368 where one applies).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ten mutants exist as dialect variants with Rust twins (or host seams), each loadable by `--mutant` in a conformance build only; a release build rejects the option.
- [ ] Each mutant fails every axis it is keyed on, on `batch-cpu` and on each device family where it is admitted, and the first-divergence record names the planted kernel and quantity listed above.
- [ ] Each deterministic mutant passes every axis it is not keyed on and passes `repeat` (the specificity matrix has no unexpected failure).
- [ ] The three controls of the known-answer table are caught: `batch-wide-reduction`, `pad-plus-zero`, `normalizer-in-segment`; so are `capacity-shaped-tree` and `neighbour-row-read`.
- [ ] The worked values hold bit for bit (the four cases above).
- [ ] Every mutant run is marked `conformance_mutant` in its envelope and receipt and is refused as an input by any citing verb.
- [ ] The four fixture worlds are part of the invariance test's frozen world set, with content hashes.
- [ ] Ten rows are present in the negative-control registry with their specificity matrices.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `mutant_loader_conformance_only`: the option parses in a conformance build and is a schema refusal otherwise.
- `mutant_kernel_ids_distinct`: each variant's kernel identity and cache key differ from the production kernel's.
- `worked_batch_mean`, `worked_pad_plus_zero`, `worked_normalizer`, `worked_capacity_tree` (the four worked cases, asserting bit patterns).
- `specificity_matrix_short` (ten mutants × five axes + `repeat`, 50 control intervals, 8 worlds, `batch-cpu`): the observed pass and fail pattern equals the table.
- `racy_mutant_admission`: with a synthetic trial log (labelled `synthetic`) of 20 pairs with 20, 19 and 12 differing: registered, registered, `not-discriminating`.
- `mutant_runs_not_citable`: an envelope from a mutant run offered to the citation check is refused.
- `fixture_worlds_hash_frozen`.

### End-to-end script and logging
Exercised by `scripts/e2e/num_batch_invariance.sh` (bead N35-17), step `controls`: for each mutant and each
axis it is keyed on, `frob --json sim run --mutant <name>` in the two arrangements, then
`frob --json telemetry divergence`. It logs through the STD-E2E helper, per mutant and axis, an `assert` line
(`expected: differ`, `observed`), a `metric` line with the first divergent control interval, stage, kernel and
quantity against the expected ones, and for the racy mutant the admission counts; on an unexpected pass, the
paths of both runs' digest chains.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-785, KA-786, KA-787 (the three rows of the known-answer table whose controls are built here).
- CX-367 (+0.0 padding), CX-368 (batch normalizer); the grouping case with 10⁸.
- Metamorphic relation MR-01 (batch-composition invariance) with its two registered mutations, a reduction
  tree shaped by capacity and a batch-wide normalizer; MR-03 (a draw depends on address, step and stream only)
  with `slot-keyed-draws`.
- The controls of this bead are themselves tested for specificity; the control of the controls is the matrix.
- Determinism lanes: each deterministic mutant twice in one arrangement gives identical digests.

## Considerations

- The gate's phrase "both negative controls" does not say which two of the table's rows it means. The reading
  used: every control in this bead that the table or the metamorphic register names for this property must be
  caught, which includes any pair. The ambiguity is listed for the lead in the coverage file.
- BET-02's other control, a planted fused multiply-add contraction in one backend, does not break batch
  independence (a kernel that contracts consistently is still independent of its batch). It is caught by the
  cross-backend comparison and by the compile-flag audit (N35-18), not here.
- Mutants need lint waivers (a floating reduction across worlds, a neighbour read, an atomic used as an
  index). The waiver marker is honoured only inside the conformance crate.
- The data-dependent mutants are silent on worlds that never trigger them, which is why the three fixture
  worlds are part of the frozen world set and not optional extras.
