## Background

Comparing two designs on the same world addresses is meant to expose both to the same world: the same gust,
the same slick tile, the same sensor noise. Addressed random streams make the *draws* reproducible, but a
second condition is needed for them to be the same *physical field* in both arms: a stream must be keyed by
the physical entity it perturbs, wherever that entity exists independently of the design: a gust by time,
floor friction by tile, a sensor's noise by the hash of its port name. Pairing by seed is otherwise not
pairing by physical field.

The smallest case: a sequential stream r₀, r₁, r₂. Design A draws a push (r₀) and a friction (r₁). Design B has
one more leg and draws a per-leg value first, so its push is r₁ and its friction r₂. The seeds are equal and
the two designs were pushed differently. The same failure appears with sensors: if noise is handed out in call
order, adding, removing or reordering a sensor that no controller consumes shifts the draws of every other
sensor, so the first added sensor changes what the others read.

The task chapter's counterexamples show the rule is also about *which* key: a push keyed by control tick 1,500
occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms step and at 1.5 s with a 1 ms step; a push keyed by "the tenth footfall" occurs at
5.0 s under a gait period of 0.5 s and at 4.0 s under 0.4 s, so a disturbance meant to be timed follows gait
phase and the two arms were pushed in different states. And the comparison chapter's pairing rule 2: exogenous
fields are indexed by physical coordinate, not by event order; a comparison that pairs "the k-th friction
draw" has paired nothing.

This bead fixes the keying helpers that every consumer uses to build a draw address, removes call order from
the draw interface, and carries the known answer and its negative control. It serves hypothesis H-26 (common
randomness compiled from the task: separate address domains for world fields, absolute time and
task-triggered events keep paired draws paired across retiming and topology changes; a paired contrast's
variance scales with 1 − ρ). Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the keying helpers per kind of entity; the absence of any ordinal in the draw interface; the two-design
and added-sensor fixtures; the sequential-stream negative control; guidance text for `CONTRACT.md`. Out: the
draw service and layout (N35-22); the registry (N35-23); the task compiler's exposure table, the coupling
test and its gate (the task unit); randomization from cards (BD-200); proposals (N35-25).

## Technical approach

**Keying rules (the entity and tick of a draw address, by what is perturbed).**

| What is perturbed | Exists independently of the design? | Stream (domain) | Entity sub-key | Tick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A disturbance in physical time (a gust, a timed push, a command switch) | Yes | `disturbance-process`, `command-schedule` (process) | Hash of the disturbance or channel name | The physical-time node (integer ticks of the rational base unit), never a step count or a control tick |
| A space-indexed field (floor friction, roughness) | Yes | `scene-field` (field) | The anchored cell or tile index, or the fixture identifier; the component carries the field (below) | 0: sampled once per world at stable coordinates |
| A property of an object in the scene (the mass of a cart) | Yes | `scene-field` (field) | The fixture identifier; the component carries the field (below) | 0 |
| A disturbance defined by a mission event (a push when the base first crosses a gate) | Yes, as a role | `disturbance-event` (event) | Hash of the mission role | The occurrence index of that role's event, never the order of contacts, footfalls or draws |
| A randomized parameter of the design or of a card (a per-leg stiffness) | No: it belongs to the design | `task-parameter` (field) | Hash of the parameter's stable path in the design term | 0 |
| A sensor's or an actuator's noise; a link's packet timing | Only with that port or link | `sensor-noise`, `actuator-noise`, `link-timing` (private) | Hash of the port name (the link name for `link-timing`) | The sample index of that port's own clock: a sensor's sample, an actuator's control tick, a link's packet index (N35-23's rows); the component is the channel |
| An initial or reset pose of a joint or body | No: it belongs to the design | `reset-pose` (field) | Hash of the joint's or body's stable path, never its index (an index shifts when a limb is added or legs are reordered); the reserved path `world` for a draw that chooses a whole-world pose (BD-195's cached-pose index), which two designs at one address share | The episode-local reset count |
| An engine draw: sampling, permutation, search, audit (not an exposure of the simulated world) | Not an exposure | an `engine` row of N35-23 | The row's integer identifier (campaign, statistic, epoch, …) or 0 | The row's index (decision, update, resample, sample, proposal) |

**Helpers (DESIGN; `frob_core::streams::key`).** `by_time(stream, name, tick)`, `by_cell(stream, cell, field)`,
`by_fixture(stream, fixture_id, field)`, `by_role(stream, role, occurrence)`, `by_param(stream, path)`,
`by_port(stream, port_name, sample, channel)`, `by_reset(stream, path, reset)` and `by_engine(stream, id,
index)`. Each takes the world address and returns a `DrawAddr`. They are the only public constructors of
`DrawAddr` outside `frob-core`'s tests; the struct's fields are not publicly writable, so a consumer cannot
assemble an address from a loop counter. `by_engine` is the one helper that takes an integer index, and it
accepts only a stream whose registry row has `domain = "engine"`: given an exposure stream it returns the typed
error `StreamDomainMismatch` (a programming error, asserted in debug builds), so a world exposure can never be
keyed by an index through it.

**Components (DESIGN, the rule BD-200 applies).** `by_port` sets the component to the channel. Every other helper
returns the address of the draw's first block, and `DrawAddr::part(j)` gives its j-th block (a further value the
consumer needs at the same entity and tick, or attempt j of a law that redraws, as BD-200's truncated normal does
in eight attempts); it is the only way to change the component, and the rejection path of N35-22's `below` uses
it. For `by_time`, `by_role`, `by_param`, `by_reset` and `by_engine` the component is j. Two fields of one tile or one fixture share the
entity, so `by_cell` and `by_fixture` take the field's stable path and put it in the component: component =
(low 29 bits of `entity_of_name("scene-field", <field path>)`)·8 + j, with j = 0…7 (j ≥ 8 would reach the next
field's blocks: a debug assertion and the property test below catch it). Friction and restitution of one tile
therefore read different blocks. Two field paths of one task whose low 29 bits coincide are refused at admission
like any entity collision of N35-23 (that bead's refusal, naming both paths).

**No ordinal anywhere.** The draw service has no `next()`, no cursor and no per-caller counter. A consumer that
needs several values of one entity at one tick uses the `component` index, which is part of the address. A
workspace lint rejects a `DrawAddr` whose entity or tick expression contains an iterator index named in a
`for … in 0..` loop over a design's components unless the index is passed through `by_param` or `by_port` with
the component's stable name (the lint is syntactic and conservative; a waiver names the reason).

**Consequences that become tests.**
1. *Extra leg.* Design A (four legs) and design B (five legs), same world address, a timed push at 3 s and
   tile friction: the push impulse and the friction of each tile are bit-identical in both runs. B's fifth
   leg's randomized stiffness is a draw keyed by its own parameter path and has no counterpart in A.
2. *Added sensor.* Adding an unused sensor to a design leaves every other sensor's noise sequence
   bit-identical.
3. *Reordered components.* Declaring a design's sensors or legs in a different order (the same names) leaves
   every draw unchanged.
4. *Rate change.* Halving the physics step leaves every `field` value unchanged and the timed push at the
   same physical time.

**Negative control (conformance only).** `SequentialStream`: a generator that returns successive words r₀, r₁,
r₂, … of one stream in call order. Substituted for the keyed helpers in the extra-leg fixture, it gives A's
push as r₀ and B's as r₁. It is built under N35-CX's plant identifier for CX-371, `sequential-stream` (kind
`mutation`; the Cargo feature `sequential-stream` of `frob-core`, conformance builds only), so that the control
N38b-05's seeding tool derives from CX-371 finds its plant; N35-30's row 6 runs the same build. This bead
writes no row of `controls.toml`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every public constructor of a draw address is one of the eight helpers; there is no draw API with a cursor or a call counter (compile-time test over the public surface); `by_engine` on an exposure stream returns `StreamDomainMismatch`.
- [ ] Reset poses are keyed by joint or body path: reordering or adding legs leaves every other joint's reset draw unchanged.
- [ ] Extra-leg fixture: the push words and the tile friction words read by designs A and B are identical; with `SequentialStream` they differ (A reads r₀ for the push, B reads r₁).
- [ ] Added-sensor fixture: the noise words of the three original sensors are identical with and without a fourth sensor; with noise keyed by sensor index (the conformance variant) inserting the new sensor first shifts all three.
- [ ] Reordering the declaration of legs and sensors changes no draw word.
- [ ] Two randomized fields of one tile (friction and restitution) read different blocks through the field part
      of the component, and two field paths whose low 29 bits collide are refused at admission with both paths.
- [ ] Halving the step leaves `scene-field` words unchanged and the timed push at tick-equivalent 3 s in both runs; the conformance variant keyed by step index moves the push from 3.0 s to 1.5 s.
- [ ] Two archetype designs of BD-109 that differ by one limb, keyed through the helpers on one world address with a push at 3 s and tile friction, read bit-identical push and friction words, and the extra limb's draws have no counterpart. (The run-level form, the same values recorded by runs of a four-leg and a five-leg design, is step 3 of BD-200's script, which depends on this bead; a referee run at this bead's milestone has no task randomization or timed disturbance.)
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-core` carries the keying table.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `extra_leg_keyed_draws_agree`: A requests [push at 3 s, friction of tile (4, 2)]; B requests [stiffness of `leg5`, push at 3 s, friction of tile (4, 2)]; the two common requests return identical blocks.
- `extra_leg_shifts_sequential_draws`: the same requests through `SequentialStream`: A's push is r₀ and friction r₁; B's push is r₁ and friction r₂.
- `added_sensor_keeps_others`: ports `imu.gyro`, `imu.accel`, `enc.hip` with and without `aux.temp`.
- `sensor_index_keying_shifts`: the conformance variant.
- `declaration_order_irrelevant`: all permutations of three ports, two parameter paths and four leg joints (their reset poses through `by_reset`) give the same address set.
- `engine_helper_refuses_exposure_stream`: `by_engine(bootstrap, 3, 17)` returns an address; `by_engine(scene-field, 3, 0)` and `by_engine(sensor-noise, 0, 5)` return `StreamDomainMismatch`.
- `time_key_is_physical`: a push "at 3 s" resolves to the same tick for steps of 2 ms and 1 ms; keyed by step index 1,500 it resolves to 3.0 s and 1.5 s.
- `footfall_keying_follows_gait`: a push keyed by "the tenth footfall" lands at 5.0 s for a gait period of 0.5 s and 4.0 s for 0.4 s (the event-order keying that `by_role` refuses unless the task declares the event as a mission role).
- `no_public_draw_cursor` (compile-time).
- `two_fields_of_one_tile_differ`: `by_cell(scene-field, (4, 2), "floor.friction")` and
  `by_cell(scene-field, (4, 2), "floor.restitution")` give equal entities and different components
  (low 29 bits of each path's hash, times 8), hence different blocks; the conformance variant without the field
  part returns the same block for both and fails.
- `field_parts_stay_in_their_slot`: `part(0…7)` on a `by_cell` address gives eight distinct components inside
  that field's slot of 8 and never another field's; `part(8)` trips the assertion; a planted pair of field paths
  with equal low 29 bits (found by search and pinned) is refused at task admission.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_entity_keying.sh [--seed N]`: (1) `frob --json design templates` to instantiate two
archetype designs that differ by one limb (BD-109) and read their stable joint, parameter and port paths;
(2) `cargo run -p frob-core --release --example keying_fixture -- --design <A> --design <B> --seed <N>`: the
helpers on one world address for both designs (a push at 3 s, the friction of tile (4, 2), each sensor's
noise, each joint's reset pose): assert the common draws' words are bit-identical and the extra limb's draws
have no counterpart; (3) the same with an added unused sensor on one design: assert the other sensors' noise
words are unchanged; (4) the same example built with the plant, `cargo run -p frob-core --features sequential-stream --example
keying_fixture -- …` (the feature implies `conformance` and, like every plant, never compiles under `--release`,
N38b-04's guard): assert the pushes differ (A r₀, B r₁);
(5) the step halved: field words unchanged and the push at the same physical-time tick. The script calls no
run verb: at this bead's milestone a referee run takes no task randomization and no timed disturbance (they
come with N16b-25 and BD-200, after this bead). The later lane is step 3 of BD-200's
`scripts/e2e/randomization_from_cards.sh` (a four-leg and a five-leg design per address: equal tile
frictions and common knee constants). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/num_entity_keying/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code and
wall time; the evidence block (determinism D0 for the words, identities, seed); `metric` lines with the push and
tile friction words of both designs; `assert` lines; on failure the two draw addresses and both words; a
`summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-793: with sequential draws and one extra leg, addressed streams give both designs the same push.
  Negative control: a sequential stream gives r₀ against r₁.
- CX-371 (equal seeds do not give two designs the same disturbances); the sensor chapter's entry that a noise
  generator must not hand out draws in call order; the task chapter's tick-keyed and footfall-keyed cases.
- Metamorphic: insertion of unused draws, sensors or limbs changes no other exposure; halving the step changes
  no field value.
- Determinism lanes: the fixtures twice and across worker counts on the referee and, at M2, on `batch-cpu`
  and devices through the draw-table test.

## Considerations

- A parameter that belongs to the design (a per-leg stiffness) is intentionally *not* shared between designs
  with different legs; what must be shared is everything in the world. Whether two designs' corresponding
  legs should read the same draw is decided by their stable paths: equal paths, equal draws.
- The choice among several legal keyings (friction by tile or by fixture) changes the joint law of two arms,
  never either arm's own distribution; choosing among them by measured contrast variance is the task unit's
  Frontier mechanism and its kill gate, not this bead's.
- Port names and parameter paths must be stable under edits for this rule to survive a design edit; the design
  language's typed edits preserve them, and an edit that renames a port changes its noise stream by intent.
- The lint is a guard against the common mistake, not a proof. The draw-table test and the fixtures here are
  what a regression would fail.
