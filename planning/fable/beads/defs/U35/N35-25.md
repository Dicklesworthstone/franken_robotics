## Background

Rare failures are estimated by sampling worlds from a *proposal* distribution that visits dangerous conditions
more often than the nominal distribution does, and correcting each sample by its importance weight
w = p(x)/q(x), the ratio of the nominal density to the proposal density at the sampled value. If the weight is
wrong, the estimate is wrong in a way no confidence interval reveals.

In this system every random draw is addressed: it is a pure function of the world address, the stream, the
entity and the tick. The numerics chapter draws the consequence: **proposal draws carry exact weights.**
Because every draw is addressed, a disturbance drawn from a tilted proposal has an importance weight that is
computed, not estimated. The value a world received and the weight that corrects for it are two functions of
the same integer words, so the weight can be recomputed from the address alone at any time, on any machine,
and audited.

The consumer is the certification lane for rare failures: bounded-weight importance sampling with a
*defensive mixture* q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p_shifted, whose likelihood ratio is at most 1/ε, so that ε·w·1[failure]
is an observation in [0, 1] with mean ε·p and a confidence sequence on that mean bounds p at every stopping
time (construction C-27: rare failures; bounded weights certify, splitting explores). The same mixture is used
for discrete scene edits, where the weight bound holds for the same reason. This bead is the draw-side half:
proposals as addressed transforms with their weights. Tier: Solid.

Alternative rejected: estimating weights from samples (self-normalized importance sampling). It is biased at
finite sample size and has no bound per observation, so it cannot feed a certifying sequence.

## Scope

In (the draw side, in `frob-core` and `frob-fsim`): the proposal types; the one branch draw of an episode; the
germ factor (normal germs with a mean shift, pushed through a declared monotone transform that this bead takes
as given); the finite factor (rational category probabilities, realized thresholds, exact rational weight);
weight computation and its class for a proposal of one factor; the type separation between bounded and
unbounded weights; recomputation from the address; the per-draw record; law tests; the row of the stream
`proposal-branch` in `registries/streams.toml`, appended under the registry's rules (N35-23); the example
target `proposal_weights` of `frob-fsim` (draws, records and audit of the end-to-end script).

Out, with the bead that has it:
- The transforms themselves and the mixture of several factors (N26a-24, in `frob-nemesis`, which depends on
  this bead): the germ map and its inverse for each distribution family of a card field (normal, lognormal,
  uniform, truncated normal, triangular) and the table that declares them; the joint defensive mixture of an
  episode over all its germs and discrete features with its log-space numerics and the scaled observation;
  the sampler that realizes a whole world from the factor draws of this bead; a nominal law truncated by a
  legality set; unbiasedness on each family. The two beads were drafted with the same weight formula and
  different sampling rules; the rule is now one (below, "One branch per episode").
- The estimator, the confidence sequence, admission of non-nominal episodes and their refusals (the
  adversarial-search unit); choosing a proposal (fitted on a separate pilot, never on the data it corrects);
  splitting, which re-keys streams by a child address and needs no weight.

## Technical approach

**Principle.** A proposal draw at address a is `(x, w) = sample(a, nominal, proposal)`, where x is a
deterministic function of the block words at a (and of one more block for the mixture branch), and w is the
closed-form density ratio p(x)/q(x) evaluated at that x. Nothing about w depends on other samples.

**Germ form (continuous parameters).** A nominal parameter is a monotone transform θ = T(z) of a standard
normal germ z (T is declared with the card field or scene parameter; the identity for a Gaussian). The
proposal is a defensive mixture in germ space with nominal share ε ∈ (0, 1] and shift μ:

1. Draw the germ z₀ = `normal_f64(a with component c)`, and read the episode's branch (below).
2. On the nominal branch z = z₀; on the shifted branch z = z₀ + μ.
3. Weight: w(z) = φ(z) / (ε·φ(z) + (1 − ε)·φ(z − μ)) = 1 / (ε + (1 − ε)·exp(μ·z − μ²/2)), with φ the standard
   normal density. It depends on z only, not on which branch produced z, and satisfies w ≤ 1/ε.
4. θ = T(z). Since T is a bijection, the weight in θ-space equals the weight in germ space.

For d independent germs with shift vector μ applied jointly (one branch draw for the vector):
w = 1 / (ε + (1 − ε)·exp(μ·z − ‖μ‖²/2)). The exponential and the normal construction use the deterministic
elementary functions of the sibling library on the host, so w is class D2 and identical across CPU
architectures to the extent those functions are.

**One branch per episode (DESIGN, agreed with N26a-24).** An episode has one mixture indicator, whatever the
number of proposal factors in it: one addressed 64-bit word B on the stream `proposal-branch` of the world
address, compared as an integer, nominal branch when B < ⌊ε·2⁶⁴⌋ and shifted branch otherwise. Every germ
factor and every finite factor of the episode reads that branch. Reason: with q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p′ taken over
the whole episode, q ≥ ε·p holds pointwise and the weight is at most 1/ε; with one indicator per factor the
bound would be (1/ε)ᵈ for d factors, which certifies nothing. (The first draft of this bead drew a branch
uniform beside each germ vector, at component c + 1, and sampled a finite factor from its own mixture with a
single draw; an episode with a tilted friction and a tilted scene edit would then have carried two
indicators. The realized nominal share is ε̃ = ⌊ε·2⁶⁴⌋/2⁶⁴.) The stream is registered by this bead as the
next free identifier of `registries/streams.toml`: name `proposal-branch`, domain `engine`, entity `0`, tick
`0`, component `0` (B is the 64-bit word `(b₁ ≪ 32) | b₀` of the block at that address), owner `frob-core`.
Being an appended row it changes no existing draw, and the registry version it creates enters the pins of
every run that draws a proposal. The germ and finite-factor draws themselves stay on the nominal parameter's
own stream and address (for example `task-parameter` keyed by the parameter path), so a nominal-branch episode
reads exactly the draws of the unshifted world.

**Finite form (discrete scene edits and categories).** Nominal probabilities pᵢ and shifted probabilities rᵢ
are exact rationals, with rᵢ > 0 only where pᵢ > 0; the proposal is qᵢ = ε·pᵢ + (1 − ε)·rᵢ. Sampling: the
episode's branch selects the row, p on the nominal branch and r on the shifted branch; one 64-bit addressed
draw U is compared with the integer thresholds of that row, Tᵢ = ⌊(s₁ + … + sᵢ)·2⁶⁴⌋ with s the selected row;
category i is chosen when Tᵢ₋₁ ≤ U < Tᵢ. The probabilities the sampler realizes are p̃ᵢ and r̃ᵢ (differences
of thresholds over 2⁶⁴) and, over both branches, q̃ᵢ = ε̃·p̃ᵢ + (1 − ε̃)·r̃ᵢ. For a proposal whose only factor
is this one the weight is wᵢ = pᵢ / q̃ᵢ, an exact rational (class D0). Using the realized q̃ᵢ, not the intended
qᵢ, makes the weight exact for the sampler that actually ran; the difference is below 2⁻⁶³ per category and
the bound w ≤ 1/ε is checked on q̃ (a proposal whose quantization would break the bound for some category is
refused at construction).

**Several factors in one episode.** This bead returns, per factor, the value and the factor's ratio of shifted
to nominal law at that value: exp(μ·z − ‖μ‖²/2) for a germ vector, r̃ᵢ/p̃ᵢ (an exact rational) for a finite
factor. The weight of the episode, 1/(ε + (1 − ε)·R) with R the product of the factor ratios, its evaluation
in log space and the `BoundedWeight` it yields are N26a-24's joint mixture; the types and `reweigh` here serve
both the one-factor and the joint case.

**Types (DESIGN).**

```rust
pub struct Defensive { eps: Rational /* 0 < eps <= 1 */, shift: Shift }      // constructor refuses eps = 0
pub struct BoundedWeight { w: f64 /* or Rational */, bound: Rational }        // w <= 1/eps by construction
pub struct ExplorationWeight(f64);                                            // from a pure tilt; no bound
pub struct ProposalDraw<T, W> { addr: DrawAddr, value: T, weight: W, nominal: LawRef, proposal: ProposalId }
```

A pure tilt (ε = 0) can only produce `ExplorationWeight`; a certifying estimator's interface accepts
`BoundedWeight` only, so offering a tilted episode to a bound is a type error before it is a run-time refusal.

**Recomputation.** `reweigh(addr, nominal, proposal) -> (value, weight)` recomputes both from the address. An
audit calls it for every stored proposal draw and requires bit equality with the stored pair.

**Record.** Each proposal draw stores the address, the nominal law's reference (a card field or scene
parameter with its content hash), the proposal's identity (ε, μ or the rᵢ, and the pilot it was fitted on),
the value and the weight. A summary over draws reports the largest weight, the bound 1/ε and the effective
sample size (Σw)²/Σw².

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The weight formula is reproduced at hand values (ε = 0.1, μ = 4): w(0) = 9.9699, w(2) = 1, w(4) = 3.7272·10⁻⁴; every weight over 10⁶ draws is at most 10.
- [ ] Unbiasedness in the germ form: over 10⁶ addressed draws the mean weight is within ±0.013 of 1, and the weighted frequency of z > 4 is within 2% of 3.167·10⁻⁵.
- [ ] Finite form: for nominal (0.95, 0.05), shifted (0, 1) and ε = 0.1 the weights are 10 and 10/181 up to the quantization of the thresholds (below 2⁻⁶³), exact rationals, and Σ q̃ᵢ·wᵢ = 1 exactly.
- [ ] One branch per episode: an episode with a germ factor and a finite factor reads one branch word; both factors are on the nominal branch or both on the shifted branch in every one of 10⁶ addressed episodes.
- [ ] `reweigh` reproduces every stored (value, weight) pair bit for bit from the address alone, on both CPU architectures.
- [ ] A value and its weight do not change when the world runs in another slot, batch, bucket or shard (the draw-table arrangements applied to proposal draws).
- [ ] `Defensive` cannot be constructed with ε = 0; an `ExplorationWeight` cannot be passed where a `BoundedWeight` is required (compile-fail test).
- [ ] A finite proposal whose quantized q̃ would give a weight above 1/ε for some category is refused at construction, naming the category.
- [ ] `registries/streams.toml` has the `proposal-branch` row (domain `engine`) with the next free identifier; its loader and generated-constant checks pass, and the five pinned blocks of N35-22 are unchanged after it is appended.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `weight_hand_values`: the three values above; and for ε = 1 (no shift mass) w = 1 for every z.
- `weight_bound`: for 10⁶ random z in [−8, 12] and μ in [0, 6], w ≤ 1/ε.
- `weight_independent_of_branch`: the same z reached through the nominal branch and through the shifted branch (constructed by choosing z₀) has the same weight bits.
- `one_branch_word_per_episode`: a germ factor and a finite factor of one episode never disagree on the branch; the branch is a function of the world address alone.
- `proposal_branch_stream_registered`: the constant `PROPOSAL_BRANCH` exists and equals the row's identifier; appending it leaves every pinned block unchanged.
- `nominal_branch_reads_nominal_draws`: on the nominal branch the germ z equals the unshifted world's draw at the same address, bit for bit.
- `finite_rows_by_branch`: with nominal (0.95, 0.05) and shifted (0, 1), every shifted-branch episode draws category 2, and the realized q̃ equals ε̃·p̃ + (1 − ε̃)·r̃ as exact rationals.
- `mean_weight_is_one`; `gaussian_tail_recovered` (10⁶ draws each, addresses from one run seed).
- `finite_weights_exact`: the two-category case; a five-category case with rational probabilities whose weights sum check Σ q̃ᵢ·wᵢ = Σ pᵢ = 1 in exact arithmetic.
- `finite_quantization_bound`: a constructed proposal with a category of probability 2⁻⁷⁰ is refused.
- `monotone_transform_preserves_weight`: with T(z) = exp(0.5·z) (a log-normal card field) the weight of θ equals the weight of z.
- `reweigh_roundtrip`; `types_separate_bounded_from_exploration` (compile-fail).
- `vector_shift_weight`: d = 2, μ = (3, 4), z = (3, 4): μ·z − ‖μ‖²/2 = 25 − 12.5 = 12.5, w = 1/(ε + (1 − ε)·e^12.5).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_proposal_weights.sh [--seed N]`: (1) `cargo test -p frob-fsim proposal_` and
`cargo test -p frob-core proposal_`; (2) `cargo run -p frob-fsim --example proposal_weights -- --seed N
--eps 1/10 --shift 4 --draws 1000000 --out <dir>` on the Gaussian-tail fixture: 10⁶ addressed proposal draws,
their per-draw records (address, nominal reference, proposal identity, value, weight) written to
`<dir>/draws.ndjson`, and the metrics below; run twice with the same seed, the two files' digests must be
equal; (3) the same example with `--audit <dir>/draws.ndjson`, which calls `reweigh` on every stored draw and
compares bit for bit; (4) `--finite 0.95,0.05:0,1 --joint --draws 1000000`, episodes with one germ factor and
the finite factor, printing the two weights as rationals, Σ q̃ᵢ·wᵢ and the count of episodes whose factors
disagree on the branch. The verb `nemesis bound` is not called here: it is built on this bead (N26a-24 and
BD-276 depend on it); the same fixture through the verb (every weight at most 10, the tail inside the
interval) is step 6 of N26a-30's script (KA-665), and the audit of the pairs a run stored is N26a-24's
`joint_weight_reweigh_roundtrip`. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/num_proposal_weights/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code
and wall time; the evidence block (class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); `metric` lines `max_weight`
against the bound 10, `mean_weight` against 1 ± 0.013 (about 4.6 standard errors at 10⁶ draws), the tail
estimate against 3.167·10⁻⁵ ± 2%, the effective sample size, `reweigh_mismatches` against 0,
`branch_disagreements` against 0, the finite weights against 10 and 10/181 and the rerun digests; on failure
the first draw address whose value or weight differs, with both bit patterns; a `summary` line.

### Known answers and negative controls
- The adversarial chapter's known answer: the Gaussian tail by defensive mixture with shift 4 and ε = 0.1
  recovers P(z > 4) = 3.167·10⁻⁵ with every weight at most 10; its control is a mixture without its nominal
  share on a mis-centred proposal.
- The bounded-weight identity: n⁻¹·Σ wᵢ·1[fail] with w = dp/dq ≤ w_max is unbiased with terms in [0, w_max];
  with nominal share ε the weights are at most 1/ε, and zero failures certify only p ≤ ln(1/δ)/(ε·n).
- Negative controls: a weight computed with the intended qᵢ where the realized q̃ᵢ differs by a deliberately
  coarse 16-bit quantization (Σ q̃ᵢ·wᵢ ≠ 1; caught by `finite_weights_exact`); a weight estimated by
  self-normalization (not equal to `reweigh`; caught by the audit); a branch-dependent weight
  (`weight_independent_of_branch` fails).
- Determinism lanes: all tests twice, across worker counts, debug and release, and on both CPU architectures.

## Considerations

- On devices the proposal value is computed in single precision by the device kernel, while the certifying
  weight is recomputed on the host in double precision from the same address. The value the world actually
  received and the host's value differ by rounding in the germ; the weight is a smooth function of z, so the
  relative effect is of the order of 10⁻⁷, far below the estimator's interval, and the record stores both the
  device value's bits and the host weight.
- With weights bounded by 1/ε, the upper bound after n runs without a failure cannot fall below about
  ln(1/δ)/(ε·n): importance sampling is never the cheaper way to certify absence. This bead does not change
  that; it makes the weights trustworthy where the method is used.
- A proposal must be chosen before the run or fitted on a separate completed pilot whose data are never
  reused; that discipline is the estimator's, and the proposal's identity recorded here includes the pilot.
