## Background

Devices solve the per-world contact system H = A + JᵀGJ in single precision. Two things matter: the condition
number that governs the accuracy of that solve, and the meaning of the solver's tolerances. Decision
ADR-NUM-04: the contact solve runs in power-of-two-scaled variables and reports a conditioning figure, because
tolerances and regularization must mean the same for every row and every robot in a batch.

The facts the design rests on (textbook results, and two recorded computations on reference robots with
simplified contact rows; source register):

- Cholesky factorization without pivoting is invariant under two-sided scaling by powers of two: absent
  overflow and underflow, the computed factor of D·H·D is exactly D times the computed factor of H. So scaling
  does not change one bit of the solve; the attainable accuracy is governed by the condition number of the
  unit-diagonal-scaled matrix whether or not the scaling is applied, and scaling to unit diagonal is within a
  factor n of the best diagonal scaling of a symmetric positive definite matrix.
- For a 29-joint humanoid over 201 poses, κ₂(H) is 3·10⁴ to 1·10⁵ raw and 2·10³ to 9·10³ scaled at default
  contact impedance, and 2·10⁶ to 3·10⁶ raw and 2·10⁵ to 5·10⁵ scaled near-rigid. Over sampled contact states
  of three robots the scaled medians are 1.2·10³ to 2.7·10³. With unit round-off u = 6.0·10⁻⁸, κ·u in scaled
  variables is 1·10⁻⁴ to 5·10⁻⁴ at default impedance and 0.012 to 0.03 near-rigid.

The design therefore has five parts: exact scaling D_ii = 2^(−⌈e_i/2⌉) from the binary exponent e_i of H_ii
(scaled diagonal in [½, 2)); every tolerance stated on the scaled system; a conditioning figure in the
certificate, κ̂ = (max L̂_ii / min L̂_ii)² with κ̂·u, a lower bound on κ₂ of the scaled matrix and named a lower
bound; two precision profiles, `default` (no refinement) and `stiff` (one step of iterative refinement with a
double-float residual; one step multiplies the relative error by about κ·u, taking an error of order 0.03 to
order 10⁻³; the step count is a constant of the shape class); and a limit: a world whose κ̂·u exceeds 0.1 is
marked `Degraded: ill-conditioned`, and under the `refuse` policy the run refuses (`FROB-REF-248`).

Two of those numbers are open. **OPEN-NUM-4**: the limit for `Degraded: ill-conditioned` and the refinement
count of the `stiff` profile. Default: κ̂·u above 0.1; one step. Owner: Spark lead. **Decided by
GATE-SPARK-18** (Known answer; TARGET; M2): the scaling invariance holds on each backend; on 10⁴ contact
states per reference robot the relative error of the `f32` Newton step against `f64` is at most 10·κ₂·u, and
at most 10⁻³ on near-rigid states under the `stiff` profile; κ̂ never exceeds the κ₂ computed in `f64`. The
gate is measured by the precision-battery bead (BD-179). This bead reads its measurements and makes the
decision. It serves BET-08 (single precision is adequate on the GPU).

## Scope

In: the evidence to read and the two extra measurements to request; the decision rules, declared before the
data are read; the record (registry row and ADR); the default in force until then; consequences of a change.
Out: the scaling, factorization and refinement kernels (BD-156); the conditioning figure and its harness and
the gate record (the Spark unit; BD-179); admission of near-rigid interface cards on single-precision tiers
(the contact unit's stiffness admission, which estimates κ·u at admission time and has its own refusal).

## Technical approach

**Default in force until this bead closes.** Limit 0.1 on κ̂·u; one refinement step under `stiff`. The
profile option and the refusal read these from `registries/decisions.toml` if the row exists and from the
defaults otherwise; every `numerics` block records which was used.

**Why the default limit is not obviously right.** κ̂ is a *lower* bound. If on real contact states κ̂ is
typically several times below the true κ₂, a world can have κ̂·u = 0.05 (not flagged) and a true κ₂·u of 0.3
(a solve with tens of percent error). The limit must be set on the quantity we have (κ̂·u) so that what it
lets through is acceptable in the quantity we care about (the error of the Newton step).

**Evidence (from GATE-SPARK-18's harness; per reference robot, backend and precision profile).**
1. The joint sample of (κ̂·u, κ₂·u, ε) over 10⁴ contact states, where ε = ‖Δ₃₂ − Δ₆₄‖/‖Δ₆₄‖ is the relative
   error of the `f32` Newton step in the scaled norm.
2. The looseness ratio κ₂/κ̂: its median, 99th percentile and maximum.
3. The same sample on the near-rigid subset with 0, 1 and 2 refinement steps (the harness run three times
   with the count set through its hook `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS`, N21b-38; the gate itself uses the default
   count).
4. The share of world-steps on the standard battery that each candidate limit would flag.
5. The cost of a refinement step relative to the factorization: about 3n² operations against n³/3 (for the
   humanoid's n = 35: 3,675 against 14,292, about 26% per step; for the quadruped's n = 18: 972 against 1,944,
   50% per step).

**Decision rules (DESIGN; written before the evidence is read).**
- *Limit.* Candidates L ∈ {0.3, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01}. A candidate is admissible if, among states with κ̂·u ≤ L
  under the `default` profile: (a) the 99.9th percentile of ε is at most 10·L, and (b) no state has ε above 1.
  Choose the largest admissible candidate not above the default, that is: keep 0.1 if admissible, otherwise
  0.03, otherwise 0.01. A candidate above the default (0.3) is chosen only if 0.1 would flag more than 10⁻⁴ of
  world-steps on the standard battery at default impedance while 0.3 is admissible, and the reason is
  written.
- *Refinement count.* The smallest r ∈ {1, 2} for which every near-rigid state has ε ≤ 10⁻³ under `stiff`
  with r steps. If r = 1 suffices, keep it. If only r = 2 does, set 2 and record the added cost. If neither
  does, the count stays 1, the gate's near-rigid clause is recorded as failed for the `f32` path, and the
  fallback of BET-08 applies to near-rigid contact: such worlds are evaluated on `batch-cpu` in double
  precision and labelled, and near-rigid interface cards stay outside the single-precision tiers.
- *Consistency.* After the decision, a state flagged under the chosen limit must be either refused
  (`FROB-REF-248`) or carried with its flag; the chosen count is a constant of every shape class.

**Record.** A row in `registries/decisions.toml` (`id = "OPEN-NUM-4"`, `status = "decided"`,
`kappa_u_limit`, `stiff_refinement_steps`, `evidence` (receipt hashes of items 1 to 5 and of the
GATE-SPARK-18 rows), `decided_by`, `date`, `commit`) and `docs/adr/ADR-NUM-OPEN4.md` with the measurements,
the rule applied, the alternatives and the re-run list.

**Consequences of a value other than the default.** A different refinement count changes the identity of
every `stiff` step plan (the count is in the plan), so kernel caches, digests and memoized episodes under
`stiff` are invalidated and the `stiff` rows of the precision battery are re-measured. A different limit
changes no arithmetic; it changes which worlds are flagged from then on. Stored runs keep the flags they were
recorded with, and each `numerics` block names the limit record it used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision is made only after GATE-SPARK-18 has recorded rows for `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal (or for the backends that exist under the M2 no-GPU variant), and it cites their receipt hashes.
- [ ] The evidence bundle holds the five items; the joint sample has at least 10⁴ states per reference robot and backend; the near-rigid subset was run with 0, 1 and 2 refinement steps.
- [ ] The rules above are committed (in the ADR draft, with a content hash) before the evidence bundle's first read, and the decision function applied to the bundle reproduces the recorded outcome.
- [ ] `registries/decisions.toml` has the OPEN-NUM-4 row and `docs/adr/ADR-NUM-OPEN4.md` exists; the profile option, the refusal `FROB-REF-248` and the `numerics` block read the limit and the count from the row.
- [ ] If the refinement count changed, the `stiff` step plans have new identities and the affected battery rows are re-measured; if the limit changed, a test shows a world at κ̂·u between the old and new limits is flagged under one and not the other.
- [ ] If neither refinement count meets 10⁻³ on near-rigid states, the recorded outcome names the fallback applied (double precision on `batch-cpu`, labelled) and the gate's clause is recorded as failed for the `f32` path, not waived.
- [ ] The decision is recorded by a session that did not implement the conditioning harness.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
(The decision function is real code in `frob-conformance`; the measurement tables in these tests are
constructed and labelled `synthetic`.)
- `limit_rule_keeps_default`: a table in which states with κ̂·u ≤ 0.1 have a 99.9th-percentile ε of 0.4 and maximum 0.8 → 0.1 is admissible (0.4 ≤ 1.0) and kept.
- `limit_rule_tightens`: 99.9th-percentile ε of 1.6 among κ̂·u ≤ 0.1 (inadmissible), 0.2 among κ̂·u ≤ 0.03 (admissible, since 0.2 ≤ 0.3) → 0.03.
- `limit_rule_rejects_on_any_error_above_one`: one state with κ̂·u = 0.02 and ε = 1.3 makes 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3 inadmissible → 0.01.
- `limit_rule_loosens_only_with_reason`: 0.1 flags 3·10⁻⁴ of world-steps and 0.3 is admissible → 0.3 with the reason field required.
- `refinement_rule`: near-rigid maxima of ε = (0.03, 8·10⁻⁴, 3·10⁻⁵) for r = (0, 1, 2) → 1; maxima (0.03, 4·10⁻³, 2·10⁻⁴) → 2; maxima (0.5, 0.2, 0.05) → stays 1 with the fallback recorded.
- `refinement_cost_arithmetic`: n = 35 → 3,675 and 14,292 (ratio 0.257); n = 18 → 972 and 1,944 (ratio 0.5).
- `kappa_u_illustration`: κ = 2·10³ and 9·10³ → κ·u = 1.2·10⁻⁴ and 5.4·10⁻⁴; κ = 2·10⁵ and 5·10⁵ → 0.012 and 0.030 (the figures the design quotes).
- `decision_row_binds_consumers`: with the row present, the profile option reports the row's limit; without it, 0.1 and the label `default`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_open4_evidence.sh [--tier …]`: (1) read the GATE-SPARK-18 receipts through
`frob --json artifact read`; (2) run the conditioning harness of N21b-38 three times with its refinement-count
hook, `FROB_STIFF_REFINE_STEPS=<r> FROB_BACKEND=<b> FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance --release
--test g1_known_answers -- KA-799` for r = 0, 1 and 2 on each backend, and read the near-rigid subset's ε maxima
and the recorded count from each run's lines (the runs are labelled `exploratory`, never the gate's row; the
variable is also requested as a pass-through option of the precision battery's script, BD-179); (3) compute the
looseness ratio and the flagged
share per candidate limit from the battery's `numerics` blocks; (4) apply the decision function and print the
outcome without writing the registry (the recording session does that). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/num_open4_evidence/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code
and wall time; the evidence block (claim known-answer, class, tier, determinism, identities with fingerprint
digest); `metric` lines for, per robot and backend, the percentiles of ε among unflagged states for each
candidate, the looseness ratio's median, 99th percentile and maximum, the near-rigid maxima of ε for r = 0, 1,
2 against 10⁻³, and the flagged share against 10⁻⁴; `assert` lines for κ̂ ≤ κ₂ on every state; a `summary`
line naming GATE-SPARK-18 and the proposed values.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-796 (the factor of D·H·D against D times the factor of H is bit-identical; control: a scaling by 10);
  KA-797 (exact scaling; scaled diagonal in [½, 2)); KA-798 (H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) with residual (0, 10⁻⁷): the
  scaled test fails, the unscaled absolute test passes); KA-799 (κ̂ never exceeds κ₂; ε at most 10·κ₂·u; at
  most 10⁻³ near-rigid under `stiff`).
- CX-372 (scaling does not make the solve more accurate); CX-373 (one absolute tolerance does not mean the
  same for every row); the rejected half-precision factorization (κ·u at or above one in 37 to 64% of sampled
  contact states at half-precision round-off).
- Negative controls: a decision function that reads the evidence before the rule's hash is committed (the
  check on commit order fails); a planted evidence table in which κ̂ exceeds κ₂ for one state (the script's
  assertion fails and no decision is proposed).
- Determinism: the decision function is exact on its inputs; the harness runs are those of the gate.

## Considerations

- RISK row of the failure-mode review: "single precision failed in large scenes and long runs"; indicators:
  the precision gate, the share of worlds marked `Degraded: ill-conditioned`, the guarded-predicate fallback
  rate; residual risk named in advance: near-rigid cards stay outside `f32`. This decision is where that
  residual risk is either accepted with numbers or avoided.
- The recorded computations behind the default used simplified contact rows on reference robots; the gate's
  sample comes from the batched battery's real contact states, which is why the decision waits for it.
- The optional hard-contact law, a later-milestone item, also runs under `stiff`; if it needs a different
  count it reopens this decision with its own evidence.
- A limit tighter than the default raises the `Degraded` share; if that share exceeds 10⁻⁴ of world-steps at
  default impedance on a reference robot, the right repair is in the contact model's impedance or in the
  refinement, not a looser limit, and the ADR must say which was chosen.
