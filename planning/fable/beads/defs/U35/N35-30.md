## Background

The numerics chapter closes with a table of ten known answers, each paired with the negative control that must
be caught:

| Known answer | Expected | Negative control that must be caught |
|---|---|---|
| One world alone, in 100 random co-tenancies, at two capacities, on two shards | Identical digests | A kernel with a batch-wide floating reduction feeding state |
| Sum of the one active element −0.0 at two capacities | −0.0 both times | Padding with +0.0 |
| Two worlds observing 1 and 3 | Each world's actions equal those of its isolated run | A normalizer updated inside the segment |
| Success predicate at 1.0000001 m against 0.9999999 m | Reported as discordant and `boundary-sensitive` | A check that compares states only |
| Offset −2⁻²⁵ m in a [0, 8) cell | The centred convention shifts exactly | The uncentred shift returns 8.0 |
| Sequential draws with one extra leg | Addressed streams give both designs the same push | A sequential stream gives r₀ against r₁ |
| Factor of D·H·D against D times the factor of H | Bit-identical | A scaling by 10 instead of a power of two |
| H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) with residual (0, 10⁻⁷) | The scaled test fails | The unscaled absolute test passes |
| The set {0, 3, 5, 9} histogrammed in two orders | Identical histograms | A closest-pair sketch |
| A `fast-training` policy evaluated twice under `device-replay` | Identical digests | Citing its training return is refused |

Each row is measured at full scale by a gate or by its mechanism's own bead, on devices where that applies.
What is still needed is one small, fast suite that states all ten in their smallest form, runs on any machine
with no device, and runs the ten controls beside them. It is the first thing to run when a determinism
regression is suspected, and it is where four rows whose mechanisms belong to other units (anchored positions
and diagonal scaling) get a host-level statement owned by this chapter. The program's rule that a gate counts
only when its registered negative control has been shown to fail is applied here row by row. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: registration of the battery `numerics`; the host form of each of the ten rows, either written here or
bound to the owner bead's test; the ten controls; the proof-obligation host forms (re-anchoring exactness,
uniform conversion, scaling invariance); the script. Out: the gate-scale and device forms (BD-181, BD-177,
BD-269, BD-179); the mechanisms.

**Host forms written here.**

*Row 5 (anchored positions; the mechanism is the Spark unit's and the step kernel's).* Single precision
resolves about 7.6 µm at 100 m from the origin and worse beyond, which is too coarse for contact. A world
position is therefore an integer cell index (a signed 32-bit integer per axis; a scene declares its bounds)
plus an `f32` offset from the centre of a cell of side 8 m; the offset lies in [−4, 4), where resolution is at
worst 2⁻²² m. Scene fields are addressed cell-relative, and quantities that are differences (gaps,
link-relative poses, velocities) never touch absolute position: the difference of two anchored positions is
the float difference of the offsets plus 8 times the integer difference of the cells, formed only between
neighbours. When the magnitude of an offset reaches 4, the anchoring kernel subtracts or adds 8.0 and moves the
cell index by one; offsets are checked every step and a body cannot cross 12 m in one step, so the shifted
magnitude lies between 4 and 16, and a step that would break that refuses (`FROB-REF-249`). Decision
ADR-NUM-06: offsets are centred on their cell, because a shift by the cell side is then always exact; the
rejected alternative, offsets kept in [0, 8), has a worst spacing of 0.48 µm and an inexact shift, as the
first test below shows.
- `uncentred_shift_returns_eight`: in `f32`, (−2⁻²⁵) + 8.0 has pattern 0x41000000 (8.0), because 8 − 2⁻²⁵ is
  not representable (the spacing just below 8 is 2⁻²¹): under an uncentred [0, 8) convention the position has
  moved and left the cell's range. Under the centred convention −2⁻²⁵ is in range and nothing is added.
- `sterbenz_exhaustive_host` (PO-NUM-01): for every `f32` x with 4 ≤ |x| < 16 (the binades [4, 8) and [8, 16),
  two signs, 2²⁵ values) the `f32` result of x − 8·sign(x) equals the exact value, checked by computing the
  same difference in `f64`, where it is exact. Plus the endpoint |x| = 16.
- `f32_spacing_table`: the spacing 2^(e−23) in [2^e, 2^(e+1)): 2⁻¹⁷ m (7.63 µm) at 100 m; 2⁻¹⁴ m (61.0 µm) at
  1 km; 2⁻²² m (0.238 µm) in [2, 4); 2⁻²¹ m (0.477 µm) in [4, 8).

*Row 7 (diagonal scaling; the kernels are the contact unit's).* A reference Cholesky factorization without
pivoting, written in the test in `f32` with a fixed operation order (it is a reference, the same algorithm
the kernel implements, not a stand-in for it):
- `chol_pow2_scaling_is_bitwise_invariant` (PO-NUM-02, host form): for 10⁴ seeded symmetric positive definite
  matrices of order 2 to 35 and diagonals D of powers of two with exponents in [−20, 20], the computed factor
  of D·H·D equals D times the computed factor of H, bit for bit. Matrices whose entries would overflow or
  underflow under the scaling are excluded and counted.
- `chol_scale_by_ten_breaks_bits`: the same with D = 10·I on a matrix chosen so that at least one entry
  rounds differently; the factors differ in at least one bit.
- `k_scale_rule`: D_ii = 2^(−⌈e_i/2⌉) with e_i the binary exponent of H_ii read from the bit pattern: for 10⁶
  random positive `f32` diagonals the scaled diagonal D_ii²·H_ii lies in [½, 2), and D_ii²·H_ii·D_ii⁻² returns
  the original bits. Worked value: H_ii = 10⁻⁸ has e = −27, D = 2¹³ = 8,192, scaled diagonal 0.671.

*Row 8.* `unscaled_tolerance_passes_wrong_answer`: H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸), residual r = (0, 10⁻⁷). The unscaled
norm 10⁻⁷ passes an absolute tolerance of 10⁻⁶, while the error in the second unknown is r₂/H₂₂ = 10. With the
power-of-two rule D = diag(1, 8,192), the scaled residual is (0, 8.192·10⁻⁴) and the same tolerance fails it.

**Plants and their identifiers.** Every control of the table is a plant registered under one identifier,
built once (N35-CX's plant table), and the battery loads it by that identifier:
- built here, in host form (Cargo features of `frob-conformance`, conformance builds only): `uncentred-offset-range`
  (CX-369, row 5), `chol-scale-by-ten` (CX-372, row 7; the test `chol_scale_by_ten_breaks_bits` runs it) and
  `unscaled-residual-tolerance` (CX-373, row 8). Their device forms carry the same identifiers and are BD-179's
  (CX-369, CX-372) and BD-156's kernels (CX-373); BD-179 also owns `absolute-f32-positions` (CX-370), which has
  no host row here.
- reused by name, not built again: `batch-wide-reduction` and `normalizer-in-segment` (N35-15), `pad-plus-zero`
  (N35-13), `states-only-comparator` (N35-09, CX-364), `sequential-stream` (N35-24, CX-371), the two-centroid
  sketch (N35-21), and row 10's citation case (N35-06).
This bead writes no row of `controls.toml`: each control is the one N38b-05's seeding tool derives from the CX
entry or the KA row's control cell, and the battery refers to it by its NC identifier.

**Runner.** `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test numerics_battery` runs the ten rows
and then the ten controls through the negative-control runner (N21c-30's `--test negctl`, one registered control
id per row); a row passes only if its known answer holds *and* its control
is caught in the same invocation. Rows bound to a bead that has not landed are reported `pending` with the
milestone and make the battery's status `incomplete`, not `pass`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The battery `numerics` is registered with a content hash and ten rows; each row names its known answers, its test and its control.
- [ ] The six tests written here pass on both CPU architectures: 0x41000000 for the uncentred shift; zero mismatches over the 2²⁵ values of the exhaustive test; the four spacings; bitwise invariance on 10⁴ matrices; the scaling rule on 10⁶ diagonals with the worked value; the tolerance case.
- [ ] Each of the ten controls is caught in the same run as its row; a row whose control is not caught fails the battery.
- [ ] The battery runs with no device in under ten minutes inside the default compute envelope (the exhaustive tests are the long part and are parallel over the logical index only).
- [ ] Bound rows resolve to existing tests; a row whose owner test was renamed or removed fails the registry check.
- [ ] Row 10 is reported `pending: M3` until the profile bead lands and is then bound; the battery's status says `incomplete` in the meantime.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
The six tests named above, plus:
- `numerics_battery_rows`: ten rows; the union of their known answers is KA-785 to KA-801 except KA-789, -794, -795, -799 (which are carried by N35-10, N35-22, N35-16 and BD-179).
- `numerics_battery_controls_caught`: the runner returns `caught` for all ten.
- `reference_cholesky_matches_small_hand_case`: H = [[4, 2], [2, 3]] → L = [[2, 0], [1, √2]] with √2 = 0x3FB504F3.
- `scaling_excludes_overflow_cases`: a matrix with an entry of 2¹²⁰ and a scale of 2²⁰ is excluded and counted, not compared.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/num_known_answers.sh [--row N|all] --seed <s>`: (1) verify the battery's content hash; (2)
`FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance --release --test numerics_battery -- --nocapture`, parsing
the per-row report, and each row's control through `FROB_TEST_SEED=<s> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
negctl -- <control id>`; (3) for rows with a `frob`-level form, the short commands, each run verb with its own
key (`--key nka-<row>-<arm>-<run-id>`; arms compared with each other never share one, and each envelope shows
`idempotent_replay: false`): row 1, `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --workload Q --worlds 16 --seconds 1 --seed
<s> --key nka-1-base-<run-id> --json`, then for world 3 `frob sim replay --run <r> --world 3 --alone --key
nka-1-alone-<run-id> --json` and `frob sim replay --run <r> --world 3 --batch-size 64 --co-tenants <s> --key
nka-1-cot-<run-id> --json` (exit 0, equal digests), and the two replays compared with `frob telemetry divergence
<a> <b> --mode exact --json`; row 3, `frob spark check --conditions --workload Q --backend batch-cpu --seed <s>
--key nka-3-<run-id> --json`; row 4, `frob spark check --decisions --pair batch-cpu:batch-cpu --workload Q
--seed <s> --key nka-4-<run-id> --json` on the boundary fixture; row 6, steps 1 and 2 of N35-24's
`scripts/e2e/num_entity_keying.sh` (the design templates and the keying fixture for the two designs; no run verb,
so no key). Row 9's `frob`-level form
is `telemetry.histogram` (N29a-15, not a dependency of this bead) and row 10's is the D4 re-evaluation of
N35-06 (M3): each is logged as a `skip` event naming its later lane (`scripts/e2e/telemetry_sampling_histograms.sh`
of N29a-16; `scripts/e2e/num_licences_d4.sh` of N35-32), never counted as passed; their host forms are in step 2.
Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/num_known_answers/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript:
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code and wall time; the
evidence block (seed included); per row one `assert` line with the KA numbers, expected and observed values or
bit patterns, and one `metric` line `control_caught` with the control's identifier; `pending` rows logged as
such; on failure the first differing value and the fixture path; a `summary` line with rows passed, pending,
skipped and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls

## Considerations

- The reference Cholesky in the test must use the same operation order as the kernel for the device-level
  form to be comparable, but the host form's claim does not depend on that: the invariance holds for any fixed
  order.
- PO-NUM-01's and PO-NUM-02's obligations are discharged per backend by the precision-battery bead; the host
  forms here are the fast, always-available statement and do not replace the per-backend runs.
- OPEN-NUM-3 (the cell side; default 8 m, centred; blocks nothing) is visible in row 5: a different cell side
  changes the constants of the three tests, which read the side from the same registry row the kernel uses.
- The exhaustive test over 2²⁵ values takes seconds in release and minutes in debug; the debug lane runs a
  sampled version (2²⁰ values including all binade boundaries).
