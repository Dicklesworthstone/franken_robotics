## Background

Every refusal of the system has a stable code, a message and ranked repairs. A refusal is a typed answer, not
an error string: it names the field at fault, what was observed against what was required, and what to do
next. Codes are grouped in nine families by what is refused: `1xx` design and model; `2xx` numerics and
integrator admission; `3xx` scene; `4xx` control and estimation; `5xx` learning and evaluation protocol; `6xx`
backend and device; `7xx` budget and capability; `8xx` evidence and protocol; `9xx` internal. The
machine-readable registry is `registries/refusals.toml`; the foundation crate `frob-core` embeds it and
generates the code type from it (BD-005); an unknown code is a load-time error; a code is never reused.

The printed registry (294 rows at the time of writing) is **compiled from the chapters that define the
codes: the chapter governs the meaning and the repairs**. Each chapter therefore has a registry bead that
enters its own rows. What no chapter bead owns is the registry as a whole, and four things fall in that hole:

1. *Refusals stated in words.* Subsections added late state a refusal ("is refused", "refuses with a typed
   reason") and give no number. The chapter registries can only record a proposal, and proposals collide: two
   chapter registries proposed `FROB-REF-127` for different conditions (the algebraic core, N13-REF, has since
   moved its proposal to 128; 127 stays the design language's, N14-REF, filed in the second round, N35x-02).
2. *Sub-families without an exit code.* The exit-code table of the command-line surface assigns exit 3 to
   `1xx` to `5xx` and to `8xx` other than conflicts, exit 4 to `70x`, exit 5 to `72x` and `73x`, exit 6 to
   `6xx`, exit 7 to conflicts and exit 9 to integrity faults (`FROB-REF-810`, `9xx`). The registry also holds
   `74x` (740, 741), `76x` (760, 761) and `79x` (790, 791) rows, which the table does not name.
3. *Neighbouring codes.* Pairs of codes from two chapters describe nearly the same condition at two raising
   sites (for example 561 and 825; 861 and 867; 562 and 584; 864 and 605; 248 and 244). Each chapter records
   its reading; nothing checks that the two readings agree.
4. *The printout.* The printed registry must be generated from the file, or the two drift.

This bead is the owner of that whole: it allocates numbers, completes the exit rule, checks the registry
across chapters and generates the printout. It decides no meaning and no repair. Tier: Solid. It serves the
three standing rules of refusal (refuse, never truncate; a budget stop publishes nothing partial; a misspelt
parameter is a refusal, not a default), which hold only if every refusal a chapter states can be raised by
code.

Alternatives rejected. *Each chapter takes the next free number in its own block:* that is what produced the
collision above, and a collision is found only when both rows land, as a load-time error. *A central owner of
meanings and repairs:* contradicts the rule that the chapter governs them, and one bead cannot know 294
conditions.

## Scope

In: the allocation ledger and the allocation rule; the first allocation round for the requests on file; the
exit rule per sub-family, as data; the cross-chapter checks of the registry; the neighbour-pair table; the
generated printout; the count check against the printed registry. Out: the content of any row (each
chapter's registry bead; for this chapter N35-REF); the refusal type, the loader and the generated code type
(BD-005); the envelope's refusal block and the surface's exit-code table (N32a-03); the sweep that tests the
three standing rules (N35-26); the gate that exercises every code on both surfaces (BD-299).

## Technical approach

**Allocation ledger (DESIGN).** `registries/refusal_allocations.toml`, schema `frob.refalloc/1`, one row per
request: `request` (a stable slug), `requested_by` (the bead), `defined_in` (the plan subsection that states
the refusal), `condition` (one sentence), `family`, `subfamily` (the decade, such as `76x`: required in `7xx`, whose exit
varies by decade, optional elsewhere), `proposed` (a number, optional), `interim` (`none`, or the existing
code under which the refusal is raised until a number is assigned, or a list of such codes when the raising
sites have different nearest codes (rule 7), with the discriminators of rule 7 that tell the two apart:
`interim_detail`, optional, the `detail` string when it is not the slug, and `interim_pointer`, optional, the
`pointer` value the request names), `assigned`, `status` (`requested`, `assigned`, `merged`, `withdrawn`, and `held`, the fifth status
that the owner decision N35x-01 adds with its `[[hold]]` table), `reason` (for a request left `requested` or
`held`: `range-full`, `owner-decision` or `contested`), `date`, `commit`. A second list, `retired`, holds every
number that was printed or assigned and is no longer in use, with the number that replaced it and the
subsection; at plan time it holds 730 (replaced by 840, §24.13) and 882 (replaced by 582, §27.4): the plan prints
296 numbers, of which 294 are in use. DESIGN: a number a committed bead proposed for a meaning since withdrawn is
also listed in `retired` (with `replaced_by` empty), so that no later request receives a number readers already
associate with another condition; the second round lists 323, 326 and 901 to 907 there (N35x-02).

**Allocation rule (DESIGN; a pure function of the ledger and the registry, so the outcome does not depend on
who asks first).**
1. *Deduplicate before allocating.* A request whose condition is one an existing code already states gets no
   number: its status is `merged`, and its bead is added to that row's `raised_by`.
2. *Family by what is refused*, never by the chapter that asks.
3. *Order.* Requests are processed in order of `defined_in` (chapter, then subsection, then position in the
   subsection's text), not in order of arrival.
4. *Number.* The search range is the request's `subfamily` decade when it has one, otherwise its family. In
   that range: the lowest number above the highest code the defining chapter already holds there, if it is
   free in the registry, in the ledger and in `retired`; otherwise the lowest free number of the range that
   lies in no chapter's block (a block is the span from a chapter's lowest to its highest code in the range).
   A range with no such number leaves the request `requested` with the reason `range-full`, listed at each
   exit. DESIGN: the exit of a `7xx` code is fixed by its decade (`70x` 4, `72x` 5, `76x` 4, `79x` 5), so a
   family-wide search could hand a budget refusal a number whose decade exits 5; for example a `76x` request
   of the adversarial chapter (whose 760 is followed by the taken 761) would receive 726 family-wide and
   receives 762 within its decade.
5. *A proposal is a preference.* If `proposed` lies in the request's range and is free under rule 4's test it
   is taken; if two requests
   propose the same number, the earlier in rule 3's order takes it and the other is numbered by rule 4. DESIGN:
   within one run, rule 4 never offers a request a number that another request of the run proposes (when
   nothing else is free the request is left `requested` with `range-full`); otherwise whether a proposal is
   honoured would depend on whether an
   unrelated request without a proposal sorts before it (`rut-capacity-overflow`, §18, would take 726 from the
   surface's `mechanism-not-admitted`, §32, which beads already cite by that number).
6. *Never reused.* A withdrawn or retired number is never assigned again.
7. *Interim codes.* Until `assigned` is filled, the raising bead uses `interim` behind one named constant.
   Assignment is then a one-line change and a schema-snapshot update. A registry check lists every interim
   use; a milestone exit with an interim use older than one milestone is a finding. DESIGN, the admitted forms:
   `interim` is an existing code of the registry, raised with `detail` equal to the request's slug (and the
   `pointer` the request names), or it is `none`. A row may declare another fixed `detail` string in place of
   its slug (`interim_detail`): `prediction-after-outcome` (N28cx-01) is raised as 883 with `detail =
   "registration-after-outcome"`. A row whose raising sites have different nearest codes names one code per
   site, each raised with the row's `detail` (`duplicate-member-name`, N35x-02: 802, 103 or 891). `detail` is
   an optional string member of the refusal block (BD-005's `Refusal`, serialized in the envelope's `refusal`
   object by N32a-03) that exists for this rule: it is absent on every raise of a code under its own meaning and
   present on every interim raise, beside the `pointer`, `observed` and `required` the request's own block
   states. No two unsettled requests share a pair (code, `detail`), which is a ledger error, so the interim list
   (one line per unsettled request: slug, code or `none`, `detail`, `pointer`, owning bead, status) maps every
   interim raise back to one request. A proposed, held or unassigned number is never an interim (it
   would be raised, logged and published before the rule has decided it, and rule 5 may give another number),
   and a bare slug is not one (a refusal block needs a registered code; BD-005's loader refuses an unknown one).
   A request with `interim = none` keeps its raising bead open on that point: the mechanism still refuses and
   is not shipped as passing (N35x-01's default).
8. *No `8xx` number before the owner decision is in the ledger (DESIGN).* The `8xx` family has four free numbers
   (836 to 839) against more than twenty requests (N35x-01). Until N35x-01's `[[hold]]` table or its ruling is
   in the ledger, the function assigns no `8xx` number: an `8xx` request stays `requested` with reason
   `owner-decision`, and becomes `held` when the hold lands. Reason: a first round that ran before the hold
   would hand the family's last numbers to whichever `8xx` requests sort first, which is the outcome the owner
   decision exists to choose.

Requests that arrive later follow the same rule; the ledger is append-only. A chapter registry may append its
own later rows (N24-REF and N19c-42 do); the second round, N35x-02, files the rest in one run.

**Exit rule per sub-family (data, `[exit_rule]` in the registry; the loader of BD-005 reads it).** One row per
range: `1xx` to `5xx` → 3; `6xx` → 6; `70x` → 4; `72x`, `73x` → 5; `8xx` → 3; `9xx` → 9. Three exception lists
override the range for the codes they name, whatever the family, following the surface's exit table: `conflict`
→ 7 (key reuse with different content, a stale parent, a lineage or plan conflict: 803, 804 and 809, N32-REF;
and 112, a stale parent in a design patch, which lies in `1xx`, N14-REF); `integrity` → 9 (810, a chunk
conflict, N32-REF); `capability` → 5 (an `8xx` code that reports a missing scope or capability: 840, a session
without `qual:admin` asking for sealed confirmation data, N24-REF and N37-REF). A code is in at most one list,
and a listed code must exist in the registry; either violation fails the load. A later row whose chapter
states an exit other than its range's is added, in the same change as the row, to the list of the class the
surface's exit table gives that exit. The three unnamed sub-families get rows
here (DESIGN, each confirmed against the exit its defining registry bead states): `74x` → 4 and `76x` → 4
(a budget or a capacity refused at admission); `79x` → 5 (a capability this build does not have). A `7xx`
decade with a code and no row is a load-time error, so the gap cannot reopen. If two chapter registries
state different exits for one sub-family, the load fails and the choice is recorded as an ADR in this bead.

**Cross-chapter checks (run by `cargo test -p frob-core` and by `xtask check-generated`).**
1. Codes are unique and sorted; none is in `retired`.
2. Every row has `defined_in`, a message, at least two ranked repairs or the reason it has fewer, one `retry`
   value, and `raised_by` naming a bead or the word `unbound`.
3. Every row's exit equals its sub-family's.
4. Every ledger row with `assigned` has a registry row of that number, and every registry row that is not in
   the printed 294 has a ledger row.
5. *Neighbour pairs.* A `related` field must be symmetric, and each pair carries one sentence per side that
   says which raising site uses which code; a pair whose two sentences name the same site is an error.
6. *Count.* At each milestone exit the rows present are compared with the printed 294 by chapter; a missing
   row is listed as `pending: <registry bead>` until that bead's milestone and is an error after it.

**Printout.** `docs/generated/refusals.md`: one table per family (code, meaning, defined in, exit), generated
from the registry and checked by `xtask check-generated` (BD-004). It replaces hand-edited copies.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/refusal_allocations.toml` exists with every first-round request as a row, each `assigned` or `merged`, except the `8xx` requests, which are `requested` with reason `owner-decision` (or `held` once N35x-01's hold is in the ledger); no two rows share an assigned number; the Marrow request proposing 128 and the design language's 127 (second round) are distinct rows, and no first-round row takes 127.
- [ ] No `8xx` number is assigned by the first round; `retired` holds 730 and 882 with their replacements, and every withdrawn proposal the second round lists.
- [ ] Every `interim` in the ledger is an existing code or `none`; no interim is a proposed or held number or a bare slug.
- [ ] The allocation function is pure: the first round run on the requests in 100 shuffled orders gives the same assignment every time.
- [ ] Every `7xx` ledger row names its `subfamily`, and every number assigned in `7xx` lies in that decade, so its exit is the one the request states.
- [ ] The stream-entity collision has its own code, N35-23's loader raises it, and its interim use of 103 is gone from the interim list.
- [ ] `[exit_rule]` covers every sub-family that has a code, including `74x`, `76x` and `79x`; `FROB-REF-761` loads with exit 4; a planted `7xx` code in a decade with no row fails the load.
- [ ] The exception lists hold `conflict` = 112, 803, 804, 809 (exit 7), `integrity` = 810 (exit 9) and `capability` = 840 (exit 5); each of those rows loads with its list's exit, and a planted copy with its range's exit (112 with 3, 840 with 3) fails check 3.
- [ ] Checks 1 to 6 run in `cargo test -p frob-core` and `xtask check-generated`; each has a planted violation that it catches (below).
- [ ] The five neighbour pairs named in the Background are present, symmetric, with one sentence per side.
- [ ] `docs/generated/refusals.md` is generated, is byte-stable across two runs, and a hand edit of it fails `xtask check-generated`.
- [ ] The ledger check produces the interim list of rule 7 (one line per unsettled request: slug, code or `none`, `detail`, `pointer`, owning bead, status) and refuses two unsettled requests that share a pair (code, `detail`). Serving it as the `interims` member of the refusal table of `frob schema --refusals` (and the bare `frob --json schema`) is BD-022's, which depends on this bead and runs stages (2) and (4) of this bead's script against the binary it builds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
- `alloc_order_independent`: 100 seeded shuffles of the first-round requests, one assignment.
- `alloc_same_proposal_twice`: two synthetic requests proposing 127, defined in different chapters: the earlier chapter keeps 127, the other receives the number rule 4 gives; neither is lost.
- `alloc_merges_duplicate_condition`: a request whose condition equals an existing row's is `merged` and receives no number.
- `alloc_never_reuses_retired`: with 253 in `retired`, the next `2xx` request of this chapter receives 254.
- `alloc_block_rule`: a chapter whose block ends at 252 with 253 to 259 free receives 253; with 253 taken by another chapter's row it receives the lowest free number outside every block.
- `alloc_proposal_not_offered_to_others`: a `72x` request without a proposal defined in §18 and a `72x` request
  defined in §32 proposing 726 in one run: the §32 request receives 726 and the §18 request 727; with 727 to 729
  planted as taken, the §32 request still receives 726 and the §18 request is `requested` with `range-full`;
  with the §32 request removed, the §18 request receives 726.
- `alloc_respects_subfamily`: on the plan-time registry a `76x` request defined in §26 receives 762 (not 726,
  which a family-wide search gives, in `72x` with exit 5); a `7xx` request with no `subfamily` is a ledger
  error; a proposal outside the request's decade is not taken; a planted full decade leaves its request
  `requested` with `range-full`.
- `first_round_assigns_no_8xx`: on the first-round ledger with no `[[hold]]` table, every `8xx` request is
  `requested` with reason `owner-decision` and 836 to 839 stay free; with the hold of N35x-01 added, the same

- `interim_forms_admitted`: planted rows with `interim = 838` (a held proposal), `interim = 726` (a number not in

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/refusal_registry.sh`: (1) `cargo test -p frob-core refusal_` and `cargo xtask check-generated`,
logging the interim list the ledger check produces. Stages (2) to (4) drive a `frob` binary, which this bead
neither builds nor depends on: each runs only when `e2e_init` resolves one (`FROB_BIN`, STD-E2E) and is
otherwise logged through `e2e_skip "binary absent"`, never as passed; BD-022 runs stages (2) and (4) against the
binary it builds. (2) `frob --json schema` and a comparison of the code list in its refusal table with the
registry file; (3) for three codes of three families (one of them 761) a request that provokes the code through
`frob --json`, asserting the code and the exit of its sub-family, each logged `skipped: capability absent`
where the raising mechanism does not exist yet, never as passed; (4) `frob schema --refusals --json` and a
comparison of its `interims` member with the interim list of stage (1). It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/refusal_registry/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code and
wall time; `refusal` events with code, exit, `pointer` and first repair; `metric` lines for rows present
against 294, rows pending by chapter, interim uses, allocations assigned and merged; `assert` lines per
check; a `summary` line. No private-scene data is involved.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: the family and exit of 761 (budget, exit 4); 112 → 7 and 840 → 5 by the exception lists; the
  block arithmetic of `alloc_block_rule`.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): a duplicated code; a reused retired code; a `76x` row with exit 3;
  an 840 row with exit 3; two rows assigned one number in the ledger; two unsettled requests on one pair
  (code, `detail`); an asymmetric neighbour pair; a hand-edited printout.
- Metamorphic: permuting the requests, or the rows of the registry file before sorting, changes nothing.
- Determinism: every computation is on integers and strings, class D0, identical on every platform.

## Considerations

- The boundary with the chapters is strict: this bead may refuse a row (duplicate, wrong exit, missing
  field) and may number a request; it may not edit a message, a trigger or a repair. A disagreement about a
  neighbour pair is settled by the two chapter registries and recorded here.
- A number that has appeared in a result, a log or a schema snapshot is public. Renumbering after that is a
  retirement plus a new assignment, never an in-place change; interim codes exist so that this is rare.
- The first round should close before the M1 exit, because the M1 mechanisms raise several of these
  refusals; a late round leaves them on interim codes, which is visible in the interim list and blocks
  nothing.
- The exits proposed for `74x`, `76x` and `79x` are DESIGN. If an owning chapter states otherwise, its
  statement governs and the row here follows it.
- What would make us revisit: a family running out of numbers, which would need a wider code and is a format
  change of the envelope. The `8xx` family has reached it (four free numbers against more than twenty
  requests); the question, its hold and its fail-closed default are the owner decision N35x-01, and the
  second allocation round is N35x-02. Rule 8 is what keeps the first round from settling it by order of
  processing; N35x-01's sentence that a first-round `8xx` assignment made before the hold "stands" therefore
  never applies.
