## Background

A refusal is a typed answer with a stable code `FROB-REF-nnn`, a message and ranked repairs; codes are grouped in
families by what is refused (`1xx` design and model, `2xx` numerics and integrator admission, `3xx` scene, `4xx`
control and estimation, `5xx` learning and evaluation protocol, `6xx` backend and device, `7xx` budget and
capability, `8xx` evidence and protocol, `9xx` internal) and are never reused. The registry
`registries/refusals.toml` is owned as a whole by N35-33: an allocation ledger
`registries/refusal_allocations.toml` (one row per request: `request`, `requested_by`, `defined_in`,
`condition`, `family`, `proposed`, `interim`, `assigned`, `status`), a pure allocation rule (deduplicate against
existing codes; family by what is refused; requests ordered by defining chapter, subsection and position; the
lowest free number above the defining chapter's highest code in the family, otherwise the lowest free number
outside every chapter's block; a proposal is a preference; nothing retired is reused; interim codes behind one
named constant until assignment) and a first round with the requests of eight registries (the algebraic core,
dynamics, the GPU backends, learning, tasks, performance, the numerics loader and the surface).

Fifteen other chapters also state refusals in words with no number, and each of their registry beads handled it
alone. FACT (read from those beads): 43 such refusals are on file, and the handling collides:

- One proposal names a number that is already a printed code: `FROB-REF-448` is "a skill's task wrench lies
  outside the body's attainable set" and is proposed again for a sensor refusal.
- Two chapter decision beads enter their proposed numbers into the registry by default, before any owner answers
  (318, 319, 322, 323 for scenes; 170, 171 and 901 to 907 for structure, the last block since withdrawn by the
  Foundry decision N31b-45), one registry bead loads a proposed row (792), and one uses a provisional number (838).
- Nineteen requests ask for the evidence family `8xx` (twenty-two with `synthesis-out-of-scope`,
  `packet-inconsistent` and `fixture-reference-unresolved`, filed since), which has four free numbers (the owner
  decision N35x-01).
- One request is an integrity fault proposed in `3xx` with exit 9, which the exit rule of the family forbids.

This bead is the second round: it files every one of the 43, and the twenty-nine filed since (six at the
request of round-1 reviews, twelve found by the round-3 review, one of them of a sixteenth chapter, §42, and
eleven that raising or registry beads of seven chapters name as ledger rows, among them a seventeenth chapter,
the ten-fold test, §39), as a ledger row with its family decided by what is refused, runs the rule, and makes
the ledger, not each chapter's default, the place a number comes from. It
decides no message and no repair. Tier: Solid. Rejected: leaving each chapter to file its own row whenever its
bead is worked (the outcome would depend on which bead is worked first, and the registry check that every new
row has a ledger row would fail the defaults one at a time); assigning the numbers by hand here (the rule is a
function precisely so that nobody types a number).

## Scope

retired; the family of each with its reason; the run of the allocation function and its recorded

every row, its trigger fixture and its emit site (each chapter's registry and mechanism beads); the code type and
loader (BD-005).
