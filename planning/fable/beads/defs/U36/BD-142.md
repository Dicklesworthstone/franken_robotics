## Background

GATE-SPARK-21, the parity-baseline gate (claim: Parity), closes at M1. Its M0 half put the `trj` rows that need no
agent on record. This bead completes it: the rows on the Mac (an M5 Ultra: 36 CPU cores, an 80-core GPU, 256 GB
of unified memory) and the native-settings rows on `trj`. After it, every ratio the performance program will ever
state has a measured denominator on the same hardware, and the table of incumbent rates that the engine's targets
need is complete.

Why two settings. Incumbents do not share a decimation (one publishes its quadruped task at a 20 ms control period
over a 4 ms physics step, d = 5; another reports 1.20 M env-steps/s of physics as 4.8 M physics steps per second,
d = 4), while our reference workloads use h = 2 ms and d = 10. Parity is therefore measured twice: at **matched
settings** (the incumbent at our h, d and iteration count where its solver allows; done at M0) and at **native
settings** (each stack at its own recommended step for the task), the second in env-steps/s and in time to a
return threshold only, because a comparison in world-steps at different decimations is a comparison of settings.

Why the Mac rows. Two targets are ratios to incumbents on that machine: the `batch-cpu` rows (at least 0.5 of CPU
MuJoCo on the same machine and threads) and the Metal row, which is reported beside an incumbent with a Metal path
measured on the same machine. No published rigid-body benchmark on this chip was found, so without this bead those
rows have no comparator at all.

## Scope

In: the M1 campaign (Mac CPU rows, Mac Metal incumbent row, `trj` native rows), reproduction judgements and
explanations, the completed gate record, the reconciliation of the plan-time CPU readings. Out: the M0 rows
(BD-040); the agent-driven incumbent design round and its ledger (BD-143); our own engine's rows (M2).

## Technical approach

**Rows.**

| Row | Hardware and settings | Repeats | Planning-time context (FACT about a plan-time reading, to be replaced) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CPU MuJoCo, H | The Mac; `f64`; the 35-degree-of-freedom humanoid at h = 2 ms; 1, 24 and 36 threads; physics only | 10 per thread count, interleaved, after the soak | Under load: 24 k world-steps/s on one thread, 550 k on 24, 576 k on 36. Published for a lighter humanoid: 650 k on a laptop chip and 1.8 M on a 64-core workstation |
| CPU MuJoCo, Q | The same machine and thread counts; the reference quadruped | As above | No published quadruped CPU figure was found. A separate plan-time check recorded about 410,000 steps/s for a reference quadruped at 2 ms on 36 threads under background load |
| Metal-path incumbent, Q | The Mac; Genesis on its Metal path; physics only; its recommended settings and, where possible, matched ones | 10 | None published on this chip |
| Physics and full step, Q and H, native | `trj`, one RTX 4090; each stack at its own step and decimation; env-steps/s | 10 | 1.20 M env-steps/s of physics and 347 k for the full step at d = 4 (on an RTX 5090) |
| Time to threshold, native | `trj`; already native at M0 (the incumbent's own recipe); re-read here only if the M0 run used a matched variant | — | About 410 s and about 325 s |
| Humanoid time to threshold, two cards | `trj`; the incumbent's first-stage flat-ground humanoid recipe on both cards (the comparator of the humanoid training-time target) | 3 runs; median and range | Under 30 minutes on two RTX 4090 for two humanoids; about 1 h for another stack's default rough-terrain run on one card (derived) |
| Remaining published figures of the target table | The mixed-batch ratio of an incumbent's mixed-topology solver (0.86 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates, at 2,048 environments); the training-rate triple of another stack (94 k, 88 k, 82 k env-steps/s) | A short run each under the same harness, or a written explanation | As stated |

**Procedure, numbered.**
1. On the Mac a **20-minute sustained load precedes measurement** so that the thermal state is steady; the thermal
   state before and after and the soak duration go into the disclosure block. On `trj` the host load is recorded.
2. Thread rows: the incumbent's own thread pool at 1, 24 and 36 threads, pinned to performance cores where the
   operating system allows; the thread count and core kinds are disclosed. Under the default envelope (8 cores)
   the 1-thread row and an 8-thread row are measured and the 24- and 36-thread rows are recorded `not run`.
3. Run `scripts/e2e/perf_parity_baseline.sh --milestone m1 --machine mac --seed <s>` and `--machine trj`. The two
   machines' logs are collated; only receipts, digests and summaries cross between machines.
4. Scaling sanity per thread sweep: record the speedup from 1 to 24 and from 24 to 36 threads (the plan-time
   reading gives 22.9 and 1.05), because the `batch-cpu` target is stated at 24 threads and the choice of 24 is
   justified by the flattening beyond it.
5. Reconcile the plan-time CPU readings. The humanoid reading (576 k world-steps/s on 36 threads) and the
   quadruped reading (about 410 k steps/s on 36 threads) cannot both describe a machine on which "a quadruped step
   is cheaper", so at least one was taken under different load. Both are replaced by this bead's measurements,
   and the explanation file states which one moved and by how much.
6. Reproduction judgement for each published figure touched (inside [0.8, 1.2] of the published value, or an
   explanation in writing); measured values replace planning values in the target register. In particular the
   floor of the humanoid CPU row (275 k, half of a rate measured on a loaded machine and therefore a lower bound
   on the incumbent) is replaced by 0.5 × the quiet-machine measurement.
7. Sweep the target table for published figures not yet touched: each is reproduced by a run under the harness
   or explained in writing. A figure that another experiment will measure with its own incumbent arm (the
   mixed-batch ratio, in the mixed-batch head-to-head) may carry the explanation "published context until that
   arm runs", and the target register keeps it marked `to-re-read`; it is not cited as a comparator meanwhile.
8. Complete the gate record: every clause of the criterion has a row that is `pass` (measured and reproduced or
   explained), or `not run` with its envelope reason. The gate's status is `pass` only if no clause is `not run`
   for a reason other than an unapproved envelope class recorded by the owner's decision.

**Evidence.** Claim Parity, class `Tested`. Each rate is a Performance figure of the incumbent under its own
disclosure block (subject `incumbent`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::baseline_m1`:
- `thread_scaling`: (24,000; 550,000; 576,000) → 22.9 and 1.047.
- `cpu_floor_from_measurement`: incumbent 620 k at 24 threads → tolerance 310 k; with no measurement → 275 k, status `interim-floor`.
- `native_comparison_units`: a native row offered in world-steps/s is rejected for comparison; in env-steps/s it is accepted.
- `soak_required_on_mac`: a Mac row whose block has `soak_s` under 1,200 is rejected.
- `gate_complete_requires_every_clause`: removing the Metal-incumbent row makes the record incomplete.
- `not_run_for_envelope_does_not_fail_gate_but_is_listed`.
- `reconciliation_note_required`: the record fails if both plan-time CPU readings are still cited.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_parity_baseline.sh --milestone m1` (N36-29) on each machine, then the gate test
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g2 -- GATE-SPARK-21 --milestone m1`. Logged per repeat: comparator and
revision, thread count or device, settings, the rate with unit and decimation, soak and thermal state or host
load, the block identifier, the published value and ratio; a `summary` with every clause of the gate and its
status.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic on the plan-time readings (thread scaling above; 0.5 × 550 k = 275 k).
- Negative controls (registered with the gate): a Mac row measured without the soak; a native-settings row compared in world-steps; a clause silently dropped from the record.
- A/A sanity: the same CPU configuration measured twice back to back agrees within the row's recorded spread.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-2 and RISK-10: the 24- and 36-thread rows exceed the default of 8 cores. If the class is not approved the `batch-cpu` targets are judged at 8 threads against an 8-thread incumbent row, and the record says so.
- BET-09 (Metal on Apple silicon is a real rollout tier; falsified below 25% of one RTX 4090 at matched settings): the Metal-incumbent row measured here is what the M2 figure is reported beside. It is context for that bet, not its test.
- If the Metal-path incumbent cannot run the reference quadruped on this machine at all, the row is recorded `unavailable` with the evidence, and the M2 Metal figure is reported with that gap named.
- Thermal state matters on unified-memory hardware: CPU and GPU share power and bandwidth, so a GPU row and a CPU row are never run concurrently.
- RISK-25: this bead is the last correction of the denominator before mechanisms are funded against it; a lever whose removable share is under 0.10 on the measured ledger is struck from the speed case before M2 begins (the ten-fold chapter's rule, applied by its own bead).
