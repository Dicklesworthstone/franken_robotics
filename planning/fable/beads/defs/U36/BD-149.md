## Background

Stage 1 of the batched physics step computes, for every world, the pose and the twist of every link, walking the
kinematic tree from the root outward. Everything after it reads its outputs: collision geometry poses, the mass
matrix and bias forces, contact Jacobians, sensors. The two kernels of this bead are that stage: `K-fk-level`
(forward kinematics: link poses) and `K-twist-level` (link twists). They run on the `batch-cpu` tier (Rust, the
permanent CPU reference) and, from the same derived arithmetic, on CUDA and Metal.

The tree recursion is parallel across depth, which is the shape a batched device wants: links are numbered
topologically (the parent's index is less than the child's) and sorted by depth, so one launch handles "worlds ×
links at one depth" with no dependency inside the launch.

Decisions that bind these kernels, with their reasons:

- **Joints are data, not code paths.** A joint is its screw basis S: a 6 × 3 matrix with k = 1, 2 or 3 active
  columns (k = 1 for revolute, prismatic and helical joints, 2 for cylindrical and universal, 3 for spherical and
  planar; k = 0 marks a padding link). The joint map is the product of one-parameter motions over the active
  columns, with masked columns as the identity. One derived kernel covers every joint class with no branch on
  joint kind. Rejected: an enumeration of joint kinds with one hand-written path each.
- **No trigonometry in a batched kernel** (ADR-MARROW-03). A revolute coordinate is stored as a unit complex
  number (c, s) plus an integer turn count; no raw angle is in state. Reason: bitwise agreement between CPU and
  devices is impossible with built-in sine and cosine, and policies consume (cos, sin) anyway.
- **Derived arithmetic, hand-written skeleton.** The body of a kernel (one link's joint map, one composition, one
  adjoint transport) is produced at build time by symbolic derivation, lowered to the kernel intermediate
  representation and emitted as Rust and as dialect text with its certificates. Loops over levels and links are
  written by hand. Derivation is per kernel body, never per whole robot.
- **Padding is exact.** Padding links are identity transforms appended after the real links; the real part of
  every output is bit-identical to the unpadded computation.

Fallback if derivation cannot express the bodies (kill gate KILL-KIR, decided at M2 by its own bead): the
arithmetic is hand-written in Rust and the dialect and bound by differential tests; skeletons and tests here are
unchanged. Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**Conventions.** A transform `a_from_b` = (R, p) maps coordinates in b to a: x_a = R·x_b + p. A twist is
ξ = (ω, v), angular part first, in the body frame of its link. For g = (R, p), the twist map is
Ad_g(ω, v) = (R·ω, R·v + p × R·ω), and Ad of the inverse is Ad_g⁻¹(ω, v) = (Rᵀ·ω, Rᵀ·(v − p × ω)).

**Inputs.** From the model slot of each world (read-only): `parent[i]`, `level_start[ℓ]`, `placement[i]` (the
transform `parent_from_joint` at the reference configuration), `screw[i]` (6 × 3, each column (ω_k, v_k)),
`screw_cols[i]` (k). Each active column is normalized at model compile time: either ‖ω_k‖ = 1 (a rotational
column with pitch η_k = ω_k·v_k) or ω_k = 0 and ‖v_k‖ = 1 (a translational column); a per-column kind flag
selects the formula by `select`, never by a branch. From state: for each column its coordinate, (c, s, turn) for
a rotational column and a length in metres for a translational one, and its rate q̇; for a floating base the root
pose (unit quaternion and anchored position) and root twist.

**One-parameter motion of column k.**
- Rotational, with coordinate (c, s): R_k = I + s·ω̂ + (1 − c)·ω̂², p_k = (I − R_k)·(ω × v) + η·θ·ω, where ω̂ is
  the cross-product matrix of ω and θ = 2π·turn + atan2(s, c) is needed only when the pitch η is not zero
  (a helical joint), computed by the deterministic elementary function of the kernel library, not a built-in.
- Translational, with coordinate q: R_k = I, p_k = q·v.
- Masked: the identity.

**`K-fk-level`, per lane (world w, link i at level ℓ).**
1. Joint map J_i = E₁·E₂·E₃ (the three column motions in order; masked ones are the identity).
2. `parent_from_link` = placement_i ∘ J_i. Store its inverse `link_from_parent` = (Rᵀ, −Rᵀ·p) as X_i; later
   stages apply Ad of it to twists and its transpose to wrenches.
3. `world_from_link(i)` = `world_from_link(parent(i))` ∘ `parent_from_link(i)`. For the root, the pose comes from
   state. Positions are stored as `f32` offsets from the world's anchor cell; rotations as 3 × 3 matrices.
4. Effective motion subspace S̃_i (6 × k, in the link's own frame): column 3 is S₃; column 2 is Ad of E₃⁻¹ applied
   to S₂; column 1 is Ad of (E₂·E₃)⁻¹ applied to S₁. For k = 1 it is S itself.

**`K-twist-level`, per lane.** ξ_i = Ad(X_i)·ξ_parent(i) + S̃_i·q̇_i, with the root's twist from state. Outputs
ξ_i and the joint twist S̃_i·q̇_i (used by the bias-force recursion).

**Skeleton.** For ℓ in 0..depth capacity: one launch (or one loop on `batch-cpu`) over lanes worlds × links in
[`level_start[ℓ]`, `level_start[ℓ + 1]`), first `K-fk-level`, then `K-twist-level`. A lane reads only its own row
and its parent's row from the previous level; there is no reduction across lanes, so the kernels are trivially
independent of worker count, batch size and slot. Loop bounds are capacity constants of the shape class; indices
are unsigned 32-bit; worlds of one model are contiguous in runs of the execution width so the level loop does not
diverge inside a group.

**Manifests (`frob.kernel/1`).** Class `float`. Read footprint: model-slot rows of the lane and its parent;
state coordinates of the lane. Write footprint: `pose[w, i]`, `X[w, i]`, `S_eff[w, i]`, `xi[w, i]`. No aliasing.
Neutral element for padding: the identity transform and the zero twist. Determinism claimed: D2 on `batch-cpu`
(bit-identical run to run and in any batch); D1 against devices only where the algebraic-kernel bit-equality gate
has passed. Precision: `f64` and `f32` instantiations of one program. Banned operations (fused multiply-add,
built-in transcendentals, fast-math) are absent by lint.

**Derivation and certificates.** Bodies derived: `screw_motion` (rotation and translation from (c, s) or q),
`se3_compose`, `se3_inverse`, `adjoint_inverse_apply`, `motion_subspace`. Each is a typed straight-line program:
positions in metres, twists in (rad/s, m/s), screw columns in (1, m) per unit coordinate; a sum that mixes them
does not type-check. Certificates bound to the content hash: R_k·R_kᵀ = I as a polynomial identity modulo
c² + s² = 1 and ω·ω = 1; Ad(g·h) = Ad(g)·Ad(h); the twist recursion equals g⁻¹·ġ for the pose recursion (by
differentiating the derived pose program). Generated sources are checked in with their digest; the build
regenerates and compares; generated code is never edited by hand.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed done-condition: parity with `fs-mbd` forward kinematics: the kinematics row of GATE-KIN-03 passes on `batch-cpu` in `f64`, link poses and link twists within 10⁻¹⁰ relative on 10⁴ states per reference robot, measured by the shared harness and recorded.
- [ ] The planar two-link fixture is reproduced exactly: tool point (0.4, 0.3) m; tool velocity (−0.03, 0.08) m/s for joint rates (0.2, −0.1) rad/s.
- [ ] A three-column joint gives the same poses and twists as three one-column joints chained through massless intermediate links (to round-off, bound on file).
- [ ] Rotation outputs are orthonormal within 4 ulp per entry after any state in the sample set.
- [ ] Bits are identical across worker counts 1, 8 and 24, across batch sizes 1 and 10⁴, and with padding links appended.
- [ ] Each manifest has its stage signature; the dimension checker accepts both programs and rejects the planted mixed-unit sum; the banned-operation lint passes on the Rust and on the dialect text.
- [ ] Certificates for the three identities are produced and accepted by the build-time checker; a hand-edited generated body fails the generated-source check.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-kernels` lists both kernels (inputs, outputs, invariants, determinism class, oracle: `fs-mbd` at the stack-lock pin).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels fk twist`:
- `fk::planar_2r_fixture` (KA-049): lengths 0.4 m and 0.3 m, axes +z, coordinates (c, s) = (1, 0) and (0, 1) → tool point (0.4, 0.3, 0) m, exact in rational arithmetic and within 1 ulp in `f32`.
- `twist::planar_2r_rates` (KA-049): rates (0.2, −0.1) rad/s → tool-point velocity (−0.03, 0.08) m/s.
- `fk::revolute_offset_axis`: ω = (0, 0, 1), axis through (1, 0, 0) so v = (0, −1, 0), (c, s) = (0, 1) → R is a quarter turn about z and p = (1, −1, 0).
- `fk::prismatic`: v = (0, 0, 1), q = 0.25 m → R = I, p = (0, 0, 0.25).
- `fk::helical_pitch`: ω = z, pitch 0.01 m/rad, half a turn → axial travel 0.0314159 m (within 2 ulp of the `f64` value).
- `fk::three_columns_equal_three_links`; `fk::masked_columns_are_identity`.
- `fk::rotation_orthonormal` (property test over random unit (c, s) and unit axes).
- `twist::root_passthrough`; `twist::matches_finite_difference` (in `f64`, central difference of poses, within 10⁻⁷).
- `kernels::worker_and_batch_invariance`; `kernels::padding_links_inert`.
- `kernels::dimension_check_rejects`: a program adding an angular velocity to a linear velocity.
- `kernels::no_banned_operations`.
- Law tests (property): Ad(g·h) = Ad(g)·Ad(h) on tiny angles, rotations near π from both sides and translations near the origin; the pairing of a wrench and a twist is unchanged by a change of frame.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kin_parity_fsmbd.sh --row kinematics` (owned by the parity-harness bead): runs the kernels on the
10⁴ recorded states per robot as 10⁴ worlds of one batch and compares with the oracle through the airlock. Its
`metric` lines carry the robot, the row, the maximum relative error against 10⁻¹⁰ for poses and for twists, the
`KernelId` of each kernel and the stack-lock digest; on failure the first differing state index, link and
component with both values. The batched step's own script exercises both kernels inside a full step.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-360 (kinematics row): agreement within 10⁻¹⁰ relative. KA-049: the planar fixture.
- Negative controls, each a planted defect a test must catch: the adjoint applied in the wrong direction in the twist recursion (must fail the composition law, the pairing and the planar fixture, three independent fixtures); `parent_from_link` composed on the wrong side of the parent pose; (c, s) exchanged; a built-in sine in the body (the lint refuses it at build time).
- Metamorphic: re-expressing the root pose in another world frame transforms every link pose by the same transform and leaves body twists unchanged; permuting world indices permutes results and changes nothing else.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; worker counts 1, 8, 24; debug and release; the `f32` instantiation against devices is checked by the backend beads.

## Considerations

- Ceiling stated in advance: kinematics and smooth dynamics together are about 20% of an incumbent's humanoid step (FACT from a published event trace, to be re-read), so these kernels are not a throughput lever; derivation is kept for correctness, certificates and derivative programs.
- The plan does not say how a helical joint's axial travel is stored. This bead computes it from the lifted angle; the alternative, a separate lifted travel coordinate in state, is cheaper per step and is a change confined to the packed lowering if the profile asks for it.
- The oracle has only fixed, revolute, prismatic and helical joints; multi-column joints are compared against their one-column products, which is exact for kinematics.
- Memory: the kinematic and dynamic cache is budgeted at 4·(61·bodies + 17·n) bytes per world in `f32` (4,396 bytes for the quadruped workload, 9,700 for the humanoid); the outputs here must fit inside that budget or the byte model is updated with them.
- If KILL-KIR fires, bodies are hand-written and certificates fall back to differential tests; the gate row is unchanged.
