## Background

`trj` has two RTX 4090 cards with no link between them. Scaling from one card to two is measured **both ways,
because they answer different questions**:

- **Weak scaling** holds the load per device constant: each card runs the full single-card workload, as two
  independent shards. It answers "is the second card a second lane". Because shards are independent, the
  tolerance is 1.8 (ambition 1.95).
- **Strong scaling** holds the job constant: the same training to the same return threshold at equal task quality.
  It answers "does one job finish sooner on two cards". FACT (published; re-measured in the parity baseline): an
  incumbent's own two-card run gains 1.26 (about 325 s against about 410 s). Fixed costs explain it: about 45 s
  of its 410 s is compilation, the learner runs on one stream, and sample efficiency falls as the batch grows.
  The tolerance is 1.25; the ambition is 1.6.

This is the seed bead in which both are measured, the scaling gate is recorded, and the measurement that decides
the kill gate KILL-SHARD is produced. KILL-SHARD: after one optimization pass, if the paired lower bound of strong
scaling at equal task quality is below 1.6, no further sharding work is funded (asynchronous collection, the
nondeterministic training profile) and the two cards run as two independent job lanes; the tolerance of 1.25
still applies to synchronous sharding where it is used. The synchronous update itself follows the global
normalization rule (per-shard sums, added in device order, divided by the global valid count; an empty shard
contributes zero and still participates), implemented by the shard runtime and the learning unit's reducer.

Two gates read the same strong-scaling figure and hold the same number: GATE-SPARK-20 (claim: Performance; M2):
weak scaling at least 1.8 and strong scaling at least 1.25 at equal task quality, both as paired lower bounds;
the gradient known answer holds and its control is caught. GATE-CORTEX-08 (claim: Parity; M2): its governing part
is the agreement of two-shard and one-device updates, and its scaling clause (at least 1.25 at constant global
workload and equal task quality) is evaluated here.

## Scope

In: the two campaigns; the equal-quality rule; the one optimization pass; the gate records; the verdict input for
the kill gate. Out: the shard runtime (N36-08); the reducer, statistics merge and partition property test
(the learning unit); the ceiling pilot (N36-09); the decision ADR (N36-10); the script (N36-31).

## Technical approach

**Weak scaling (target row 6).** Workload: the reference quadruped, physics only, 8,192 worlds on each card.
Arm A: both cards stepping simultaneously as independent shards; figure = the sum of the two cards' primary rates
over the same wall-clock window. Arm B: one card alone at 8,192 worlds. Ratio A/B, at least ten pairs,
interleaved; the paired lower bound is judged against 1.8. What keeps it under 2: the host feeds both cards
(threads, memory bandwidth, power and thermal headroom), so the single-card arm must run with the other card idle
and the two-card arm with both loaded, and the disclosure blocks say so.

**Strong scaling (target row 5).** Job: PPO training of the reference flat velocity task from a cold start to its
return threshold, compilation and admission included. Arm B: one card (this is the time of target row 4). Arm A:
the same job with `--shards 2` at **constant global workload** (the same world count split across the cards, the
same minibatch stream by address, the same hyperparameters). Ratio = arm B's time ÷ arm A's time, at least ten
pairs with fresh seeds per pair (the same seed in both arms of a pair), interleaved; the paired lower bound is
judged against 1.25 (tolerance) and 1.6 (ambition and kill threshold). If the ceiling pilot predicted that
constant workload cannot reach the tolerance, the doubled-batch configuration is measured as a second, separately
labelled campaign; it is never substituted silently.

**Equal task quality, numbered.**
1. Both arms' times are read under the attainment rule: the return threshold must be held and confirmed by an
   evaluation pass, not merely crossed once.
2. Each arm's final policy is re-evaluated on the same evaluation battery under the deterministic replay profile.
3. Non-inferiority: the two-card arm's evaluation return is not below the one-card arm's by more than the margin
   δ fixed before the campaign (DESIGN default: the interquartile range of the one-card arm's evaluation return
   across its ten seeds). A pair that fails it counts as a two-card time of infinity for that pair, which voids
   the ratio for that pair and therefore the campaign: speed bought with quality is not scaling.
4. The margin, the battery identity and both returns are in the pairs record.

**One optimization pass.** After the first strong-scaling campaign, the transfer meter and the phase ledger of
the two-card arm name the costs (waiting on the other card, gradient-sum traffic, the single-stream optimizer,
duplicated compilation). Each funded lever goes through the optimization ledger (ceiling computed beforehand,
identical digests or an unchanged fidelity battery, paired lower bound at least 1.03), once, in descending order
of ceiling. Then the strong-scaling campaign is repeated; **the second campaign's bound is the one the kill gate
reads**.

**Records.**
- GATE-SPARK-20: weak bound against 1.8, strong bound against 1.25, pairs, both disclosure blocks per pair, the
  equal-quality figures, the gradient known answer (0.25 on real cards for every split) and its caught control.
- GATE-CORTEX-08, scaling clause: the same strong bound against 1.25; the parity clauses are cited from the
  learning unit's record.
- Kill-gate input: the post-pass strong bound against 1.6 with the three-way reading: below 1.25 (gate fails and
  the kill fires); 1.25 to below 1.6 (gate passes and the kill fires); at least 1.6 (gate passes, not fired).
- The ceiling pilot's prediction is scored against the measured bound.

**Budget.** Ten strong pairs cost about 10 × (410 + 2 × 325) device-seconds ≈ 2.9 device-hours at incumbent-like
times; two campaigns and the weak campaign fit the 12 device-hours of the gate-measurement class.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-20 is recorded as measured, pass or fail (seed done-condition): the weak-scaling paired lower bound against 1.8 and the strong-scaling paired lower bound at equal task quality against 1.25, each from at least ten pairs under complete disclosure blocks; the gradient known answer holds on the cards and the mean-of-shard-means control is caught.
- [ ] The scaling clause of GATE-CORTEX-08 is recorded from the same strong bound (seed done-condition), with the parity clauses cited from their own record.
- [ ] The deciding measurement for KILL-SHARD is on record (seed done-condition "KILL-SHARD decided"): the post-pass strong bound against 1.6 and its three-way reading, handed to the decision bead, whose ADR closes the kill gate.
- [ ] Equal task quality is shown per pair (attainment, re-evaluation, non-inferiority against the pre-fixed margin).
- [ ] The optimization pass is recorded lever by lever in the optimization ledger, and the pre- and post-pass bounds are both reported.
- [ ] Under the default compute envelope the gate is recorded `not run` with the reason and the kill gate's fail-closed default applies; nothing is estimated in its place.
- [ ] The ceiling pilot's prediction is scored.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::scaling`:
- `weak_ratio_is_sum_over_single`: cards at 1.9 M and 1.8 M against 2.0 M alone → 1.85.
- `strong_ratio_orientation`: one card 480 s, two cards 380 s → 1.263.
- `three_way_reading`: bounds 1.20, 1.25, 1.40, 1.60, 1.70 → {gate fail and kill fires; gate pass and kill fires; gate pass and kill fires; gate pass, not fired; gate pass, not fired} (1.6 itself is not "below 1.6").
- `non_inferiority`: one-card return 100 with margin 5: two-card 96 passes, 94 voids the pair.
- `void_pair_voids_campaign`.
- `kill_gate_reads_post_pass_bound`: pre-pass 1.3, post-pass 1.65 → not fired.
- `doubled_batch_is_separate_campaign`.
- `budget_estimate`: 10 × (410 + 650) s = 10,600 s = 2.94 device-hours.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_shard_scaling.sh` (N36-31) on `trj`: steps `weak`, `strong`, `quality`, `optimize`,
`strong_post`, `gate`. Logged per pair: arm order, seeds, each card's rate or each arm's time to attainment,
evaluation returns against the margin, transfer bytes and synchronization counts, host load, both block
identifiers; per campaign n, ℓ̄, s, t and the lower bound against its thresholds; the `summary` names
GATE-SPARK-20, GATE-CORTEX-08 and KILL-SHARD with each outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-813 (gradient 0.25 on one and two shards and with an empty shard; control: 0.5). CX-379 (two cards train twice as fast), CX-380 (mean of shard means).
- A/A control: a "two-card" arm that is the one-card job run twice must give a strong-scaling bound that does not clear 1.02.
- Negative controls registered against GATE-SPARK-20: a strong-scaling figure computed from the crossing time instead of attainment; a two-card arm with a doubled batch reported as constant workload (the disclosure blocks' world counts differ and the block diff rejects it); a ratio of means with no bound (`FROB-REF-863`).
- Determinism lanes: within an arm, a fixed layout repeats bit for bit; across arms the updates agree in class D3.

## Considerations

- RISK-46 (scaling across two cards or two machines vanishes under transfer cost, or under vision workloads; likelihood medium; early sign: strong scaling under 1.25 here). Mitigations are the kill gate and the independent lanes; only seeds, weights and receipts cross devices.
- OPEN-SPARK-2: the measurement needs two devices, which the default envelope forbids; the package's request is the gate-measurement class, and without it the gate is `not run`.
- The 1.25 tolerance is deliberately the incumbent's own figure: parity, not advantage. The 1.6 is an ambition on which nothing else in the plan depends; the division of labour between the machines already uses the two cards as independent lanes.
- Strong scaling of a 400-second job is dominated by fixed costs; a longer job (the humanoid training row on two cards) scales differently and is measured as its own row, not extrapolated from this one.
- BET-35 (the two-machine division of labour pays) is a different bet with its own gate; nothing here tests it.
