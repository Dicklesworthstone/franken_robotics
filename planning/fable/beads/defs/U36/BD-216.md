## Background

The performance program has three jobs: keep the engine inside a stated tolerance of the strongest incumbent
measured on the same hardware; measure in a way that cannot flatter us; and supply the phase ledger from which the
ten-fold test is computed. This bead is the second job made mechanical. It is the instrument through which every
speed figure of the project is produced from M2 on: the throughput rows, the training-time rows, the scaling rows,
the optimization pass and the estimator's calibration data all come out of it, and a figure that did not come out
of it cannot be cited as a Performance claim.

Its rules are the method of the program (Solid):

1. **Account for the whole workflow.** Transfers, synchronization, compilation, resets and telemetry stay in the
   denominator (FACT: the same rule is in the sibling simulation suite's accelerator doctrine).
2. **Paired, interleaved runs** on a quiet host, with a thermal soak on the Mac; `trj` is often heavily loaded
   (FACT), so its measurements record the load.
3. **Calibrate across sizes.** Every row is measured at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds and upward to capacity, giving a
   throughput-against-worlds curve. Rows that do not fit in memory stay in the table, marked.
4. **Timers cover completion, never enqueue alone** (INV-PERF-02). A run reports its latency distribution,
   env-steps/s, world-seconds per wall-second, completed episodes per second, valid steps, peak memory, and energy
   where available.
5. **Every report is checked**: unit ordering (INV-PERF-01), a decimation on every rate, a complete disclosure
   block, a paired lower bound on every ratio, a computed ceiling on every speed experiment.

The harness has no agent verb and no build-tool subcommand: it is a library and test targets of
`frob-conformance`, and it calls the product's verbs exactly as an agent would. Reason: a harness driven through
the surface it judges is a path to self-certification. Rejected alternative: a `frob perf` verb.

## Scope

In: the campaign runner; timers and the completion rule; the run report; the size sweep; report validation; the
block diff between arms; the GATE-SPARK-19 gate test and record. Out: the unit types (N36-03), the disclosure
schema (N36-13), the paired estimator (N36-06), the bound and ceiling record (N36-05), which this bead composes;
the step profile and funding screen (the Spark unit's profile bead); measuring the parity rows (BD-217, BD-218,
BD-199); the regression lane (N36-14); the optimization ledger (N36-07).

## Technical approach

**Module layout (`crates/frob-conformance/src/perf/`).** `campaign.rs` (declaration and runner), `timing.rs`
(intervals and the completion rule), `report.rs` (the record and its validation), `sweep.rs`, `blockdiff.rs`;
gate tests in `tests/g1.rs` under the names `GATE-SPARK-19::*`.

**Campaign declaration (a TOML file under `perf/campaigns/`).** Row of the target register, workload identity,
arms (ours by commit and placement request; a comparator by registry name), settings (matched or native), sizes,
duration and warm-up, pairs, seed, kind (`measurement`, `ratio`, `optimization`, `speed-experiment`). A campaign of
kind `optimization` or `speed-experiment` must reference a ceiling record filed before its first run, else
`FROB-REF-866`.

**Running one arm, numbered.**
1. Admission: build the demand and pass the compute-envelope check (`FROB-REF-761` otherwise; the row becomes
   `not run` with the reason).
2. Record host state (load; on the Mac, thermal state and the soak just completed).
3. Start the submission timer, then invoke the verb (`frob sim run --tier <t> --workload <W> --worlds <N>
   --seconds <T> --seed <s> --json`, or `frob train start …`, or the incumbent harness command).
4. Stop the timer at completion: the envelope has been received and the device's last chunk is committed.
5. Read the run's receipts and assemble the figures and the disclosure block from them, never from the request.

**Timing and INV-PERF-02.** An interval is (submission, completion). On a device, completion is the completion
event of the last launch or command buffer of the last chunk (the device-timed receipt), plus the readback of the
statistics ring; a timer stopped after the last launch was enqueued would count work the device has not done. The
harness asserts, for every timed interval, `timer_stop ≥ device_completion` and that the steps counted have a
commit epoch at or before the completed epoch. Two figures are recorded per arm: the **primary rate** = valid
steps ÷ (submission to completion), which includes compilation or cache load, admission, upload, graph capture,
warm-up, synchronization, transfers, resets, telemetry, fallback and verification; and the **steady-window rate**
= valid steps inside the declared measurement window ÷ its duration, which still includes synchronization,
transfers, resets and telemetry inside the window. Gates and ratios read the primary rate; the steady-window rate
and the compile and admission times are disclosed beside it so that fixed costs are visible and never hidden by a
long run.

**Run report (DESIGN name `frob.perf.report/1`).** Per arm and size: primary and steady-window rates with unit and
decimation; latency distribution of a step (median, 99th percentile, integer log-bin histogram); env-steps/s;
world-seconds per wall-second; completed episodes per second; valid, `Degraded` and `Faulted` step counts; peak
memory; energy or its named gap; the disclosure block identifier. Per campaign: the size curve; the pairs record
and bound for a ratio; the ceiling record for an experiment; validation results.

**Size sweep.** Sizes 1, 8, 64, 256, then 1,024, 4,096, 8,192 and doubling upward until the memory estimate no
longer fits the device budget. A size that does not fit is a row with `fits: false` and the estimate that
excluded it; it is never dropped. The curve is what calibrates the time model of the estimator.

**Report validation (`report::validate`), in order; the first failure rejects the report.**
1. Every step rate has unit, decimation, step and scope (else `FROB-REF-862`, pointer to the rate).
2. Unit ordering per (hardware, workload, world count, settings) group: in env-steps/s, training ≤ full step ≤
   physics only (rejected with pointer `rates.ordering`).
3. Every figure has a complete disclosure block (`FROB-REF-862`).
4. Every ratio has a paired lower bound from a campaign of at least ten pairs (`FROB-REF-863`).
5. Every campaign of kind `optimization` or `speed-experiment` has its ceiling, filed first and not below its goal
   (`FROB-REF-866`).
6. Block diff for before-and-after arms: workload identity, world count, step, decimation, iterations, capacities,
   sensors, scene, inclusion flags and determinism class must be equal; `mean_active_contacts`, `Degraded` and
   `Faulted` counts are watched, and a watched value that moves beyond its recorded run-to-run spread sets
   `semantics-moved`, which blocks the campaign from supporting an optimization.

**GATE-SPARK-19** (claim: Known answer; M2). The harness rejects a report that violates the unit ordering or omits
a decimation; the paired-bound known answer holds; of 100 A/A campaigns at most 9 clear a threshold of 1.02; a
figure without a complete disclosure block is refused. The gate test runs the four clauses and writes a receipt
naming the commit, the fingerprint digest and the seed.

**Evidence and determinism.** Figures are Performance claims of class `Tested`. The harness itself must not change
results: per-world digests of a harness-driven run equal those of the same run invoked by hand (class D2).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-19 is recorded as measured (seed done-condition): the ordering and omitted-decimation reports are rejected; ten pairs at ratio 1.5 give a bound of 1.5; the A/A count is at most 9 of 100, with the count recorded; a figure with an incomplete block is refused.
- [ ] Every report produced by the runner passes `validate`; each of the six validation rules has a planted report that fails exactly that rule.
- [ ] INV-PERF-02: with a planted backend shim that returns before device completion, the harness detects `timer_stop < device_completion` and rejects the run.
- [ ] The size sweep emits a point for every size including those that do not fit, marked.
- [ ] Both rates, the latency distribution, env-steps/s, world-seconds per wall-second, episodes per second, valid steps, peak memory and energy-or-gap are present in every report.
- [ ] The block diff flags a planted change of `mean_active_contacts` between arms as `semantics-moved`.
- [ ] Harness-driven and hand-invoked runs give identical per-world digests.
- [ ] Cancellation is tested: cancelling a campaign drains the running arm, leaks no device memory and records the arm's device time in the envelope meter.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-conformance` documents the module with its refusals (`FROB-REF-862`, `-863`, `-866`, `-761`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::`:
- `validate_ordering_violation` (KA-806): training 400 k env-steps/s at d = 10 beside physics 2.0 M world-steps/s → rejected; `validate_omitted_decimation` → `FROB-REF-862`.
- `validate_incomplete_block`; `validate_ratio_without_bound` → `FROB-REF-863`; `validate_experiment_without_ceiling` → `FROB-REF-866`.
- `timer_covers_completion`: a synthetic receipt with completion 5 ms after the timer stop → rejected.
- `primary_includes_fixed_costs`: 1,000,000 valid steps, 0.4 s of compile and admission, 10.0 s window → primary 96,154 steps/s, steady window 100,000 steps/s.
- `numerator_is_valid_steps_only`.
- `sweep_keeps_non_fitting_sizes`: sizes above the budget appear with `fits: false`.
- `blockdiff_fixed_fields`; `blockdiff_watched_fields`.
- `digest_unchanged_by_harness` (runs `batch-cpu` at 64 worlds twice, by hand and through the runner).
- `campaign_cancel_drains_and_meters`.
- Property test: any report accepted by `validate` is still accepted after a canonical serialization round trip.
- Property test (INV-PERF-02): for random sequences of enqueue and completion timestamps per chunk, the rate the harness reports never exceeds completed valid steps ÷ time to the last completion, and a timer stop placed before any completion is always rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_harness_gate19.sh` (N36-30) drives the real `frob` binary and the gate test target and writes
the structured log; this bead's code is what it exercises. The harness also emits its own NDJSON per arm (campaign,
pair, arm, size, command line, exit code, both rates with units, host state, block identifier) to the directory
the end-to-end helper names.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-806 (ordering), KA-807 (paired bound), KA-825 (the two refusals), KA-812 (ceiling refusal): each is a clause of the gate or a validation rule.
- CX-374 (env-steps and world-steps quoted side by side without a decimation).
- Negative controls registered against GATE-SPARK-19 before its passing run: the KA-806 report with the decimation omitted; a block with one field deleted; an estimator returning the mean; the early-returning timer shim. Each must be caught.
- Determinism lanes: the digest equality above; the A/A count on `batch-cpu` and on each device family.

## Considerations

- RISK-09: `trj` is loaded and compiles are slow; the runner records load per arm, interleaves arms, and the A/A count is the standing check that pairing still cancels the noise.
- RISK-45: an optimization that changes unrelated semantics shows as rising throughput while a count in the disclosure block moves; rule 6 of validation is the detector, together with digest identity in the optimization ledger.
- The primary rate being submission-to-completion is the plan's definition of a rate; short runs are therefore penalized by their fixed costs, on purpose. A row's duration is part of its campaign declaration and of its disclosure.
- OPEN-SPARK-2: under the default envelope a campaign uses one device for at most an hour; ten-pair campaigns of long rows may need the gate-measurement class, and are `not run` without it.
- A separate open question of the verification chapter asks whether 10⁴ scenarios per metamorphic relation per tier per release is affordable for referee relations; its owner is this program, and the answer comes from this harness's referee rate (target row 16) once measured.
