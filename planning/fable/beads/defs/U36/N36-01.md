## Background

**Position of the performance program.** Engine throughput is a parity requirement. It is not the source of the
project's ten-fold claim, and arithmetic on incumbent figures shows it cannot be (an infinitely fast environment
step speeds training by at most about 7.7 on one incumbent stack). The program has three jobs: keep the engine
inside a stated tolerance of the strongest incumbent measured on the same hardware; measure in a way that cannot
flatter us; and supply the phase ledger from which the ten-fold test is computed. The measurement rules are Solid.
The numbers are TARGETs: none is a measurement.

**Decisions (each with its reason and the rejected alternative).**

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-PERF-01 | Engine targets are ratios to incumbents measured on our own hardware; published figures give the planning values | Published incumbent figures exist for the reference tasks; an absolute target without a comparator hides a gap | Absolute targets chosen by us (such targets can sit below published incumbent figures without anyone noticing) |
| ADR-PERF-02 | Two units, world-steps and env-steps, each written with its decimation | An env-step count means nothing without the number of physics steps inside it | One unit "steps/s" |
| ADR-PERF-03 | Every target is a tolerance or an ambition and says which | A reader must know which numbers the plan depends on | One number per row with no status |
| ADR-PERF-04 | A ratio is admitted on a paired lower bound, never on a point estimate | A mean of noisy runs on a loaded host flatters whoever ran last | Means, best-of-n |
| ADR-PERF-05 | Ceiling first: no speed experiment without its computed ceiling | A recorded pilot ran a speed test whose ceiling (about 1.46) was computable beforehand and below its goal | Measure first, explain later |

A **tolerance** is a parity band: the plan's argument holds anywhere inside it, and leaving it has a named
consequence (the parity kill gate, a recorded defect, or a failed gate). An **ambition** is a better number that
we intend to reach and on which nothing else in the plan depends. Where an incumbent can be run on our hardware
the tolerance is a ratio to that measurement and the absolute figure beside it is the planning value implied by
the published number. Incumbent references are FACTs from the source register, each to be re-read from its
primary source before a gate cites it.

**Why 0.67.** The tolerance ratio for physics rows is 0.67: a step at most 1.5 times slower than the incumbent's.
The environment step is between 0.29 and 0.87 of training wall-clock on incumbent stacks, so such a step
lengthens training by a factor between 1.15 and 1.44 (1 + 0.5·share), which the plan's other levers must absorb
and which the ten-fold test charges in full. Beyond that the engine is a liability.

This bead turns the table into a registry so that no gate, script or report restates a threshold by hand.

## Scope

In: the 22 rows as data, the row schema, the loader and lint, the helpers gates call, the three consistency notes
as tests. Out: measuring any row (the carrier beads named per row); the workloads (N36-02); the decisions that may
change the numbers (N36-24, N36-26).

## Technical approach

`interim_floor` (optional), `ambition`, `planning_value`, `incumbent_reference` (text, source mark, status
`to-re-read`), `gated_in`, `carrier`.

**Helpers.** `threshold(row, measured_incumbent) -> Threshold` returns the binding number with its status
(`tolerance`, `interim-floor`, `reported-only`); `judge(row, bound) -> Verdict` compares a paired lower bound (or
an upper bound for `at_most` rows) with it; `planning_value(row)`. A gate that reads a row by number receives the
status with the number, so "interim floor" can never be recorded as "tolerance".

**Three consistency notes, as lints.** (1) The planning values of rows 1 to 3 are mutually consistent in one unit
by construction: 400 k, 340 k and 280 k env-steps/s. (2) Row 13's planning value is half of an incumbent CPU rate
that was measured on a loaded machine and is therefore a lower bound on the incumbent. (3) Every ratio in the
table is a paired lower bound, not a mean: a row of kind `ratio_*` cannot be judged with a point estimate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/perf_targets.toml` holds 22 rows with every column of the tables, each row naming its carrier bead and gate; the registry lint passes.
- [ ] Each row is marked `tolerance` or `ambition` per number; a number with no status fails the lint (ADR-PERF-03).
- [ ] Row 1 returns the interim floor 2.0 M with status `interim-floor` when no incumbent measurement is on record, and 0.67 × the measured incumbent rate with status `tolerance` when one is.
- [ ] The consistency lints hold: 0.85 × 400 k = 340 k; 0.70 × 400 k = 280 k; 0.75 × 200 k = 150 k; 0.60 × 200 k = 120 k; 1.25 × 410 s = 512.5 s, printed 513 s; 0.5 × 550 k = 275 k; 0.67 × 4.0 M = 2.68 M; 0.67 × 2.55 M = 1.71 M; 0.67 × 1.68 M = 1.13 M.
- [ ] `judge` refuses a point estimate for a ratio row (`FROB-REF-863`).
- [ ] Every incumbent reference carries `status = "to-re-read"` until the parity baseline replaces it with a measured value and its disclosure block identifier.
- [ ] The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-PERF-01..05.md` exists with each decision, its reason and the rejected alternative.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::targets`:
- `rows_present_1_to_22`; `every_number_has_status`.
- `row1_interim_floor_then_tolerance`: no measurement → (2.0 M, `interim-floor`); incumbent 3.6 M measured → (2.412 M, `tolerance`).
- `planning_values_consistent`: the nine products listed in the acceptance criteria.
- `row21_derived`: 2.0 M × 0.70 ÷ 80,000 = 17.5, printed 17; 4.0 M × 0.86 ÷ 80,000 = 43.
- `row5_reference_ratio`: 410 ÷ 325 = 1.26.
- `row2_overhead`: 1 − 347/1,200 = 0.711.
- `ratio_row_refuses_point_estimate`.
- `at_most_rows_judge_upper_bound`: row 4 with an upper bound of 1.20 passes, 1.30 fails.
- `unknown_row_refused`; `row_without_carrier_fails_lint`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_parity_baseline.sh` (N36-29) reads the incumbent references from this file and logs, for each,
the published value, the measured value and their ratio against the 20% reproduction band. The M2 scripts read
thresholds through `threshold` and log the status beside each number.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The arithmetic of the table (exact products above).
- Negative control: a scratch registry in which row 1's tolerance is recorded as 2.0 M with status `tolerance` must fail the lint (the interim floor is not the tolerance).
- Negative control: a row whose unit is world-steps/s with no decimation fails the loader.

## Considerations

- OPEN-PERF-1 (the tolerance 0.67 and the band below it) may change rows 1, 7 and 8 only by a dated amendment made before the measurement it governs (N36-24). OPEN-PERF-4: if the physics step moves from 2 ms every row is restated with its new decimation before any gate cites it (N36-26).
- The published references are on different cards (an RTX 5090, an RTX 6000 Ada, an A100) from ours; the parity baseline re-measures each on `trj` and the Mac, and the measured value replaces the planning value in this file.
- Pre-mortem entry: "throughput landed far under the parity band and agents stopped waiting"; early sign: a paired lower bound under 0.67 of the incumbent at the M2 profile. RISK-30 (throughput or training time below the parity band, likelihood high).
- Row 12 is the falsifier of BET-09; its kill gate belongs to the Metal backend's decision bead.
