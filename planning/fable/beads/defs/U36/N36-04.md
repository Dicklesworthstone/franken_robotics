## Background

The performance program rests on one piece of arithmetic: **no single phase of a quadruped design loop can deliver
a ten-fold speedup**, so engine throughput is a parity requirement and not the product's advantage. The argument is
Amdahl's law applied to published figures of incumbent stacks. Each statement below is arithmetic on a FACT from
the source register; none is a measurement of ours, and each FACT is re-read from its primary source, and then
replaced by a measurement on our own hardware, before a gate cites it.

The seven limits, with their inputs:

1. **An infinitely fast environment step.** On Isaac Lab with one RTX 4090 the environment step is 87% of
   training wall-clock for a humanoid rough-terrain task (94 k, 88 k and 82 k env-steps/s for step, step with
   inference, and step with inference and training, nested), so removing it entirely speeds training by at most
   1/(1 − 0.87) ≈ 7.7; for a dexterous hand 6.7 (share 85%); for a cart-pole 1.9 (share 46%).
2. **An infinitely fast physics kernel.** On mjlab with MuJoCo Warp physics is about 29% of the environment step
   (347 k against 1.20 M env-steps/s), so a faster physics kernel alone gains at most 1.4.
3. **Inference and learning.** On Isaac Lab they are about 13% of training wall-clock, so removing both gains at
   most 1.15.
4. **The design cycle.** A cycle of 120 s with no training step, against an incumbent quadruped cycle of 360 to
   420 s (300 s of training plus an agent turn of 60 to 120 s), is 3.0 to 3.5. Against a 30-minute humanoid run it
   is about 15. Both hold only if a baseline controller can stand in for a trained policy, which is untested
   (HYPOTHESIS; settled by the rank-agreement experiment between evaluation rungs).
5. **Compute per decision.** With an incumbent cost of about 410 device-seconds per trained candidate, a screening
   cost c_s per design, a fraction f of designs that go on to training and a training cost c_t of our own, the
   ratio is 410/(c_s + f·c_t). Ten-fold needs c_s + f·c_t ≤ 41 device-seconds. At c_t = 410: f ≤ 7.6% if c_s = 10,
   f ≤ 0.7% if c_s = 38, and it is impossible if c_s ≥ 41.
6. **Agent latency.** If an agent takes L = 60 to 120 s per turn and the incumbent trains for about 300 s per
   turn (a round planning value just under the published 325 s on two RTX 4090), deciding one design per turn
   gains at most (300 + L)/L, between 3.5 and 6, with zero compute on our side.
7. **Deleting training.** For the composite quadruped iteration of about 5.9 minutes (354 s), with compilation
   inside the published training time, deleting training and compilation altogether gives 6.6.

Conclusion: on quadruped briefs no single phase reaches ten-fold: not the step, not the cycle, not compute per
decision unless few designs are trained and screening is cheap. The factors the plan bets on beyond these (designs
decided per agent turn, fewer turns to a qualified design, a cheaper training rung through family policies and
inheritance) are tested elsewhere; this chapter's duty to them is parity and honest measurement.

This bead makes the argument executable, so that the day the incumbent is measured on `trj` the seven numbers are
recomputed from measured shares instead of published ones, and the conclusion is either confirmed or changed in
writing. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: keeping the arithmetic as prose (the published composite joins figures
from different stacks and is expected to move when measured; RISK-25 is exactly that the denominator is wrong).

## Scope

In: the seven limits of the performance chapter as an executable report: the three functions that only this
chapter has (`step_share` and `physics_share`, which read a share from nested rates, and `slowdown_penalty`,
used to justify the 0.67 tolerance); six unit-typed adapters over the thesis calculators; the published-input
fixtures; the report section `limits` computed from a ledger, with its published and measured columns, the
`unmeasured` rule and the flag `conclusion_changed`; the rounding rule; the known answers KA-818 to KA-820 and
the control that nested shares do not multiply.

Out, with the bead that has it:

- the arithmetic of the formulas this chapter shares with the thesis (1/(1 − s), the cycle ratio, the cap per
  agent turn, compute per decision, the largest trained fraction, total ÷ untouched), in exact rationals with
  their domain checks and the known answers KA-009 to KA-016: N00-17, module `frob_conformance::thesis`, the
  one implementation in the workspace. This bead calls it and holds no copy;
- the six ceilings of the ten-fold gate per cell, from ledger-closed runs of the rival, with the ceiling record
  and the binding ceiling: N39a-37 (M1; it reads `frob.tenfold.ledger/1` records, this bead reads the parity
  baseline's phase rows at M0);
- the general speedup bound with overhead and the ceiling-first refusal (N36-05); measuring the ledger (BD-040,
  BD-142, BD-143); the ten-fold decision rule; the worked card of three of these ceilings (GB-09).

## Technical approach

Module `frob_conformance::perf::amdahl` (pure host arithmetic; inputs and outputs carry units through the
`frob-core` quantity types; every function returns its inputs with the result so a report shows what it used).

DESIGN: one implementation of each formula. Six of the nine functions this chapter names state formulas that
the thesis module of N00-17 already holds as exact-rational calculators, and that module's acceptance requires
it to be their only implementation in the workspace. Those six are therefore adapters: an adapter converts its
unit-typed arguments to exact rationals, calls the named calculator, and returns the result with its inputs; it
contains no formula and no domain check of its own (the calculator's refusal is passed through with the
adapter's name added). Reason: an earlier draft of this bead carried its own copies under other names and
another argument order (`compute_per_decision(C, c_s, f, c_t)` against the thesis module's `(C, c, c_T, f)`),
and two copies of one formula drift.

Own functions (formula held here):

| Function | Formula | Symbols |
|---|---|---|
| `step_share(r_step, r_train)` | s = (1/r_step) ÷ (1/r_train) = r_train/r_step | r in env-steps/s for the nested loops; from 94 k and 82 k: 82/94 = 41/47 = 0.872 |
| `physics_share(r_full, r_phys)` | r_full/r_phys | 347 k/1.20 M = 347/1200 = 0.289 |
| `slowdown_penalty(s, ρ)` | 1 + s·(1/ρ − 1) | training lengthening when a phase of share s runs at ratio ρ of the incumbent's speed |

Adapters (formula held by N00-17):

| Adapter | Calls | Formula | Symbols |
|---|---|---|---|
| `remove_phase(s)` | `thesis::step_share_ceiling(s)` | 1/(1 − s) | s the share of the phase removed, 0 ≤ s < 1 |
| `cycle_ratio(T_inc, T_ours)` | `thesis::cycle_ratio(T_inc, T_ours)` | T_inc/T_ours | seconds per design cycle |
| `compute_per_decision(C, c_s, c_t, f)` | `thesis::compute_per_decision(C, c_s, c_t, f)` | C/(c_s + f·c_t) | C incumbent device-seconds per trained candidate; c_s screening cost per design; c_t our training cost; f fraction trained. Argument order is the thesis module's |
| `max_trained_fraction(C, c_s, c_t, goal)` | `thesis::max_trained_fraction(C, c_s, c_t, goal)` | (C/goal − c_s)/c_t; `None` (printed `impossible`) if c_s > C/goal; exactly 0 if c_s = C/goal | goal the ratio wanted |
| `agent_latency_cap(T_train, L)` | `thesis::turn_cap(T_train, L)` | (T_train + L)/L | L agent seconds per turn |
| `delete_phases(T_total, T_removed)` | `thesis::residual_ceiling(T_total, T_total − T_removed)` | T_total/(T_total − T_removed) | 354/(354 − 300) |

The boundary of `max_trained_fraction` (DESIGN, the same in N00-17). The plan states it twice and the two differ
at c_s = C/goal exactly: KA-819 says "impossible if c_s ≥ 41", KA-013 says no f reaches ten if the cheap rung
costs *more than* 41. The formula's own condition, c_s + f·c_t ≤ 41, admits f = 0 at c_s = 41, where the ratio is
exactly ten with nothing trained. The function follows the formula: 0 at c_s = 41, `None` above it. The report
prints the zero with the note "only with no design trained", which is KA-819's "impossible" for any loop that
trains at all.

**Report section.** `limits_from_ledger(ledger) -> LimitsReport`: reads the measured phase rows (compile, train
with the learner's share, the step and full-step rates, agent turn time where an agent ran) and returns the seven
limits with, for each, the published-input value, the measured-input value, and the flag `conclusion_changed` if a
single-phase limit reaches 10 on measured inputs. A limit whose inputs were not measured prints `unmeasured` and
keeps the published value labelled as such; it never silently substitutes.

**Rounding rule.** Limits are computed as exact rationals from unrounded shares and printed to two significant
figures, and the report prints both: 1/(1 − 0.87) = 100/13 = 7.69 (printed 7.7), while the unrounded share
82/94 gives 94/12 = 47/6 = 7.83. The difference is a recorded erratum of the plan and is the reason
`step_share` takes rates, not a rounded share.

**Evidence.** Outputs computed from published inputs are class `Estimated` with the label `published-context`;
outputs computed from a `ledger-closed` measurement are class `Tested`. Recorded with the parity-baseline report.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The nine functions exist with unit-typed arguments and return their inputs alongside the result: three
      hold their formula here, six are adapters over `frob_conformance::thesis` (N00-17), and a source scan of
      `perf/amdahl.rs` finds no `1/(1 − s)`, no `(T + L)/L` and no `C/(c_s + f·c_t)` written out.
- [ ] KA-818: 1/(1 − 0.87) = 7.7; physics share 0.29 → at most 1.4; inference and learning 0.13 → at most 1.15; a step 1.5 times slower lengthens training by 1.15 to 1.44 for a step share of 0.29 to 0.87.
- [ ] KA-819: ten-fold needs c_s + f·c_t ≤ 41 device-seconds; at c_t = 410, f ≤ 7.6% for c_s = 10 and f ≤ 0.7% for c_s = 38; at c_s = 41 the admissible fraction is 0 (printed with "only with no design trained"); `None`, printed `impossible`, for c_s > 41.
- [ ] KA-820: 360/120 = 3.0 and 420/120 = 3.5; 1,800/120 = 15; (300 + 60)/60 = 6 and (300 + 120)/120 = 3.5; 354/54 = 6.6.
- [ ] `limits_from_ledger` on the published fixture reproduces the seven statements; on a ledger with one phase missing it prints `unmeasured` for the limits that need it.
- [ ] The parity-baseline report (BD-040) contains the `limits` section with published and measured columns.
- [ ] No function accepts a share outside [0, 1) or a rate without its decimation.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::amdahl` (values are exact rationals; the decimals are what the report
prints):
- `step_share_from_nested_rates`: (94,000; 82,000) → 41/47 = 0.8723; `remove_phase` → 47/6 = 7.83; with
  s = 87/100 → 100/13 = 7.69.
- `hand_and_cartpole`: s = 0.85 → 20/3 = 6.67; s = 0.46 → 50/27 = 1.85 (printed 1.9).
- `physics_share`: 347,000/1,200,000 = 347/1200 = 0.2892; `remove_phase` → 1200/853 = 1.407.
- `learner_share`: 1 − 41/47 = 6/47 = 0.1277; `remove_phase(13/100)` → 100/87 = 1.149.
- `slowdown_penalty_band`: (0.29, 1/1.5) → 1.145; (0.87, 1/1.5) → 1.435; (0.87, 0.33) → 2.77.
- `cycle_ratios`: 3.0, 3.5, 15.0.
- `compute_per_decision_cases`, arguments (C, c_s, c_t, f): (410, 10, 410, 31/410) → 10 exactly;
  `max_trained_fraction(410, 10, 410, 10)` = 31/410 = 0.0756; (410, 38, 410, 10) → 3/410 = 0.0073; c_s = 41 → 0
  (the ratio at f = 0 is exactly 10); c_s = 42 → `None`; f = 0 at c_s = 38 → 205/19 = 10.8.
- `agent_latency_cap`: L = 60 → 6.0; L = 120 → 3.5; against 410 s: 47/6 = 7.83 and 53/12 = 4.42.
- `delete_training`: 354/(354 − 300) = 59/9 = 6.556.
- `share_out_of_range_refused`: s = 1.0 and s = −0.1 (the refusal comes from the thesis calculator and carries
  the adapter's name).
- `adapters_agree_with_thesis`: for each of the six adapters, on its fixture inputs, the result equals the
  direct call of the named `thesis` function, bit for bit as a rational.
- `limits_from_ledger_published_fixture` (fixture labelled `synthetic`, built from the published inputs).
- `unmeasured_is_printed_not_substituted`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_parity_baseline.sh` (N36-29), step `limits`: after the incumbent rows are recorded, runs the
calculator on the recorded ledger and logs one `metric` line per limit with `published`, `measured`, the inputs
with units, and `conclusion_changed`. The script asserts only that every limit is either computed or `unmeasured`;
it does not assert the values, which are findings.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-818, KA-819, KA-820 with the values above (tolerance: exact arithmetic on the stated inputs, compared to the printed precision).
- CX-375 ("a faster environment step delivers the ten-fold"): the test `no_single_phase_reaches_ten` asserts that on the published fixture every single-phase limit is below 10.
- Negative control: a calculator that multiplies the step limit by the physics limit (7.7 × 1.4) as if they were independent factors must be rejected by `nested_shares_do_not_multiply` (physics is inside the step: removing both is still at most 7.7).
- Metamorphic: scaling every rate by a constant leaves every limit unchanged.

## Considerations

- RISK-25 (the denominator of the ten-fold ratio is wrong: incumbents are faster, or their loop is shorter, than the published composite; likelihood high). This calculator is how the correction is absorbed: the measured ledger replaces the fixture and the report shows which conclusions moved.
- Pre-mortem entry: "the ten-fold claim failed on quadruped missions because incumbents already train in minutes"; the quadruped ceiling cannot be engineered away, and the class may leave the claim. The M0 stop rule (rewrite the thesis if the measured ledger puts every ceiling under 3) reads this report.
- Limit 4 depends on a HYPOTHESIS (a baseline controller can rank designs like a trained policy); the report prints it with that label.
- The functions are a serial cost model; overlap between phases is handled by the exclusive attribution of the ledger, not here.
- Three outputs print ceilings from a ledger and must not be confused: this bead's `limits` section (seven limits of the performance chapter, at the parity baseline, M0); the thesis report of N00-17 (nine ceilings on one flat ledger); the per-cell ceiling record of the ten-fold gate (N39a-37, M1). All three evaluate their formulas with the functions of N00-17, so a value that appears in two of them is the same rational.
