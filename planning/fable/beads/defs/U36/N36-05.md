## Background

**ADR-PERF-05: ceiling first.** No optimization and no speed experiment is funded before the largest ratio it could
show has been computed from the current profile. The reason is a recorded failure. FACT (the plan-time review
log): a specialized solver was tested against a composed one and missed its speed gate at 1.345 times warm and
1.088 times cold; a later measurement showed that the specialized batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the
solver does not touch (parsing, assembly, checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver
of zero cost would have reached only about 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7). The pilot's figures: specialized full batch
610.1 µs, of which the specialized solve is 34.4 µs; composed full batch 842.8 µs; untouched residual
610.1 − 34.4 = 575.7 µs. The ceiling was computable before the experiment ran, and nobody computed it. Rejected
alternative: run the experiment, then interpret it.

**The bound.** If a fraction f of the strong baseline's matched wall time is improved by a factor r, and the
method adds overhead h as a fraction of that baseline time, then

    speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

"Matched" means the same completed work on the strongest baseline's critical path. As r → ∞ the bound tends to
1/(1 − f + h). Worked values: infinite acceleration of 85% of the time gives at most 6.7; f = 0.97, r = 40 and
h = 0.02 give about 13.5; with h = 0.02 no r reaches ten-fold unless f ≥ 0.92.

**Applied to a family batch** (many different designs in one batch). A generation of 256 designs at 16 worlds and
10 s each is 20.5 M world-steps, about 10 s of simulation at 2.0 M world-steps/s. Run design by design, it also
pays 256 switches (compile or upload, graph capture). What a mixed batch removes is the switching; what it adds is
padding, here taken as 30% of the simulation time. If a switch costs 1 s, f = 256/266 = 0.96 and the bound is
266/13 ≈ 20. If a switch costs 0.1 s, f = 0.72 and the bound is 35.6/13 ≈ 2.7. The gain of family batching
therefore depends almost entirely on the incumbent's cost of switching designs, which is unpublished (HYPOTHESIS
for both values) and is measured in the incumbent parity baseline before the mixed-batch experiment is funded.

Tier: Solid.

## Technical approach

**Functions (module `frob_conformance::perf::bound`).** DESIGN, one implementation of each formula: the plan
does not say where the bound's code lives, and the thesis module of N00-17 (M0, the same crate) already holds it
as an exact-rational calculator that its acceptance declares the only implementation in the workspace, called by
the doctrine check D-10 and by the ten-fold gate's ceilings. A second copy here (an earlier draft of this bead
put one in `frob_core::perf`, in `f64`) is how two ceilings of one experiment come to differ in the last digit.
Every caller of a ceiling is in `frob-conformance` (the harness, the funding screen over the step profile, the
method-experiment statistics, the pilot register), so nothing needs the arithmetic below that crate.

1. `ceiling(f, r, h) -> Ceiling`: an adapter. It takes unit-free shares as exact rationals (0 ≤ f ≤ 1, r ≥ 1 or
   `Infinite`, h ≥ 0), calls `thesis::general_bound(f, r, h)` = 1/(1 − f + f/r + h), and returns the rational with
   an `f64` view for printing, the unremovable cost u = 1 − f + h and the inputs. Invalid ranges are refused by
   the calculator, never clamped; the refusal is passed through with the adapter's name added. The one input at
   which the bound has no finite value (f = 1, r infinite, h = 0) is returned as `Unbounded` without calling the
   calculator. With r infinite the bound is 1/(1 − f + h), the same number as the baseline's total time divided
   by the time the mechanism leaves untouched (T_base ÷ T_untouched, `thesis::residual_ceiling`); the phase-table
   wrapper of N38a-08 computes f and h in that form and calls this function.
2. `min_fraction_for(goal, h) = 1 + h − 1/goal`: the smallest f at which any r reaches `goal` (0.92 for goal 10
   and h = 0.02).
3. `family_batch_bound(designs, worlds, seconds, h_step, rate, switch_cost, padding) -> FamilyBound`: world-steps
   = designs × worlds × seconds/h_step; simulation time t_sim = world-steps/rate; baseline = designs × switch_cost
   + t_sim; f = designs × switch_cost/baseline; mixed time = t_sim·(1 + padding); bound = baseline/mixed time.
   In the bound's own terms this is r = ∞ on the switching fraction and h = padding·t_sim/baseline, and the
   function computes it exactly that way, as `ceiling(f, ∞, h)`; it returns the mixed time beside it as a check
   (the two agree exactly in rationals).

**Three rules for using the bound (DESIGN), each enforced by a check on the ceiling record.**

1. f, r and h are measured on the strong baseline's critical path: a comparator that also caches graphs, factors
   and compiled kernels. The record must name the baseline's profile (a `ledger-closed` phase ledger or a step
   profile) and its cache state `warm`; a ceiling computed on a cold or uncached baseline is refused. The
   record's `baseline.fingerprint` must be the fingerprint identity of the machine the experiment will run on: a
   record with no baseline profile, or with a profile taken under another fingerprint (a driver or capacity
   change makes a profile stale), has no ceiling "from the current profile" and is refused in the same way.
2. A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a device speedup that was measured on work already removed. The smallest
   case: 100 candidates of 10 s each; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors are the hard ones and accelerate by 2,
   not by the 5 measured on the full set. The result is 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50. A record that composes two
   factors must state, for the second, the population it was measured on; the check turns the record's `factors`
   into the levers of `thesis::compose` (N00-17), whose order rule refuses a factor measured on work an earlier
   lever removes, and maps that refusal to the flag `invalid-composition` on the record; a correctly ordered
   composition returns the calculator's value (20).
3. Queue time for proofs and transfers counts toward decision time: the baseline and candidate times in a record
   include waiting, and a record with a zero `wait` phase must say that waiting was measured as zero.

**The ceiling record (DESIGN name `frob.perf.ceiling/1`).**

```json
{ "schema": "frob.perf.ceiling/1", "experiment": "…", "goal": "2.0",
  "baseline": {"profile": "b3:…", "cache": "warm", "fingerprint": "b3:…"},
  "f": "0.317", "r": "inf", "h": "0.0", "ceiling": "1.464",
  "factors": [ {"name": "…", "value": "…", "measured_on": "…"} ],
  "computed_at": "…", "before_experiment": true }
```

**The refusal.** `FROB-REF-866`: "a speed experiment requested without its computed ceiling, or with a ceiling
below its goal". Exit code 3; `retry: after-change`; pointer `ceiling` (absent) or `goal`. Ranked repairs, as
the registry row of N35-REF orders them (quoted, not restated; a test reads them from the row):
(1) compute the ceiling from the current profile; (2) lower the goal to at most the ceiling; (3) choose a target
that holds a larger share of the baseline; (4) fund the work for a reason other than speed, in writing, with no
speed figure. It is raised by `admit_experiment(record)` in the harness whenever a campaign is declared as a
speed experiment or an optimization, and by the pilot register's lint for any pilot whose falsifier is a speed
ratio. `admit_experiment` is the only place the code is raised: the funding screen over the step profile
(N21a-27) and the optimization ledger (N36-07) build a record and call it; they carry no second copy of the
comparison of ceiling with goal and no second list of repairs.

**Evidence.** A ceiling is class `Estimated` when its inputs are hypotheses and `Tested` when they come from a
recorded profile. The record is stored in the ledger before the experiment's first run; `before_experiment` is
verified by comparing ledger order, not a self-declared flag.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-809: `ceiling(0.97, 40, 0.02)` = 13.5 (13.47); `ceiling(0.85, ∞, 0)` = 6.7; `min_fraction_for(10, 0.02)` = 0.92.
- [ ] KA-810: the family batch of 256 designs × 16 worlds × 10 s at h = 2 ms is 20,480,000 world-steps (printed 20.5 M), 10.24 s at 2.0 M world-steps/s; switch 1 s: f = 0.96, bound ≈ 20; switch 0.1 s: f = 0.72, bound ≈ 2.7.
- [ ] KA-811: the pruning case returns 20 and flags the product 10 × 5 = 50 as `invalid-composition`.
- [ ] KA-812: an experiment with no ceiling record, and one with ceiling 1.46 and goal 2.0, are both refused with `FROB-REF-866`; the recorded pilot's numbers (842.8 ÷ 575.7) reproduce the ceiling 1.46.
- [ ] One implementation: `ceiling` returns the rational of `thesis::general_bound` bit for bit, the composition
      check returns or refuses exactly as `thesis::compose` does, and the source scan of N00-17 finds no
      `1/(1 − f + f/r + h)` written out in `perf/bound.rs` or anywhere in `frob-core`.
- [ ] A ceiling computed against a cold or uncached baseline is refused (rule 1); so is a record with no
      baseline profile, and one whose baseline fingerprint is not the machine's.
- [ ] A record filed after the experiment's first run is rejected (`before_experiment` verified from ledger order).
- [ ] The parity-baseline report carries the family-batch bound computed from the measured incumbent switch cost, labelled HYPOTHESIS until that cost is measured.
- [ ] The four plants this bead builds for N36-CX exist under that registry's identifiers (`pruning-times-speedup`, `ceiling-recorded-after`, `phase-ceiling-below-goal`, `family-batch-no-switch-cost`): each mutation makes its named test fail and each planted input is refused with `FROB-REF-866`.

## Considerations

- The same bound appears in the body-family chapter (design-family lanes), in the program-level ceilings, in the funding screen over the M2 step profile (N21a-27) and in the method-experiment statistics (N38a-08); there is one implementation of the arithmetic, `thesis::general_bound` (N00-17), and one entry point for speed experiments, this bead's `ceiling` with its record and refusal, which the funding screen and the method-experiment statistics call. Its path is `frob_conformance::perf::bound::ceiling`, the name those two beads and the register of method experiments (N38a-07) use (all three are in `frob-conformance`).
- The bound is a serial cost model. Where phases overlap (background work during an agent turn) the exclusive attribution of the phase ledger decides which phase owns an instant before f is computed.
- A ceiling below the goal is not a failure of the idea: the work may still be funded for trust, coverage or correctness, with that reason written and no speed figure.
- The incumbent's design-switch cost is the single most valuable unpublished number for the family-batch argument; it is measured at M0 for a parameter edit and a topology edit.
