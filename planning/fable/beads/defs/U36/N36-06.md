## Background

**ADR-PERF-04.** A performance ratio (ours against an incumbent, after against before, two devices against one) is
admitted only as the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of a paired ratio, never on a point estimate. Reasons:
both machines are noisy in ways that favour whoever ran at the quiet moment (`trj`, the Linux host with two RTX
4090, is often heavily loaded; the Mac throttles with temperature); a mean of unpaired runs mixes that noise into
the ratio; and a best-of-n figure is a selection, not a measurement. Pairing two arms back to back on the same
host state cancels the common noise; taking the lower bound makes the remaining uncertainty count against us.
A gate threshold applies to the bound, never to the mean. A ratio stated without one is refused
(`FROB-REF-863`).

Rejected alternatives: the mean of ratios or the ratio of means; the median with no interval; a two-sided
interval (we only ever need to show "at least"); a bootstrap interval (more machinery for ten pairs, and no
closed-form known answer to test it with).

Every ratio row of the target table, every optimization, the scaling rows on two cards, the mixed-batch row and
the fleet's campaign speedup use this one estimator. Tier: Solid.

The estimator is written once for the whole program, by the experiment-statistics bead of the Gauntlet
(N38a-08, M1), because the method-experiment register needs the same bound a milestone earlier and has a proof
obligation on its exactness (PO-GAUNT-01). This bead is the performance harness built on it: what a campaign of
pairs is, how it is run and recorded, the type that makes a ratio without a bound unrepresentable, the refusal,
and the calibration of a machine.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**The rule.**
1. At least ten pairs. The two arms of a pair run back to back, in randomized order, on the same host state, on
   the same workload and the same world addresses.
2. With ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ) for pair i, mean ℓ̄ = (1/n)·Σℓᵢ and sample standard deviation
   s = √(Σ(ℓᵢ − ℓ̄)²/(n − 1)), the bound is **L = exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n)**, where t is the 95th percentile of Student's
   distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10; 1.729 for n = 20; 1.699 for n = 30). The
   harness does not compute this itself: it hands the campaign's pairs to `frob_core::perf::paired_lower_bound`
   (N38a-08) and receives a `PairedBound`. The quantile is that bead's `student_t95(n − 1)`, computed for the
   exact degrees of freedom and rounded upward (DESIGN, one rule for both beads: an earlier draft of the
   Gauntlet's library looked t up in a table at the next smaller tabulated degrees of freedom, 1.725 for 23
   pairs where the exact value is 1.717; the plan's rule is the percentile at n − 1, so the same campaign has
   one bound whoever computes it).
3. The incumbent arm is its strongest warm or cached configuration, frozen after tuning on development fixtures
   only.
4. Compilation, admission, synchronization, transfer, fallback, refusal and verification are all in the
   denominator: each arm's figure is completed valid work over wall-clock from submission to completion.
5. On the Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement; on `trj` the host load is recorded for every arm.

**Orientation.** a is always the arm whose advantage is being claimed and the ratio is oriented so that larger
is better: for rates, a/b = our rate ÷ the comparator's rate; for times, a/b = the comparator's time ÷ our time.
A target written "at most 1.25 times the incumbent's time" is judged as L ≥ 1/1.25 = 0.80. There is no upper-bound
variant to choose from, so the orientation cannot be picked after the fact.

**Protocol details (DESIGN).**
- Arm order within pair i is drawn from a counter-based stream keyed by (campaign seed, i), so a campaign can be
  re-run in the same order and audited.
- "Same host state": no other job is started by the harness between the two arms of a pair; host load (and on the
  Mac the thermal state) is sampled before each arm and stored. A pair whose two samples differ by more than a
  declared limit (DESIGN default: load average differing by more than 2.0, or a thermal state change) is kept and
  flagged; it is never dropped.
- **No pair is discarded.** If either arm fails to complete, the campaign is void and is reported with the
  failure; it restarts from the first pair once the cause is fixed. A ratio over the pairs that happened to work
  is the survivorship error the rule exists to prevent. A fallback or a refusal inside an arm is not a failure of
  the campaign: its time counts and is disclosed.
- Every arm passes the compute-envelope admission check before it starts.

**Types.** In `frob_core::perf`: `PerfRatio`, whose only constructor takes a `PairedBound`, so any crate can
state a ratio only through the estimator. `PairedBound { n, mean_ln, sd_ln, t, lower_bound, level: 0.95,
campaign: Option<CampaignId> }`, the pure function `paired_lower_bound(pairs) -> Result<PairedBound, PairError>`
and the Student quantile are N38a-08's and live in the same module tree (`frob_core::perf::paired`); this bead
adds `PerfRatio::from_bound(bound: PairedBound) -> Result<PerfRatio, PerfRatioError>` (DESIGN), which refuses a
bound whose `campaign` is absent or names a void campaign: the estimator may be called on any ten pairs (the
Gauntlet calls it on experiment arms), and only a bound that came out of a recorded campaign of this protocol
may become a performance ratio. On the wire a ratio is `{"lower_bound": "…", "pairs": n, "level": "0.95",
"mean": "…", "campaign": "b3:…"}`; the mean is informative and is never what a threshold reads.

**Campaign record (DESIGN name `frob.perf.pairs/1`, stored in the ledger).** Campaign identity; seed; the two arms
(commit and kernel-set identity, or comparator revision and configuration hash); per pair: index, order, each
arm's figure with unit and decimation, disclosure block identifier, host-state samples, flags; then n, ℓ̄, s, t, L.

**Refusal.** `FROB-REF-863`: "a performance ratio without a paired lower bound". Exit code 3; `retry:
after-change`; pointer to the ratio field. Raised when a ratio is stated with no campaign, with fewer than ten
pairs, with arms in different units or decimations that are not comparable, or from a void campaign. Ranked
repairs: run a paired campaign of at least ten pairs; state the two figures separately with no ratio; cite the
figure as context with no claim. The estimator's errors map onto it here, at the harness boundary:
`PairError::TooFewPairs` becomes `FROB-REF-863`; `PairError::NonPositive` (an arm that reported a zero or
negative figure) voids the campaign, and a ratio from a void campaign is `FROB-REF-863`. The estimator itself
knows no refusal codes.

**A/A calibration.** An A/A campaign runs the same build as both arms. Its true ratio is 1, so its lower bound
exceeds 1 with probability 5% and exceeds 1.02 less often. The harness is calibrated when, of 100 A/A campaigns,
at most 9 clear a threshold of 1.02 (9 is the 97th percentile of a binomial count at a 5% rate, so an honest
harness passes this check about 97 times in 100, while one that clears 1.02 in 15% of campaigns passes about 1
time in 20). More than 9 means pairing is not cancelling the host's noise (order effects, cache warming between
arms, thermal drift) and no ratio from that machine is admitted until it is fixed.
Two rules, two owners, one procedure: a single A/A campaign is N38a-08's `aa(run)`, whose pass rule for one
experiment's own A/A arm is that its bound does not exceed 1.02 (strict: a single clearing voids that
experiment). The calibration of a machine is this bead's: it runs that same campaign 100 times under this
protocol and applies the count rule. The two do not conflict: the first is a tripwire inside one experiment,
the second is the calibrated test that the gate records, and a machine may pass the second with up to nine
campaigns that would each have voided an experiment.

**Evidence.** A ratio is a Performance claim of class `Tested` under two disclosure blocks, one per arm.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-807 through the harness: a recorded campaign of ten pairs each with ratio exactly 1.5 yields a
      `PerfRatio` whose lower bound is exactly 1.5 with s = 0 (the estimator's own exactness, the fixture
      exp(0.2 − 1.833 × 0.1/√10) = 1.153 and the quantile values are tested once, in N38a-08).
- [ ] Every bound of the harness is the return value of `frob_core::perf::paired_lower_bound`: no other
      function in `frob-core` or `frob-conformance` computes a paired bound or a Student quantile.
- [ ] Nine pairs, a void campaign, and arms in non-comparable units are each refused with `FROB-REF-863`.
- [ ] `PerfRatio` cannot be constructed without a `PairedBound` (compile-fail test); a serialized ratio without `lower_bound` and `pairs` fails schema validation with `FROB-REF-863`.
- [ ] Arm order is reproducible from (seed, pair index) and is not constant across pairs.
- [ ] The calibration is a library procedure (`calibrate(machine, tier, run)`: 100 A/A campaigns of ten pairs under this protocol, then the count rule) whose rule is tested here on constructed campaigns (`aa_count_rule`, `synthetic_aa_rate`) and on a `synthetic` arm (`calibrate_on_synthetic_arm`). The measured counts on `batch-cpu` and on each device need the campaign runner and the run verbs, which depend on this bead: they are taken by the gate (BD-216, through step `aa_campaigns` of N36-30's script), recorded for GATE-SPARK-19 there, and are not a condition of this bead.
- [ ] The campaign record holds every pair, flags included; no API exists to remove a pair.
- [ ] A `PairedBound` with no campaign, or with a void campaign, cannot become a `PerfRatio`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The estimator's unit tests (`known_answer_all_equal`, `formula_fixture`, `student_quantiles`,
`fewer_than_ten_refused`, `lower_bound_below_geometric_mean`, `scale_invariance`, `permutation_invariance`,
`swap_is_not_inverse`) are N38a-08's and are not repeated here.

`cargo test -p frob-core perf::ratio`:
- `perf_ratio_needs_bound` (compile-fail): no constructor of `PerfRatio` without a `PairedBound`.
- `perf_ratio_needs_campaign`: a `PairedBound` with `campaign: None` → `PerfRatioError`; with a void campaign →
  the same.
- `campaign_known_answer` (KA-807): a campaign of ten pairs at 1.5 → `PerfRatio` with lower bound 1.5.
- `too_few_pairs_maps_to_863`: n = 9 → `PairError::TooFewPairs` from the estimator, `FROB-REF-863` from the
  harness, exit code 3, pointer to the ratio field.
- `orientation_time_rows`: comparator 410 s, ours 480 s in every pair → ratio 0.854, L = 0.854 ≥ 0.80 passes a 1.25 rule; ours 530 s → 0.774 fails.
- `non_positive_figure_voids_campaign`: `PairError::NonPositive` from the estimator voids the campaign; a ratio
  from it is `FROB-REF-863`.
- `synthetic_aa_rate`: 10,000 simulated A/A campaigns of ten pairs with independent log-normal noise (σ = 0.02) clear 1.0 in 5% ± 0.7% of campaigns (fixture labelled `synthetic`).
- `aa_count_rule`: 9 of 100 clearing 1.02 → calibrated; 10 of 100 → not calibrated, and the machine's ratios
  are refused.
- `calibrate_on_synthetic_arm`: `calibrate` driven by a `synthetic` arm (figures drawn from a seeded log-normal
  stream, σ = 0.02, the same stream for both arms) runs 100 campaigns of ten pairs, records every pair, and
  returns the count and the verdict `calibrated` for the pinned seed; with the second slot's figures raised by
  3% and a fixed arm order it returns `not calibrated` (about 26 of 100 campaigns clear 1.02).
- `order_stream_reproducible_and_mixed`.
- `trend_flagged`: a campaign whose ℓᵢ rise linearly with the pair index is flagged for a soak and a repeat.

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::pairs`: `record_keeps_flagged_pairs`; `void_on_failed_arm`; `fallback_time_counts`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_harness_gate19.sh` (N36-30), steps `paired_known_answer` and `aa_campaigns`: the first feeds a
recorded campaign of ten pairs at ratio 1.5 (labelled `synthetic`) through the real estimator and asserts 1.5; the
second runs 100 A/A campaigns of ten pairs on the real binary with short runs and asserts the count clearing 1.02
is at most 9. Logged per pair: campaign, pair index, order, both figures with units, host-state samples; per
campaign: n, ℓ̄, s, t, L against the threshold; and the final count.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-807 (exact; tier G1, host), here through the campaign path. Negative control: an A/A campaign must not clear 1.02 (of 100 at most 9).
- Planted defects registered against GATE-SPARK-19: an estimator that reports exp(ℓ̄) (the mean) as the bound must fail `lower_bound_below_geometric_mean` on a noisy fixture (the test is N38a-08's; the plant is registered against this gate because the gate's known answer reads the same function); a runner that always runs the comparator arm b first, so that the claimed arm a always takes the warm second slot, must fail the A/A count on a machine with a warm-up effect (planted by a fixture whose second arm is 3% faster: with log-normal noise σ = 0.02 per arm about 26% of campaigns clear 1.02, so the count exceeds 9 of 100 except with probability below 10⁻⁴); the mirror defect, a always first, biases every ratio against us, which the one-sided count cannot see, and is caught by `order_stream_reproducible_and_mixed` (arm order constant across pairs); a harness build with its own copy of the bound that uses the normal quantile 1.645 must fail the acceptance check that every bound is the estimator's return value.
- Metamorphic: permuting the pairs of a campaign record leaves its L unchanged; swapping the arms inverts ℓ̄ and does not give 1/L (the bound is one-sided), so the orientation rule and not an inversion decides a time row (both relations are asserted on the estimator by N38a-08).

## Considerations

- RISK-09 (`trj` is heavily loaded, so measurements are noisy and compiles slow; early signs: the spread of A/A campaigns and the recorded host load): the A/A count is the detector, interleaving and the lower bound are the mitigation.
- Ten pairs is a floor. A row that sits near its threshold needs more; the bound tightens as s/√n, and the record shows n.
- Cost: a campaign of ten pairs doubles the device time of ten single runs; campaigns are sized inside the compute envelope in force, and a row that cannot afford ten pairs is recorded `not run`, not judged on fewer.
- Paired differences across device families are only meaningful as speed ratios; pairing of physical outcomes across families is restricted by the determinism rules and is not this estimator's business.
- The log-ratio model assumes multiplicative noise; a campaign whose ℓᵢ show a trend with pair index (thermal drift) is flagged by a simple slope test and repeated after a soak.
- One estimator, two users: the Gauntlet's method experiments (N38a-08, M1) and this harness (M2). A change to the bound, its level or its quantile is made in N38a-08 and is a change to every recorded ratio's meaning; it is recorded as a new version of the campaign record schema.
