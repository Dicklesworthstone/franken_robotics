## Background

**ADR-PERF-04.** A performance ratio (ours against an incumbent, after against before, two devices against one) is
admitted only as the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of a paired ratio, never on a point estimate. Reasons:
both machines are noisy in ways that favour whoever ran at the quiet moment (`trj`, the Linux host with two RTX
4090, is often heavily loaded; the Mac throttles with temperature); a mean of unpaired runs mixes that noise into
the ratio; and a best-of-n figure is a selection, not a measurement. Pairing two arms back to back on the same
host state cancels the common noise; taking the lower bound makes the remaining uncertainty count against us.
A gate threshold applies to the bound, never to the mean. A ratio stated without one is refused
(`FROB-REF-863`).

Rejected alternatives: the mean of ratios or the ratio of means; the median with no interval; a two-sided
interval (we only ever need to show "at least"); a bootstrap interval (more machinery for ten pairs, and no
closed-form known answer to test it with).

Every ratio row of the target table, every optimization, the scaling rows on two cards, the mixed-batch row and
the fleet's campaign speedup use this one estimator. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the estimator; the campaign protocol (pairing, order, host-state rules, what is inside the timed interval);
the campaign record; the `PerfRatio` type that cannot exist without a bound; the A/A calibration; the refusal.
Out: running our engine or incumbents (the runner, BD-216; the incumbent harness); the 1.03 optimization
threshold's ledger (N36-07); judging rows (the target register).

## Technical approach

**The rule.**
1. At least ten pairs. The two arms of a pair run back to back, in randomized order, on the same host state, on
   the same workload and the same world addresses.
2. With ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ) for pair i, mean ℓ̄ = (1/n)·Σℓᵢ and sample standard deviation
   s = √(Σ(ℓᵢ − ℓ̄)²/(n − 1)), the bound is **L = exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n)**, where t is the 95th percentile of Student's
   distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10; 1.729 for n = 20; 1.699 for n = 30).
3. The incumbent arm is its strongest warm or cached configuration, frozen after tuning on development fixtures
   only.
4. Compilation, admission, synchronization, transfer, fallback, refusal and verification are all in the
   denominator: each arm's figure is completed valid work over wall-clock from submission to completion.
5. On the Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement; on `trj` the host load is recorded for every arm.

**Orientation.** a is always the arm whose advantage is being claimed and the ratio is oriented so that larger
is better: for rates, a/b = our rate ÷ the comparator's rate; for times, a/b = the comparator's time ÷ our time.
A target written "at most 1.25 times the incumbent's time" is judged as L ≥ 1/1.25 = 0.80. There is no upper-bound
variant to choose from, so the orientation cannot be picked after the fact.

**Protocol details (DESIGN).**
- Arm order within pair i is drawn from a counter-based stream keyed by (campaign seed, i), so a campaign can be
  re-run in the same order and audited.
- "Same host state": no other job is started by the harness between the two arms of a pair; host load (and on the
  Mac the thermal state) is sampled before each arm and stored. A pair whose two samples differ by more than a
  declared limit (DESIGN default: load average differing by more than 2.0, or a thermal state change) is kept and
  flagged; it is never dropped.
- **No pair is discarded.** If either arm fails to complete, the campaign is void and is reported with the
  failure; it restarts from the first pair once the cause is fixed. A ratio over the pairs that happened to work
  is the survivorship error the rule exists to prevent. A fallback or a refusal inside an arm is not a failure of
  the campaign: its time counts and is disclosed.
- Every arm passes the compute-envelope admission check before it starts.

**Types.** In `frob_core::perf`: `PairedBound { n, mean_ln, sd_ln, t, lower_bound, level: 0.95, campaign }` and
`PerfRatio`, whose only constructor takes a `PairedBound`. The pure function `paired_lower_bound(pairs) ->
Result<PairedBound, PairError>` and the Student quantile (computed, with table values as tests) live there too, so
any crate can state a ratio only through it. On the wire a ratio is `{"lower_bound": "…", "pairs": n, "level":
"0.95", "mean": "…", "campaign": "b3:…"}`; the mean is informative and is never what a threshold reads.

**Campaign record (DESIGN name `frob.perf.pairs/1`, stored in the ledger).** Campaign identity; seed; the two arms
(commit and kernel-set identity, or comparator revision and configuration hash); per pair: index, order, each
arm's figure with unit and decimation, disclosure block identifier, host-state samples, flags; then n, ℓ̄, s, t, L.

**Refusal.** `FROB-REF-863`: "a performance ratio without a paired lower bound". Exit code 3; `retry:
after-change`; pointer to the ratio field. Raised when a ratio is stated with no campaign, with fewer than ten
pairs, with arms in different units or decimations that are not comparable, or from a void campaign. Ranked
repairs: run a paired campaign of at least ten pairs; state the two figures separately with no ratio; cite the
figure as context with no claim.

**A/A calibration.** An A/A campaign runs the same build as both arms. Its true ratio is 1, so its lower bound
exceeds 1 with probability 5% and exceeds 1.02 less often. The harness is calibrated when, of 100 A/A campaigns,
at most 9 clear a threshold of 1.02 (9 is the 97th percentile of a binomial count at a 5% rate, so an honest
harness passes this check about 97 times in 100, while one that clears 1.02 in 15% of campaigns passes about 1
time in 20). More than 9 means pairing is not cancelling the host's noise (order effects, cache warming between
arms, thermal drift) and no ratio from that machine is admitted until it is fixed.

**Evidence.** A ratio is a Performance claim of class `Tested` under two disclosure blocks, one per arm.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-807: ten pairs each with ratio exactly 1.5 give s = 0 and a lower bound of exactly 1.5.
- [ ] Ten ratios with ℓ̄ = 0.2 and s = 0.1 give exp(0.2 − 1.833 × 0.1/√10) = 1.153.
- [ ] Nine pairs, a void campaign, and arms in non-comparable units are each refused with `FROB-REF-863`.
- [ ] `PerfRatio` cannot be constructed without a `PairedBound` (compile-fail test); a serialized ratio without `lower_bound` and `pairs` fails schema validation with `FROB-REF-863`.
- [ ] Arm order is reproducible from (seed, pair index) and is not constant across pairs.
- [ ] A/A calibration on `batch-cpu` and on each available device: of 100 A/A campaigns at most 9 clear 1.02; the count is recorded for GATE-SPARK-19.
- [ ] The campaign record holds every pair, flags included; no API exists to remove a pair.
- [ ] The Student quantile matches table values to 10⁻³ for n − 1 from 9 to 99.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-core perf::paired`:
- `known_answer_all_equal` (KA-807): [1.5; 10] → L = 1.5, s = 0.
- `formula_fixture`: ℓ = 0.2 ± pattern with sample standard deviation exactly 0.1 → L = 1.1526.
- `student_quantiles`: 9 → 1.833; 10 → 1.812; 14 → 1.761; 19 → 1.729; 29 → 1.699; 99 → 1.660.
- `fewer_than_ten_refused`: n = 9 → `PairError::TooFewPairs` mapped to `FROB-REF-863`.
- `orientation_time_rows`: comparator 410 s, ours 480 s in every pair → ratio 0.854, L = 0.854 ≥ 0.80 passes a 1.25 rule; ours 530 s → 0.774 fails.
- `non_positive_figure_voids_campaign`.
- `lower_bound_below_geometric_mean` (property): L ≤ exp(ℓ̄), with equality only when s = 0.
- `scale_invariance` (property): multiplying every aᵢ by k multiplies L by k.
- `synthetic_aa_rate`: 10,000 simulated A/A campaigns of ten pairs with independent log-normal noise (σ = 0.02) clear 1.0 in 5% ± 0.7% of campaigns (fixture labelled `synthetic`).
- `order_stream_reproducible_and_mixed`.

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::pairs`: `record_keeps_flagged_pairs`; `void_on_failed_arm`; `fallback_time_counts`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_harness_gate19.sh` (N36-30), steps `paired_known_answer` and `aa_campaigns`: the first feeds a
recorded campaign of ten pairs at ratio 1.5 (labelled `synthetic`) through the real estimator and asserts 1.5; the
second runs 100 A/A campaigns of ten pairs on the real binary with short runs and asserts the count clearing 1.02
is at most 9. Logged per pair: campaign, pair index, order, both figures with units, host-state samples; per
campaign: n, ℓ̄, s, t, L against the threshold; and the final count.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-807 (exact; tier G1, host). Negative control: an A/A campaign must not clear 1.02 (of 100 at most 9).
- Planted defects registered against GATE-SPARK-19: an estimator that reports exp(ℓ̄) (the mean) as the bound must fail `lower_bound_below_geometric_mean` on a noisy fixture; a runner that always runs arm A first must fail the A/A count on a machine with a warm-up effect (planted by a fixture whose second arm is 3% faster).
- Metamorphic: permuting the pairs leaves L unchanged; swapping the arms inverts ℓ̄ and does not give 1/L (the bound is one-sided), which the test asserts so that nobody "inverts" a bound.

## Considerations

- RISK-09 (`trj` is heavily loaded, so measurements are noisy and compiles slow; early signs: the spread of A/A campaigns and the recorded host load): the A/A count is the detector, interleaving and the lower bound are the mitigation.
- Ten pairs is a floor. A row that sits near its threshold needs more; the bound tightens as s/√n, and the record shows n.
- Cost: a campaign of ten pairs doubles the device time of ten single runs; campaigns are sized inside the compute envelope in force, and a row that cannot afford ten pairs is recorded `not run`, not judged on fewer.
- Paired differences across device families are only meaningful as speed ratios; pairing of physical outcomes across families is restricted by the determinism rules and is not this estimator's business.
- The log-ratio model assumes multiplicative noise; a campaign whose ℓᵢ show a trend with pair index (thermal drift) is flagged by a simple slope test and repeated after a soak.
