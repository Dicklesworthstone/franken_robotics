## Background

Three method rules of the performance program govern how an optimization may land:

- **Profile before optimizing.** Kernel microbenchmarks precede physics tuning; the first profile at M2 decides
  where effort goes.
- **One lever at a time, with a behaviour proof.** An optimization lands only with identical digests (determinism
  classes D1 and D2) or an unchanged fidelity battery.
- **Ceiling first**, and the sibling suite's own screen: effort goes to a kernel only if the top three kernels
  hold at least 50% of end-to-end wall time and the chosen one at least 15% (FACT: thresholds of the suite's
  accelerator doctrine).

Stated as one rule: an optimization lands only with its ceiling computed beforehand, with identical digests
(classes D1 and D2) or an unchanged fidelity battery, and with a paired lower bound of at least 1.03 on the row it
claims to improve.

Why a ledger. Each of the three conditions is easy to satisfy in spirit and skip in practice: a ceiling written
after the result, a "should be bit-identical" that nobody ran, a speedup read off one lucky run. RISK-45 names the
failure this protects against: an optimization changes unrelated semantics, so the kernel is faster while contact
counts or sensor rates fall; its early sign is throughput rising while a count in the disclosure block moves. The
ledger makes each optimization a record with artifacts that a test can check, and the landing check makes a
missing artifact a test failure. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: review by convention (the recorded solver
pilot shows a speed test run without its ceiling by people who knew the rule).

Terms. *Digest identity*: per-world run digests (integer mixes of committed state) are equal before and after for
every world of the proof workload; class D1 means the same bits on `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal, class D2 the same
bits run to run on one device family in any batch composition. *Fidelity battery unchanged*: for a change that
legitimately alters bits (a reordered reduction, a different factorization), the disagreement distributions
against the trusted tier stay inside their frozen envelopes on the standard battery, and the task decisions agree.

## Scope

In: the ledger schema and file; the landing check as a test target; the one-lever rule; the "unexplained speedup"
hand-off to the regression lane. Out: choosing and implementing levers and running the pass (BD-217); the profile
and the doctrine screen (the Spark unit's profile bead); the bound and ceiling record (N36-05); the paired
estimator (N36-06).

## Technical approach

**Ledger (`registries/optimizations.toml`, DESIGN schema `frob.perf.optimization/1`).** One entry per landed or
rejected optimization:

```toml
[[optimization]]
id          = "opt-0007"
row         = 1                         # the target row it claims to improve
lever       = "line search that does not re-read the Jacobian"
commits     = { before = "…", after = "…" }
ceiling     = "b3:…"                    # frob.perf.ceiling/1, filed before the first measurement
screen      = { top_three_share = "0.71", candidate_share = "0.22", verdict = "funded", profile = "b3:…" }
proof       = { kind = "identical-digests", classes = ["D1", "D2"], workload = "w:…", worlds = 8192,
                steps = 1000, digest_before = "b3:…", digest_after = "b3:…" }
pairs       = "b3:…"                    # frob.perf.pairs/1 on the claimed row
lower_bound = "1.047"
blockdiff   = "clean"
status      = "landed"                  # proposed | landed | rejected(<reason>)
```

For `proof.kind = "fidelity-battery-unchanged"` the proof fields are the battery identity, the fidelity-ledger
rows before and after, and the decision-agreement figure.

**Landing check (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1 -- optimizations`).** For every entry with
`status = "landed"`:
1. The ceiling record exists, predates the campaign's first pair in ledger order, was computed on a warm baseline
   profile, and is not below the entry's goal.
2. The screen verdict is `funded` on a profile whose fingerprint and step-plan identity match the measured row.
3. The proof holds: digests equal for every world (identical-digests), or every battery row inside its frozen
   envelope with decision agreement unchanged (fidelity-battery-unchanged). A change that claims bit equality and
   fails it is rejected; it may be resubmitted under the other proof kind, which is a different, weaker claim and
   is recorded as such.
4. The pairs campaign has at least ten pairs on the claimed row with before and after as its arms, and its lower
   bound is at least 1.03.
5. The block diff between the arms is `clean`: fixed fields equal, watched counts inside their run-to-run spread.
6. One lever: `commits.after` differs from `commits.before` by exactly the change set the entry names; two
   optimizations measured together are one entry only if they cannot be separated, and the entry says why.
A `rejected` entry stays in the file with its reason; a rejected optimization is a result.

**Unexplained changes.** A speed change beyond the regression lane's band between two releases with no ledger
entry is flagged `unexplained-speedup` or `regression` by that lane (N36-14); unexplained gains are treated as
suspect until an entry with a behaviour proof exists.

**Candidate levers named in advance** (each profile-gated; the first suspect is the dense constraint Jacobian,
whose traffic alone can equal the byte budget of the quadruped ambition): a fused assemble, factor and solve
chain; storing a contact row only over the columns it touches (the base and one leg); a line search that does not
re-read the Jacobian. They are tested at sizes 18, 30 and 60 degrees of freedom. Their microbenchmarks are the
bytes-touched bead's; whether each lands is decided here.

**Evidence.** The entry is the evidence record of the optimization; a Performance claim about the improved row
cites the pairs campaign and both disclosure blocks.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/optimizations.toml` exists with the schema above and passes the registry lint.
- [ ] The landing check fails on each planted defect: no ceiling; a ceiling filed after the first pair; a ceiling below the goal; a screen verdict of `refused`; one world's digest changed; a battery row outside its envelope; nine pairs; a lower bound of 1.02; a moved `mean_active_contacts`; two levers in one entry without a reason.
- [ ] A well-formed entry with a bound of 1.03 passes; 1.029 fails (the threshold applies to the bound, not the mean).
- [ ] An optimization that fails bit equality can be re-recorded only under `fidelity-battery-unchanged`, and the entry shows the downgrade.
- [ ] Rejected entries remain in the ledger with reasons.
- [ ] The optimization pass of the throughput-rows bead records every funded lever here exactly once, in descending order of ceiling.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::optledger`:
- `landed_entry_requires_all_artifacts` (parametrized over the ten planted defects of the acceptance list).
- `threshold_on_bound`: mean 1.06 with bound 1.02 → rejected; bound 1.03 → accepted.
- `ceiling_order_from_ledger`: filing time taken from ledger order, not from a field in the entry.
- `digest_proof_every_world`: 8,192 digests with one differing → rejected with the world address.
- `fidelity_proof_envelopes`: a row at 1.01 times its envelope → rejected.
- `one_lever_rule`.
- `rejected_entries_are_kept`.
- `proof_kind_downgrade_is_recorded`.

### End-to-end script and logging

The throughput-rows script (`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh`, owned by BD-217) calls the landing check after its
optimization pass and logs, per entry: id, row, ceiling, screen shares against 0.50 and 0.15, proof kind and
result, pairs, the lower bound against 1.03, block-diff status. `scripts/e2e/perf_harness_gate19.sh` (N36-30)
runs the check against a scratch ledger holding the planted defects and asserts each failure with its reason.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The recorded solver pilot as a fixture: ceiling 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7), measured 1.345 warm; an entry with goal 2.0 is rejected at step 1.
- Negative controls: the ten planted defects above; in particular the RISK-45 case (faster kernel, fewer active contacts) must be caught by both the digest proof and the block diff.
- Metamorphic: reordering entries in the file changes no verdict.

## Considerations

- The 1.03 threshold is on the lower bound of a ten-pair campaign; an optimization worth 3% on a noisy host needs more pairs to show it, which is the intended cost of small gains.
- No order of magnitude is available per step (an incumbent already measures 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s on this class of card), so the pass aims at the tolerance, and most entries will be small.
- If the parity kill gate records a defect (a physics row between 0.33 and 0.67 of the incumbent), the further optimization pass it requires is recorded here under the same rules.
- Bit-identical rewrites are also what specialized kernels must satisfy against their generic kernels; that fence is the Spark unit's and reuses the digest proof.
