## Background

`trj`, the training machine, has two RTX 4090 cards of 24 GB with **no link between them**: nothing passes from
one card to the other except through the host. To use both for one training job the batch is split into
**shards**: each device is one shard that holds complete worlds, replicated immutable packs (model, scene and task
tables) and frozen behaviour weights. The policy update must then be the same update a single device would have
made on the whole batch, which is what the distributed normalization rule guarantees.

**The rule for synchronous training on two shards (DESIGN).**

1. Behaviour weights and normalizers are frozen during collection.
2. Each shard reduces its loss-gradient **sums** and its count of valid samples. The host adds the sums in device
   order and divides by the **global** valid count.
3. An empty shard contributes zero and still participates. If the global count is zero the update is skipped.
4. Minibatch order is fixed by address-keyed streams whenever equivalence between shard counts is being tested.
5. Weights are published only after a complete update. An asynchronous variant needs a declared bound on version
   lag and runs only under the labelled nondeterministic training profile.

Known answer: one sample with gradient 1 and three with gradient 0 give 0.25, on one shard and on two, however
the four are split. The shortcut that fails: averaging the shard means. Split as {1} and {0, 0, 0} it gives
(1 + 0)/2 = 0.5; with an empty shard it divides by zero or halves the answer.

The arithmetic of the rule (the reducer, the statistics merge, the property test over random partitions) is the
learning unit's bead and is tested there on simulated shards. This bead is the device side: making two physical
cards behave as two shards of one job, with admission, placement, transfers through the host and failure handling.
Tier: Solid for synchronous sharding at the scaling tolerance; scaling beyond the incumbent's own two-card gain is
Frontier and is governed by the sharding kill gate. Rejected alternatives: averaging parameters after independent
updates (a different algorithm with different convergence); per-shard normalization with a correction factor
(wrong for higher moments and for empty shards); moving world state between cards (compiled scene fields, world
state and rollout buffers never cross devices or machines).

## Scope

In: shard placement; replication of immutable packs; per-shard admission; the device-side update protocol; the
transfer meter; receipts; fault handling; the two operating modes (independent shards, and one synchronous job);
the gradient known answer on real cards. Out: the reducer and statistics merge themselves (the learning unit);
the scaling measurement and gates (BD-199); the asynchronous variant (N36-12); the kill-gate decision (N36-10).

## Technical approach

**Placement.** A world belongs to exactly one shard. `shard_of(world address)` is a pure function of the address
and the shard layout: worlds are sorted by address and cut into contiguous runs sized to each device's share,
each run rounded to a multiple of the execution width (32). Results are keyed by world address, never by shard or
arrival, so the layout is not an input to any world's trajectory (class D2 per world; the shard lane of the
batch-composition invariance test checks it).

**Replication.** Model tables, scene variants (the brick pool and directories), task tables and the policy's
frozen behaviour weights are uploaded to each card. Scenes and model tables are immutable for the job; behaviour
weights are replaced only at a publish.

**Admission, numbered.**
1. Read each device's record from the hardware fingerprint: free memory at admission, bandwidth, and `peer`
   (device-to-device access: yes, no or untested; on `trj` no link exists, so every transfer is planned through
   the host at the measured host rate).
2. **Each shard must pass the memory estimator on its own**: its worlds, its replicated packs, its rollout storage
   and its share of learner state against its own card's budget. A split that fits in total and not per card is
   refused (`FROB-REF-602`, naming the card and the largest per-card world count).
3. The job's demand (two devices) passes the compute-envelope check; under the default class (one device) a
   two-shard job is refused with `FROB-REF-761`.
4. One device worker per card is started; each captures its own launch graph.

**One synchronous update on two cards, numbered.**
1. *Collect.* Both cards step their worlds with the same frozen weights (the host checks the two weight digests
   are equal before collection starts) and frozen normalizers. Rollout storage stays on each card.
2. *Statistics pass.* Each card reduces its advantage, return and observation statistics to per-shard triples
   (count, mean, sum of squared deviations) over valid samples; the host merges them in device order and
   broadcasts the global constants.
3. *Gradient passes.* For each minibatch (a set of sample identifiers drawn by address-keyed streams, independent
   of the layout), each card evaluates the loss on its members with the global constants and returns its gradient
   sum (one parameter-sized vector), its valid count and its loss sums. A card with no members returns zeros and a
   count of zero, and still replies.
4. *Reduce and step.* The host adds the gradient sums in device order (device 0, then device 1), adds the counts,
   divides by the global count, clips, and applies one optimizer step. If the global count is zero the step is
   skipped and counted.
5. *Publish.* After the last minibatch of the last epoch the new weights are sent to both cards and the behaviour
   slot is swapped on both. No card collects with unpublished weights; a publish that reaches only one card is
   rolled back on it and retried.

**Transfer meter.** Per update the receipt records, per card: bytes host-to-device (weights, global constants)
and device-to-host (gradient sums, triples, statistics ring), the number of host synchronizations, and the time
spent waiting on the other card. For a policy of P parameters, M minibatches and E epochs the gradient traffic is
4·P·M·E bytes per card per update; the meter prints it against the measured host rate. These figures are the
overhead term of the scaling ceiling.

**Modes.** `independent`: two shards run two independent environments (no update protocol); this is the
weak-scaling configuration and also the fallback of the kill gate. `synchronous`: one training job under the
protocol above, requested with `frob train start <design> --task <task> --backend gpu-cuda:0,1 --shards 2 --key
<key> --json`: the input `shards` is the layout field the learning unit's reducer bead (N23a-19) defines and
tests on one host, and this bead places its shards on the cards the placement names.

**Faults.** A lost device (`FROB-REF-601`) or a failed launch after bounded retries on one card aborts the update
in progress; nothing partial is applied (rule 5). The job stops with the last published weights intact and a
typed refusal naming the card; resuming on one card is a new job with a new layout, never a silent continuation.

**Determinism and evidence.** With a fixed layout and device order the update is class D2 per backend
(bit-identical run to run). Across layouts (one device against two shards) it is class D3 with the envelope
recorded. The run receipt carries the layout, the device order, both placement receipts and both fingerprint
digests.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-813 on real cards: gradients (1, 0, 0, 0) give 0.25 with splits {1 | 0,0,0}, {1,0 | 0,0}, {1,0,0,0 | ∅} and {∅ | 1,0,0,0}; a build with the mean-of-shard-means reducer gives 0.5 on the first split and is caught.
- [ ] A two-shard job whose total fits and whose per-card share does not is refused before allocation, naming the card.
- [ ] Under the default compute envelope a two-shard job is refused with `FROB-REF-761`; under the gate-measurement class it runs, when it is started under the approved scope (through `frob-launch` or the campaign runner, whose envelope token gives the verb its scope, or directly with `--envelope-scope gate:GATE-SPARK-20`, N36-20; started directly without the citation its scope is `adhoc` and it is refused).
- [ ] Two runs with the same layout give bit-identical reduced gradients and final weights (class D2); a two-shard run and a one-device run of the same job agree inside the recorded D3 envelope.
- [ ] Per-world digests of collection are identical whether a world sat on card 0 or card 1 (the shard lane of batch-composition invariance).
- [ ] Weights are never observed in a half-published state: with a fault injected between the two publishes, both cards end on the old weights.
- [ ] The receipt's transfer meter matches an instrumented count of bytes and synchronizations within one count.
- [ ] Cancellation of a two-shard job drains both device workers and leaks no device memory on either card.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-813 (exact; tier G5, CUDA): 0.25 on one shard and on two, however the four are split, and with an empty shard. Negative control: the mean of shard means, 0.5.
- CX-380 (gradients from shards combined as the mean of shard means). Its control is the plant `mean-of-shard-means` of the counterexample registry N36-CX (kind `mutation`), built once by this bead: a `cfg` branch of the device-order reducer, behind the Cargo feature the control's record names (N38b-05) and compiled only under `gauntlet-mutations`, that averages the shard means instead of dividing the sum of sums by the global valid count. `mean_of_shard_means_is_wrong` and the KA-813 run of the control runner use it; the learning chapter's statement of the same case (CX-253, N23-CX) keeps its own control and is served by the same planted reducer.
- Further planted defects: a reducer ordered by reply arrival (fails the D2 lane under an injected delay); weights published to one card early (fails the freeze check); a layout that splits one world's rows across cards (fails placement).
- Determinism lanes: fixed layout twice (bitwise); one device against two shards (D3); the shard lane of batch-composition invariance.

## Considerations

- RISK-46 (scaling across two cards vanishes under transfer cost, or under vision workloads; likelihood medium). The transfer meter is the early sign; for a policy of about 10⁵ parameters the gradient traffic is negligible, for a multi-million-parameter network it is not.
- The fingerprint's `peer` field is `no` or `untested` on `trj`; if the device-to-device experiment ever shows a usable path the planner may use it, with the host path as the permanent reference.
- The memory estimator dependency means a shard job cannot be admitted before the estimator exists; until then two-card runs are limited to the independent mode with a per-card byte-model check.
- Only seeds, recipes, variant tables, weights, receipts and summaries cross between devices; a design that needed to move rollout buffers between cards is out of bounds.
- If the kill gate fires, this runtime remains as the independent mode plus synchronous sharding at its tolerance where used; nothing here is thrown away.
