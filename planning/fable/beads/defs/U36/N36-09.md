## Background

Two-device strong scaling beyond the incumbent's own is the one Frontier item of the performance program, under
the kill gate KILL-SHARD (trigger: after one optimization pass, the paired lower bound of strong scaling at equal
task quality is below 1.6). The program's rule for every Frontier mechanism is that a paper-scale pilot with a
falsifier runs before its work is funded, and its rule for every speed experiment is ceiling first.

The tempting belief is that two cards train twice as fast. FACT (a published incumbent figure, to be re-read and
then re-measured on `trj`): the incumbent's own two-card run takes about 325 s against about 410 s on one card, a
gain of 1.26. Fixed costs explain it: about 45 s of its 410 s is compilation, the learner runs on one stream, and
sample efficiency falls as the batch grows.

There is a second, less obvious cap. Strong scaling at **constant global workload** splits the same batch across
the cards. A device whose throughput is still proportional to its world count at that batch size gains nothing
from being handed half the worlds: its batch step takes the same time at half the size, so each card needs as
long for its half as the single card needed for the whole batch. Collection speeds up only by r_c = 2·T(W/2)/T(W), where T is the throughput-against-worlds curve: 2 if the
card is saturated at W/2 worlds, 1 if throughput is linear in worlds. The size curve is measured by the
performance harness, so this cap is computable before any two-card run.

This pilot computes the ceiling of strong scaling for our own quadruped training job from recorded one-card
measurements only, states it before the two-card measurement, and names what would make the Frontier work not
worth funding. Tier: Frontier (pilot).

## Scope

In: the ceiling model; its inputs from one-card ledgers and the size curve; the incumbent's ledger as a sanity
fixture; the prediction record; the falsifier. Out: the two-card runtime (N36-08); the measurement (BD-199); the
decision (N36-10).

## Technical approach

**Inputs (all from recorded runs; no two-card run is used).**
- Our one-card phase ledger of the reference flat velocity task trained to its return threshold: t_fixed
  (compilation or cache load, admission, capture, final evaluation), t_collect (environment steps), t_grad (loss
  forward and backward over the stored rollouts), t_opt (the optimizer step and bookkeeping, single stream),
  t_wait.
- The size curve T(W) of the full environment step for the quadruped workload at W and W/2 worlds.
- The per-update transfer volume of the two-card protocol (gradient sums, statistics triples, weights) and the
  measured host transfer rate from the hardware fingerprint.
- The incumbent's one-card and two-card ledgers from the parity baseline.

**Model, numbered.**
1. Shares: f_c = t_collect/T₁, f_g = t_grad/T₁, with T₁ the one-card time; the rest (fixed, optimizer, waiting)
   does not shrink.
2. Local factors: r_c = 2·T(W/2)/T(W), clamped to [1, 2]; r_g likewise from the learner's batch-size curve, or
   r_g = 1 when the learner is not sharded.
3. Overhead: h = (t_transfer + t_sync_extra)/T₁, where t_transfer = updates × bytes per update ÷ host rate and
   t_sync_extra is one extra host synchronization per minibatch per card at the fingerprint's measured cost.
4. Ceiling: S_max = 1/(1 − f_c − f_g + f_c/r_c + f_g/r_g + h). This is the general speedup bound applied to two
   improved fractions. It is computed by calls, never by a local copy of the formula (N00-17's single-implementation
   rule, whose source scan covers this module): with the learner not sharded (r_g = 1, the case the thresholds
   below assume) it is N36-05's `ceiling(f_c, r_c, h)`; with a sharded learner the two levers go through
   `thesis::compose` in the order collection, then learner (the learner's lever acting on its share of the time
   collection leaves, f_g ÷ (1 − f_c + f_c/r_c)), and the overhead is added to the composed time:
   S_max = 1/(1/compose + h).
5. Thresholds on the collectable share when the learner is not sharded and r_c = 2: the tolerance 1.25 needs
   f_c ≥ 0.40 + 2h; the kill gate's 1.6 needs f_c ≥ 0.75 + 2h.
6. Doubled-batch variant (each card keeps W worlds): collection per update is unchanged and the number of updates
   to the threshold falls by a sample-efficiency factor e ≤ 2 that is unknown before measurement; the pilot
   reports the ceiling as a function of e and does not assume a value.

**Sanity fixture.** Applied to the incumbent's published pair, the model must reproduce about 1.26: with 45 s of
410 s fixed (0.11) and the observed gain, the implied improved share at r = 2 is 2·(1 − 1/1.26 + h) ≈ 0.41 + 2h.

**Registration and prediction record.** The pilot is one record of the pilot register
(`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, the one register of every pilot,
N38a-09), registered with status `registered` before the inputs are read: `id = "PILOT-SHARD-01"` (DESIGN, in the
register's grammar); `mechanism` "two-card strong scaling beyond the synchronous tolerance: asynchronous two-card
collection"; `tier = "F"`; `owner = "§36.6"`; `bead = "N36-09"`; `work_package = "WP-48"`; `gated_beads =
["N36-12"]`; `protocol` (`docs/pilots/PILOT-SHARD-01.md`: the model, the thresholds and the scoring rule of this
bead, hashed and frozen at registration); `falsifier` "the computed ceiling S_max of the constant-workload variant
is below 1.6"; `ceiling = { statement = "two cards at constant global workload gain at most 2", value = "2.0",
basis = "r_c ≤ 2 and r_g ≤ 2 in the bound" }`; `fallback = { ships = "the two cards as two independent job
lanes", kill_gate = "KILL-SHARD" }`; `cost` (host arithmetic on recorded ledgers: no device time, envelope
`default`); `tool` (the module `perf::shard_ceiling`, site `host`); `integrated_gate = "GATE-SPARK-20"`. The
prediction is the appended `[[pilot.result]]`, filed before BD-199's first two-card pair: the inputs with their
ledger identities, S_max for the constant-workload variant, S_max(e) for the doubled-batch variant, the
prediction interval obtained by propagating the run-to-run spread of the shares, and the verdict
(`falsifier-holds` when S_max < 1.6, which blocks the funding of N36-12 through the register's `fundable_bead`;
`falsifier-rejected` otherwise). The pilot result sets no part of GATE-SPARK-20 and decides nothing about
KILL-SHARD, which is decided on the measurement.

**Falsifier and consequences.**
- If S_max < 1.6: the Frontier work beyond the tolerance (the asynchronous variant) is not funded on this
  evidence; the measurement still runs, because the kill gate is decided by measurement, but no optimization of
  the sharding path may claim a goal above S_max (`FROB-REF-866`).
- If S_max < 1.25: the scaling gate's tolerance is predicted to fail at constant workload; this is reported to
  the decision bead before the device-hours of the measurement are spent, and the doubled-batch variant becomes
  the configuration measured.
- The pilot itself is falsified if the measured paired lower bound exceeds S_max: the model missed a parallel
  fraction, and the ceiling arithmetic is corrected before it is used again.

**Evidence.** Class `Estimated` (a prediction from measured inputs), scored against BD-199's measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::shard_ceiling`:
- `ceiling_known_values`: f_c = 0.6, r_c = 2, f_g = 0, h = 0.02 → 1/(0.4 + 0.3 + 0.02) = 1.389; f_c = 0.8, h = 0.02 → 1/(0.2 + 0.4 + 0.02) = 1.613; f_c = 0.8, r_c = 1 → 1/(1.02) = 0.98 (no gain when throughput is linear in worlds). Each value is the rational N36-05's `ceiling(f_c, r_c, h)` returns, compared exactly.
- `two_levers_through_compose`: f_c = 0.6, r_c = 2, f_g = 0.2, r_g = 2, h = 0.02 → `thesis::compose` gives 5/3 (composed time 0.6), and S_max = 1/(0.6 + 0.02) = 50/31 ≈ 1.613; with r_g = 1 the compose path equals `ceiling(f_c, r_c, h)` exactly.
- `no_local_bound_formula`: the source scan of N00-17 finds no `1/(1 − f + f/r + h)` in `perf::shard_ceiling`.
- `threshold_shares`: h = 0.02 → 0.44 for 1.25 and 0.79 for 1.6.
- `incumbent_fixture`: 410 s and 325 s → 1.262; implied share at h = 0: 2·(1 − 325/410) = 0.415.
- `local_factor_from_size_curve`: T(8,192) = 2.0 M, T(4,096) = 1.6 M → r_c = 1.6; T(4,096) = 1.0 M → r_c = 1.0.
- `doubled_batch_curve`: e = 1 → no gain from updates; e = 2 → collection-limited ceiling.
- `transfer_overhead`: 1,000 updates × 16 MB ÷ 2 GB/s = 8 s; over T₁ = 400 s → h = 0.02.
- `prediction_must_precede_measurement` (ledger order).

### Known answers and negative controls

- CX-379 ("two cards train twice as fast"): the incumbent fixture, 1.26.
- The speedup bound's own known answers (f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 → 13.5) guard the shared function.
- Negative control: a model that sets r_c = 2 regardless of the size curve over-predicts on the linear fixture and is caught by `local_factor_from_size_curve`.
- Negative control: a prediction filed after the measurement is rejected. This is the control of CX-379 in the counterexample registry N36-CX, the planted input `late-two-card-prediction`, built by this bead: the `synthetic` fixture `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/perf/late-two-card-prediction.toml`, a pilot result whose ledger position follows a two-card pair of the same campaign; `prediction_must_precede_measurement` must reject it, never accept it as a prediction.

## Considerations

- The most likely finding is unglamorous: at the world counts where one card is not saturated, synchronous strong scaling of collection is bounded near 1, and the honest use of the second card is a second job. That is exactly the fallback the kill gate names, and learning it from arithmetic costs no device-hours.
- "Equal task quality" is part of the measured claim, not of this ceiling; a two-card run that reaches the threshold sooner with a worse policy does not count, and the ceiling says nothing about it.
- The asynchronous variant changes the model (collectors no longer wait for the learner); its own ceiling is computed in its own bead, from the waiting share this pilot reports.
- RISK-46: transfers through the host are the overhead term here; a larger policy raises h linearly in its parameter count.
