## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped. A kill gate that fires
is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.

**KILL-SHARD.** Trigger: after one optimization pass, the paired lower bound of strong scaling at equal task
quality is below 1.6. Fallback: no further sharding work is funded (asynchronous collection, the nondeterministic
training profile's two-device variant); the two cards run as two independent job lanes, which the division of
labour between the machines uses anyway. The tolerance of 1.25 still applies to synchronous sharding where it is
used. Decided at M2 and recorded as an ADR.

Why the gate exists. Two-device strong scaling beyond the incumbent's own is the one Frontier item of the
performance program. The incumbent's own two-card run gains 1.26, because compilation is not shared, the learner
runs on one stream and sample efficiency falls as the batch grows. Beating that takes real work (an asynchronous
collector with a bounded version lag), which is worth funding only if the synchronous baseline shows there is
something to gain. The number 1.6 is an ambition on which nothing else in the plan depends: the two cards are
already useful as two lanes.

Terms. *Strong scaling*: one-card time to the return threshold ÷ two-card time for the same training job.
*Equal task quality*: both arms attain the threshold and the two-card policy is not worse on re-evaluation by
more than a margin fixed beforehand. *Paired lower bound*: the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of the ratio
over at least ten interleaved pairs; a threshold applies to the bound, never to the mean.

## Scope

In: the decision procedure; the ADR; the fail-closed default when the measurement cannot run; what changes
downstream on each branch. Out: the measurement (BD-199); the ceiling prediction (N36-09); the fallback's wiring
(N36-11); the work funded on the other branch (N36-12).

## Technical approach

**Deciding measurement.** The strong-scaling paired lower bound L of the campaign run **after** the single
optimization pass of the sharding path, at equal task quality, from the scaling seed bead's record. The pre-pass
bound and the ceiling pilot's prediction are attached as context and decide nothing.

**Reading, fixed in advance.**

| Post-pass bound L | Scaling gate (tolerance 1.25) | KILL-SHARD | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| L < 1.25 | Fails | Fires | Fallback. Synchronous sharding is not used for training by default; the scaling gate's measured failing value stays on record and its status becomes `killed`, with the kill gate and its fallback as the detail |
| 1.25 ≤ L < 1.6 | Passes | Fires | Fallback. Synchronous sharding stays available where a job asks for it, at its measured gain |
| L ≥ 1.6 | Passes | Does not fire | Further sharding work may be funded, each item still behind its own pilot and ceiling |

**Procedure, numbered.**
1. Confirm the record is admissible: at least ten pairs, complete disclosure blocks, equal-quality figures per
   pair, the A/A control under 1.02, the optimization pass recorded lever by lever.
2. Read L against the table. No other number enters. A bound of exactly 1.6 does not fire the gate.
3. Write the ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHARD-M2.md` (every kill-gate decision is
   `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), with the fields every kill-gate ADR carries: the kill gate; the milestone; the
   trigger as registered and any amendments applied; the measured L with its pairs record and receipt hash, set
   beside the trigger's number 1.6; the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries
   from now on ("two independent job lanes on `trj`"); the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback"
   or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; and the session that recorded it, which must not be a
   session that implemented the shard runtime. As context, and deciding nothing: the pre-pass bound, the pilot's
   predicted ceiling and whether the pilot was falsified.
4. Write the kill row's decision in the gate register, `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`
   (N40-03; `receipts` holds the hash of the post-pass campaign's receipt, and a `not-fired` entry without one is
   refused), with `outcome = "not-fired"` or `outcome = "fired"` (the fallback is in force). The
   register's propagation then gives a failed gate among the row's reads the status `killed` with the detail
   "KILL-SHARD: two independent job lanes" (that is the case L < 1.25; in the middle row the scaling gate
   passed and keeps its `pass`).
5. Apply the branch: on `fired`, the fallback bead (N36-11) becomes ready and the asynchronous-variant bead
   (N36-12) closes `withdrawn`, citing this ADR (N41-17, rule 5: the close type of a bead that exists only on the
   branch a decision did not take); on `not-fired`, the asynchronous-variant bead becomes eligible (it still
   depends on its own pilot) and the fallback bead closes `withdrawn` the same way.

**If the measurement cannot run.** Two devices exceed the default compute envelope. If the gate-measurement class
is not approved by the M2 exit, the decision's outcome is `deferred` (the register's word for a decision an ADR
moves to a named later exit; its reads stay `not-run`), with `reason = "blocked: OPEN-SPARK-2"` and the next
exit named in `deferred_to`, and the fail-closed default holds: the fallback is in force (independent lanes; no sharding work
funded) until a measurement exists. A default removes a capability; it never adds one. This is recorded in the
same ADR file at the deadline, and the exit review reports it with the capability it removed.

**Amendment rule.** The trigger, the threshold 1.6 or the fallback change only by a dated amendment to the gate
register, with its reason, made before the measurement it governs. A second optimization pass "to get over 1.6"
is such a change and is not available after the fact.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHARD-M2.md` exists at the M2 exit with the required fields: the measured post-pass bound, its pairs record and receipt hash, the reading from the table and the outcome (`not-fired` or `fired`); or, if the measurement could not run, the outcome `deferred` with the reason `blocked: OPEN-SPARK-2`, the later exit and the fallback adopted by default.
- [ ] The KILL-SHARD row's `decision` entry is N40-03's `{ outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`: `adr` the path above; for `not-fired` the hash of the post-pass campaign's receipt; for `deferred` also `deferred_to` and `reason`. `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>` (N40-03), with KILL-SHARD's identifier in the file, exits 0 on the committed register, read-only, after the ADR is written.
- [ ] The branch is applied in the bead graph: exactly one of the fallback bead and the asynchronous-variant bead is open for work after the decision.
- [ ] The decision used the post-pass bound and nothing else; the ADR shows the pre-pass bound and the pilot's ceiling only as context.
- [ ] Reports that mention two-card training after a `fired` outcome describe the capability by the fallback's name (two independent job lanes).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance gates::kill_shard`:
- `reading_table`: L = 1.24 → (fail, fires); 1.25 → (pass, fires); 1.59 → (pass, fires); 1.60 → (pass, not fired); 1.80 → (pass, not fired).
- `decides_on_post_pass_bound_only`: pre 1.7, post 1.5 → fires.
- `inadmissible_record_cannot_decide`: nine pairs, or a missing equal-quality figure, → the decision function refuses and the status stays `not run`.
- `blocked_without_envelope_defaults_to_fallback`: with no admissible record at the M2 exit the decision is `deferred`, names a later exit and the reason, and the fallback is reported in force.
- `amendment_must_predate_measurement`.
- `mean_is_ignored`: a record with mean 1.75 and bound 1.55 fires.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_shard_scaling.sh --phase post` (N36-31) ends with the `gate` step, which prints the three-way
reading; this bead adds no script. The decision function is run by `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
kill_shard -- --receipt <hash of the post-pass campaign's receipt> --json`, an example target this bead adds
(`crates/frob-conformance/examples/kill_shard.rs`, a thin argument parser over
`frob_conformance::gates::kill_shard` that prints one JSON object; the crate has no default binary, BD-025; a kill
row has no `tier`, so no Gauntlet target runs it, N40-03), on the recorded campaign; it writes nothing and logs
the bound, the thresholds 1.25 and 1.6, the outcome and the ADR path. After the recording session writes the ADR
and the decision entry, `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>` with KILL-SHARD's identifier exits
0.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The reading table is the known answer. Negative controls: deciding on the mean; deciding on the pre-pass campaign; treating `not run` as "not fired".
- CX-379 ("two cards train twice as fast") is the context fixture: the incumbent's 1.26 read through the same table is (pass, fires).

## Considerations

- RISK-46: scaling across two cards vanishes under transfer cost or under vision workloads; this gate is one of its mitigations, and its early sign is strong scaling under 1.25.
- Most likely outcome on the incumbent's evidence: the middle row. That is a normal result: synchronous sharding at parity with the incumbent, and the second card used as a second lane.
- What does not change on any branch: the distributed normalization rule stays the only correct way to combine shard gradients; weak scaling and independent lanes are unaffected.
- Downstream, if the gate fires: the asynchronous two-device variant behind the nondeterministic training profile is not built; that profile keeps its single-device scope; training-time plans that assumed a two-card speedup above 1.25 use the measured figure.
- The ten-fold arithmetic never counted on two-card strong scaling, so no program-level claim moves with this decision.
