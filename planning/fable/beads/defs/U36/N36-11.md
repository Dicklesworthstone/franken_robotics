## Background

This bead exists only on one branch of a kill-gate decision. KILL-SHARD fires if, after one optimization pass,
the paired lower bound of two-card strong scaling at equal task quality is below 1.6. Its fallback, named in
advance: no further sharding work is funded (asynchronous collection and the two-device variant of the
nondeterministic training profile); **the two cards run as two independent job lanes**, which the division of
labour between the machines uses anyway; the tolerance of 1.25 still applies to synchronous sharding where it is
used. The learning chapter states the same fallback from its side: below the scaling target, further sharding is
not funded, and the second card runs independent seeds or a second design.

The same arrangement is also the fail-closed default while the deciding measurement cannot run for lack of an
approved compute envelope.

Why independent lanes are a good fallback and not a consolation. Most of what a design studio asks of a training
machine is many moderately sized jobs: several seeds of one design (a result needs five), or two designs being
compared. Two independent lanes serve those at the weak-scaling figure (tolerance 1.8 of one card), with no
transfer between cards, no update protocol and no change of determinism class. Strong scaling of a single job
only matters for jobs too long for one card, which the plan does not rely on.

Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the default routing of training and rollout jobs on `trj` after the decision; the seed-lane helper; the
status of the synchronous mode; documentation and labels; tests of independence. Out: the shard runtime itself
(N36-08, unchanged); the daemon's queues (its own beads); the asynchronous variant (closed as not funded on this
branch).

## Technical approach

1. **Default.** `frob train start` and `frob sim run` on `trj` use one device per job. With no explicit device
   the job goes to an idle card, the lower device index first; when both are busy it waits for the card that
   frees first. Two submitted jobs therefore occupy the two cards concurrently. Assignment by shortest estimated
   work belongs to the daemon's size-class bead of the next milestone (BD-296, related); when it lands it replaces
   this rule and the lanes are unchanged.
2. **Seed lanes.** A multi-seed request (the evaluation protocol asks for five seeds) is expanded into one job per
   seed with distinct idempotency keys; the jobs are distributed over both cards. The keys are a pure function
   of the request (DESIGN): the job for seed s carries the key `<request key>/seed-<s>`, so a re-sent request
   maps onto the same five jobs and submits nothing new. Their results are keyed by
   world address and seed, so the set is identical to the set a single card would have produced one seed at a
   time.
3. **Second design.** Two designs under comparison each take a card; the comparison's pairing is by world
   address, so running the arms on different cards of the same device family is licensed where that family holds
   its run-to-run determinism class, and is otherwise refused by the determinism licence, not by this bead.
4. **Synchronous sharding stays, labelled.** `--shards 2` remains available. If the scaling gate passed its 1.25
   tolerance, a two-shard result prints its measured strong-scaling bound; if the gate failed, the option prints
   a warning that it is slower than its tolerance and is off the recommended path. No capability is silently
   removed.
5. **What is closed.** The asynchronous two-card collector is not built; its bead (N36-12) closes `withdrawn`,
   citing the kill gate's ADR (N41-17, rule 5); the nondeterministic training profile keeps only its
   single-device scope. If the gate does not fire, this bead is the one on the untaken branch and closes
   `withdrawn` the same way.
6. **Wording.** Reports and the generated agent documentation describe the capability by the fallback's name:
   "two independent job lanes on `trj`", with the measured weak-scaling bound, never "two-card training".

**Independence, as a tested property.** A job's per-world digests are the same whether the other card is idle,
running a different job, or running another seed of the same job (class D2 for the job; the other card is not an
input). Its throughput with the other card loaded is the weak-scaling figure and is disclosed as such.

**Evidence.** Each lane's results carry their own placement receipt and fingerprint digest; nothing crosses
between the cards.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After the kill row's decision is `fired` (or `deferred` with the fail-closed default in force), two training jobs submitted without a device argument run concurrently on the two cards.
- [ ] A five-seed request yields five jobs spread over both cards, with results identical (per-world digests and final weights) to the same seeds run one at a time on one card.
- [ ] A job's digests are unchanged by what the other card is doing; its rate with the other card loaded is within the measured weak-scaling tolerance of its solo rate (sum of both at least 1.8 of one).
- [ ] `--shards 2` still works and prints either its measured bound or the off-path warning, according to the scaling gate's record.
- [ ] The asynchronous-variant bead is closed `withdrawn`, citing the ADR.
- [ ] The agent documentation and status table name the capability "two independent job lanes".

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-verbs lanes` (device tests are skipped with a logged reason where two cards are absent; the
routing logic is tested with two `batch-cpu` lanes everywhere):
- `two_jobs_two_devices`: the second job goes to the idle device.
- `seed_lane_expansion`: five seeds → five jobs with distinct keys; a re-sent request is a no-op.
- `lane_results_equal_serial_results` (digests and weights).
- `other_lane_is_not_an_input`: digests equal with the neighbour idle, busy and cancelled mid-run.
- `shards_two_label`: prints the bound when the scaling gate's record is `pass`, the warning when it is `fail` or `killed`.
- `wording_uses_fallback_name` (a generated-documentation test).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_shard_scaling.sh` (N36-31) gains a step `independent_lanes`, added by this bead and run when
the kill row's decision is `fired` or `deferred`: submits two `frob train start <reference quadruped> --task
<reference flat velocity> --seed <s> --key lane-<run-id>-<s> --json` jobs with different seeds and no device argument
(`<run-id>` the script run's UTC timestamp, so a re-run submits new jobs), asserts both run at once on
different devices, compares their digests with solo runs of the same seeds under their own keys
(`--key lane-solo-<run-id>-<s>`, so that a solo run is a new job and not the stored receipt of the concurrent
one), and logs each lane's rate beside the weak-scaling
bound. Logged: job identities, device per job, digests solo and concurrent, rates with units, the gate record's
status.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Negative control: a router that sends both jobs to device 0 fails `two_jobs_two_devices`.
- Negative control: a lane that shares a normalizer or a random stream with its neighbour changes digests under co-running and fails `other_lane_is_not_an_input`.
- Determinism lanes: the solo-against-concurrent digest comparison above; repeat twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The weak-scaling tolerance of 1.8 is the performance figure that describes this fallback; if weak scaling itself fails, the cause is host contention and is a defect of the host feeding path, not of sharding.
- A comparison whose arms ran on different cards is paired only under the determinism rules of the numerics chapter; across device families it is unpaired unless bit equality with the CPU reference holds.
- Nothing in the ten-fold arithmetic depended on strong scaling, so adopting this fallback changes no program-level claim.
- If a later measurement (a new optimization idea, a larger policy) justifies re-opening sharding work, that is a dated amendment to the gate register before a new measurement, not a quiet reversal.
