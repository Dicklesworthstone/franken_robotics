## Background

This bead exists only if the kill gate KILL-SHARD does **not** fire, that is, if after one optimization pass the
paired lower bound of synchronous two-card strong scaling at equal task quality is at least 1.6. It is the
"further sharding work" that the gate funds or withholds, and it is the performance program's one Frontier
mechanism: two-device strong scaling beyond the incumbent's own.

In the synchronous protocol the two cards take turns with the learner: both collect, both wait while gradients
are reduced and the optimizer steps, both receive weights, both collect again. An **asynchronous** variant lets
the cards keep collecting while the learner updates: shards deliver collected segments and gradient sums out of
step, and a segment may have been collected with weights that are a few versions old. The rule that admits it:
an asynchronous variant needs a **declared bound on version lag** and runs only under the `fast-training`
profile.

`fast-training` is the labelled nondeterministic profile (determinism class D4). The physics step stays class D2
in every profile; the profile relaxes only the order in which shards deliver collected segments and gradient sums
(with the declared lag bound), the learner's reduction order and matrix-product implementation, and minibatch
composition by arrival. Its only output is a set of weights tagged `trained_under: D4`. Three rules bind it:

1. No number observed during such a run (a learning curve, a training return) is evidence (`FROB-REF-861`).
2. Every claim about the resulting policy comes from re-evaluation under the deterministic replay profile or a
   stricter one, which is deterministic given the weights.
3. The profile is off by default and is enabled per job, never per installation.

Ceiling, stated before any test. Inference and learning together are about 13% of training wall-clock on one
incumbent stack, so relaxing them gains at most 1.15 on one device; the profile's real use is this two-device
variant, whose gain is bounded by the share of time the collectors spend waiting in the synchronous protocol.

Tier: Frontier. Rejected alternatives: an asynchronous variant inside a deterministic profile (it cannot be
replayed, and passing it off as deterministic is exactly what the labelled profile exists to prevent); an
unbounded lag (stale-policy data with no stated limit makes the off-policy error unbounded).

## Scope

In: the asynchronous collector and learner loop on two cards; the version-lag bound and its enforcement; the D4
labelling of outputs; the ceiling record; the comparison against synchronous sharding. Out: the profile's
licences and refusals (the numerics unit's seed bead); the synchronous runtime (N36-08); any use of the profile
outside training.

## Technical approach

**Ceiling first (filed before any code is funded).** From the synchronous two-card phase ledger: w = the share of
two-card wall-clock during which the collectors wait on the learner or on each other; the asynchronous variant
removes at most that share, so the speedup over synchronous sharding is at most 1/(1 − w + h), with h the added
overhead (version bookkeeping, discarded stale segments). The work is funded only if this ceiling is at least
1.2 over the synchronous two-card time (DESIGN goal), and is refused otherwise (`FROB-REF-866`).

**Mechanism, numbered.**
1. Weights carry a monotonically increasing version number. Each card collects segments with the newest version
   it holds and tags every segment with that version.
2. The learner consumes segments as they arrive. Before a segment's samples enter a minibatch its lag is checked:
   lag = (current learner version) − (segment version). A segment with lag above the declared bound L_max is
   discarded and counted; it is never used "just this once". DESIGN default: L_max = 1 (a segment may be at most
   one update old); the bound is a job parameter recorded in the receipt.
3. Gradient sums from the shards are still **sums with counts**, divided by the global valid count of the
   minibatch actually assembled; the mean of shard means remains forbidden in this mode too.
4. New weights are sent to each card when ready; a card switches at its next segment boundary. Cards are never
   stopped to wait for a publish.
5. Normalizer statistics are merged by the exact pairwise formula from per-segment triples, in arrival order
   (which is what makes the run class D4).
6. The run's receipt lists: L_max, the histogram of observed lags, segments discarded, the shares of time each
   card spent collecting and waiting, and `trained_under: D4`.

**Outputs and their class.** Weights tagged `trained_under: D4`, and nothing else that may be cited. A request to
cite a training return or a learning curve from the run is refused (`FROB-REF-861`). A policy trained this way
without a stricter re-evaluation cannot be cited either. Re-evaluation of the weights under the deterministic
replay profile gives identical digests on repeat.

**Comparison (what would count as success).** Time to *attained and re-evaluated* threshold: the asynchronous
two-card job against the synchronous two-card job, at least ten pairs, equal task quality judged on the
re-evaluated policies (never on training returns), paired lower bound at least 1.2 (DESIGN goal, a TARGET set
with the ceiling record). Because the asynchronous arm is class D4, the comparison is a speed ratio between runs
and is not a paired statistic over world outcomes.

**Kill rule of this item (named in advance).** If the paired lower bound against synchronous sharding is below
1.1 after one optimization pass, or equal task quality fails on more than one pair in ten, the variant is
withdrawn and synchronous sharding remains the two-card training mode.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The kill-gate decision is on record as not fired, the pilot's scored prediction is attached, and this item's ceiling record is filed before implementation starts; if the ceiling is below the goal the bead is closed with `FROB-REF-866` as its reason.
- [ ] With L_max = 1, no minibatch contains a sample whose segment lag exceeds 1; discarded segments are counted in the receipt; a planted delay on one card raises the discard count and never the lag.
- [ ] Every artifact of the run is tagged `trained_under: D4`; citing its training return is refused with `FROB-REF-861`; the weights re-evaluate to the same digest twice under the deterministic replay profile.
- [ ] The profile is off by default and cannot be enabled by configuration file, only per job.
- [ ] The gradient known answer (0.25 for gradients (1, 0, 0, 0) however split, an empty shard included) holds in the asynchronous reducer.
- [ ] The comparison campaign against synchronous sharding is recorded with its paired lower bound and equal-quality figures, and the item's own kill rule is evaluated.
- [ ] Cancellation drains both cards and leaks no device memory; a lost device stops the job with the last complete weights intact.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-learn async_shards` (on two `batch-cpu` shards with an injected scheduler; on real cards where present):
- `lag_bound_enforced`: versions (5, 5, 4, 3) at learner version 5 with L_max = 1 → the version-3 segment is discarded.
- `discard_is_counted_not_hidden`.
- `gradient_sums_not_means`: the 0.25 fixture under arrival orders (0, 1) and (1, 0), and with an empty shard.
- `version_monotone`; `switch_at_segment_boundary`.
- `outputs_tagged_d4`; `training_return_citation_refused` (`FROB-REF-861`).
- `reevaluation_digest_stable`: two re-evaluations of the same weights give identical digests.
- `profile_off_by_default`; `profile_not_enabled_by_config_file`.
- `ceiling_from_wait_share`: w = 0.30, h = 0.03 → 1/(0.73) = 1.37; w = 0.10, h = 0.03 → 1.075 (refused against a goal of 1.2).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_async_shards.sh` (owned by this bead; sources the shared end-to-end helper; `trj` only; needs
the gate-measurement compute class): runs the paired campaign of asynchronous against synchronous two-card
training with `frob train start … --profile fast-training --shards 2 --json`, re-evaluates every final policy
with `frob eval run` under the deterministic replay profile, attempts to cite a training return and asserts the
refusal. Logged per pair: arm order, seed, time to attained threshold, lag histogram, discards, wait shares,
re-evaluated returns against the margin, both disclosure block identifiers (profile `fast-training`, determinism
`D4`); per campaign the paired lower bound against 1.2 and 1.1; a `summary` with the kill rule's outcome.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-813 (0.25 on two shards and with an empty shard) in the asynchronous reducer; the known answer of the profile (a policy trained under it re-evaluates to identical digests twice; citing its training return is refused).
- Negative controls: a collector that uses a stale segment beyond L_max; an output missing the D4 tag (the schema refuses it); a comparison judged on training returns instead of re-evaluated returns.
- Determinism lanes: the physics digests of collected worlds remain class D2 (checked on a sample of worlds replayed from their addresses); the learner is D4 by declaration, and no lane claims more.

## Considerations

- This is the only place in the system where arrival order is allowed to influence a number, and it is fenced on purpose: the label, the single kind of output, and the refusal to cite anything observed during the run.
- Stale data lowers sample efficiency; the equal-quality rule and the attained-threshold clock charge that cost in full. A variant that collects faster and learns slower can lose.
- If the kill gate fired, this bead is closed unbuilt with the ADR as its reason; that is the expected outcome on the incumbent's evidence (its own two-card gain is 1.26).
- Compute: the comparison campaign needs two devices for several device-hours and is admitted only under an approved envelope class.
- RISK-46 applies with more force: the asynchronous path adds traffic through the host.
