## Background

Before our own step exists, the plan can only say where time *should* go, by analogy with an incumbent.

FACT (an incumbent's published event trace of a humanoid at 8,192 worlds; to be re-read): of 262 ns per
world-step, the solve is 47%, constraint construction 15%, kinematics and smooth dynamics 20%, collision 4%,
integration 4%. A manipulation scene at 513 ns shifts to 44% solve and 32% collision. On a full environment step
of another stack, physics is under a third.

HYPOTHESIS for our step, to be replaced by the M2 profile: the constraint solve 40 to 60%, collision 15 to 25%
once worlds sample a scene distance field, dynamics terms 10 to 20%.

The same section fixes what the **referee** (the slow, trusted tier: one world on one CPU core, event-located)
can and cannot do for a device run. A device produces T·h world-seconds per second (T in world-steps/s, h the
step); a referee on c cores at a real-time factor ρ per core covers c·ρ of them. For the humanoid workload H at
its tolerance, T·h = 1.7 M × 2 ms = 3,400; 24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0 cover 4.8 to 24, that is 0.14 to 0.7%. For the
quadruped workload Q at its floor (2.0 M) it is 0.12 to 0.6%, and at the ambitions the shares fall to between a
half and two thirds of those. So **the referee is a sampler by necessity**: about 0.1 to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds
across device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward, at a referee speed of 0.2 to 1.0 times real time
per core on 24 cores (HYPOTHESIS; measured at M2); the target table asks only that its share be reported.
HYPOTHESIS: the `batch-cpu` tier, not the referee, is the tier that can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid worlds,
since its tolerance of 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400.

A hypothesis in this project carries the test that settles it, and counts only if it was on record before the
measurement. The six predictions and the coverage function are registered by N36p1-02, on which the profile bead
and the referee bead depend, so they are on record before either measurement. This bead computes their outcomes
from the M2 measurements and replaces the HYPOTHESIS labels by measured values, so that later decisions (where to
optimize, how much of a run can be shadowed, whether `batch-cpu` shadowing is worth building out) rest on
measurements. DESIGN (split in polish round 1): as drafted this bead also registered the predictions while
depending on the profile bead, which the graph could not order; registration moved to N36p1-02. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the outcome computation from the profile record and from the shadow-referee measurement; filling `outcome`
and `measured` of the six entries once; the report section; replacing the HYPOTHESIS labels; the command
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example perf -- where-time-goes`, a subcommand this bead adds to the example
target `perf` that N36p1-02 declares (the crate has no default binary, BD-025). Out: the register,
its six entries, its lint and the coverage function (N36p1-02, a dependency; this bead calls the function and
holds no copy); the profiler and the funding screen (the Spark unit); the sampled referee itself and its gate
(BD-214); the optimization pass (BD-217).

## Technical approach

**Inputs.** The six entries of the perf hypothesis register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/hypotheses/perf.toml`,
N36p1-02): H-TIME-1 (solve share, 0.40 to 0.60), H-TIME-2 (collision share on a scene workload, 0.15 to 0.25),
H-TIME-3 (dynamics terms, 0.10 to 0.20), H-TIME-4 (physics as a share of our full environment step; no
prediction of ours, the incumbent's "under one third" as context), H-COV-1 (referee coverage, 0.001 to 0.024),
H-COV-2 (`batch-cpu` shadow share of humanoid worlds, at least 0.10). The coverage figures are computed with
N36p1-02's `frob_conformance::perf::coverage` (`share(c, ρ, T, h) = c·ρ/(T·h)`, `shadow_share_cpu`), called, not
copied. The profile records of N21a-27; the referee's measured ρ per core and shadow share from BD-214.

**Outcome computation, numbered.**
1. Read the profile record for each (workload, backend); map its ten stage shares to the hypotheses (solve: stage
   8; collision: stages 2 to 5; dynamics terms: stage 6).
2. An outcome is `confirmed` if the measured share lies inside the predicted interval on the reference backend
   (CUDA where available, else `batch-cpu`), `refuted` otherwise; every backend's value is recorded. H-TIME-4,
   which has no prediction of ours, takes `recorded` with the measured share beside the incumbent's context value
   and its reading against the 25% non-physics overhead tolerance. The register's lint (N36p1-02) allows `outcome`
   and `measured` to be filled once and refuses any change to a prediction.
3. Read the measured referee real-time factor and the shadow share from the sampled-referee measurement; compute
   the predicted share with the measured T and compare with the measured share (they must agree within the
   sampler's own accounting tolerance, else the sampler's meter is wrong). The measured share is also passed,
   with its inputs, to N36p1-02's `check_claim`: a share above c·ρ/(T·h) is rejected with
   `coverage-claim-exceeds-capacity` and is not written to the register (the rule CX-383 protects).
4. Write the report section `where_time_goes`: the incumbent trace beside our measured shares, each hypothesis
   with its outcome, and the two coverage figures.
5. Replace the HYPOTHESIS labels: the register's `measured` values become the cited figures, and documentation
   generated from the register prints them with their source.

**Evidence.** Outcomes are class `Tested` diagnostics about our own step on named hardware; none is a Performance
claim.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::where_time_goes` (the coverage and register tests are N36p1-02's):
- `stage_mapping`: a profile fixture with ten stage shares maps solve to stage 8, collision to stages 2 to 5 and
  dynamics terms to stage 6; the three shares of the fixture (0.52, 0.18, 0.14) are read exactly.
- `outcome_rules`: measured 0.52 → `confirmed` for H-TIME-1; 0.33 → `refuted`; the bounds are inclusive (0.40 →
  `confirmed`).
- `reference_backend`: CUDA's value decides where present, `batch-cpu`'s otherwise; every backend's value is
  stored.
- `no_prediction_is_recorded`: H-TIME-4 with measured 0.62 → `recorded`, never `confirmed` or `refuted`.
- `coverage_from_measured_inputs`: measured ρ = 0.35 on 24 cores, T = 1.9·10⁶, h = 0.002 → share 8.4/3,800 =
  0.00221 through N36p1-02's `share`; a measured shadow share of 0.0021 agrees within a tolerance of 10%; 0.0015
  does not and is filed against the sampler.
- `late_registration_flagged`: an entry whose `registered_at` follows its profile record is scored and flagged
  `registered-after-measurement`.
- `outcome_written_once`: a second scoring of a scored entry is refused by the register's lint.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_where_time_goes.sh` (owned by this bead; sources the shared end-to-end helper; seed-explicit;
reads recorded measurements and runs nothing on a device). Steps: `preflight` (the fingerprint digest of each
machine whose profile is read); `register` (load the six entries, assert each is `unmeasured` or already scored,
print their ledger positions); `profile` (read the M2 profile records of N21a-27 for Q and H at 8,192 worlds and
the scene workload, per backend, and assert each passes its own accounting identity); `referee` (read BD-214's
measured ρ per core and shadow share); `score` (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example perf -- where-time-goes --json`
computes and writes the outcomes); `report` (assert the `where_time_goes` section exists with the incumbent trace
beside ours); `controls` (on a scratch copy: an unnormalized profile and an entry registered late, each asserted
caught). NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/perf_where_time_goes/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it;
every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code and wall time; one `metric`
line per hypothesis with the predicted interval, the measured value per backend, the reference backend, the
outcome and the profile record's identity; the two coverage figures with their inputs and units; the final
`summary` lists outcomes, flags and assertions passed and failed. On failure: the entry, the first differing
value and both record paths.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-821 and CX-383 are pinned by N36p1-02's tests on the function this bead calls; here the fixture
  `coverage_from_measured_inputs` checks the call with measured inputs, and a further test,
  `full_coverage_claim_not_scored`, offers the scoring step N36p1-02's planted record
  `full-referee-coverage-claim` (a claimed share of 1.0 where its inputs give 0.00706; the control of CX-383 in
  N36-CX), which must be rejected and leave H-COV-1 unscored.
- Negative control: an outcome computed from the profiled (per-kernel synchronized) times without the overhead normalization over-weights launch-heavy stages; the test compares against the normalized shares of a fixture.
- Negative control: scoring an entry against a profile recorded before the entry fails to carry the `registered-after-measurement` flag in a planted build and is caught by `late_registration_flagged`.

## Considerations

- A refuted hypothesis is a result. In particular, if the solve is well under 40% the bandwidth and Jacobian arguments weaken, and if collision exceeds 25% scene sampling becomes the first target; both change the optimization pass, not the gates.
- The referee's real-time factor is itself a target row (at least 0.2 for a humanoid on one core, ambition 1.0); the share ambition of 1% on the humanoid needs that ambition on all 36 cores of the Mac.
- Shares depend on workload: flat ground with few contacts moves time out of the solve; the register records the workload variant beside each measured value.
