## Background

Before our own step exists, the plan can only say where time *should* go, by analogy with an incumbent.

FACT (an incumbent's published event trace of a humanoid at 8,192 worlds; to be re-read): of 262 ns per
world-step, the solve is 47%, constraint construction 15%, kinematics and smooth dynamics 20%, collision 4%,
integration 4%. A manipulation scene at 513 ns shifts to 44% solve and 32% collision. On a full environment step
of another stack, physics is under a third.

HYPOTHESIS for our step, to be replaced by the M2 profile: the constraint solve 40 to 60%, collision 15 to 25%
once worlds sample a scene distance field, dynamics terms 10 to 20%.

The same section fixes what the **referee** (the slow, trusted tier: one world on one CPU core, event-located)
can and cannot do for a device run. A device produces T·h world-seconds per second (T in world-steps/s, h the
step); a referee on c cores at a real-time factor ρ per core covers c·ρ of them. For the humanoid workload H at
its tolerance, T·h = 1.7 M × 2 ms = 3,400; 24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0 cover 4.8 to 24, that is 0.14 to 0.7%. For the
quadruped workload Q at its floor (2.0 M) it is 0.12 to 0.6%, and at the ambitions the shares fall to between a
half and two thirds of those. So **the referee is a sampler by necessity**: about 0.1 to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds
across device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward, at a referee speed of 0.2 to 1.0 times real time
per core on 24 cores (HYPOTHESIS; measured at M2); the target table asks only that its share be reported.
HYPOTHESIS: the `batch-cpu` tier, not the referee, is the tier that can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid worlds,
since its tolerance of 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400.

A hypothesis in this project carries the test that settles it. This bead registers these, computes their
outcomes from the M2 measurements, and replaces the HYPOTHESIS labels by measured values, so that later decisions
(where to optimize, how much of a run can be shadowed, whether `batch-cpu` shadowing is worth building out) rest
on measurements. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the hypothesis register entries; the coverage function; the outcome computation from the profile record and
from the shadow-referee measurement; the report. Out: the profiler and the funding screen (the Spark unit); the
sampled referee itself and its gate (BD-214); the optimization pass (BD-217).

## Technical approach

**Register (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/hypotheses/perf.toml`).** One entry per hypothesis: `id`,
`statement`, `predicted` (an interval), `settled_by` (the measurement and its bead), `registered_at` (must precede
the measurement in ledger order), `outcome` (`unmeasured`, `confirmed`, `refuted`), `measured`, `consequence`.

| Id | Statement | Predicted | Settled by | Consequence if refuted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-TIME-1 | Share of our world-step in the constraint solve (stage 8) | 0.40 to 0.60 | The M2 profile, Q and H at 8,192 worlds, each backend | The optimization pass re-ranks its levers; the solve remedies lose or gain priority |
| H-TIME-2 | Share in collision (stages 2 to 5) once worlds sample a scene distance field | 0.15 to 0.25 | The same profile on a scene workload | The scene-sampling kernels become the first target if above 0.25 |
| H-TIME-3 | Share in dynamics terms (stage 6) | 0.10 to 0.20 | The same profile | Bounds what design-specialized recursions could gain |
| H-TIME-4 | Physics as a share of our full environment step | Under one third on an incumbent; ours unpredicted, so the entry records the incumbent's value as context | The full-step and physics rows | Sets the non-physics overhead against its 25% tolerance |
| H-COV-1 | Share of device world-seconds a referee sampler covers | 0.001 to 0.024 over device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward, at ρ of 0.2 to 1.0 on 24 cores | The measured ρ per core and the measured shadow share | If ρ is below 0.2 the share falls below 0.1% and the sampler's coverage figure says so |
| H-COV-2 | `batch-cpu` can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid device worlds | At least 0.10 (0.16 at the tolerances) | The measured `batch-cpu` humanoid rate against the measured device rate | `batch-cpu` shadowing is not built out as a coverage tier |

**Coverage function (`frob_conformance::perf::coverage`).** `share(c, ρ, T, h) = c·ρ/(T·h)`, with c cores, ρ the
real-time factor per core (simulated seconds per wall second), T the device rate in world-steps/s and h the step
in seconds. `shadow_share_cpu(T_cpu, T_dev) = T_cpu/T_dev` when both use the same h. Inputs carry units; a rate
without its decimation is refused by type.

**Outcome computation, numbered.**
1. Read the profile record for each (workload, backend); map its ten stage shares to the hypotheses (solve: stage
   8; collision: stages 2 to 5; dynamics terms: stage 6).
2. An outcome is `confirmed` if the measured share lies inside the predicted interval on the reference backend
   (CUDA where available, else `batch-cpu`), `refuted` otherwise; every backend's value is recorded.
3. Read the measured referee real-time factor and the shadow share from the sampled-referee measurement; compute
   the predicted share with the measured T and compare with the measured share (they must agree within the
   sampler's own accounting tolerance, else the sampler's meter is wrong).
4. Write the report section `where_time_goes`: the incumbent trace beside our measured shares, each hypothesis
   with its outcome, and the two coverage figures.
5. Replace the HYPOTHESIS labels: the register's `measured` values become the cited figures, and documentation
   generated from the register prints them with their source.

**Evidence.** Outcomes are class `Tested` diagnostics about our own step on named hardware; none is a Performance
claim.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::coverage perf::hypotheses`:
- `coverage_humanoid` (KA-821): share(24, 0.2, 1.7·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.00141; share(24, 1.0, …) = 0.00706.
- `coverage_quadruped_floor`: share(24, 0.2, 2.0·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.0012; at ρ = 1.0, 0.006.
- `coverage_at_ambitions`: Q at 4.0 M → 0.0006 to 0.003 (half); H at 2.55 M → 0.00094 to 0.0047 (two thirds).
- `coverage_upper_end`: share(24, 1.0, 500,000, 0.002) = 0.024 (a device at 1,000 world-seconds per second).
- `all_cores_ambition`: share(36, 1.0, 1.7·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.0106 (the 1% ambition needs a real-time referee on all 36 cores).
- `batch_cpu_shadow`: 275,000 ÷ 1,700,000 = 0.162.
- `incumbent_trace_shares`: 0.47, 0.15, 0.20, 0.04, 0.04 of 262 ns → 123.1, 39.3, 52.4, 10.5, 10.5 ns; the remainder 0.10 is unattributed in the source and is printed as such.
- `outcome_rules`: measured 0.52 → `confirmed` for H-TIME-1; 0.33 → `refuted`.
- `registered_before_measured`.

### End-to-end script and logging

The throughput-rows script (`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh`, owned by BD-217) runs the profile; this bead adds
a step `where_time_goes` that computes the outcomes from the recorded profile and the shadow measurement and logs
one `metric` line per hypothesis with predicted interval, measured value per backend and outcome, and the two
coverage figures with their inputs and units.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-821 (coverage arithmetic). CX-383 ("the referee can check every device world"): the test `referee_cannot_cover_everything` asserts that with 24 cores, ρ ≤ 1 and h = 2 ms the share is below 1 for every device rate above 12,000 world-steps/s (500 world-steps/s per referee core), which is two orders of magnitude under any device row.
- Negative control: a coverage function that divides by T instead of T·h reports a share 500 times too small at h = 2 ms and fails `coverage_humanoid`.
- Negative control: an outcome computed from the profiled (per-kernel synchronized) times without the overhead normalization over-weights launch-heavy stages; the test compares against the normalized shares of a fixture.

## Considerations

- A refuted hypothesis is a result. In particular, if the solve is well under 40% the bandwidth and Jacobian arguments weaken, and if collision exceeds 25% scene sampling becomes the first target; both change the optimization pass, not the gates.
- The referee's real-time factor is itself a target row (at least 0.2 for a humanoid on one core, ambition 1.0); the share ambition of 1% on the humanoid needs that ambition on all 36 cores of the Mac.
- The humanoid figure printed in the dynamics chapter for the sampler's share was computed at a device rate that is not a row of the target table (0.5 M world-steps/s, giving 0.5 to 2.4%); this bead uses the row's 1.7 M and prints 0.14 to 0.7%, and both lie in the order-of-magnitude range of 0.1 to 2.4%.
- Shares depend on workload: flat ground with few contacts moves time out of the solve; the register records the workload variant beside each measured value.
