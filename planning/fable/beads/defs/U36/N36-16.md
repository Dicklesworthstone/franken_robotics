## Background

Worlds are cheap in memory; scenes are not. A scene on the batched tiers is a signed distance field sampled on a
grid and stored in bricks, and its size grows with volume over spacing cubed. The memory estimator therefore
needs a second model beside the per-world byte model, and the conclusions that model forces are design rules of
the whole system:

- **One scene per world is impossible.** 8,192 homes at 75 MB each are 614 GB. Scenes are shared: a device holds K
  scene variants over one brick pool and every world indexes one.
- **The two machines have different jobs.** With 12 to 16 GB of a card for scenes, a card holds 160 to 210 homes
  at 2 cm, 20 to 27 at 1 cm, two or three warehouses at 2 cm and none at 1 cm. With 203 GB, the Mac holds about
  2,700 homes at 2 cm, 340 at 1 cm, 42 warehouses at 2 cm, five at 1 cm, and fourteen buildings at 2 cm. Hence
  1 cm warehouses and whole buildings are Mac-only, and the Mac is the world bank.

The arithmetic:

**Dense storage** of a 16-bit field is 2·V/s³ bytes for volume V and spacing s. **A narrow band** keeps a fraction
of about (A/V)·t·s of it, for surface area A and a band of t voxels (8 to 20 with brick rounding).

| Scene (volume) | 1 cm dense | 1 cm band | 2 cm dense | 2 cm band | 4 cm dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home (300 m³) | 0.6 GB | 0.16 to 0.4 GB | 75 MB | 40 to 75 MB | 9 MB |
| Warehouse (19,200 m³) | 38 GB | 6 to 14 GB | 4.8 GB | 1.4 to 3.5 GB | 0.6 GB |
| Six-storey building (57,600 m³) | 115 GB | 10 to 26 GB | 14.4 GB | 2.6 to 6.5 GB | 1.8 GB |

Dense values are arithmetic. Band values rest on assumed surface areas and are HYPOTHESES until the interior
archetypes are compiled (the test that settles each: the brick count of the compiled archetype). The band columns
exclude the per-brick labels, which add 12.5%. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternatives recorded by the plan: copying a
scene per world (the 614 GB above); hashed or succinct brick directories (a dense directory is under 1.5% of the
scene it indexes, and a hash adds a second verifying read to every lookup).

## Scope

In: the scene group of the job estimator, built on the one scene byte model: the two modes (before compile: from
volume, extent, spacing and an area estimate, as a labelled interval; after compile: exact from the scene pack's
recount); the shared-pool accounting for K variants; the capacity statement for a device budget; the slab
entries, the group value `memory.scenes` and the scene-bound repair list that the estimator takes from this bead.
Out, with the bead that has each: the byte arithmetic itself, that is the functions for dense samples and bytes,
band fraction and bytes, labels, coarse-cell counts, directory slot bytes, variant bytes and resident counts, the
recount of a compiled pack, and the tests that pin the printed tables (N19c-16, `crates/frob-scene/src/bytes.rs`;
this bead depends on it, calls it and holds no second copy: `frob-kernels` is one layer above `frob-scene`); the
estimate record, the verb and the fit test over the whole request (BD-219, the estimator's verb: it depends on
this bead and embeds the group, and this bead does not wait for it); the gate record (N36-32's gate test, recorded
by the capstone N36p1-01); the scene compiler and brick pool
themselves (seeds BD-162 to BD-164); the world bank's eviction and admission verb (the scene chapter); camera
buffers (N36-19).

## Technical approach

**Functions (`frob_kernels::estimate::scene`).** The module adds one function of its own and calls the byte
model of `frob-scene` (N19c-16) for everything else:
1. Dense bytes 2·V/s³ (16-bit samples; V in m³, s in m): `frob_scene::bytes::dense_bytes`.
2. Band fraction min(1, (A/V)·t·s) and band bytes: `band_fraction`, `band_bytes`.
3. Labels, × 1.125: the `with_labels` argument of `band_bytes`.
4. Per-variant bytes: `variant_bytes(coarse_cells(extent, 8·s))`, six bytes per coarse cell on the lattice of
   the extent (one 32-bit pool slot and one 16-bit coarse sample). For a 10 × 10 × 3 m interior at 2 cm the
   lattice is 63 × 63 × 19 = 75,411 cells: 452,466 bytes per variant, of which the slots are 301,644 bytes
   (0.3 MB); a 60 × 40 × 8 m warehouse has 587,500 cells at 4 cm and 4,687,500 at 2 cm (slots 2.35 MB and
   18.75 MB; variant bytes 3.5 MB and 28.1 MB).
   DESIGN (the conflict settled, stated identically in N19c-16). An earlier draft of this bead had its own
   `directory_bytes(extent, s)` of four bytes per slot on the lattice, and N19c-16 had six bytes per cell on
   V/S³ (73,242 cells, 0.44 MB for the home). The plan prints both figures, for different things: KA-551 counts
   slots alone on the lattice, to reject hashed directories; KA-462 counts a variant's slot and coarse sample
   from the volume. The byte model now has both cell counts and both byte figures as separate functions, and
   what a device holds per resident variant, and what this estimator charges, is six bytes on the lattice
   count. Four bytes left the coarse samples uncharged; V/S³ is 3% low for the home because 10 m and 3 m are
   not multiples of 16 cm.
5. `pool_bytes(variants)` (this bead's own function): the bricks of all K resident variants after
   deduplication by content hash, plus the per-variant bytes of item 4 for each variant. Before compile the
   deduplication ratio is unknown and taken as 1 (no sharing), which over-estimates; after compile it is exact.
   The charge per brick differs by mode, and both figures are N19c-16's: the predicted mode charges the plan's
   band figure, 1,152 bytes per brick (`band_brick_bytes`: 512 16-bit samples plus the labels' 12.5%), so that
   the planning table stays the plan's; the compiled mode charges what the pool stores, 1,164 bytes per unique
   brick (`pooled_bytes(unique_bricks)`: the brick record's 8-byte palette and each brick's 4-byte metadata word
   on top of the 1,152), which is the length of the pack's pool blob. An admission that charged 1,152 for a
   compiled pack would be 1.04% under the bytes the device holds.

**Two modes.**
- *Predicted* (no compiled pack): inputs V, extent, s, an area estimate A and a band width t; output an interval
  (t from 8 to 20 voxels) labelled HYPOTHESIS, class `Estimated`. The surface areas the table's band columns
  imply are about 1,000 m² for the home, 35,000 m² for the warehouse and 65,000 m² for the building (derived here
  from the table; they are the fixtures of the tests and are replaced by measured areas).
- *Compiled*: `frob_scene::bytes::recount(pack)` gives brick count, label bytes, pooled bytes
  (`pooled_bytes` of the unique bricks), directory slot bytes and variant bytes from the scene pack; the
  estimate charges the pooled bytes and the figure is exact.

**Capacity table.** `scenes_that_fit(budget, scene, mode)` is `frob_scene::bytes::resident_count` applied to
the mode's byte figure: with 12 to 16 GB of a card for scenes: 160 to 213 homes at 2 cm dense (75 MB), 20 to 26
at 1 cm (0.6 GB), 2 to 3 warehouses at 2 cm (4.8 GB), none at 1 cm (38.4 GB). With 203 GB: 2,706 homes at 2 cm,
338 at 1 cm, 42 warehouses at 2 cm, 5 at 1 cm, 14 buildings at 2 cm (14.4 GB). DESIGN (stated in N19c-16 as
well): the fixture budget of the table is the plan's printed column header, exactly 203 × 10⁹ bytes; N19c-16's
draft had divided 203.2 GB (2,709 homes) and the cap computed from the measured limit, 0.85 × 222.7 GiB =
203.25 GB, would give 2,710. One number is pinned so that the two beads cannot differ; a run uses the measured
budget. The table itself is pinned by N19c-16's test `fit_table`.

**What the estimator receives.** This bead exports three things and the estimator (BD-219) calls them:
`scene_slabs(variants)`, the brick pool and directory slabs as entries of the arena layout planner's slab
catalogue, subject to the Mac's largest-buffer split; the group value `SceneMemory`, which the estimate record
prints as `memory.scenes`, apart from `memory.worlds`; and `scene_repairs()`. When a request does not fit because
of scenes, the ranked repairs are: fewer distinct scene variants; a coarser spacing where the task allows it
(spacing is part of the scene identity, so this is a different scene, stated as such); the Mac. Fewer worlds is
not offered: it would not help. The fit test over the whole request and the refusal itself belong to the
estimator.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The predicted mode returns, through the byte model of N19c-16, the dense values of the table: 0.6 GB, 75 MB, 9.4 MB (home); 38.4 GB, 4.8 GB, 0.6 GB (warehouse); 115.2 GB, 14.4 GB, 1.8 GB (building).
- [ ] With the implied areas, the predicted interval is the table's range for t from 8 to 20 voxels; labels add exactly 12.5%.
- [ ] `frob_kernels::estimate::scene` contains no arithmetic of its own for dense, band, label, directory or variant bytes: a source check finds only calls into `frob_scene::bytes` (one model, no second copy).
- [ ] The per-variant charge is six bytes per lattice cell: 452,466 bytes for the 10 × 10 × 3 m home at 2 cm; a four-byte charge is caught.
- [ ] 8,192 homes at 2 cm dense are 614.4 GB; for one scene per world on 8,192 worlds the scene slabs alone exceed every device budget and `scene_repairs()` puts the shared-variant repair first (the verb-level refusal is asserted in BD-219).
- [ ] The capacity table reproduces 160 to 213, 20 to 26, 2 to 3 and 0 whole scenes for a card (the rounded statement "20 to 27" comes from 26.7), and 2,706, 338, 42, 5 and 14 for 203 GB.
- [ ] For a compiled scene pack the estimate equals the pack's own byte counts exactly, its brick term being `pooled_bytes` of the unique bricks (1,164 bytes each, the length of the pool blob) and not the planning figure of 1,152; the comparison with the measured device high-water mark of the scene slabs (within 5%) is a row of the GATE-SPARK-22 record, assembled by the gate test of the script bead (N36-32) from this bead's figures and recorded by the gate's capstone (N36p1-01).
- [ ] Predicted-mode figures are labelled HYPOTHESIS and print as intervals; compiled-mode figures are exact.
- [ ] The group value `memory.scenes` is separate from the per-world group, and the scene-bound repair list never contains fewer worlds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels estimate::scene`:
- `predicted_mode_dense_values`: (300, 0.01) → 6.0·10⁸; (300, 0.02) → 7.5·10⁷; (300, 0.04) → 9.375·10⁶; (19,200, 0.02) → 4.8·10⁹; (57,600, 0.01) → 1.152·10¹¹, each equal to the byte model's value.
- `predicted_mode_interval`: home at 2 cm, A = 1,000 → [40.0 MB, 75 MB] for t in [8, 20], labelled HYPOTHESIS, class `Estimated`; warehouse at 2 cm, A = 35,000 → [1.4 GB, 3.5 GB]; building at 1 cm, A = 65,000 → [10.4 GB, 26 GB].
- `variant_charge_is_six_bytes_on_lattice`: 452,466 bytes for the home; `pool_bytes` of K = 4 undeduplicated copies is four times the band bytes plus 4 × 452,466.
- `pool_bytes_dedup`: two variants sharing 95% of their bricks cost one pack plus 5% plus two variant records.
- `no_second_byte_model` (source check).
- `one_scene_per_world_is_614_gb`: the scene slabs of 8,192 private homes exceed every device budget.
- `capacity_table_card_and_mac`: the counts above through `resident_count`.
- `compiled_mode_is_exact` (a small generated scene pack; fixture labelled `synthetic`).
- `compiled_mode_charges_pooled_bytes`: a pack of 1,000 unique bricks is charged 1,164,000 bytes of bricks in
  compiled mode and equals the length of its pool blob; the same brick count in predicted mode is 1,152,000.
- `scene_refusal_repairs_exclude_fewer_worlds`.
The tests of the byte functions themselves are N19c-16's, in `crates/frob-scene/tests/bytes.rs` (`dense_table`,
`band_fractions`, `band_1cm`, `labels_add_one_eighth`, `variant_sizes_from_volume`,
`directory_slots_on_lattice`, `fit_table`, `spacing_doubling_divides_by_eight`); they are not repeated here.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh` (N36-32), step `scenes`: `frob scene compile` on the source geometry of one
small interior (a public fixture; at M2 `scene.gen` generates terrain only), then `frob sim estimate` with 1, 4
and 16 variants of it and `frob sim run` at the same counts, each run under its own `--key`;
compares `memory.scenes` with the measured scene slabs. Logged: variant count, brick count, pool bytes, directory
bytes, estimate, measured mark, relative difference against 0.05. Only public fixtures and generated scenes are
used; no private scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The dense table and the capacity counts (arithmetic); CX-381 (memory limits distinct scenes and camera frames, not worlds).
- Negative control: an estimator that charges the scene once per world must exceed every budget on the 8,192-world request and is caught by `one_scene_per_world_is_614_gb` being a refusal, not an admission.
- Negative control: an estimator that omits the labels under-estimates a compiled pack by one ninth of its total and fails the 5% check.
- Negative control: an estimator that charges four bytes per coarse cell for a variant (slots without coarse samples) fails `variant_charge_is_six_bytes_on_lattice`.
- Negative control: an estimator that charges a compiled pack the planning figure of 1,152 bytes per brick fails `compiled_mode_charges_pooled_bytes` (12 bytes per unique brick short of the pool blob).
- Metamorphic: doubling the spacing divides the predicted dense figure by eight (the function-level relation is N19c-16's).

## Considerations

- The band fraction formula is first-order; thin or cluttered geometry raises the effective area, which is why predicted-mode figures are intervals and are replaced by compiled counts as soon as a pack exists.
- Deduplication between variants (the brick pool's sharing ratio) is measured by the scene chapter's kill gate; a ratio below 1.5 on the archetype battery makes dense per-variant fields the fallback, and this model then uses ratio 1.
- A card's "12 to 16 GB for scenes" presumes few worlds and no rollout storage; the estimator computes the split for the actual request instead of assuming it.
- Scene traffic also matters for throughput (collision sampling reads the field every step); that is the bytes-touched figure's concern, not this model's.
