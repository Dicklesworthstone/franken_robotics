## Background

Method rule: **every estimate is an interval.** `sim.estimate` returns a calibrated interval for time and an
exact figure for memory, never a point for time; its calibration is per hardware fingerprint and is redone when
the fingerprint digest changes. A point estimate of run time on a shared machine is false precision, and the
systems that consume the estimate need its upper end: the daemon admits a job only if the upper end fits the
remaining budget (an underestimate wastes a whole budget stop, since a stopped job publishes nothing; an
overestimate costs only a refusal the agent can answer with a smaller question); the compute-envelope check uses
the upper end; the fleet scheduler orders jobs by estimated work.

The interval is **conformal**: it is built from the actual errors of past predictions, makes no assumption about
their distribution, and has a finite-sample coverage guarantee under exchangeability. GATE-SPARK-22 requires that
time estimates fall inside their intervals on at least 90% of held-out runs. Tier: Solid. Rejected alternatives:
a parametric error model (run times on a loaded host are heavy-tailed); a fixed safety factor everywhere (too
wide where the model is good, too narrow where it is not); a point estimate with a "typical error" note.

## Scope

In: the point model; the strata; the residual store; the interval at two levels; the `uncalibrated` state;
recalibration on a fingerprint change; the held-out coverage check. Out: the memory model (BD-219); the
daemon-wide calibration for every verb and budget admission (BD-297); the scheduler.

## Technical approach

**Point model ŷ (seconds of wall-clock from submission to completion; internal, never printed alone).**

    ŷ = t_start + n_chunks·t_sync + S/T(W) + n_updates·t_learn + t_end

- t_start: compilation or cache load (from the fingerprint: cold seconds per kernel set, cache-hit milliseconds),
  admission, upload, launch-graph capture; the cold or warm branch is chosen from the cache state.
- S: world-steps requested (worlds × simulated seconds ÷ h); T(W): the throughput at world count W from the
  harness's size sweep (measured at 1, 8, 64, 256 worlds and upward), interpolated in log-log between measured
  sizes for this workload class, backend and fingerprint. A world count outside the measured range is
  `extrapolated` and treated as uncalibrated.
- n_chunks·t_sync: one host synchronization per chunk.
- n_updates·t_learn: for training, the learner's time per update (policy forward, backward, optimizer).
- t_end: final readback and teardown.
Every term is read from recorded measurements keyed by the fingerprint digest; a missing term makes the estimate
`uncalibrated`, not zero.

**Strata.** One stratum per (verb, tier, device family, shape class) and fingerprint digest. A driver, compiler
or kernel-version change starts a new stratum, since exchangeability across it is not credible.

**The interval, numbered.**
1. For each finished job the store appends the absolute log residual r = |ln(y/ŷ)|, y the measured wall time.
   The store keeps the last n residuals per stratum (DESIGN: a window of 200).
2. At miscoverage α the half-width q_α is the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest residual, and the interval is
   [ŷ·e^(−q_α), ŷ·e^(q_α)]. Under exchangeability of the residuals within a stratum this covers the actual time
   with probability at least 1 − α (the split-conformal rank argument).
3. The index exists only for n ≥ 9 at α = 0.10 and n ≥ 19 at α = 0.05, because ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉ must not exceed
   n. A stratum is called **calibrated from n = 19**, so one stratum serves both levels.
4. Below that the record marks the interval `uncalibrated` and uses a fixed factor of 2: [ŷ/2, 2·ŷ] (DESIGN,
   conservative).
5. **Two levels are reported (DESIGN).** The headline interval `lo_s … hi_s` is the 95% one (α = 0.05); admission,
   the envelope class and the gate use it. The 90% interval (`lo90_s … hi90_s`), which is what the daemon's
   general budget rule reports, is carried beside it for scheduling order. Reason for the wider headline: an
   interval with exactly 90% coverage lands below 90% on about half of all finite held-out sets, so a gate that
   asks for "at least 90% inside" needs an interval with margin; the 95% interval gives about two and a half
   standard errors of margin at 100 held-out runs, and erring wide only costs refusals.
6. Device-seconds are the same interval times the device count.

**Recalibration.** When the fingerprint digest changes, the old stratum is closed (kept for the record) and the
new one starts empty and `uncalibrated`. The size sweep and the fixed-overhead measurement are re-run before the
stratum can become calibrated.

**Held-out coverage check (the third clause of GATE-SPARK-22).** Calibration runs and held-out runs are disjoint
and declared (as hashed job lists) before either is run. For each device family: at least 100 held-out runs
(DESIGN) spanning the quadruped and humanoid reference workloads, world counts from 64 to 8,192, durations from
2 to 120 simulated seconds, cold and warm starts, physics-only and training jobs. Coverage = the fraction whose
measured wall time lies inside the headline interval estimated beforehand. Pass: at least 0.90. The coverage of
the 90% interval is recorded beside it and is not gated.

**Record.** `time {lo_s, hi_s, level: "0.95", lo90_s, hi90_s, calibrated: true|false, n, stratum, extrapolated}`.
There is no field for ŷ. Class `Estimated`; the estimator's intervals are registered predictions and are scored
when the job finishes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The estimate never contains a single time value: the schema has interval ends and no point field, and a schema test enforces it.
- [ ] Index arithmetic: n = 19, α = 0.10 → the 18th smallest; n = 9 → the 9th; n = 8 → no index (uncalibrated); α = 0.05: n = 19 → the 19th; n = 18 → no index.
- [ ] A stratum with fewer than 19 residuals prints `calibrated: false` and the factor-2 interval.
- [ ] A changed fingerprint digest starts a new, uncalibrated stratum; the old residuals are not used.
- [ ] Coverage clause of GATE-SPARK-22: on the declared held-out set, at least 90% of runs fall inside their headline intervals, per device family, with the counts for both levels recorded.
- [ ] A world count outside the swept range is flagged `extrapolated` and uncalibrated.
- [ ] Admission paths (budget, envelope) read `hi_s`; a test shows a job with ŷ of 50 minutes and an upper end of 70 minutes is refused under a one-hour limit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels estimate::time`:
- `conformal_index`: the five cases of the acceptance list.
- `interval_from_fixture`: residuals 0.01, 0.02, …, 0.19 (n = 19): α = 0.10 → q = 0.18, and ŷ = 100 s gives [83.5, 119.7] s; α = 0.05 → q = 0.19, [82.7, 120.9] s.
- `uncalibrated_factor_two`: n = 5, ŷ = 100 s → [50, 200] s, `calibrated: false`.
- `window_keeps_last_n`.
- `point_model_terms`: S = 8,192 × 10 s ÷ 0.002 s = 40,960,000 world-steps at T = 2.0 M/s → 20.48 s; plus t_start 0.9 s warm, 5 chunks × 2 ms, t_end 0.05 s → ŷ = 21.44 s.
- `cold_branch_uses_compile_seconds`.
- `size_curve_interpolation`: T(64) = 0.4 M, T(256) = 1.2 M → T(128) = 0.693 M (geometric interpolation).
- `extrapolation_flagged`.
- `new_fingerprint_new_stratum`.
- `coverage_on_synthetic_exchangeable_residuals`: over 2,000 simulated strata with n = 19 the mean coverage is 0.90 ± 0.01 for the 90% interval and 0.95 ± 0.01 for the 95% interval (fixture labelled `synthetic`).
- `no_point_field_in_schema`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh` (N36-32), steps `calibrate` and `heldout`: runs the declared calibration
set, then for each held-out job calls `frob sim estimate … --json`, stores both intervals, runs the job and
compares. Logged per job: stratum, n, calibrated flag, the interval ends at both levels, the measured wall time,
inside or outside; per device family the coverage against 0.90.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-826 (time clause): at least 90% of held-out runs inside their intervals. The conformal index arithmetic is exact.
- Negative controls registered against GATE-SPARK-22: an estimator that calibrates on the held-out runs themselves (detected because the two job lists are declared and hashed beforehand); an interval of zero width around ŷ (coverage collapses); a store that mixes two fingerprints in one stratum (a planted 30% slowdown under a new digest drops coverage and must be reported, not absorbed).
- Metamorphic: scaling every measured time and every model term by a constant leaves coverage unchanged.

## Considerations

- The choice of a 95% headline interval is this bead's reading of the gate and departs from the daemon rule's "reports the 90% interval"; both are carried, nothing is hidden, and the reading is recorded for the gate's owner in this unit's coverage file. If the owner prefers the 90% interval as the headline, the gate's literal criterion becomes a coin flip and should be restated with a finite-sample allowance; the interval is never widened after seeing held-out results.
- Exchangeability fails when the host's load regime changes (`trj` is often loaded). The estimator records host load with each residual, and a stratum whose recent residuals drift is flagged; it does not model load.
- The first runs on a new machine are all `uncalibrated` with a factor of 2, which makes admission conservative exactly when the least is known.
- The daemon's M3 work generalizes this to every verb and to budgets; this bead's residual store uses the same record so nothing is recomputed.
