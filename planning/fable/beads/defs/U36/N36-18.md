## Background

**The observation** (a derived estimate; a HYPOTHESIS until GATE-SPARK-22 measures bytes touched per world-step):
at the state sizes of the reference workloads an RTX 4090's physics step is bound by memory bandwidth, not by
arithmetic.

- FACT (source register, to be re-read): an RTX 4090 has about 1,008 GB/s of memory bandwidth and 72 MB of L2
  cache; the M5 Ultra has 1.2 TB/s, shared with its CPU.
- A batch outgrows the L2 cache above about 2,100 worlds of the quadruped workload Q (about 35 kB per world) or
  750 of the humanoid workload H (about 97 kB), so at 8,192 worlds every kernel streams from device memory.
- If a world-step touches B bytes, the ceiling is η·(bandwidth)/B with an efficiency η of 0.5 to 0.8
  (assumption). With an assumed traffic of 100 to 500 kB for Q and 0.2 to 1.5 MB for H, the ceilings are 1 to 8 M
  and 0.3 to 4 M world-steps/s. Arithmetic is not the bound: 1 to 3·10⁵ operations per step of Q at 5 to 20% of
  the card's peak rate would allow 14 to 165 M world-steps/s.
- **The estimate is contradicted in its middle**: its central humanoid ceiling, 0.7 to 1.1 M, is below the 2.55 M
  that an incumbent measures on the same class of card. Either a step touches fewer bytes than assumed or η is
  higher. That is why the quantity is measured and not assumed.

Consequences (DESIGN):

1. **Bytes touched per world-step is a first-class figure**, measured per launch and reported in the disclosure
   block. The ambition of 4.0 M world-steps/s for Q allows at most 126 to 202 kB per world-step.
2. **The dense Jacobian is the first suspect.** At 11.5 kB (4·R·n for R = 160, n = 18), three passes in each of
   four Newton iterations are 138 kB, the whole budget. Profile-gated remedies, each with a behaviour proof: a
   fused assemble, factor and solve chain; storing a contact row only over the columns it touches (the base and
   one leg); a line search that does not re-read the Jacobian. They are tested at sizes 18, 30 and 60.
3. **No order of magnitude is available per step**: an incumbent already measures 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s
   on this class of card.
4. **On Metal the bound is elsewhere.** The Metal bet's threshold, a quarter of 2.0 M world-steps/s of Q at an
   assumed 240 kB per step, needs 120 GB/s, a tenth of the chip's bandwidth. Command-buffer overhead and
   arithmetic rate are what the fingerprint must measure.

Tier: Solid for the measurement; the observation itself is a HYPOTHESIS this bead settles.

## Technical approach

**The figure.** For launch ℓ of the step plan, B_ℓ = Σ over buffers of (elements read + elements written) ×
element size, evaluated from the kernel manifest's footprints at the run's *actual* active counts (worlds, active
contacts, active rows, links at the level), not at capacities. B = Σ_ℓ B_ℓ × (launches of ℓ per world-step) ÷
worlds. The backend accumulates the active counts it already has in the statistics ring; the host evaluates B per
chunk and stores per-launch and total figures in the run receipt and the disclosure block
(`bytes_touched_per_world_step`). Cost target: no extra device work. The figure is a function of the step plan
and the active counts only, never of timing or launch structure: a profiling-mode run (one synchronized launch
or one batch per kernel, N21a-27) and a normal run of the same plan and seed carry the same per-launch table,
keyed by kernel. The receipt's table has, per launch: stage, kernel identity, bytes read and bytes written per
world-step, and launches per world-step, which is what the step profile's per-kernel rows copy.

**Validation.** On `batch-cpu` the access recorder counts real loads and stores for the same step plan and
workload. The footprint-derived figure must be at least the recorded figure (a footprint that under-declares is a
manifest defect) and within 2% of it (DESIGN) for kernels whose footprints are exact; a kernel whose footprint is
a conservative range is listed with its slack.

**Functions (`frob_conformance::perf::bandwidth`).**
- `l2_crossover_worlds(l2_bytes, bytes_per_world)`: 72 MB ÷ 34.6 kB ≈ 2,080 (printed "about 2,100"); 72 MB ÷
  96.7 kB ≈ 745 ("about 750").
- `ceiling(bandwidth, B, η) = η·bandwidth/B`.
- `budget(bandwidth, target_rate, η) = η·bandwidth/target_rate`: at 4.0 M world-steps/s and 1,008 GB/s, 126 kB
  (η = 0.5) to 202 kB (η = 0.8).
- `efficiency(measured_rate, B, bandwidth) = measured_rate·B/bandwidth`, the measured η, using the fingerprint's
  measured bandwidth.
- `jacobian_traffic(R, n, passes, iterations) = 4·R·n·passes·iterations`: 4 × 160 × 18 × 3 × 4 = 138,240 bytes.
- `citation_label(ceiling_citation) -> Label`: a bandwidth ceiling η·bandwidth/B may be cited as measured only
  when its B is the `bytes_touched_per_world_step` of a run receipt named in the citation; a ceiling whose B is
  an assumed traffic keeps the label HYPOTHESIS, and a citation of it without that label is rejected with the
  finding `bandwidth-ceiling-unlabelled`. The generated documentation and the disclosure block's notes print a
  ceiling only through this function.

**The hypothesis test, numbered.**
1. Read B per launch for Q and H at 8,192 worlds on each device, and the size curve at world counts on both
   sides of the L2 crossover.
2. Compute η_measured = T·B/bandwidth.
3. Verdict per (workload, device): `bandwidth-bound` if η_measured ≥ 0.5 and the per-world step time rises across
   the crossover; `not-bandwidth-bound` if η_measured < 0.5 at 8,192 worlds; `inconclusive` otherwise. The verdict
   and its inputs replace the HYPOTHESIS label wherever the observation is cited.
4. On Metal, also report the share of step time in command-buffer commit and wait (from the fingerprint's launch
   costs and the run's launch count), since the bound there is expected to be overhead, not bandwidth.

**Jacobian-traffic microbenchmarks.** Three variants of the solve path against the staged baseline, each at
n = 18, 30 and 60 degrees of freedom with row capacities scaled as in the workloads, on `batch-cpu` and on each
device: (a) fused assemble, factor and solve; (b) contact rows stored only over the columns they touch (for a
legged robot the six base columns and one leg's columns); (c) a line search that caches the Jacobian products it
needs. For each: bytes touched per world-step (footprints), time per world-step (paired against the baseline),
and the behaviour proof (identical per-world digests where the variant claims bit equality; otherwise the
fidelity battery). Before a variant is measured its ceiling record `frob.perf.ceiling/1` is filed with N36-05's
`ceiling` and passed to `admit_experiment`, the one place `FROB-REF-866` is raised: a variant whose traffic
reduction predicts a ceiling below its goal is not funded and is not measured. The results are the inputs of the
optimization ledger entries.

**Evidence.** B is class `Tested` (computed from declared footprints validated against recorded accesses). The
verdict is a recorded test of a registered hypothesis.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every run receipt and disclosure block from M2 carries `bytes_touched_per_world_step` per launch and in total; the fourth clause of GATE-SPARK-22 ("bytes touched per world-step are measured per launch") is recorded for Q and H on each device.
- [ ] On `batch-cpu` the footprint-derived figure is at least the access recorder's and within 2% for exact-footprint kernels.
- [ ] KA-822: the L2 crossover is about 2,100 worlds of Q and 750 of H; 4.0 M world-steps/s allows 126 to 202 kB per world-step on 1,008 GB/s; the Jacobian traffic is 138 kB; a quarter of 2.0 M world-steps/s at 240 kB needs 120 GB/s.
- [ ] The hypothesis verdict (`bandwidth-bound`, `not-bandwidth-bound` or `inconclusive`) is recorded per workload and device with η_measured, and the observation's HYPOTHESIS label is replaced by it.
- [ ] The three microbenchmark variants are measured at n = 18, 30 and 60 with traffic, paired time ratio and behaviour proof, and each has a ceiling record.
- [ ] A planted kernel that reads a buffer twice raises B by that buffer's bytes and is visible per launch.
- [ ] A bandwidth ceiling is printed as measured only with a run receipt's bytes-touched figure behind it; the planted citation `unmeasured-bandwidth-ceiling` is rejected, and every ceiling in generated documentation that rests on assumed traffic carries the label HYPOTHESIS.
- [ ] The per-launch table of a profiling-mode run equals that of a normal run of the same step plan and seed;
      no other crate evaluates footprints into bytes (the step profile of N21a-27 reads this table).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::bandwidth` and `cargo test -p frob-kernels traffic`:
- `l2_crossover`: 72·10⁶ ÷ 34,556 = 2,083; 72·10⁶ ÷ 96,704 = 744.
- `budget_at_ambition`: 0.5 × 1.008·10¹² ÷ 4.0·10⁶ = 126,000 bytes; 0.8 → 201,600 bytes.
- `ceilings_from_assumed_traffic`: Q: 0.5 × 1.008·10¹² ÷ 500,000 = 1.0 M and 0.8 × 1.008·10¹² ÷ 100,000 = 8.1 M; H: ÷ 1.5 MB → 0.34 M and ÷ 0.2 MB → 4.0 M.
- `jacobian_traffic`: 138,240 bytes for Q; 4 × 320 × 35 × 3 × 4 = 537,600 bytes for H.
- `metal_requirement`: 0.25 × 2.0·10⁶ × 240,000 = 1.2·10¹¹ bytes/s = 120 GB/s, one tenth of 1.2 TB/s.
- `arithmetic_is_not_the_bound`: 3·10⁵ operations per step at 5% of a peak rate of 8.3·10¹³ operations/s → 13.8 M; 10⁵ at 20% → 166 M.
- `bytes_from_footprints`: a synthetic plan with two launches reading 100 bytes and writing 40 bytes per world each → 280 bytes per world-step.
- `active_counts_not_capacities`: half the rows active halves the row-indexed terms.
- `independent_of_launch_structure`: the same synthetic plan run as one captured graph and as one launch per kernel reports the same per-kernel table and the same 280 bytes per world-step.
- `verdict_rules`: η = 0.6 with a rising curve → `bandwidth-bound`; η = 0.2 → `not-bandwidth-bound`.
- `efficiency_contradiction_fixture`: T = 2.55 M, bandwidth 1,008 GB/s, B = 0.85 MB → η = 2.15, flagged impossible (η > 1 means B is over-stated), which is the published contradiction as a test.
- `unmeasured_ceiling_keeps_hypothesis_label`: the planted citation `unmeasured-bandwidth-ceiling` (the central humanoid ceiling of 0.7 to 1.1 M world-steps/s, from an assumed traffic of 0.2 to 1.5 MB, cited with no receipt and no label) is rejected with `bandwidth-ceiling-unlabelled`; the same citation labelled HYPOTHESIS is accepted, and one whose B names a run receipt's measured figure is accepted as measured.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh` (N36-32), step `bytes_touched`: `frob sim run --workload Q|H --tier <t>
--worlds 8192 --seconds 5 --seed <s> --key bt-<W>-<t>-<s> --json` on each device; asserts the per-launch table is present and sums to the total; computes

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-822 (the arithmetic; inputs are a HYPOTHESIS until measured). CX-382 (a derived bandwidth ceiling used as a planning figure): the contradiction fixture above. Its control in the counterexample registry N36-CX is the planted input `unmeasured-bandwidth-ceiling`, built by this bead: the `synthetic` fixture `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/perf/unmeasured-bandwidth-ceiling.json`, a citation of a bandwidth ceiling with no measured bytes-touched figure and no HYPOTHESIS label, which `unmeasured_ceiling_keeps_hypothesis_label` must reject.
- Negative controls: the double-read kernel; a backend that evaluates footprints at capacities instead of active counts (over-states B on a half-empty workload and fails `active_counts_not_capacities`); an under-declared footprint (caught by the access recorder).
- Determinism: B is an exact function of the step plan and the active counts; identical across repeats of one seed.

## Considerations

- The card's peak arithmetic rate is implied by the plan's own figures (about 8.3·10¹³ single-precision operations per second) and is not in the source register; it is re-read from a primary source before any claim rests on it.
- Footprint accounting counts every byte a kernel may touch at its active counts; cache reuse inside a launch makes the true traffic lower, so η_measured is an upper estimate of efficiency. A decision that needs the traffic to be large is safe; one that needs it small should use the microbenchmarks.
- The path-table Jacobian (a contact row stores only the coordinates on its support path) is remedy (b) in its general form and is another bead's default once digests match; this bead measures its traffic.
- If the verdict is `not-bandwidth-bound`, the optimization pass turns to launch overhead and arithmetic, and the Jacobian remedies lose priority; that is the point of measuring first.
