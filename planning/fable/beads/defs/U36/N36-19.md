## Background

Camera frames are the one per-world quantity that does bind memory. The arithmetic:

- A 640 × 480 frame with 4 bytes of colour and 4 bytes of depth per pixel is 2,457,600 bytes.
- Two such cameras on 256 worlds are 1.17 GiB per frame set (1,258,291,200 bytes); on 8,192 worlds, 37.5 GiB,
  more than a card holds.
- With 16 GB usable a card holds at most about 3,200 two-camera worlds before any physics state.
- A 64 × 64 depth image in `f32` is 16,384 bytes: two per world on 8,192 worlds are 0.27 GB.
- Exposure-time and rolling-shutter bins multiply the ray work, not the stored frame.

Compare with the physics state: a quadruped world is about 35 kB, so one two-camera frame set at 640 × 480 is
about 140 worlds' worth of state per world. A user who sizes a camera task by the world counts of a
proprioceptive task will be refused, and the system must say why and what to do.

Rules (DESIGN):

1. Frame buffers are slabs of the device arena like any other and enter `sim.estimate`.
2. A camera's resolution and cadence are part of the task identity, because changing the cadence changes the
   control problem, so neither is a performance knob.
3. Camera-heavy evaluation belongs on the Mac (large unified memory), not on the training cards.

Tier: Solid. Rejected alternative: letting the engine lower resolution or cadence under memory pressure (it would
silently change what the policy observes, and two results would stop being comparable).

## Scope

In: the frame-memory functions; the slab entries for frame buffers; the estimate group `memory.frames`; the
refusal repairs for camera-bound requests; the identity rule's test. Out: the estimate record, the verb and the

## Technical approach

**Functions (`frob_kernels::estimate::frames`).**
- `frame_bytes(width, height, bytes_per_pixel)`: 640 × 480 × 8 = 2,457,600; 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384.
- `frame_set_bytes(cameras[], worlds) = worlds × Σ frame_bytes(camera)`.
- `worlds_that_fit(budget, cameras[]) = ⌊budget ÷ Σ frame_bytes⌋`: 16·10⁹ ÷ 4,915,200 = 3,255 (printed "about
  3,200").
- Stored frames per camera: one current frame per world. A sensor history or a rollout buffer of frames is
  charged explicitly as `history × frame_set_bytes`; the model never assumes it away.
- Exposure and rolling-shutter bins: a camera with b time bins traces b times the rays and stores one frame;
  the function takes b only for the time model, not for bytes.

**Slabs.** `frame_slabs(cameras, worlds_padded)` returns the entries the estimator adds to the arena layout
planner's slab catalogue. For each camera of the task's sensor layout the arena catalogue gets one slab of
`worlds_padded × frame_bytes` per stored frame, with a live interval covering the sensor stage through the
observation stage, subject to the largest-single-buffer split on the Mac (a 37.5 GiB frame set is under the
167.0 GiB limit; larger sets are split per camera, then per world range).

**Estimate group.** `memory.frames { per_world_bytes, total_bytes, cameras[] }`, separate from `memory.worlds` and
`memory.scenes`.

**Refusal repairs.** When a request does not fit because of frames (`FROB-REF-602` at admission), the ranked
repairs are: (1) fewer worlds, with the largest world count that fits named; (2) the Mac, with the count that
fits its budget; (3) a task with lower-resolution cameras, stated as a *different task* by its identity. The
repairs never include "lower the resolution of this task" or "render every k-th tick": those are not offered
because they change the task.

**Identity rule.** Camera resolution, channel layout and cadence are fields of the sensor layout, whose identity
is hashed into the task identity. A run request that overrides any of them is a request for a different task and
gets a different identity; there is no run-time flag for them. The test below is the fence.

**Evidence.** Frame bytes are exact arithmetic on the sensor layout; class `Estimated` labelled exact-by-model,
checked against measured high-water marks with the rest of GATE-SPARK-22.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] KA-817: 640 × 480 at 8 bytes per pixel is 2,457,600 bytes; two cameras on 256 worlds are 1,258,291,200 bytes (1.17 GiB); on 8,192 worlds 40,265,318,400 bytes (37.5 GiB); 16 GB hold at most 3,255 two-camera worlds; a 64 × 64 `f32` depth image is 16,384 bytes and two per world on 8,192 worlds are 268,435,456 bytes (0.27 GB).
- [ ] The group value for a two-camera task is separate from the per-world group; for 8,192 worlds the frame slabs alone exceed a 24 GB card's budget and the repair list holds the three repairs in the stated order (the verb-level `fits: false` is asserted in BD-219).
- [ ] For a camera task that fits, the measured device high-water mark is within 5% of the estimate at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds (rows of the GATE-SPARK-22 record, assembled by the gate test of the script bead N36-32 from this bead's figures and recorded by the gate's capstone N36p1-01).
- [ ] Changing a camera's resolution or cadence changes the task identity; no run-time option changes either without changing the identity.
- [ ] The number of exposure or rolling-shutter bins changes the time estimate and not the byte estimate.
- [ ] A frame history of k frames multiplies `memory.frames` by k and is printed as such.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-kernels estimate::frames`:
- `frame_bytes_known_answers`: 2,457,600 and 16,384.
- `frame_sets`: 2 × 256 × 2,457,600 = 1,258,291,200; 2 × 8,192 × 2,457,600 = 40,265,318,400; 2 × 8,192 × 16,384 = 268,435,456.
- `worlds_that_fit_card`: ⌊16·10⁹ ÷ 4,915,200⌋ = 3,255; with the Mac's 203 GB: 41,300.
- `bins_do_not_change_bytes`: 1 bin and 8 bins give equal bytes.
- `history_multiplies_bytes`.
- `repairs_never_touch_resolution_or_cadence`: the repair list of a camera-bound refusal contains no edit of the sensor layout of the same task.
- `resolution_changes_task_identity`; `cadence_changes_task_identity`.
- `frames_vs_state`: one two-camera frame set per world is 4,915,200 bytes against 34,556 bytes of quadruped state (a ratio of 142).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh` (N36-32), step `cameras`: `frob sim estimate` and `frob sim run` for a
64 × 64 depth task at 8,192 worlds and a two-camera 640 × 480 task at 256 worlds on each device; asserts the frame
group and the 5% agreement; then requests the 640 × 480 task at 8,192 worlds on a card and asserts the refusal,
its repairs and that nothing was allocated. Logged: cameras, per-world and total frame bytes, estimate, measured
mark, the refusal envelope.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-817 (exact). CX-381 (memory limits distinct scenes and camera frames, not worlds).
- Negative control: an estimator that charges one frame per device instead of per world fits the 8,192-world request and is caught by the refusal assertion.
- Negative control: a build with a `--camera-stride` run option (render every k-th tick) must fail `cadence_changes_task_identity`.
- Metamorphic: doubling width and height multiplies frame bytes by four.

## Considerations

- "16 GB usable" on a 24 GB card is the plan's assumption; the fingerprint's free-memory reading replaces it at admission.
- Colour at 4 bytes and depth at 4 bytes per pixel is the stored format assumed by the arithmetic; a sensor layout with other channel sizes changes the figure through `bytes_per_pixel`, never through a hidden conversion.
- Camera worlds also change where time goes (ray work per frame times the bins); that belongs to the time model and the profile.
- The Mac's role as the place for camera-heavy evaluation depends on its Metal throughput being adequate for evaluation, which is measured by the Metal row and its kill gate; memory alone says only that the frames fit.
