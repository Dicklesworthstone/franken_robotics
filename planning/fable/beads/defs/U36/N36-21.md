## Background

Three of the target rows are **end-to-end** quantities. They are defined and gated by the ten-fold test, not by
the performance program; they are listed in the performance table because the program measures their engine-side
inputs and owns none of their thresholds except where stated.

| # | Quantity | Definition | What the performance program measures | TARGET | Gated in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | Edit to first step | Wall-clock from acceptance of a typed design edit to completion of the first device world-step of the edited design, at the 50th and 95th percentiles | Compilation or cache hit, variant admission, graph capture, first chunk | Tolerance at the 95th percentile, with the ambition after it. (a) A parameter edit inside a shape class: 0.5 s; 0.1 s; no compile and no capture. (b) A topology edit inside an existing bucket: 2 s; 0.5 s. (c) An edit that needs a new shape class: 5 s with a warm cache; the cold time is reported, not targeted | The ten-fold test; (a) and (b) also at the variants-on-devices gate |
| 21 | Designs decided per hour | The ten-fold test's | Distinct designs screened per device-second, derived from rows 1 and 10: at 16 worlds and 10 s per design (80,000 world-steps), 17 at the tolerances (2.0 M × 0.70) and 43 at the ambitions (4.0 M × 0.86) | None of its own; a derived figure | The ten-fold test |
| 22 | Time to a qualified design | Restricted mean time to an independently accepted answer | The phase ledger: compilation, admission, screening, training, evaluation, promotion, qualification and waiting, per device and per agent turn | None here | The ten-fold test |

FACT for row 20 (source register, to be re-read): no incumbent publishes this latency. An incumbent model edit
needs a host compile, a model upload and a new graph capture; one incumbent's cold kernel compilation takes about
96 s on an RTX 6000 Ada; an idle-host compile of one kernel in the sibling suite's renderer takes about 5 s. The
incumbent's figure is measured in the parity baseline.

A *shape class* is the set of capacities one static step plan is built for; a *bucket* is one shape class on one
device. A parameter edit changes numbers inside a model table; a topology edit changes the tree; either may or may
not need a new shape class.

Why this bead exists: without the engine-side split, the ten-fold ledger's `compile` and `screen` phases are
opaque totals, and when a ratio disappoints nobody can say which part of the engine lost it. The third job of the
performance program is to supply the phase ledger from which the ten-fold test is computed. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the phase split of an edit-to-first-step measurement; the derived row-21 figure; the engine-side phase
intervals of every job, per device and per agent turn; their export in the ten-fold ledger's vocabulary. Out: the
ledger schema, attribution rules and ratio (the ten-fold harness); measuring our edit latency and its gate
(BD-184); the incumbent's edit latency (BD-040).

## Technical approach

**Row 20: the phase split.** For each timed edit the harness records the intervals, in order: `accept` (the edit
is accepted and has a new design identity), `compile_or_hit` (model compile with memoized subterms, or cache hit),
`lower` (to a model table), `admit` (variant admission into a free slot, or a new bucket's buffers), `capture`
(launch-graph capture; zero for edits inside a shape class), `first_chunk` (to completion of the first device
world-step of the edited design). Their sum is the row's latency. The record states the edit class (a, b or c),
the cache state (warm or cold), and asserts the structural claims: class (a) has `compile_or_hit` as a hit and
`capture = 0` ("no compile and no capture"); class (b) has `capture = 0`; class (c) has exactly one capture.
Percentiles are taken over at least 200 edits per class (DESIGN) drawn from the mutation corpus by a seeded
stream; the 50th and 95th are reported with the split of the median edit and of the 95th-percentile edit, since
the tail usually has a different cause than the median.

**Row 21: the derived figure.** `designs_per_device_second(rate, mixed_ratio, worlds, seconds, h)` =
rate × mixed_ratio ÷ (worlds × seconds ÷ h). With 16 worlds, 10 s and h = 2 ms a design costs 80,000 world-steps:
2.0 M × 0.70 ÷ 80,000 = 17.5 (printed 17) at the tolerances and 4.0 M × 0.86 ÷ 80,000 = 43 at the ambitions.
Computed again from measured values of rows 1 and 10 once they exist, and labelled *derived*; it has no threshold
of its own and is never cited as a measurement of designs decided, which needs the whole loop.

**Row 22: engine-side intervals.** Every job's receipt already attributes its wall-clock to engine phases. This
bead maps them onto the ten-fold ledger's phases and exports them per device and per agent turn:

| Engine interval | Ledger phase |
|---|---|
| Compilation or cache lookup, model upload, variant admission, graph capture, scene compile, up to the first device step | `compile` |
| Rollouts on rungs that train nothing | `screen` |
| Policy training, fine-tuning and distillation, with the learner's share recorded in the resource table | `train` |
| Evaluation batteries, comparisons, promotion to a higher tier | `evaluate` |
| The blinded confirmation before submission | `qualify` |
| Queueing, device transfer, idle time | `wait` |

Export: for a job, a list of intervals (start, end, phase, device, device-seconds, core-seconds) appended to the
run's ledger stream; the ten-fold harness derives its exclusive and resource tables from them. The exported
intervals of one job must sum to its submission-to-completion wall-clock within 1% (tighter than the ledger's 2%
closure, so that engine accounting is never the reason a ledger is flagged open).

**Evidence.** The split and the intervals are class `Tested` measurements under the run's disclosure block; the
row-21 figure is class `Estimated` (derived).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every edit-to-first-step measurement carries the six-interval split, the edit class and the cache state; class (a) shows a cache hit and zero capture, class (b) zero capture, class (c) one capture, and a measurement that violates its class's structure is rejected.
- [ ] Percentiles at the 50th and 95th are reported per class with the split of the median and tail edits; the cold time of class (c) is reported and not judged.
- [ ] KA-824: 17 designs per device-second at the tolerances and 43 at the ambitions for 16 worlds and 10 s per design; the same function on measured rows 1 and 10 prints the measured figure labelled *derived*.
- [ ] Every job's engine intervals are exported in the ledger's phase vocabulary, per device and per agent turn, and sum to the job's wall-clock within 1%.
- [ ] The incumbent's measured edit latencies for a parameter edit and a topology edit are printed beside ours with their own disclosure blocks.
- [ ] The learner's share of `train` is present in the resource figures.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::e2e_rows`:
- `designs_per_device_second` (KA-824): (2.0·10⁶, 0.70, 16, 10 s, 0.002 s) → 17.5; (4.0·10⁶, 0.86, …) → 43.0; world-steps per design = 80,000.
- `edit_split_sums_to_latency`.
- `class_a_requires_hit_and_no_capture`; `class_b_requires_no_capture`; `class_c_requires_one_capture`.
- `cold_is_reported_not_judged`: a cold class (c) time of 96 s produces no verdict.
- `row20_judgement`: class (a) 95th percentile 0.45 s passes the 0.5 s tolerance and misses the 0.1 s ambition; class (b) 2.3 s fails.
- `interval_export_closure`: intervals summing to 98.5% of the wall-clock fail the 1% rule.
- `phase_mapping_total`: every engine interval kind maps to exactly one ledger phase.
- `derived_is_never_a_measurement`: the row-21 record cannot be attached to a Performance claim.

### End-to-end script and logging

The admission script of the body-family chapter (owned by BD-184) runs the edits on our engine; this bead adds to
`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh` (owned by the throughput-rows bead) a step `e2e_rows` that prints the split per
edit class, computes row 21 from the measured rows 1 and 10, and exports the engine intervals of the script's own
jobs, asserting closure. Logged: edit class, cache state, the six intervals in milliseconds, percentiles against
their tolerances and ambitions, the derived figure with its inputs, the interval sum against the wall-clock.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-824 (derived figure).
- Negative controls: an edit of class (a) that silently recompiles (the split shows `compile_or_hit` as a compile and the record is rejected); a timer that stops at admission instead of the first completed world-step (the `first_chunk` interval is missing; rejected by the completion rule INV-PERF-02); an export that drops the `wait` intervals (closure fails).
- Metamorphic: the split of an edit does not depend on which slot the variant was admitted into.

## Considerations

- The family-batch argument depends on the incumbent's switch cost: with a 1 s switch a mixed batch could gain about 20 on a 256-design generation, with 0.1 s about 2.7. Row 20 measured on both sides is that number.
- The targets of row 20 restate the body-family chapter's admission targets (warm path 0.1 s at the 50th and 0.5 s at the 95th percentile; a new shape class with cached kernels 1 s and 5 s); one registry row serves both.
- Row 22's ledger is also where two kill gates promise to record a lost factor if they fire (the parity kill gate and the rank-agreement one); the export keeps the engine's part of that record honest.
- Agent turns are outside the engine; "per agent turn" here means the engine intervals are tagged with the turn that requested them, so the ten-fold harness can attribute waiting correctly.
