## Background

**The question.** OPEN-PERF-4: the default physics step for the reference workloads is another open question's to
decide (OPEN-KIN-1, owned by the dynamics chapter). If it moves from 2 ms, every row of the target table is
restated with its new decimation before any gate cites it. Owner of the restatement: the performance lead. It
blocks every gate that cites the target table, conditionally: only if the step moves.

**Why a step change is not cosmetic.** The reference workloads use a step h = 2 ms and a decimation d = 10, that
is a 50 Hz control period of 20 ms. Throughput targets are stated in world-steps/s (physics steps) and env-steps/s
(control ticks) with world-steps/s = d × env-steps/s. If h changes while the control period stays 20 ms, d changes
(h = 4 ms gives d = 5; h = 2.5 ms gives d = 8; h = 1 ms gives d = 20), and then:

- a physics rate in world-steps/s no longer buys the same simulated time: 2.0 M world-steps/s is 4,000
  world-seconds per second at 2 ms and 8,000 at 4 ms;
- the env-step figures tied to it by "one tenth" are tied by a different factor;
- matched-settings comparisons with incumbents must be re-run at the new h, because the incumbent's cost per step
  (its solver iterations, its contact work) changes with the step too;
- derived figures move: the referee coverage arithmetic (world-seconds per second), designs screened per
  device-second (world-steps per design), the height-scan cost per env-step, the bytes-touched budget per
  world-step at a given env-step ambition.

A table silently reused at a new step would compare unlike quantities. The fail-closed default: h = 2 ms and
d = 10 stand; if the step decision changes them, no gate may cite a target row until this bead's restatement is
on record.

## Scope

In: the restatement procedure; the function that restates a row; the freeze on gate citations between a step
change and the restatement; the record. Out: deciding the step (the dynamics unit); re-measuring incumbents and
our rows at the new step (their own beads, re-opened by this procedure).

## Technical approach

**Restatement function (`frob_conformance::perf::targets::restate(row, h_new, control_period)`).** Rules, by kind
of row:

1. *Ratio tolerances* (rows 1, 7, 8 against the incumbent; rows 2, 3 against row 1; rows 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13,
   14): the ratio is unit-free and unchanged; the row's unit line is rewritten with the new decimation
   d_new = control period ÷ h_new (a reduced rational if not an integer); its comparator settings are marked
   `to re-measure at h_new`, and the row cannot be judged until the incumbent arm has been re-measured at matched
   settings with the new step.
2. *Planning values and interim floors in world-steps/s* (rows 1, 7, 8, 13): restated at constant simulated-time
   throughput, value_new = value × (h_old/h_new), and labelled `restated planning value`; for example the 4.0 M
   planning value at 2 ms becomes 2.0 M at 4 ms and 8.0 M at 1 ms. They remain planning values only.
3. *Planning values and floors in env-steps/s* (rows 2, 3): the control period is unchanged, so the env-step
   figures are kept and only the "one tenth" conversion note changes to 1/d_new.
4. *Wall-clock rows* (rows 4, 9, 19, 20): unchanged in unit; the incumbent comparators of rows 4 and 9 are
   re-measured only if the incumbent recipe's own step was matched to ours.
5. *Derived figures*: world-steps per design (row 21) becomes worlds × seconds ÷ h_new; the coverage arithmetic
   uses T·h_new; the height-scan row keeps its per-env-step definition.
6. *Rows with no step-dependent threshold* (rows 15, 16, 17, 22): row 15 has no tolerance, and only its unit line
   takes d_new; row 16 is a multiple of real time with no decimation and is unchanged; row 17's share and its
   5% loss are ratios, unchanged as in rule 1, and its arithmetic follows rule 5; row 22 states no figure here.
   They are carried through the restatement like the others, so the restated table has all 22 rows.
7. Every restated row carries `restated_from` (the old row identity) and the ADR reference of the step decision.

**Procedure, numbered.**
1. Trigger: the step decision is recorded with h_new ≠ 2 ms for a reference workload.
2. Freeze: the target register marks every row `stale: step changed`; the reading helper used by gates refuses a
   stale row, so no gate can record against it.
3. Restate all 22 rows with the function above; update the workload records (h and d).
4. Re-open the measurements that depend on matched settings: the incumbent baseline rows at the new step, then
   our rows.
5. Record the restatement as an ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-PERF-4.md` (every decision record lives in `docs/adr/`),
   with the old and new tables side by side; unfreeze.
6. Results measured under the old step stay on record under their old workload identity and are never mixed with
   new ones in a ratio (the comparability check on step and decimation enforces it).

**If the step does not move.** The decision bead of the step records "2 ms confirmed"; this bead records that no
restatement was needed and closes with that record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The restatement function exists and is exercised on the whole table for h_new = 4 ms, 2.5 ms, 1 ms and 3 ms (decimations 5, 8, 20 and the reduced rational 20/3 for a 20 ms control period), with the outputs reviewed into fixtures.
- [ ] With a step change recorded in a scratch registry, every gate reading of a target row is refused until the restatement record exists.
- [ ] After restatement, ratio rows keep their ratios and are marked `to re-measure`; planning values are restated at constant simulated-time throughput and labelled.
- [ ] Old-step and new-step results cannot be combined in one ratio.
- [ ] The outcome is on record either as a restatement ADR or as "no restatement needed", citing the step decision.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The decimation and restatement arithmetic above.
- Negative control: a restatement that scales the ratio tolerances (0.67 → 0.335 at a doubled step) must be rejected: ratios are unit-free.
- Negative control: a gate that reads a stale row and records a pass.

## Considerations

- A smaller step raises solver stability margins and lowers the cost per step differently on each stack; that is why matched-settings parity must be re-measured, not rescaled.
- The control period could also move (a task at 100 Hz); the function takes it as an argument, and a task with rational clocks reports its mean world-steps per control tick as a rational.
- The restatement touches thresholds that kill gates read; it is a dated amendment to the gate register made before the measurements it governs, which is exactly the rule those gates require.
- Cost of a late step change: the incumbent baseline (an M0 and M1 deliverable) is partly repeated. Deciding the step before the matched-settings rows are measured avoids it; this bead only guarantees that a late change cannot be absorbed silently.
