## Background

A kill gate names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; a kill gate that fires
is not a failure of the program, because the fallback is the plan.

**KILL-PARITY.** Trigger: at M2 exit, after one optimization pass guided by the profile, the paired lower bound of
a physics row (row 1: quadruped physics on one RTX 4090; row 7: humanoid physics on flat ground; row 8: humanoid
physics on a heightfield) is below half its tolerance, that is, below 0.33 of the incumbent on the same card.
Fallback: feature work on the device tier stops and effort goes to the top three profile entries until the row is
inside tolerance; the ten-fold arithmetic is redone with measured rates before the partial ten-fold gate at M3;
and whether to ship the product's layer over an incumbent engine is decided at that gate under its own rules, not
here. A measured ratio between 0.33 and 0.67 is a recorded defect: one further optimization pass, and a
disclosure on every Performance claim until it closes.

Why the gate exists. Engine throughput is a parity requirement, not the product's advantage; the tolerance of
0.67 (a step at most 1.5 times slower than the incumbent's) is what the rest of the plan can absorb. A device
tier at a third of the incumbent's speed is a liability: every training run, every screening batch and the
ten-fold test (which charges the engine's measured rate in full) pay for it, and continuing to add features on
top of it compounds the debt. The gate forces the choice at M2, when it is cheapest. RISK-30 is the risk it
answers; the pre-mortem entry is "throughput landed far under the parity band and agents stopped waiting".

Terms. *Paired lower bound*: the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of our rate ÷ the incumbent's rate at
matched settings, over at least ten interleaved pairs on the same card. *One optimization pass*: every funded
lever attempted once, in descending order of computed ceiling, each with a behaviour proof.

## Scope

In: the decision procedure and the decision function (the only implementation of the three-band reading); the
ADR; the gate-register row of KILL-PARITY; the fail-closed default when rows could not be measured; what
changes downstream; the `decide` step this bead appends to the throughput-rows script.

Out, with the bead that has it: profiling, optimizing and measuring the rows, and storing the post-pass bounds
of rows 1, 7 and 8 as the receipt this decision reads (the throughput-rows seed BD-217, a dependency of this
bead; its done-condition "KILL-PARITY decided" is met by this bead's record, and BD-217 itself only makes the
gate decidable); the fallback's work and the defect-band disclosure (N36-28); the tolerance value (N36-24); the
decision about shipping a layer over an incumbent engine (the partial ten-fold gate).

## Technical approach

**Deciding measurement.** For each of rows 1, 7 and 8: the paired lower bound L recorded by the throughput-rows
bead **after** its single profile-guided optimization pass, against the incumbent measured on the same card in
the parity baseline. The tolerance T (default 0.67) and the kill threshold K (default 0.33) are read from the
tolerance decision's record.

**Reading, fixed in advance, per row.**

| Bound L | Reading | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| L ≥ T | Inside tolerance | None. The throughput gate passes for this row |
| K ≤ L < T | Recorded defect | One further optimization pass on this row; every Performance claim carries the parity-defect disclosure until the row is inside tolerance |
| L < K | Kill | The gate fires |

The gate fires if **any** of the three rows reads `kill`. A bound exactly equal to K is a defect, not a kill; a
bound exactly equal to T is inside tolerance.

**Procedure, numbered.**
1. Confirm admissibility for each row: at least ten pairs at matched settings, complete disclosure blocks for
   both arms, the harness's A/A control passed on that machine, the optimization pass recorded lever by lever, the
   incumbent arm frozen before the campaign.
2. Read each row against the table. No other number enters; the mean ratio and the pre-pass bound are context.
3. Write the ADR, `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-PARITY-M2.md` (every kill-gate decision is
   `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`), with the fields every kill-gate ADR carries (N40-03): the kill
   gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments applied; per row the measured bound with
   its pairs record and receipt hash, set beside the trigger's number K; the outcome; the fallback now in force
   and the name the capability carries from now on ("device tier below parity; feature work paused"), with the
   profile's top three entries, where the fallback sends effort; the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the
   fallback" or "superseded by this ADR"; what changes downstream; and the session that recorded it, which must
   not be a session that implemented the device tier. As context, and deciding nothing: the mean ratios and the
   pre-pass bounds.
4. Write the kill row's decision in the gate register, `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`
   (N40-03), with `outcome = "not-fired"` (no row reads `kill`; `receipts` holds the hashes of the three rows'
   receipts) or `outcome = "fired"` (the fallback is in force). The register's propagation then gives a failed
   throughput gate among the row's reads the status `killed` with the detail "KILL-PARITY: device tier below
   parity; feature work paused". Record each defect row separately with its owner; a defect does not fire the
   gate.
5. Apply the branch. On `fired`: the fallback bead becomes ready; device-tier feature beads of later milestones
   are marked blocked by it (the list is generated from the bead graph by label: device-tier features in the
   backend, variants-on-devices, fleet and specialization work packages); the ten-fold unit is notified to redo
   its arithmetic with measured rates; the lost factor is written into the ten-fold phase ledger's record of
   kill gates. On a defect only: the further pass and the disclosure are scheduled through the fallback bead's
   defect path, and nothing is blocked.

**If the rows could not be measured.** If no device family holds batch-composition invariance, or the device
backends are not admitted, M2 exits in its no-GPU variant: the device gates stay `not-run` with the reason, the
CPU rows are the Performance rows, and the decision's outcome is `deferred` (the register's word for a decision
an ADR moves to a named later exit; its reads stay `not-run`), with `reason = "no device rows"` and `deferred_to`
the first exit at which device rows exist, where it is decided. If the rows exist but the incumbent was never
measured on the card, the row is judged against the interim floor only as a gate status and cannot fire or clear
the kill gate: the outcome is likewise `deferred`, with `reason = "blocked: parity baseline not measured on the
card"` and the next exit named. Either is written in the same ADR file at the M2 exit.

**Amendment rule.** T, K, the rows or the fallback change only by a dated amendment to the gate register made
before the measurement they govern. "One more pass before deciding" is such a change and is not available after
the fact; the defect band already provides exactly one further pass for rows that are not killed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At the M2 exit `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-PARITY-M2.md` exists with the required fields: for each of rows 1, 7 and 8 the post-pass paired lower bound with its pairs record and receipt hash, its reading from the table, and the outcome (`not-fired` or `fired`); or, if the rows could not be measured or judged, the outcome `deferred` with its reason and the later exit.
- [ ] The gate register row for KILL-PARITY cites the ADR, the commit, the command, its output and the receipt hash.
- [ ] If the gate fired: the fallback bead is ready, the generated list of blocked device-tier feature beads is attached to the ADR, and the ten-fold unit's recomputation is requested in writing.
- [ ] Each defect row is recorded with its bound, its owner, the single further pass and the disclosure flag set.
- [ ] The decision used post-pass paired lower bounds against the recorded T and K and nothing else.
- [ ] Reports after a `fired` outcome describe the device tier by the fallback ("device tier below parity; feature work paused") and never by its targets.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance gates::kill_parity`:
- `reading_table`: bounds 0.32, 0.33, 0.50, 0.669, 0.67, 0.80 → {kill, defect, defect, defect, inside, inside}.
- `any_row_kills`: (0.80, 0.70, 0.30) → fired; (0.80, 0.70, 0.40) → not fired with one defect.
- `post_pass_bound_decides`: pre 0.30, post 0.45 → defect, not kill.
- `mean_is_ignored`: mean 0.40 with bound 0.31 → kill.
- `inadmissible_campaign_cannot_decide` (nine pairs; missing block; no A/A record).
- `interim_floor_cannot_decide_kill`: rows judged only against the interim floor give the outcome `deferred` with the baseline reason, never `fired` or `not-fired`.
- `no_gpu_variant_is_deferred`: with no device rows at the M2 exit the decision is `deferred`, names a later exit and the reason `no device rows`, and the reads stay `not-run`.
- `not_fired_cites_receipts`: a `not-fired` decision with an empty `receipts` list is refused by the register (N40-03).
- `thresholds_from_decision_record`: T = 0.80 in the record → K = 0.40 and the same bounds re-read accordingly.
- `blocked_bead_list_is_generated_by_label` (on a fixture graph).

### End-to-end script and logging

The throughput-rows script (`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh`, owned by BD-217) ends, as BD-217 delivers it,
with the step `kill_inputs`, which writes the receipt of the deciding bounds. This bead appends the step
`decide`, which prints each row's bound and reading by calling this bead's decision function through
`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g2_parity -- KILL-PARITY` (the tier target of the
gate's record). Logged: per row the bound, T, K, pairs, the reading; the outcome; the ADR path; on `fired` the
count of blocked beads.

### Known answers and negative controls

- The reading table is the known answer. Negative controls: deciding on the mean; deciding on the pre-pass campaign; a decision taken on native-settings ratios (rejected: the trigger is at matched settings on the same card); treating `not run` as "not fired".
- The interim floor of row 1 (2.0 M world-steps/s) is half the planning value and is not the tolerance; a test asserts that passing the floor does not record the row as inside tolerance.

## Considerations

- RISK-30 (engine throughput or training time lands below the parity band; likelihood high). Its mitigations are this gate, the learner's own kill gate and the rule that the profile decides where effort goes.
- No order of magnitude is available per step: an incumbent already measures 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s on this class of card. A kill here would most likely mean a structural cost (launch overhead, memory traffic of the dense Jacobian), which is why the fallback points at the top three profile entries and not at micro-optimizations.
- What the gate does not decide: whether the product should run its layer over an incumbent engine. That is decided at the partial ten-fold gate under its own outcome rules, with the measured rates this gate caused to be recorded.
- The claims of the project that never depended on speed (replay of any world from its address, error-controlled comparisons, independently checked family answers, minimal replayable failures, typed evidence, a closing energy ledger) are unaffected by any outcome here.
- The Metal row has its own bet and kill gate; the CPU rows are not kill-gated.
