## Background

This bead exists on one branch of the parity kill-gate decision, plus one sub-branch that is used whenever a row
lands in the defect band.

**The kill gate's fallback** (if, at M2 exit, after one profile-guided optimization pass, the paired lower bound
of a physics row is below 0.33 of the incumbent on the same card): feature work on the device tier stops and
effort goes to the top three profile entries until the row is inside tolerance; the ten-fold arithmetic is redone
with measured rates before the partial ten-fold gate at M3; and whether to ship the layer over an incumbent engine
is decided at that gate under its own rules.

**The defect band** (a measured ratio between 0.33 and 0.67): a recorded defect; one further optimization pass;
and a disclosure on every Performance claim until it closes.

Neither is a failure state to be hidden. The plan's rule is that a fired kill gate adopts its fallback openly and
that results are described by the fallback's name. What this bead adds is the machinery that makes the two
promises real: a pause that is actually enforced in the work graph, a disclosure that actually appears on every
claim, and a hand-off of measured rates that actually reaches the ten-fold arithmetic. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the pause of device-tier feature work and its release condition; the second pass; the parity-defect
disclosure field and its lifecycle; the measured-rate packet for the ten-fold unit; the closing measurement. Out:
the decision (N36-27); the optimization levers themselves (recorded through the optimization ledger); the
ten-fold recomputation and the ship-the-layer decision (the ten-fold unit).

## Technical approach

**A. Kill branch, numbered.**
1. *Pause.* From the ADR's generated list, every open device-tier feature bead (backends beyond the M2 scope,
   variants on devices beyond their gate, the fleet scheduler, specialized kernels, the fused step kernel) gets a
   blocking dependency on this bead. Correctness, determinism and safety work on the device tier is not paused:
   only features. CPU-tier work is unaffected.
2. *Profile again.* A fresh step profile of the killed row's workload on the affected backend, after the first
   pass. The three largest entries of end-to-end wall time are named in this bead's record.
3. *Work the top three.* Each candidate lever goes through the funding screen and the optimization ledger exactly
   as in the first pass: ceiling computed beforehand from the new profile, a behaviour proof (identical digests
   in classes D1 and D2, or an unchanged fidelity battery), a paired lower bound of at least 1.03 on the row.
   The byte-traffic remedies of the dense Jacobian and launch-overhead reductions are the named first suspects.
4. *Re-measure.* The row is re-measured as a full paired campaign against the frozen incumbent. Release
   condition: its paired lower bound is at least the tolerance (default 0.67). Then the pause is lifted and the
   kill gate's record gains a dated closing entry. A bound that rises into the defect band lifts nothing: the
   row is then handled by part B and the pause stays until the tolerance is met or the partial ten-fold gate
   decides otherwise.
5. *Hand-off.* A measured-rate packet goes to the ten-fold unit before the partial gate: for every throughput and
   training-time row, the measured bound, its disclosure blocks and the phase ledger of our own training run;
   the Amdahl limits recomputed with our measured step share; and the lost factor (tolerance ÷ measured bound)
   for the phase ledger's record of kill gates. The ten-fold arithmetic and the table of expected ranges are
   redone from this packet by their owners; this bead only guarantees the packet exists and is complete.

**B. Defect band, numbered** (used for any physics row with K ≤ bound < T, whether or not another row was
killed).
1. Record the defect: row, bound, tolerance, owner, date.
2. Set the **parity-defect disclosure**: the disclosure block's `parity_defect` field is filled on every
   Performance claim produced on the affected backend and workload class: `{row, measured_bound, tolerance,
   incumbent_block, since}`. The completeness check refuses a Performance claim on that backend whose block omits
   it (`FROB-REF-862`, pointer `parity_defect`) while the defect is open; reports print the line "device tier at
   ⟨bound⟩ of the incumbent on row ⟨n⟩ (tolerance ⟨T⟩)".
3. One further optimization pass on that row, under the same ledger rules.
4. Re-measure. If the bound reaches the tolerance the defect is closed with a dated record and the field
   returns to null. If not, the defect stays open and disclosed; there is no third pass by default, and the row
   is reported at its measured value at every exit review until a dated amendment funds more work.

**State.** `registries/parity_defects.toml`: one entry per row with `state` (`open`, `closed`), bounds before and
after, pass references, and the ADR; its hand-written JSON-Schema is `schemas/registries/parity_defects.schema.json`,
as for every registry file (BD-002). The disclosure filler reads it; nothing else decides whether the field is
set.

**Evidence.** The closing measurements are Performance claims of class `Tested` with their pairs records; the
packet is a set of references to recorded evidence, not new numbers.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After a `killed` record, every listed device-tier feature bead is blocked by this bead in the work graph and none can be started; correctness and determinism beads are not blocked.
- [ ] The second profile and its top three entries are recorded; every lever worked has an optimization ledger entry with ceiling, proof and paired bound.
- [ ] The pause is lifted only by a recorded paired lower bound at or above the tolerance for the killed row, with a dated closing entry.
- [ ] The measured-rate packet exists before the partial ten-fold gate, complete for every throughput and training-time row, with the lost factor stated.
- [ ] While a defect is open, every Performance claim on the affected backend and workload class carries the `parity_defect` field, and one without it is refused; when the defect closes the field is null on new claims.
- [ ] A defect row gets exactly one further pass by default; its state and bounds are in the registry, which validates against `schemas/registries/parity_defects.schema.json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::parity_defect`:
- `defect_sets_disclosure_field`: an open entry for row 1 on `gpu-cuda` fills the field on a quadruped CUDA claim and not on a `batch-cpu` claim.
- `claim_without_field_refused_while_open` (`FROB-REF-862`, pointer `parity_defect`).
- `closing_requires_bound_at_tolerance`: 0.66 does not close; 0.67 closes.
- `kill_release_condition`: 0.50 after the second pass keeps the pause and opens the defect path.
- `lost_factor`: tolerance 0.67, bound 0.30 → 2.23.
- `packet_completeness`: a packet missing the training-time row is rejected.
- `only_features_are_paused` (on a fixture graph with labelled beads).
- `one_further_pass_by_default`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/spark_parity_rows.sh` (owned by the throughput-rows bead) gains a mode `--after-kill` (and
`--after-defect`): it re-runs the profile, the landing check for the second pass, the paired campaign of the
affected row, and asserts the `parity_defect` field in the disclosure block of a fresh harness report of that
row (BD-216's run report, whose block `from_receipt` fills; a `sim.run` envelope carries no disclosure block)
while the registry entry is open, and a null field after closing. Logged: the registry state, the bound before and after against the
tolerance and the kill threshold, the field's contents, the packet's row list, the pause list's size.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Arithmetic: the penalty of running at the measured ratio, 1 + s·(1/ρ − 1), printed in the packet for the measured step share (for example ρ = 0.30 and s = 0.87 lengthen training by 3.03); it and the recomputed Amdahl limits are N36-04's functions (`slowdown_penalty`, `limits_from_ledger`), called, never restated here.
- Negative controls: a claim on the affected backend emitted without the field (must be refused); a release of the pause on a point estimate (the release function takes a paired bound only); a packet assembled from planning values instead of measurements (rejected: every figure must reference a disclosure block).
- Determinism: the second pass's behaviour proofs are the digest lanes of the optimization ledger.

## Considerations

- Pausing features is the costly part and is meant to be: the gate exists because a device tier at a third of the incumbent's speed taxes everything built on it. The CPU studio, mission queries and every claim that does not depend on device speed continue.
- The M2 exit can still happen with the pause in force; the exit review reports the device tier by the fallback's name and lists the paused beads.
- If the second pass also fails, nothing here decides the engine's future; the partial ten-fold gate does, with the measured rates, under rules that include shipping the layer over an incumbent engine for the affected strata.
- The disclosure is deliberately attached to claims, not to a status page: a figure quoted out of context still carries it.
- RISK-30 and the pre-mortem entry on throughput under the parity band are the risks this bead is the response to.
