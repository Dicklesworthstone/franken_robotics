## Background

`sim.estimate` is the read verb an agent calls before it spends compute: what a run will cost in memory and in
time, whether it fits the device, and which compute-envelope class it needs. Its two rules are that every time
estimate is an interval (a calibrated conformal interval, never a point) and that memory is an exact figure from
a byte model, with a job that does not fit refused before anything is allocated (`FROB-REF-602`). An agent may
trust those figures only after they have been checked against what the devices actually did. That check is
GATE-SPARK-22 (claim: Known answer; earliest milestone M2; thresholds are TARGETs):

- `sim.estimate` memory is within 5% of measured high-water marks at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds and at capacity,
  for the reference quadruped workload Q (18 degrees of freedom, 13 bodies, contact capacity 32, row capacity
  160) and the reference humanoid workload H (35, 30, 64, 320), on each device;
- a job that does not fit is refused before allocation;
- time estimates fall inside their intervals on at least 90% of held-out runs;
- bytes touched per world-step are measured per launch.

Terms. A *high-water mark* is the largest number of device bytes the arena held at once during a run, read from
the allocator's counter in the run receipt. *Capacity* is the largest world count the estimate says fits the
device's budget (`largest_worlds_that_fit`). A *held-out run* is a job whose wall time was never used to
calibrate the interval that predicts it; held-out and calibration jobs are declared as two job lists and hashed
into the ledger before either is run. The *gate-measurement class* E1 is the compute envelope for a named gate
measurement: both machines, all cores, memory up to the device budget, 2 hours per run, 12 device-hours per gate
(the default class E0 allows one device, 8 cores, 16 GiB and one hour). *Devices*: on `trj` (the Linux host)
the two RTX 4090 cards of 24 GB, CUDA devices 0 and 1, each measured because free memory and the fixed overhead
are read per card and the host is shared; on the Mac (the M5 Ultra) its Metal device, whose working-set limit is
about 222.7 GiB and largest single buffer about 167.0 GiB.

This bead carries the done-condition of the plan's seed BD-219 ("the `sim.estimate` cost and memory model",
done-condition GATE-SPARK-22, dependency BD-217). DESIGN (split in polish round 1): BD-219 keeps the verb and
the model and no longer waits on any measurement campaign, so that the many beads that need only the verb do
not wait for the M2 parity campaign; the gate, its measurement on every device and the seed's dependency on the
throughput rows (BD-217) are here. The arrangement is the one the parity baseline uses: the script bead (N36-32)
owns the script and the gate test target that assembles the record; the measuring bead runs it on each machine,
judges and records. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

In: the gate campaign on every device; the declaration and hashing of the calibration and held-out job lists
for the coverage clause; planting and catching the registered negative controls in the recorded run; the
judgement of each clause and point; the diagnosis rule for a failed point; the GATE-SPARK-22 record with its
status per device, read by the M2 exit.

Out, with the bead that has it: the verb, the memory model, the device budgets and the refusal (BD-219); the
scene and camera groups (N36-16, N36-19); the time model, its interval and the held-out coverage computation
(N36-17); the per-launch bytes-touched figure and the bandwidth verdict (N36-18); the script, its logging and the
gate test target that assembles the four clauses (N36-32); the run admission and the device-hour meter that
refuse a point above the envelope in force (N36-20); the owner's decision on the envelope (N21c-35, N44-01).

## Technical approach

**Preconditions, numbered.**
1. The fingerprint of each device is current (`frob spark devices --json`, N21c-04; a `stale` or `missing` one is
   re-measured by the script's `preflight` with `frob spark fingerprint --device <id> --soak 20m --key <key>
   --json`, BD-029); a changed digest invalidates the per-fingerprint fixed overhead and the time calibration,
   which are re-measured first.
2. The envelope class in force is read. Under E0 only the points that fit one device, one hour and 16 GiB are
   run; capacity on the Mac (far above 16 GiB) and the long held-out sets need E1, and without it those points
   are recorded `not run` with the refusal `FROB-REF-761` and the class needed, never estimated. The row that
   admits them is R1 of the envelope register (N44-01: the measurements of packages WP-34 and WP-47, class E1),
   which admits `gate:GATE-SPARK-22` (R1's gates are GATE-SPARK-21, GATE-SPARK-03 and GATE-SPARK-22); this bead
   cites it, and the owner's ruling on R1 is the one that applies here. The points above E0 are started by the script through `frob-launch --scope
   gate:GATE-SPARK-22 --request R1 …` (N36-32, step `capacity`), so that their device-hours are metered against
   the gate's 12.
3. The campaign's device time is budgeted before it starts with the estimator's own upper ends
   (`frob sim estimate … --json`, field `device_seconds.hi`, summed over the job lists) and printed against the
   12 device-hours of E1; a campaign whose upper end exceeds the budget is reduced in held-out duration or count
   (never below the 100 held-out jobs per family the coverage clause needs), and the reduction is written into
   the record. Dividing jobs between the two cards of `trj` shortens the wall time, not the device-hours.

**Campaign, numbered.**
1. *Controls first.* Each negative control registered against the gate (below) is planted in a scratch build and
   the script is run on the smallest admissible point for that control; the script must fail with the control's
   named assertion. The caught controls are written into the same record as the passing run (the gate recorder's
   rule: a gate that nothing can fail validates nothing).
2. *Job lists.* For each device family (CUDA, Metal) a calibration list and a held-out list are generated from a
   seed, spanning Q and H, world counts from 64 to 8,192, simulated durations from 2 to 120 s, cold and warm
   starts, physics-only and training jobs, at least 100 held-out jobs per family; both lists are hashed into the
   ledger before the first calibration job runs. The two lists are disjoint by job identity.
3. *Runs.* `scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh --machine trj --device cuda:0 --seed <s>`, then `--device cuda:1`,
   then `--machine mac --device metal:0`; each run executes the script's steps `memory`, `capacity`, `refusal`,
   `scenes`, `cameras`, `calibrate`, `heldout`, `bytes_touched`, `envelope_class` and `gate`. The two cards of
   `trj` are never measured concurrently for the memory clause (each card's free memory is read at admission);
   the coverage lists may be divided between them, with each job's card in its record.
4. *Judgement per point.* Memory: |estimate − measured| ÷ measured ≤ 0.05 at 1, 8, 64, 256 worlds and at
   capacity, for Q and H, and for the scene rows (1, 4 and 16 variants of one compiled interior) and the camera
   rows (a 64 × 64 depth task and a two-camera 640 × 480 task at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds, and the depth task at
   8,192). Refusal: exit code 6,
   `FROB-REF-602`, the fingerprint digest in the envelope, the repairs in the registry's order for the slab class
   named, and the device's allocated-bytes counter unchanged across the call. Coverage: the fraction of held-out
   jobs whose measured wall time lies inside the headline (95%) interval estimated beforehand is at least 0.90
   per device family; the 90% interval's coverage is recorded beside it and not judged. Traffic: the run receipt of
   Q and H at 8,192 worlds holds the per-launch table of bytes read and written per world-step and it sums to the
   total.
5. *A failed point* is diagnosed before anything else changes: a memory point beyond 5% at one world usually
   means a stale fixed overhead (re-measured, then the point is re-run under the same seed); a difference that
   grows with the world count is a defect in a per-world term (filed against BD-219 or the component that owns
   the slab class); a coverage below 0.90 is a defect against N36-17 with the residuals attached. The tolerance,
   the coverage level and the job lists are never changed after results are seen; a re-run after a fix uses a new
   seed and freshly declared lists.
6. *Record.* The gate test target of N36-32 writes one record per device, in its receipt: commit, fingerprint
   digest, seeds, the
   two job-list hashes, every point with estimate, measured mark and relative difference, the refusal envelopes,
   the coverage counts at both levels, the per-launch traffic tables, the controls caught, and the `not run`
   points with their refusal and reason. The gate's status is entered by this bead, the gate's measuring bead,
   from the receipt of the final `gate` run, made after the three devices' records have been collated on one
   machine (only receipts, digests and summaries cross between machines), with `cargo run -p frob-conformance
   --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-SPARK-22 --receipt <hash>`, in a session that did not implement the
   estimator (N38b-19); the script and the test target write no row of the register. Gate status (the register's statuses, N40-01): `pass` only when every
   clause and every point, capacity included, is measured and passes on every device; `fail` when a measured
   point fails; `blocked` with detail `OPEN-SPARK-2` when every measured point passes and the only points not
   run are those
   an unapproved envelope class refused (each listed with `FROB-REF-761` and the class needed), which the exit
   query (N40-04) stops counting as blocking once OPEN-SPARK-2 is answered or has taken its default by ADR, and
   which is never printed as `pass` (a gate whose capacity point never ran on the Mac has not shown the clause
   "at capacity on each device"). A record that is `incomplete` (any other missing point or clause, a device
   absent) is not recordable: the register has no such status, and the gate stays `not-run` until a complete
   record exists. The M2 exit review reads the register row and the receipts it cites.

**Evidence.** Claim kind Known answer, class `Tested`, per device; the estimates themselves are class `Estimated`
(exact-by-model for memory) and become comparable with measurements only through this record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-22 is recorded as measured (the seed done-condition of BD-219, carried here) on CUDA devices 0 and 1 of `trj` and on the Mac's Metal device, with for each device, workload and size the estimate, the measured high-water mark and their relative difference against 0.05.
- [ ] Memory within 5% at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds and at capacity for Q and H on each device, and for the scene rows (1, 4, 16 variants) and the camera rows; a capacity point not admitted under the envelope in force is `not run` with `FROB-REF-761` and its reason.
- [ ] On each device a request above capacity is refused with `FROB-REF-602`, exit code 6, the fingerprint digest and the ranked repairs, and the allocated-bytes counter is unchanged across the call.
- [ ] Held-out coverage of the headline interval is at least 0.90 per device family on at least 100 jobs whose list was hashed into the ledger before the first calibration job; the 90% interval's coverage is recorded.
- [ ] The per-launch bytes-touched table is present for Q and H at 8,192 worlds on each device and sums to the total.
- [ ] Every registered negative control was planted and caught, and the catches are in the same record as the passing run.
- [ ] The record names the commit, each device's fingerprint digest, the seeds and both job-list hashes; the gate's status follows the rule above.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The assembly of the record is tested in N36-32 (`gate22::assemble`). This bead adds the campaign's own checks,
`cargo test -p frob-conformance gate22::campaign`:
- `lists_hashed_before_calibration`: a held-out list whose hash entered the ledger after the first calibration
  job → the coverage clause is `invalid`, not `pass`.
- `lists_disjoint`: a job identity present in both lists → refused before the campaign starts.
- `status_rule`: all clauses and points pass on three devices → `pass`; every measured point passes and the Mac
  capacity point is `not run` under an unapproved E1 (`FROB-REF-761`, class needed E1) → `blocked` with detail
  `OPEN-SPARK-2` and the point listed, never `pass`; the same point missing with no refusal recorded → the
  record is `incomplete` and nothing is recordable (the gate stays `not-run`); one memory point at 0.06 → `fail`.
- `every_device_present`: a `trj` record with only CUDA device 0 → `incomplete`, not recordable.
- `controls_in_same_record`: a passing record without its caught controls → refused by the gate recorder.
- `budget_from_upper_ends`: job lists whose summed `device_seconds.hi` is 13 device-hours → the campaign is
  reduced before it starts, and the reduction is written; the same lists divided between two cards still sum to
  13 and are not accepted as a remedy.

### End-to-end script and logging

The campaign is `scripts/e2e/sim_estimate_gate22.sh` (N36-32), run once per device with an explicit seed; it
sources the shared end-to-end helper and writes `target/e2e-logs/sim_estimate_gate22/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`
with a transcript beside it. Every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`N36p1-01` for this campaign), `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's evidence block; `metric` lines carry the
workload, device, world count, the estimate's breakdown by slab class, the budget, the measured mark and the
relative difference against 0.05, and for time the stratum, both interval ends, the measured wall time and inside
or outside; `refusal` lines carry the whole refusal object with allocated bytes before and after. A campaign line
per device gives the controls caught, the job-list hashes, the device-hours spent against the budget, and the
status; the final `summary` names `gates: ["GATE-SPARK-22"]` with the status per device and every `not run`
point with its reason. On failure: the first point outside its threshold, both records' paths and the command
that reproduces it.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-826 (the gate's three numeric clauses: 5%, refusal before allocation, 0.90 coverage), KA-814 and KA-816
  (the byte model's terms and consequences printed for Q and H: 34,556 and 96,704 bytes per world before
  rounding; 0.28 GB and 0.79 GB at 8,192 worlds), KA-817 (two 640 × 480 cameras on 256 worlds: 1,258,291,200
  bytes), KA-822 (the bytes-touched budget printed beside the measurement).
- Registered controls, each planted and caught in the recorded run: an estimator that sums all slabs instead of
  taking the live-interval peak; an admission path that allocates before checking the fit; an estimator
  calibrated on the held-out list; a backend that reports bytes touched at capacities instead of active counts;
  an estimator that charges a scene per world.
- Determinism lanes: estimates are identical on repeat and across the two cards for the same fingerprint record;
  measured marks are exact allocator counts and identical on repeat for the same request and seed; coverage is a
  measurement and is not repeated for agreement.

## Considerations

- OPEN-SPARK-2 (owner: the project owner; fail-closed default E0): without E1 the Mac's capacity point and the
  longer held-out jobs are `not run`, and the gate is recorded `blocked: OPEN-SPARK-2`, never `pass`; it stops
  blocking the M2 exit once the open question is answered or its default is recorded by ADR, and it reaches
  `pass` only by a later run under an approved class. The script never works around a refusal.
- `trj` is often heavily loaded (RISK-09): the capacity point uses the fingerprint's free-memory reading at the
  time, which may be well under the planning figure of 16 to 19 GB usable, and the record states it.
- A change of fingerprint, arena layout, slab catalogue, scene pack format or reference-workload capacities
  (OPEN-SPARK-3) invalidates the record; the campaign is re-run, never rescaled.
- The 95% headline interval is the time model's reading of the coverage clause (N36-17); the record carries both
  levels so that the gate's owner can see the 90% interval's coverage too.
- Pre-mortem: an agent that trusted an unverified estimate either wastes a budget stop (an underestimate; a
  stopped job publishes nothing) or is refused needlessly (an overestimate). The first is the costly failure,
  which is why admission reads the interval's upper end and why this record must exist before M2 exits.
