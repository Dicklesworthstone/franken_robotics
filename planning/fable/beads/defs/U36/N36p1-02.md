## Background

Before our own step exists, the plan can only say where time *should* go, by analogy with an incumbent. FACT (an
incumbent's published event trace of a humanoid at 8,192 worlds; to be re-read): of 262 ns per world-step, the
solve is 47%, constraint construction 15%, kinematics and smooth dynamics 20%, collision 4%, integration 4% (the
remaining 10% is not attributed in the source). A manipulation scene at 513 ns shifts to 44% solve and 32%
collision. On a full environment step of another stack, physics is under a third. HYPOTHESIS for our step: the
constraint solve 40 to 60%, collision 15 to 25% once worlds sample a scene distance field, dynamics terms 10 to
20%.

The same section fixes what the **referee** (the slow, trusted tier: one world on one CPU core, event-located)
can do for a device run. A device produces T·h world-seconds per second (T in world-steps/s, h the step); a
referee on c cores at a real-time factor ρ per core covers c·ρ of them. For the humanoid workload H at its
tolerance, T·h = 1.7 M × 2 ms = 3,400; 24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0 cover 4.8 to 24, that is 0.14 to 0.7%. For the
quadruped workload Q at its floor (2.0 M) it is 0.12 to 0.6%. So **the referee is a sampler by necessity**: about
0.1 to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds across device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward (HYPOTHESIS;
measured at M2). HYPOTHESIS: the `batch-cpu` tier, not the referee, can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid worlds,
since its tolerance of 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400.

A hypothesis in this project carries the test that settles it, and a prediction counts only if it was on record
before the measurement. The M2 step profile (N21a-27) records the stage shares of Q and H at 8,192 worlds as part
of its own acceptance, and the sampled referee (BD-214) measures ρ and the shadow share at its gate. This bead
puts the six predictions on record before either measurement exists, with the coverage function that computes
the coverage predictions; the outcomes are computed after the measurements by N36-15. DESIGN (split in polish
round 1): as drafted, one bead both registered the predictions and scored them, while depending on the profile
bead, so the graph could not place the registration before the profile; registration and scoring are now two
beads, with the profile and the referee depending on this one. Tier: Solid.

## Scope

its lint (an entry registered after its settling measurement is rejected; a registered prediction is never
edited); the six entries; the coverage function `frob_conformance::perf::coverage` with the known answers of

JSON output); the incumbent trace as a context fixture. The command is an example target, which this bead
declares: `crates/frob-conformance/examples/perf.rs`, a thin argument parser over the library functions of
`frob_conformance::perf` that prints one JSON object and exits non-zero when its own assertions fail (BD-025: the
crate has no default binary, so a command must name the example). Its first subcommand
is `coverage`; N36-15 adds `where-time-goes` and N36-26 adds `restate`, each in its own bead.

Out, with the bead that has it: computing outcomes from the profile and the referee measurement, the report
section `where_time_goes`, replacing the HYPOTHESIS labels (N36-15); the profile and its record (N21a-27); the
sampled referee and its measurement (BD-214); the verdict register of the ten-fold hypotheses
(`crates/frob-conformance/registry/hypotheses.toml`, N00-10), which is a different record (states ACTIVE,
NARROWED, REFUTED, DEFERRED with an append-only history) and shares no identifiers with this one (ids here start
`H-TIME-` and `H-COV-`).

## Technical approach

**Register.** One entry per prediction: `id`, `statement`, `predicted` (a closed interval, or `none` with the
context value when ours is not predicted), `measure` (what is compared: a stage share, a coverage share, a rate
ratio), `settled_by` (the measurement and its bead), `registered_at` (the ledger sequence number and commit at
which the entry was added), `outcome` (`unmeasured` at registration), `measured` (empty), `consequence`.

| Id | Statement | Predicted | Settled by | Consequence if refuted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-TIME-1 | Share of our world-step in the constraint solve (stage 8) | 0.40 to 0.60 | The M2 profile (N21a-27), Q and H at 8,192 worlds, each backend | The optimization pass re-ranks its levers; the solve remedies lose or gain priority |
| H-TIME-2 | Share in collision (stages 2 to 5) once worlds sample a scene distance field | 0.15 to 0.25 | The same profile on a scene workload | The scene-sampling kernels become the first target if above 0.25 |
| H-TIME-3 | Share in dynamics terms (stage 6) | 0.10 to 0.20 | The same profile | Bounds what design-specialized recursions could gain |
| H-TIME-4 | Physics as a share of our full environment step | none; context: under one third on an incumbent | The full-step and physics rows | Sets the non-physics overhead against its 25% tolerance |
| H-COV-1 | Share of device world-seconds a referee sampler covers | 0.001 to 0.024 over device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward, at ρ of 0.2 to 1.0 on 24 cores | The measured ρ per core and the measured shadow share (BD-214) | If ρ is below 0.2 the share falls below 0.1% and the sampler's coverage figure says so |
| H-COV-2 | `batch-cpu` can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid device worlds | at least 0.10 (0.16 at the tolerances) | The measured `batch-cpu` humanoid rate against the measured device rate | `batch-cpu` shadowing is not built out as a coverage tier |

**Lint (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test hypotheses_perf`), numbered.**
1. Every entry has every field; `outcome` is one of `unmeasured`, `confirmed`, `refuted`, `recorded` (the last
   only for an entry whose `predicted` is `none`).
2. Compared with the merge base, `statement`, `predicted`, `measure` and `settled_by` of an existing entry never
   change; only `outcome` and `measured` may be filled, once, from `unmeasured`.
3. An entry whose `registered_at` is later than the first ledger record of its settling measurement is rejected
   (finding `hyp.registered-after-measurement`, naming both positions).

**Coverage function (`frob_conformance::perf::coverage`).** `share(c, ρ, T, h) = c·ρ/(T·h)`, with c cores, ρ the
real-time factor per core (simulated seconds per wall second), T the device rate in world-steps/s and h the step
in seconds; `shadow_share_cpu(T_cpu, T_dev) = T_cpu/T_dev` when both use the same h. Inputs carry units (the rate
types of N36-03); a rate without its decimation is refused by type. Exact rationals inside; the printed figures
are rounded. A coverage record (`{cores, rho, rate, h, claimed_share}`: the inputs and the share a report
states; on the command line, the `coverage` subcommand's option `--claim <share>`, beside `--rate` in
world-steps/s with its `--h` and `--decimation`, from which the typed rate is built) is checked by
`check_claim(record)`: a claimed share
above `share(c, ρ, T, h)` computed from the record's own inputs is rejected with the finding
`coverage-claim-exceeds-capacity`, naming both values, so that a report cannot say the referee checked more
worlds than its cores can simulate.

**Evidence.** The entries are class `Estimated` (predictions); the coverage figures are arithmetic on stated
inputs, exact.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The six entries are in the register with `outcome = "unmeasured"` and a `registered_at` earlier than the first M2 profile record and the first shadow-referee measurement in ledger order (the profile bead N21a-27 and the referee bead BD-214 depend on this bead).
- [ ] KA-821: 1.7 M × 2 ms = 3,400 world-seconds per second; 24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0 cover 4.8 to 24, 0.14 to 0.7%; for Q at 2.0 M, 0.12 to 0.6%; `batch-cpu` at 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400.
- [ ] CX-383 ("the referee can check every device world"): with 24 cores, ρ ≤ 1 and h = 2 ms the share is below 1 for every device rate above 12,000 world-steps/s, and a coverage record claiming full coverage of a 1.7 M world-steps/s run (the plant `full-referee-coverage-claim`) is rejected by `check_claim`.
- [ ] `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example perf -- coverage … --json` prints one JSON object and exits 0 on the KA-821 inputs; the example is declared in the crate's manifest and passes BD-025's target lint.
- [ ] The lint rejects an entry registered after its measurement, an edited prediction, and a `recorded` outcome on an entry that has a prediction.
- [ ] The incumbent trace is stored as context with its per-stage times (123, 39, 52, 10 and 10 ns of 262 ns) and its unattributed 10%.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance perf::coverage perf::hypotheses`:
- `coverage_humanoid` (KA-821): share(24, 0.2, 1.7·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.00141; share(24, 1.0, …) = 0.00706.
- `coverage_quadruped_floor`: share(24, 0.2, 2.0·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.0012; at ρ = 1.0, 0.006.
- `coverage_at_ambitions`: Q at 4.0 M → 0.0006 to 0.003 (half); H at 2.55 M → 0.00094 to 0.0047 (two thirds).
- `coverage_upper_end`: share(24, 1.0, 500,000, 0.002) = 0.024 (a device at 1,000 world-seconds per second).
- `all_cores_ambition`: share(36, 1.0, 1.7·10⁶, 0.002) = 0.0106 (the 1% ambition needs a real-time referee on all
  36 cores).
- `batch_cpu_shadow`: 275,000 ÷ 1,700,000 = 0.162.
- `referee_cannot_cover_everything` (CX-383): share < 1 for every T above 12,000 at c = 24, ρ = 1, h = 0.002
  (500 world-steps/s per referee core), and exactly 1 at 12,000; and the planted record
  `full-referee-coverage-claim` (24 cores, ρ = 1.0, T = 1.7·10⁶, h = 0.002, claimed share 1.0, where the function
  gives 0.00706) is rejected by `check_claim` with `coverage-claim-exceeds-capacity`, while the same record with
  a claimed share of 0.007 is accepted.
- `incumbent_trace_shares`: 0.47, 0.15, 0.20, 0.04, 0.04 of 262 ns → 123.1, 39.3, 52.4, 10.5, 10.5 ns; the
  remainder 0.10 is printed as unattributed.
- `registered_before_measured`: an entry at ledger position 120 with its profile record at 100 → rejected; at 80
  → accepted.
- `prediction_is_immutable`: changing H-TIME-1's interval to 0.35 to 0.60 against the merge base → rejected.
- `recorded_only_without_prediction`: H-TIME-4 may take `recorded`; H-TIME-1 may not.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/perf_hypotheses_register.sh` (owned by this bead; sources the shared end-to-end helper; seed-free,
deterministic): step `load` validates the register and prints each entry; step `coverage` runs
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example perf -- coverage --cores 24 --rho 0.2,1.0 --rate 1.7e6,2.0e6 --h 0.002
--decimation 10 --json`
and asserts the KA-821 figures; step `order` reads the ledger positions of the six entries and asserts that no
settling measurement record precedes them; step `controls` plants, in a scratch copy of the register, an entry
registered after a planted profile record and an edited interval, and asserts the two lint findings, then runs
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example perf -- coverage --cores 24 --rho 1.0 --rate 1.7e6 --h 0.002 --decimation
10 --claim 1.0 --json` (the planted record `full-referee-coverage-claim`) and asserts a non-zero exit with the finding
`coverage-claim-exceeds-capacity`. NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/perf_hypotheses_register/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript beside it; every line has
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines carry the
entry id, the predicted interval, the inputs with units and the computed share; `assert` lines carry expected and
observed; the final `summary` lists assertions passed and failed and the entries on record with their positions.
On failure: the first differing value and the register path.

### Known answers and negative controls

- KA-821 (coverage arithmetic, exact); CX-383 (`referee_cannot_cover_everything`). The control of CX-383 in the
  counterexample registry N36-CX is the planted input `full-referee-coverage-claim`, built by this bead: the
  `synthetic` fixture `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/perf/full-referee-coverage-claim.json`, a coverage record
  claiming that the referee checks every device world; `check_claim` must reject it. N36-15 applies the same
  check to the measured shadow share when it scores the entries at M2.
- Negative control: a coverage function that divides by T instead of T·h reports a share 500 times too small at
  h = 2 ms and fails `coverage_humanoid`.
- Negative control: an entry registered after its measurement, and a prediction widened after registration, are
  each caught by the lint.

## Considerations

- The humanoid figure printed in the dynamics chapter for the sampler's share was computed at a device rate that is not a row of the target table (0.5 M world-steps/s, giving 0.5 to 2.4%); this register uses the row's 1.7 M and prints 0.14 to 0.7%, and both lie in the order-of-magnitude range of 0.1 to 2.4%.
- The referee's real-time factor is itself a target row (at least 0.2 for a humanoid on one core, ambition 1.0); the share ambition of 1% on the humanoid needs that ambition on all 36 cores of the Mac.
- A prediction registered here is never adjusted toward a measurement; a wrong prediction is a result, scored by N36-15.
- Risk: the profile bead records Q and H when it closes. N21a-27 and BD-214 depend on this bead, so neither measurement can be taken before the predictions are registered; a measurement recorded first anyway is reported `hyp.registered-after-measurement` by the lint and its predictions cannot count. The remedy is the dependency, never a back-dated `registered_at`.
