## Background

The safety chapter of the system fixes four things: what may enter the system, what may leave it, who may read
what, and what no result is allowed to mean. Its mechanisms are enforced in `frob-verbs` (the dispatcher and the
envelope), `frob-io` (importers), `frob-chronicle` (marks and lineage), `frob-cert` (the offline auditor) and the
daemon `frobd`. None of them is on by request: each is the default and has no off switch below an owner-held
configuration. Every one is tier Solid unless marked otherwise.

Mechanisms without a stated threat model drift: a defence is added for a threat nobody listed, or a listed threat
loses its only defence in a refactor and nothing notices. This bead turns the threat model into a registry file
that a test reads, so that "every in-scope threat has a mechanism, a gate and a planted failure that the gate
catches" is a checked statement and not a paragraph.

The model (DESIGN, binding). Five assets; for each, the threats that are in scope and the ones that are
deliberately out of scope.

| # | Asset | Threat in scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | The owner's machines and devices | An agent that is confused, wasteful or acting on injected text; a hostile imported file | A local user with root |
| T2 | Evidence: results, verdicts, certificates | An optimizing agent that adapts to held-out worlds; a result cited as more than it is; a stale or forged artifact | An owner who edits the ledger by hand |
| T3 | Private places | A scan, or anything derived from it, leaving the machine or appearing in a shared view | none |
| T4 | Budgets and other agents' work | A client that retries blindly; an agent writing into another's lineage | none |
| T5 | People near a physical robot | An exported artifact mistaken for permission to run hardware | Operation of hardware, which this project never performs |

Why the out-of-scope column matters: it is what keeps the mechanisms honest about their reach. A capability token
does not defend against root; a ledger hash chain does not defend against its owner; the no-authorization flags
say nothing about what a person does with a printed part. Tests must not pretend otherwise, and reports must not
claim it.

The five architecture decisions of the chapter are recorded here as the register's rationale column (each is
restated in the bead that implements it):

| ADR | Decision | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-SAFE-01 | The three no-authorization flags are a type with one inhabitant, not three booleans | A boolean can be set; a constant cannot | Three `bool` fields with a review rule |
| ADR-SAFE-02 | Isolation of confirmation worlds is enforced by capability, by a separate store and by address namespace, all three | Statistical error control across adaptive retries is void if one read path exists | One scope check in the dispatcher |
| ADR-SAFE-03 | Privacy and licence marks propagate through lineage as a join; nothing derived can be less restricted than an input | Marks attached by hand are forgotten on the second derivation | A `private` field set by whoever writes the artifact |
| ADR-SAFE-04 | Paths are resolved beneath retained directory handles, never by checking a string and reopening | A check on a string is defeated by a link swapped between the check and the open | Canonicalize, compare prefix, open |
| ADR-SAFE-05 | Imported strings have their own type and can reach only fields marked untrusted | A rule about "being careful" with text cannot be tested; a type can | Escaping at output sites |

## Scope

In: `registries/threats.toml` with its schema; the loader and the traceability test in `frob-conformance`; a
generated table in `docs/SAFETY.md` (one page, generated, never hand-edited); the rule that a new verb, format
or importer must name the threat rows it touches. Out: every mechanism itself (each has its own bead, named in
the register); the owner-held configuration and the no-off-switch sweep (N37-02); the known-answer,
counterexample and refusal registries of the chapter (N37-KA, N37-CX, N37-REF).

## Technical approach

1. **File and schema (DESIGN).** `registries/threats.toml`, read with the strict TOML-subset reader of
   `frob-core` (unknown key or duplicate key is an error with line and column). One `[[asset]]` table per asset
   (`id = "T1"`, `name`, `out_of_scope = ["a local user with root"]`), and one `[[threat]]` table per in-scope
   threat:
   ```toml
   [[threat]]
   id         = "T2.b"                       # stable, never reused
   asset      = "T2"
   statement  = "a result cited as more than it is"
   mechanisms = ["claim-product", "citation-check", "d4-non-evidence"]
   invariants = ["INV-SAFE-06", "INV-SAFE-07"]
   refusals   = ["FROB-REF-819", "FROB-REF-825"]
   gates      = ["GATE-SAFE-08", "GATE-SAFE-09"]
   controls   = ["NC-…"]                     # negative-control identifiers that plant this threat
   risks      = []                           # RISK-nn rows of the risk register it mitigates
   ```
2. **Rows at plan time (the content the file starts with).**
   | Threat | Mechanisms | Invariants | Refusals | Gates | Risk |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | T1.a confused, wasteful or injected agent | data-not-code admission; capability scopes; session budgets; admission guard; path jail | INV-SAFE-01 | 828, 734 (and the budget and scope codes 701 to 704, 720, 723) | GATE-SAFE-10 | — |
   | T1.b hostile imported file | importer limits; fuzz targets; weight-file admission; imported text as data | INV-SAFE-01, INV-SAFE-02 | 832, 828 | GATE-SAFE-07, GATE-SAFE-02 | RISK-49 |
   | T2.a agent adapts to held-out worlds | confirmation isolation in three layers | INV-SAFE-03 | 840 | GATE-SAFE-03 | RISK-34 |
   | T2.b result cited as more than it is | claim product at the boundary; citation check; D4 as non-evidence | INV-SAFE-06, INV-SAFE-07 | 819, 825 | GATE-SAFE-08, GATE-SAFE-09 | — |
   | T2.c stale or forged artifact | content addressing; kernel-cache hash check; one runtime package; the std-only auditor | INV-SAFE-08 | 816 (foreign artifact) | GATE-SAFE-06 | — |
   | T3.a private scan or derivative leaves or is listed | mark join over lineage; private root; views; export scope and flag; `scene.purge`; licence join | INV-SAFE-04 | 731, 733 | GATE-SAFE-04, GATE-SAFE-05 | RISK-12, RISK-11 |
   | T4.a blind retry | idempotency binding (one job, one charge) | — (INV-TETHER-05, owned by the surface chapter) | 803, 805 | GATE-TETHER-05 | — |
   | T4.b write into another's lineage | namespaces | — | 725 | GATE-SAFE-10 (path half); the scope tests of the capability bead | — |
   | T5.a artifact mistaken for permission | the no-authorization type and its reader-side refusal | INV-SAFE-05 | 816 | GATE-SAFE-01 | — |
3. **The traceability test** (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/threat_register.rs`). It loads the register, the
   gate register, the refusal registry and the negative-control registry and asserts, for every `[[threat]]`:
   at least one mechanism; every gate identifier exists; every refusal code exists; at least one control whose
   `gate` list intersects the threat's gates and whose `last_seen.caught` is true once the gate's milestone has
   closed (before that the control must exist with `caught` unset). Every `GATE-SAFE-nn` appears under at least
   one threat, and every `INV-SAFE-nn` under at least one threat. An asset with no in-scope threat fails.
4. **The touch rule.** Each verb row of `registries/verbs.toml` and each importer row of the import-profile
   registry carries `threats = ["T1.b", …]` (DESIGN field). The test fails on a verb of kind `mutate`, `run` or
   `admin`, or any importer, with an empty list, and on a list naming an unknown threat. This is how a new
   surface is forced to state which rows it widens.
5. **Generated page.** `docs/SAFETY.md` is generated from the register (the two tables above, then one line per
   threat with its gate status from the gate register). The generated-file check compares it; it is listed in
   the generator's manifest so no seventh workspace check is added.
6. **Determinism and evidence.** Pure data handling, class D0. The test's output is a Known answer (Tested).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/threats.toml` holds the five assets and the nine threat rows above and loads under the strict
      reader.
- [ ] The traceability test passes; removing the only control of a threat, deleting a gate identifier from the
      gate register, or adding a `run` verb with an empty `threats` list each makes it fail with the row named.
- [ ] Every `GATE-SAFE-01` to `GATE-SAFE-10` and every `INV-SAFE-01` to `INV-SAFE-08` is reachable from a row.
- [ ] `docs/SAFETY.md` regenerates byte-identically; a hand edit is caught by the generated-file check.
- [ ] The out-of-scope strings are printed by `frob robot-docs` under a heading "What these mechanisms do not
      defend against" (generated from the register).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test threat_register` (real registry files; the mutated copies are labelled
`synthetic`):
- `register_loads_five_assets_nine_threats`.
- `every_threat_has_mechanism_gate_control`.
- `every_safe_gate_is_reached` / `every_safe_invariant_is_reached`.
- `unknown_gate_fails` (synthetic copy naming `GATE-SAFE-99`): error names the row and the identifier.
- `unknown_refusal_fails` (synthetic copy naming `FROB-REF-999`).
- `verb_without_threats_fails` (synthetic verb row of kind `run`).
- `asset_without_threat_fails`.
- `toml_duplicate_key_is_error` on the register itself.

### End-to-end script and logging
No verb of its own. `scripts/e2e/safe_flags.sh` (N37-24) and the other safety scripts each begin with a step
`threat_register` that runs the test above through `cargo test` and logs, per the shared helper of STD-E2E, one
`assert` line per threat row (`threat`, `gates`, `controls`, `status`) and the count of rows; the `summary`
names no gate (this bead measures none).

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answer: 5 assets, 9 threats, 10 gates reached, 8 invariants reached. Negative controls are the four
synthetic mutations above (each must fail the test). Determinism lanes: the test output is identical twice on
one machine, in debug and release, on Linux and macOS (D0).

## Considerations

- The register is a map, not a proof: a threat with a mechanism, a gate and a caught control can still be
  under-defended. The exit review reads the page; rule 4 of the negative-control registry (a control that has
  never failed its gate is suspect) is what catches a gate that is not looking.
- Out-of-scope does not mean ignored in documentation: reports and `robot-docs` print the list so that nobody
  reads a capability scope as a defence against root.
- Risk rows served: RISK-11 (asset licences restrict what can ship), RISK-12 (a private scan leaks through a
  derived artifact), RISK-34 (qualification gamed by a leak from sealed worlds), RISK-49 (imported text carries
  instructions to an agent). Pre-mortem item "qualification was gamed" notes that whoever holds the owner's
  qualification scope is trusted: that is the out-of-scope entry of T2.
- Revisit when a new surface appears that none of the nine rows covers (for example a network listener beyond
  loopback): that is a new threat row, decided by ADR, not a new verb flag.
