## Background

An authorized agent can still be confused or wasteful: it can ask for 10¹² worlds, trace every channel of
every world, open a thousand subscriptions, or retry one submission in a loop. The threat model protects the
owner's machines and devices from such an agent, and other agents' work from a client that retries blindly.
The protection is **resource governance**: budgets, quotas and draining cancellation; device-memory and disk
preflight; and the compute envelope (the owner-approved class of work a job may be; the fail-closed default
is 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores, one hour and one device).

This bead owns the additions the safety chapter states on top of the job engine:

1. allocation sizes are validated before any device work;
2. each call has a wall-clock ceiling, and each session a budget in every unit a budget can cap;
3. the MCP server applies the library's rate limiting and a bound on subscription listeners;
4. trace bytes are a budgeted quantity, so telemetry cannot fill a disk;
5. the admission guard holds device utilization under 0.8 (TARGET).

(A sixth, the remote worker's manifest, is N37-14.) Much of the machinery is built elsewhere: estimates with
calibrated intervals, admission against the upper end of the interval, size classes and the guard, the
crash-consistent job protocol, one job and one charge per idempotency key. What is missing without this bead
is the guarantee that **no request path reaches a device, a large host allocation or the disk before those
checks**, in every unit, with nothing defaulting to unlimited. Rejected alternative: relying on the operating
system to fail allocations. An out-of-memory kill takes the daemon and every other agent's jobs with it.

## Scope

In: checked size arithmetic from request to bytes; the order of all preflight checks; per-verb wall-clock
ceilings; completion of the budget vector (no unbounded value; the fail-closed session defaults; disk bytes as
a ninth unit on the token); the debit of trace bytes at each chunk boundary; disk preflight; the values of the
MCP bounds (the rate limit and its burst, where they may be configured, the listener capacity, the request
timeout as a call's ceiling); a session-count cap; the exhaustion battery.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- The session budget ledger this bead completes (N32a-15, M2; this bead depends on it): the eight capped
  quantities and their unit keys, reservation of the estimate's upper end at admission, metering, settlement
  and release, `FROB-REF-701` and `-702` as budget refusals, and the charge formula for attributable device
  time with its overhead line and worked value. This bead restates none of that; it adds the defaults, the
  ninth unit and the chunk-boundary debit on top of that ledger's tables and event kinds.
- The wiring of the MCP bounds into the server (N32a-27, M3; this bead depends on it): `frob-mcp` is built with
  the library's rate limiter, `.subscription_capacity(64)` and `.request_timeout(40)` on both transports, reads
  the limit from the owner-held safety configuration and tests that it is in force. This bead owns the values
  and the battery cases that try to exhaust them.
- The compute-envelope check of preflight step 3: needed class, device-hour meter, `FROB-REF-761` and the rule
  for which envelope refusal fires where (N36-20; classes and record N21c-35).
- The estimator and its intervals (BD-219, BD-297); the scheduler, the guard's implementation and
  preempt-resume (BD-296); the job protocol and cancellation; tokens and scopes (BD-258); the remote worker
  (N37-14).

## Technical approach

1. **Sizes before work.** Every quantity a request can scale (worlds, episodes, horizon ticks, bucket
   capacities, contact and row capacities, traced worlds and channels, telemetry level, camera count,
   resolution and cadence, scene variants, population size, pairs, cells) is an `int53` or a typed quantity
   in the input schema, with a schema maximum. The dispatcher computes the request's byte and work figures
   with checked integer arithmetic (an overflow is a refusal `FROB-REF-802` naming the field, never a wrap)
   by calling the same model `sim.estimate` exposes: device bytes (arena slabs, frame buffers, scene fields),
   host bytes, trace bytes, disk bytes, device seconds, CPU core-seconds. Only then may admission proceed.
   No handler allocates a buffer whose size came from a request without that figure having passed preflight
   (a lint forbids `Vec::with_capacity`, `vec![…; n]` and device-buffer creation on a value of the request
   type outside the admission module).
2. **Preflight order** (each failure is a typed refusal and nothing later runs):
   1. schema and range (`FROB-REF-802`, exit 3);
   2. device memory: the estimate's peak against the budget of the requested device (`FROB-REF-602`; exit 6).
      The fit test precedes the envelope check by the rule of the estimator bead (BD-219, DESIGN, tested there
      and in N36-REF as `fit_precedes_envelope`): a job that cannot fit the device is not repaired by approving
      a larger class, so it is refused with 602 and its fit repairs, never with 761's "request the class";
   3. the compute envelope: the job's class against the class in force for its scope (`FROB-REF-761`: a job
      exceeds the compute envelope in force; exit 4). This is the check of N36-20, called here in its place in
      the order. `FROB-REF-704` (a campaign-class job without an approved envelope; exit 4) is not built at
      this step: by the rule of N36-20 the daemon's size classifier (BD-296) refuses a queued `campaign`-class
      job with no approved envelope for its scope with 704 when it is submitted, before envelope admission is
      reached, and the scheduler refuses with 704 a queued job whose approval expires or is withdrawn before
      dispatch; `admit` never builds 704. Both envelope refusals come before the session-budget check of step 4;
   4. session budgets, using the upper end of the estimate interval in each unit (`FROB-REF-701` with the
      estimate attached; exit 4);
   5. disk: free space on the store's file system minus a reserve (DESIGN: the larger of 8 GiB and 10% of the
      volume) must exceed the upper end of the disk estimate (`FROB-REF-701` with unit `disk_bytes`);
   6. the admission guard: device utilization at or above 0.8 (TARGET), or the per-environment job cap
      reached (`FROB-REF-703`, `retry: backoff`; exit 4);
   7. the idempotency binding, the budget reservation and the job row in one transaction.
3. **The budget vector, complete and never unlimited.** The units a budget can cap are the eight of the
   session budget ledger (N32a-15: `wall_s`, `sim_s`, `gpu_s`, `cpu_core_s`, `device_mem_bytes`,
   `trace_bytes`, `episodes`, `solver_iters`); this bead adds a ninth carried by the token, `disk_bytes`. A
   unit the token does not name takes the session default from the owner-held configuration (DESIGN defaults
   equal to the envelope class E0 and, for trace and disk bytes, 1 GiB and 2 GiB); the type has no "unbounded"
   value. How a job is charged under shared batches (attributable device time) is N32a-15's formula and is
   not restated here.
4. **Wall-clock ceiling per call.** Each verb row has `max_wall_ms` (DESIGN defaults: `read` and `mutate`
   30,000; `run` the smaller of its wall-time budget and the envelope class's limit). The field is a member
   of N32a-02's closed entry schema (optional, an `int53` of milliseconds; absent means the default of the
   row's kind); the dispatcher reads the kind default for every row that does not carry it. In-process handlers
   check the job context's deadline at their cancellation checkpoints; a job that reaches its ceiling stops at
   a safe boundary, publishes nothing partial as a result, and ends with `FROB-REF-702` naming the last
   committed tick. The MCP server's request timeout is 40 s, above the longest bounded wait of 30 s.
5. **Trace bytes.** At admission the job's `trace_bytes` are reserved like every other unit: the upper end of
   the trace estimate is debited from the session's remaining budget as a `reserve` event (N32a-15), and a
   request whose upper end does not fit is refused with `FROB-REF-701` (unit `trace_bytes`). What this bead
   adds is the publisher side: in the publishing transaction of each chunk, the chunk publisher records the
   chunk's encoded size as a `charge` event against the job's `trace_bytes` reservation. If the charge would
   exceed the reservation (the estimate was too low) or the call's own budget, the job stops at that chunk
   boundary with `FROB-REF-702` (unit `trace_bytes`): committed chunks stay, nothing partial is published as a
   result. The telemetry level is never silently lowered to fit.
6. **MCP bounds.** The values are set here and nowhere else: the library's rate limiter at 600 requests per
   minute per session with a burst of 60 (DESIGN default; configurable only in the owner-held file. The plan
   gives no figure. This one is chosen over the alternative of 20 calls per second with a burst of 40, which
   the transport bead first carried, for one reason: the limit is a safety value, so it has one owner, the
   owner-held safety configuration of N37-02, which already states 600 per minute and 60, and the Rate case of
   the battery below is sized on it), a subscription listener capacity of 64 streams server-wide (FACT: the library's
   stated limit), and a request timeout of 40 s. The transport bead N32a-27 builds the server with these
   (`.rate_limiting`, `.subscription_capacity(64)`, `.request_timeout(40)`), reading the rate from the
   owner-held file, on stdio and on HTTP. A subscription beyond capacity is refused by the library; the client
   falls back to polling `job.wait`. Requests over the rate receive the library's rate-limit error before any
   handler runs and are charged nothing.
7. **Session count.** At most 256 open sessions per principal, counting derived ones (DESIGN); the 257th
   `session.open` is `FROB-REF-703` with `observed: sessions` (a code `session.open`'s row in VERBS-session
   lists for this cap).
8. **Blind retries.** One job and one budget charge per (principal, verb, idempotency key) under any
   interleaving; a mutating or job-submitting call without a key is `FROB-REF-805`. This bead adds the storm
   test below to the idempotency gate's evidence.
9. **Classes and records.** Admission decisions are D0 given the estimate. Each refusal is a ledger row with
   the figures that caused it. No wall-clock quantity enters a result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The exhaustion battery (below) ends, case by case, in the stated refusal, and for every refused case the
      device allocated-bytes counter, the store's size and the process's peak resident memory are unchanged
      within noise (TARGET: device 0 bytes; store 0 bytes; host under 16 MiB above baseline).
- [ ] A token that names no trace or disk budget is limited by the defaults (1 GiB and 2 GiB), not unlimited.
- [ ] A job whose traces would exceed the trace budget is refused at admission by the estimate, and a job
      whose estimate was too low stops at the chunk boundary with `FROB-REF-702` and a contiguous frontier.
- [ ] With utilization forced to 0.8, a new submission is `FROB-REF-703` with `retry: backoff`.
- [ ] The allocation lint fails on a planted `Vec::with_capacity(request.worlds)` in a handler.
- [ ] 1,000 parallel submissions with one idempotency key leave one job and one charge.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-verbs --test governance` and `-p frob-cli --test admission_order` (the real ledger; the
device is `batch-cpu`; no mocks):
- `size_arithmetic_checked`: worlds 9,007,199,254,740,991 × horizon 9,007,199,254,740,991 → `FROB-REF-802`
  (overflow), no panic.
- `frame_bytes_known_answer`: a 640 × 480 frame with 4 bytes of colour and 4 of depth is 2,457,600 bytes; two
  cameras on 256 worlds are 1,258,291,200 bytes.
- `preflight_order`: a request failing steps 2, 3 and 4 at once (too large for the device, above E0 and over
  the session budget) reports `FROB-REF-602` (the first); with the device fit restored it reports
  `FROB-REF-761`; with the class raised as well, `FROB-REF-701`.
- `budget_vector_has_no_unbounded`: compile-time (no such variant) and a default-fill test.
- `wall_ceiling_stops_at_boundary`: a `batch-cpu` run with a 2 s ceiling ends `FROB-REF-702` with a committed
  tick and no result.
- `trace_reservation_stops_at_chunk`.
- `disk_reserve_rule`: free space 9 GiB on a 50 GiB volume (reserve 8 GiB) admits a 0.9 GiB estimate and
  refuses 1.1 GiB.
- `session_cap_257`.
- `planted_allocation_fails_lint`.

**The exhaustion battery** (`governance_battery`, also driven end to end):
| Case | Request | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Worlds | `sim.run` with 10¹² worlds | `FROB-REF-802` (over the schema maximum) |
| Memory | 8,192 worlds with two 640 × 480 cameras each (37.5 GiB of frames) on a 24 GB device | `FROB-REF-602` |
| Envelope | A 3-hour job under class E0 | `FROB-REF-761` |
| Budget | A job whose estimate upper end is 601 GPU seconds with 600 left | `FROB-REF-701`, estimate attached |
| Traces | The highest telemetry level on 8,192 worlds with a 1 GiB trace budget | `FROB-REF-701` (unit `trace_bytes`) |
| Utilization | Submissions until utilization reaches 0.8 | `FROB-REF-703`, `retry: backoff` |
| Subscriptions | 65 resource subscriptions | The 65th refused; 64 active |
| Rate | 2,000 requests in one minute over MCP | Requests over the limit refused before any handler; no charge |
| Retry storm | 1,000 parallel `study.submit` with one key | One job, one charge, 999 replays |
| Sessions | 257 derived sessions | The 257th `FROB-REF-703` |

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/safe_capabilities.sh` (N37-15), step `governance`, runs the battery through `frob` and
`frob-mcp` against `frobd` and logs per STD-E2E: each command line, exit code, refusal code with `observed`,
`required` and `unit`, the estimate block, and `metric` lines for device bytes allocated, store bytes and
peak resident memory before and after each case against their thresholds.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the frame arithmetic above (KA-817). The slot-share arithmetic of attributable device time (a
96-pair comparison occupies 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.34%, KA-761) is N32a-15's known answer and is tested there.
Counterexample protected: "device memory limits the number of worlds" is false for state (a world is about 35
kB for a quadruped and 97 kB for a humanoid, so 8,192 worlds are 0.28 GB and 0.79 GB) and true for camera
frames, which is why frames enter the estimate (CX-381). Negative controls (built with `--features
gauntlet-mutations`): a build that allocates
before preflight (the device-bytes metric must move and the battery must fail); one whose trace budget
defaults to unlimited; the two-statement idempotency variant (two jobs under the storm). Determinism lanes:
admission verdicts identical twice on one machine for the same ledger state.

## Considerations

- The safety chapter defines no gate of its own for resource governance; its evidence is this battery plus the
  surface chapter's gates on estimates, idempotency and scheduling. The battery's controls are registered
  against those gates so that it is run whenever they are.
- The guard's threshold and the per-environment job cap are TARGETs to be refitted from the M2 queue trace.
- A rate limit protects the daemon, not fairness between agents; fairness is the scheduler's deficit
  round-robin.
- The disk reserve is a DESIGN default; on the training machine the store may live on a volume shared with
  other projects, and the owner sets the reserve accordingly.
