## Background

The capability under test, from the user's side: several agents use one daemon, each under a capability token
that says what it may read, what it may spend and what it may publish; a request outside the token is refused
with no partial data; a sub-agent's token can only be narrower than its parent's; no agent writes into
another's lineage; no request reaches a device or the disk before its sizes and budgets are checked; and none
of this can be switched off by an argument.

There is no gate of the safety chapter dedicated to scopes and budgets (the path-jail gate has its own
script, and confirmation isolation has its own). This script is the acceptance evidence for the capability
bead's second done-condition, "a session without the scope receives the refusal and no partial data", and for
the resource-governance and worker-manifest beads.

## Scope

In: the script with its steps; its fixtures; assertions; logging. What this script proves, as distinct from the
surface chapter's script on the same mechanisms: breadth. The missing-scope refusal for every verb and every
required scope, with no existence oracle; the whole table of derivations that must be refused; the
cross-namespace export path; resource governance; no off switch; the worker manifest.

Out, with the bead that has it: depth on a few calls, in `scripts/e2e/tether_capabilities.sh` (N32b-05): one
refusal followed into the ledger and the session roots; the altered-token and expired-token refusals; the
accounting of a derived token that spends through a real job (the parent's `children` row, the exact regain at
close); the write refusal `FROB-REF-725` on another agent's design, forks and job listings across namespaces;
sealed reads (`FROB-REF-840`); a client killed mid-job; shared views compared between agents. The two scripts
share no assertion. Also out: the path-jail sweep (`safe_path_jail.sh`); confirmation isolation (the
qualification-lane script and N37-16); private places (`safe_private_licence.sh`).

## Technical approach

`scripts/e2e/safe_capabilities.sh [--mcp] [--seed N]` (plain shell; sources the shared helper of STD-E2E;
idempotent; a fresh state root and project under `target/e2e/safe_capabilities/<run>/`; `frobd` started on
`batch-cpu`). Before `frobd` starts, the script writes the two grants with STD-E2E's `e2e_session grant
agent-a --scopes <list> --namespace agent-a` and the same for `agent-b` with namespace `agent-b` (the
`principal.<id>` tables of N37-02, which the daemon reads once at start). `<list>` is every scope that a row
of `frob schema --verbs --json` cites, in `capabilities`, `option_capabilities` or an action's list, except
the `owner_only` scopes of N32b-01's catalogue (an agent's table cannot hold them), plus the budget scopes
`gpu:seconds=600`, `cpu:core_seconds=7200` and `disk:bytes=2147483648`; so each grant holds everything its
steps ask for.

Steps:

1. `sessions`: `e2e_session open a --principal agent-a --scopes <list> --budget gpu_s=600,cpu_core_s=7200`
   and `e2e_session open b --principal agent-b` with the same scopes and budget. The helper runs `frob --json
   session open … --key "$(e2e_key <label>)"` (`session.open` is a `mutate` verb whose key is required,
   `FROB-REF-805` without it) with `FROB_SESSION_TOKEN` removed from that command's environment (a root open
   presenting a token is `FROB-REF-723`, N32b-01), and names the scopes, since omitted scopes grant no
   non-budget scope. Every derivation below, `session open --derive` under the parent's token in
   `FROB_SESSION_TOKEN`, names its scopes and carries its own key as well. `session show` for each. Assert
   each shows its scopes, budgets, namespace and the safety configuration digest. Tokens stay in the
   helper's token files (`e2e_session use a` and `use b` switch between them) and never reach the log.
2. `scope_matrix`: `frob schema --verbs --json` gives every verb with its required scopes. For each verb, for
   each required scope: derive from `agent-a` a token lacking exactly that scope (and, for a `<c>:read` scope,
   also lacking `<c>:run` and `<c>:write`, which imply it under the capability bead's implied-read rule and
   would otherwise bring it back at derivation) and call the verb on an
   existing object and on a non-existing identifier. Assert both calls: exit 5, `refusal.code =
   FROB-REF-720`, `refusal.required` names the missing scope, the envelope has no `result`, no `cursor` and no
   `job` member, and the two envelopes are byte-identical apart from the request echo (no existence oracle).
   Then with the full token: exit 0, 2 or 8. A scope that is `owner_only` (`qual:admin`, `session:audit`) is
   checked on the refusal side only: `agent-a` cannot hold it, so its full-token call is not made.
3. `attenuation`: derive a child with a subset of scopes and 350 of 600 GPU seconds while the parent has 250
   spent (expect success; parent shows 0 left); attempt children with one extra scope, with 1 second more than
   remaining, with a later expiry, with a foreign namespace (each `FROB-REF-723`, exit 5, the refusal naming
   the element that widens: the scope, the budget field, the expiry or the namespace); close the child
   after it spent 100 (parent regains 250). Attempt to derive the owner's qualification scope (refused).
   Derive a child asking `sim:run` alone: its `session show` lists `sim:read` and `sim:run` (the implied
   read, closed at derivation, BD-258), and it is not refused as a widening.
   This step is the only place the refused derivations are asserted. (A token with one character changed, and
   an expired token, are asserted in `tether_capabilities.sh`.)
4. `namespaces`: `agent-a` creates a design and edits it; `agent-b` reads it through the shared archive
   (succeeds, read-only: the archive view offers no write) and names `exports/agent-a/…` as an output path
   (expect `FROB-REF-734`). (The write refusal `FROB-REF-725` on another agent's design is asserted in
   `tether_capabilities.sh`, group `ns-*`.)
5. `governance`: the exhaustion battery, one command per case: 10¹² worlds (`FROB-REF-802`); camera frames
   beyond device memory (`FROB-REF-602`, skipped with a logged reason on a host with no device, where the
   `batch-cpu` memory budget is used instead); a 3-hour job under the default envelope (`FROB-REF-761`); an
   estimate over the remaining budget (`FROB-REF-701` with the estimate attached); traces over the trace
   budget (`FROB-REF-701`, unit `trace_bytes`); utilization at the guard (`FROB-REF-703`, `retry: backoff`);
   a 1,000-way retry storm with one idempotency key (one job, one charge; 999 envelopes with
   `idempotent_replay: true`); the 257th open session of one principal, derived ones counted
   (`FROB-REF-703`, N37-13). Before and after each refused case the helper records
   device bytes allocated (`frob --json spark devices`), store size (`frob --json store status`) and the
   daemon's peak resident memory.
6. `no_off_switch`: calls `scripts/e2e/safe_no_off_switch.sh` (created at M1 with the owner-held
   configuration bead), extended here with the M3 cases: refused requests (a path outside the roots, an
   over-limit import, a request lacking a scope, a private export) replayed with each of `--force`, `--no-jail`, `--trusted`, `--skip-limits` and a JSON member
   `"unsafe": true`. Assert `FROB-REF-802` for the unknown arguments and that the original refusal is
   unchanged without them.
7. `worker_manifest` (N37-14's cases): a referee rollout through the worker process (`frob` built with
   feature `referee-remote`); then with one input tampered in the sender's store (refused, job not started);
   then a manifest with a 1 MiB output quota submitted to the real worker through `cargo run -p frob-fsim
   --example worker_submit -- --manifest <file>` (N37-14's example; there is no `frob-conformance` binary)
   (stopped at a chunk boundary, `FROB-REF-702`, the receiver holding at most 1,048,576 bytes).
8. `mcp` (with `--mcp`): steps 2 (a sample of 20 verbs), 3 and 4 over stdio and HTTP; a tool call under a
   token lacking the scope returns an error result whose structured content is the same refusal envelope.

**Logging.** `target/e2e-logs/safe_capabilities/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a transcript. Every line: `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`N37-15`), `step`, `event`, the exact command line with the token replaced by its
identifier, exit code, wall time, the envelope's `evidence` block, the refusal code with `observed`,
`required` and `unit`, scopes held and required, budgets before and after with units; for step 5 `metric`
lines `device_bytes_delta`, `store_bytes_delta`, `rss_delta` against thresholds 0, 0 and 16 MiB; for step 2
the digest of both refusal envelopes; at start one `metric` line `retention` with the three values of
OPEN-SAFE-4 read from `frob doctor --json` and the ADR identifier or `default` (N37-30 relies on this line).
On failure: the first differing value and the saved envelope paths.
`summary`: assertions passed and failed; `gates: []` (no gate of its own; the surface chapter's gates are
named in the transcript where a step contributes evidence to them).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 on a clean build on a host with `batch-cpu`; device-only cases are either run or
      logged as skipped with the reason.
- [ ] Step 2 covers every verb in the registry and every required scope of each; every refusal has no
      `result`, and refusals for existing and non-existing objects are identical.
- [ ] Step 3's arithmetic holds exactly: 600 − 250 = 350 grantable; after the child spends 100 and closes, the
      parent has 250.
- [ ] Step 5's deltas are within thresholds for every refused case; the retry storm leaves one job and one
      charge.
- [ ] No token appears in the log or the transcript (the helper scans both for each token's bytes).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test caps_helpers`: `derive_token_lacking_exactly_one_scope`;
`lacking_read_also_drops_run_and_write` (the derived token for a missing `<c>:read` holds neither `<c>:run`
nor `<c>:write`, so the closure cannot restore it); `scope_free_verbs_listed_and_read_or_public` (a planted
scope-free `mutate` row without `public = true` fails); `envelope_diff_ignores_request_echo_only`;
`log_redaction_removes_tokens`.

### End-to-end script and logging
This bead is the script; it runs in the ordinary end-to-end suite from M3, and its `sessions`, `namespaces`
and `no_off_switch` steps run from M2 when tokens and budgets first exist.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the attenuation arithmetic; one job and one charge under the storm. Negative controls (builds
with `--features gauntlet-mutations`, each must make the script fail at the named step): scopes checked after argument
resolution (step 2: the two refusal envelopes differ); superset derivation accepted (step 3); allocation
before preflight (step 5: `device_bytes_delta` above 0); `--trusted` honoured (step 6); a worker that skips
input verification (step 7). Determinism lanes: verdicts identical twice on one machine and across Linux and
macOS for the same seed.

## Considerations

- The scope matrix is generated from the registry, so a new verb is covered the day it is registered. A verb
  whose row cites no scope has no matrix row of its own (its `option_capabilities`, N32b-01, are rows); the
  script logs every such verb and asserts that each is of kind `read` or carries `public = true`: the
  `mutate` rows `session.open` (a root open runs before any token exists and mints from the owner-held
  grant), `session.focus` and `session.close`, which act only on the caller's own session (VERBS-session).
  Any other scope-free `run`, `mutate` or `admin` verb fails step 2.
- The script never uses the owner's qualification scope; steps that would need it belong to the
  qualification-lane script.
- Runtime is dominated by step 2 (verbs × scopes × two calls); all are refusals before any work, so the whole
  matrix runs in seconds.
