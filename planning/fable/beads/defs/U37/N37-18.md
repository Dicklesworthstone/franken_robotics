## Background

Licences are enforced at pack time. Each asset has a manifest entry (its source, its licence and any
restriction on redistribution), and the entry travels with every pack that contains the asset. **Research-only
assets cannot enter an exported report or a redistributed pack.**

The classes are `redistributable`, `attribution`, `research-only`, `proprietary` and `unknown`, and `unknown`
is treated as the most restrictive. The licence set of a derived artifact is the join over its inputs and
travels in its manifest (the mark lattice and its join are N37-17); an export whose set forbids it is refused
with `FROB-REF-733`, and an `attribution` class adds the notices to the exported report.

FACT (a research pass on 2026-11-21; licences are re-checked at admission; an asset whose licence was not read
is marked so and stays outside the default pipeline). Reference robots: MuJoCo Menagerie models under BSD-3
(Unitree Go1, Go2, A1, H1, G1; ANYmal B and C; Spot), MIT (Cassie) and Apache-2.0 (Stretch 3, TIAGo, Booster
T1, Apptronik Apollo, Fourier N1, OP3, TALOS, Barkour); the suite's own robot models. Scene assets: this
project's procedural generators; Infinigen Indoors (BSD-3); Google Scanned Objects (CC-BY 4.0); RoboCasa (code
MIT, assets CC BY 4.0); ProcTHOR (code Apache-2.0, dataset licence not verified: generator reference only);
HSSD (CC BY-NC 4.0), HM3D (academic, non-commercial), PartNet-Mobility (non-commercial research terms) and
BEHAVIOR-1K (its own agreement): user-supplied import only, never redistributed; Objaverse-XL (per object, not
used by default). The starter pack ships only synthetic and redistributable fixtures.

**Dependencies are assets too.** FACT: the suite's MCP library and its mail server are under an MIT licence
with a rider (`fastmcp_rust/LICENSE`); that text is carried in the dependency's admission record and in any
redistributed binary's notices.

Risk served: RISK-11 (asset licences restrict what can ship; signal: a pack whose licence join is not
redistributable; mitigation: procedural first, licence manifests enforced at pack time, GATE-SAFE-05).
Rejected alternative: checking licences only when a scene pack is built. A research-only asset reaches a
report through a run, a study and a scorecard without any pack being built on the way.

## Scope

In: the single export admission function every exporting verb calls; the licence decision per export kind;
`FROB-REF-733` with the lineage path to the offending asset; the assembly and embedding of the notices (the
Notices section of a report, the `NOTICES` file of a pack, run, packet or policy export, the header block of a
replay) for `attribution`; generation of third-party notices for binaries; the laundering battery; the
measurement of GATE-SAFE-05.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- The lattice and join (N37-17).
- The per-asset licence manifest of a scene pack, the asset-source register, the starter-pack check, and the
  scene pack's own pack-time check `ScenePack::export_check` with its code `FROB-REF-310`: N19b-02, a
  dependency of this bead. The door calls that check for a scene pack and returns its refusal unchanged
  (step 1); it never raises `FROB-REF-310` itself, and that check never decides a report or any other
  derived artifact.
- The private mark's read and export rule with `FROB-REF-731` (BD-305).
- The registry rows of the exporting verbs, which list the door's codes (the report rows: VERBS-report).
- The dependency admission records themselves (BD-306).

## Technical approach

1. **One export door.** `frob_verbs::export::admit(artifact, kind, session) -> Result<ExportGrant, Refusal>`
   is the only producer of an `ExportGrant`, and every writer of bytes outside the store (the jailed export
   writer) requires one. Checks, in this order: the no-authorization flags are present in the artifact's
   manifest (`FROB-REF-816` otherwise); the privacy mark against scope and flag (`FROB-REF-731`); the licence
   set against the export kind (`FROB-REF-733`); the `export:*` scope that the verb's registry row lists in
   its `capabilities`, or in the `option_capabilities` of the option that writes (`FROB-REF-720`); the output
   path through the path jail (`FROB-REF-734`). A registry lint asserts that every verb whose entry has
   `exports = true` (a bool in the entry schema) routes through `admit` (handlers cannot reach the export
   writer without a grant: the grant type has no public constructor).

   **The exporters that land before the door (DESIGN).** Two exporters exist at M2 and make the door's checks
   in their own handlers until this bead lands: `telemetry.export-html --out` (N24a-41, its step 5b; export
   kind `replay`) and `run.export` (N29a-02; export kind `run`). This bead replaces those handler calls by
   `admit` for both verbs; the codes, the exit codes (816 exit 3; 731, 733, 734 exit 5), the fields of each
   refusal and the order do not change, and each verb's tests that assert them (N24a-41's
   `private_scene_export_refused_731`, `licence_forbids_export_733`, `input_flags_816`, `out_path_jail_734`,
   `export_checks_precede_writing`; N29a-02's `input_flags_816` and `licence_733`) pass unchanged against the
   door. The order is the door's (flags, privacy, licence, scope, path), which the M2 handlers adopt so that the
   switch changes no answer (requested of N24a-41 and N29a-02, whose text lists privacy and licence before
   flags; no test of either distinguishes the two orders, since each refused case fails one check).
   `export_m2_exporters_unchanged_behind_door` (below) re-runs those cases through the door.

   **Diagnostic writers are exports; there is no exception (DESIGN).** An operator option that writes a file
   outside the store with no manifest of its own, such as `sim.run --resolved-out` (the resolved
   randomization lists as JSON lines), takes an `ExportGrant` like any other writer, under export kind
   `diagnostic`. Reasons: a resolved list of a world in a private or research-only scene is derived from
   that scene and would leave the machine's store without its mark; and the flag sweep (BD-303) fails any
   file under the export root that no locator row matches. The file's flags carrier is a header line
   (locator carrier `header`): its first JSON line is `{"schema": "frob.diag-header/1", "kind": "<artifact
   kind>", "authorization": {the three false fields}, "privacy": "<mark>", "licences": [<set>]}`, written by
   the writer from the grant, and every following line is data. The `FROB-REF-816` check for such a file runs
   on the header object the writer submits to `admit` before it writes a byte: a descriptor without the
   object, or a locator row for the option that names no carrier, is `FROB-REF-816`. The verb's row then
   carries `exports = true`, lists 731, 733 and 816 beside 734, and puts its publish scope (`export:report`)
   in the option's `option_capabilities`, so a call without the option needs no publish scope. The path
   jail alone was the rejected alternative: it keeps the bytes under the export root but lets a private or
   research-only derivative out unmarked.

   **A scene pack is decided by its own check (DESIGN).** When the artifact is a scene pack, a scene
   variant, or a kit or starter pack that contains scene packs (export kind `pack`), `admit` replaces its
   privacy and licence steps by a call to `ScenePack::export_check` (N19b-02) for each scene pack contained,
   after the flags check and before the scope and path checks, and returns that function's refusal
   unchanged: `FROB-REF-310`, exit 3, for a private pack or for an entry that is `research-only`,
   `proprietary`, `unknown` or not read. Every other artifact, of every kind (a report, a run bundle, a
   still, a replay, a policy, a certificate bundle, a build packet, a design, and a kit that contains no
   scene pack), gets `FROB-REF-731` or `FROB-REF-733` from the door, exit 5. So a report that would carry a
   research-only asset is `FROB-REF-733`, and one derived from a private scene is `FROB-REF-731`; neither
   is ever `FROB-REF-310`. The plan defines code 310 in the scene chapter with the text "a pack or report
   would export a research-only or private asset" and codes 731 and 733 in this chapter, and does not
   divide them. Reasons for this division: the plan says a pack is refused "at pack time (`FROB-REF-310`,
   and §37)", which makes the pack the subject of 310 and leaves the rest to §37; a report holds no pack
   manifest, so only the lineage join can find the asset, and the `via` path of step 3 is what the repair
   needs; one request must get exactly one code; and a private scene pack is the scan itself, which stays
   on the machine that holds it whatever scope and flag the caller holds, while the artifacts derived
   from it can be released under `private:export` (BD-305). When the check passes, the notice items it
   returns are the items of step 4 for that pack.
2. **Decision by class (DESIGN).** Export kinds (ten): `report`, `pack` (a scene pack, a kit, a starter pack),
   `run` (a run bundle or a view of it; this includes the counterexample-world file `.fmcw` that
   `nemesis.shrink --out` writes, a derived view of a run's failing world, VERBS-nemesis and BD-274), `replay`
   (the self-contained page), `policy`, `bundle` (a certificate bundle), `packet` (a build packet),
   `design`, `diagnostic` (an operator diagnostic file with a header line, step 1), `geometry` (a loose
   geometry file outside a packet, written by `foundry.export --geometry-only`, BD-407 and N31a-34 step 7: its
   flags carrier is the sidecar `<file>.flags.json` beside the file, N37-23's locator carrier `sidecar`, which
   the door reads for the flags check; a file handed over without its sidecar is `FROB-REF-816`).
   | Class of the artifact's licence set | Decision for every export kind |
   |---|---|
   | `redistributable` | Allowed |
   | `attribution` | Allowed; the notices of every attribution entry are added to the export (step 4) |
   | `research-only` | Refused, `FROB-REF-733` |
   | `proprietary` | Refused, `FROB-REF-733` |
   | `unknown` | Refused, `FROB-REF-733` (treated as the most restrictive) |
   The decision (allowed or refused) is the same for a scene pack; only the code of a refusal differs
   there, `FROB-REF-310` from the pack's own check (step 1).
   Reading an artifact through a verb is not an export and is not affected.
3. **The refusal.** `FROB-REF-733`, "the licence set of the artifact forbids this export". Exit 5;
   `retry: after-change`. Fields: `pointer` (the artifact), `observed` (the class and the offending entries:
   source, licence, class), `required` (`class at most attribution`), and `via`: for each offending entry the
   shortest `derived-from` path from the artifact to the root asset, as a list of (kind, digest), so the
   caller can see which input brought the restriction. Ranked repairs (DESIGN): (1) replace or remove the
   named asset and re-derive the result (the `via` path names what must be recomputed); (2) for `unknown`,
   record the asset's licence and source in its manifest entry so that it is classified; (3) keep the
   artifact local and read it through verbs. Licence strings are imported text: in the envelope they sit in
   fields marked untrusted.
4. **Notices.** For an `attribution` set the exporter assembles, deterministically (entries sorted by source
   then licence), a notices block: one item per entry with the source, the licence identifier and the notice
   text addressed by `notice`. A `report` gains a final section "Notices"; a `pack`, `run`, `packet` or
   `policy` export gains a `NOTICES` file listed in its manifest with its hash; a `replay` embeds the block in
   its header; a `diagnostic` file carries it as the field `notices` of its header line; a `geometry` file
   carries it as the field `notices` of its sidecar `<file>.flags.json`, so the notices travel with each loose
   file. An attribution entry with no notice text is treated as `unknown` (the notice cannot be honoured).
5. **Same bytes, same or higher mark.** Importing bytes whose digest already exists in the store joins the
   declared root mark with the existing object's mark; a re-import can raise a mark and never lower it. So an
   artifact read out through a verb and imported again under a permissive declaration keeps its restriction.
6. **Third-party notices for binaries.** A generator (part of the existing generated-artifact check, so no
   new workspace check is added) reads each workspace's lockfile and `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md` and writes
   `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES`: for every package in the closure of a shipped binary, its name, exact version or
   commit, licence identifier and, where the admission record carries one, the full licence text (the MIT
   text with its rider for the MCP library and the mail server). A package in a shipped binary's closure with
   no licence in its admission record fails generation. `frob doctor --notices` prints the file embedded in
   the binary.
7. **The laundering battery (the second clause of the gate).** Fixture: a scene containing one stand-in
   asset declared `research-only`. Each row is an attempt to get it, or something computed with it, exported:
   | Attempt | Expected |
   |---|---|
   | Export the compiled pack | Refused at pack time: `FROB-REF-310`, exit 3, from `ScenePack::export_check` (N19b-02), returned by the door unchanged |
   | Run, then export the run bundle | `FROB-REF-733` |
   | Still of the run, exported | `FROB-REF-733` |
   | Self-contained replay | `FROB-REF-733` |
   | Scorecard over 100 runs, one of which used the scene, as a report | `FROB-REF-733`, `via` naming that run |
   | Train on the scene, export the policy | `FROB-REF-733` while `licence.weights_inherit = true` (the default, option A of OPEN-SAFE-2); under a recorded option B or D, admitted with the weights' own licence set. The expected value is read from `frob doctor --json` (N37-20, a dependency: its option is recorded before this gate is measured) |
   | A comparison whose answer was served from the memo store | `FROB-REF-733` (the memo node carries the mark) |
   | A certificate bundle for a mission answer over the scene | `FROB-REF-733` |
   | Read the still's bytes with `artifact.read`, import them as a new asset declared `redistributable`, export | `FROB-REF-733` (step 5) |
   | A report citing a claim whose evidence closure contains the run | `FROB-REF-733` |
   After the asset is replaced by a redistributable one and the chain re-derived, every export succeeds.
8. **Classes.** D0. The licence class is a statement about declared inputs, not a legal judgement.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] GATE-SAFE-05 (TARGET): over random derivation graphs the licence set equals the join of the inputs (the
      property of N37-17, 10⁴ graphs), and a research-only asset cannot be made to enter an exported report or
      pack (all ten rows of the battery refused; the policy row as the OPEN-SAFE-2 option in force states, refused
      under the default). Measured with counts and the option by this bead's tests and N37-21's script; the
      gate is recorded by BD-305, its one recorder, from the tier receipt in which this bead's three
      registered controls are `caught`.
- [ ] Every exporting verb in the registry routes through `admit`; a planted exporter that writes without a
      grant does not compile; a planted registry row with `exports = true` and no route fails the lint.
- [ ] `telemetry.export-html --out` and `run.export` route through `admit` with the codes, exits, fields and
      order they answered at M2; `nemesis.shrink --out` is decided under kind `run`; a loose geometry file of
      `foundry.export --geometry-only` is decided under kind `geometry`, its sidecar carrying flags and notices,
      and without the sidecar it is `FROB-REF-816` with nothing written.
- [ ] `sim.run --resolved-out` on a run in the research-only fixture scene is `FROB-REF-733` and writes no
      byte; on a redistributable scene it writes a file whose first line is the `frob.diag-header/1` object
      with the three fields `false`; a planted diagnostic writer that submits no header is `FROB-REF-816`.
- [ ] An `attribution` export contains the notices block with one item per attribution entry, byte-identical
      across two exports; an attribution entry without notice text is refused as `unknown`.
- [ ] `FROB-REF-733` names the offending entries and a `via` path that ends at the root asset.
- [ ] Exactly one code per request: the pack row of the battery answers `FROB-REF-310`, exit 3, and no other
      row does; the nine other rows answer `FROB-REF-733`, exit 5; the door has no code path of its own that
      produces `FROB-REF-310`.
- [ ] `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` lists every package of each shipped binary's closure and includes the MIT text
      with its rider; removing a licence from one admission record fails generation.
- [ ] Re-import of existing bytes under a weaker declaration does not lower the mark.

## Considerations

- The appendix on known answers states no negative control for KA-832; the three planted builds above are
  registered as its controls so the gate can record PASS.
- Two chapters of the plan each define a code for a refused export: the scene chapter's `FROB-REF-310` and
  this chapter's `FROB-REF-733` (with `FROB-REF-731` for the private mark). Both are kept and divided by
  the artifact being exported (the DESIGN of step 1, stated identically in N19b-02): a scene pack answers
  310 from its own check; everything else, a report included, answers 733 or 731 at the door. The battery
  accepts 310 only on the pack row. A bead or registry row that expects 310 for a still, a run or a report
  is in error against this rule.
- There is no aggregate exception: a scorecard or any other aggregate whose lineage holds one research-only
  or proprietary input is refused like the input itself (battery row 5), whatever the share of that input.
  A bead elsewhere that assumes an aggregate may be exported over such lineage is in error against this
  table. Should the owner ever want a logged override for aggregates, it is a change to the door (a new
  owner-held scope checked in step 1 and recorded in the ledger), never a carve-out in an exporter.
- The tool enforces declared classes and refuses on doubt; it gives no legal advice. An owner who is entitled
  to export research-only material for a permitted use does so outside the tool or changes the declared class
  with a recorded source.
- Policy weights inherit the licence set of their training scenes by default (open question for the owner;
  N37-20).
- A model cannot redistribute what it cannot export, but `artifact.read` returns bytes to the caller; what a
  caller does with bytes it has read is outside the tool's reach and is stated as such in the agent guide.
