## Background

A scene pack built from a scan of a real home or workplace is private; it is stored under a configured
private root outside the repository, and everything derived from it inherits the mark. The owner must also be
able to take it back out: **`scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything
derived from it, and writes a receipt listing the removed digests. Content addressing makes the closure
computable.** It is the registered verb `scene.purge` (kind `admin`, first milestone M3, in the `scene`
cluster).

Why a verb and not "delete the directory": derived artifacts do not live in the scene's directory. Episodes,
chunks, snapshots, stills, replays, reports, fixtures, memoized results and policy weights trained in the
scene are separate objects, some of them cited by claims and pinned against collection. Deleting the pack
alone leaves all of them; deleting by hand leaves dangling evidence that later prints as available. The
artifact graph already records, for every stored object, what it was derived from; the purge walks that graph.

Part of the privacy gate rests on this verb: GATE-SAFE-04 (Known answer, TARGET, M3) requires that after
`scene.purge` no digest of the closure remains in the store. Risk served: RISK-12 (a scan of a private place
leaks through a still, a fixture or policy weights; mitigation includes `scene.purge`).

Rejected alternatives: expiring private data by retention policy (the owner wants it gone now, and a pinned
object never expires); a purge that refuses whenever a claim cites the data and offers no way through (it would
make a private scan permanent the moment anything is concluded from it; the rule adopted below refuses only
until the caller says the claims are to be withdrawn); cryptographic erasure by discarding a per-pack key
(attractive, and compatible with this design later, but derived artifacts are stored under the private root
as ordinary objects at M3).

## Scope

In: the verb, its arguments, scopes and schemas; the closure computation; the two-phase, crash-consistent
removal; the rule for a purge that meets pinned evidence (refusal `FROB-REF-813` unless the caller asks for the
claims to be withdrawn), withdrawal of claims and release of pins; eviction from the memo store, the world bank
and device arenas; the receipt; the export log's role; refusals. This bead is the only place the purge and its
meeting with the pin rule are specified. The query "live claims whose evidence closure meets this member
set" is answered here from the pin table (every claim writer pins its whole closure in the transaction that
writes the claim, N29b-13, so the pins on the members name exactly those claims). Out: the mark and its join
(N37-17); private storage, views and export rules (BD-305); the store functions the purge calls, which exist
before it (`withdraw(claim, reason)`, pin release, the tombstone writer and the pin rule with `FROB-REF-813`:
the store's beads and N29b-13, a dependency); what the purge means for Chronicle's telemetry of a private
scene, which N29b-14 states and tests after this bead (it depends on this one); the general collector (store
chapter); retention periods (N37-30).

## Technical approach

1. **Registry row (the row of VERBS-scene, restated).** `name = "scene.purge"`, `cluster = "scene"`,
   `kind = "admin"`, `capabilities = ["scene:admin", "private:read"]`,
   `input_schema = "frob.scene.purge.in/1"`, `output_schema = "frob.scene.purge.out/1"`, chapter refusals
   `["FROB-REF-813"]` (the surface codes, 720 and 802 among them, are appended by the registry and not listed
   here), `annotations.destructive = true` on the MCP tool. Arguments: `scene` (a `SceneId`); `dry_run`
   (Boolean, default true; CLI `--dry-run`, the real call `--dry-run=false`); `confirm` (the closure digest
   returned by a dry run; required when `dry_run` is false; CLI `--confirm <digest>`); `withdraw_claims`
   (Boolean, default false; CLI `--withdraw-claims`). No idempotency key and no `FROB-REF-805` (DESIGN, the
   `admin` kind of NJL-01, which takes no key, as BD-236 does for the bank's admin actions): the confirm
   digest guards against an unintended call, and a repeat of a completed purge finds the tombstones and
   returns the stored receipt as a no-op, so the call is idempotent by effect. A session without
   `private:read` meets `FROB-REF-720` (a listed scope is missing) and learns nothing about the scene, so
   `FROB-REF-731` is not raised by this verb.

   **A purge that meets pinned evidence** (DESIGN, the same in N29b-14; it settles two drafts, one of which
   withdrew claims silently inside the purge and never raised 813, the other of which refused with 813 unless a
   flag was passed). The store's pin rule refuses a deletion that would remove a pinned object, `FROB-REF-813`,
   with the repair "withdraw the claim first", and the verb registry lists 813 among this verb's refusals. The
   purge may perform that repair itself, but only when told to: if any member of the closure is in the evidence
   closure of a live claim, a non-dry call with `withdraw_claims = false` is refused with `FROB-REF-813` (the
   store's refusal, with the exit code the refusal registry gives it), listing the claims and the repair "repeat with `--withdraw-claims`, or withdraw them
   first"; with `withdraw_claims = true` the claims are withdrawn and the pins released in phase 1 below. Reason:
   withdrawing a claim changes what the project may assert, so it is never a side effect the caller did not ask
   for; and because the flag exists, a claim never makes a private scan permanent. With no live claim on any
   member the flag is not needed.
2. **Admission.** The session must hold both scopes (`FROB-REF-720` otherwise; a session without
   `private:read` learns nothing about whether the scene exists). The scene must exist and be marked private:
   a non-private scene is refused with `FROB-REF-802` (pointer `scene`, message "not a private scene pack; use
   the store's collector"). A non-dry call without `confirm`, or with a `confirm` that differs from the
   current closure digest (something was derived since the dry run), is refused with `FROB-REF-802` and the
   new digest.
3. **The closure.** Starting from the pack, follow `derived-from` edges forward (to children) to a fixed
   point, taking every edge whose `carries` includes privacy: every edge except a training-run edge written
   while the owner key `private.weights_inherit` was `false` (the filtered edge of N37-17, step 4a). Members
   by kind: compiled scenes and clearance complexes; episodes, runs, chunks, snapshots, events;
   stills, contact sheets, replay packets, telemetry summaries; studies, comparisons, verdicts and scorecard
   rows; policy weights and checkpoints trained on it (when weights inherit, the default; a checkpoint whose
   training-run edge does not carry privacy is not a member, is left in place, and is listed by the dry run
   and the receipt under `kept_by_weights_rule` with the configuration digest of its job row); memo nodes; reports,
   bundles, packets and kits that embed or cite a member; fixtures extracted from its runs; the ledger
   rows of all of these; and the job-log rows and idempotency-key rows of every job that read or wrote a
   member, removed whatever the retention period in force (the constraint of OPEN-SAFE-4, N37-30: a purge
   is not bounded below by the log period). Their tombstones carry reason `private-purge`. The closure digest is BLAKE3 over the sorted list of member digests.
   An aggregate that also has non-private inputs (a scorecard over 100 runs, one in the scene) is a member:
   it was derived from the scene, so it goes. The receipt lists it with `mixed: true` and the identifiers of
   its other inputs, so the owner can recompute it without the purged run. A study plan bound to the pack
   (`study.plan`) is not a member: it is a request record that holds identities, with no `derived-from` edge
   (N37-17's parents table has no row for it), and a digest reveals no content; a later `study.submit` of it
   is refused by N32a-13's re-validation with `FROB-REF-809`, exit 7, naming the removed pack (current
   identity: none), with nothing charged.
4. **Dry run.** Returns the closure digest, counts and bytes by kind, the live claims whose evidence closure
   meets the members (those a real call must be told to withdraw, with `withdraw_claims_required: true` when
   the list is non-empty), the pins that will be released, jobs currently running on the scene (the purge refuses to proceed while any is
   running: `FROB-REF-802` naming the jobs, with the repair "cancel them with `job.cancel`"), and the list of
   earlier exports of any member (from the export log), each with its time, kind, export path relative to
   the export root and digest: these left the store under the private export scope and flag and are **not**
   recalled by a purge.
5. **Removal, in two phases (a job; crash-consistent).**
   1. One ledger transaction: verify `confirm`; recompute the live claims whose evidence closure intersects
      the members and, if there are any and `withdraw_claims` is false, abort with `FROB-REF-813` and write
      nothing; otherwise write a `purge` row in state `purging` with the member list; withdraw every such claim
      (each becomes a tombstone with reason `private-purge`, the one spelling of that reason); release the
      members' retention pins; mark every member row `purging` (from this
      instant no verb can read a member: reads return the tombstone refusal).
   2. Idempotent deletion: evict memo nodes; drop the scene and its variants from the world bank and from any
      device arena at the next safe boundary; delete each member's blob beneath the private root through the
      store's retained handles; then a second transaction replaces each member's ledger rows with a tombstone
      (content hash, class, time, reason `private-purge`) and sets the purge row to `complete`.
   On restart the daemon resumes any purge in state `purging` from phase 2. A purge is never rolled back.
   3. When claims were withdrawn, the re-affirmation pass of recorded PASS parts and closing lines (N29bp1-01,
      which runs after any explicit withdrawal or scene purge) is run before the receipt is written; its
      report's digest goes into the receipt as `reaffirm_report` (null when no claim was withdrawn), so every
      demotion it records traces to this purge.
6. **The receipt** (schema `frob.purge-receipt/1`, canonical JSON, stored under the private root, marked
   private, and itself exempt from the purge it records):
   ```json
   { "schema": "frob.purge-receipt/1", "scene": "s:…", "closure_digest": "b3:…",
     "removed": [ { "digest": "b3:…", "kind": "still", "bytes": "48211", "mixed": false } ],
     "counts": { "episode": 1024, "chunk": 4096, "still": 12, "policy": 1, "report": 2 },
     "claims_withdrawn": ["c:…"], "pins_released": 17, "reaffirm_report": "b3:…",
     "exports_not_recalled": [ { "digest": "b3:…", "kind": "report", "at": "…", "relative_path": "…" } ],
     "kept_by_weights_rule": [],
     "peers_holding_copies": [], "completed_seq": "u64 as string",
     "authorization": { "execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false,
                        "sim_to_real_certified": false } }
   ```
   The envelope returns the receipt's digest and the counts.
7. **After a purge.** Reading any removed digest returns the tombstone refusal of the store (the citation
   refusal `FROB-REF-869` for a claim whose closure is tombstoned). A full scan of both store roots finds no
   blob whose BLAKE3 is in `removed`, and no ledger row of a member other than its tombstone. Tombstones and
   the receipt hold digests only; a digest does not reveal content.
8. **Other machines.** Private-marked objects cross to the other machine only as an export under the private
   export scope and flag, which the export log records. If the log shows such a transfer, the receipt lists
   the peer under `peers_holding_copies`; the owner runs `scene.purge` there with the same scene identifier.
9. **Classes.** D0; the closure is a function of the graph. The verb produces no evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After `scene.purge` on a private scene with a derived run, replay, stills, a report, a memoized
      comparison and trained weights, no digest of the closure remains in the store: a scan of both roots and
      of the ledger finds only tombstones, and `artifact.read` of each removed digest is refused.
- [ ] The receipt lists every removed digest; its `closure_digest` equals the dry run's; it carries the three
      no-authorization flags.
- [ ] With a live claim supported by a member, a real purge without `--withdraw-claims` is refused with
      `FROB-REF-813` listing the claim, and the store is unchanged (no purge row, no tombstone); with the flag
      the claim is withdrawn in the same transaction, with reason `private-purge`, and afterwards cannot be
      cited. A claim issued between the dry run and the real call is covered by the same rule, because the
      claims are recomputed inside the transaction.
- [ ] Killing the daemon at each of the two phase boundaries and during blob deletion, then restarting,
      completes the purge with the same receipt.
- [ ] A session lacking either scope is refused with no information about the scene; a non-private scene is
      refused; a stale `confirm` is refused with the new digest; a running job on the scene blocks the purge.
- [ ] Earlier exports of members are listed under `exports_not_recalled`.
- [ ] Objects not derived from the scene are untouched (their digests and marks are unchanged).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test purge -p frob-verbs --test scene_purge` (a real store and ledger in a
temporary state root; scenes are `synthetic` stand-ins marked private):
- `closure_forward_fixed_point`: a 6-level lineage with a diamond → every descendant once.
- `closure_digest_is_order_independent`.
- `mixed_aggregate_is_member`.
- `closure_follows_privacy_carrying_edges`: under the default a checkpoint trained in the scene is a member;
  with a checkpoint written under `private.weights_inherit = false` (test configuration) it is not, it
  survives the purge, and it is listed under `kept_by_weights_rule`.
- `purge_refused_813_without_withdraw_claims`: the refusal lists the claims; ledger and both roots are
  byte-identical before and after.
- `purge_withdraws_claims_and_releases_pins`: with `withdraw_claims = true` no `FROB-REF-813` is raised, each
  claim's tombstone has reason `private-purge`, and the pins are gone.
- `purge_without_claims_needs_no_flag`.
- `reaffirm_runs_after_withdrawing_purge`: a recorded PASS part resting on the withdrawn claim is demoted by
  the pass, and the receipt's `reaffirm_report` is that report's digest; a purge with no claim has
  `reaffirm_report: null` and runs no pass.
- `claim_issued_after_dry_run_is_caught`: a claim citing a member, issued after the dry run, makes the real
  call without the flag fail with 813 although `confirm` still matches.
- `purge_crash_matrix`: a crash injected after phase 1, after each blob deletion (property over a random
  subset), and before the final transaction → the same final state and receipt.
- `reads_refused_from_phase_one`.
- `purge_removes_job_log_rows_before_period`: a job on the scene finished one day ago (log period 30 days):
  after the purge its log rows and key row are tombstones with reason `private-purge`, and `job.find` with its
  key reports it collected.
- `no_blob_with_removed_digest_remains` (hashes every file under both roots).
- `non_private_scene_refused_802`; `stale_confirm_refused`; `running_job_blocks`.
- `unrelated_objects_untouched`.
- `receipt_schema_and_flags`.
- `scope_refusal_reveals_nothing`: identical refusals for an existing and a non-existing scene.
- `repeated_purge_returns_stored_receipt`: a second real call with the same scene and `confirm` after
  completion removes nothing and returns the stored receipt's digest; the store is byte-identical before and
  after it.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/safe_private_licence.sh` (N37-21, the script's owner), steps `purge_dry_run`, `purge`,
`purge_verify` and `purge_plan_stale`, through the real `frob` binary. `purge_plan_stale` is N32a-13's
removed-scene case of `FROB-REF-809` (that bead keeps it as a unit test; `scene.purge` is the only M3 verb
that removes a pack): before the purge, `frob study plan <manifest bound to the private pack> --json` returns a
plan identifier; after the purge, `frob study submit <plan> --key pst-1 --json` is `FROB-REF-809`, exit 7,
naming the removed scene identity (current identity: none), and the budget shown by `frob session show
--json` is the same before and after the submit (nothing charged). A repeat of the real purge returns the
stored receipt's digest. Logged per STD-E2E: command lines, exit codes, the closure digest, counts by kind,
claims withdrawn, the 809 refusal with its `observed` identity and the budget before and after, and the
result of the post-purge scan (`remaining_digests`, threshold 0). Removed digests are logged; no content is.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-827's control (after `scene.purge` no digest of the closure remains; GATE-SAFE-04). Known answer: a diamond
lineage of 1 pack, 2 runs, 4 stills and 1 report citing both runs has a closure of 8 members. Negative
controls (built with `--features gauntlet-mutations`): a build that follows edges one level only (stills
survive; the scan must find them); one
that deletes blobs but keeps memo nodes; one that skips claim withdrawal (a citation of the purged run must
then still succeed and the test must fail); one that deletes pinned evidence without the flag (the 813 test
must fail). Determinism lanes: closure digest and receipt bytes identical on
Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A purge is destructive and irreversible; the default is a dry run, the real call needs the dry run's digest
  (an `admin` verb takes no idempotency key; a repeated purge returns the stored receipt), and the MCP tool is
  annotated destructive.
- "No digest remains" means no object: tombstones and the receipt keep digests so that later citations fail
  with a reason and the owner has proof of what was removed.
- Copies outside the store (earlier exports, files a caller read and saved) are beyond the verb; the receipt
  says so explicitly and never claims otherwise.
- Backups of the state root made by the owner are outside the verb's reach; the documentation says so.
- Purging weights trained partly on the scene is the fail-closed consequence of inheritance; if the owner
  decides weights do not inherit, they leave the closure.
