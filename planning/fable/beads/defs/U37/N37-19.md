## Background

A scene pack built from a scan of a real home or workplace is private; it is stored under a configured
private root outside the repository, and everything derived from it inherits the mark. The owner must also be
able to take it back out: **`scene.purge` removes a private scene pack and the lineage closure of everything
derived from it, and writes a receipt listing the removed digests. Content addressing makes the closure
computable.** It is the registered verb `scene.purge` (kind `admin`, first milestone M3, in the `scene`
cluster).

Why a verb and not "delete the directory": derived artifacts do not live in the scene's directory. Episodes,
chunks, snapshots, stills, replays, reports, fixtures, memoized results and policy weights trained in the
scene are separate objects, some of them cited by claims and pinned against collection. Deleting the pack
alone leaves all of them; deleting by hand leaves dangling evidence that later prints as available. The
artifact graph already records, for every stored object, what it was derived from; the purge walks that graph.

Part of the privacy gate rests on this verb: GATE-SAFE-04 (Known answer, TARGET, M3) requires that after
`scene.purge` no digest of the closure remains in the store. Risk served: RISK-12 (a scan of a private place
leaks through a still, a fixture or policy weights; mitigation includes `scene.purge`).

Rejected alternatives: expiring private data by retention policy (the owner wants it gone now, and a pinned
object never expires); a purge that refuses whenever a claim cites the data (it would make a private scan
permanent the moment anything is concluded from it); cryptographic erasure by discarding a per-pack key
(attractive, and compatible with this design later, but derived artifacts are stored under the private root
as ordinary objects at M3).

## Scope

In: the verb, its arguments, scopes and schemas; the closure computation; the two-phase, crash-consistent
removal; withdrawal of claims and release of pins; eviction from the memo store, the world bank and device
arenas; the receipt; the export log's role; refusals. Out: the mark and its join (N37-17); private storage,
views and export rules (BD-305); the general collector (store chapter); retention periods (N37-30).

## Technical approach

1. **Registry row (DESIGN).** `name = "scene.purge"`, `cluster = "scene"`, `kind = "admin"`,
   `capabilities = ["scene:admin", "private:read"]`, `idempotent = true` (by key),
   `input_schema = "frob.scene.purge.in/1"`, `output_schema = "frob.scene.purge.out/1"`,
   `refusals = ["FROB-REF-720", "FROB-REF-731", "FROB-REF-802", "FROB-REF-805"]`,
   `annotations.destructive = true` on the MCP tool. Arguments: `scene` (a `SceneId`); `dry_run` (Boolean,
   default true); `confirm` (the closure digest returned by a dry run; required when `dry_run` is false);
   `idempotency_key`.
2. **Admission.** The session must hold both scopes (`FROB-REF-720` otherwise; a session without
   `private:read` learns nothing about whether the scene exists). The scene must exist and be marked private:
   a non-private scene is refused with `FROB-REF-802` (pointer `scene`, message "not a private scene pack; use
   the store's collector"). A non-dry call without `confirm`, or with a `confirm` that differs from the
   current closure digest (something was derived since the dry run), is refused with `FROB-REF-802` and the
   new digest.
3. **The closure.** Starting from the pack, follow `derived-from` edges forward (to children) to a fixed
   point. Members by kind: compiled scenes and clearance complexes; episodes, runs, chunks, snapshots, events;
   stills, contact sheets, replay packets, telemetry summaries; studies, comparisons, verdicts and scorecard
   rows; policy weights and checkpoints trained on it (when weights inherit, the default); memo nodes; reports,
   bundles, packets and kits that embed or cite a member; fixtures extracted from its runs; and the ledger
   rows of all of these. The closure digest is BLAKE3 over the sorted list of member digests.
   An aggregate that also has non-private inputs (a scorecard over 100 runs, one in the scene) is a member:
   it was derived from the scene, so it goes. The receipt lists it with `mixed: true` and the identifiers of
   its other inputs, so the owner can recompute it without the purged run.
4. **Dry run.** Returns the closure digest, counts and bytes by kind, the claims that will be withdrawn, the
   pins that will be released, jobs currently running on the scene (the purge refuses to proceed while any is
   running: `FROB-REF-802` naming the jobs, with the repair "cancel them with `job.cancel`"), and the list of
   earlier exports of any member (from the export log), each with its time, kind, export path relative to
   the export root and digest: these left the store under the private export scope and flag and are **not**
   recalled by a purge.
5. **Removal, in two phases (a job; crash-consistent).**
   1. One ledger transaction: verify `confirm`; write a `purge` row in state `purging` with the member list;
      withdraw every claim whose evidence closure intersects the members (each becomes a tombstone with
      reason `private-purge`); release the members' retention pins; mark every member row `purging` (from this
      instant no verb can read a member: reads return the tombstone refusal).
   2. Idempotent deletion: evict memo nodes; drop the scene and its variants from the world bank and from any
      device arena at the next safe boundary; delete each member's blob beneath the private root through the
      store's retained handles; then a second transaction replaces each member's ledger rows with a tombstone
      (content hash, class, time, reason `private-purge`) and sets the purge row to `complete`.
   On restart the daemon resumes any purge in state `purging` from phase 2. A purge is never rolled back.
6. **The receipt** (schema `frob.purge-receipt/1`, canonical JSON, stored under the private root, marked
   private, and itself exempt from the purge it records):
   ```json
   { "schema": "frob.purge-receipt/1", "scene": "s:…", "closure_digest": "b3:…",
     "removed": [ { "digest": "b3:…", "kind": "still", "bytes": "48211", "mixed": false } ],
     "counts": { "episode": 1024, "chunk": 4096, "still": 12, "policy": 1, "report": 2 },
     "claims_withdrawn": ["c:…"], "pins_released": 17,
     "exports_not_recalled": [ { "digest": "b3:…", "kind": "report", "at": "…", "relative_path": "…" } ],
     "peers_holding_copies": [], "completed_seq": "u64 as string",
     "authorization": { "execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false,
                        "sim_to_real_certified": false } }
   ```
   The envelope returns the receipt's digest and the counts.
7. **After a purge.** Reading any removed digest returns the tombstone refusal of the store (the citation
   refusal `FROB-REF-869` for a claim whose closure is tombstoned). A full scan of both store roots finds no
   blob whose BLAKE3 is in `removed`, and no ledger row of a member other than its tombstone. Tombstones and
   the receipt hold digests only; a digest does not reveal content.
8. **Other machines.** Private-marked objects cross to the other machine only as an export under the private
   export scope and flag, which the export log records. If the log shows such a transfer, the receipt lists
   the peer under `peers_holding_copies`; the owner runs `scene.purge` there with the same scene identifier.
9. **Classes.** D0; the closure is a function of the graph. The verb produces no evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] After `scene.purge` on a private scene with a derived run, replay, stills, a report, a memoized
      comparison and trained weights, no digest of the closure remains in the store: a scan of both roots and
      of the ledger finds only tombstones, and `artifact.read` of each removed digest is refused.
- [ ] The receipt lists every removed digest; its `closure_digest` equals the dry run's; it carries the three
      no-authorization flags.
- [ ] A claim supported by a member is withdrawn in the same transaction and afterwards cannot be cited.
- [ ] Killing the daemon at each of the two phase boundaries and during blob deletion, then restarting,
      completes the purge with the same receipt.
- [ ] A session lacking either scope is refused with no information about the scene; a non-private scene is
      refused; a stale `confirm` is refused with the new digest; a running job on the scene blocks the purge.
- [ ] Earlier exports of members are listed under `exports_not_recalled`.
- [ ] Objects not derived from the scene are untouched (their digests and marks are unchanged).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test purge -p frob-verbs --test scene_purge` (a real store and ledger in a
temporary state root; scenes are `synthetic` stand-ins marked private):
- `closure_forward_fixed_point`: a 6-level lineage with a diamond → every descendant once.
- `closure_digest_is_order_independent`.
- `mixed_aggregate_is_member`.
- `purge_withdraws_claims_and_releases_pins` (no `FROB-REF-813` is raised).
- `purge_crash_matrix`: a crash injected after phase 1, after each blob deletion (property over a random
  subset), and before the final transaction → the same final state and receipt.
- `reads_refused_from_phase_one`.
- `no_blob_with_removed_digest_remains` (hashes every file under both roots).
- `non_private_scene_refused_802`; `stale_confirm_refused`; `running_job_blocks`.
- `unrelated_objects_untouched`.
- `receipt_schema_and_flags`.
- `scope_refusal_reveals_nothing`: identical refusals for an existing and a non-existing scene.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/safe_private_licence.sh` (N37-21), steps `purge_dry_run`, `purge` and `purge_verify`, through
the real `frob` binary. Logged per STD-E2E: command lines, exit codes, the closure digest, counts by kind,
claims withdrawn, and the result of the post-purge scan (`remaining_digests`, threshold 0). Removed digests
are logged; no content is.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-827's control (after `scene.purge` no digest of the closure remains; GATE-SAFE-04). Known answer: a diamond
lineage of 1 pack, 2 runs, 4 stills and 1 report citing both runs has a closure of 8 members. Negative
controls: a `mutants` build that follows edges one level only (stills survive; the scan must find them); one
that deletes blobs but keeps memo nodes; one that skips claim withdrawal (a citation of the purged run must
then still succeed and the test must fail). Determinism lanes: closure digest and receipt bytes identical on
Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A purge is destructive and irreversible; the default is a dry run, the real call needs the dry run's digest
  and an idempotency key, and the MCP tool is annotated destructive.
- "No digest remains" means no object: tombstones and the receipt keep digests so that later citations fail
  with a reason and the owner has proof of what was removed.
- Copies outside the store (earlier exports, files a caller read and saved) are beyond the verb; the receipt
  says so explicitly and never claims otherwise.
- Backups of the state root made by the owner are outside the verb's reach; the documentation says so.
- Purging weights trained partly on the scene is the fail-closed consequence of inheritance; if the owner
  decides weights do not inherit, they leave the closure.
