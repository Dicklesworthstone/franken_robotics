## Background

Every result carries a claim product: the meet over its inputs of proof class, tier, determinism class,
standing and the reality and model-conditional labels. Carrying the product is half of the rule. The other
half is that nothing downstream may treat a result as stronger than its product says. The safety chapter:
**citing a result above its product is refused (`FROB-REF-819`): for example, offering a provisional
fast-tier result where an established one is required.**

The threat is "a result cited as more than it is", by an optimizing agent that wants a promotion, a
certificate or a build packet and holds only weaker evidence. Without this check the product would be a label
that consumers are free to ignore.

Several chapters own refusals for particular over-citations. This bead owns the general requirement check
and fixes how the codes relate (DESIGN):

| Situation | Code | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| A consumer declares a minimum for evidence it is offered, and the offered result's product is below it in any member | `FROB-REF-819` | This bead |
| A statement labels a result `established` when its label is anything else, or a `model-conditional` ranking is used to authorize spend | `FROB-REF-820` | Trust ladder |
| A result of determinism class D4 is offered as evidence | `FROB-REF-825` | N37-28 |
| A result is cited as qualified without a promoting receipt | `FROB-REF-845` | Qualification lane |
| Inside a certificate bundle, a result is presented above the class of its weakest input | `FROB-REF-834` | The auditor |

The distinction between 819 and 820: 819 is raised on the **requirement side** (a verb needs established
evidence and is handed provisional evidence); 820 on the **assertion side** (a report or request calls a
provisional result established). Both can apply to one request; the precedence rule below picks one.
Rejected alternative: one code for every over-citation. The repairs differ (promote the result; re-run under
a stricter profile; obtain a receipt), and an agent acts on the repair.

## Scope

In: the requirement type; the registry declarations on verb arguments that consume evidence; the admission
function; the precedence among codes; `FROB-REF-819` with its fields and repairs; citations inside reports;
the monotonicity property. Out: the product and its propagation (BD-308); the D4 paths (N37-28); the trust
ladder, the qualification lane and the auditor (their own beads); what each consumer requires in detail
beyond the table below (each consumer's bead sets its row).

## Technical approach

1. **Requirement (DESIGN sketch, in the foundation crate).**
   ```rust
   pub enum EvidenceUse { PairedStatistics, Promotion, Qualification, Certificate, BuildPacket, Report,
                          Leaderboard, PrototypeSpend, Baseline, Revalidation }
   pub struct Requirement { use_: EvidenceUse, kinds: &'static [ClaimKind], min_class: EvidenceClass,
                            min_standing: Standing, min_determinism: DetClass, tiers: TierRule,
                            reality: RealityRule, forbid: &'static [Condition] }
   pub fn admit(product: &ClaimProduct, req: &Requirement) -> Result<(), Refusal>;
   ```
   `admit` is the only function consumers call. It is total and pure.
2. **Registry declarations.** This bead adds the field `consumes_evidence` to the closed entry schema of
   `registries/verbs.toml` (N32a-02's later-field row: a list of tables `{ arg, use, requirement }`, default
   `[]`), with its member on the emitted `VerbEntry` and its lint, in one change; before that change a row
   carrying the field fails `unknown-field`. A verb argument that takes a stored result (a run, a study, a
   comparison, a verdict, a certificate, a policy evaluation, a receipt) declares, for example,
   `consumes_evidence = [{ arg = "result", use = "Promotion", requirement = "promotion/1" }]`, and
   `registries/evidence-requirements.toml` holds each named requirement. The dispatcher loads the referenced
   result's stored product and calls `admit` before the handler runs. The lint fails on an argument of a
   result-reference type with no declaration: a consumer cannot take evidence without stating what it needs.
   Its scope (DESIGN): every such argument of a verb whose evidence label is neither *pass-through* nor
   *screening*, and every parameter marked `accepts = "verdict"` whatever the label (N28c-12's citation
   parameters). An argument of a *pass-through* or *screening* verb that is not so marked (`explain.compare`'s
   comparison, `eval.detect`'s `result`) reads a result back with its own product unchanged and states no new
   claim from it, so it cannot cite above the product and needs no declaration. A verb with a declaration lists
   819 and 825 in its chapter list (N37-REF's list test).
   **The declarations at M3** (this bead writes them in the change that adds the field; a later consumer's
   bead adds its own row with its argument):
   | Verb | Argument | Use | Requirement |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `eval.promote` | `result` | `Promotion` | `promotion/1` |
   | `eval.compare` | `run` (the cross-decimation form, N16c-44) | `PairedStatistics` | `paired-statistics/1` |
   | `study.compare` | the stored study (positional), whose arm results are admitted | `PairedStatistics` | `paired-statistics/1` |
   | `prove.bundle` | the stored results the bundle cites (numeric inputs and cited results, VERBS-prove) | `Certificate` | `certificate/1` |
   | `report.design`, `report.run`, `report.mission` | `cite` | `Report` | `report/1`, with each sentence's requirement derived from its wording (step 6) |
   | `testprobe.cite` (builds with the feature `conformance` only) | `result` | `BuildPacket` | `build-packet/1` |
   The qualification path's declaration on `eval.qualify` is N37-28's (which depends on this bead); the build
   packet's evidence argument and prototype spend arrive at M5 with their verbs (`foundry.export`, N31ax-01).
   **Which M3 argument requires `established`.** On a release build at M3 two declarations have minimum
   standing `established`, and neither is the requirement side for a Design comparison: `prove.bundle`'s
   Certificate row admits only the kind Proof at class Certified, so a comparison fails at the kind member
   (step 4 (2)) before its standing is read; and the report verbs' `--cite` cites in the role `Established`
   (VERBS-report; BD-302's `resolve_cited` through N16b-04's `cite_as_established`), which is the assertion
   side, so a provisional result there is `FROB-REF-820` (step 4 (4)): that is the consumer N16b-35's step 14
   uses. The Leaderboard row has no verb (a view the daemon writes, N32b-03), and the Qualification row's
   baseline is an arm of `eval.qualify`, not a stored result. So that the requirement side is reached through
   the real dispatcher at M3, this bead adds the test-support verb `testprobe.cite` (DESIGN, the pattern of
   N37-25's `testprobe.combine`: kind `read`, cluster `testprobe`, present only in builds with the feature
   `conformance`, absent from the release registry and tool list, labelled `synthetic`): positional `result`
   with the declaration above; output an empty result whose `evidence` is the cited result's stored product;
   chapter list 819, 825, 830 and 845 (the codes `admit` can return for the build-packet row). A provisional
   comparison offered to it is `FROB-REF-819` with `required: established`; this is the argument N37-27's
   steps 3 and 4 offer the comparison to. From M5 the build packet's evidence argument is the release consumer
   of the same row.
3. **Requirement rows at M3** (DESIGN defaults assembled from the rules of the chapters that own each use:
   the minimum each use demands; a consumer's own bead may be stricter and governs where it differs):
   | Use | Kinds | Min class | Min standing | Min determinism | Forbidden conditions |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Paired statistics (an arm offered from store) | Design comparison, Fidelity | Tested | any | D2 on one device family (D3 allowed only as `paired-by-address`, labelled) | — |
   | Promotion (the result to be promoted) | Design comparison, Fidelity | Tested | provisional | D3 with a ledger envelope | `energy-created`, `capacity-biased` |
   | Qualification (the baseline's standing) | Design comparison | Tested | established | D2 | `model-conditional` |
   | Certificate (a numeric input) | Proof | Certified | established | D0 | — |
   | Build packet (cited evidence) | Design comparison, Proof | Tested | established, unless the packet is flagged exploratory | D2 | `model-conditional` |
   | Report sentence | The kind whose permitted wording the sentence uses | The class the wording states | The standing the wording states | — | none may be dropped from the sentence |
   | Leaderboard row | Design comparison | Tested | established | D2 | `capacity-biased` |
   | Prototype spend | Design comparison | Tested | established | D2 | `model-conditional`, `tier-sensitive` |
   | Baseline (a stored result used as the reference arm of a comparison or a regression check) | Design comparison, Fidelity, Performance | Tested | any (the comparison's own product then carries the baseline's standing) | D2 on one device family | — |
   | Revalidation (a verdict carried forward after an edit) | The carried verdict's kind | The class it was issued with | The standing it was issued with | The class it was issued with | none may be dropped; whether the verdict survives the edit is the dependency graph's decision (§28), and this row only stops a carried verdict being stated above its product |
   Every `EvidenceUse` variant has its default row here (`<use>/1`). A consumer's stricter requirement is a
   further named row of the same use, and the lint checks it is at least the default in every member; a
   declaration naming a requirement with no row fails the lint.
4. **Order of evaluation and precedence.** `admit` checks members in a fixed order and returns the first
   failure with the most specific code registered for it: (1) determinism D4 → `FROB-REF-825`; (2) the
   claim kind is not in the result's `claims` (for example a Parity result offered where Reality is required,
   or a Design comparison offered as a Proof) → `FROB-REF-819`; (3) class below `min_class` → `FROB-REF-819`;
   (4) standing below `min_standing` → `FROB-REF-819` when a consumer requires it, and `FROB-REF-820` when the
   request itself asserted `established`; (5) determinism below the minimum (other than D4) → the numerics
   chapter's code for that use where one exists (a paired statistic: `FROB-REF-561`; attribution:
   `FROB-REF-562`), otherwise `FROB-REF-819`; (6) tier or reality rule → `FROB-REF-819`; (7) a forbidden
   condition present → the condition's own code where one exists (`model-conditional` for promotion, build or
   prototype budget: `FROB-REF-830`; `capacity-biased` cited as a verdict: `FROB-REF-822`), otherwise
   `FROB-REF-819`; (8) qualification standing without a promoting receipt → `FROB-REF-845`.
5. **The refusal.** `FROB-REF-819`, "a result cited above its claim product". Exit 3; `retry: after-change`.
   Fields: `pointer` (the argument and the result's identifier), `observed` (the product's value in the
   failing member), `required` (the requirement's value), `affected_claims` (the claim identifiers that
   would have rested on the citation), and `weakest` (the product's weakest input: content hash and field
   path). Ranked repairs (DESIGN): (1) raise the weakest input: the registered action for its source kind
   (`eval promote` for a provisional fast-tier result; a re-run on the referee; a measurement that replaces an
   assumed card field); (2) cite the result for what it supports: the envelope lists its `claims` and the
   permitted wording; (3) use a consumer that accepts the product (for example an exploratory build packet).
6. **Reports are citations.** The report generator composes each sentence from a claim kind's permitted
   wording and a result; it calls `admit` with a requirement derived from the wording (class and standing as
   stated, no condition dropped). A template that would print a result more strongly than its product does
   not render: the generator returns the refusal and the report is not written.
7. **Monotonicity (the law that makes the check sound).** If `admit(p, r)` succeeds and p′ is at least as
   strong as p in every member, then `admit(p′, r)` succeeds; if it fails for p it fails for every weaker
   product. So weakening an input can never turn a refusal into an acceptance, and combining with more inputs
   can never make a citation admissible that was not.
8. **Classes and records.** D0. Each refused citation is a ledger row (session, verb, result, requirement,
   failing member).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The smallest case end to end: design A evaluated on the referee and design B on a fast tier, still
      provisional; their comparison's product is provisional; offering it where an established result is
      required (`testprobe.cite`, the build-packet requirement) is refused with `FROB-REF-819`,
      `observed = provisional`, `required = established`, `weakest` naming B's fast-tier run, first repair
      `eval promote`; after B's result is promoted and the comparison re-run, the same call is admitted.
- [ ] `consumes_evidence` is in the entry schema with its member and lint; the rows of step 2's table carry
      their declarations; `testprobe.cite` is absent from a release build's registry and tool list.
- [ ] Each row of the precedence list returns its stated code on a planted case (eight cases).
- [ ] Every verb argument of a result-reference type has a `consumes_evidence` declaration; a planted
      argument without one fails the lint.
- [ ] A report sentence that would state `established` for a provisional result is not rendered
      (`FROB-REF-820`); one that would use the Proof wording for a Tested result is not rendered
      (`FROB-REF-819`).
- [ ] The monotonicity property holds over 10⁴ random (product, stronger product, requirement) triples.
- [ ] A refused citation charges no budget and writes one ledger row.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-core --test admit_evidence -p frob-verbs --test consumes_evidence`:
- `requirement_table_rows`: one test per row of step 3 (ten rows) with a product exactly at the minimum
  (admitted) and one step below in each member (refused with the expected code).
- `every_use_has_default_row`: each `EvidenceUse` variant has its `<use>/1` row in
  `registries/evidence-requirements.toml`; a planted declaration naming a missing requirement fails the lint,
  and so does a planted consumer row weaker than its use's default in one member.
- `precedence_d4_first`: a product that is D4, Estimated and provisional → `FROB-REF-825`.
- `kind_not_in_claims_refused`: a Parity result offered for a Reality requirement → `FROB-REF-819`.
- `standing_requirement_vs_assertion`: the same provisional result → 819 when required, 820 when asserted.
- `forbidden_condition_codes`: `model-conditional` to prototype spend → `FROB-REF-830`; `capacity-biased` to
  the leaderboard → `FROB-REF-822`; `energy-created` to promotion → `FROB-REF-819`.
- `weakest_and_first_repair`: provisional fast-tier → `eval promote`; assumed card field → the measurement.
- `monotone_admit` (property, 10⁴ triples, seed 1).
- `admit_is_pure`: equal inputs give equal results across 10³ repetitions and worker counts.
- `planted_undeclared_result_argument_fails_lint`; `pass_through_reader_needs_no_declaration` (an undeclared
  comparison argument of a *pass-through* verb passes; the same argument marked `accepts = "verdict"` fails);
  `declared_verb_lists_819_825` (a planted declared row without 825 fails).
- `declaration_table_rows`: the registry holds exactly the declarations of step 2's table.
- `testprobe_cite_requirement_side`: a provisional comparison → `FROB-REF-819`, `observed: provisional`,
  `required: established`, `weakest` the fast-tier arm, first repair `eval promote`, one ledger row; the
  promoted re-run is admitted; `testprobe_cite_absent_in_release`.
- `report_sentence_refused_not_rendered` (two cases).

### End-to-end script and logging
None of its own: the shared script `scripts/e2e/safe_claim_boundary.sh` has one owner, N37-27 (convention 11),
whose steps `cite_provisional` and `promote_then_cite` offer the comparison to `frob --json testprobe cite`
(a build with the feature `conformance`) and whose step `report_wording` exercises step 6, through the real
`frob` binary and daemon; logged per STD-E2E with the offered product, the requirement, the failing member,
the code and the first repair.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-829 (the product of {Tested on the referee, Tested and provisional on a fast tier} is provisional), from
which the smallest case follows. Negative controls (builds with `--features gauntlet-mutations`): one whose `admit` compares only the class
(the provisional citation is admitted and the test must fail); one in which a consumer handler runs before
`admit` (the ledger shows work charged for a refused citation); a report template that prints a fixed
"established". Metamorphic relation: adding an input to the cited result can only keep or cause a refusal.
Determinism lanes: identical verdicts and codes on Linux and macOS, debug and release.

## Considerations

- Two codes for neighbouring situations (819 and 820) is a deliberate reading of two chapters that each
  define one; the requirement-side and assertion-side split is recorded in the refusal registry bead so that
  no third meaning appears.
- Requirements are minima; a consumer may demand more, and its own bead says what.
- The check adds one stored-row read per cited result at admission; it never runs inside a step.
- If a later chapter adds a label or a condition, it adds its forbidden-condition code to step 4's list or
  falls through to 819; the fall-through is safe because it refuses.
