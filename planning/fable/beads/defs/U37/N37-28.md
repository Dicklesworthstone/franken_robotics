## Background

Every result records a determinism class: D0 exact; D1 bitwise across backends; D2 bitwise per backend and
independent of batch composition; D3 agreement inside a recorded tolerance with agreement of task decisions;
**D4 unspecified, which guarantees nothing**. Training may run under a profile that gives up run-to-run
reproducibility for throughput (the profile `fast-training`: asynchronous delivery of collected segments and
gradient sums between shards, a relaxed reduction order and matrix-product path in the learner, minibatches
composed by arrival). It is allowed on one condition: **it is labeled, and the label has consequences.**

- Every result, checkpoint and policy produced under it carries determinism class D4 and the profile's name.
- A D4 result is never evidence. Paired statistics, promotion, qualification, certificates and build packets
  refuse it (`FROB-REF-825`).
- Weights trained under it are admissible as data, like any other policy file. Every claim about them comes
  from re-evaluation under D2 or stricter, and that evaluation, not the training run, is what is cited.
- The profile relaxes ordering and placement only. The bans on fast-math relaxation, fused multiply-add,
  built-in transcendentals in batched kernels and floating-point atomics hold under it.

Why the refusal is by code and not by convention: under class D4 nothing about an arm can be replayed, so its
verdict could not be reproduced, promoted or attributed. An agent that trains fast and then cites the training
curve as a comparison is the threat "a result cited as more than it is". The profile's own ceiling is small
(inference and learning are about 13% of training wall-clock on one measured incumbent stack, so relaxing them
gains at most 1.15 on one device; its real use is the asynchronous two-device variant), which is one more
reason nothing may lean on its numbers.

Invariant **INV-SAFE-07**: no D4 result is consumed by paired statistics, promotion, qualification, a
certificate or a build packet. Gate **GATE-SAFE-09** (Known answer, TARGET, M3): a result produced under the
nondeterministic training profile is refused on each of the five paths of INV-SAFE-07; the same policy
re-evaluated under D2 is accepted. The Gauntlet's certifier table adds: a D4 result offered unlabeled is
refused on every path.

The profile, the class licences and their own refusals belong to the numerics chapter's beads (the profile
N35-06, the licence check N35-07, and the seed BD-269 that joins them at the verbs). This bead is the
safety-side enforcement: the label cannot be lost, the five consumers refuse, and weights pass as data.

## Technical approach

1. **What the boundary reads.** The labels are written by the profile bead N35-06 (a job started with
   `train.start --determinism fast-training` puts `determinism: D4` and `profile: fast-training` on its job
   row, receipt, training-time results, checkpoints and exported manifest; the manifest's tag is
   `trained_under: "D4"` and its `trained_on.determinism` field reads `"D4"`). The boundary reads one thing:
   the determinism member of a stored result's claim product. Determinism combines by "weakest wins", so
   anything computed from a D4 result is D4 when it reaches `admit`. (The option is `--determinism`, as the
   verb appendix has it for determinism profiles; `--profile` names a step profile.)
2. **Absent means D4.** A stored result with no recorded determinism class, or a result presented from
   outside the store with no product, is treated as D4 for every check below (the same fail-closed reading as
   a missing authorization flag). This is what defeats "the D4 result offered unlabeled": stripping the label
   does not produce a stronger class, because class is never inferred upward.
3. **Results against weights (DESIGN).** A *result* is a number with a claim product. A *weights artifact* is
   data with an identity. When weights are an input to an evaluation they contribute their content hash to
   the evaluation's inputs and **no determinism class**: the evaluation's class is that of its own kernels,
   placement and profile. So a policy trained under D4 and evaluated under `device-replay` yields a D2 result
   (deterministic given the weights), and that result is what may be cited. The manifest's `trained_under`
   tag and `trained_on` block stay as provenance and are printed in reports ("trained under fast-training
   (D4); evaluated under device-replay (D2)").
4. **The five paths.** Each consumer takes stored results through the common admission function
   `admit(product, requirement)`, whose first check is determinism: D4 or unrecorded → `FROB-REF-825`.
   | Path | Verb and argument | What is offered in the gate's planted case |
   |---|---|---|
   | Paired statistics | `study.compare` reading a stored study whose arm results include the D4 result (code 825). The same result named as an arm of `eval.compare` is refused one step earlier, at that verb's own admission, by `pairing::admit`, which runs first, with `FROB-REF-542` (N28-REF; VERBS-eval, DESIGN); planned with `study.plan` it is `FROB-REF-533` from `study::admit` (N24-REF, the layer rule): the path refuses either way, and the code is the raising site's | The in-training evaluation of the D4 run as one arm |
   | Promotion | `eval.promote <result>` | The D4 run's final training return |
   | Qualification | `eval.qualify` (a baseline or candidate standing, or a request whose tier plan names the D4 profile) | The D4 result as the candidate's supporting evidence; a tier plan with `fast-training` |
   | Certificate | `prove.bundle` with a numeric input or a cited result | The D4 result as a cited input of the bundle |
   | Build packet | The build packet's evidence argument | The D4 result as cited evidence (at M3 through the admission function with the build-packet requirement; with the real verb at M5) |
   The check runs in the dispatcher before the handler, so a refused request spends nothing.
5. **The refusal.** `FROB-REF-825`, "a result of class D4 offered as evidence". Exit 3; `retry:
   after-change`. Fields: `pointer` (the argument and the result), `observed` (`D4` or `unrecorded`, and the
   profile name when known), `required` (the minimum class of the path), `affected_claims`. Ranked repairs
   (DESIGN): (1) re-evaluate the policy under `device-replay` (D2) or stricter and offer that result: the
   refusal carries the ready `eval run` command built from identifiers; (2) re-run the training under the
   default deterministic profile if the training-time number itself is what is needed; (3) none other: a
   class is never raised by relabeling, only by running again under a stricter profile.
6. **Neighbouring codes (so that each request gets exactly one; the reading is the one the verb registries
   and the refusal registries carry under the layer rule of N24-REF and N28-REF).** `FROB-REF-542`: a paired
   comparison asked of `eval.compare` with an arm of class D4 (or arms of different classes); raised by
   `pairing::admit`, which runs before the determinism licence (VERBS-eval, DESIGN), and names the arm and the
   pairing. `FROB-REF-533`: the same defect in a study planned with `study.plan`, raised by `study::admit`
   (N24b-14) at its determinism check; the Arena twin of 542, and a verb lists one code of the pair, never
   both. `FROB-REF-561`: the determinism licence of N35-07, which on `eval.compare` answers only what 542
   does not cover (an arm of unrecorded class, a pair on different device families demanded `exact` without
   class D1, a D3 pair demanded `exact`). `FROB-REF-861`: a number from a `fast-training` run is *cited* in a
   report or a claim, or a policy trained under it is offered for promotion or qualification with no
   stricter re-evaluation on record. `FROB-REF-825`: a stored D4 *result* is offered to promotion,
   qualification, a certificate or a build packet, or sits inside a stored study given to `study.compare`. On
   the five paths of the gate the expected code is 825, with the one exception stated in the table: the
   `eval.compare` form of the first path is 542 (and its `study.plan` form 533).
7. **Re-evaluation is accepted.** `frob --json eval run --policy <the D4-trained weights> --task <task>
   --stream dev --seeds 1 --determinism device-replay --key <k>` (the policy form of VERBS-eval: the design is
   the policy's design parent, and the population is the task with its development stream), run twice under
   two distinct keys, gives identical digests (the profile bead's known answer, read here and not re-specified); its
   result is D2 and passes the determinism check of each of the five paths (other requirements of a path,
   such as standing, apply as usual).
8. **What this bead hands the capstone.** The five-path test of the boundary function (the planted D4 result
   refused with 825 per path; the unlabeled variant refused; the D2 re-evaluation accepted), the property
   test and the three mutants. BD-269 runs them as the boundary-function part of GATE-SAFE-09, offers the
   same result through the real verbs, and enters the row with the digest equality of two re-evaluations.
   The profile's own checks (the bans under the profile, the closed knob set, the equality of physics
   digests, the per-job-only enablement) are acceptance criteria of N35-06 and are not repeated here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The component clause of GATE-SAFE-09 (TARGET): a result produced under `fast-training` is refused by
      `admit` with `FROB-REF-825` for each of the five uses (the stored-study form of the paired-statistics
      path included); the same policy re-evaluated under D2 passes the determinism check for each. The test
      prints one line per path, which the capstone BD-269 reads; the gate row is BD-269's.
- [ ] The same result with its determinism member removed (unrecorded) is refused on each path.
- [ ] The boundary sees the label N35-06 wrote on each kind of artifact a `fast-training` job produces (job
      row, receipt, `train.status` envelope, checkpoint, exported manifest): for each kind, the result offered
      from it reaches `admit` with determinism D4.
- [ ] An evaluation of the D4-trained policy under `device-replay` reaches `admit` with
      `evidence.determinism` D2 and the policy's hash among its inputs.
- [ ] Citing the training return in a report is refused (the numerics chapter's `FROB-REF-861`); a paired
      comparison with a D4 arm asked of `eval.compare` is refused with `FROB-REF-542` (`pairing::admit` runs
      first), and the same planned with `study.plan` with `FROB-REF-533`; an arm of unrecorded class asked of
      `eval.compare` is `FROB-REF-561`: one code per request.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-core --test admit_d4 -p frob-verbs --test d4_paths -p frob-learn --test d4_labels` (a
short `batch-cpu` training job under the profile; no mocks):
- `d4_first_in_precedence`: a product that is D4, Estimated and provisional → `FROB-REF-825`.
- `unrecorded_is_d4`.
- `five_paths_refuse_825`: one case per path with the planted result of the table (the paired-statistics
  case is the stored study given to `study.compare`).
- `five_paths_accept_d2_reeval`: the determinism check passes on each path for the re-evaluated result.
- `label_reaches_admit_from_every_artifact`: a result taken from the job row, the receipt, a status envelope,
  a checkpoint manifest and the exported manifest each reaches `admit` as D4 (the writing of those labels is
  tested in N35-06 by `labels_on_every_artifact`).
- `weights_contribute_identity_not_class`: an evaluation whose policy was trained under D4 has
  `determinism` equal to the evaluation profile's class and lists the policy's hash among its inputs.
- `derived_from_d4_is_d4`: a scorecard that includes one D4 result is D4.
- `eval_compare_d4_arm_is_542`; `eval_compare_unrecorded_arm_is_561`; `planned_d4_arm_is_533`;
  `cited_training_return_is_861` (the neighbours: each request gets exactly one code).
- Property test (INV-SAFE-07): for 10⁴ random products with determinism D4 or unrecorded and any other
  members, `admit` fails for each of the five uses.

Moved to N35-06, which owns what they test: `labels_on_every_artifact`, `profile_knobs_closed`,
`physics_digests_equal_under_profile`, `ban_lint_independent_of_profile`, `no_default_enable_key`, and the
digest equality of two re-evaluations (its KA-801 test).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/safe_d4.sh` (N37-29) runs the five paths through the real `frob` binary and daemon with the
structured logging of STD-E2E: per path the command line, exit code, refusal code, `observed`, `required`, the
envelope's `evidence` block and the repair command. Its paired-statistics step offers the stored result to
`study compare` (825) and its neighbours step plans a study with a D4 arm (533) and asks `eval compare` with
one (542), which is the reading above.
The numerics script `scripts/e2e/num_licences_d4.sh` (N35-32) covers GATE-SPARK-17 only and offers nothing on
the five paths. The gate row of GATE-SAFE-09 is entered once, by BD-269, with its own measured outcomes and
this bead's test output (both scripts depend on BD-269, so neither can be cited when it closes). N37-29's
script produces a tier receipt for the gate and writes nothing to the row (one recorder per gate); its log
is evidence beside the record.

### Known answers and negative controls
KA-834 (a result produced under the nondeterministic training profile is refused on each of the five paths of
INV-SAFE-07; the same policy re-evaluated under D2 is accepted; exact, verdict; tier G5, host; control: the
D4 result offered unlabeled). Known answer adopted from the numerics chapter: a `fast-training` policy
evaluated twice under `device-replay` gives identical digests, and citing its training return is refused.
Negative controls registered against GATE-SAFE-09 (built with `--features gauntlet-mutations`): a build in
which unrecorded determinism is read
as D2 (the unlabeled result is accepted; the test must fail); one in which the promotion consumer skips
`admit`; one in which the weights' training class is propagated into the evaluation (the re-evaluation is
then refused and the gate's second clause fails). Determinism lanes: the refusals are D0; the re-evaluation is
run twice on one machine and across worker counts.

## Considerations

- Qualification has no natural "D4 result" input, since a request names a candidate and worlds are run by the
  lane; the planted cases are a supporting result and a tier plan naming the profile. If the qualification
  bead narrows what a request may carry, the planted case follows it.
- The fifth path uses the admission function directly until the build packet exists; its bead re-runs the
  planted case with the real verb, and the outcome is recorded as a later part of the gate from that bead's
  own tier receipt (`record_gate … --part M5`), never appended to the row BD-269 entered.
- Several chapters each gave a code for a D4 arm in a paired statistic (561 in the class-licence table, 542
  and 533 in the pairing and Arena admissions, 825 in the list of five consumers). The reading used here,
  in the verb registries and under the layer rule of N24-REF and N28-REF: 542 at `eval.compare` (its
  `pairing::admit` runs before the licence check, which there answers only the cases 542 does not cover with
  561), 533 at `study.plan`, 825 for a stored study given to `study.compare` and on the other four paths. The
  gate's statement names no code, so every form satisfies "refused on the path".
- Asynchronous two-device training (the profile's real use) is Frontier under its own kill gate; if it is
  killed the profile may never be used, and this bead's refusals cost nothing to keep.
- A D3 result is not D4: it is admissible where its licence allows (for example as `paired-by-address`,
  labelled); only D4 and unrecorded are refused outright here.
