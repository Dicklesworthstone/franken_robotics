## Background

**OPEN-SAFE-4** (open question of the safety chapter). *Retention periods for job logs, idempotency keys and
retired confirmation clusters.* Owner: the project owner. Blocks: M3 (the milestone at which the daemon serves
several agents, the blinded qualification lane opens campaigns, and the store's collector runs for real).
**Fail-closed default: keep job logs and keys 30 days; keep retired clusters sealed indefinitely.** The open
question register groups it with the store's own question on failure captures (30 days within a 64 GB budget
by default) and lists the M3 milestone as wanting the owner's input.

Why these three periods are a safety matter and not housekeeping:

- **Idempotency keys.** One job and one budget charge per (principal, verb, key) is the defence against a
  client that retries blindly. It holds only while the key is remembered: a retry that arrives after the key
  was collected is a new job and a second charge. The period is therefore the length of the window in which a
  blind retry is harmless.
- **Job logs.** A job log persists independently of any client connection and is how a disconnected agent
  recovers what happened. It can also hold text that came from imports (in marked fields) and rows derived
  from private scenes (which keep their privacy class through retention). Too short loses recoverability; too
  long keeps private-derived rows around after their runs are gone.
- **Retired confirmation clusters.** A cluster of sealed worlds is retired when the owner's audit scope opens
  it (it is never drawn from again) or when its campaign closes (its family is retired into discovery and
  never serves as a confirmation family again). Audit-retired clusters of a still-open campaign must stay
  sealed, or the audit would leak; and the campaign's replay by the offline auditor needs the cluster
  derivation and receipts to exist. Deleting them early destroys the ability to re-check a qualification;
  unsealing them early breaks blinding.

## Scope

In: the three periods; the constraints any choice must satisfy; where the values live; what tests show for the
chosen values; the record. Out: the collector and its tombstones (store chapter); the idempotency binding
(surface chapter); cluster derivation and close (qualification chapter); failure-capture retention and store
budgets (the store's own open question).

## Technical approach

1. **Values and where they live.** Three keys of the owner-held safety configuration, with the defaults:
   `retention.job_log_days = 30`, `retention.idempotency_key_days = 30`,
   `retention.retired_clusters = "sealed-indefinitely"` (alternatives: `"sealed-days=N"` then deleted;
   `"reveal-at-close"` for clusters of a closed campaign, which the lane's protocol already does for the
   campaign's own clusters). The configuration digest is recorded with every job, so the period in force for
   a record is known.
2. **Constraints (hold for every admissible choice; checked at start-up, a violation stops the daemon).**
   - A key is kept at least as long as its job record: `idempotency_key_days ≥ job_log_days`. Periods count
     from the job's terminal state; a key can never expire while its job is admitted or running.
   - For mutating verbs that create no job the key window is 24 hours (TARGET, the surface chapter's value)
     and is not lengthened or shortened by this decision.
   - Retention never overrides a pin: chunks, snapshots, ledger rows, certificates and receipts in the
     closure of a live claim, qualification receipts and registered predictions are kept until the claim is
     withdrawn, whatever the periods say.
   - The ledger of levels of a campaign (the index, each level spent, each receipt) is append-only and is
     never collected by any of these periods.
   - An audit-retired cluster of an open campaign is sealed until the campaign closes, under every option.
   - A purge of a private scene removes its job-log rows regardless of the log period.
3. **What collection does.** When a job record's period ends, the collector removes its log and its key row
   and writes a tombstone for each (content hash, class, time, reason `retention`). A later `job.find` with
   that key reports that the record was collected; a later submission with that key is a new request.
4. **Options the owner may choose, with costs.**
   | Choice | Effect |
   |---|---|
   | Shorter logs and keys (7 days) | Less private-derived residue; a retry after a week-long outage double-charges |
   | Default (30 and 30) | A month of harmless retries and recoverable logs; storage is small (keys are a few hundred bytes; logs are bounded by job budgets) |
   | Longer (90 days or the life of the run record) | Simplest for audits; more residue |
   | Retired clusters deleted after N days | Frees storage; a closed campaign can no longer be replayed by the offline auditor after deletion, so N must exceed the date of that replay gate, and receipts stay |
   | Retired clusters sealed indefinitely (default) | Blinding can never be broken by a retention bug; storage grows with every audit |
5. **Record.** An ADR for OPEN-SAFE-4 signed by the owner (values, reason, rejected values), the three keys,
   the open-question register updated. Deadline: before the M3 exit review. If undecided, "default adopted"
   is recorded.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An ADR for OPEN-SAFE-4 exists, or the M3 exit record states "default adopted: 30 days, 30 days, sealed
      indefinitely".
- [ ] The daemon refuses to start with `idempotency_key_days < job_log_days`.
- [ ] With the default, a submission retried with the same key 29 days after the job's terminal state returns
      the stored receipt with `idempotent_replay: true`, and one retried after 31 days is treated as a new
      request (shown under a virtual clock on the deterministic runtime).
- [ ] A key never expires while its job is running, at any period (virtual-clock test with a job longer than
      the period).
- [ ] An audit-retired cluster of an open campaign is unreadable by a discovery session at any clock value;
      under the default it stays so after close.
- [ ] Pinned evidence survives collection at every period; the level ledger is never collected.
- [ ] `frob doctor --json` prints the three values and whether each is a default; `frob store status` reports
      what each retention class holds.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-chronicle --test retention_periods -p frob-cli --test retention_config` (the real ledger
on the deterministic runtime with virtual time; no mocks):
- `defaults_30_30_sealed`.
- `config_constraint_key_ge_log`.
- `retry_day_29_replays_day_31_new`.
- `key_outlives_running_job`.
- `mutate_key_window_24h_unchanged`.
- `pins_dominate_retention`; `level_ledger_never_collected`.
- `audit_retired_cluster_stays_sealed` (clock advanced 10 years).
- `collection_writes_tombstones`; `job_find_after_collection_reports_collected`.
- `purge_removes_log_rows_before_period`.

### End-to-end script and logging
None of its own. `scripts/e2e/safe_capabilities.sh` logs the three values from `frob doctor --json` as a
`metric` line (`retention`, with the ADR identifier or `default`), and the qualification-lane script asserts
the sealed state of an audit-retired cluster after close.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: 29 days replays and 31 days does not, under the 30-day default; an audit-retired cluster is
sealed at any clock value. Negative controls: a `mutants` collector that counts the key period from
submission instead of from the terminal state (a long job's key expires while it runs; the test must fail);
one that collects pinned receipts.

## Considerations

- After a key is collected, reuse of that key is indistinguishable from first use. If the owner wants reuse of
  an expired key to be detectable, the ADR can add a permanent tombstone per key (its digest and request
  digest, a few tens of bytes); this is a possible addition, not part of the default.
- Private-derived log rows are private while they exist; the period bounds how long they exist once the run is
  no longer pinned.
- Storage for sealed retired clusters is budgeted in the store's status report so that "indefinitely" is a
  visible cost, not a hidden one.
- The store's own open question (failure captures for 30 days within 64 GB) is decided with this one in
  practice; its bead is the store chapter's.
