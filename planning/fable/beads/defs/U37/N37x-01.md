## Background

The safety chapter fixes what may enter the system, what may leave it and who may read what. Its threat model
names five assets (the owner's machines and devices; evidence; private places; budgets and other agents' work;
people near a physical robot) and, for each, the threats in scope. The machine-readable form is
`registries/threats.toml`: nine threat rows, each traced to mechanisms, invariants, refusal codes, gates and
negative controls, with a test that fails when a row loses its control or a verb states no row it widens. Every
one of the nine assumes a **local** adversary: a confused or injected agent, a hostile imported file, a client
that retries blindly. None is a party on a network, because at plan time the system has no network surface: the
daemon listens on a Unix-domain socket under its state root with mode 0600, and the MCP server speaks stdio or
HTTP bound to 127.0.0.1.

Two planned mechanisms change that. (1) The MCP HTTP transport may be bound to another address when configured
(a non-loopback bind needs `--bind <addr>` and `--allow-remote`, refuses to start without capability tokens and
logs a warning). (2) The peer link [F] forwards whole jobs between the owner's two machines over a mutually
authenticated, encrypted connection; only seeds, recipes, design and module differences, receipts and policy
weights cross it; it is built only if the owner's decision on the link is `build` (default: standalone
machines). The peer link's own acceptance says "passes the threat review's checklist", and the threat register's
bead says what must happen when such a surface appears: "a new threat row, decided by ADR, not a new verb flag".
Neither the rows, nor the ADR, nor the checklist existed. This bead writes them, and adds the one test that holds
whether or not either mechanism is ever enabled: by default nothing listens on a network. Tier: Solid.

Rejected: treating the peer as trusted because both machines are the owner's (a compromised or misconfigured
peer is the realistic case, and the link carries capability tokens); a per-mechanism review with no register
(the next listener would arrive unreviewed); a flag that waives the checklist (no safety mechanism has an off
switch below the owner-held configuration).

## Scope

In: the ADR; four threat rows; the listener register and its source scan; the owner-held key that enables a
listener; the checklist with an owner per item; the listener block of `frob doctor`; the default-closed test;
the review record's form. Out: the peer link itself and its tests of each checklist item (N32a-35); the HTTP
transport and its bind flags (N32a-27); capability tokens and attenuation (BD-258); the owner-held configuration
file and its protection (N37-02); the mark lattice and export door for private data (the private-places beads);
the decision whether the link is built (N32a-34).

## Technical approach

**1. ADR (`docs/adr/ADR-SAFE-06-network-listeners.md`; DESIGN number, the next free of the safety series).**
Decision: a process of this system opens a network listener only if the listener is a row of a closed register,
is enabled in the owner-held configuration, and has a recorded review against the checklist below. Reason: a
listener is the one surface whose adversary does not need an account on the machine.

**2. Rows added to `registries/threats.toml`** (the fields of N37-01's schema: `id`, `asset`, `statement`,
`mechanisms`, `invariants`, `refusals`, `gates`, `controls`, `risks`).

| Threat (`id`, statement) | `asset` | Mechanisms | Refusals | `gates` | Plants (the `controls` field) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1.c a network party with no valid credential reaches a listener | T1 (the owner's machines) | Closed listener register; loopback or off by default; authentication before any request is parsed; connection, frame and time limits | 723 (token invalid or expired) | GATE-SAFE-10 | A connection with no token; with an expired token; from an unconfigured peer key |
| T1.d a configured peer that is compromised or misbehaving | T1 | Allowed transfer classes only; attenuated token (scopes and budget never widen); scenes regenerated from recipes and compared by identity; job manifest allowlist, never a shell | 618 (a transfer class that may not cross machines), 723, 720 (missing scope), 734 (a path outside the session's roots) | GATE-SAFE-10 | A peer that asks for a state slab; that offers a widened token; that sends a scene whose identity differs from its recipe |
| T3.b private data or anything derived from it crosses a link | T3 (private places) | The mark join over lineage, checked at the link's send side for every object; a private scan has no recipe and is never forwardable | 731 (a private object without the private scope and flag) | GATE-SAFE-04 | A job whose design lineage includes a private scan, offered for forwarding |
| T4.c a network client or peer exhausts budgets or starves local agents | T4 (budgets and other agents' work) | Reservation at the origin at the interval's upper end; the peer's token budget equals the reservation; rate limiting; a cap on concurrent connections | 701, 703 (admission guard) | GATE-SAFE-10 | A flood of offers; an offer whose cost interval exceeds the reservation |

`invariants` and `risks` are empty on the four rows. The gates are those whose mechanisms the rows extend over a
listener: GATE-SAFE-10 (capability tokens, scopes, budgets and the path jail; BD-258's full surface) and
GATE-SAFE-04 (private places). N37-01's traceability test reads the control registry by gate, so the rows pass
it through those gates' registered controls from the day they are added. The `controls` field lists the
registered controls that plant each row: on T1.c the control this bead registers in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (seed `LST-X01`, kind `mutation`, `gate = ["GATE-SAFE-10"]`,
defect: the HTTP transport's `AuthProvider` attaches a capability token without checking its expiry; caught by
step 5 of this bead's script, run under `--features gauntlet-mutations`); on T1.d, T3.b and T4.c, whose
mechanisms are the peer link's, the controls N32a-35 registers in the change that builds the link (the field
is empty until then; the link is off unless the owner decides `build`). The count test of N37-01
(`register_loads_five_assets_nine_threats`) becomes `register_loads_five_assets_thirteen_threats` in the same
change.

**3. The listener register (`registries/listeners.toml`, DESIGN; schema `frob.listeners/1`).** One row per
listener any binary may open: `id`, `binary`, `transport`, `default`, `enabled_by`, `auth`, `threats`,
`review`. Rows at this bead:

| `id` | Binary | Transport | Default | Enabled by | Authentication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `frobd-rpc` | `frobd` | Unix-domain socket, mode 0600, under the state root | on | always | file permission (the effective user) |
| `mcp-http` | `frob-mcp`, `frobd --mcp` | TCP | 127.0.0.1 only | a non-loopback address needs `--bind`, `--allow-remote`, tokens enabled and the configuration key below | bearer token mapped to a capability token |
| `peer-link` | `frobd` | TCP, encrypted | off | the configuration key below, and only if the link was decided `build` | mutual, against one configured public key |

A source scan in the conformance crate (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/listener_register.rs`) fails with
`listener.unregistered` on any call that binds or listens on a socket (the standard library's listeners, the
MCP library's `bind_http` and `bind_websocket`, the runtime's listeners) outside the modules the register names.
No workspace check is added.

**4. The owner-held key (DESIGN).** The schema `frob.safety/1` of `$FROB_STATE/safety.toml` gains
`network_listeners = []`, a list of register identifiers with their bind addresses; default empty. A listener
that is not `on` by default starts only if listed, and a wildcard address must be written literally. The file
is the owner's, is read once at start and its digest is in every job row, so a result shows whether a network
listener was enabled when it was produced.

**5. The checklist (what "reviewed" means for a row; each item names who tests it).**
1. Off or loopback by default; enabled only through the owner-held key (this bead's test).
2. Authentication completes before any frame beyond the handshake is parsed; exactly the configured identities
   are accepted (the mechanism's bead).
3. Encryption in transit for every non-loopback listener (the mechanism's bead).
4. Limits: handshake and idle timeouts, frame length, blob size per class, concurrent connections; each limit
   has a refusal and a test at the limit and one past it (the mechanism's bead).
5. No new authority: a remote principal acts under an attenuated capability token; scopes and budgets never
   widen; no path outside the session's roots; no shell (the capability bead's tests, run over the listener).
6. Data classes: only the allowed transfer classes cross; private-marked objects never cross; every string
   received is typed untrusted (the mechanism's bead and the private-places tests, run over the listener).
7. Replay: a re-sent request with the same idempotency key is a no-op that returns the existing job.
8. A fuzz target on the frame parser with 10⁷ executions and no crash (TARGET; the figure the importer gate
   uses), and the crafted limit corpus.
9. Every accepted and refused connection is a ledger event with the remote identity and no payload.
10. A review by a fresh-context session that did not implement the listener, recorded in
    `docs/delivery/reviews/listener-<id>.md` with the result of each item; the register row's `review` field
    cites it, and a row whose mechanism has shipped without a review fails the register test.

**6. Reporting.** `frob doctor --json` gains a read-only section `listeners`, contributed through BD-022's
section interface (selectable with `--section listeners`): for each open listener its register `id`,
transport, bound address and whether authentication is enforced. The default-closed test compares it with the
operating system's view. Determinism class D0 for the register checks; evidence class Tested.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists; `registries/threats.toml` holds thirteen rows (the nine and T1.c, T1.d, T3.b, T4.c) and
      the traceability test passes with the new rows.
- [ ] `registries/listeners.toml` holds the three rows; the source scan passes on the tree and fails with
      `listener.unregistered` on a planted `TcpListener::bind` in `frob-chronicle`.
- [ ] On a default configuration, `frobd` and `frobd --mcp --mcp-http 127.0.0.1:<port>` open no listener other
      than the Unix socket and the loopback port, by the daemon's own report and by the operating system's.
- [ ] `frobd --mcp --mcp-http 0.0.0.0:<port>` without the configuration key does not start and names the key;
      with `--bind` and `--allow-remote` and without the key it still does not start.
- [ ] `safety.toml` with an unknown listener identifier is a start-up error.
- [ ] A register row marked shipped with an empty `review` fails the register test.
- [ ] The ADR carries the checklist with the tester of each item, so that a listener's bead can cite it item
      by item; control `LST-X01` is registered and caught by step 5 of the script.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test listener_register` (the real registers; plants are scratch copies
labelled `synthetic`):
- `register_rows_exact`: three rows with the fields above.
- `scan_finds_unregistered_bind`: the planted bind fails naming file and line; the registered modules pass.
- `threat_rows_trace`: each of the four new rows has a mechanism, existing refusal codes and a control.
- `shipped_row_needs_review`; `unknown_listener_id_in_safety_file_fails`.
`cargo test -p frob-verbs listeners`: `default_configuration_enables_none`; `wildcard_needs_literal`;
`non_loopback_http_needs_key_and_flags`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/safe_listeners.sh`: (1) starts `frobd` in a scratch state root with the default `safety.toml`;
`frob doctor --json`, asserting the `listeners` block holds exactly `frobd-rpc`; (2) the operating system's list
of listening sockets of that process (`lsof -nP -iTCP -sTCP:LISTEN -a -p <pid>` on macOS, `ss -ltnp` on Linux),
asserting no TCP listener; (3) restarts with `--mcp --mcp-http 127.0.0.1:<port>`, asserting one TCP listener on
127.0.0.1 and none elsewhere; (4) attempts the two non-loopback starts of the fourth criterion, asserting a
non-zero exit and the message naming `network_listeners`; (5) two `tools/call` requests to the loopback port
with `curl` (the CLI does not speak to the HTTP transport): one with no `Authorization` header, asserting the
library's unauthorized response and no handler run (N32a-27's table), and one whose bearer token belongs to a
session opened with `frob --json session open --ttl-s 1 --key "$(e2e_key short-ttl)"` and left to expire,
asserting an envelope with `FROB-REF-723` and exit code 5; then the same expired-token request against the
build with control `LST-X01` (`--features gauntlet-mutations`), which must be admitted, so the step proves it
can fail. It stops every process it started. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/safe_listeners/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd`
lines with exact command line, exit code and wall time; a `metric` line per listener found (id, transport,
address); `refusal` events with code and exit; one `assert` per step; the configuration digest; a `summary`
line. No private-scene data is involved.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: thirteen threat rows; three listener rows; one listener on a default daemon; zero TCP listeners
  outside 127.0.0.1 on a default configuration.
- Negative controls (each must be caught): the planted unregistered bind; a build in which the HTTP transport
  ignores the bind rule (step 4 must fail it); a daemon whose `listeners` block omits an open socket (step 2
  disagrees with step 1); a shipped row without a review.
- Determinism: the register checks are pure functions of the tree; the script is idempotent and uses a free
  port chosen once and logged.

## Considerations

- If the owner decides the link is not built, the `peer-link` row stays in the register as `off` with no
  review, and the default-closed test is the whole of this bead's lasting effect. That is the intended minimum.
- A machine with a firewall is not a mitigation the register counts; the checklist assumes the listener is
  reachable by anyone who can route to the address.
- Policy weights cross the peer link and are hostile data on arrival (plain layout, allowlisted family, finite
  values, size cap), exactly as when an agent submits them.
- What would make us revisit: a third listener (a metrics endpoint, a viewer server). It needs a row, the key
  and a review before its first bind, and the source scan is what forces the question.
