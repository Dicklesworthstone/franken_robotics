## Background

FrankenRobotics simulates wheeled and tracked robots on deformable ground with two laws that are Frontier-tier
[F] (published terramechanics that mainstream robot simulators have not productized): pressure–sinkage
p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ for a contact of width b (so a plate under pressure p sinks z = (p/(k_c/b + k_φ))^(1/n)), and
shear τ = (c + σ·tan φ)·(1 − exp(−j/K)) for shear displacement j. Those laws, the soil cards that carry their
constants and ranges, and a first rut fixture land at M4 (BD-366) under the gate GATE-TRAC-08 (law evaluations
within 10⁻⁶ of the closed forms in `f64`; simulated plate sinkage and drawbar pull within 2%; rut leak zero over
10³ resets) and the kill gate KILL-SOIL (fallback: a rigid floor with rolling resistance and a drag card
labelled `assumed`).

This epic is the next step, at M6 and off every critical path: terrain that deforms and stays deformed, at batch
scale. Ruts and footprints are Moonshot-tier [M]: a per-world mutable buffer over shared static tiles. The
benefit is coverage of repeated-pass tasks (a second pass over one's own rut; a tracked base turning in place);
there is no speed benefit to claim. The risk is specific: mutable terrain is state that outlives a step, and at
batch scale state can leak, between worlds of a batch or from one episode into the next. A rut made in one
episode must have no effect on another. That is why the done-condition is a leak count, not a fidelity number.

As an epic of M6 it is split into tasks when the milestone opens. Part of its done-condition is that a short
plan exists and that its paper-scale pilot has run; an epic whose mechanism has no gate in the gate register
writes its kill gate and its fallback as part of its plan.

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to be written to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-417.md` when M6 opens), in outline.**
1. Statement of the mechanism: per-world rut state as copy-on-write tiles over the shared static terrain;
   sinkage written by the soil law; read back by contact as a height offset Δz ≤ 0, with the second-pass rule
   treating the ground below a stored depth as compacted (the rut field of N18b-17, one layout on every tier).
2. The pilot and its falsifier (N18b-16), run on the CPU batch tier at M4 before anything is funded: falsifier any
   leak (a rut made in one world or episode changes a digest of another world or of a later episode that did not
   declare persistence, or a marked world's digest differs from its solo run); its memory and step-cost figures
   are funding flags, not falsifiers, and are answered by the kill gate below.
3. Ceiling, stated first: coverage of repeated-pass tasks; nil on decision time and on throughput (the buffers
   cost memory and a scatter per step).
4. Kill gate and fallback, written before funding. Proposed wording (DESIGN, to be registered as KILL-RUT):
   *Trigger*: the pilot's falsifier holds; or the pilot's funding flags stand (memory above 256 KiB per world on
   its fixture, or the paired lower bound of its step-cost ratio above 1.25); or at batch scale any leak in 10³
   resets on a backend the epic claims; or the rut buffers break the fixed launch shape or the determinism class
   of a batch; or they add more than 25% to step cost on the reference tracked workload (paired lower bound of
   the ratio above 1.25). *Fallback*: persistent deformation off; terrain resets each episode (sinkage is then
   computed per step from the law with no memory). *Decided*: M6, per backend. The registered row is N38a-37's.
5. Tasks: N38a-29 (the mechanism on `batch-cpu`, then CUDA and Metal); known answers; the M6 exit record.
6. Memory arithmetic (planning values, DESIGN): rut tiles of 8 × 8 offsets in `f32` (the layout of N18b-17 that
   N38a-29 keeps) are 256 B each; the default budget T_rut = 1,024 tiles per world is 256 KiB per world (the
   same memory and rutted area as the 64 tiles of 32 × 32, 4 KiB each, of the first planning figures), 1 GiB at
   4,096 worlds and 2 GiB at 8,192; a world that exceeds its tile budget is marked `Degraded: rut-capacity` and
   handled by the caller's policy (under `refuse`, the named refusal `rut-capacity-overflow`), never truncated.

**Done-condition of the epic (seed).** A short plan exists and its pilot has run; rut state stays per-world with
zero leak over 10³ resets at batch scale.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the child beads: the leak fixture, reset and checkpoint fixtures, and the tile-budget refusal
(N18b-16, N38a-29). The epic itself adds one check: `epic_bd417_children_recorded`, a query that the pilot
record, the kill-gate record and the plan file exist before the epic may close, and that the KILL-RUT
amendment's ledger sequence precedes the first commit of N38a-29.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/soil_ruts_leak.sh` (owned by N38a-29) is the epic's measurement: it logs, through the shared
helper, resets run, leak count against the threshold zero, memory per world, and the step-cost ratio against
1.25.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers the epic rests on: plate sinkage with b = 0.1 m, k_c = 1 kPa/mⁿ⁻¹, k_φ = 1,500 kPa/mⁿ, n = 1.1,
p = 20 kPa gives z = 19.6 mm (KA-415, illustrative); track thrust for two tracks of 0.5 m by 0.1 m under 500 N
with c = 1 kPa, φ = 30°, K = 25 mm has a limit of 389 N and gives 293 N at 20% slip (KA-416); the power-law
fixture (KA-837) with n = 0.5, k_c = 0.41 kN/m^1.5, k_φ = 21.6 kN/m^2.5, g = 9.81 m/s², two tracks of length
0.55 m: 45 kg on 8 cm tracks sinks 3.52 cm, 75 kg on 8 cm sinks 9.79 cm, 75 kg on 12 cm sinks 4.96 cm, and the
exponent 0.7 gives 0.091646, 0.190127 and 0.117076 m (9.16, 19.01 and 11.71 cm; its negative control, computed
from the unrounded pressure p = 5.01648 kPa: the plan's 9.17 came from p rounded to 5.017 kPa). Rut isolation: a rut made in one episode is absent from every other.
Negative control of the epic: a shared (not per-world) rut buffer, which the leak fixture must catch.

## Considerations

- A soil card declares the ranges of pressure, slip and sinkage over which it holds; a query outside them is
  refused (`FROB-REF-243`). Every value a gate pins is derived on file from a cited soil table read at its
  primary source; no physical claim about a real soil follows from an illustrative card.
- At most four Moonshot items are active at once; this epic waits its turn in the default order of funding.
- If KILL-SOIL fired at M4, this epic is not funded: there is no soil law to deform.
- The whole milestone pauses whenever the critical path is slipping, since nothing here is on it.
