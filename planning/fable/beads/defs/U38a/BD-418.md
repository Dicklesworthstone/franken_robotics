## Background

FrankenRobotics models contact between a robot and the world with a small number of contact samples per body,
each a *four-dimensional contact*: one normal component, two tangential components and one spin (torsional)
component, with a friction cone over all four. That is enough for feet, wheels and palms. It is not enough to
say what a fingertip *feels*. A tactile sensor is an array of small sensing elements (taxels) under a compliant
pad; what it reports depends on how pressure and shear are distributed over the pad, and whether a grasp is
about to slip shows first at the pad's edge.

This epic admits that capability at the Moonshot tier [M], at M6, off every critical path, behind a feature
flag. Two pieces, which only make sense together: (1) *compliant pads*: a fingertip pad represented as a small
array of micro-patches, 2 × 2 or 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts, instead of one patch; (2) *tactile arrays*:
a sensor model with finite taxel footprints and saturation in normal and shear load, fed by those micro-patch
contacts. They are what the "dexterous hands" variant of the design language needs; no task before M6 needs
them. Dexterous in-hand manipulation benchmarks remain out of scope of the program; reach-and-carry mobile
manipulation is in scope without tactile sensing.

The ceiling is stated before any test: realism of tactile sensing; no speed benefit; cost rises with the contact
count (a 4 × 4 pad is sixteen contacts where there was one). A related, stronger idea was killed in review and
stays killed: micro-slip precursors (vibration modes coupled to tangential micro-slip, so that a policy senses
slip before it happens) cannot be validated without tactile-array data; it may be reopened only behind the
data-import lane, with real measurements.

As an epic of M6 it is split into tasks when the milestone opens. Its seed done-condition: a short plan exists
and its pilot has run; a kill gate and its fallback are written before any work package is funded.

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to be written to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-418.md` when M6 opens), in outline.**
1. Mechanism: a pad as an N × N grid of micro-patches (N = 2 or 4), each a four-dimensional contact with its own
   normal stiffness from the pad's compliance card; a taxel array whose reading is formed from the micro-patch
   loads over the sensor's exposure window.
2. Pilot and falsifier (N38a-30): a fingertip pad as 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts on the `strict`
   profile of the CPU batch tier, feeding the sensor model, against one patch per pad. Falsifier: no change in
   grasp-slip outcomes against one patch.
3. Ceiling: tactile sensing realism only.
4. Kill gate and fallback, written before funding. Proposed wording (DESIGN, to be registered as KILL-TACTILE):
   *Trigger*: the pilot's falsifier holds; or the micro-patch pad adds more than 25% to step cost on the grasp
   workload; or the pad's contact count cannot be admitted on any tier without removing contacts (two 4 × 4 pads
   are 32 contacts, above the referee's limit of 21 in one solve); or the taxel resultant fails its known answer.
   *Fallback*: one patch per pad, with the sensor reading the single patch's wrench; the "dexterous hands"
   variant is then described by that fallback. *Decided*: M6.
5. Tasks: N38a-31 (pads and the tactile array on `strict`, then the batch tiers if cost allows); known answers;
   the M6 exit record.
6. Funding rule for a Frontier sensor model, which applies here: it is funded only when a registered task needs
   it, names one analytic fixture as its pilot, has no speed benefit to claim, and falls back to the simpler
   model under the same cost rule.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the child beads. The epic adds `epic_bd418_gate_before_funding`: a query that the kill-gate record's
ledger sequence precedes the first commit of any child task other than the pilot.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tactile_pad_grasp.sh` (owned by N38a-31) is the epic's measurement: grasp-slip outcomes with one
patch, 2 × 2 and 4 × 4, the taxel resultant check, and the step-cost ratio against 1.25, logged through the
shared helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers the epic rests on: the resultant load of a tactile array equals the sum over its taxels (KA-523;
its tolerance and its negative control are not stated in the source and are set by N38a-31); the torsional lever
of a uniformly loaded square pad of side s about its centre is 0.3826·s, which a 2 × 2 grid of point contacts
approximates as 0.3536·s and a 4 × 4 grid as 0.3744·s (derived in N38a-30). Negative control of the epic: a
taxel array that reports the pad's total load in every taxel (resultant wrong by the taxel count).

## Considerations

- Capacity: the referee tier admits at most 21 contacts in one solve, so the pilot and first implementation run
  on the `strict` profile; a device path must respect the rule against capacity bias (never remove contacts
  silently).
- At most four Moonshot items are active at once; this epic is queued fourth in the default order of funding.
- No reality claim: without tactile-array measurements imported through the data lane, every tactile result is
  labelled with its card's provenance (`generic` or `assumed`).
