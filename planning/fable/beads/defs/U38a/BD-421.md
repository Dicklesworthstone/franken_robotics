## Background

FrankenRobotics generates interior scenes (homes, apartments, offices, warehouses, plants, retail stores,
kitchens) in which robot designs are evaluated. Real interiors contain things that move on their own: people,
pets, forklifts, carts. The hazard lists of the scene archetypes name the failures they provoke: an unexpected
obstacle within stopping distance (a pet, 0.2 to 0.6 m tall, moving), a collision within stopping distance (a
forklift or cart), a stopping-distance failure near a shopper. These dimensions are recorded as `assumed`; no
norm or dataset was read for them.

This epic admits such movers at the Frontier tier [F], at M6, off every critical path, in the weakest form that
is still useful: *kinematic capsule actors on route graphs*. An actor is a capsule that moves along the edges of
the scene's route graph with a prescribed speed profile and a yielding rule. It is not simulated: it does not
perceive, decide or react except through its declared rule, and nothing pushes it. Every object that can move
in a scene declares exactly one behaviour, recorded in the episode manifest, and an actor's is `prescribed`.

The limit is the point of the epic and its done-condition: actors are scenario dressing, and **no claim about
human behaviour is made from them, because it could not be checked.** Simulated humans as validated agents are
an explicit non-goal of the program; the claim-bearing form of "simulated human co-inhabitants" was rejected in
review. What an actor may support is a claim about the robot under a stated script (it stopped in time; it
collided), labelled as conditional on that script.

Ceiling, stated first: nil on decision time; actors widen geometric and dynamic variety. Kill gate (already in
the gate register, decided for asset-populated interiors at M5 by N19a-41 on the measurements of BD-399, and
re-read at M6 on the actors themselves by N19ax-05): KILL-DRESSING. Trigger: either
dressing item breaks the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch, adds more than 25% to step
cost, or exposes no failure class beyond the structural proxies on the M5 battery. Fallback: structural proxies
and static clutter only.

As an epic of M6 it is split into tasks when the milestone opens; its seed done-condition: a short plan exists
and its pilot has run; no claim about human behaviour is issued from an actor.

## Scope

In: the short plan; the pilot (N38a-32); the mechanism (N38a-33) if funded; the M6 re-reading of KILL-DRESSING

pushed by the robot.

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to be written to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-421.md` when M6 opens), in outline.**
1. Mechanism: capsule actors with a route, a speed profile and a yielding rule, as pure functions of the world
   address and time, colliding with the robot as kinematic bodies.
2. Pilot and falsifier (N38a-32): for one archetype, list the failure classes of the hazard registry and mark
   which a moving actor could provoke that the structural proxy cannot; if the list is empty the tier is not
   funded.
3. Ceiling: no gain in decision time; variety only.
4. Kill gate and fallback: KILL-DRESSING as stated above; this epic re-reads its trigger on the actor mechanism
   itself (launch shape, determinism class, step cost, new failure classes on the battery), by N19ax-05, which
   records the decision as `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DRESSING-M6.md` (N40-03's form: its required fields, one of the
   outcomes `not-fired`, `fired` or `not-reached`, scope `item:actors`), an addendum citing the M5 record.
5. The no-behaviour-claim rule, enforced by type and by refusal: actor trajectories are scenario inputs with no
   evidence class of their own; a requirement, metric or report whose subject is an actor (its comfort, its
   delay, its "reaction") is refused with the uncoded slug `actor-subject-claim` (`3xx`, exit 3; listed by slug
   in the Habitat registry N19-REF, row 10 of the numbering decision N19c-42, which takes the number from the
   allocation ledger of N35-33; raised by N38a-33 through one constant, never a number of its own); every result
   from a scene with actors carries the condition flag `actor-scripted`, joined by union through the claim
   product. With the feature `frontier-actors` off (the default) or the actors' decision `fired`, a manifest with
   an actor still parses and its compilation is refused with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, naming the capability and
   its status (`not-in-build` or `killed`).
6. Tasks: N38a-33; N19ax-05; known answers (stopping distance); the M6 exit record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the child beads. The epic adds `epic_bd421_no_behaviour_claims`: a scan of the verb registry and the
metric registry that no metric, requirement atom or report field has an actor as its subject.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_actors.sh` (owned by N38a-33) is the epic's measurement: the stopping-distance known answer
with an actor, the refusal of an actor-subject metric, the determinism and launch-shape checks, and the
step-cost ratio against 1.25, logged through the shared helper.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer the epic rests on: a robot moving at speed v with reaction latency τ and deceleration a stops in
d = v·τ + v²/(2a); for v = 1.0 m/s, τ = 0.2 s, a = 2.0 m/s², d = 0.45 m, so an actor that enters the path 0.40 m
ahead is struck and one that enters 0.50 m ahead is not. Negative control of the epic: a report that states "the
pedestrian yielded in 90% of episodes" as a finding (it must be refused: the yielding is the script).

## Considerations

- Privacy and tone: actors are capsules; no likeness, identity or demographic attribute exists in the model.
- Actors must not break the fixed launch shape of a batch: their count per scene is padded to a declared
  capacity, and their motion is evaluated from the address, so it adds no per-world branching.
- If KILL-DRESSING fired at M5 for populated interiors, this epic is re-read: actors may still be funded on
  their own pilot only if their own trigger clauses pass.
- The hazard dimensions are `assumed`; a result that depends on them says so.
