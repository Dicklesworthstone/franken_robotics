## Background

The thesis of FrankenRobotics is tested by one measurement: the wall-clock time from the moment an agent
receives a mission card to the moment an *accepted* answer exists, compared between this system (arm A) and the
strongest composed rival (arm B, and a short program over an incumbent engine, arm C). Everything turns on the
word "accepted". If an arm could accept its own answers, or if answers were scored in the arm's own simulator,
the time ratio would measure how lenient each arm is with itself. This epic is the work that puts acceptance in
the hands of a third party.

Three parties exist for the life of a gate, and a person or an agent session holds exactly one role
(INV-TENFOLD-01): the *builders* wrote FrankenRobotics; the *rival advocates* build and tune the rival arms and
have written no FrankenRobotics code; the *adjudicator* writes the sealed missions and the referee protocol,
runs acceptance and releases receipts, and belongs to neither group.

As an epic of M7 it is split into tasks when the milestone opens. Its seed done-condition: a short plan exists
and its pilot has run; an answer is accepted only by the adjudicator, and a planted false acceptance in a dry
run is caught.

## Scope

In: the short plan for adjudication at the tournament; the dry run (N38a-34); the acceptance rules below as the
contract the tasks implement; the labels on results. Out: the sealed suite (BD-428); the decision rule and its
power analysis (BD-429); the arms (BD-430, BD-431); publication (BD-433); replay of qualification campaigns by
the offline auditor (BD-383), which this epic uses.

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-432.md` when M7 opens) covers the following contract.**

1. **Answer kinds; a mission card names exactly one.**

| Kind | Submitted | Accepted when |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified design | A design in the mission's family, a controller behind the standard observation and action interface, the declared clocks | The acceptance battery passes in the adjudicating simulator: over n sealed world clusters, the exact one-sided lower confidence bound on the requirement pass rate is at least the mission's threshold |
| Family answer | Inner and outer design sets with witnesses, exclusions with impossibility witnesses, the unresolved band | Every mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligation of the card is discharged; the unresolved band or optimality gap is within the card's maximum; every certificate checks; direct evaluation at the adjudicator's sealed probe points contradicts nothing. Strict and closed endpoints are part of acceptance where an exact boundary is asked for. A vacuous enclosure discharges nothing |
| Amendment verdict | For a changed mission: which earlier verdicts survive, which are withdrawn, and the repaired answer | The repaired answer passes as its own kind; no kept verdict is one the adjudicator's ground truth withdraws. A missed withdrawal fails the mission; unnecessary invalidations are counted and reported |
| Truthful unknown | A statement that the evidence does not identify the choice, with the required witness (two admissible worlds or models under which the best choices differ, or the distinguishing experiment) | Only on missions whose question is whether the evidence identifies the choice; elsewhere `unknown` is not an answer and the clock keeps running |
| Counterexample and repair | A replayable failing world inside the legality bounds, and a repaired design | The failure reproduces in the adjudicating simulator, and the repaired design passes the battery of the first row |

2. **The neutral adjudicating simulator.** Dynamic acceptance batteries run in a simulator that neither arm used
   for training, tuning or screening on that stratum, whose contact model was derived independently of both
   arms, that runs deterministically on a CPU, and that is pinned by revision. Reason: an arm scored in its own
   engine is scored on its own modelling errors, and two programs that share a solver have correlated error.
   Default candidate: a CPU multibody engine with its own convex contact solver at a pinned release. Where that
   engine is used by an arm, or cannot represent the mission, another engine meeting the four conditions is
   substituted. If none exists the stratum falls back to *cross-home adjudication* (an answer must pass in the
   highest-trust tier of both arms, run by the adjudicator), labelled `adjudication: cross-home`
   (KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ; if neither home referee can run the other arm's answers, the stratum is `descriptive`).
   Models reach the adjudicating simulator through a neutral exchange description read by the adjudicator's own
   importer; a controller runs as a separate process behind the observation and action interface with the
   mission's clocks. The gap between an arm's home simulator and the adjudicator is that arm's risk.
3. **Certificates.** Every submitted mathematical assertion is replayed by the offline auditor `frob-verify`,
   whose schema is public and which any arm may target. Because the builders wrote that checker, it is not the
   last word: the adjudicator also evaluates each family answer directly (exact rational evaluation at sealed
   probe points, an SMT or interval tool on the raw mission statement, dense sampling in the adjudicating
   simulator). A disagreement between an accepted certificate and direct evaluation is a false certificate, and
   one false certificate disqualifies the arm from the scoped claim (KILL-TENFOLD-CERT).
4. **The referee protocol is not written by the builders.** The adjudicator writes it from the public class
   descriptions, the mission grammar and the schemas; builders and advocates comment through one logged channel
   until the suite hash is committed; then it is frozen and its hash is in the ledger. Builders supply schemas,
   the Gymnasium-compatible adapter and the development missions, and no acceptance code.
5. **Submissions and retries.** Acceptance uses the protocol of the blinded qualification lane on the
   adjudicator's own sealed key: a submission is committed before any world is drawn; each submission draws
   fresh world clusters; the i-th submission on a mission is tested at level α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] with α = 0.05;
   the receipt holds only the decision, the index, the level spent, the units used and a hash; a rejected
   submission returns no per-world outcome. Retrying costs clock time and cannot buy acceptance by repetition.
   Where the format allows, answers are scored without knowing which arm produced them; paired results are
   released only after every arm of the gate has closed.
6. **Who.** An external party with no authorship in any arm. Fail-closed default if none can be engaged: a
   fresh-context agent session with no write access to any arm, operating from the protocol document alone
   under a named human, every action logged; every ratio so measured is labelled `self-adjudicated`, and the
   words "independently accepted" and "ten-fold" wait until an external party has re-run the replication kit.
7. **Three rules that protect the bar.** The time score is never improved by lowering what counts as accepted.
   An answer accepted and later shown wrong within its scope scores that mission as unresolved at the deadline
   for the arm that submitted it. A correct refusal that arrives after the deadline is a missed deadline.
8. **Adjudicator acceptance (GATE-TENFOLD-05, TARGET; M3 and M7).** The adjudicating simulator passes the known
   answers it can express inside its own stated tolerances; of 20 planted answers per stratum (10 that must
   pass, 10 that must fail) it classifies 20 correctly; no arm's logs show it in training or tuning; on a 5%
   sample of accepted answers the discordance with each arm's home referee is reported.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The short plan exists, states the eight parts above, names the adjudicator (or records the fail-closed
      default and its label) and the simulator per stratum.
- [ ] The dry run N38a-34 has run and its result is recorded; the planted false acceptance was caught.
- [ ] An answer is accepted only by the adjudicator (seed done-condition): no receipt exists that was not signed
      under the adjudicator's sealed key, and a planted arm-issued receipt is refused.
- [ ] GATE-TENFOLD-05 has a recorded value per stratum before the sealed suite is opened, or KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ's
      decision is recorded with the fallback label.
- [ ] Roles are disjoint for the life of the gate; a planted session holding two roles is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the dry run and by the harness beads of the ten-fold test. The epic adds
`epic_bd432_receipts_only_from_adjudicator`: a query over the gate's ledger that every acceptance receipt
verifies under the adjudicator's committed key.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/adjudication_dry_run.sh` (owned by N38a-34): a complete acceptance cycle on development missions
with planted answers, logged through the shared helper with every decision, level spent and refusal.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the level series α_i = 0.05/[i·(i + 1)] (0.025, 0.00833, 0.00417, …) sums to 0.05·m/(m + 1) < 0.05;
20 of 20 planted answers classified. Negative controls the harness must catch: a planted false certificate (the
arm is disqualified); a canary value from a sealed world in a transcript (the cell is void); an arm that touched
the adjudicating simulator during tuning (disqualified); a planted false acceptance in the dry run (caught).

## Considerations

- Open (owner decision, due before any sealed world family exists): who adjudicates and in which simulator;
  default as in part 6, with every ratio labelled `self-adjudicated`.
- Acceptance happens in simulation; the adjudicating simulator is a third model, not the world; no reality
  claim follows from any acceptance.
- A tournament is voided and re-run only for a breach of protocol (a pin moved during the freeze, sealed
  material read by an arm, an arm changed after its freeze); a disqualification awards nothing to the other arm.
- Risk named in the program's register: no neutral party is named, so nobody outside can confirm the result;
  the countermeasure is the label and the replication kit.
