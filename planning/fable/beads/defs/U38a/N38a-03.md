## Background

The Gauntlet (the verification subsystem of FrankenRobotics) reads its six tiers (G0 laws, G1 known answers, G2
parity, G3 metamorphic, G4 chaos, G5 determinism) for each subsystem this version of the design adds. For nine
subjects the design names one leading test per tier. The gates are the owning subsystem's; the Gauntlet's part is
that no cell is silently empty: a subject that has laws but no chaos test, or a determinism claim that nothing
exercises, is a hole that a reader of a pass table cannot see.

DESIGN: the matrix is data (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/tier_matrix.toml`), each cell is bound to a
registered runner entry, and a meta-test fails on an unbound cell that is due. Reason: registries are data and
documents are their printout. Rejected: prose in each subsystem's contract (no join is possible across crates).

## Scope

In: the registry of 54 cells, the binding check, the printout. Out: implementing each cell's test (the owning
subsystem's beads, which bind by entry id).

## Technical approach

**Record.** `[[cell]] subject, tier, statement, entry (runner entry id or "—"), gate, milestone, owner_section`.
A cell printed "—" is an explicit absence with its reason; it is not an omission.

**Check (`tests/tier_matrix.rs`).**
1. Load the registry; require exactly 9 subjects × 6 tiers = 54 cells, 52 with an entry and 2 marked "—" with a
   reason.
2. For each cell with an entry whose milestone is at or before the current one, require that the runner's table
   contains that entry id, that its tier equals the cell's tier, and that the entry names the cell's gate.
3. For each bound G3 cell, require the relation to exist in the relation registry with a registered mutation; for
   each bound G1 cell, require its known-answer rows to exist; for each bound G4 cell, require a chaos family.
4. Print the matrix with the status of each cell (`bound`, `due-unbound`, `not-yet-due`, `—`); `due-unbound`
   fails the test.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The registry holds 54 cells with the statements above, each with its owner section, gate and milestone.
- [ ] The check passes when every due cell is bound, and fails naming the cell when one is unbound.
- [ ] A cell bound to an entry of the wrong tier is reported.
- [ ] The printed matrix regenerates byte for byte from the registry.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `matrix_has_54_cells`: 9 × 6, two explicit absences.
- `due_unbound_fails`: with the current milestone set to M2 and the cell "families of bodies / G5" unbound, the
  test fails and prints `due-unbound: families-of-bodies/G5`.
- `wrong_tier_binding_reported`: a G4 cell bound to a G1 entry is rejected.
- `absence_needs_reason`: a "—" cell with an empty reason is rejected.
- `printout_regenerates`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gauntlet_tier_matrix.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-03 milestone=M1 labels=gauntlet,registry
plants=unbind-cell`). It runs the check at the current milestone, prints the matrix, then plants `unbind-cell`
and requires the failure. Log at `target/e2e-logs/gauntlet_tier_matrix/<utc>.ndjson`: the cargo commands, the
count of cells per status as `metric` lines, one `assert` per due cell, the negative-control assertion, and the
`summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

The cell statements carry their own numbers (orbit counts 86,185, 500,500 and 92; 19/21; L = 1,200; 1,024
coalitions; 10⁵ faults); this bead does not re-derive them and asserts only that each is bound to a registered
test of the owning subsystem. Negative controls: an unbound due cell; a wrong-tier binding; a silently deleted
row (53 cells). Determinism lane: the check is class D0; identical output twice.

## Considerations

- The matrix is a floor, not the suite: each subsystem has many more tests per tier.
- The two "—" cells are decisions of the design, not gaps: neither the qualification lane nor the clock contract
  has an external oracle; if one appears the cell is filled by a registry edit with its reason.
- The incumbent mixed-batch figure is context only and is never a threshold.
