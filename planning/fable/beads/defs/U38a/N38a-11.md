## Background

Twenty small pilots were run on the ideas of FrankenRobotics before any of its code existed; each must be re-run
inside this repository at M0 and recorded before a gate cites it (GATE-GAUNT-08). Sibling beads re-run the
docking pilot (BD-041), the stance pilot (BD-042), the other mission-query pilots (BD-043) and the native
boundary screens (BD-030). Six rows remain, and this bead re-runs them. Each is the recorded origin of a rule:

- the fixed-n enumeration is why a reachability pre-analysis is mandatory before any fixed-sample study;
- the power simulation is why the ten-fold decision rule was rewritten as a pooled ratio with a paired lower bound;
- the wheel-loop pilot is why a held command is modelled exactly instead of by a per-step update;
- the rotor and cadence sweep is why a design comparison pins rotor inertia and controller rate;
- the two conditioning surveys are why half-precision contact solves are rejected and single precision is used
  with diagonal scaling and a guard.

As with every plan-time pilot: none used FrankenSim dynamics, contact or a real sensor pipeline (rows 19 and 20
are re-created here on a dynamics source, which is new); every strong baseline tied or won; no physical claim
follows.

## Scope

In: rows 13, 14, 17, 18, 19 and 20 of the plan-time ledger: locating or re-creating each program, running it,
comparing with the recorded values, recording. Three of these rows were also drafted elsewhere; each is run
once, here:
- Rows 19 and 20 (the two conditioning surveys) are also one of the three checks "whose scripts were not
  retained". This bead writes and runs the one retained program, `tools/oracle/rerun/contact_conditioning.py`.
  N00-35 keeps the other two unretained checks (CPU reference rates, Metal memory limits), reads this bead's
  result for the conditioning check, and applies its consequences to the hypothesis rows.
- Row 14 (the draft ten-fold rule) is simulated here, at M0, by the one implementation of that simulation. The
  M7 pilot of the tournament decision rule (N39b-16) calls this module and cites this record instead of
  re-creating the calculation; the power table of the pooled rule and the binding pre-analysis stay there.
- Row 17 (the wheel loop) is re-run here from the archive; the rule that the fixture's three figures come from
  one plant, the regression test on the fixture file and the stale-value scan are NEG-10.

Out: the product implementations that later make these known answers (the pre-analysis verb, the fixture's
analytic layer, the conditioning certificate), which belong to their subsystems; the update of hypothesis H-15
and of the scaling requirement when a conditioning result differs (N00-35).

## Technical approach

**Common procedure.** Recover the cited revision of the reproduction archive from repository history
(`git show a635b91:planning/astra-10x-reproduction.zip`: 241,568 bytes, SHA-256
`0326e89d76b88a2aa7d198d53556f80ab131fe5a902113dd37f7f32e93b09a4f`; it holds `sill/promotion_rule.py`,
`followups/wheel_sample.py`, `sill/winding-law-sensitivity.tsv`, `sill/planar_sill_pilot.py` and
`followups/N2_benchmark.py`, FACT re-observed at review), digest it, and extract it into
`target/pilots/<digest prefix>/` (never into the source tree); the drivers' output on this revision is the one
compared with the recorded values, and a run on the working-tree revision (`9620da42…`, 275,178 bytes) is a
second result (the source register's rule, NAB-15); record the environment; run each driver with assertions enabled; compare counts exactly,
printed decimals to the printed digits, ranges by containment; append one result per row to its ledger record
(`PILOT-PT-13`, `-14`, `-17`, `-18`, `-19`, `-20`) with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example pilots --
append-result <record> --result <file.json>` (N38a-10's append path, which copies the ledger's limits into the
result and derives `reproduced` or `differs`; this bead never writes a status), and read the derived status
back with `-- rerun-status <record> --json`.

**Catalogue entries that rest on these rows.** Four counterexample entries are FACT about these pilots and carry
`rerun = false` until the pilot is reproduced in this repository (the pair `rerun`, `rerun_receipt` of the
catalogue, N38b-05): CX-003 (N00-CX: a draft ten-fold rule that confirms a true ratio of 10 in at most 18% of
campaigns; row 14), CX-018 (N09-CX: a model assumption left in the prose around a claim; its wheel-loop case is

row 19). The `receipt` of this bead's result on the row is that entry's re-run receipt: where the record's
derived status is `reproduced`, the entry takes `rerun = true` and `rerun_receipt` equal to that hash; where it
is `differs`, the entry keeps `rerun = false` and the difference is listed for the entry's owner. CX-018 has two
licensing re-runs (N09-CX): it takes `rerun = true` only when both `PILOT-PT-17` and `PILOT-PT-07` read
`reproduced`, and its `rerun_receipt` then holds both receipt hashes in re-run order; until then it keeps
`rerun = false` with an empty receipt, and whichever of this bead and BD-041 appends the second reproducing

they write their rows; N00-CX and N09-CX are M0 beads with no order against this one, and an entry that is
already in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml` when the result is appended has its pair set

receipt hash.

**Row 13. Fixed-n rule enumeration.** Driver in the archive: `sill/promotion_rule.py` (standard library).
The rule compares two designs on n = 64 paired worlds with a five-point margin; a pair is concordant or favours
one design; a = count favouring A only, b = count favouring B only, a + b ≤ 64, giving (66 choose 2) = 2,145
count pairs. Each pair is classified `prefer`, `inferior`, `practical tie` or `inconclusive` from an exact
interval on the difference of success rates. Expected: 521 prefer, 521 inferior, 0 ties, 1,103 inconclusive;
with a = b = 0 the verdict is inconclusive with interval [−0.0662, +0.0662]; with a = 0 the least b that promotes
is 15 (interval [+0.0604, +0.3743]); with a = 1, 2, 3 it is 17, 19, 21; the smallest n at which a = b = 0 yields
a practical tie is 86. Note for the record: the half-width 0.0662 equals 1 − 0.0125^(1/64), a two-sided 97.5%
bound; at 95% it would be 0.0560 and the first tie would be at n = 72. The level is part of the rule.

**Row 14. Power of the draft ten-fold rule.** The draft rule, in full (the design's account of why the rule was
re-specified): on at least four of six briefs, including both held-out ones, a ratio of medians (rival time
over own time) of at least 10 with a lower bound above 5, over five seeds per brief. The short form used
elsewhere, "the point estimate of the time ratio is at least 10 on every brief", is a paraphrase of it.
Recorded (FACT, plan-time): under a true ratio of 10 it confirms in at most 18% of simulated campaigns
(log-normal run times, 5 to 20 seeds), in 0 to 11% with five seeds, and reaches 80% only near true ratios of 15
to 20. The cause the re-run must show: each brief needs its own point estimate above 10, which a true 10
delivers half the time, and the requirement compounds across briefs. Binomial core, before the lower-bound
condition: with success probability ½ per brief, both held-out briefs and at least two of the other four give
¼ · 11/16 = 17.19%.

Step 1: search both archive revisions for the original script (candidates: `followups/N2_benchmark.py`, in
both; `followups/decision_round.py` and `followups/decision_tolerance_audit.py`, present only in the
working-tree revision, FACT re-observed at review); if one is found to compute the draft rule's confirmation
rate, run it and record which revision it came from. Step 2, always:
re-create the simulation in this repository with the seed fixed, as a module with no dependency beyond the
standard library (DESIGN: `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/draft_rule.rs`; seeded, counter-based random
numbers so that counts are identical across thread counts; N39b-16 calls this module at M7). Grid: six briefs,
two of them held out; seeds per arm and brief k ∈ {5, 10, 20}; log-standard deviation σ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0};
true ratio R ∈ {5, 7, 8.5, 10, 12, 15, 20}; rival time ~ LogNormal(ln R, σ), own time ~ LogNormal(0, σ) (true
ratio of medians R); per-brief estimate = ratio of sample medians; per-brief lower bound (DESIGN, because the
record states the bound's threshold and not its construction; the original script governs if step 1 finds it):
the one-sided 95% bound exp(Δ − 1.645·√(s_B²/k + s_A²/k)) with Δ the difference of the mean logarithms and
s_A, s_B the sample standard deviations of the logarithms; a campaign confirms if at least four briefs,
including both held-out ones, have estimate ≥ 10 and bound > 5; 10⁵ campaigns per cell. Report the confirmation
rate per cell; the maximum over (k, σ) at R = 10, compared with the recorded "at most 18%"; the range over σ at
k = 5 and R = 10, compared with "0 to 11%"; and the least R of the grid at which any cell reaches 80%, compared
with "15 to 20". A value outside its recorded statement is recorded as `differs` with the cell that attains it.

Also computed, as context and not compared with the recorded 18%: the paraphrased rule "at least 10 on every
brief" for B ∈ {3, 6, 12, 24} briefs with the per-brief estimate the ratio of sample means. Sanity bound: an
estimator whose median is the true ratio exceeds it with probability ½ per brief, so B independent briefs
confirm with probability 2^(−B): 12.5% at B = 3 and 1.6% at B = 6. (An earlier draft of this bead compared the
18% with this variant's maximum; the figure belongs to the four-of-six rule above.)

**Row 17. Wheel-loop feedforward.** Driver: `followups/wheel_sample.py`. Model: the 75 mm front wheel of the
cart fixture with 0.0002 kg·m² added rotor inertia (total spin inertia 0.00107890625 kg·m²), torque constant
0.2 N·m/A, current command 2 A·s/rad times the rate error held for 10 ms, and the fixture's shaft damping
b = 0.002 N·m·s/rad. Expected output, all from that one plant: with the target-current feed-forward term
R·i_ref the one-tick spectral radius is 1.4820 by the exact flow (1.5248 at a 50 µs substep, 1.4875 at 6.25 µs);
with the measured-current term R·i it is 0.9207 (0.9124 and 0.9197); the instantaneous-current scalar magnitude
is 2.6917 (= |a − (0.4 ÷ b)·(1 − a)| with a = e^(−b·T/J), T = 0.01 s, J = 0.00107890625 kg·m²). The figure
2.7075 (= |1 − 2 × 0.2 × 0.01 ÷ 0.00107890625|) is the same scalar with the shaft damping left out. It is what
the first print of the fixture table showed beside 1.4820 and 0.9207, which mixed two plant models
(discrepancy E7-7, resolved by NEG-10); here it is reproduced only as a control, and every record, log line and
table that carries it labels it `undamped control`, so that the stale-value scan of NEG-10 passes. Recorded
beside it: an explicit current-then-wheel update at 0.5 ms gives 1.497 where 0.921 is right (and 1.854 for the
target-current law). Second part: `sill/winding-law-sensitivity.tsv`
with the planar pilot: 24 of 156 outcome labels change between the two feed-forward terms.

**Row 18. Rotor inertia and cadence.** Driver: `sill/planar_sill_pilot.py` with the settings recorded in
`sill/sensitivity/*.out` (tags for 100 Hz and 1 kHz control and for added rotor inertia of 2·10⁻⁴ to
1.5·10⁻³ kg·m²). Expected: success fractions 0.093 (design A) and 0.780 (design B) at 100 Hz with zero rotor
inertia; 1.000 and 1.000 at 1 kHz or with added inertia. The recorded loop multipliers at 1 kHz are
|1 − 1.11| = 0.11 for A and |1 − 0.46| = 0.54 for B. This row is exploratory by its own record; the re-run
reports per-cell counts and does not smooth them.

**Rows 19 and 20. Conditioning surveys (re-created; the original scripts were not retained).** These two rows
are the check the plan-time record lists as "condition numbers of sampled contact Hessians against
half-precision round-off", made by this program's own investigators with a script that was not kept; they are
what refuted half-precision contact solves (hypothesis H-15) and what the diagonal-scaling requirement of
single-precision solves cites. The program is committed as `tools/oracle/rerun/contact_conditioning.py`
(double precision with an extended-precision cross-check of the eigenvalues), with its tests in
`tools/oracle/rerun/test_conditioning.py`; it prints its inputs, seed and machine fingerprint.
1. Source of matrices: for each sampled state, the symmetric positive-definite matrix H that the contact solve
   factors, taken from the referee's dynamics source, the adapter `RefereeDynamics` over FrankenSim's
   `fs_mbd::articulated` at the pin of `franken-stack.lock` (BD-007, a dependency of this bead), and labelled
   `referee:fs_mbd@<pin>` (a run on the development oracle instead is labelled `development-oracle`). Per pose:
   the joint-space inertia M from the adapter, the contact Jacobian J of the feet (or wheels) in stance from the
   same model's frame kinematics (simplified contact rows: three translational rows per stance point), and
   H = M + Jᵀ·D·J, with D the diagonal of contact stiffness terms at the chosen impedance (default, and a
   near-rigid setting). The matrices are exported by an ignored-by-default test target of this bead,
   `crates/frob-conformance/tests/export_contact_hessians.rs` (run with `-- --ignored`), which writes M, J and D
   per pose as canonical JSON with the model digest, the seed and the pin under
   `target/pilots/conditioning/`; the Python program reads those files and nothing else from the workspace, so
   the computation that is compared stays outside the product. The program writes a pilot result, never an
   oracle fixture, so the oracle register's rule that a capture script takes no workspace product as input
   (N38a-04) does not apply to it; that check scans `tools/oracle/<oracle>/` capture scripts and not
   `tools/oracle/rerun/`.
2. Scaling: D_s = diag(H)^(−1/2); H_s = D_s·H·D_s has unit diagonal. κ₂ = λ_max ÷ λ_min of the matrix in
   question, from a symmetric eigenvalue solve in double precision.
3. Row 19: three robots, at least 200 contact states each from a fixed seed (201 poses per robot is the
   sample used): the reference humanoid, and the two others of the original survey if they can be identified
   from the ledger, else the reference quadruped and one wheeled base, recorded as substitutes. The humanoid of
   the original survey is described in the planning claim C061 as a G1 of 35.1 kg with 29 joints; the model the
   referee adapter builds is FrankenSim's catalog G1, the reduced profile `g1-reduced-30` (30 links,
   31.96314202 kg, its omitted attachments declared: N33c-41). The re-run uses that model, records it as a
   substitute for the 35.1 kg description, and its result cites the reduction (profile and omitted mass), like
   every result on that profile. u = 2⁻¹¹ (half-precision unit round-off). Report per robot the share of
   states with κ₂(H_s)·u ≥ 1, and the share in which iterative refinement in single precision from a
   half-precision Cholesky factor fails outright. Recorded ranges: 37 to 64%, and 9 to 18%. Also reported, for
   the reader of H-15 and not compared with a recorded value: the same share of κ₂·u ≥ 1 for single precision
   (u = 2⁻²⁴) and double precision (u = 2⁻⁵³), raw and scaled.
   "Fails outright" (DESIGN; the record gives the phrase and not the criterion, and the two drafts of this
   re-run chose differently): the factorization breaks down, or the residual does not fall below its starting
   value in 10 refinement steps. Reason: it is the literal reading of "outright", and it does not depend on a
   convergence tolerance nobody recorded. The stricter count, refinement not converged to single-precision
   working accuracy within 5 steps, is reported beside it as `not_converged_in_5` and is not the figure
   compared with 9 to 18%.
4. Row 20: the humanoid at 201 poses with simplified contact rows. Report the range of κ₂(H) raw and scaled at
   default contact impedance (recorded 3·10⁴ to 1·10⁵ and 2·10³ to 9·10³) and near-rigid (2·10⁶ to 3·10⁶ and
   2·10⁵ to 5·10⁵).
5. Verdict (DESIGN, one rule for both drafts): the status is by containment, as for every row of this bead: a
   re-created range or share inside the recorded range is `reproduced`, anything else `differs`, with the
   model, the source and the sampling rule; `differs` here is a finding about a quantity that was never
   reproducible, not a failed re-run. Each κ₂ result also carries the field `within_factor_10` (true when both
   ends of the re-created range are within a factor of 10 of the recorded range, the refutation condition of
   the unaudited planning claim C061). The status is what the pilot register stores; `within_factor_10` and the
   shares are what N00-35 reads to decide whether H-15 or the scaling requirement must be reviewed.

Determinism: rows 13 and 17 (closed-form parts) are class D0 or printed-digit comparisons; row 14 is seeded
Monte Carlo (same seed, same counts); rows 18 to 20 depend on a dynamics source and are judged at class D3.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilots_rerun_misc.rs` (reads the real outputs; no mocks):
- `count_pairs_is_2145`: Σ over a + b ≤ 64 of 1 equals 2,145.
- `verdict_counts_sum`: 521 + 521 + 0 + 1,103 = 2,145.
- `interval_half_width`: 1 − 0.0125^(1/64) = 0.0662 to four decimals; 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.0457 is not the
  recorded value (guards against the wrong level).
- `instantaneous_multiplier`: with shaft damping 0.002 N·m·s/rad, |a − 200·(1 − a)| with
  a = e^(−0.002 × 0.01 ÷ 0.00107890625) is 2.6917 to four decimals; with the damping left out,
  |1 − 0.4 × 0.01 ÷ 0.00107890625| = 2.7075, asserted under the name `undamped control`.
- `spin_inertia_sum`: 8.7890625·10⁻⁴ + 2·10⁻⁴ = 1.07890625·10⁻³ kg·m².
- `draft_rule_binomial_core`: ¼ · 11/16 = 0.1719 (both held-out briefs, and at least two of the other four,
  each with probability ½).
- `power_sanity_bound`: 2⁻³ = 0.125 and 2⁻⁶ = 0.015625 (the every-brief variant).
- `power_simulation_seeded`: two runs with one seed give identical counts, at 1 and at 8 threads.
- `kappa_of_scaled_identity`: for H = diag(1, 10⁴), κ₂ = 10⁴ raw and 1 after unit-diagonal scaling.
- `half_precision_criterion`: κ = 2,048 gives κ·u = 1 exactly; κ = 2,047 gives less than 1.

`tools/oracle/rerun/test_conditioning.py` (the tests of the conditioning program): `diag_case_scales_to_one`
(H = diag(1, 10⁶): raw 10⁶, scaled 1); `scaling_never_increases_kappa_on_spd_2x2` (for
[[1, 0.9·10³], [0.9·10³, 10⁶]] raw κ₂ ≈ 5.3 × 10⁶ and scaled κ₂ = (1 + 0.9)/(1 − 0.9) = 19);
`half_precision_unit_roundoff_is_2_pow_minus_11`; `share_counts_sum_to_poses`;
`fails_outright_is_not_not_converged` (a matrix whose refinement residual falls but does not reach working
accuracy in 5 steps counts under `not_converged_in_5` and not under the outright failures).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers touched: the fixed-n enumeration of the cart fixture (owned by the evaluation subsystem's rows);
the wheel-loop spectral radii 1.4820 and 0.9207 with the mutant 1.497 (the registry's example control NC-0412,
seed CX-262), with the instantaneous-current figure 2.6917 and its undamped control 2.7075; the power-of-two
scaling of the conditioning certificate. Negative controls: the wrong interval level (first tie at 72); an
explicit current-then-wheel update at 0.5 ms (1.497); a flipped archive byte; a conditioning run with the
scaling skipped, which must report raw ranges in the "scaled" column and so fail the comparison; a wheel-loop
record that prints 2.7075 without the label `undamped control`, which the stale-value scan must flag.
Determinism lanes: rows 13, 14 and 17 twice on one machine and on both platforms give identical records.

## Considerations

- Row 14's original script may not be in the archive; the re-creation is then the record, marked `re-created`.
  The lower bound's construction is this bead's choice in that case and is recorded with the result; no honest
  rule confirms a true ratio of exactly 10 more than about half the time, which the re-run documents and does
  not tune away.
- Rows 19 and 20 need matrices from a dynamics source that the original pilot did not have in this form; the
  label of the source (`development-oracle` or referee) travels with the result. The original robots and poses
  may not be recoverable; the re-run states its sample openly and the comparison is by range, not by pose.
- These are measurements about arithmetic on small models, not about FrankenRobotics' speed; they carry no
  performance claim for the product.
- Row 18's driver has known scorer defects found by an audit (a latched dwell timer, no strike guard); the
  re-run reproduces the recorded behaviour and says so; it is not evidence about the fixture.
- Timing is not asserted anywhere in this bead.
