## Background

FrankenRobotics runs dynamics on several tiers: a trusted CPU referee and faster batch tiers. Whether a fast tier
may be believed is recorded in the *fidelity ledger*: per-quantity disagreement between tiers, measured on a
fixed set of scenarios. That fixed set is the *standard battery*. It is the one battery the Gauntlet (the
verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`) owns; the other named batteries belong to the evaluation
subsystem. Four consumers read it: the fidelity ledger, the divergence ledger (first divergences between tiers),
the tier-correction ledger (a paired estimator that corrects fast-tier means with referee samples) and the
exploitation detectors (which flag policies that profit from simulator artifacts). It is also the fallback of
the covering scene selection when that selection cannot be computed.

Because so much reads it, the battery must not move quietly. A change to the battery is a recorded event with a
new battery identity, and a silently shortened battery changes identity (the scorecard gate GATE-ARENA-07
checks that). The fidelity gate allows exactly one re-baseline at M2, with a written rationale, and then freezes.

DESIGN: the battery is a manifest whose hash is its identity; scenarios are generated from rows and seeds, never
hand-listed. Rejected: a directory of hand-made scenario files (cannot show that nothing was dropped).

## Scope

In: the scenario grid per robot class; the randomization and seeds; the manifest content and identity; the
freeze and change events; the membership checks. Out: the manifest's file format and scorecards (`needs`); the
freeze itself and the fidelity ledger's envelopes (BD-212); wiring into the M2 conformance run (BD-223); the
hard-world set (N38a-21).

## Technical approach

**Rows (about 200 scenarios per robot class, TARGET).**

| Class | Rows |
|---|---|
| Legged (quadruped, biped, humanoid, hexapod) | stand; push recovery (impulses from front, back, left, right); each gait of the class's atlas at three speeds (slow, nominal, fast: 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 times the gait's nominal speed); turning (left, right); stairs up; stairs down; a slope; a low-friction patch; a payload; a drop |
| Wheeled, tracked, wheel-legged | the legged rows with the gait rows replaced by: straight travel (three speeds); turning in place (left, right); a sill; a ramp; a split-grip strip |
| Manipulating bases | not defined at plan time (open question; no fidelity claim for the class until its battery is frozen) |

**Construction (DESIGN).**
1. R = number of rows for the class. The fourteen rows common to every class are: 1 stand, 4 pushes, 2 turns,
   2 stairs (up, down), 2 slopes (along and across the fall line), 1 low-friction patch, 1 payload, 1 drop. A
   legged class with G atlas gaits adds 3·G gait rows: R = 14 + 3·G. A wheeled, tracked or wheel-legged class
   adds 8 rows instead (straight travel at three speeds, turning in place left and right, a sill, a ramp, a
   split-grip strip): R = 22. A row the class cannot attempt (stairs for a base with no stair capability) is
   kept, marked `not applicable`, and still counted in the denominator of a scorecard.
2. D = draws per row = the integer nearest 200 ÷ R, at least 4. A quadruped with G = 4 has R = 26 and D = 8:
   208 scenarios. A wheeled class has R = 22 and D = 9: 198 scenarios.
3. Each scenario is (class, row, draw index d). Its world address is derived from the battery seed, the class,
   the row name and d; all randomness is a function of that address.
4. Randomized parameters per draw, with ranges that are fields of the manifest (defaults, DESIGN): ground
   friction coefficient 0.4 to 1.0; payload 0 to 20% of body mass at a random admissible mount; push impulse
   magnitude in three levels up to the class's recoverable limit as stated by its baseline controller card;
   slope 5° to 20°; stair riser 0.10 to 0.18 m for classes that climb; low-friction patch coefficient 0.1 to
   0.3; drop height 0.05 to 0.30 m; actuator strength scale 0.9 to 1.1; sensor latency within its card's stated
   uncertainty.
5. Controllers: the class's baseline controller, frozen with the battery, so that tier disagreement is not
   confounded with policy change.
6. Horizon: 2 s for stand, push and drop rows; 10 s for travel rows. Recorded quantities per the fidelity ledger:
   base displacement over 2 s, energy per metre, stance impulse.

**Manifest and identity.** The manifest lists: schema version; battery name `standard`; class; the row table;
D; the battery seed; the parameter ranges; the controller identities; the horizon rule; the generator's version.
`BatteryId` = BLAKE3 of the canonical bytes of the manifest. The identity is written to the ledger at freeze.
Every scorecard, fidelity record and detector output names the `BatteryId` it used.

**Events.** `battery.freeze` (once per class, at M2, with the manifest and its identity); `battery.rebaseline`
(at most one for the fidelity gate, with a written rationale); `battery.change` (any later edit: a new identity,
the reason, and the list of consumers whose records are thereby stale). A consumer that reads a record made
under a different `BatteryId` reports `stale-battery` and does not compare.

**Checks (`tests/standard_battery.rs`).** (a) expansion of the manifest yields exactly R × D scenarios with
distinct addresses; (b) removing a row, lowering D or narrowing a range changes `BatteryId`; (c) the scorecard
columns of a run over the battery sum to R × D, including infeasible and `not applicable` scenarios; (d) the
same manifest expands to the same scenarios on both machines; (e) the battery contains no world from the
hard-world set unless listed under `regression` (N38a-21).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A manifest exists for each class that has a product at M2, with about 200 scenarios (within 10%).
- [ ] The five checks pass; a silently shortened battery (one row removed with the name kept) has a different
      identity and is refused by every consumer as `stale-battery`.
- [ ] The freeze event is in the ledger with the identity; a second re-baseline is refused.
- [ ] A class whose scenario list is undecided has no manifest and reports `no fidelity claim: battery not
      frozen`.
- [ ] The battery expands identically from its seed on the Mac and on the Linux host.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `row_count_legged`: G = 4 gives R = 26; D = 8; 208 scenarios. `row_count_wheeled`: R = 22; D = 9; 198.
- `addresses_distinct_and_seeded`: 208 distinct addresses; changing the battery seed changes all of them;
  changing one row's name changes only that row's addresses.
- `identity_changes_on_shortening`: removing the `drop` row changes `BatteryId`; so does D = 7; so does a
  friction range of 0.5 to 1.0.
- `columns_sum_to_size`: on 100 random batteries the scorecard columns sum to R × D with a planted infeasible
  scenario counted in its column and in the denominator.
- `stale_battery_refused`: a fidelity record made under another identity is not compared.
- `second_rebaseline_refused`.
- `expansion_platform_independent`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/standard_battery.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-20 milestone=M2 labels=gauntlet,battery
gates=GATE-ARENA-07 plants=shorten-silently`). It expands the manifest of the reference quadruped, runs
`frob eval run --battery standard --tier batch-cpu --json` on the first eight scenarios (all of them in release
mode), checks the scorecard accounting and the `BatteryId` in the envelope, then plants `shorten-silently` and
requires the identity to change and a consumer to refuse. Log at `target/e2e-logs/standard_battery/<utc>.ndjson`:
commands with exit codes and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the scenario count and the identity as
`metric` and digest lines, the accounting assertions, the negative control and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: R = 14 + 3·G; 26 × 8 = 208; 22 × 9 = 198; columns sum to the battery size. Negative controls: the
silent shortening; a narrowed range; a scenario drawn from an unseeded source (two expansions differ). Metamorphic
relation: permuting the order of rows in the manifest's source changes neither the scenario set nor, after
canonicalization, the identity. Determinism lane: expansion is class D0, identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The source text names an earlier work item as the place where the battery is frozen; the seed bead that
  actually freezes it is BD-212 (the standard battery, frozen, with the fidelity ledger). This bead defines what
  is frozen.
- Open: scenario lists for wheeled, tracked, wheel-legged and manipulating classes; fail-closed default: the
  class has no fidelity claim until its battery is frozen. The wheeled rows above are the design's stated
  replacements and become binding only when that question is closed.
- Risk named in the program's register: agents optimize the scorecard, including by moving failures out of the
  denominator; the identity and the column-sum check are the countermeasure.
- The battery is a floor for fidelity, not a robustness claim; nothing outside it is covered by the ledger.
