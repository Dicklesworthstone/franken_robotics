## Background

FrankenRobotics runs one robot model on several tiers: a slow trusted CPU `referee`, a batched CPU reference
(`batch-cpu`) and GPU tiers. A fast answer is only useful if its distance from a trusted answer is a measured
number, so whether a fast tier may be believed is recorded in the *fidelity ledger*: per robot class and
quantity, the distribution of disagreement between a faster and a slower configuration. A distribution needs a
fixed population to be measured on. That population is the *standard battery*: about 200 scenarios per robot
class, each a task on a scene with randomized parameters under fixed seeds. It is the one battery the Gauntlet
(the verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`) owns; the other named batteries belong to the evaluation
subsystem. Four consumers read it: the fidelity ledger (BD-212), the divergence ledger (first divergences
between tiers, on the device back ends), the tier-correction ledger (BD-264: a paired estimator that corrects
fast-tier means with referee samples) and the exploitation detectors (which flag policies that profit from
simulator artifacts). It is also the fallback of the covering scene selection when that selection cannot be
computed.

Because so much reads it, the battery must not move quietly. If it could change silently, every one of those
ledgers would compare numbers measured on different populations. A change to the battery is a recorded event
with a new battery identity, and a silently shortened battery changes identity (the scorecard gate
GATE-ARENA-07 checks that). The fidelity gate allows exactly one re-baseline at M2, with a written rationale,
and then freezes.

DESIGN: the battery is an immutable, content-addressed manifest whose hash is its identity; scenarios are
generated from rows and seeds, never hand-listed. Rejected: (a) a directory of hand-made scenario or task files
edited in place (it cannot show that nothing was dropped, and nothing then proves that two ledger rows were
measured on the same scenarios); (b) a battery composed of past failures (the hard-world set), because a battery
made of past failures overfits them and is discovery data, never a confirmation sample.

This bead was drafted twice: here, as the Gauntlet's definition of the grid, the identity and the events, and
in the dynamics unit (N16b-01, now superseded by this bead) as the first sub-bead of seed bead BD-212 with a
manifest format, an address scheme and a freeze record. This is the one kept, because the verification chapter
owns the `standard` battery, and it carries the content of both. It defines the scenario lists, the identity
and the freeze of the battery, and it blocks every bead that measures on the battery; BD-212 is the capstone
that freezes the ledger's envelopes on it.

## Scope

In: the scenario grid per robot class; the randomization and seeds; the address scheme; the manifest content
and identity; the freeze record; the freeze, re-baseline and change events; the membership checks; the manifests
of the reference classes.
Out: the manifest file format `frob.battery/1`, the rule that computes `BatteryId`, the membership-event schema
and the loader that resolves a battery name at run time (N24b-01, a dependency: this battery is one manifest in
that format, and this bead defines no second format); the batteries `smoke`, `fixtures`, `full`, `hard` and
`covering` and the scorecard accounting (BD-254); running a battery on two tiers and pairing the rows by address
(N16bp2-01, in `eval.run`); the comparison of a pair and the ledger strata that store it (N16b-02); the freeze
of the fidelity ledger's envelopes (BD-212); wiring into the M2 conformance run (BD-223);
the hard-world set (N38a-21); the decision on the lists of the classes left open (N38b-37).

## Technical approach

**Rows (about 200 scenarios per robot class, TARGET).**

| Class | Rows |
|---|---|
| Legged (quadruped, biped, humanoid, hexapod) | stand; push recovery (impulses from front, back, left, right); each gait of the class's atlas at three speeds (slow, nominal, fast: 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 times the gait's nominal speed); turning (left, right); stairs up; stairs down; a slope; a low-friction patch; a payload; a drop |
| Wheeled, tracked, wheel-legged | the legged rows with the gait rows replaced by: straight travel (three speeds); turning in place (left, right); a sill; a ramp; a split-grip strip |
| Manipulating bases | not defined at plan time (open question; no fidelity claim for the class until its battery is frozen) |

For wheeled classes the sill reuses the geometry of fixture 1 (`fixture:cart-sill-dock`, BD-123) and the
split-grip strip that of fixture 2 (`fixture:quad-split-grip`, BD-202).

**Construction (DESIGN; the two drafts merged here chose different breakdowns, and this is the one kept).**
1. R = number of rows for the class. The fourteen rows common to every class are: 1 stand, 4 pushes, 2 turns,
   2 stairs (up, down), 2 slopes (along and across the fall line), 1 low-friction patch, 1 payload, 1 drop. A
   legged class with G gaits adds 3·G gait rows: R = 14 + 3·G. A wheeled, tracked or wheel-legged class
   adds 8 rows instead (straight travel at three speeds, turning in place left and right, a sill, a ramp, a
   split-grip strip): R = 22. A row the class cannot attempt (stairs for a base with no stair capability) is
   kept, marked `not applicable`, and still counted in the denominator of a scorecard.
2. Which gaits count in G. The gait atlas lands at M4. At M2 the gait rows are the gaits the legged baseline
   controller offers (BD-116: walk and trot, so G = 2). When the atlas lands, its gaits are added by a recorded
   change, which is a new `BatteryId`; rows measured on the M2 battery stay attached to it.
3. D = draws per row = the integer nearest 200 ÷ R, at least 4. A legged class at M2 with G = 2 has R = 20 and
   D = 10: 200 scenarios. A quadruped with G = 4 has R = 26 and D = 8: 208 scenarios. A wheeled class has R = 22
   and D = 9: 198 scenarios.
4. Each scenario is (class, row, draw index d). Its world address is derived from the battery seed, the class,
   the row name and d; all randomness is a function of that address (see Addresses).
5. Randomized parameters per draw, with ranges that are fields of the grid (defaults, DESIGN): ground
   friction coefficient 0.4 to 1.0; payload 0 to 20% of body mass at a random admissible mount; push impulse
   magnitude in three levels up to the class's recoverable limit as stated by its baseline controller card,
   with the push time drawn; turn yaw rate at two levels; slope 5° to 20°, with the along-the-fall-line row
   drawn uphill or downhill; stair riser 0.10 to 0.18 m for classes that climb; low-friction patch coefficient
   0.1 to 0.3; drop height 0.05 to 0.30 m; actuator strength scale 0.9 to 1.1; sensor latency within its
   card's stated uncertainty; card-uncertainty parameters within their cards' intervals.
6. Controllers: the class's baseline controller, frozen with the battery, so that tier disagreement is not
   confounded with policy change.
7. Horizon: 2 s for stand, push and drop rows; 10 s for travel rows. Recorded quantities per the fidelity ledger:
   base displacement over 2 s, energy per metre, stance impulse.

The other draft's breakdown, recorded so that the choice can be revisited: 38 base scenarios (stand 1; push
recovery in 4 directions at 2 impulse sizes, 8; walk and trot at three speeds, 6; turning in 2 directions at 2
yaw rates, 4; stairs up and down at 2 riser heights, 4; slope up, down and across at 2 angles, 6; a low-friction
patch at 3 levels, 3; a payload in 3 mass and offset cases, 3; a drop from 3 heights, 3) with 5 randomization
replicates each, 190 scenarios. It fixes the variants by hand where the kept construction draws them from
ranges; the kept construction was preferred because one rule (R rows, D draws, ranges in the grid) covers every
class, including those OPEN-GAUNT-7 has yet to list, and its variants (two impulse sizes, two yaw rates, two
riser heights, three slope directions, three friction levels, three payloads, three drop heights) are all
inside the ranges and levels of step 5. The plan fixes only the kinds and the TARGET of about 200.

**Grid, manifest and identity.** Two files per class, the second generated from the first (DESIGN).
- The grid, `registries/batteries/standard.<robot-class>.grid.toml`: schema version; battery name `standard`;
  class; the row table (per row: key, kind, the task as a `TaskId` of an `.ftask`, the scene, the command
  profile, the horizon, the registered stream names of its randomization, the metric names); D; the battery
  seed; the parameter ranges; the controller identities; the horizon rule; the generator's version. Its digest
  (BLAKE3 of its canonical bytes) is the grid digest.
- The manifest, `registries/batteries/standard.<robot-class>.toml`, in the format `frob.battery/1` of N24b-01
  with `name = "standard"`, `lane = "discovery"` and `selection = "listed"`: one item per scenario (task, scene
  and world address, disturbance law, family of initial conditions), written by the generator from the grid and
  never by hand. `BatteryId` is the identity that format defines: the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical JSON of the
  manifest with its items sorted by their own content hash. Reordering the file therefore does not change the
  identity, and adding, removing or editing a scenario does; removing a row, lowering D or narrowing a range
  changes the generated items and so changes `BatteryId`.
The two drafts merged here had each defined their own file and hash (a JSON document per class under
`registries/batteries/standard/`, and a hash of the grid alone); both are replaced by the one manifest format
the evaluation subsystem already defines for every battery, so that `eval.run --battery standard` loads this
battery like any other. A check regenerates the manifest from the grid and requires equal items and an equal
`BatteryId`. The identity is written to the ledger at freeze. Every scorecard, fidelity record and detector
output names the `BatteryId` it used.

**Addresses.** A scenario's world address, in the triple form (run seed, world, episode) of the manifest format,
has the battery seed as its run seed and a world index that the generator assigns from (class, row name, d);
the episode is 0. It is not derived from the `BatteryId` (DESIGN; the other draft keyed addresses by
(`BatteryId`, scenario, replicate)): an address keyed by the identity would move every world whenever one row
changed, and the unchanged rows of a revised battery could then not be compared with the old ones. Changing the
battery seed changes every address; changing one row's name changes only that row's addresses. Every random
draw of a scenario (terrain, friction, payload offset, push direction and time, card-uncertainty parameters)
comes from a counter-based stream keyed by that address and a registered stream name (BD-200), so the same
scenario is the same world on every tier and every machine.

**Freeze.** Before any pair runs, a ledger row `battery.freeze { id, class, version, grid_digest, frozen_at,
commit, scenario_count }` is written, once per class, at M2. After the row exists the files are read-only by
convention and verified by hash on every load: a manifest whose hash differs from the frozen `BatteryId` of
that version is refused with both hashes, not silently adopted (this is the loader's rule that a manifest
without an event chain back to a registered identity does not run).

**Events.** `battery.freeze` (above). `battery.rebaseline` (at most one for the fidelity gate, with a written
rationale; a second is refused). Any later edit is a change: a new version of the grid and the manifest, and a
membership event in the schema of N24b-01 (`frob.battery.event/1`: the old and the new `BatteryId`, the items
added and removed, a required reason, the actor), which this bead reports together with the list of consumers
whose records are thereby stale. (The two drafts called this event `battery.revise` and `battery.change`; there
is one event, in the format's schema.) No code path edits a frozen version in place. Ledger strata are keyed by
battery version, so rows measured on the old battery stay attached to it and envelopes frozen on it do not
transfer. A consumer that reads a record made under a different `BatteryId` reports `stale-battery` and does
not compare.

**Classes.** At M2 the legged reference classes (quadruped, workload Q; humanoid, workload H) are frozen by
this bead. OPEN-GAUNT-7 (owner: Arena with Kinesis) leaves the lists for wheeled, tracked, wheel-legged and
manipulating classes open. The grid and manifest of the wheeled base are generated here from the stated
replacement rows, and they are frozen when that question is closed (N38b-37, also at M2); the wheeled rows
become binding only then. (The other draft froze the wheeled base at M2 outright and listed only tracked,
wheel-legged and manipulating classes as open; the open question as registered includes the wheeled class.)
Fail-closed default: a class without a frozen battery has no manifest in force and no fidelity claim, its
strata do not exist, `fidelity.ledger` reports `no fidelity claim: battery not frozen`, and promotion in it is
refused with `FROB-REF-821`.

**Checks (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/standard_battery.rs`).** (a) expansion of the grid yields exactly
R × D scenarios with distinct addresses; (b) removing a row, lowering D or narrowing a range changes
`BatteryId`; (c) the scorecard columns of a run over the battery sum to R × D, including infeasible and
`not applicable` scenarios; (d) the same grid expands to the same scenarios on both machines; (e) every item of
the manifest is one the grid expands to, so the battery holds no hand-picked or search-selected world: an item
the grid does not generate fails the regeneration check with the item named. (The hard-world set itself, the
refusal of a battery whose only content is hard worlds and the `regression` section in which a battery may hold
them arrive at M3 with BD-275 and N38a-21, after this bead; an earlier draft tested that refusal here, at M2,
before the set existed.)

**Evidence.** A battery is an input, not a claim. Results measured on it carry its `BatteryId` in their
identities. Determinism: the grid, the manifest and the address scheme are exact (class D0).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/batteries/standard.{quadruped,humanoid}.toml` and their grids exist, validate against
      `frob.battery/1`, are frozen, and each holds about 200 scenarios (within 10%: between 180 and 220); the
      wheeled-base grid and manifest exist and are frozen when OPEN-GAUNT-7 is closed.
- [ ] The five checks pass; a silently shortened battery (one row removed with the name kept) has a different
      identity and is refused by every consumer as `stale-battery`.
- [ ] `BatteryId` is stable under file reordering and whitespace, and changes under any added, removed or edited
      scenario (tested); regenerating the manifest from its grid reproduces it.
- [ ] The freeze event is in the ledger with the identity before the first paired run; a load of a manifest
      whose hash differs from the frozen identity is refused; a second re-baseline is refused.
- [ ] A change writes one membership event with both identities; no code path edits a frozen version in place.
- [ ] Every scenario of the frozen batteries runs to completion or to a typed outcome on `batch-cpu`, and its
      draws are identical across two runs and across worker counts (digest equality).
- [ ] A class whose scenario list is undecided has no manifest in force: resolving `standard` for it returns the
      fail-closed answer `battery not frozen`. The two consequences stated above, the line `no fidelity claim:
      battery not frozen` of `fidelity.ledger` and the refusal `FROB-REF-821` of `eval.promote`, are asserted by
      the beads that deliver those verbs (BD-212; BD-263 at M3), which come after this one.
- [ ] The battery expands identically from its seed on the Mac and on the Linux host.
- [ ] `frob-conformance/CONTRACT.md` lists the grid, the identity rule it relies on, the events and the
      fail-closed default; the contract of `frob-arena` names `standard` among the batteries its loader resolves.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/standard_battery.rs`:
- `row_count_legged`: G = 2 gives R = 20, D = 10, 200 scenarios; G = 4 gives R = 26, D = 8, 208 scenarios.
  `row_count_wheeled`: R = 22; D = 9; 198.
- `addresses_distinct_and_seeded`: distinct addresses for every scenario; changing the battery seed changes all
  of them; changing one row's name changes only that row's addresses.
- `addresses_are_stable`: scenario 17, draw 3 draws the same friction, payload offset and push on two runs and
  under 1, 4 and 24 worker threads.
- `battery_id_ignores_order`: two manifests with the same scenarios in different order hash equal.
- `battery_id_sees_every_edit`: for each scenario of the quadruped battery, deleting it, or changing its horizon
  by 1 ms, changes the identity (two cases per scenario, all different from the base).
- `identity_changes_on_shortening`: removing the `drop` row changes `BatteryId`; so does lowering D by one; so
  does a friction range of 0.5 to 1.0.
- `manifest_regenerates_from_grid`.
- `columns_sum_to_size`: on 100 random batteries the scorecard columns sum to R × D with a planted infeasible
  scenario counted in its column and in the denominator.
- `frozen_file_tamper_refused`: write the freeze row, flip one byte of the manifest, load: refusal with both
  hashes.
- `change_records_event`: a change yields exactly one membership event naming the old and the new identity.
- `stale_battery_refused`: a fidelity record made under another identity is not compared.
- `second_rebaseline_refused`.
- `class_without_battery_has_no_claim`: `tracked` returns the fail-closed answer.
- `hand_added_item_refused`: a manifest with one extra item that the grid does not generate fails regeneration,
  naming the item; the same item removed again restores the frozen identity.
- `expansion_platform_independent`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/standard_battery.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-20 milestone=M2 labels=gauntlet,battery
gates=GATE-ARENA-07 plants=shorten-silently`). It expands the grid of the reference quadruped, runs
`frob eval run quad-a --battery standard --tier batch-cpu --key sb-<run> --json` (the registry form of
`eval.run`, with the reference quadruped design `quad-a` and an idempotency key) over the whole battery (no
option runs part of a battery, and a shortened battery would be another `BatteryId`), checks the scorecard
accounting (the outcome columns sum to the battery's size) and the `BatteryId` in the envelope against the
frozen one, then plants `shorten-silently` and
requires the identity to change and a consumer to refuse. Log at `target/e2e-logs/standard_battery/<utc>.ndjson`:
commands with exit codes and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, the scenario count against the bounds
180 and 220 and the identity as `metric` and digest lines, the accounting assertions, the negative control, on
failure the first differing scenario key, and the `summary`. The first steps of `scripts/e2e/fidelity_ledger.sh`
(bead N16b-30) also list the battery, print its identity and scenario count, tamper with a copy and assert the
refusal.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: R = 14 + 3·G; 20 × 10 = 200; 26 × 8 = 208; 22 × 9 = 198; columns sum to the battery size.
Negative controls: the silent shortening (a battery with one scenario removed and no event: a ledger write
under the old identity must be refused; registered against GATE-FID-01 and GATE-ARENA-07); a narrowed range; a
scenario drawn from an unseeded source (two expansions differ); a hand-added item the grid does not generate
(refused by regeneration; the battery made only of hard worlds is N38a-21's control, at M3). Metamorphic relation: permuting the order of rows in the grid changes neither the
scenario set nor, after canonicalization, the identity. Determinism lanes: expansion is class D0, identical
twice on one machine, across worker counts, in debug and release, and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The source text names an earlier work item as the place where the battery is frozen, a bead number that
  belongs to another analysis; the seed bead that freezes the ledger on it is BD-212 (the standard battery,
  frozen, with the fidelity ledger), and this bead is its first part: it defines what is frozen and writes the
  battery's own freeze row.
- The grid's counts are a DESIGN default written here; the freeze commit is the place where the numbers become
  binding.
- Open: scenario lists for wheeled, tracked, wheel-legged and manipulating classes; fail-closed default: the
  class has no fidelity claim until its battery is frozen.
- Battery size drives referee cost. With the horizons of step 7, one pass over a legged class at M2 simulates
  D × (6 rows × 2 s + 14 rows × 10 s) = 10 × 152 s = 1,520 s; at a referee speed of 0.2 to 1.0 times real time
  per core that is 0.42 to 2.1 core-hours per class per pass (TARGET), small against the M2 budget.
- Risk named in the program's register (RISK-15): agents optimize the scorecard, including by moving failures
  out of the denominator; the identity and the column-sum check are the countermeasure, and a numerical fault
  stays a row of the battery and is never dropped.
- Revisit if the baseline controllers cannot complete a kind on a class: that kind is kept and reported as a
  typed failure, never deleted to make the battery pass.
- The battery is a floor for fidelity, not a robustness claim; nothing outside it is covered by the ledger.
