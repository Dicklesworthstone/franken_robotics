## Background

Nemesis, the adversarial-world subsystem of FrankenRobotics, searches for worlds that break a design, shrinks
each failure to a minimal counterexample world, and keeps, for each task family and robot class, a versioned
list of those worlds: the *hard-world set* (schema `frob.hardset/1`, a list of counterexample-world identities
with the failure mode each exposes). The list is valuable and dangerous. It is valuable for regression: a design
that once failed a hard world should be re-run on it after a repair. It is dangerous because it is a sample
selected for failure: any statistic computed on it as if it were a nominal sample is biased, and a test suite
made only of past failures overfits them.

The Gauntlet (the verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`) therefore reads the set under three
constraints, each enforced by a refusal that the adversarial subsystem's registry (N26-REF) already holds (two
coded, `FROB-REF-567` and `FROB-REF-560`; one a registered slug awaiting its number), and labels what it produces.

Terms. *Discovery data*: anything found by search; never used to confirm. *Confirmation sample*: sealed worlds
drawn fresh for the qualification lane. *Nominal draw*: an episode drawn from the declared world distribution,
the only kind a rare-event bound may count (or draws with recorded bounded weights). *Witness*: evidence that a
world is fair: `solvable-causal` (a causal controller exists that succeeds) or `plant-feasible` (the plant can
physically do it). *Promotion*: re-evaluation of a fast-tier result on the referee.

## Scope

In: the Gauntlet's reader of the hard-world set; the three constraints; the admission check; the regression
lane and its label. Out: producing, shrinking and versioning the set (BD-275); the `hard` battery as a named
battery of the evaluation subsystem; the covering selection; the M3 wiring of batteries (BD-318).

## Technical approach

**Who raises what.** Each refusal is raised where the use happens, by its owner: `FROB-REF-567` at the
qualification lane's commit and at citation (BD-259, BD-275), `FROB-REF-560` by the bound's lane firewall
(N26a-23), `hard-world-set-inadmissible` at battery definition (BD-275). This bead adds no second check and no
second mark: its reader carries the discovery mark those checks read, and its tests assert each refusal from
the Gauntlet's side. (An earlier draft gave the reader a tag of its own, `origin: hardset`, beside BD-275's mark;
there is one mark.)

**Constraint 1. Discovery data, never a confirmation sample.** A request that offers a hard-world or
search-selected world as a confirmation sample is refused with `FROB-REF-567`. Every world the Gauntlet's reader
returns carries BD-275's discovery mark (`discovery: hard-set`, answered by `frob_nemesis::is_discovery_world`),
held as a condition flag that joins by union through the claim product, so a derived artifact cannot drop it.
Repair offered by the refusal: draw sealed clusters through the qualification lane.

**Constraint 2. Never the nominal draw of a rare-event bound.** A bound that is offered episodes not drawn
nominally, or without recorded bounded weights, is refused with `FROB-REF-560`. Hard worlds have no nominal
inclusion probability, so they can contribute to exploration and to refutation by example, never to the count
of a zero-failure rule p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) or of a confidence sequence.

**Constraint 3. Never the only content of a battery.** A battery manifest whose scenarios all carry the
discovery mark is rejected at definition with the uncoded refusal `hard-world-set-inadmissible` (`3xx`,
exit 3, `pointer` naming the battery and the failed check; registered by N26-REF and requested in the allocation
ledger; until a number is assigned it is raised in the ledger's interim form, never as a generic error, and the
number is read from the ledger once assigned). A battery may contain hard worlds
only in a section named `regression`, and its scorecard reports that section separately; the headline pass rate
excludes it.

**Admission (checked on read).** A world is read from the set only if its record carries a witness of kind
`solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible` and a promotion record on the referee. A world lacking either is skipped
and counted under `inadmissible`, with the reason; the count is printed.

**Regression lane.** `tests/hardset_regression.rs` and, from the user's side, `frob nemesis hardset --action run`:
for a design and the set of its class, replay each admitted world and report pass or fail per world. Each result
is labelled `regression` in its evidence, with the permitted wording "regression: ⟨design⟩ passes ⟨k⟩ of ⟨n⟩ hard
worlds of set ⟨version⟩". The result is never worded as robustness and never enters a design-comparison verdict,
a qualification request or a failure-rate bound.

**Why the label matters (smallest case).** A design fails 3 of 1,000 nominal worlds; the three are shrunk and
stored; a repair fixes exactly those three. The regression lane reports 3 of 3 passing. The nominal failure rate
after the repair is unknown: it may still be 3 in 1,000 through worlds the search never visited. Only fresh
nominal draws can say.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Offering a hard world to the qualification lane is refused with `FROB-REF-567`, exit code 3.
- [ ] Offering hard-world episodes to a bound is refused with `FROB-REF-560`, exit code 3.
- [ ] A battery made only of hard worlds is rejected at definition with `hard-world-set-inadmissible` (exit 3);
      one with a `regression` section is accepted and its headline excludes that section.
- [ ] A world with no witness, or no referee promotion, is not read and is counted as inadmissible.
- [ ] Every regression result carries the label `regression` and the permitted wording; a planted regression
      result submitted as a design-comparison input is refused.
- [ ] The discovery mark survives derivation (a summary of a replay of a hard world carries it), and no tag
      other than BD-275's mark is written.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/hardset_reader.rs`:
- `confirmation_refused_567`, `bound_refused_560`: the refusal envelopes carry the code, a repair and
  `retry: after-change`.
- `battery_only_hardset_rejected`: a manifest of 12 hard worlds is rejected with `hard-world-set-inadmissible`
  (exit 3, pointer to the battery); 200 nominal scenarios plus a `regression` section of 12 is accepted with
  headline denominator 200.
- `admission_requires_witness_and_promotion`: of four planted records (both present; no witness; witness
  `unknown`; no promotion) exactly one is read and three are counted inadmissible.
- `discovery_mark_propagates`: a summary of the replay of a hard world, and a summary of that summary, both
  answer true through `is_discovery_world`.
- `regression_wording`: the rendered sentence matches the permitted form and does not contain "robust".
- `zero_failure_rule_excludes_hard_worlds`: with n = 29,956 nominal failure-free episodes and δ = 0.05 the bound
  is 10⁻⁴; adding 50 hard-world passes leaves n at 29,956.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/hardset_rules.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-21 milestone=M3 labels=gauntlet,nemesis
gates=GATE-GAUNT-01 plants=hardset-as-confirmation`). With a small registered hard-world set of the reference
quadruped it runs `frob nemesis hardset --class quadruped --task <t> --json` (the default action `show`, which
takes no `--action`: the set and its version) and `frob nemesis hardset --action run quad-a --class quadruped
--task <t> --key hs-<run> --json` (an action is selected only with `--action <name>`, NJL-01 action rule 1; the
forms the set's own script uses, N15a-28), then issues the three forbidden requests with
`e2e_expect_refusal` (567, 560) and the battery definition, then plants `hardset-as-confirmation` (the discovery
mark stripped from a world in a scratch ledger) and requires the audit to catch it. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/hardset_rules/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit codes and wall time, the evidence block with
the `regression` label, the three `refusal` lines with code, message and repairs, counts of admitted and
inadmissible worlds as `metric` lines, the negative control and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: ⌈ln 0.05 ÷ ln(1 − 10⁻⁴)⌉ = 29,956 failure-free nominal episodes for a bound of 10⁻⁴ at δ = 0.05;
the three-of-three case above. Negative controls: the stripped tag; a battery of hard worlds only; a world with
a fabricated witness kind. Determinism lane: a stored counterexample world replays to its state hash bit for bit
on the tier that found it (the relation the adversarial subsystem registers); the reader's decisions are class
D0.

## Considerations

- The set is versioned; a regression result names the version, and a world leaves the set only by a recorded
  decision.
- Privacy: a hard world built on a private scene inherits the `private` mark; its identity may be logged, its
  content may not.
- If the adversarial search is killed at its gate, the set is filled by card randomization with shrinking; the
  three constraints are unchanged.
