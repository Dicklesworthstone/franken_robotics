## Background

Tier G4 of the Gauntlet (the verification subsystem of FrankenRobotics) injects faults and requires each case to
end in its typed refusal or in a state the invariants accept, with no leaked resource. This bead covers the four
fault families that live on the kernel side of the system, where batches of worlds are stepped on a CPU batch
tier or on a GPU: cancellation, device faults, faults inside a transactional step, and capacity overflow. The
mechanisms belong to the GPU-backend and dynamics subsystems; the Gauntlet owns the schedules, the oracles and
the count.

Terms. *Kernel batch*: one launch of a kernel over all worlds of a batch. *Committed slab*: the last state of a
chunk of ticks that has been written and acknowledged. *Kernel cache*: compiled kernel binaries stored on disk
by hash. *Arena lease*: a time-limited grant of device memory. *Strict-backend mismatch*: the device a job was
scored for is not the device it would run on. *Transactional step*: the referee's step, which runs checks, then
commits; a retry must equal a clean run. *`commit_epoch`*: the counter that names which commit a read saw.
*Capacity*: the fixed maximum of contact rows, contact islands, packet-queue slots or bucket sizes in a batch.

## Scope

In: schedules, injections and oracles for the four families; their counts; their regressions. Out: the
framework (N38a-23); the protocol and daemon families (N38a-25); malformed input and the firewall (N38a-26).

## Technical approach

| Family | Injection | Oracle |
|---|---|---|
| Cancellation | At every kernel-batch boundary under a deterministic schedule; at every await point of the publish and submit paths | The receipt reports the last committed tick per world; a dropped handle is not a cancellation receipt |
| Device faults | Device loss mid-step; a kernel cache entry with one flipped byte; an arena lease revoked; a strict-backend mismatch before scoring; out-of-memory at admission | A chunk replayed from the committed slab gives the unfaulted digests; the flipped entry is refused |
| Transaction faults | 10⁵ faults between the checks and the commit of a transactional step (TARGET) | Zero uncommitted reads: every read names a `commit_epoch` |
| Capacity | Contact rows, islands, packet queues and buckets forced past capacity | The typed refusal or the `capacity-biased` label, never a silent truncation |

**Cancellation (procedure).**
1. Enumerate the kernel-batch boundaries of the step plan for the workload (stage by stage) and the await points
   of the publish and submit paths.
2. For each boundary b, run the workload, request cancellation exactly at b, and let the system drain and
   finalize.
3. Assert: the caller receives a cancellation receipt naming, per world, the last committed tick; replaying each
   world to that tick from its address gives the receipt's digest; the resource audit is clean; a client that
   merely drops its handle (no cancel request) produces no cancellation receipt and the job continues or is
   reaped by its lease, as its contract states.
4. Count: every boundary and every await point is hit at least once per release.

**Device faults (procedure).** For each of the five injections: (a) device loss mid-step: the backend reports
the loss with its `6xx` device refusal (exit code 6), the job restarts from the committed slab on a surviving
device or the CPU reference path, and the replayed chunk's digests equal those of an unfaulted run; (b) flipped
cache byte: the cache entry's hash check fails, the entry is refused and rebuilt, and no kernel from the corrupt
entry is launched; (c) lease revoked: the step stops at the next boundary, releases its buffers and reports the
last committed tick; (d) strict-backend mismatch before scoring: the job is refused before any world is stepped;
(e) out-of-memory at admission: the memory estimator's refusal (a budget refusal, exit code 4) is returned and
nothing partial is published.

**Transaction faults (procedure).** Inject a fault (panic of a worker, forced retry, a rejected sub-interval, a
simulated power cut of the process) at a seeded instant between the checks and the commit of a transactional
step, 10⁵ times across random worlds and steps. After each: every value any reader observed carries a
`commit_epoch` equal to a completed commit (zero uncommitted reads); the retried step's digest equals the clean
step's digest; the noise drawn for the step is the same draw (no redraw on rejection).

**Capacity (procedure).** Build scenes and batches that exceed each capacity by one and by a large factor: more
simultaneous contact rows than the solve admits (the referee's cap is 21 contacts in one solve); more islands;
more queued packets than a queue holds; a design whose size exceeds its bucket. Each must end in the named
refusal for that capacity, or in a result that carries the label `capacity-biased` produced by a documented
patch reduction or a larger admitted solve. A result with fewer contacts than the scene has and no label is a
violation (silent truncation).

**Sites and counts.** Cancellation and device faults on `lockstep`, `cuda` and `metal`; transaction faults on
`referee` and `strict`; capacity on `strict`, `lockstep`, `cuda`, `metal`. Per release: every boundary; each
device injection 100 times at seeded instants; 10⁵ transaction faults; each capacity at +1 and ×4.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Cancellation at every kernel-batch boundary and every await point ends with a receipt that names the last
      committed tick per world, verified by replay, with a clean audit.
- [ ] A dropped handle yields no cancellation receipt.
- [ ] Each of the five device injections ends in its refusal or in bit-equal replayed digests; the flipped cache
      entry is never executed.
- [ ] 10⁵ transaction faults give zero uncommitted reads and retry digests equal to clean digests.
- [ ] Every forced overflow ends in its typed refusal or carries `capacity-biased`; no silent truncation.
- [ ] Each family has at least one caught negative control (below) and its violations, if any, are minimized
      regressions; the six controls are records of `registry/controls.toml` naming GATE-GAUNT-06 (five seeded
      `GATE-GAUNT-06` by this bead, one MR-09's), each planted through its `nc-<nnnn>` feature.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/chaos_kernel.rs` (real `batch-cpu` tier; device entries skip where no device):
- `cancel_at_each_boundary_batch_cpu`: for the built-in reference model, 8 worlds, 50 ticks: one run per
  boundary; each receipt's per-world tick replays to its digest.
- `dropped_handle_is_not_cancellation`.
- `cache_entry_flipped_byte_refused`: flipping byte 100 of a cache entry causes a rebuild and a logged refusal.
- `oom_at_admission_refused`: a request for 2²⁰ worlds of the humanoid on a 24 GB card is refused at admission
  with exit code 4 and no partial output.
- `tx_fault_no_uncommitted_read`: 1,000 seeded faults in the unit run (10⁵ in the tier run).
- `tx_retry_equals_clean`: forced-retry digest equals the clean digest.
- `contact_overflow_refused_or_labelled`: 22 simultaneous contacts on the referee end in the capacity refusal;
  the fast tier's patch reduction prints `capacity-biased`.
- `queue_overflow_typed`: a packet queue of capacity 8 offered 9 packets refuses or drops by its declared policy
  with the drop counted in telemetry.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chaos_kernel.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-24 milestone=M2 labels=gauntlet,chaos,gate
gates=GATE-GAUNT-06 needs=cpu plants=abandon-batch,silent-truncation`). It is the M2 measurement of the gate's
kernel part and is later called by `scripts/e2e/chaos_g4.sh` (N38a-27) under the family filter `kernel`. Steps
(every `sim.run` and `sim.replay` call carries its own `--key ck-<step>-<site>[-<world>]`, so no call returns
another's stored receipt):
start `frob sim run` on `batch-cpu` (and on each device present), cancel at a seeded tick with
`frob job cancel`, check the receipt's per-world ticks by `frob sim replay`; corrupt one cache byte in a scratch
cache; request an inadmissible batch; run the tier target for transaction faults and capacity; submit one
over-capacity scene from the surface. Log at `target/e2e-logs/chaos_kernel/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit
codes and wall time, evidence blocks, per family and site `metric` lines for schedules run, violations
(`== 0`), boundaries hit against boundaries total, and audit counters before and after; `refusal` lines with
code, exit code and repairs; digests of replayed ticks; the two negative controls; the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: zero uncommitted reads in 10⁵ faults; forced-retry digest equal to the clean digest; the
referee's contact cap of 21. Negative controls (each must be caught): a build in which cancellation abandons the
batch without committing (the receipt's tick does not replay); a cancellation path that skips releasing its
device buffers (the resource audit reports the leased buffer; the first control the gate's table states); a
build that executes a cache entry without checking its hash; a transactional step that publishes before its
commit fence (an uncommitted read appears); redraw on rejection (the share of positive accepted draws becomes
¾); a contact list truncated silently at capacity. Each is a `mutation` record of `registry/controls.toml`
planted by the suite's one mechanism (N38b-04: a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch in the owning crate, built
with `--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/nc-<nnnn>`), so the control runner runs it whenever GATE-GAUNT-06
runs. This bead registers five of them with `seed = "GATE-GAUNT-06"` (the gate's own stated failures; no
catalogue entry states them). Redraw on rejection is MR-09's registered mutation (N38a-16), reused through its
feature, whose record names GATE-GAUNT-06 beside GATE-GAUNT-03: one branch, not a second plant. Determinism
lanes: schedules replay identically; replayed chunk digests are compared as bits (class D2 on one device
family).

## Considerations

- Device loss cannot be produced on demand on every platform; where it cannot, the backend's fault hook
  simulates the driver's error at the same call site, and the entry is labelled `simulated`.
- Counts on real devices are bounded by the default machine envelope; the release run names its budget.
- The capacity rule exists because removing contacts silently biases results toward designs with many contacts;
  three responses are allowed (patch reduction with its qualification fixture, a larger admitted solve, a
  refusal) and removal is not one of them.
