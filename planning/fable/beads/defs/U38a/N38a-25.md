## Background

Tier G4 of the Gauntlet (the verification subsystem of FrankenRobotics) injects faults and requires each case to
end in its typed refusal or in a state the invariants accept, with no leaked resource. This bead covers the
four families that concern the long-running parts of the system: the job protocol (how a request becomes one
job with one budget charge and one receipt), the chunk protocol (how telemetry extents are committed to the
ledger), the daemon and ledger as concurrent programs, and the worker-process fallback that runs the sibling
simulation library in a separate process.

These are concurrent code whose failures are rare interleavings. They are tested by deterministic simulation,
not by waiting: the sibling runtime library asupersync has a lab runtime with virtual time, seeded scheduling,
fault injection, lineage-driven fault selection, recorded replay and minimization of failing schedules (FACT:
its `src/lab/` contains `chaos.rs`, `injection.rs`, `ldfi.rs`, `replay.rs`, `replay_minimization.rs`,
`schedule_minimizer.rs`, `dual_run.rs` and `explorer.rs`). The device is replaced by a simulated device whose
episode outcomes are a pure function of the world address, so a whole campaign simulates in milliseconds. That
stand-in is the one sanctioned exception to the rule against fakes, and it is kept honest by dual runs.

## Scope

In: schedules, injections and oracles for the four families; coverage of await points; dual runs. Out: the
framework (N38a-23); the lab-runtime wiring of the ledger and submit path at M1 (BD-096), which this bead
extends to the full daemon; kernel-side families (N38a-24).

## Technical approach

| Family | Injection | Oracle |
|---|---|---|
| Job protocol | A crash after the idempotency commit and before the receipt; duplicate and reordered delivery; a client that disconnects with a job running and reconnects by submission key; two requests with one key interleaved | One job, one charge, the original receipt |
| Chunk protocol | A torn ledger write; a crash after commit; lease expiry and takeover by a second writer; overlapping tick ranges after a shard split; a reset that reuses tick zero; a full disk; a clock jump | One row and one receipt per extent; different bytes for a committed extent are `FROB-REF-810`; the frontier is contiguous |
| Daemon and ledger | Deterministic simulation with virtual time, seeded scheduling, lineage-driven fault selection and schedule minimization; a simulated device whose outcomes are a pure function of the world address | The invariants below; one job one charge; dual runs against `batch-cpu` keep the simulated device honest |
| Worker fallback | The FrankenSim worker process killed mid-job and the job resubmitted | Bit-equal chunk digests |

**Invariants used as oracles (statements).**
- INV-CHRON-01: a committed chunk key maps to one content hash for the life of the run.
- INV-CHRON-02: for every (run, world, episode, stream) the committed chunks are exactly the indices 0 to the
  frontier.
- INV-CHRON-03: one idempotency key yields one job, one budget charge and one receipt, under any crash or retry.
- INV-CHRON-04: a paginated query returns every row visible at its pin exactly once.
- INV-CHRON-12: collection never removes a pinned object, and every removal leaves a tombstone.
- INV-ATEL-02: a study's statistic is a pure function of the per-address outcomes taken in address order; it
  does not depend on completion order, preemption, co-tenancy, shard count or memo hits.
- INV-ATEL-06: a decision core's action is a pure function of its table version, the ledger state at a pinned
  frontier and addresses; it reads no clock.
- One job and one budget charge per (principal, verb, idempotency key), under any interleaving and crash point.

**Procedure for the simulated families.**
1. Build the daemon's job state machine, idempotency binding, chunk publisher, pagination, lease manager, memo
   store and dispatcher against the lab runtime, with the simulated device.
2. Fault menu: a crash at every await point of the publish and submit paths; a torn ledger write; duplicate and
   reordered delivery; delay; lease expiry and takeover by a second writer; device loss; a full disk; a clock
   jump; cancellation at every boundary; a client that disconnects with a job running and reconnects by
   submission key.
3. Selection: from the lineage of a successful run, try first the faults that could have broken its outcome;
   then seeded random faults.
4. After each schedule, check every invariant on the final ledger, then the resource audit.
5. A caller that received no receipt (transport failure, exit code 9) never resubmits blindly: the scripted
   client reconnects and looks the job up by its idempotency key; exactly one job and one charge exist, or none.
6. Different bytes offered for an already committed extent are refused with `FROB-REF-810` (chunk conflict;
   an integrity fault, exit code 9), and the committed bytes are unchanged.
7. Count: 10⁵ seeded schedules per release (TARGET); every await point on the publish and submit paths hit by a
   crash fault at least once.
8. A violating run is reduced to a seed, a fault list and a schedule of minimal length, stored as a regression
   that replays in under one second.

**Keeping the simulated device honest (dual runs).** A fixed set of small jobs (8 worlds, 20 ticks, the built-in
reference model) is executed against the simulated device and against the real `batch-cpu` tier; the two ledger
states must be equal in every protocol-visible respect (job states, chunk keys and counts, frontier, charges,
receipts' structure), so the stand-in cannot drift into testing a protocol the real workers do not follow.

**Worker fallback.** Run a job through the worker process, kill the worker with a signal at a seeded instant
mid-job, resubmit by the same key; the chunk digests equal those of the same job run in process and those of an
unkilled worker run, bit for bit; no orphan worker process remains.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every job-protocol injection ends with one job, one charge and the original receipt.
- [ ] Every chunk-protocol injection ends with one row and one receipt per extent and a contiguous frontier;
      conflicting bytes are refused with `FROB-REF-810`.
- [ ] 10⁵ simulated schedules per release with zero invariant violations; every publish and submit await point
      is hit by a crash at least once; the count actually run is printed.
- [ ] Dual runs agree on every protocol-visible field for every job of the fixed set.
- [ ] The killed-and-resubmitted worker job gives bit-equal chunk digests and leaves no orphan process.
- [ ] Each family has a caught negative control; each violation found is a minimized regression under one
      second; the five controls are existing records of `registry/controls.toml` naming GATE-GAUNT-06 (three of
      BD-096's, the two-statement binding being also MR-36's; MR-36's offset pagination; MR-37's), each planted
      through the feature its record writes; this bead registers none.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/chaos_protocols.rs` (lab runtime; entries labelled `simulated`):
- `crash_between_idempotency_commit_and_receipt`: on reconnect the original receipt is returned; charges = 1.
- `interleaved_same_key`: two requests with one key and equal content yield one job; with different content the
  second is refused as a conflict (exit code 7).
- `duplicate_and_reordered_delivery_one_job`.
- `client_disconnect_reconnect_by_key`: the client drops its connection while the job runs and reconnects by
  submission key; it receives the job's handle and, at completion, the original receipt; jobs = 1, charges = 1.
- `every_publish_and_submit_await_point_crashed`: over the seeded schedules of the test's own count, the
  framework's coverage account (N38a-23) reports zero await points of the two paths without a crash hit.
- Negative controls as tests, each in a mutant build with its record's own feature (`--features
  gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>`, the feature exactly as the record writes it; `gauntlet-mutations`
  alone plants nothing) that must make the named
  test fail: `nc_two_statement_key` (two jobs under `interleaved_same_key`), `nc_offset_pagination` (a row seen
  twice under concurrent appends), `nc_commit_before_durable` (a lost extent after a crash),
  `nc_worker_other_commit` (chunk digests differ), `nc_order_dependent_device` (`dual_run_equal_ledgers` fails).
- `chaos_protocol_controls_registered`: the five records exist in `registry/controls.toml`, each lists
  GATE-GAUNT-06 in `gate` and is listed by the gate's row; MR-36's two and MR-37's one also list GATE-GAUNT-03;
  BD-096's three also list the gates BD-096 registered them for; this bead adds no record.
- `torn_write_recovers`: a write torn after 17 bytes leaves no visible row; the retry commits one row.
- `second_writer_after_lease_expiry`: the first writer's late commit is refused; one row per extent.
- `overlapping_ranges_after_shard_split`, `reset_reuses_tick_zero`: distinct episode indices keep extents
  disjoint; the frontier stays contiguous.
- `full_disk_refuses_cleanly`, `clock_jump_no_effect_on_decisions` (INV-ATEL-06).
- `conflicting_bytes_810`.
- `dual_run_equal_ledgers`: for the fixed set.
- `worker_kill_resubmit_bit_equal`: real worker process and real `batch-cpu`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/chaos_protocols.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-25 milestone=M3 labels=gauntlet,chaos,gate
gates=GATE-GAUNT-06 needs=cpu,frobd plants=two-statement-key,offset-pagination`), this bead's own script, which
`scripts/e2e/chaos_g4.sh` (N38a-27) later calls under the family filter `protocols`, as it calls
`chaos_kernel.sh` (N38a-24) under `kernel`. Steps: start `frobd` on a scratch ledger; submit
`frob sim run quad-a … --key cp-<n> --json`, stop the daemon at the named await point between the idempotency
commit and the receipt through its fault hook, restart it, and recover with
`frob job find --verb sim.run --key cp-<n> --json` (one job; resubmitting returns the original receipt; one
charge; a different body under the key exits 7); kill the daemon during chunk publication and check a contiguous
frontier per world and stream with `frob telemetry query <run> --stream <s> --json`; offer different bytes for
a committed extent through the test hook (`FROB-REF-810`, exit 9); run a job in the worker process, kill the
worker, resubmit by key and compare chunk digests with the in-process run; run the dual-run set; run
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g4_chaos -- daemon --count N` (10⁵ in release mode, 10³ in smoke mode)
and read its receipt; then build each plant in a scratch build with its registered feature
(`--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>` as the record writes it; `two-statement-key`: the lookup and
insert of the idempotency key as two statements, BD-096's record, also MR-36's; `offset-pagination`: MR-36's
second mutation) and require the tier to report the violation. Log
at `target/e2e-logs/chaos_protocols/<utc>.ndjson`: each command with exit code and wall time (signals sent
logged as commands), evidence blocks, per family `metric` lines for schedules run, violations (threshold zero),
await points hit of await points total and charges per key, the refusals met (`FROB-REF-810`, conflicts) with
code, exit code and repairs, digests compared, the two negative-control assertions and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: one job and one charge per key; a contiguous frontier; bit-equal digests after a kill and
resubmission. Negative controls (each must be caught): a two-statement lookup and insert for the idempotency
key (two jobs under interleaving); offset pagination (a row seen twice under concurrent appends); a publisher
that commits before the ledger write is durable (a lost extent after a crash); a worker built at a different
commit (digests differ); a simulated device made order-dependent (the dual run disagrees). Each is a `mutation`
record of `registry/controls.toml` planted by the suite's one mechanism (N38b-04: a `cfg(feature = "<feature>")`
branch in the owning crate, built with `--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>`, the feature as the
record writes it), so the control runner runs it whenever GATE-GAUNT-06 runs. All five defects already have a
registered record and are reused through its feature, one branch and not a second plant (as N38a-24 reuses
CX-144's): three are controls BD-096 registers at M1 for INT-ASUP-02 and GATE-CHRON-11, namely the
look-up-then-insert idempotency binding in two statements (which MR-36's first mutation also names, N38a-18), the
planted lost-chunk bug (the publisher advances the frontier before the row insert is durable: the publisher that
commits before the ledger write is durable) and the fake device whose outcome depends on arrival order; offset
pagination is MR-36's second mutation and the worker built at a different commit is MR-37's (N38a-18, whose
records name GATE-GAUNT-06 beside GATE-GAUNT-03). This bead adds GATE-GAUNT-06 to the `gate` list of the lost-chunk
and fake-device records of BD-096 (N38a-18 adds it, with GATE-GAUNT-03, to the two-statement binding's), and adds
the five identifiers to the gate row's `controls` (the two-way link of BD-025). It registers no record of its
own. Determinism lanes: a
schedule replays to the same ledger bytes (class D0 under the simulation runtime); worker digests are compared
as bits.

## Considerations

- The simulation covers interleavings, not real device timing; kernel-side faults on real devices are the
  sibling bead's.
- The lab runtime is a dev-dependency only and is the same single asupersync version as the rest of the build.
- Coverage of await points is recomputed when the publish or submit path changes; a new await point with no
  schedule is a coverage hole and fails the tier.
