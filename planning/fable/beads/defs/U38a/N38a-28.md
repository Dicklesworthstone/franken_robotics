## Background

In FrankenRobotics a Moonshot mechanism is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run and
been recorded. Persistent terrain deformation (ruts and footprints that stay after a pass) is such a mechanism:
a per-world mutable buffer laid over terrain tiles that are otherwise shared, read-only, by every world of a
batch. Its named pilot is "the leak fixture on `batch-cpu`": show, on the CPU batch tier and with the soil law
that already exists at M4, that a rut made in one world or one episode has no effect on any other, before any
device code is written. Its ceiling is coverage of repeated-pass tasks; there is no speed to gain. Its fallback
is "off: terrain resets each episode".

The pilot is worth running because the failure it looks for is cheap to cause and expensive to find later: one
shared pointer to a mutable tile, one reset that forgets a buffer, one checkpoint that omits it, and every
statistic computed on a batch is contaminated by its neighbours.

## Scope

In: the pilot's protocol, frozen before execution; the fixture; the run; the record in the pilot register; the
recommendation to fund or to fall back. Out: the device implementation and the batch-scale gate (N38a-29); the
soil law itself (BD-366).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen and hashed in the pilot register before the first run).**
1. *Fixture.* A flat soil patch of 4 m by 4 m, tile size 32 by 32 cells at 2 cm, soil card: the power-law
   fixture (n = 0.5, k_c = 0.41 kN/m^1.5, k_φ = 21.6 kN/m^2.5). A tracked base of 75 kg on two tracks 0.55 m long
   and 8 cm wide drives one straight pass at 0.3 m/s for 5 s. Expected static sinkage under the tracks: 9.79 cm
   (closed form), so the rut is unmistakable.
2. *Batch.* 64 worlds on `batch-cpu`. Worlds are divided by address into *marked* worlds (the base drives) and
   *clean* worlds (the base stays still off the patch), interleaved in slots in a seeded random order.
3. *Reset schedule.* 1,000 resets in total, at seeded ticks, each resetting a seeded subset of worlds; after a
   reset a world's role (marked or clean) is redrawn.
4. *Checkpoint schedule.* At 20 seeded instants the batch is checkpointed and restored.
5. *Measurements after every reset and restore.* For every clean world: the maximum absolute rut depth over all
   its tiles (must be exactly 0); the digest of its terrain state (must equal the digest of the pristine
   terrain). For every marked world: its rut digest equals the digest of the same world run alone from its
   address. For every reset world: its terrain digest equals pristine immediately after the reset.
6. *Leak count.* The number of (world, instant) pairs violating step 5.
7. *Cost.* Memory per world (dirty tiles × 4 KiB) and the step-time ratio against the same batch with
   deformation off, by the paired lower and upper bounds over ten pairs.

**Falsifier (any one holds).** Leak count above zero. Or a marked world's digest differs from its solo run
(deformation makes a world depend on its batch). Or memory above 256 KiB per world on this fixture. Or the step
cost ratio's lower bound above 1.25.

**Ceiling, stated before the run.** No speed benefit. Coverage: the repeated-pass task rows that a
reset-each-episode terrain cannot express (a second pass in one's own rut; turning in place on soft ground).

**Fallback and kill gate.** Off; terrain resets each episode. The kill gate is the epic's (proposed KILL-RUT).

**Outputs.** One pilot record with status and verdict (`falsifier-rejected` funds the mechanism bead;
`falsifier-holds` ships the fallback); the recommendation; the measured memory and cost. Evidence class
`Tested`, labelled `synthetic` (an illustrative soil card).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol's hash and registration precede the first run in the ledger.
- [ ] The run reports the leak count, the solo-equality result, memory per world and the cost ratio with its
      bounds.
- [ ] The planted shared-buffer defect produces a nonzero leak count (the fixture can fail).
- [ ] The verdict and the recommendation are recorded, including a negative one.
- [ ] The static sinkage of the fixture matches the closed form (9.79 cm) within 2% in the simulated pass.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/rut_leak_pilot.rs` (real `batch-cpu` tier, no mocks):
- `clean_world_stays_pristine`: after 100 steps of a marked neighbour, a clean world's maximum rut depth is 0
  and its terrain digest is the pristine digest.
- `reset_restores_pristine`.
- `checkpoint_roundtrip_keeps_rut`: restore reproduces the rut digest bit for bit.
- `marked_world_equals_solo`.
- `sinkage_closed_form`: 75 kg on 8 cm tracks: p = 75 × 9.81 ÷ (2 × 0.08 × 0.55) = 8.361 kPa;
  z = (8.361 ÷ 26.725)² m = 9.79 cm.
- `nc_shared_buffer_leaks`: with the tiles shared between worlds the clean world's depth is nonzero.
- `nc_reset_skips_buffer`: a reset that leaves the buffer is caught at the first reset.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_rut_leak.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-28 milestone=M6 labels=pilot,traction
gates=GATE-GAUNT-08 plants=shared-buffer`). It registers the protocol, runs the fixture through
`frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --json` with the reset and checkpoint schedules, computes the measurements from
the telemetry, and appends the result. Log at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_rut_leak/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit
codes and wall time, the evidence block, `metric` lines for resets (1,000), leak count (`== 0`), memory per
world (`<= 256 KiB`), cost ratio bounds (`<= 1.25`), terrain digests where state is compared, the negative
control, and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: sinkage 9.79 cm (and 3.52 cm for 45 kg; 4.96 cm for 75 kg on 12 cm tracks); rut isolation (a rut
made in one episode is absent from every other). Negative controls: the shared buffer; the reset that skips the
buffer; a checkpoint that omits the buffer. Metamorphic relation: permuting slots permutes the terrain digests
and changes nothing else. Determinism lanes: the run twice on one machine and at 1 and 8 workers gives identical
digests (class D2 on `batch-cpu`).

## Considerations

- A pass verdict funds a Moonshot item; it still waits for a slot under the cap of four active Moonshot items.
- The soil card is illustrative; no claim about a real soil follows, and the pilot says so in its record.
- If the M4 soil gate failed and its kill gate fired, this pilot is not run and is recorded `withdrawn`.
