## Background

This is the mechanism of the epic "soft soil at scale" in FrankenRobotics: terrain that deforms under wheels,
tracks and feet and stays deformed, for every world of a batch independently, on the CPU batch tier and on the
GPU backends. It is Moonshot-tier, lives behind the feature `moonshot-ruts` (off by default) and is started
only if its pilot (N38a-28) rejected its falsifier and the epic's kill gate is registered.

The soil law already exists (pressure–sinkage p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ and its shear law). What this bead adds is
memory: the sinkage a pass leaves behind is stored and seen by the next pass. The design problem is isolation
and determinism at scale. The static terrain is shared by all worlds of a batch and is read-only; deformation
must be per world, must reset with the world, must travel in checkpoints, and must not make a world's
trajectory depend on which other worlds share its batch.

Rejected alternatives: a full per-world copy of the terrain (memory grows with scene size times world count); a
single shared deformable terrain (worlds would interact: exactly the leak the gate forbids).

## Scope

In: the tile store, its write and read paths in the contact step, reset, checkpoint, capacity refusal,
telemetry, and the batch-scale leak measurement. Out: the pilot; the soil law and cards; granular media under
legs; visual rendering of ruts beyond a height overlay.

## Technical approach

**Data layout (DESIGN).**
- Static terrain: shared tiles of 32 × 32 cells (cell size from the scene, 2 cm default for soil patches).
- Per world: a *rut table* of fixed capacity T (default 64) mapping tile index → slot in a per-world pool of
  mutable tiles; each mutable tile holds per-cell sinkage z (`f32`, metres, non-negative): 4 KiB per tile; at
  T = 64, 256 KiB per world. A cell is compacted exactly when its stored z is positive.
- A tile is allocated to a world on the first write (copy-on-write from an all-zero tile); unallocated tiles
  read as zero sinkage.

**Step integration.**
1. Contact detection samples the terrain height as static height − z(cell) for the world's own table.
2. The soil law gives the equilibrium sinkage z* for each loaded sample from its pressure.
3. Write-back: z ← max(z, z*) (deformation is monotone within an episode). The scatter is ordered by (tile
   index, cell index) within a world, so duplicate writes to one cell in one step resolve deterministically by
   the maximum.
4. A second pass over a compacted cell uses the card's reloading stiffness for z ≤ stored z (DESIGN default:
   rigid below the stored depth; a card may supply an unloading–reloading slope).

**Isolation rules.** No kernel reads or writes another world's rut table or pool; pools are indexed by world
slot and sized by capacity, with unused slots holding zeros; a mask is a select, never a branch on batch
content; allocation order within a world depends only on that world's writes.

**Reset.** Resetting a world zeroes its table and returns its tiles to its own pool (the pool is per world, so
nothing crosses worlds); the digest of a reset world's terrain equals the pristine digest.

**Checkpoint and fork.** The table and pool are part of the world's checkpoint; a restored or forked world has
the rut state of its parent at the fork instant.

**Capacity.** A write that needs tile T + 1 ends the step for that world with the named capacity refusal for
rut tiles and marks the world `Faulted` in telemetry; the batch continues. Sinkage is never dropped silently.

**Telemetry.** Per world: dirty tiles, maximum sinkage, total displaced volume Σ z·cell area; these enter the
energy ledger as work done on the soil (the product of the soil pressure and the sinkage increment per cell).

**Determinism.** Class D2 on each backend, including independence of batch size, slot, co-tenants and bucket
capacity; the rut digest is part of the world digest.

**The leak measurement at batch scale.** The pilot's protocol at 4,096 worlds on each backend claimed: marked
and clean worlds interleaved; 10³ resets and 20 checkpoint round trips; leak count = number of (world, instant)
pairs where a clean or freshly reset world's terrain digest is not pristine, or a marked world's rut digest
differs from its solo run.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed done-condition of the epic: rut state stays per-world with zero leak over 10³ resets at batch
      scale (4,096 worlds), on `batch-cpu` and on each device backend claimed.
- [ ] A marked world's digest equals its solo run at capacities 32, 64 and 128 and on one and two shards.
- [ ] Reset gives a pristine digest; checkpoint and fork carry the rut state bit for bit.
- [ ] Exceeding the tile capacity ends in the named refusal for that world; no silent truncation.
- [ ] Simulated plate sinkage within 2% of the closed form; a second pass in the same rut sinks no further at
      equal load.
- [ ] Step cost with the feature on is at most 1.25 times the cost with it off on the reference tracked
      workload (paired upper bound), or the kill gate's trigger is recorded.
- [ ] The feature is off by default; with it off no rut buffer is allocated.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of the crate states the mechanism's inputs, outputs, refusals, determinism class and gaps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/ruts.rs`:
- `cow_allocates_on_first_write`: a world that never loads soil has zero dirty tiles.
- `write_is_monotone_max`: writes of 3 cm then 2 cm leave 3 cm; two writes to one cell in one step leave the
  larger.
- `second_pass_no_further_sinkage`: equal load on a 9.79 cm rut adds nothing.
- `reset_pristine_digest`, `checkpoint_bits`, `fork_inherits_rut`.
- `capacity_refusal_at_tile_65`.
- `soil_work_in_ledger`: for uniform pressure p over area A sinking to z with exponent n, the work is
  p·A·z ÷ (n + 1); for the 75 kg fixture (p = 8.361 kPa, A = 0.088 m², z = 0.0979 m, n = 0.5) it is 48.0 J.
- `inv_batch_independence_ruts`: solo against 100 co-tenancies (property test, 1,000 worlds).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/soil_ruts_leak.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-29 milestone=M6 labels=traction,ruts gates=GATE-TRAC-08
plants=shared-pool,reset-skips-pool timeout_s=3600`). It runs the batch-scale protocol through
`frob sim run --json` on each available backend, then the solo comparisons through `frob sim replay`. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/soil_ruts_leak/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit codes and wall time; each envelope's evidence
block (tier, determinism class, identities, seed); `metric` lines for worlds, resets (`>= 1000`), leak count
(`== 0`), memory per world, cost ratio (`<= 1.25`); digests compared; refusals met; the two negative controls;
the `summary` naming GATE-TRAC-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: sinkage 9.79 cm for the fixture; soil work 48.0 J; rut isolation. Negative controls: a pool
shared across worlds; a reset that skips the pool; a scatter in arrival order (digests differ across worker
counts). Metamorphic relations: MR-01 (batch-composition invariance) with ruts on; splitting a rollout at a
snapshot equals running it whole. Determinism lanes: twice; worker counts 1, 2, 8; capacities; shards; each
backend against class D2.

## Considerations

- Kill gate named in advance: the epic's (trigger: any leak on devices, a broken launch shape or determinism
  class, or more than 25% step cost; fallback: terrain resets each episode).
- Memory: 256 KiB per world at the default capacity is 1 GiB at 4,096 worlds and 2 GiB at 8,192; the admission
  estimator counts it, and a batch that does not fit is refused at admission.
- The soil card is `assumed` unless calibrated; results on deformable ground are labelled exploratory until a
  card with provenance exists.
