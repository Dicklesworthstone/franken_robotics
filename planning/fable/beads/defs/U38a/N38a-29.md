## Background

This is the mechanism of the epic "soft soil at scale" in FrankenRobotics: terrain that deforms under wheels,
tracks and feet and stays deformed, for every world of a batch independently, on the CPU batch tier and on the
GPU backends. It is Moonshot-tier, lives behind the feature `moonshot-ruts` (off by default) and is started
only if its pilot (N18b-16, a dependency, run at M4 with the soil work; it superseded the M6 draft N38a-28)
rejected its falsifier with no funding flag standing, and the epic's kill gate is registered (KILL-RUT, N38a-37).
If the pilot's falsifier holds, or its result flags a funding concern (memory above 256 KiB per world on its
fixture, or a paired lower bound of its step-cost ratio above 1.25: the second clause of KILL-RUT), or the pilot
is recorded `withdrawn` (KILL-SOIL fired at M4, or the epic was not selected under the Moonshot cap), this bead
is not started and closes `withdrawn` with a pointer to N38a-38, which records KILL-RUT (N41-17: it delivers
nothing and never counts as work done).

The soil law already exists (pressure–sinkage p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ and its shear law). What this bead adds is
memory: the sinkage a pass leaves behind is stored and seen by the next pass. The design problem is isolation
and determinism at scale. The static terrain is shared by all worlds of a batch and is read-only; deformation
must be per world, must reset with the world, must travel in checkpoints, and must not make a world's
trajectory depend on which other worlds share its batch.

Rejected alternatives: a full per-world copy of the terrain (memory grows with scene size times world count); a
single shared deformable terrain (worlds would interact: exactly the leak the gate forbids).

## Scope

In: the M6 form of the rut field: the same field on the device back ends and at batch scale. Device storage
for the per-world directory and pool, copy-on-write allocation by kernels, the read path and the write-back
scatter as kernels in the contact step, the isolation rules on a device, reset and checkpoint of the device
pools, capacity behaviour under the caller's policy, telemetry and the soil work in the energy ledger, the
step-cost bound, the feature flag, and the batch-scale leak measurement at 4,096 worlds on `batch-cpu` and on
each device backend claimed.

Out: the definition of the rut field, which is N18b-17's (M4; this bead depends on it through the soil seed
BD-366): what a cell stores and its sign (the offset Δz ≤ 0), the 8 × 8 rut tile over a static tile, the
directory and the pool, the read path with its interpolation bound, the write rule with the elastic rebound
fraction, the second-pass rule, reset and declared persistence across episodes, snapshot, restore and fork, the
status `Degraded: rut-capacity`, and the M4 leak row on `batch-cpu` under the transactional profile. This bead
changes none of them and holds no second layout. Also out: the pilot; the soil law and cards; granular media
under legs; visual rendering of ruts beyond a height overlay.

## Technical approach

**Data layout: the field of N18b-17, on devices (DESIGN).** The state is the M4 field unchanged, so that a
world's rut state has one meaning on every tier and its digest can be compared across them.
- Static terrain: the scene's immutable shared tiles (cell size from the scene, 2 cm default for soil patches).
- Per world: a *rut directory* of fixed capacity mapping static tile index → slot in a per-world pool of
  T_rut mutable rut tiles; each rut tile is an 8 × 8 array of `f32` offsets Δz ≤ 0 in metres at the scene's
  fine resolution (256 bytes per tile), zeroed on allocation. A cell is compacted exactly when its stored Δz
  is negative.
- A tile is allocated to a world on the first write (copy-on-write from an all-zero tile); unallocated tiles
  read as Δz = 0.
- One layout, one sign (DESIGN). An earlier form of this bead used tiles of 32 × 32 cells holding a
  non-negative sinkage z, 64 of them per world. Two layouts would have given one mechanism of one crate two
  capacities and two signs; the M4 layout stands because this bead depends on it and because it is keyed like
  the scene's own bricks (8 fine samples to a coarse cell).
- Capacity at batch scale (set here, DESIGN): T_rut = 1,024 by default, which is the same 256 KiB per world
  and the same rutted area (about 26 m² at 2 cm cells) as 64 tiles of 32 × 32; the M4 fixture of N18b-17 uses
  64. T_rut is a field of the shape class and so of `StepPlanId`.
- Device storage: the directories and the pools of all worlds of a batch are two slabs indexed by (world slot,
  entry); allocation of a tile is a kernel stage inside the world's own pool (count, exclusive scan, write),
  never a host round trip.

**Step integration.**
1. Contact detection samples the terrain height as static height + Δz(cell) for the world's own directory (the
   read path of N18b-17, with its interpolation bound).
2. The soil law gives the equilibrium sinkage z* for each loaded sample from its pressure.
3. Write-back, after the step commits: Δz ← min(Δz, −(1 − ρ_e)·z*), the write rule of N18b-17 with the card's
   elastic rebound fraction ρ_e (default 0, for which the rule keeps the deeper of the two); deformation is
   monotone within an episode. The scatter is ordered by (tile index, cell index) within a world, so duplicate
   writes to one cell in one step resolve deterministically by the minimum.
4. A second pass over a compacted cell follows the second-pass rule of N18b-17: the reference surface is the
   rutted height and the ground is rigid below the stored depth on reloading, unless the card supplies an
   unloading–reloading slope.

**Isolation rules.** No kernel reads or writes another world's rut table or pool; pools are indexed by world
slot and sized by capacity, with unused slots holding zeros; a mask is a select, never a branch on batch
content; allocation order within a world depends only on that world's writes.

**Reset.** Resetting a world zeroes its table and returns its tiles to its own pool (the pool is per world, so
nothing crosses worlds); the digest of a reset world's terrain equals the pristine digest.

**Checkpoint and fork.** The table and pool are part of the world's checkpoint; a restored or forked world has
the rut state of its parent at the fork instant.

**Setting T_rut (DESIGN).** T_rut is the member `rut-tiles=<T>` of `sim.run`'s `--capacity` input (the capacity
spelling of N16a-14, `--capacity contacts=<C>[,rows=<R>][,rut-tiles=<T>]`; the member is added by this bead and
requested of the verb registry VERBS-sim): a positive integer, part of `StepPlanId` like the other capacities;
absent, it is the fixture's declared value (the M4 fixture of N18b-17 declares 64) and otherwise the default
1,024. On a build without `moonshot-ruts`, or on a backend for which KILL-RUT's decision is `fired`, the member
is refused with `FROB-REF-724` (exit 5), naming the capability and its status (`not-in-build` or `killed`);
zero or a non-integer is `FROB-REF-802`. `sim.estimate` counts T_rut × 256 B per world in its memory estimate.

**Capacity.** A write that needs a tile beyond T_rut marks the world `Degraded: rut-capacity` with the tile
that could not be allocated, as N18b-17 defines and as the rule for every capacity requires (`Faulted` is
reserved for non-finite values; an earlier form of this bead used it here). The caller's capacity policy then
chooses: `refuse` ends the step for that world with the rut-capacity refusal; `reset` resets it;
`continue-flagged` skips the write, applies it to no other tile, and flags the world's later results. The batch
continues in every case. Sinkage is never dropped silently. The rut-capacity refusal is the refusal-allocation
ledger's row `rut-capacity-overflow`, registered with Traction's refusals by N18-REF and the same one N18b-17
raises on `batch-cpu`: proposed family `7xx` beside 722, exit code 5, retry `after-change`, `pointer` to the
world address and the tile that could not be allocated, ranked repairs (1) raise T_rut (a new `StepPlanId`),
(2) reset, (3) continue-flagged. `FROB-REF-722` is not reused for it: that code is defined for contact or row
capacity only. Until the ledger assigns its number the ledger's interim form of the slug is emitted (the same
on every backend), never a generic error and never a device fault code.

**Telemetry.** Per world: dirty tiles, maximum sinkage (the largest −Δz), total displaced volume
Σ (−Δz)·cell area; these enter the
energy ledger as work done on the soil (the product of the soil pressure and the sinkage increment per cell).

**Determinism.** Class D2 on each backend, including independence of batch size, slot, co-tenants and bucket
capacity; the rut digest is part of the world digest.

**The leak measurement at batch scale.** The pilot's protocol (N18b-16's fixture `traction/rut_leak`: the 75 kg
tracked base and the probe on the 4 m soil patch, with the task's reset and checkpoint schedules) at 4,096
worlds on each backend claimed: marked and clean worlds interleaved; 10³ resets and 20 checkpoint round trips;
leak count = number of (world, instant) pairs where a clean or freshly reset world's terrain digest is not
pristine, or a marked world's rut digest differs from its solo run.

**The cost measurement.** The reference tracked workload (DESIGN: the same fixture at 4,096 worlds, the batch on
which the pilot measured its own ratio) is run in ten pairs, ruts on against ruts off, the "off" arm being the
same request on a build without `moonshot-ruts` (no rut buffer allocated); the paired lower and upper bounds of
the step-cost ratio are recorded per backend. The acceptance asks the upper bound to be at most 1.25; KILL-RUT's
cost clause fires on a lower bound above 1.25; bounds that straddle 1.25 are recorded and fire nothing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The seed done-condition of the epic: rut state stays per-world with zero leak over 10³ resets at batch
      scale (4,096 worlds), on `batch-cpu` and on each device backend claimed.
- [ ] A marked world's digest equals its solo run at rut capacities T_rut = 512, 1,024 and 2,048 (the areas the
      earlier 32, 64 and 128 tiles of 32 × 32 covered) and on one and two shards.
- [ ] The rut state is the field of N18b-17 on every tier: a world replayed on `batch-cpu` from a device
      checkpoint has the same rut digest, and `crates/frob-contact` contains one tile layout (8 × 8 offsets
      Δz ≤ 0).
- [ ] Reset gives a pristine digest; checkpoint and fork carry the rut state bit for bit.
- [ ] Exceeding the tile capacity marks the world `Degraded: rut-capacity` and, under the policy `refuse`, ends
      in the `rut-capacity-overflow` refusal for that world (exit 5, its three repairs, the pointer to the world
      and tile; the interim form until a number is assigned; never `FROB-REF-722`), identical on `batch-cpu`
      and each device backend; under `continue-flagged` the write is skipped and flagged (exit 2); no silent
      truncation, and no world is marked `Faulted` for it.
- [ ] Simulated plate sinkage within 2% of the closed form; a second pass in the same rut sinks no further at
      equal load.
- [ ] Step cost with the feature on is at most 1.25 times the cost with it off on the reference tracked
      workload (paired upper bound over ten pairs, per backend), or the measured bounds are recorded for
      KILL-RUT's cost clause.
- [ ] `--capacity rut-tiles=<T>` sets T_rut and enters `StepPlanId`; on a build without the feature it is refused
      with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5.
- [ ] The feature is off by default; with it off no rut buffer is allocated.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of the crate states the mechanism's inputs, outputs, refusals, determinism class and gaps.
- [ ] Close (N41-17 rules 1 and 4: a gate in a done-condition is its recorded value with its caught negative
      controls, and acceptance is the gate's row): a leak, a broken launch shape or class, or a cost ratio above
      its bound is repaired once (a fix and a full re-run with new receipts, the first receipts kept); a value that
      still misses its TARGET is recorded as failed, this bead closes on that record, and KILL-RUT (N38a-38, which
      depends on this bead) decides on it, so the decision never waits on a pass it exists to judge.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/ruts.rs`:
- `cow_allocates_on_first_write`: a world that never loads soil has zero dirty tiles.
- `write_rule_on_each_backend`: sinkages of 3 cm then 2 cm leave Δz = −3 cm; two writes to one cell in one step
  leave the deeper (the M4 test `write_min_rule` of N18b-17, run through the device kernels).
- `second_pass_no_further_sinkage`: equal load on a 9.79 cm rut adds nothing.
- `reset_pristine_digest`, `checkpoint_bits`, `fork_inherits_rut`.
- `rut_tiles_member`: `--capacity rut-tiles=512` gives a step plan with T_rut = 512 and a `StepPlanId` different
  from the default's; `rut-tiles=0` is `FROB-REF-802`; on a build without `moonshot-ruts` the member is
  `FROB-REF-724` with status `not-in-build`.
- `capacity_degraded_beyond_t_rut`: with T_rut = 64, the write that needs tile 65 marks the world `Degraded:
  rut-capacity`; under `refuse` the step ends with the `rut-capacity-overflow` refusal (exit 5, repairs in
  the order raise T_rut, reset, continue-flagged; not `FROB-REF-722`), under `continue-flagged` the write is
  skipped and no other tile changes; the same at the default 1,024 with tile 1,025, on each backend.
- `soil_work_in_ledger`: for uniform pressure p over area A sinking to z with exponent n, the work is
  p·A·z ÷ (n + 1); for the 75 kg fixture (p = 8.361 kPa, A = 0.088 m², z = 0.0979 m, n = 0.5) it is 48.0 J.
- `inv_batch_independence_ruts`: solo against 100 co-tenancies (property test, 1,000 worlds).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/soil_ruts_leak.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-29 milestone=M6 labels=traction,ruts gates=GATE-TRAC-08
plants=shared-pool,reset-skips-pool timeout_s=3600`), on a build with `moonshot-ruts`. Every run carries its own
key, so none returns another's stored receipt. It runs the batch-scale protocol on each available backend T
(`batch-cpu`, and `gpu-cuda` or `gpu-metal` where present): `frob sim run --fixture traction/rut_leak --tier T
--worlds 4096 --capacity rut-tiles=1024 --seed 29 --key srl-batch-T --json` (the fixture of the pilot N18b-16:
its design, scene, soil card and the task's horizon and reset and checkpoint schedules); the capacity sweep with
`--capacity rut-tiles=512` and `rut-tiles=2048` and `--key srl-cap<T_rut>-T`; then the solo comparisons of the
marked worlds, `frob sim replay --run <run> --world <w> --tier T --key srl-solo-<w>-T --json`; then the cost
pairs, the batch request on this build and on a build without `moonshot-ruts` (there without the `rut-tiles`
member), ten pairs with keys `srl-cost-<on|off>-<i>-T`; then a pool-exhaustion run with `--capacity rut-tiles=8`
(the pass must overflow it: a rut of 2.05 m, 1.5 m of travel plus the 0.55 m track, crosses at least 13 tiles of
16 cm) and `--on-overflow refuse --key srl-overflow-refuse-T` (asserted with
`e2e_expect_refusal` on the slug's interim form or its assigned code, exit 5; never `FROB-REF-722` on `batch-cpu`
or `FROB-REF-610` on a GPU tier, which are the contact and row capacity refusals) and with `--on-overflow
continue-flagged --key srl-overflow-flagged-T` (exit 2, `Degraded: rut-capacity`); on the build without the
feature, `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 --` the batch request with the `rut-tiles` member (key
`srl-nofeature-T`); the two plants, each a build with its own registered feature (`--features
gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/nc-<nnnn>`; `gauntlet-mutations` alone plants nothing), with keys
`srl-plant-<name>-T`. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/soil_ruts_leak/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`
("N38a-29"), `step`, `event`; commands with exit codes and wall time; each envelope's evidence
block (tier, determinism class, identities, seed); `metric` lines for worlds, resets (`>= 1000`), leak count
(`== 0`), memory per world, the cost ratio's upper bound (`<= 1.25`) with its lower bound beside it; digests compared; refusals met; the two negative controls, each an `assert` with `caught`; the `summary` with
assertions passed and failed, naming GATE-TRAC-08.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: sinkage 9.79 cm for the fixture; soil work 48.0 J; rut isolation (KA-419). Negative controls: a
pool shared across worlds; a reset that skips the pool; a scatter in arrival order (digests differ across worker
counts). Each is a `mutation` record of `registry/controls.toml` that this bead registers with `seed = "KA-419"`
and gate GATE-TRAC-08, planted as a `cfg(feature = "nc-<nnnn>")` branch at the device kernel it mutates (the
suite's one mutation mechanism, N38b-04); the M4 controls of the CPU field stay N18b-17's. Metamorphic relations: MR-01 (batch-composition invariance) with ruts on; splitting a rollout at a
snapshot equals running it whole. Determinism lanes: twice; worker counts 1, 2, 8; capacities; shards; each
backend against class D2.

## Considerations

- Kill gate named in advance: KILL-RUT (N38a-37's row, decided per backend by N38a-38; trigger: the pilot's
  falsifier or funding flags, any leak at batch scale, a broken launch shape or determinism class, or a paired
  lower bound of the step-cost ratio above 1.25; fallback: terrain resets each episode).
- Memory: 256 KiB per world at the default capacity is 1 GiB at 4,096 worlds and 2 GiB at 8,192; the admission
  estimator counts it, and a batch that does not fit is refused at admission.
- The soil card is `assumed` unless calibrated; results on deformable ground are labelled exploratory until a
  card with provenance exists.
