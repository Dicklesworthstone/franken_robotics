## Background

In FrankenRobotics a Moonshot mechanism is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run.
Tactile micro-patches are such a mechanism: representing a fingertip pad not as one contact patch but as a small
grid of them, so that a tactile sensor model has a load distribution to read and a grasp can begin to slip at
its edge. The pilot named for it: a fingertip pad as 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts (normal, two
tangential, spin) feeding the sensor models, on the `strict` profile, against one patch. Falsifier: no change in
grasp-slip outcomes against one patch. Ceiling: tactile sensing realism. Fallback: one patch per pad.

The pilot is honest only if it can fail, and it can: a single four-dimensional contact already carries a
torsional capacity through its effective radius, so for many grasps the single patch gives the same slip
decision as a grid. If it does everywhere that matters, the grid buys nothing and is not built.

## Scope

In: the frozen protocol; the grasp fixture in three pad resolutions; the hand-derived capacities; the run on
`strict`; the record and the recommendation. Out: the tactile sensor model as a product (N38a-31); any device
implementation.

## Technical approach

**Hand-derived quantities (to `docs/derivations/pad-micro-patches.md`).** A square pad of side s is pressed with
normal force N at uniform pressure; friction coefficient μ.
- Translational capacity (pure sliding): μ·N for every resolution.
- Torsional capacity about the pad centre (pure twisting): μ·N·ℓ, where ℓ is the mean distance of the load from
  the centre. Exact for a uniform square: ℓ = (s/6)·(√2 + ln(1 + √2)) = 0.3826·s. One patch reproduces it only if
  its effective torsional radius is set to that value.
- A 2 × 2 grid of equal point loads at (±s/4, ±s/4): ℓ = s·√2/4 = 0.3536·s (7.6% below exact).
- A 4 × 4 grid at coordinates ±s/8 and ±3s/8: ℓ = (4·0.17678 + 8·0.39528 + 4·0.53033)·s/16 = 0.3744·s (2.1%
  below exact).
- Off-centre normal load (the grasped object's weight applying a moment about a pad axis): one patch has a
  single normal force and no pressure gradient; a grid redistributes normal load, so the micro-patch on the
  unloading edge reaches its cone first. With a linear pressure gradient that unloads one edge to zero, the
  edge row of a 4 × 4 grid carries 1/16 of N where uniform loading gives it 1/4 (row means 1/16, 3/16, 5/16,
  7/16 of N), so local slip can begin at about a quarter of the tangential load per row that a uniform pad
  would predict.

**Protocol (frozen and hashed before the first run).**
1. *Fixture.* A parallel two-finger gripper holds a rigid box of 0.5 kg between two square pads of side 20 mm;
   μ = 0.8; pad normal stiffness from a generic compliance card (`assumed`). Three resolutions: 1 × 1 with
   effective radius 0.3826·s; 2 × 2; 4 × 4.
2. *Load cases* (a grid of 5 × 5 × 3 = 75 cases): grip force N at 5 levels from 1.0 to 3.0 times the minimum
   that holds the weight in pure friction (m·g ÷ (2μ) = 3.07 N per pad); the box's centre of mass offset from
   the pad axis at 5 levels from 0 to 40 mm (a gravity moment m·g·d about the pad normal, shared by two pads);
   and a disturbance: none, a tangential pull ramped to 2 N, a twist ramped to 0.05 N·m.
3. *Outcomes per case and resolution.* Slip or hold at the end of a 2 s hold; the load at slip onset; the
   rotation of the box at 2 s; for grids, which micro-patch left its cone first.
4. *Run.* `strict` profile, `f64`, one world per case.
5. *Comparison.* The number of cases whose slip-or-hold outcome differs between 1 × 1 and 2 × 2, and between
   1 × 1 and 4 × 4; the distribution of onset-load differences.

**Falsifier.** No change in grasp-slip outcomes against one patch: zero differing cases of 75 for both grids,
and onset loads within 2% in every case.

**Further stop conditions recorded with the result.** Step cost of the 4 × 4 pad above 1.25 times the 1 × 1 pad
on this fixture (paired lower bound); a contact count the tier cannot admit.

**Outputs.** One pilot record: counts, the table of differing cases, costs, verdict and recommendation.
Evidence class `Tested`, labelled `synthetic` (generic pad card).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-contact/tests/pad_micro_patches.rs` (real `strict` tier):
- `torsional_lever_square`: (1/6)·(√2 + ln(1 + √2)) = 0.38260.
- `lever_2x2`: √2/4 = 0.35355. `lever_4x4`: 0.37442.
- `min_grip_force`: 0.5 kg × 9.81 m/s² ÷ (2 × 0.8) = 3.066 N per pad.
- `pure_translation_same_for_all`: the tangential load at slip is μ·N for 1 × 1, 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 within 0.5%.
- `pure_twist_capacity_ordering`: twist capacity ratios to μ·N·s are 0.3826, 0.3536 and 0.3744 within 1%.
- `row_shares_under_gradient`: 1/16, 3/16, 5/16, 7/16.
- `planted_offset_case_differs`: with the centre of mass 40 mm off the pad axis and grip at 1.2 times the
  minimum, the gravity moment per pad is 0.5 × 9.81 × 0.040 ÷ 2 = 0.0981 N·m against a single-patch torsional
  capacity of 0.8 × 3.68 × 0.3826 × 0.020 = 0.0225 N·m: both models must report rotation; the test asserts that
  the two resolutions disagree on at least one case of the 75 or the falsifier is declared to hold.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_pad_micro_patches.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-30 milestone=M6 labels=pilot,traction,sensorium
gates=GATE-GAUNT-08 plants=single-patch-radius-zero`). It registers the protocol, runs the 225 simulations
through `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --profile strict --json`, compares outcomes and appends the result. The
plant sets the single patch's effective radius to zero (its twist capacity vanishes), which must change the
1 × 1 outcomes in the twist cases. Log at `target/e2e-logs/pilot_pad_micro_patches/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with
exit codes and wall time, evidence blocks, `metric` lines for differing cases (the falsifier's threshold `> 0`),
onset-load differences, the cost ratio (`<= 1.25`), the negative control and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.3826·s, 0.3536·s, 0.3744·s; 3.066 N; μ·N in pure translation. Negative controls: the zero
effective radius; a grid whose micro-patches share one normal force (no redistribution: it then equals the
single patch exactly, and the comparison must report zero differences for that build). Determinism lanes: each
case twice on one machine gives identical digests (class D2 on the CPU tier).

## Considerations

- 75 cases are a paper-scale probe, not a benchmark; the verdict is about whether the mechanism can matter.
- The pad card is `assumed`; no claim about a real fingertip follows.
- Two 4 × 4 pads are 32 contacts, above the referee's limit of 21 in one solve; the pilot therefore runs on
  `strict`, and a positive verdict does not imply that any other tier can admit the pads.
