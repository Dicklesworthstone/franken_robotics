## Background

This is the mechanism of the epic "tactile sensing and compliant pads" in FrankenRobotics: a fingertip pad
modelled as a grid of micro-patch contacts, and a tactile-array sensor that reads them. It is Moonshot-tier,
lives behind the feature `moonshot-tactile` (off by default) and is started only if its pilot (N38a-30) rejected
its falsifier and the epic's kill gate is registered.

The sensor model stated by the design: tactile arrays have finite taxel footprints with normal and shear
saturation; the first check is the resultant load against the sum over taxels. Like every sensor of the system
it obeys the clock contract (a rational period, an exposure window, a delivered packet with capture and arrival
instants) and reads only what the contact step produced; it never reaches into state the instrument could not
see.

Rejected alternatives: a taxel image synthesized from a single patch by an assumed pressure profile (it would
report a distribution the dynamics never computed); a finite-element pad (out of reach at batch speed and
without data to validate it).

## Scope

In: the pad as micro-patches in the contact model; the pad compliance card; the tactile-array card and sensor;
readings, saturation and packets; known answers; the cost measurement. Out: the pilot; learning from tactile
input; micro-slip precursors (killed; reopenable only with imported data); device tiers unless cost and
capacity allow (decided by the kill gate's clauses).

## Technical approach

**Pad (in `frob-contact`).** A pad is declared on a link by a rectangle (centre, axes, sides a × b), a grid
N_u × N_v (1 × 1, 2 × 2 or 4 × 4) and a compliance card (normal stiffness per area k_n in N/m³, damping ratio,
friction pair). Each grid cell becomes one four-dimensional contact sample at the cell centre with area
A_c = a·b/(N_u·N_v), normal stiffness k_n·A_c, and an effective torsional radius equal to the mean lever of its
own cell (0.3826 times the cell side for a square cell). The pad's samples count toward contact capacity; a pad
that would exceed the admitted capacity of the tier is refused at model compile, or reduced by the documented
patch reduction with the label `capacity-biased`; contacts are never dropped silently.

**Sensor (in `frob-sense`).** A tactile array is declared on a pad by a taxel grid T_u × T_v, a footprint (each
taxel integrates load over its own rectangle), saturation limits F_n,max (normal) and F_t,max (shear) per taxel,
a resolution quantum, a noise model and a clock (period, exposure, latency).
1. Over the exposure window, accumulate each micro-patch's contact impulse (normal and the two tangential
   components, in the pad frame); each impact impulse is added once; divide by the exposure duration to obtain
   mean forces per micro-patch.
2. Map micro-patch forces to taxels by area overlap: taxel j receives Σ_c w_jc·f_c with w_jc = overlap area of
   taxel j and cell c divided by the cell's area; Σ_j w_jc = 1 for a cell fully covered by taxels.
3. Unsaturated reading r_j = (f_n, f_tu, f_tv)_j. The resultant check applies here: Σ_j r_j equals the pad's
   total contact force over the exposure.
4. Saturation: f_n is clamped to [0, F_n,max]; the shear vector is scaled to magnitude at most F_t,max; a
   saturated taxel sets a flag in the packet.
5. Noise from the taxel's own address-keyed stream; quantization to the resolution quantum; the packet is
   queued with its capture and arrival instants.

**Evidence and labels.** Tier and determinism class of the contact step that fed the sensor; class `Tested`
for known answers; card provenance (`generic`, `assumed`) carried into every reading's claim product.

**Verbs.** No new verb: the pad is part of the design (`design.check` reports its contact count and capacity),
the sensor's clock appears in `sense.schedule`, its cost and footprint in `sense.profile`, and it is read
through observations like any other sensor.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A pad of 2 × 2 and of 4 × 4 micro-patches compiles, counts toward capacity and is refused by name where
      the tier cannot admit it.
- [ ] The resultant of the unsaturated taxel readings equals the pad's total contact force over the exposure
      within 10⁻¹² relative in `f64` (the tolerance this bead sets for KA-523), for any taxel grid that covers
      the pad.
- [ ] Saturation clamps as specified and sets the flag; with saturation the resultant is below the pad force and
      the reading says so.
- [ ] Grasp-slip outcomes on the pilot's 75 cases reproduce the pilot's record on `strict`.
- [ ] Step cost of a 4 × 4 pad pair against one patch per pad is reported with paired bounds; above 1.25 the
      kill gate's clause is recorded.
- [ ] Packets obey the clock contract: checkpoint and restore at any instant reproduce every later delivered
      reading byte for byte.
- [ ] The feature is off by default; contracts of both crates state the mechanism and its gaps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-sense/tests/tactile.rs`, `crates/frob-contact/tests/pad.rs`:
- `ka_523_resultant_equals_sum`: micro-patch normal forces (1, 2, 3, 4) N on a 2 × 2 pad read by a 4 × 4 taxel
  grid: Σ taxels = 10 N; the tangential resultant likewise.
- `overlap_weights_sum_to_one`: for 2 × 2 cells and 3 × 3 taxels each cell's weights sum to 1.
- `impulse_counted_once`: a 0.2 N·s impact inside a 10 ms exposure contributes 20 N mean force, once.
- `normal_saturation`: 6 N on a taxel with F_n,max = 5 N reads 5 N and sets the flag.
- `shear_saturation_scales_vector`: shear (3, 4) N with F_t,max = 2.5 N reads (1.5, 2.0) N.
- `capacity_refusal_two_4x4_pads_referee`: 32 contacts against a limit of 21 are refused at compile on the
  referee.
- `nc_total_in_every_taxel`: a build that writes the pad total into each taxel fails the resultant test by the
  factor 16.
- `checkpoint_restores_tactile_packets`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tactile_pad_grasp.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-31 milestone=M6 labels=sensorium,traction,tactile
plants=total-in-every-taxel,drop-contacts`). It runs the grasp fixture at the three resolutions through
`frob sim run --profile strict --json`, reads tactile observations, checks the resultant against the contact
telemetry of the same run (a live-dependency proof: changing the grip force must change the readings), measures
the cost ratio, and plants the two defects. Log at `target/e2e-logs/tactile_pad_grasp/<utc>.ndjson`: commands
with exit codes and wall time, evidence blocks with card provenance, `metric` lines for the resultant error
(`<= 1e-12` relative), differing slip outcomes, cost ratio (`<= 1.25`), refusals met, both negative controls and
the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-523 (the resultant load equals the sum over taxels; tolerance set here; control registered here: the
total-in-every-taxel mutation). The lever values 0.3826, 0.3536 and 0.3744 times the pad side. Negative
controls: the mutation above; contacts dropped silently at capacity (the capacity audit must catch it); a sensor
that reads contact forces at the end of the step instead of over the exposure (the impulse test fails).
Metamorphic relations: the mirrored hand on the mirrored object returns mirrored readings; duplicating a taxel
grid at double resolution preserves the resultant. Determinism lanes: twice; worker counts; each tier against
its class.

## Considerations

- Kill gate named in advance: the epic's (trigger: the pilot's falsifier, more than 25% step cost, an
  inadmissible contact count, or a failed resultant; fallback: one patch per pad).
- Without imported tactile measurements nothing here is a reality claim; every reading carries its card's
  provenance.
- Hands with many pads multiply contacts quickly; `design.check` reports the pad contact count beside the
  tier's capacity so an agent sees the refusal before it runs.
