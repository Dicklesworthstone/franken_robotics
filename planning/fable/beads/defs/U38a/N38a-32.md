## Background

In FrankenRobotics a Frontier mechanism is funded only after a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run.
Kinematic actors (capsules that move along a scene's route graph on a script, standing in for people, pets and
vehicles) are such a mechanism. Their stated ceiling is nil on decision time: they only widen the variety of
situations a robot design meets. The pilot named for them asks the one question that decides whether that
variety is worth its cost: for one scene archetype, list the failure classes of the hazard registry and mark
which a moving actor could provoke that the structural proxy cannot; if the list is empty the tier is not
funded.

A *structural proxy* is what the scenes already have without actors: static geometry and static clutter (a box
where a person might stand, a low obstacle where a pet might lie). The pilot is paper-scale by design: a table
built by reasoning and by a few hand-checkable kinematic cases, not a simulation campaign.

## Scope

This bead is the one place where the funding verdict for kinematic actors is made. The paper pilot of the two
dressing items is split by item: the scene pilot N19a-40 (M5, a dependency) decides asset-populated interiors
and, in the same marking pass over its own archetype (the home or the commercial kitchen), records an actor
mark K(f) for every class and a candidate list L_K, with no verdict. This bead takes that list as a third
table, holds every candidate to rule 4 below, and writes the register entry `dressing.actors`; N19a-40 writes
`dressing.assets`.

In: the frozen protocol; the classification table for one archetype (the warehouse), the retail store as a
check of the method, and the re-examination of N19a-40's candidates; the hand-checked kinematic cases that
justify each "actor only" mark; the record `dressing.actors` and the recommendation. Out: the pilot and the
verdict for asset-populated interiors (N19a-40); implementing actors (N38a-33); asset-populated interiors
themselves (BD-399).

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen and hashed before the table is filled).**
1. *Archetype.* The warehouse (long aisles, racking, pallets, totes, ramps, dock plates), chosen because its
   hazard list names moving forklifts and carts with the failure "collision within stopping distance". A second
   archetype (retail store: shoppers, "stopping distance") is classified as a robustness check of the method.
2. *Rows.* Every failure class the hazard registry lists for the archetype (rack strikes, load sway, cycle time,
   charge consumed, collision within stopping distance, and the rest of the registry's list at the pilot's
   date), one row each.
3. *Columns.* (a) provoked by static structure alone; (b) provoked by a static proxy placed where an actor could
   be; (c) provoked only when the obstacle moves; (d) the kinematic argument or hand-checked case for (c);
   (e) which robot quantities the class exercises (stopping distance, sensing latency, field of view, yielding
   logic).
4. *Rule for an "actor only" mark.* A class is marked (c) only if a hand-checkable case exists in which every
   static placement of the proxy yields no failure and one scripted motion yields the failure. The case is
   written with its numbers.
5. *Two independent classifiers.* The table is filled twice, by two sessions that do not see each other's
   marks, and the disagreements are listed.
6. *The candidates of the scene pilot.* N19a-40 marked, for its archetype, the classes an actor could provoke
   and the proxy cannot, under a looser admissibility rule (an obstacle that enters the stopping distance after
   the robot has committed; occlusion that moves). Each class of its list L_K is entered here as a row of a
   third table and is marked (c) only if rule 4 holds for it, with a case and its numbers; a candidate with no
   case is marked (b) and the difference from the scene pilot's mark is listed. This is how the actor question
   is decided once: the earlier marks are evidence, and the verdict is this bead's.

**Hand-checkable cases (the pilot's known answers).**
- *Crossing actor.* A robot drives along an aisle at v = 1.0 m/s; reaction latency τ = 0.2 s; braking
  deceleration a = 2.0 m/s². Its stopping distance is d = v·τ + v²/(2a) = 0.20 + 0.25 = 0.45 m. A static obstacle
  visible from the aisle's start (10 m away) is never struck. An actor that emerges from a cross aisle into the
  path 0.40 m ahead is struck; one that emerges 0.50 m ahead is not. This class needs motion: (c).
- *Occlusion by a moving body.* A static proxy occludes a fixed part of the field of view for the whole episode;
  an actor walking beside the robot occludes a changing part, so a landmark is lost at a time the static proxy
  cannot reproduce. Whether the hazard registry has a class this provokes decides the mark.
- *Closing speed.* An actor approaching at 1.5 m/s head-on raises the closing speed to 2.5 m/s; the distance
  covered during the latency alone is 0.50 m. A static proxy has closing speed 1.0 m/s.
- *Classes that do not need motion.* Rack strikes, load sway on a ramp, charge consumed: marks (a) or (b).

**Falsifier.** The list of classes marked (c) is empty for the archetype (both classifiers agreeing).

**Ceiling, stated before the work.** No gain in decision time; the benefit is at most the number of failure
classes in column (c).

**Fallback and kill gate.** Structural proxies and static clutter only; KILL-DRESSING.

**Outputs.** The tables (`docs/pilots/actors-hazard-classes.md`), the count of (c) rows, the disagreements, the
verdict and the recommendation; one record in the pilot register `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`
(schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`, N38a-09; tier `F`; fallback `{ ships = "structural proxies and static clutter
only", kill_gate = "KILL-DRESSING" }`), mechanism `dressing.actors` (the second of the two
entries of the dressing pilot; the first, `dressing.assets`, is N19a-40's, whose table this record cites). The
verdict uses the same two values in both entries: `falsifier-holds` and `falsifier-rejected`. Evidence class `Estimated` (a reasoned
classification with hand-checked cases), and it is labelled as such.

- [ ] Two independent classifications exist and their disagreements are listed and resolved in writing.

      without one, and the differences from the scene pilot's marks are listed; the register holds exactly one
      verdict for actors, in `dressing.actors`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/pilot_actor_classes.rs` (exact rational arithmetic; no simulator):
- `stopping_distance`: v = 1, τ = 1/5, a = 2 gives d = 9/20 m.
- `crossing_actor_struck_at_040_not_050`: an obstacle appearing at 2/5 m is inside d; at 1/2 m outside.
- `static_obstacle_never_struck`: visible at 10 m, the robot stops with 9.55 m to spare.
- `closing_speed_latency_distance`: (1 + 3/2) × 1/5 = 1/2 m.
- `table_complete`: every class in the registry's list for the archetype has a row; no row lacks marks.
- `c_mark_requires_case`: a row marked (c) with no case is rejected.
- `scene_pilot_candidates_all_examined`: every class of the list L_K attached to `dressing.assets` appears in
  the third table; a candidate left out fails the completeness check.
- `one_actor_verdict`: the register has a verdict for actors in `dressing.actors` only.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/pilot_actor_classes.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-32 milestone=M6 labels=pilot,habitat
gates=GATE-GAUNT-08 plants=unjustified-mark`). It registers the protocol, validates the two tables against the
hazard registry (read with `frob scene hazards warehouse --json` and `frob scene hazards retail-store --json`,
the registry form of `scene.hazards`, which lands at M3), computes the count of (c) rows and the disagreements, and
appends the result; `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test pilot_actor_classes` runs as its own step. The plant
adds a (c) mark with no case, which the validation must reject. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E); log at
`target/e2e-logs/pilot_actor_classes/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code and wall time; `metric` lines for classes total, classes marked
(c) (the falsifier's threshold `> 0`), disagreements, the protocol's BLAKE3; the negative control as an `assert`
with `kind: "negative-control"`; on failure the first row that fails validation and the table path; and the
`summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: d = 0.45 m; struck at 0.40 m and not at 0.50 m; 0.50 m of travel during the latency at a closing
speed of 2.5 m/s. Negative controls: the unjustified mark; a hazard class deleted from the table (the
completeness check fails). Determinism lane: the arithmetic is class D0.

## Considerations

- The hazard dimensions (heights, speeds) are recorded as `assumed`; the pilot's cases use round numbers of its
  own and say so.
- A non-empty list funds a mechanism that still must pass the kill gate's cost and determinism clauses.
- The classification is about what a scene can provoke in the robot, never about what people do.
