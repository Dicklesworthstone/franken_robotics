## Background

This is the mechanism of the epic "moving people as kinematic actors" in FrankenRobotics: capsules that move
along a scene's route graph on a script, standing in for people, pets, forklifts and carts. It is Frontier-tier,
lives behind the feature `frontier-actors` (off by default) and is started only if its pilot (N38a-32) found at
least one failure class that needs a moving obstacle, and the dressing kill gate did not fire for actors.

The scene model already distinguishes how each movable object behaves (static; prescribed; articulated; free),
one behaviour per object, recorded in the episode manifest. An actor is a *prescribed* object: its pose is a
function of time, nothing in the simulation acts on it, and it acts on the robot only through contact and
through what the robot's sensors see. Actors are scenario dressing. No claim about human behaviour is made from
them, because it could not be checked: the "behaviour" is the script.

Rejected alternatives: reactive agents with their own policies (unverifiable, and a second system under test);
recorded human trajectories (privacy, licences, and no way to state what they generalize to).

## Scope

In: the actor declaration; how a generated archetype scene receives its actors (`scene.gen --actors`); route
following; the speed profile and yielding rule; collision and sensing against actors; batching and
determinism; the label and the refusals; the cost measurement. Out: the pilot; populated
static interiors; free carts and other bodies the robot can push; any metric about the actor.

## Technical approach

**Declaration (in the scene manifest, DESIGN).**
```
actor { id, kind: person | pet | forklift | cart,        // a label for hazard lists only
        capsule { radius_m, height_m },                   // pet 0.2–0.6 m tall; others from the hazard list
        route: [node ids of the scene's route graph], loop: bool,
        speed { cruise_m_s, accel_m_s2 },                 // trapezoidal profile along each edge
        start { phase_s },                                // offset; a function of the world address when randomized
        yield { rule: never | stop_within, distance_m, resume_after_s },
        behaviour: "prescribed" }
```
**Actors on a generated scene (DESIGN: the flag `--actors` of `scene.gen`; added by this bead and requested of
the verb registry VERBS-scene).** A hand-written or fixture scene declares its actors in its source, as above.
For an interior family (`warehouse`, `kitchen-commercial/galley`, `home`, `apartment`, `office`, `plant`,
`retail`), `frob scene gen --family <f> … --seed <s> --actors` makes the generator add the archetype's actor
declarations to the scene it would otherwise produce: the kinds and capsule dimensions of the archetype's
hazard registry (shoppers and carts, a pet, a forklift, as the archetype lists them), routes drawn on the
scene's own route graph, and phases, up to the scene's actor capacity. Every actor draw comes from registered
streams of its own, keyed by the scene's (family, parameters, seed), and the generator reads them after the
scene is otherwise complete, so the geometry, the route graph, the fixtures and every other draw are identical
with and without the flag (of the five aspect digests of a pack, N19b-44, `geometry`, `material`, `optics` and
`clocks` are equal and `actors` differs): the scene with `--actors` is the proxy scene of the same seed with
actors added,
which is the pairing the M6 reading of KILL-DRESSING needs (N19ax-05 compares actor scenes with the proxy
scenes of the same seeds). The two scenes have different `SceneId`s and one split group (an actor variant is a
variant of the same building). Rules: the flag needs the feature `frontier-actors`; without it, or after
KILL-DRESSING fires for actors, it is refused with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5 (as `--populated` is for assets); on a
`terrain/…` family it is `FROB-REF-802` (terrain has no route graph of an archetype); it combines with
`--seeds` and `--address`, and with `--populated`. Rejected: an `add-actor` kind of the typed scene edits
(N19b-44), because an edit list names route nodes of one compiled pack and would have to be written per seed,
while a battery pairs hundreds of seeds; the edit set stays closed.

**Motion.**
1. The route is a polyline through route-graph nodes; arc length s(t) follows a trapezoidal speed profile per
   edge (accelerate at `accel` to `cruise`, decelerate to rest at the route's end or continue through
   intermediate nodes).
2. `yield: never`: the pose at time t is a closed-form function of (address, t).
3. `yield: stop_within`: the actor decelerates at `accel` when the robot's base is within `distance_m` along its
   path ahead and resumes `resume_after_s` after the robot leaves. This makes the pose depend on the robot's
   past positions; it is evaluated inside the step from the world's own state only, at the physics tick, so it
   remains a function of the world's own history.
4. Time is the rational clock of the step; arc length is accumulated in the same exact schedule as other
   prescribed objects, so a checkpoint at any instant reproduces the actor's later poses bit for bit.

**Interaction.** Collision: the capsule is a kinematic primitive (prescribed pose and velocity, infinite
effective mass); robot–actor contact uses the ordinary contact law with the actor's interface card; an actor is
never pushed and never falls. Sensing: rays and depth tests hit the capsule analytically; semantic labels report
its `kind`. Actor–actor and actor–scene contact are not simulated; routes are validated at scene compile to
stay inside the route corridor.

**Batching.** A scene declares an actor capacity (default 8); unused slots are padded with capsules of zero
radius at a far anchor, so the launch shape of a batch is fixed. Randomized phases and route choices are
functions of the world address.

**Labels and refusals.**
- Every result from a scene with at least one active actor carries the condition flag `actor-scripted`; the flag
  joins by union and cannot be dropped by a consumer.
- Telemetry may record robot-centred quantities involving actors: minimum clearance, time to contact, contact
  events, whether the robot stopped.
- A requirement atom, metric, scorecard column or report sentence whose subject is an actor (its delay, comfort,
  path, "reaction rate") is refused at registration. The plan gives this refusal no number, so it is an uncoded
  slug (DESIGN): `actor-subject-claim`, family `3xx` (scene), exit 3, retry `after-change`, `pointer` to the
  atom, metric or column and the actor's `id`; meaning "a claim about a scripted actor was requested"; ranked
  repairs (1) state the claim about the robot under the declared script (minimum clearance, time to contact,
  contact events, whether the robot stopped), (2) drop the actor from the subject. Its meaning, message and
  repairs are listed by slug in the Habitat registry (N19-REF); its row is row 10 of the decision N19c-42, which
  requests the number from the allocation ledger (`registries/refusal_allocations.toml`, N35-33). This bead
  raises it only through the constant `ACTOR_SUBJECT_CLAIM` of `crates/frob-scene/src/refusal/late.rs` (N19c-42's
  one place for these codes), never a number of its own and never a generic error; until the number is assigned
  the constant carries the ledger's interim form.
- **Capability not available.** With the feature `frontier-actors` off (the default) or with KILL-DRESSING's
  decision for actors `fired` (N19ax-05), the declaration is still parsed (so a manifest stays readable and the
  refusal is by name, not an unknown field), and `scene.compile` or `scene.gen` of a manifest with an `actor`
  refuses with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5, naming the capability, its status (`not-in-build` or `killed`) and, when
  killed, the fallback and the ADR (the dispatch rule of N00-02, as `scene.gen --populated` after the M5 reading).
- An actor's `kind` never changes physics; it selects only the hazard-list dimensions used to draw the capsule.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] An actor follows its route with the declared profile; its pose at any time equals the closed form (rule
      `never`) to the tier's arithmetic.
- [ ] The stopping-distance known answer holds through the simulator on the referee and the CPU batch tier.
- [ ] A world with actors has the same digests alone and in any batch; padding actors change no digest; the
      launch shape is unchanged by the number of active actors.
- [ ] `scene.gen --actors` on an interior family yields the proxy scene of the same seed with actors added: equal
      `geometry`, `material`, `optics` and `clocks` digests and route graph, a different `actors` digest, at
      most the actor capacity of actors, a different `SceneId`, the same split group; on a terrain family it is
      `FROB-REF-802`; on a default build it is `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5.
- [ ] Checkpoint, restore and fork reproduce actor poses and all later observations bit for bit.
- [ ] Every result from a scene with actors prints `actor-scripted`; an actor-subject metric is refused with
      `actor-subject-claim` (exit 3, pointer to the metric and the actor; interim form until numbered).
- [ ] Step cost with eight actors is at most 1.25 times the cost with none on the reference workload (paired
      upper bound), or the kill gate's clause is recorded.
- [ ] The feature is off by default; the crate's contract states the mechanism, its label and its gaps; a default
      build refuses a manifest with an `actor` with `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-scene/tests/actors.rs`:
- `trapezoid_profile`: cruise 1.0 m/s, accel 0.5 m/s², edge 10 m: 2 s and 1 m to reach cruise, 8 m at cruise
  takes 8 s, 2 s and 1 m to stop: 12 s in total; position at t = 3 s is 2.0 m.
- `pose_is_function_of_address_and_time`: evaluating at t directly equals stepping to t.
- `yield_stop_within`: with distance 1.0 m the actor is at rest while the robot is inside and resumes 0.5 s
  after it leaves.
- `stopping_distance_known_answer`: robot at 1.0 m/s, latency 0.2 s, deceleration 2.0 m/s²; an actor entering
  0.40 m ahead yields a contact event; 0.50 m ahead yields none and a minimum clearance of 0.05 m.
- `padding_actor_neutral`: adding seven padded slots changes no digest.
- `actor_subject_metric_refused`: a scorecard column "actor delay" is refused with `actor-subject-claim`, exit 3,
  its pointer naming the column and the actor, and the first repair naming the robot-centred quantities;
  "minimum clearance to actor 1" is accepted.
- `flag_propagates`: a scorecard computed from an actor scene carries `actor-scripted`.
- `route_outside_corridor_refused_at_compile`.
- `gen_actors_same_seed_same_building`: `warehouse` at seed 7 with and without `--actors`: the `geometry`,
  `material`, `optics` and `clocks` digests and the route graph are equal and the `actors` digest differs, the
  actor scene has between 1 and 8 actors whose kinds are in the archetype's
  hazard registry, the two `SceneId`s differ and the split group is one; a second generation with `--actors`
  gives the same `SceneId`; `terrain/stairs --actors` is `FROB-REF-802`.
- `actor_refused_without_feature`: a default build compiling a manifest with one actor → `FROB-REF-724`, exit 5,
  status `not-in-build`; with the decision `fired` in a scratch register → status `killed`, the fallback named.
- `refusal_through_constant`: the refusal's code equals `ACTOR_SUBJECT_CLAIM`, and a source scan of `frob-scene`
  finds no other spelling of the slug or its number.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/scene_actors.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-33 milestone=M6 labels=habitat,actors gates=KILL-DRESSING
seed=5 needs=cpu plants=actor-subject-metric,arrival-order-phase`), on a binary built with `--features
frontier-actors`, plus one step on a default binary. Fixtures (`synthetic`, `assets/fixtures/actors/`): a warehouse
aisle with one crossing actor whose phase puts it 0.40 m (`aisle-x-040`) or 0.50 m (`aisle-x-050`) ahead of the
robot at detection, and the conformance fixture controller `fixtures/controllers/stop-on-detect.toml` (cruise
1.0 m/s, latency 0.2 s, deceleration 2.0 m/s²; never a product controller). Steps:
1. `compile`: `frob design new dd --template diff-drive --key act-d1 --json` (the wheeled reference robot);
   `frob scene compile assets/fixtures/actors/aisle-x-040 --name aisle-x-040 --key act-c1 --json` and the same for
   `aisle-x-050` (`act-c2`).
2. `stopping`: `frob sim run dd --scene aisle-x-040 --tier referee --seconds 6 --seed 5 --controller
   fixture:stop-on-detect --key act-r1 --json`, the 0.50 m case (`act-r2`), and both on `--tier batch-cpu`
   (`act-b1`, `act-b2`); `frob telemetry query <run> --stream contact --json` for contact events and minimum
   clearance.
3. `flag`: the evidence block of every run carries `actor-scripted`.
4. `refusal`: `e2e_expect_refusal <ACTOR_SUBJECT_CLAIM's code> 3 --` registering the scorecard column "actor
   delay"; the column "minimum clearance to actor 1" is admitted.
5. `independence` and `checkpoint`: the 0.40 m world alone and in a batch of 64 (`act-b3`), and restored from a
   checkpoint at t = 2 s (`act-b4`), compared with `frob telemetry divergence`.
5a. `generated`: `frob scene gen --family warehouse --seed 5 --key act-g1 --json` and the same with `--actors`

   `material`, `optics` and `clocks` digests, a different `actors` digest, an actor count between 1 and the
   capacity, different `SceneId`s and one split group.
6. `cost`: the paired step-cost ratio with eight actors against none through the performance harness (BD-216).
7. `default_binary`: `e2e_expect_refusal FROB-REF-724 5 -- frob scene compile assets/fixtures/actors/aisle-x-040
   --name aisle-x-def --key act-c3 --json`, and the same refusal for `frob scene gen --family warehouse --seed 5
   --actors --key act-g3 --json`.
The second plant draws actor phases in arrival order instead of from the address, which step 5 must catch. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/scene_actors/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`,
`event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block with the condition flags;
`metric` lines for clearance (0.05 m expected at 0.50 m), contact events (1 at 0.40 m, 0 at 0.50 m), digest

digest or event with its run identifier; and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: d = v·τ + v²/(2a) = 0.45 m; struck at 0.40 m, clear by 0.05 m at 0.50 m; the trapezoid timings.
Negative controls: the actor-subject metric; arrival-order phases; an actor omitted from the checkpoint.
Metamorphic relations: splitting a rollout at a snapshot equals running it whole; permuting world indices
permutes results; the mirrored scene with the mirrored route returns the mirrored result. Determinism lanes:
twice; worker counts; capacities 32, 64, 128; each tier against its class.

## Considerations

- Kill gate named in advance: KILL-DRESSING (trigger: a broken launch shape or determinism class, more than 25%
  step cost, or no failure class beyond the proxies; fallback: structural proxies and static clutter only).
- The yielding rule is a script, not a model of people; the design deliberately offers only two rules.
- A robot can be trapped by a never-yielding actor on a narrow route; such episodes are counted as failures of
  the robot under that script and reported with the flag, never excluded.
- Capsule dimensions and speeds come from hazard lists whose provenance is `assumed`.
