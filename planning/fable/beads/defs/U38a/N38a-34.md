## Background

In the ten-fold tournament of FrankenRobotics an answer counts only when a neutral adjudicator accepts it. The
epic that builds adjudication (BD-432) has, as part of its done-condition, a pilot: a dry run in which a planted
false acceptance is caught. A dry run is the cheapest place to find that the acceptance path has a hole: an
answer that should fail and passes, a receipt that did not come from the adjudicator, a certificate that its own
checker accepts and that is false.

The dry run uses development missions only (the published pool), fresh sessions in the three roles (builder,
rival advocate, adjudicator; one role per session), and planted answers whose correct classification is known
because they were constructed. Nothing sealed is opened, so the dry run can be repeated.

## Scope

In: the frozen dry-run protocol; the planted-answer set; the acceptance cycle; the planted false acceptances and
their detection; the record. Out: the tournament itself; the sealed suite; the decision rule; publication.

## Technical approach

**Protocol (frozen and hashed before the run).**
1. *Missions.* For each stratum that has a product at the dry run's date, two development mission families;
   at minimum the docking family (a family-answer mission with an exact boundary) and one qualified-design
   mission on the reference quadruped.
2. *Planted answers.* Twenty per stratum: ten that must pass and ten that must fail, covering every answer kind
   the stratum uses. The must-fail set includes, by construction:
   a. a family answer whose inner set includes a boundary cell (for docking: the cell (U, ε) = (¾, 5/16), where
      the strict inequality ε < (U + ½)/4 fails at equality);
   b. a family answer correct for the declared worlds and submitted for an amended mission (a third hidden gain
      33/32 added), where every positive label reverses;
   c. a certificate that its declared checker accepts and that direct evaluation at a sealed probe point
      contradicts (a false certificate);
   d. a qualified design that passes in its home simulator and fails the acceptance battery in the adjudicating
      simulator;
   e. an amendment verdict that keeps a verdict the ground truth withdraws;
   f. a "truthful unknown" on a mission whose question is not identifiability;
   g. a counterexample world outside the legality bounds;
   h. a vacuous enclosure (inner set empty, outer set everything);
   i. a correct answer submitted after the deadline;
   j. a resubmission that reuses the world clusters of an earlier rejected submission.
3. *Cycle.* The adjudicating session commits its key and the protocol hash; arms submit; each submission is
   committed before any world is drawn; fresh clusters are drawn; the i-th submission on a mission is tested at
   level α_i = 0.05/[i·(i + 1)]; receipts carry only the decision, the index, the level spent, the units used and
   a hash; `frob-verify` replays every certificate; the adjudicator evaluates family answers directly at probe
   points; results are released after both arms close.
4. *Planted false acceptances (the pilot's negative controls).*
   - An arm-issued receipt: a receipt file written by an arm's session with a valid structure and a hash, not
     signed under the adjudicator's committed key. It must be refused wherever a receipt is consumed (the clock
     does not stop; the scorer rejects it).
   - A lenient acceptance path: a build of the acceptance code in which the lower confidence bound is replaced
     by the point estimate. The planted answer (d) then passes; the audit that recomputes every decision from
     the per-world outcomes with the exact bound must flag the acceptance as false.
   - A role breach: one session identity appearing as both builder and adjudicator in the logs.
5. *Measurements.* Classification of the twenty planted answers per stratum; detection of each planted false
   acceptance; discordance between the adjudicating simulator and each arm's home referee on the accepted
   answers; time spent in the `adjudicate` phase per submission.

**Falsifier (any one holds).** Fewer than 20 of 20 planted answers classified correctly in a stratum. Or any
planted false acceptance not caught. Or an acceptance receipt that does not verify under the adjudicator's key.

**Ceiling.** None on speed: adjudication adds to both arms' clocks equally (`adjudicate` is a phase of the
ledger); the benefit is that the word "accepted" means the same for both arms.

**Fallback.** Cross-home adjudication, labelled; with no neutral party, the label `self-adjudicated` and no use
of the words "independently accepted" or "ten-fold" until an external party has re-run the replication kit.

**Outputs.** One pilot record in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`;
`fallback.kill_gate` KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ, `fallback.ships` the labelled cross-home or self-adjudicated route above)
with counts, detections, discordance and verdict; the list of holes found, each
with the change that closes it. Evidence class `Tested`, labelled `synthetic` (planted answers).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The protocol's hash and registration precede the first submission of the dry run.
- [ ] Per stratum, 20 of 20 planted answers are classified correctly, or each misclassification is recorded as
      a hole with its fix and the dry run is repeated.
- [ ] Each of the three planted false acceptances is caught and named (the epic's seed done-condition).
- [ ] Every acceptance receipt verifies under the adjudicator's committed key; the arm-issued receipt does not.
- [ ] Receipts of rejected submissions contain no per-world outcome.
- [ ] The level spent on submission i equals 0.05/[i·(i + 1)] exactly, and reused clusters are refused.
- [ ] The record states the adjudication label in force (`neutral`, `cross-home` or `self-adjudicated`).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/adjudication_dry_run.rs` (the real acceptance code on real development missions;
planted inputs are labelled `synthetic`):
- `level_schedule`: α₁ = 1/40, α₂ = 1/120, α₃ = 1/240, α₁₀ = 1/2,200; Σ over i = 1…10 equals 1/22.
- `boundary_cell_rejected`: the docking answer that includes (¾, 5/16) is rejected; the same answer without it
  is accepted.
- `amended_mission_reverses`: under the third gain 33/32 the unamended family answer is rejected.
- `false_certificate_disqualifies`: answer (c) leads to `disqualified` for the proof kind in that cell.
- `lower_bound_not_point_estimate`: 19 passes of 20 clusters against a threshold of 0.90 is rejected (exact
  one-sided 95% lower bound 0.784) although the point estimate is 0.95.
- `arm_issued_receipt_refused`.
- `rejected_receipt_has_no_outcomes`.
- `reused_clusters_refused`.
- `role_breach_detected`.
- `late_correct_answer_is_missed_deadline`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/adjudication_dry_run.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-34 milestone=M7 labels=tenfold,pilot,gate
gates=GATE-TENFOLD-05 plants=arm-issued-receipt,lenient-acceptance,role-breach timeout_s=3600`). It starts the
three role sessions on scratch roots, checks the registration row of the dry run in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` (read-only; its protocol hash and timestamp precede the first
submission), runs the cycle on the planted set through the public verbs (`frob eval qualify <answer> --task
<mission> --claim <claim> --key adr-<stratum>-<answer>-<i> --json`, a run verb with a key distinct per submission
index, so a resubmission is a new request; `frob prove verify <bundle>… --json` and `frob-verify` on every
certificate), collects receipts, runs the audit (`frob-tenfold audit --gate <the dry run's scratch gate id> --phase
pre-score` on the scratch root, N39b-23, reading its `audit.json`: INV-TENFOLD-01 catches the role breach) and the
recomputation of every decision from the per-world outcomes with the exact bound (which catches the lenient
acceptance), runs
`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test adjudication_dry_run`, and the three plants. Log at `target/e2e-logs/adjudication_dry_run/<utc>.ndjson`: every command with exit code and wall time;
evidence blocks; per submission a `metric` for the level spent with its exact expected value; one `assert` per
planted answer (expected class, observed class); `refusal` lines for every rejected submission and refused
receipt; discordance per arm as `metric` lines; the three negative-control assertions; the `summary` naming
GATE-TENFOLD-05. The log contains no sealed material, because none is opened.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the docking family ε < (U + ½)/4 with equality excluded (344 admitted, 217 excluded, nine
boundary cells of 561); the three-gain reversal (best worst-case error 1/65 m); α_i = α/[i·(i + 1)] summing to
α·m/(m + 1); the exact lower bound for 19 of 20 (0.784). Negative controls: the arm-issued receipt; the lenient
acceptance; the role breach; a canary planted in a development "sealed-like" world appearing in a transcript.
Determinism lane: classification of the planted set twice gives identical decisions for the exact kinds; the
statistical kind uses fresh clusters and is judged on its bound.

## Considerations

- The dry run is a rehearsal; it measures the acceptance path, not either arm, and no ratio is computed from it.
- Using the adjudicating simulator in an arm's development or tuning is disqualifying in the tournament; the dry
  run's arms use development missions only, and the adjudicating simulator is touched only by the adjudicating
  session.
- If no neutral simulator classifies 20 of 20 for a stratum, the kill gate's fallback (cross-home adjudication)
  is recorded for that stratum before the sealed suite is opened.
