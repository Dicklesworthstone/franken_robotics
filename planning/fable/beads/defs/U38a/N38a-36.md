## Background

FrankenRobotics contains many analyses and screens that pass judgement on robot designs: static analyses
(actuator sizing, stability, reach), cheap evaluation rungs that discard candidates, attribution of a failure to
a cause. An analysis that never fires looks exactly like an analysis applied to good designs. The program's rule
for experiments is that each has a *potency control*: a planted effect of declared size that the method must
detect. For analyses and screens the planted effects live in the *design corpus*: about 200 designs (TARGET) made
of templates, parameter perturbations of them, and designs with planted defects of known kind (an undersized
knee actuator, an inertia that breaks static stability, a wheelbase too long for an aisle), together with
planted winners for the screening audit and planted single causes for attribution. The corpus itself is built by
the studio subsystem (BD-248). The Gauntlet (the verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`) owns the rule
that makes it a control: labels are fixed before any analysis is tuned, and the corpus is the potency control of
every analysis and screen.

The order matters. If labels could be revised after an analysis has been run on the corpus, the corpus would
drift toward what the analyses already detect, and its detection rates would mean nothing. Rejected alternative:
per-analysis ad hoc test designs chosen by the analysis's author (the author plants what the analysis finds).

## Scope

In: the label freeze and its proof; the potency record per analysis and screen; the rule that an unproven
analysis cannot be cited; the split between tuning and held-out parts of the corpus. Out: building the corpus
(BD-248); the analyses themselves; the screening audit's statistics and the attribution method (`needs`).

## Technical approach

1. **Label freeze.** The corpus manifest lists each design's identity, its kind (`template`, `perturbation`,
   `planted-defect`, `planted-winner`, `planted-cause`) and, for planted items, the label (defect kind and the
   quantity that reveals it; the winner's true rank; the single cause). The manifest's BLAKE3 hash is written to
   the ledger as `corpus.freeze` before the first analysis result on the corpus exists. The check compares the
   ledger sequence numbers: any analysis result on a corpus design that precedes the freeze voids the corpus
   version.
2. **Label changes.** A label is never edited. A wrong label is corrected by a new corpus version with a new
   hash and a recorded reason; potency records made under the old version are marked stale.
3. **Split.** Planted items are divided by a hash of their identity into a tuning half and a held-out half
   (one in two). Analyses may be developed against the tuning half; a potency record counts only the held-out
   half.
4. **Potency record (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/potency.toml`).** Per analysis or screen:
   `id`, `kind` (`analysis`, `screen`, `attribution`), `corpus_version`, `defect_kinds` it claims to detect,
   `declared_rate` (the detection rate it promises, with its exact binomial lower bound), per kind
   `planted_n`, `detected`, `false_alarms` on the unplanted designs (`template`, `perturbation`), and the verdict
   `potent` or `not-potent`.
5. **Rule.** An analysis is `potent` for a defect kind when the one-sided 95% exact binomial lower bound on its
   detection rate over the held-out planted designs of that kind is at least its declared rate, and its false
   alarm rate on unplanted designs is at most its declared ceiling. A screen is `potent` when, in addition, the
   screening audit reports the removal of a planted winner by a deliberately biased variant of the screen and
   passes the unbiased screen. An attribution method is `potent` when the planted single cause is ranked first
   on at least the declared share of planted-cause designs (the owning gate asks for 90% of 200 planted causes).
6. **Consequence.** A gate row, a report or a method experiment that cites the output of an analysis or screen
   with no current `potent` record for the relevant kind is refused by the recorder (`not-potent` or
   `no potency record`). This is the potency part of the method-experiment form.
7. **Live-dependency proof.** For each `potent` analysis, one planted design is repaired (the knee actuator
   replaced by an adequate one) and the analysis must stop firing; an analysis that still fires depends on
   something other than the defect.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The freeze event precedes every analysis result on the corpus; a planted earlier result voids the version.
- [ ] An edited label is rejected; a corrected label creates a new version and stales old records.
- [ ] Potency records exist for every analysis and screen that a gate cites at M3; each uses only held-out
      planted designs.
- [ ] A planted `not-potent` analysis (one that never fires) is refused wherever it is cited.
- [ ] The repaired-design check stops each potent analysis from firing.
- [ ] Thirty clean audited discards bound the false-discard rate of a screen at 9.5% (1 − 0.05^(1/30)), and the
      record prints that bound.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/potency.rs`:
- `freeze_precedes_results`; `result_before_freeze_voids`.
- `label_edit_rejected`; `new_version_stales_records`.
- `split_is_hash_based`: a design's half does not change when designs are added.
- `potent_by_lower_bound`: 20 of 20 held-out detections give a lower bound of 0.861 (0.05^(1/20)), which meets
  a declared 0.85 and misses a declared 0.90; 18 of 20 gives 0.717.
- `never_fires_not_potent`: 0 of 20.
- `false_alarm_ceiling`: 3 alarms on 100 unplanted designs against a ceiling of 2% is `not-potent`.
- `zero_of_thirty_bound`: 1 − 0.05^(1/30) = 0.0950.
- `citation_refused_without_record`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/corpus_potency.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-36 milestone=M3 labels=gauntlet,corpus
gates=GATE-GAUNT-09 plants=silent-analysis,label-edit`). It verifies the freeze order in the ledger, runs
`frob design check --json` over the held-out planted designs of one defect kind and over the unplanted designs,
writes the potency record, repairs one design and re-checks, then plants `silent-analysis` (an analysis build
that returns no findings) and `label-edit`. Log at `target/e2e-logs/corpus_potency/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with
exit codes and wall time, the envelope's evidence block, detection and false-alarm counts as `metric` lines with
their bounds and thresholds, the freeze hash as a digest, both negative controls and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the exact binomial bounds 0.05^(1/20) = 0.861 and 1 − 0.05^(1/30) = 0.0950; with 40 designs and
the true best at cheap rank 3, a cut to two discards it although the top-ten rank correlation is 0.91 (why a
planted winner is audited). Negative controls: the analysis that never fires; the edited label; a potency
record computed on the tuning half. Determinism lane: static analyses are class D0; their results on the corpus
are identical twice and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The corpus is small; detection rates carry wide bounds, and the record prints the bound, not the point value.
- Behaviour, not plumbing: for learned screens the controls include zero-weight, random and shuffled-label
  policies, which must fail the same threshold.
- Synthetic is labelled: every corpus result prints `synthetic` in its evidence.
