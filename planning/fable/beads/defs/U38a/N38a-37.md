## Background

FrankenRobotics grades every mechanism Solid, Frontier or Moonshot. A Frontier or Moonshot mechanism must have,
before its work is funded, a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier and a *kill gate*: a trigger with a number, the
milestone at which it is decided, the measurement that decides it, and a fallback of a lower tier that the design
already contains. The gate register (`registries/gates.toml`) is the single place where a kill gate exists; a
trigger, a threshold or a fallback that is not in it cannot be decided, and one that is in it changes only by a
*dated amendment* made before the measurement it governs. The pilot register's funding rule (N38a-09, rule 3)
funds a pilot record only when its `fallback.kill_gate` names a row of the gate register; a record that says
`to-register` blocks every bead it gates with `kill-gate-to-register`.

Three upper-tier mechanisms have a fallback in the plan and no kill gate in the register as it was compiled:

- **Persistent ruts and footprints** (Moonshot; the M4 rut field N18b-17 on `batch-cpu`, and the M6 epic "soft
  soil at scale", BD-417, which carries the field to the batch tiers with N38a-29). The plan's Frontier and
  Moonshot table of the traction chapter gives the pilot (the leak fixture on `batch-cpu`, N18b-16) and the
  fallback (off; terrain resets each episode) and names no gate. The soil laws have GATE-TRAC-08 and KILL-SOIL,
  both decided at M4; nothing decides whether *persistent* deformation is kept.
- **Tactile micro-patch pads** (Moonshot; epic BD-418, mechanism N38a-31, pilot N18b-35): a fingertip pad as
  2 × 2 or 4 × 4 four-dimensional contacts. The epic's seed done-condition says that a kill gate and its fallback
  are written before any work package is funded.
- **Tactile arrays** (Frontier; mechanism N20a-19, pilot N20a-18): finite taxel footprints with normal and shear
  saturation. The sensing chapter says that every Frontier sensor model of its list is funded only for a
  registered task, names one analytic fixture as its pilot, and "falls back to the mask-only model under the same
  cost rule" as exposure integration (more than 25% added cost of the stage it joins). That fallback is not the
  pads' fallback, so it cannot be a clause of the pads' row (a row has one fallback).

This bead enters the three rows by the register's own amendment procedure, so that the mechanism beads (N18b-17,
N38a-29, N38a-31, N20a-19) can be funded and the decision beads (N38a-38 for KILL-RUT, N38a-39 for KILL-TACTILE,
N38ap3-01 for KILL-TAXEL) have a registered trigger to evaluate. DESIGN: registration is separated from decision
because the mechanism must not start before the row exists, and the decision reads measurements the mechanism
produces; one bead for both would need itself. DESIGN, milestone M4: the rut pilot N18b-16 gates the M4 rut field
N18b-17 as well as the M6 batch-scale bead, so KILL-RUT must be in the register at M4; an M6 entry would leave the
M4 bead unfundable until M6. The two tactile rows are entered in the same commit: an early entry funds nothing,
and it lets the M6 pilots N18b-35 and N20a-18 name their gate directly at registration. DESIGN, no dependency on
a pilot: the rows are registry data whose wording is fixed by the pilots' stated rules, so this bead depends only
on the register and waits for no pilot (an earlier form waited for N18b-16 to close; that pilot can stay open
until the M6 exit candidate while the rut epic awaits a slot under the Moonshot cap, and the tactile rows would
have waited with it). Rejected: leaving the wording in the epics' prose (a kill rule that lives outside the
register is not read by the exit query and can be reworded after the data are in); one row per epic for the
tactile pair (the pads and the arrays have different tiers, pilots and fallbacks, and either can be kept without
the other).

The names KILL-RUT, KILL-TACTILE and KILL-TAXEL are DESIGN: the plan names the fallbacks, not the gates.

## Scope

In: the three kill rows in the kill-row form of N40-03; the three dated `introduce` amendments; the update of any
pilot record that already says `to-register` to the registered identifier; the checks below. Out: the pilots
(N18b-16, the rut leak fixture, run at M4 with the soil work; N18b-35, the tactile micro-patch pilot at M6;
N20a-18, the tactile-array pilot at M6; the M6 drafts N38a-28 and N38a-30 are superseded by N18b-16 and N18b-35);
the mechanisms (N18b-17, N38a-29, N38a-31, N20a-19); evaluating the triggers and recording the outcomes (N38a-38
for KILL-RUT; N38a-39 for KILL-TACTILE; N38ap3-01 for KILL-TAXEL); the kill-row schema, the amendment record, its
ordering check and the scope-kind table (N40-03); the fallback ledger of late kill gates (N40-29), which gains
three lines generated from these rows.

## Technical approach

**The kill-row form (N40-03, step 1).** A kill row carries `id`, `kind = "kill"`, `section` (the owning
subsection, without the § sign), `consumers` (the subsections that state the same gate from their side),
`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads` and `decision`, plus `scope_kind` on a row decided per scope. It
carries none of a gate's measurement fields (`claim`, `claim_id`, `threshold`, `tier`, `controls`, a
`[[gate.part]]`): a kill gate measures nothing, and what it decides is read from the gates in `reads`, which carry
their own claims, thresholds and controls; a kill row with one of those fields fails the load naming it. The
measuring beads, the deciding bead and the negative control are therefore not row fields: they are named below and
in each decision record. (An earlier draft of this bead gave the rows `claim`, `measured_by`, `decided_by` and
`control`; withdrawn, since the load now refuses them.) Until a decision is in force a row's derived state is
`not-run`.

**Row 1 (DESIGN wording, from the epic's short plan and the pilot's frozen rule).**
```toml
[[gate]]
id         = "KILL-RUT"
kind       = "kill"
section    = "18.17"                    # the row "Persistent ruts" of the Frontier and Moonshot table
consumers  = ["18.10", "45.7"]          # terrain mechanics; the epic "soft soil at scale" (BD-417)
trigger    = "the pilot's falsifier holds (N18b-16: leak count above zero, or a marked world's digest differs from its solo run, or a planted leak goes undetected); or the pilot's funding flags stand (memory above 256 KiB per world on its fixture, or the paired lower bound of its step-cost ratio above 1.25); or at batch scale any leak in 10^3 resets on a backend the epic claims; or the rut buffers break the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch; or the paired lower bound of the step-cost ratio with ruts on against ruts off exceeds 1.25 on the reference tracked workload"
fallback   = "persistent deformation off: terrain resets each episode and sinkage is computed per step from the soil law with no memory"
decided    = "M6"
mode       = "exit"
reads      = ["GATE-TRAC-08"]           # its rut-leak part as N38a-29 measures it at batch scale at M6
scope_kind = "backend"                  # decided per tier: backend:batch-cpu, backend:gpu-cuda, backend:gpu-metal
decision   = { outcome = "", adr = "", date = "", session = "", receipts = [] }
```
Measured by N18b-16 (the pilot's verdict and funding flags) and N38a-29 (the batch-scale leak per backend, launch
shape, class and cost); decided by N38a-38; negative control of the leak measurement, registered with GATE-TRAC-08 by its measuring
beads: a shared (not per-world) rut buffer, which the leak fixture must catch.

**Row 2 (DESIGN wording, from the epic's short plan and the pilot's registered rule).**
```toml
[[gate]]
id         = "KILL-TACTILE"
kind       = "kill"
section    = "18.17"                    # the row "Tactile micro-patches" of the Frontier and Moonshot table
consumers  = ["20.5", "45.7"]           # the tactile sensor; the epic "tactile sensing and compliant pads" (BD-418)
trigger    = "the pilot's verdict is negative (N18b-35's registered rule: zero differing grasp-slip outcomes of 75 for both the 2 x 2 and the 4 x 4 pad against one patch with no paired difference on the off-centre and twist trials, a difference on one reading only, or a failed per-element determinacy check); or a 4 x 4 pad pair adds more than 25% to step cost on the grasp workload (paired lower bound of the ratio above 1.25); or the pad's contact count cannot be admitted on any tier without removing contacts (two 4 x 4 pads are 32 contacts, above the referee's limit of 21 in one solve); or the taxel resultant on the pads fails its known answer (the resultant load equals the sum over taxels)"
fallback   = "one patch per pad, the sensor reading that patch's wrench; the dexterous-hands variant is described by the fallback"
decided    = "M6"
mode       = "exit"
reads      = []                         # no convergence gate exists for tactile sensing
scope_kind = "resolution"               # decided per grid: resolution:2x2, resolution:4x4
decision   = { outcome = "", adr = "", date = "", session = "", receipts = [] }
```
Measured by N18b-35 and N38a-31; decided by N38a-39; negative control: a taxel array that reports the pad's total
load in every taxel (the registered control of KA-523, N20a-19).

**Row 3 (DESIGN wording, from the sensing chapter's rule and the pilot's frozen falsifier).**

```toml
id         = "KILL-TAXEL"
kind       = "kill"

consumers  = ["20.5", "45.7"]           # the list that names tactile arrays [F]; the epic BD-418

fallback   = "the mask-only model: each taxel reports only whether it is in contact, from the contact set's footprint, with no pressure or shear values; the capability is described as contact mask per taxel"
decided    = "M6"
mode       = "exit"
reads      = []                         # the pilot record and the mechanism's cost receipt; no gate
decision   = { outcome = "", adr = "", date = "", session = "", receipts = [] }
```
Measured by N20a-18 and N20a-19; decided by N38ap3-01; negative control: the same total-in-every-taxel array, and
an estimated cost of 26% that must fire the pilot clause.

**Procedure.**
1. Write the three rows and one `[[amendment]]` per row with `field = "introduce"`, `gate` = the row's
   identifier, `old = ""`, `new` = the row's `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` as one TOML inline
   table in that key order, `reason` naming what proposed it ("epic BD-417 with no kill gate in the compiled
   register; pilot N18b-16", "epic BD-418; pilot N18b-35", "§20.7 rule for Frontier sensor models; pilot
   N20a-18"), `date` and `commit`. Each `new` is the row's seeded text for every later replay.
2. Ordering. The introduce amendment's date must precede every receipt cited by any part of any gate in the row's
   `reads` at its governing milestone (N40-03's `amendment_precedes_measurement`): for KILL-RUT, the receipts of
   GATE-TRAC-08's rut-leak part at M6 (N38a-29's batch-scale runs); GATE-TRAC-08's M4 receipts govern the soil gate
   at M4 and are read by KILL-SOIL, not by this row. Beyond the check, the amendment's commit precedes the first
   commit and every receipt of each mechanism bead the row governs: N18b-17 and N38a-29 (KILL-RUT), N38a-31
   (KILL-TACTILE), N20a-19 (KILL-TAXEL); N18b-17 waits for this bead, and the epics' queries
   (`epic_bd417_children_recorded`, `epic_bd418_gate_before_funding`) check the later ones. A pilot clause reads the
   pilot's verdict under the decision rule frozen and hashed in the pilot register before the pilot's own run, so
   a pilot receipt older than its row (the M4 rut pilot, if it ran before this bead landed) is admitted (DESIGN:
   the plan requires the kill gate before the work package is funded, not before the pilot). The amendment's
   text may not cite a pilot figure that is not in that pilot's frozen rule: a row entered before its pilot
   freezes its protocol (rows 2 and 3 always; row 1 when this bead lands before N18b-16 registers) takes its
   figures from the pilot bead's stated rule, and a figure the frozen rule changes is corrected by a `trigger`
   amendment before the pilot runs.
3. In `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, a pilot record of the three mechanisms that already exists
   with `fallback.kill_gate = "to-register"` (N18b-16's, if the rut pilot registered before this bead landed) is
   set to the identifier in the commit of the introduce amendment (N40-03, step 4). A record registered after
   this bead names its row directly: `KILL-RUT` (N18b-16), `KILL-TACTILE` (N18b-35), `KILL-TAXEL` (N20a-18).
4. Regenerate the printed register (`cargo xtask check-generated`) and the fallback ledger; both list the three
   rows under M6, with derived state `not-run`.
5. KILL-SOIL (N18b-18, decided at M4) does not change this bead: row 1 is entered whichever way it falls, so that
   the register holds the gate the epic proposed. If it fires, the rut pilot N18b-16 is `withdrawn`, N18b-17 and
   N38a-29 are not started, and N38a-38 records KILL-RUT's outcome `not-reached` citing KILL-SOIL's ADR (this
   bead records no outcome).

Determinism class D0 (data and a pure check). Nothing is recorded in the run ledger beyond the amendment records.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds `KILL-RUT`, `KILL-TACTILE` and `KILL-TAXEL` with `kind = "kill"` and the eight
      kill fields (`section`, `consumers`, `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided = "M6"`, `mode = "exit"`, `reads`, an
      empty `decision`), `scope_kind = "backend"` on KILL-RUT and `"resolution"` on KILL-TACTILE, and none of
      `claim`, `claim_id`, `threshold`, `tier`, `controls` or a `[[gate.part]]`; `cargo run -p frob-conformance
      --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml` exits 0.
- [ ] Each row has exactly one `introduce` amendment whose `new` replays to the row's text and whose date precedes
      every receipt of its reads at M6 and the first commit and every receipt of the mechanism beads it governs;
      a pilot receipt older than the row is admitted only through the pilot's frozen decision rule.
- [ ] After this bead no record of the pilot register says `to-register` for these three mechanisms; `cargo run -p
      frob-conformance --example pilots -- fundable --bead N18b-17 --json` never answers `kill-gate-to-register`,
      and on a scratch register holding N18b-16's record with verdict `falsifier-rejected` it answers `Fundable`
      at M4 (the real answer follows the pilot's own record, which this bead does not wait for).
- [ ] The printed register and the fallback ledger regenerate byte for byte and contain the three rows.
- [ ] A row edited after a governing receipt without an amendment is refused (`changed-without-amendment`); an
      amendment dated after that receipt is refused (`amended-after-measurement`); a row carrying `claim` or
      `controls` fails the load naming the field.
- [ ] With KILL-SOIL recorded as fired in a scratch register, row 1 is present with derived state `not-run`, and
      the funding rule blocks N18b-17 and N38a-29 on the `withdrawn` pilot record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_rows_m6.rs`, on the real registry files and on scratch copies (the name keeps
the milestone at which the rows are decided):
- `three_rows_present_and_valid`: the three identifiers load with kind `kill`, the eight kill fields, non-empty
  trigger and fallback, `decided = "M6"`, `mode = "exit"`, `scope_kind` `backend`, `resolution` and absent.
- `kill_row_no_measurement_fields`: a scratch KILL-RUT with `claim = "persistent ruts are kept"` (the withdrawn
  draft's field) fails the load naming `claim`; with `controls = ["NC-0001"]`, naming `controls`.
- `fallback_is_lower_tier`: each fallback names a mechanism of lower tier that exists (per-step sinkage from the
  soil law; one patch per pad; the contact mask per taxel, all Solid).
- `introduce_replays`: each `new` is an inline table with the keys trigger, fallback, decided, mode, reads in that
  order and replays to the row's text; a row with no introduce amendment is `changed-without-amendment`.
- `introduce_amendment_precedes_receipts`: a scratch receipt of N38a-29's leak run (GATE-TRAC-08's M6 part) dated
  before the amendment is refused (`amended-after-measurement`); a scratch M4 receipt of the rut pilot N18b-16,
  older than the row, is admitted because the row reads it only through the pilot's frozen rule, and a scratch M4
  receipt of GATE-TRAC-08's law part is not compared (positive controls).
- `pilot_records_name_gate`: a scratch N18b-16 record registered with `to-register` is set to `KILL-RUT` by the
  introduce commit, and no record of the real register says `to-register` for the three mechanisms; a scratch
  N20a-18 record with `kill_gate = "KILL-TAXEL"` and verdict `falsifier-rejected` makes
  `fundable_bead("N20a-19")` answer `Fundable`, and the same record with `to-register` answers
  `Blocked{kill-gate-to-register}`.
- `rut_field_fundable_at_m4`: on a scratch register with N18b-16's record `falsifier-rejected` and KILL-RUT
  entered, `fundable_bead("N18b-17")` answers `Fundable`; without the row, `kill-gate-to-register`.
- `soil_fired_blocks_rut_funding`: with a scratch decision of KILL-SOIL `fired` and N18b-16's record `withdrawn`,
  row 1 loads with derived state `not-run` and `fundable_bead` answers `Blocked` for N18b-17 and N38a-29.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_rows_m6.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-37 milestone=M4 labels=gauntlet,registry,kill-gate
gates=KILL-RUT,KILL-TACTILE,KILL-TAXEL plants=edit-without-amendment,amend-after-receipt,measurement-field`). It
runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml`, `cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test kill_rows_m6`, `cargo xtask check-generated`, and `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
pilots -- fundable --bead N18b-17 --json` (asserting that the answer is the one the register's rut pilot record
implies, `missing` while N18b-16 has not registered, and never `kill-gate-to-register`); then it
applies the three plants to a scratch copy and runs the validator on it, requiring `changed-without-amendment`,
`amended-after-measurement` and the load error naming `claim`. Log at `target/e2e-logs/kill_rows_m6/<utc>.ndjson`
through the shared helper, with a transcript: every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N38a-37"), `step`,
`event`; commands with exit codes and wall time; one `assert` per row field and per amendment; the three `refusal`
events with their reasons; the funding answer for N18b-17; digests of the register before and after and of the
regenerated printout; the evidence block `{claim: known-answer, class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0}`;
on failure the first differing field and the scratch path; and the `summary` with assertions passed and failed,
naming the three gates. The script writes no gate record.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers used as the rows' numbers: rut memory per world 256 KiB, which is T_rut = 1,024 tiles of 8 × 8
`f32` offsets (1,024 × 256 B) in the one layout the pilot N18b-16 and the mechanism (N18b-17, N38a-29) keep, the
same budget as the 64 tiles of 32 × 32 `f32` (64 × 4 KiB) of the first planning figures; twist lever of a
uniformly loaded square pad 0.3826·s, 0.3536·s for 2 × 2 point contacts and 0.3744·s for 4 × 4; 32 contacts
against a referee limit of 21; the cost bound 25% (a ratio of 1.25) in all three rows. Negative controls: the row
edited without an amendment; the amendment after the receipt; a kill row carrying a measurement field; a fallback
of a higher tier than the mechanism. Determinism lane: identical output twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The wording is a DESIGN proposal of the epics and of the sensing chapter's rule, not text of the gate register as
  compiled; the owner may amend it before the pilots run, by the same procedure.
- At most four Moonshot items are active at once; registration does not fund anything, it only makes funding
  possible. KILL-TAXEL governs a Frontier model and is outside that cap.
- Whichever pilot records survive for these mechanisms, their `fallback.kill_gate` points to these rows.
- What would reopen the design: a further clause proposed after a governing receipt. It is refused; it needs a new
  amendment before the next measurement.
