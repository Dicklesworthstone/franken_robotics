## Background

A kill gate is decided, not discovered: its trigger and its fallback are registered before the measurement, the
trigger is evaluated on recorded values at its milestone, and the outcome, either way, is written as an
architecture decision record (ADR). A gate that fires is not a project failure; it replaces a capability by a
cheaper one that the design already contains.

KILL-RUT is the kill gate of the Moonshot epic "soft soil at scale" (BD-417): terrain that deforms and stays
deformed, held per world as copy-on-write tiles over shared static terrain, on the batch tiers. The benefit is
coverage of repeated-pass tasks (a second pass over one's own rut; a tracked base turning in place); there is no
speed benefit to claim. The risk is state that outlives a step: at batch scale a rut can leak between worlds of a
batch or from one episode into the next. The row is registered by N38a-37; this bead evaluates it.

Trigger as registered by N38a-37 (TARGET numbers). Any one clause fires the gate:
1. the pilot's falsifier holds (N18b-16's frozen rule): leak count above zero in its forms 1 to 4 over the 10³
   resets and 20 checkpoint instants on `batch-cpu`, or a marked world's digest differs from its solo run, or a
   planted leak goes undetected;
2. the pilot's funding flags stand: memory above 256 KiB per world on its fixture, or the paired lower bound of
   its step-cost ratio above 1.25 (findings of the pilot, not its falsifier: its verdict can be
   `falsifier-rejected` with a flag standing);
3. at batch scale (N38a-29's runs at 4,096 worlds), any leak in 10³ resets on a backend the epic claims
   (`batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`);
4. the rut buffers break the fixed launch shape or the determinism class of a batch;
5. the paired lower bound of the step-cost ratio with ruts on against ruts off exceeds 1.25 on the reference
   tracked workload (the same reading as clause 2's cost flag and as KILL-TACTILE's cost clause; the mechanism's
   own acceptance asks the upper bound to be at most 1.25, and a ratio whose bounds straddle 1.25 is recorded
   with both bounds and does not fire the clause).

Fallback: persistent deformation off. Terrain resets each episode; sinkage is computed per step from the
pressure–sinkage law p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ with no memory. Decided at M6. If KILL-SOIL fired at M4 there is no soil
law to deform: the outcome is `not-reached`, citing KILL-SOIL's ADR, with no measurement.

## Scope

In: evaluating the five clauses on recorded receipts; the decision record and the row's decision entries;
applying the fallback if the gate fires; the list of what changes downstream. Out: registering the row
(N38a-37); producing the measurements (the pilot N18b-16, run at M4, which superseded the M6 draft N38a-28; the
mechanism and its batch-scale leak run N38a-29); the M4 rut field (N18b-17); the soil laws and their own kill
gate (BD-366 and the decision of KILL-SOIL, N18b-18).

## Technical approach

**Inputs (all by receipt hash, none retyped).** From the pilot register: the record of N18b-16
(`PILOT-TRAC-<nn>`) with its status and verdict (`falsifier-rejected`, `falsifier-holds` or `withdrawn`), the
leak counts per form, the solo-equality result, the planted-leak detections, and the two cost figures with their
bounds and both reporting levels (the second level is the funding flag). From `scripts/e2e/soil_ruts_leak.sh` of
N38a-29, per backend claimed: resets run (at least 1,000), leak count, memory per world, the paired bounds of
the cost ratio, the launch-shape check and the determinism class attained against the class declared.

**Evaluation.**
1. Read the pilot record. Verdict `falsifier-holds`: clause 1 fires. Verdict `falsifier-rejected` with a funding
   flag standing in its result (memory above 256 KiB per world on its fixture, or the paired lower bound of its
   step-cost ratio above 1.25): clause 2 fires. In both cases the outcome is `fired` on every backend. Record
   `withdrawn` (KILL-SOIL fired, or the owner did not select the epic under the Moonshot cap by the M6 exit
   candidate): outcome `not-reached` on every backend. In all three cases the mechanism bead N38a-29 is not
   started and has closed `withdrawn` with a pointer to this bead (N41-17: it delivers nothing and never counts
   as work done), which is how this bead, which depends on it, becomes ready. Go to step 4.
2. Otherwise read the receipts of N38a-29, backend by backend. For a backend, clause 3 fires on a leak count
   above zero; clause 4 on a launch shape that changed with rut state or on a class below the declared one;
   clause 5 when the lower bound of the paired cost ratio is above 1.25. A clause whose measurement is missing
   is `not-run`, and the outcome `not-fired` cannot be recorded for a backend while any of its clauses is
   `not-run`.
3. One repair cycle is permitted before the decision for clauses 3 to 5, in N40-03's form (opened by the
   measuring bead's `repair-cycle` note line naming the failed receipt and the hypothesis, closed by one full
   re-run with new receipts on the same fixture and thresholds); the first receipts stay on record. A second
   cycle is a `deferred` decision with `deferred_to` and `reason`.
4. Write the decision ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-RUT-M6.md` with the fields N40-03 requires (the one directory for
   decision records), one section per backend: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered with its
   amendments; each measured value with its receipt hash, commit, fingerprint and seeds beside the trigger's
   number; the repair taken; the clause-by-clause verdict; the outcome (`not-fired`, `fired`, `not-reached` or
   `deferred`); the fallback in force and the name the capability carries; the beads re-read (each "stands on
   the fallback", "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"); what changes downstream; and
   the recording session.
5. The recording session writes the row's decision in `registries/gates.toml`, in N40-03's form. The row is
   decided per backend (`scope_kind = "backend"`, the kind N40-03's table lists for KILL-RUT), so the decision is
   an array with one entry per backend the epic claims, `{ exit = "M6", scope = "backend:<tier>", outcome, adr,
   date, session, receipts }` with `<tier>` one of `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`; an outcome that is the
   same for every backend (the three cases of step 1) is written on each entry. `receipts` lists the hashes the
   outcome rests on under `receipts/gates/`, at least one for `not-fired`. The row gains no measured value. The
   load refuses an entry whose `session` implemented the mechanism (N40-03's check; this is not the gate
   recorder of N38b-19, which records measured gates and never a kill decision). On `fired` for a backend,
   N40-03's `apply_decisions` gives the gate the row reads, GATE-TRAC-08, where its rut-leak part failed on that
   backend, the status `killed` with detail `KILL-RUT: terrain resets each episode`; the generated views print
   the fallback's name.

**Downstream.** *Not fired*: the rut feature flag may be enabled per task; GATE-TRAC-08's leak clause is
recorded for each device backend; the repeated-pass rows join the tracked battery. *Fired*: the flag is removed
from capability listings; soil cards keep their laws; every result that used persistent ruts is labelled
exploratory; the epic BD-417 closes `fallback` on "terrain resets each episode", citing the ADR; N38a-29 (a
dependency of this bead, closed on its recorded receipts) is re-read in the ADR; a per-backend kill is allowed
(ruts kept on `batch-cpu` only) and is recorded as `fired` for the devices and `not-fired` for `batch-cpu`, never
as one `not-fired`. *Not reached*: the fallback is in force without a measurement; BD-417 closes `fallback`
citing the ADR.

      with the clause that fired where it fired.
- [ ] The row KILL-RUT in `registries/gates.toml` carries one decision entry per backend claimed, `{ exit, scope =
      "backend:<tier>", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, written by a session other than the
      implementing one (an entry by an implementing session fails the load); with any clause `not-run` this
      bead's evaluation refuses to produce `not-fired`, and a `not-fired` entry with no receipt fails the load.
- [ ] If fired: the capability is listed as "terrain resets each episode", the rows that read KILL-RUT read
      `killed` with their detail, BD-417 closes `fallback` citing the ADR, N38a-29 is re-read in the ADR, and the
      list of labelled results is attached. If the pilot fired or was withdrawn: N38a-29 is closed `withdrawn`
      with a pointer to this bead.
- [ ] If not fired: zero leak over 10³ resets is recorded on every backend the epic claims, with the cost ratio's
      bounds.
- [ ] The M6 exit review can read the outcome from the register without opening this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_rut_decision.rs`, on scratch receipts (labelled `synthetic`):
- `falsifier_holds_fires_clause_1` (outcome `fired` on every backend); `withdrawn_pilot_closes_by_soil_decision`
  (outcome `not-reached`, citing KILL-SOIL's ADR).
- `funding_flag_fires_clause_2`: a `falsifier-rejected` record whose result reports 300 KiB per world, or a cost
  ratio with lower bound 1.26, gives `fired` by clause 2; 256 KiB and a lower bound of 1.24 give neither clause.
- `per_backend_scopes`: a leak on `gpu-cuda` only gives two decision entries, `backend:gpu-cuda` fired and
  `backend:batch-cpu` not-fired; an entry with `scope = "family:cuda"`, or with no `scope`, fails the load
  (`decision-scope-invalid`).
- `batch_scale_leak_of_one_fires_clause_3`: leak count 1 of 1,000 resets on one backend → `fired` for that backend,
  and `apply_decisions` prints GATE-TRAC-08 `killed` for it.
- `cost_bound_1_26_fires_clause_5`, `cost_bound_1_24_does_not`; bounds 1.20 and 1.30 are recorded and do not fire.
- `missing_measurement_blocks_not_fired`: no launch-shape receipt → `not-run`, and a `not-fired` outcome is
  refused; a `not-fired` entry with `receipts = []` fails the load.
- `decision_cites_receipts`: an ADR with a retyped number and no receipt hash is rejected.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_rut_decision.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-38 milestone=M6 labels=kill-gate,traction gates=KILL-RUT
plants=missing-receipt,self-recorded`). It collects the pilot record and the receipts of `soil_ruts_leak.sh`,
evaluates the clauses (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_rut_decision`, then the evaluation on the real
receipts), writes the ADR draft and the decision entries into a scratch copy of the register and validates it
(`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file <scratch>/gates.toml`); the plants remove
one receipt (expects `not-run` and a refused `not-fired`) and write the entry from the implementing session
(expects the load refusal). The entries are committed to `registries/gates.toml` by the recording session, not
by the script. Log at `target/e2e-logs/kill_rut_decision/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries
`ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N38a-38"), `step`, `event`; each clause as an `assert` with the
measured value, its unit and its threshold (leak `== 0`, memory `<= 256 KiB`, the cost ratio's lower bound `<= 1.25`, with its upper bound logged beside it), receipt
digests, both refusals, and the `summary` with assertions passed and failed, naming KILL-RUT, the
outcome and the ADR path.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers the measurements rest on: a rut tile of 8 × 8 `f32` offsets is 256 B and the default T_rut = 1,024
tiles are 256 KiB per world (1 GiB at 4,096 worlds, 2 GiB at 8,192), the layout of the pilot N18b-16 and of
N18b-17 that N38a-29 keeps, and the same budget as the 64 tiles of 32 × 32 (4 KiB each) of the first planning
figures; the plate with b = 0.1 m, k_c = 1 kPa/mⁿ⁻¹, k_φ = 1,500 kPa/mⁿ, n = 1.1 under
20 kPa sinks 19.6 mm. Negative controls: the shared (not per-world) rut buffer, which must give a nonzero leak
and therefore a fired gate; a decision recorded with a missing receipt; a decision recorded by the implementing
session. Determinism: the evaluation is a pure function of the receipts; identical twice.

## Considerations

- The decision is about leak, shape, class and cost. It makes no fidelity claim: a kept gate does not say that
  simulated ruts match any real soil.
- A kill here removes nothing from the critical path; M6 pauses whenever the critical path slips.
- The rut pilot and the M4 rut field are N18b-16 and N18b-17, in the soil work of M4 (the M6 draft of the pilot,
  N38a-28, is superseded); this decision reads the pilot's record and N38a-29's receipts. A fired or not-reached
  outcome on `batch-cpu` also switches off the M4 field of N18b-17, which the ADR lists among the beads re-read.
- Revisit if a registered task needs repeated-pass coverage that per-step sinkage cannot give and the cost
  clause was the only one that fired.
