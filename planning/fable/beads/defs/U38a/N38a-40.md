## Background

Everything enumerable in FrankenRobotics has a stable number in a machine-readable registry. A known answer (a
case whose truth is derived on file, with a tolerance, a tier and a negative control) is `KA-nnn` in
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/known_answers.toml`; a counterexample (a tempting shortcut with the smallest
case that breaks it) is `CX-nnn` in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/counterexamples.toml`. Gates cite known
answers by number and a negative control is found by its counterexample number, so two rules hold: registries
are data and the printed tables are their printout (ADR-GAUNT-02), and numbers are append-only, never reused and
never renumbered (INV-GAUNT-07).

At plan time the suite has 835 known answers (KA-001 to KA-835) and 384 counterexamples (CX-001 to CX-384), each
grouped by chapter. Subsections added after those tables were compiled state further cases with values and
controls and **no numbers**: the fourteen rows adopted by the verification chapter from corrected and late
fixtures; eighteen late answers and eleven late counterexamples of the mission-query chapter; the sensor
chapter's late pulse rows and four late counterexamples; and unnumbered late cases that the registry beads of
the design-language, actuator, dynamics, contact, scene, control, learning, task, counterfactual-debugging, studio,
telemetry, calibration, structure, surface and suite chapters carry under provisional tags. Each of those beads
was drafted alone. Two of them already print numbers (KA-836 to KA-849 and CX-385 to CX-400 for the verification
chapter's own rows); the others use tags such as `late-01` or `L-a`. Without one allocator, two chapters can
print the same number in their beads, tests and derivation files, and the append-only check then rejects
whichever lands second, after its number has been cited.

DESIGN: one allocation ledger, a small data file beside the registries, hands out contiguous blocks; a row's
number is valid only inside a block issued to its chapter. Rejected: first come, first served at commit time
(numbers printed in beads and derivation files before the commit would have to be rewritten); a number range per
chapter with gaps (breaks the contiguity that makes "is anything missing" a one-line check); letter suffixes on
existing numbers (a second identifier grammar for every reader).

## Scope

In: the ledger file and its schema; the first allocations; the amended contiguity rule of both loaders; the rule
for provisional tags; the checks and their planted violations. Out: the rows and entries themselves (each
chapter's registry bead); the row schema and the other loader rules (N38a-12); the catalogue schema and the
seeding of controls (N38b-05); the append-only diff of INV-GAUNT-07 across commits (N38b-22); numbers of
negative controls (`NC-nnnn`, allocated by the seeding tool); refusal codes (their own registry and their own
decision beads).

## Technical approach

**File (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/allocations.toml`, schema `frob.gauntlet.alloc/1`; DESIGN).**
```toml
[[block]]
registry = "ka"                 # ka | cx
first    = 836
last     = 849
owner    = "N38-KA"             # the registry bead that enters the rows
section  = "§38.17, §38.18"     # where the cases are stated
count    = 14
status   = "reserved"           # reserved | filling | filled | closed
```

**Rules checked by `registry::alloc::validate` (called by both loaders).**
1. *Disjoint and contiguous.* Blocks of one registry do not overlap; the first block starts at the plan-time
   maximum plus one (836 for `ka`, 385 for `cx`); each later block starts at the previous block's `last` plus one.
2. *A number needs a block.* A row with a number above the plan-time maximum that lies in no block, or in a block
   owned by another bead's chapter, is refused with the harness reason `unallocated-number`.
3. *Contiguity, amended.* The loader's rule "identifiers contiguous" becomes: every number from 1 to the highest
   present is either present or inside a block whose status is `reserved` or `filling`. A hole outside such a
   block is refused (`missing-number`). This lets a block's rows land at different milestones.
4. *Blocks are append-only.* A block is never moved, shrunk below a number already used, or reissued. A number
   of a block that will never be used (a case withdrawn because its mechanism's kill gate fired) is recorded in
   the block as `void = [n, …]` and is never given to another case. Status `closed` = every number is present or
   void.
5. *Provisional tags.* A late case may be cited by a tag (`late-01`, `L-a`) only until its block is issued. Once a
   block exists for its owner, the owner maps each tag to a number in table order, in one commit, and a tag that
   still appears in a registry, a test name or a derivation file at the owner's milestone fails
   (`provisional-tag-past-due`). Exempt: the value of a numbered row's own `slug` field (N38a-12, N38b-05),
   which keeps the working name after numbering by design; and the `id` of a plant of a numbered counterexample
   entry (`<planting bead>:<name>`, N38b-05), wherever it names that plant: in the entry's `plant` list, in a
   known-answer control cell that names it (N38b-05, step 3), and in the fields of its control that the seeding
   derives from it (`target`, and `feature` or `fixture`, which N38b-05 writes once and never rewrites). A plant
   id names a planted defect, not the case, and renaming it would be a `plant-changed` finding and a new control
   (N13-CX `N13-35:turn-count-is-state`, N16-CX `<carrier>:kin-k1` to `kin-k15`, N18-CX `N18b-40:trac-resid`
   and others carry such names). The same text anywhere else (an identifier, a `same_as` or `nc` reference, a
   gate's citation, a test name, a derivation file's name or body) is past due.
6. *Order of issue.* Blocks are issued in the order the requests are committed; a request is a `[[block]]` entry
   with `count` and `owner`, and the allocator fills `first` and `last`. Nobody types `first`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `allocations.toml` exists, validates, and holds the two first blocks with exactly the numbers above.
- [ ] Both loaders call the validator; the amended contiguity rule accepts a registry in which KA-845 is present
      and KA-846 to KA-849 are not yet, and rejects a registry in which KA-850 is present with no block.
- [ ] Two requests committed in order receive adjacent, disjoint blocks; re-running the allocator changes nothing
      (idempotent).
- [ ] A number marked `void` is never issued again; shrinking or moving a block is refused.
- [ ] A provisional tag left in a registry file at its owner's milestone fails the check and names the file; the
      value of a numbered row's own `slug` field does not, nor does a numbered entry's plant id where it names
      that plant (the entry's `plant`, a known-answer control cell, and its control's `target` and `feature` or
      `fixture`).
- [ ] The three harness reasons (`unallocated-number`, `missing-number`, `provisional-tag-past-due`) are
      registered in the Gauntlet's reason vocabulary (`registry/reasons.toml`, N38-REF, a dependency) with
      trigger and repair, and the validator prints them as `reason=<id>`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/registry_allocations.rs`, real files and scratch copies (no mocks):
- `first_blocks_exact`: `ka` 836..=849 owned by N38-KA; `cx` 385..=400 owned by N38-CX.
- `blocks_disjoint_contiguous`: a planted block 845..=860 is refused (overlap); a planted block 851..=860 after
  849 is refused (hole at 850).
- `unallocated_number_refused`: a row `KA-850` with no block → `unallocated-number`.
- `foreign_block_refused`: a row of the sensor chapter numbered inside N38-KA's block → `unallocated-number`.
- `hole_inside_reserved_block_allowed`, `hole_outside_block_refused` (`missing-number` for a deleted KA-412).
- `issue_is_idempotent_and_ordered`: requests of 18 and 2 rows issued after 849 give 850..=867 and 868..=869.
- `void_never_reissued`; `block_never_shrinks`.
- `provisional_tag_past_due`: `late-03` in a scratch registry at the owner's milestone fails naming the file.
- `slug_field_not_a_stale_tag`: after numbering, a scratch `cx` entry `CX-401` with `slug = "suite-chord-bound"`
  passes; the same text as the `nc` reference of another row, or in a test name, fails
  `provisional-tag-past-due` naming the file and the field.
- `plant_id_not_a_stale_tag`: after numbering, a scratch `cx` entry `CX-401` with `slug = "trac-resid"` and plant
  `N18b-40:trac-resid`, its seeded control with `target = "N18b-40:trac-resid"` and `feature = "trac-resid"`, and a
  scratch known-answer row whose control cell names `N18b-40:trac-resid` pass; the text `trac-resid` in a
  derivation file's body fails `provisional-tag-past-due` naming the file.
- Property test: for 1,000 random request sequences the issued blocks partition an interval with no gap.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/registry_allocations.sh` (header: `bead=N38a-40 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet,registry
plants=overlap-block,unallocated-row,stale-tag`). It runs the validator on the real registries, issues two
scratch requests, then applies the three plants to scratch copies and requires each refusal. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/registry_allocations/<utc>.ndjson` through the shared helper: commands with exit codes and wall
time, the block table as `metric` lines (registry, first, last, count), one `assert` per rule, the three
`refusal` events with their reasons, the digest of `allocations.toml`, and the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: plan-time maxima 835 and 384; first free numbers 836 and 385; the two first blocks end at 849 and
400; 18 further answers would occupy 850 to 867. Negative controls: an overlapping block; a row with no block; a
reused void number; a tag past its milestone. Metamorphic relation: permuting the order of `[[block]]` entries in
the file changes no verdict. Determinism lane: identical output twice on one machine.

## Considerations

- The numbers KA-836 to KA-849 and CX-385 to CX-400 are honoured because they are already printed in two
  registry beads; no other bead may print a number above 835 or 384 before its block is issued.
- Rows of Moonshot mechanisms may be withdrawn with their mechanism; their numbers become void, which is why the
  ledger and not the registry records them.
- Interaction with the append-only diff: that check compares commits; this one checks a single tree. Both must
  pass, so the diff (N38b-22, which depends on this bead) reads `allocations.toml` for the KA and CX families:
  a number inside a block issued to its owner is in order whatever higher numbers already exist, and a hole
  inside a `reserved` or `filling` block is not a gap.
- If the plan's appendices are later reprinted with the late rows folded in, the printout follows the registry;
  the ledger is the record of how the numbers were assigned.
