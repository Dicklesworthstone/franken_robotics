## Background

Work in FrankenRobotics is tracked as *beads* (work items) closed by agent sessions. A sibling project recorded
what can go wrong (FACT): 87 beads closed within 12 seconds with one shared close reason, serving figures from
a stand-in. The program's response has several parts; this bead is the first of them, as the verification
subsystem (the Gauntlet, crate `frob-conformance`) states it:

- **Typed close reasons.** A bead closes with a reason that cites its own test and that test's output. A shared
  close reason across many beads is itself a failure signal.
- **A closed bead is not acceptance.** Acceptance is the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml`.

The risk register carries the same failure ("the swarm closed beads that proved plumbing") with three signals:
shared close reasons; a gate without a caught negative control; a pass recorded by the session that implemented
the capability. The second and third are enforced by the control registry and the gate recorder. The first is
enforced here, by an audit run at every milestone exit and on demand.

DESIGN: the audit is a Cargo test of the conformance crate that reads the tracker's exported issue file and the
per-bead logs; it changes nothing in the tracker. Rejected: a tracker hook that blocks a close (the tracker is a
separate tool with its own rules; the audit must also catch closes made around any hook).

## Scope

In: the reason types; the six audit checks; thresholds for "shared" and "burst"; the report. This bead is the
one implementation of the typed-close check over the tracker export: hygiene rule 2 of the sixteen (listed in
N00-08, which builds no check of its own for it) and lifecycle rules 2 and 3 (stated in N41-17) are both
enforced by this audit, with the reason types, the form of a reason and the thresholds given here. Out: the
per-bead log format `frob.beadlog/1`, its append function and its closing-rule evaluator (N29a-09, M1) and the
artifact graph (BD-095, M1), of which this bead reads only the fields listed below; the resolution of a closing line's claim identifiers in
the artifact graph, which needs that graph and is added at M1 by N41-17, whose lifecycle check also calls this
audit and merges its report; the other lifecycle rules (inheritance, kill-gate re-reads, readiness, splitting,
exit reviews, upstream beads: N41-17); the refusal raised when a close is attempted without a resolving log
line from another session (existing refusal FROB-REF-815, owned by the telemetry subsystem); gate rows.

## Technical approach

**Close reason types (the first word of a bead's close reason).**

| Type | Required content | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `pass` | The bead's own test (a `cargo test` path or an end-to-end script), and a reference to that test's output: the hash of a harness receipt or the timestamp of the bead-log line | The bead's observable holds |
| `recorded` | The bead's own test; its `gate-record` line with verdict `fail`, recorded by a session that did not implement the bead, whose receipt shows every listed control `caught`; the kill gate whose trigger reads the gate; and, where that trigger's text names a repair cycle or an optimization pass, the `note` line that opened the cycle (`cause = "repair-cycle: <failed receipt>: <hypothesis>"`) | The bead measured a gate that a kill gate reads and the part is recorded `fail` after the one repair cycle (or after the one measurement, for a trigger that names no cycle); no capability is claimed |
| `fallback` | The kill gate that fired, the name of the fallback and the decision record (`docs/adr/ADR-<gate>-<Mn>.md`) | The capability is delivered by the fallback and described by its name |
| `superseded` | The identifier of the decision record | The bead needed a killed capability |
| `duplicate` | The surviving bead | Another bead carries the work; no capability claimed |
| `withdrawn` | The recorded reason; when the reason is a decision, its citation: the decision record of a kill gate whose outcome left this bead's branch untaken (`withdrawn: not needed: <kill gate> not fired, docs/adr/ADR-<gate>-<Mn>.md`), the `[[pilot.result]]` whose falsifier held, or the envelope row not approved (`withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R<n>`) | The bead is not needed; no capability claimed |

The six types and their required content are the closed set of lifecycle rule 2 (N41-17, which states the same
table). This audit checks the form: the type, its content present, and, for `pass` and `recorded`, the test,
the line and the sessions (checks 2 to 4). Whether a cited decision exists and says what the close says, and the
kill-gate read behind `recorded` (that the named kill gate's trigger reads this gate, and the repair-cycle `note`
line where that trigger names a cycle), are N41-17's checks, which need the decision records and the gate
register.

**The closing log line.** One line of `docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson` with: `ts`, `bead`, `commit`, `host`
(with hardware fingerprint), `toolchain`, `determinism`, `state_hash`, `claim_type`, `claim_ids`, `metrics`
(each with value and unit), `refusals`, `verdict`, `recorded_by`, `gate`; the schema `frob.beadlog/1` of N29a-09
adds `event` (`gate-record` when absent, `implement`, `blocked`, `unblocked`, `note`), `implemented_by` and
`cause`. Only a `gate-record` line can be a closing line: `implement`, `blocked`, `unblocked` and `note` lines
carry `verdict = not-run` and are skipped by checks 3 and 4, and the sessions of `implement` lines count as
implementing sessions in check 4. A bead closes as `pass` only with a line whose verdict is `pass`, whose claim
identifiers resolve, and whose `recorded_by` is not a session that implemented the bead; it closes as
`recorded` only with a `gate-record` line whose verdict is `fail`, under the same session rule, whose cited
receipt lists every control of the gate as `caught`. At M0, before N29a-09
lands, the audit reads the same field names from the recorder's appended lines (N38b-19).

**Audit (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/close_audit.rs`; input: the tracker's exported issue file and the log
directory).** For every closed bead:
1. *Typed.* The reason starts with one of the types and carries that type's required content; else `untyped`.
2. *Own test.* For `pass` and `recorded`: the cited test exists (in `cargo test -- --list` or under
   `scripts/e2e/`) and is bound to this bead: its header or name carries the bead's key, or the bead's own test
   plan names it. A test bound to another bead is `foreign-test`.
3. *Own output.* The cited receipt or log line exists and parses, names this bead, and its commit is an
   ancestor of the close; for `pass` its verdict is `pass` (a line with a failing verdict is `no-output`); for
   `recorded` it is a `gate-record` line with verdict `fail` whose receipt shows every listed control `caught`
   (a `pass` line, or a control not caught, is `no-output`); else `no-output`.
4. *Other session.* For `pass` and `recorded`: `recorded_by` differs from every implementing session of the
   bead; else `self-closed`.
5. *Shared reasons.* Normalize each reason (lower case; identifiers, hashes, numbers and paths replaced by
   placeholders). Two or more beads with the same normalized reason and different cited outputs are listed as
   `shared-reason`; two or more beads citing the same output are `shared-output` unless the output is a
   receipt that names every one of them.
6. *Bursts.* More than 5 closes by one session inside 60 seconds are listed as `burst` for review by the exit
   reviewer (the recorded incident was 87 in 12 seconds).
The audit fails on any `untyped`, `foreign-test`, `no-output`, `self-closed`, `shared-reason` or
`shared-output`; a `burst` is a warning that the exit review must disposition in writing.

Thresholds (DESIGN): "shared" means two or more beads, because the rule calls a reason shared by several beads
a failure signal and a legitimate batch has its own route (one receipt naming every bead); "burst" means more
than 5 closes by one session in 60 seconds, far below the recorded incident's rate (87 in 12 seconds) and above
what one session closes by hand. These are the only values: the hygiene rules (N00-08) and the lifecycle rules
(N41-17) cite them and set none of their own. Check 4 applies to every bead, so a gate bead closed by the
session that implemented it is reported `self-closed` like any other.

**If the export format changes.** The audit depends on the tracker's export format. When a field it reads is
missing or renamed it reports `export-format-unreadable` with the field, prints a warning and exits non-zero
for the exit runner to disposition; it never passes silently on an export it cannot read.

**Report.** `target/gauntlet/close_audit.json`: counts per finding, the beads involved, and for shared reasons
the normalized text. The milestone exit review reads it; a non-empty failing list blocks the exit.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A closed bead whose reason is free text, or cites no test, fails the audit as `untyped`.
- [ ] Two beads closed with one reason differing only in bead numbers are reported `shared-reason`.
- [ ] A bead closed by its implementing session is reported `self-closed`.
- [ ] The six types of lifecycle rule 2 are accepted with their required content; a `recorded` close is accepted
      only on a `fail` gate-record line with every control caught, recorded by another session, naming its kill
      gate; a `withdrawn` close may cite its decision.
- [ ] A bead whose cited test belongs to another bead, or whose cited output does not exist, is reported.
- [ ] A burst of six closes in 60 seconds by one session is listed.
- [ ] The synthetic replay of the recorded failure pattern (87 closures inside 12 seconds sharing one reason,
      the registered control CX-002) is flagged: 87 beads `shared-reason` and one `burst`.
- [ ] A gate bead closed by the session that implemented it is reported `self-closed`.
- [ ] An export with a renamed or missing field yields `export-format-unreadable` and a non-zero exit, never a
      pass.
- [ ] The audit passes on a tracker export in which every close cites its own test and output.
- [ ] The audit runs inside the milestone-exit procedure and its report is attached to the exit record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/close_audit.rs`, on fixture exports labelled `synthetic`:
- `typed_reason_required`: "done" → `untyped`; "pass: cargo test -p frob-lie law_se3_adjoint; receipt 3f9c…"
  accepted.
- `normalization`: "pass: test ka_501 ok, receipt 3f9c" and "pass: test ka_502 ok, receipt 77aa" normalize to
  the same text → `shared-reason`.
- `shared_output_detected`; `receipt_naming_all_beads_allowed`.
- `self_closed_detected`: `recorded_by` equal to an implementing session; `self_closed_gate_bead_flagged`: the
  same on a bead that carries a gate.
- `foreign_test_detected`; `missing_output_detected`; `failing_verdict_rejected` (a cited line whose verdict is
  `fail` → `no-output`); `test_named_in_own_test_plan_accepted`: a test whose name
  lacks the key but which the bead's test plan names is bound to it.
- `close_reason_without_test_id_rejected`: a `pass` reason with an output and no test → `untyped`.
- `unreadable_export_is_not_a_pass`: an export with the close-reason field renamed → `export-format-unreadable`,
  non-zero exit.
- `burst_threshold`: five closes in 60 s pass; six are listed.
- `fallback_reason_needs_kill_gate` (and its decision record); `superseded_needs_decision_id`.
- `recorded_reason_content` (synthetic identifiers): "recorded: cargo test -p frob-conformance --test
  g1_known_answers -- GATE-TEST-01; line 2026-03-02T10:15:00Z; KILL-TEST-01" citing
  a `gate-record` line with verdict `fail`, recorded by another session, whose receipt shows every control
  `caught` → accepted; the same with no kill gate → `untyped`; citing a `pass` line, or a receipt with one
  control `missed` → `no-output`; recorded by an implementing session → `self-closed`.
- `withdrawn_cites_decision`: "withdrawn: not needed: KILL-X not fired, docs/adr/ADR-KILL-X-M2.md" and
  "withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R3" are typed; "withdrawn" alone → `untyped`.
- `only_gate_record_lines_close`: a `pass` reason citing an `implement` or `note` line → `no-output`; a bead
  whose only implementer appears on an `implement` line and who recorded the closing line → `self-closed`.
- `sibling_incident_fixture`: 87 closes in 12 seconds with one reason → 87 beads in `shared-reason`, one burst.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/close_audit.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-03 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet,delivery gates=GATE-GAUNT-01
plants=shared-reason,self-close`). It runs the audit on the repository's exported issue file, then on two
planted exports (a shared reason; a self-close), each of which must fail. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/close_audit/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit codes and wall time; counts per finding as
`metric` lines (thresholds 0 for the failing kinds); the two negative-control assertions with the beads named;
the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the sibling-incident fixture yields 87 and 1; it is the registered control CX-002 (87 beads, 12
seconds, one reason) of hygiene rule 2. Negative controls: the shared reason; the
self-close; a reason that cites a test which passes but belongs to another bead. Metamorphic relation:
reordering the export changes no finding. Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- The audit reads an export and never invokes the tracker; a close made by any route is seen.
- Legitimate batches exist (a registry bead and its rows may close together): they cite one receipt that names
  every bead, which check 5 allows.
- Session identity is a discipline check, not authentication; the exit review by a fresh-context session is the
  second line.
- Needs discipline at every exit review: the residual risk is stated in the risk register and does not go
  away.
