## Background

FrankenRobotics treats counterexamples as first-class: where a plausible shortcut is wrong, the design states
the smallest hand-checkable case that breaks it. The Gauntlet (verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`)
compiles them in a counterexample catalogue: 384 entries at plan time, CX-001 to CX-384, grouped by chapter
(FACT, counted). Each entry has the tempting shortcut, the smallest case with its numbers, the rule it protects
and where it is defined. The known-answer suite (835 rows) also states a negative control for 510 of its rows
(835 less the 325 printed `none stated`).

The negative-control registry (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`; some documents call it
`registries/negative_controls.toml`, a logical alias) is seeded from both: each catalogue entry becomes at least
one control attached to the gates of the rule it protects, and each stated `Negative control` cell of the
known-answer suite becomes one. This bead is that seeding, with proof obligation PO-GAUNT-03: the seed is
complete, meaning every CX entry and every stated control cell maps to a control with at least one gate, checked
by a join over the three registries with no unmatched row.

DESIGN: seeding is a deterministic, repeatable tool with a committed map, not a hand transcription. Reason: 894
source items cannot be kept consistent by hand, and identifiers are append-only. Rejected: generating controls
at test time from the catalogue (identifiers would not be stable and a control could not carry its history).

## Scope

In: the catalogue data file schema; the mapping from kind of shortcut to kind of control; gate resolution; the
seeding tool; the seed map; the findings list; the join test; the seventeen word-only entries and the pre-slip
fixture as obligations. Out: writing each chapter's catalogue entries (that chapter's registry bead); running
controls (N38b-04); controls for the 325 rows that state none (N38b-28 to N38b-34).

## Technical approach

**Catalogue record (`registry/counterexamples.toml`).** `id` (CX-nnn, append-only), `shortcut`, `case`,
`numeric` (true, or false for "no numeric case is given"), `rule` (text), `rule_ids` (list: GATE-…, KILL-…,
INT-…, INV-…, PO-…, ADR-…, FROB-REF-…, C-nn, D-nn, T-nn, BET-nn or a section), `defined_in`, `same_as` (an
earlier CX using the same fixture), `label` (DESIGN or FACT with source; a FACT case is re-run in this
repository before it is registered as a control).

**Kind of shortcut → kind of control.**

| Kind of shortcut | Kind of control | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A wrong formula or implementation choice | `mutation`: a test build in which the shortcut replaces the rule | Explicit Euler that updates the current before the wheel speed; a pyramidal friction cone; a two-statement idempotency check |
| A wrong inference from a right computation | `planted-input`: a request that asks for the unsound conclusion and must be refused | A tie verdict at n = 64; a certificate offered with its quantifiers reordered; a scale-fitted estimator score |
| A wrong screen or prune | `planted-design`: a design the shortcut would accept or discard wrongly | A body the support-polygon screen accepts and the exact classifier rejects; the two-tick bus schedule that an additive peak rule prunes |
| A wrong assumption about worlds | `planted-world`: a world or world pair that separates the shortcut from the rule | The T-junction alias; the third docking gain 33/32; the doorway narrower than the body |
| A wrong experimental procedure | `a-a` or a scripted adversary | Peeking at a fixed-n test; resubmission to reused clusters; audit bits chosen after the outcomes |

**Gate resolution.** An entry is registered against the gates of the rule it protects. At plan time 117 entries
name a gate, kill gate or admission gate in their rule cell; 267 name only an invariant, a proof obligation, a
decision, a refusal code, a construction or a section. For those the tool resolves through
`registry/rule_gates.toml` (rule identifier → gate identifiers), filled from the gate file: an invariant or
obligation resolves to the gate whose test checks it; a refusal code to the gate that exercises it; a decision,
construction or section to the gates whose defining subsection is that section.

**Algorithm (`tests/seed_controls.rs --ignored seed`, idempotent).**
1. Load the catalogue, the known-answer registry and `gates.toml`; validate counts and `same_as` targets.
2. For each CX entry in order: choose the kind by the table (recorded in the entry as `control_kind`); resolve
   gates; if the entry is already in `registry/seed_map.toml`, keep its NC number; else allocate the next free
   number (NC-0412, NC-0398 and NC-0399 are reserved for the wheel-loop mutation of CX-262 and the two controls
   of GATE-ARENA-05). One control per entry: the same case stated in two chapters protects two rules and gets
   two controls, each caught by its own rule's gates.
3. For each known-answer row whose control cell is stated: if its planted defect is the one of a CX-seeded
   control with the same target, write that NC into the row's `control.nc`; else allocate a new control with
   `seed = "KA-nnn"` and the gate that owns the row.
4. An entry with `numeric = false`, or a FACT case not yet re-run here, is written with `state = "obligation"`:
   it has an identifier and gates but is not runnable and does not make a gate eligible.
5. An entry whose rule resolves to no gate is written to `registry/seed_findings.toml` with the entry, the rule
   and a required disposition: `gate-added` (which) or `withdrawn` (reason). Findings are reported at the next
   milestone exit.
6. Write `controls.toml`, `seed_map.toml` and the findings; never rewrite an existing identifier.

**Obligations at plan time.** (a) The seventeen word-only entries, each needing a concrete planted defect from
its owning chapter (proposals, DESIGN): CX-149 a world inside the fidelity envelope whose task decision differs;
CX-150 a build that scores a solver give-up as a failed episode; CX-166 two designs of one orbit placed on both
sides of a seed split; CX-168 and CX-187 swept volumes that overlap in space at different times; CX-175 a build
without diagonal scaling of the Newton gradient; CX-199 a build that derives friction from gloss; CX-202 linear
pose blending across an impact inside an exposure; CX-212 the Euclidean projector under anisotropic weights;
CX-215 noise drawn in call order; CX-243 the fixed-base pullback τ = JᵀF on a floating base; CX-269 a mission
verdict offered from concatenated stage episodes; CX-301 an attribution cited as an established cause; CX-312
exogenous draws paired by event order; CX-322 an impulse channel downsampled by averaging; CX-337 one limb's load
history used for its mirror; CX-339 unsigned von Mises stress fed to a cycle counter. (b) The pre-slip aliasing
fixture (CX-012) is stated without numbers everywhere; its numeric instance is owed by the energy-budget tier.
(c) The five fixtures the thesis requires are present and must each resolve to a runnable control except (b):
the quantifier-order fixture (CX-015), the midpoint counterexample (CX-010), the coupled-bus fixture (CX-011),
the pre-slip aliasing fixture (CX-012) and the omitted-law fixture (CX-013).

**The join (PO-GAUNT-03; `tests/seed_join.rs`).** Left-join catalogue → seed map → controls → gates, and stated
control cells → controls → gates; any unmatched row fails, and so does a control whose `seed` names a missing
entry.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The loader asserts 384 entries, the per-chapter counts above, 17 word-only and 13 `same_as`.
- [ ] After seeding, every CX entry and every stated control cell (510 at plan time) maps to a control with at
      least one existing gate, or appears in the findings file with a disposition requested.
- [ ] Re-running the seeding changes no byte; adding one catalogue entry appends exactly one control.
- [ ] The five thesis fixtures resolve; CX-012 is `obligation` with the reason "no numeric instance".
- [ ] `seed_join` fails on each planted omission (below) and passes on the seeded tree.
- [ ] A FACT entry carries `rerun = false` until its case has been reproduced in this repository.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/seed_join.rs` and `seed_controls.rs`:
- `catalogue_counts`: 384, 17, 13 and the table.
- `kind_by_shortcut`: CX-262 (Euler order) → `mutation`; CX-015 (quantifier order) → `planted-input`; CX-282
  (third gain 33/32) → `planted-world`; the support-polygon screen (1,596 bodies wrongly accepted) →
  `planted-design`; peeking (false-positive rate 13/256 after 8 looks) → `a-a`.
- `two_rules_two_controls`: CX-270 (`same_as` CX-010) yields a second control with its own gates.
- `rule_without_gate_is_finding`: a scratch entry whose rule names only a section with no gate.
- `seeding_idempotent`, `append_one_entry_appends_one_control`, `reserved_numbers_kept`.
- `join_no_unmatched_row`; `join_catches_missing_control`; `join_catches_missing_gate`.
- `word_only_is_obligation`: CX-301 is not runnable and makes no gate eligible.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/seed_controls.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-05 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet,registry
gates=GATE-GAUNT-01 plants=drop-seed-row,orphan-control`). It runs the seeding twice (second run must be a
no-op by digest), runs the join, then in a scratch copy deletes one seed-map row and adds one orphan control;
each must fail the join. Log at `target/e2e-logs/seed_controls/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit codes and wall
time; `metric` lines for entries, controls, obligations and findings (thresholds: unmatched = 0); the digest of
`controls.toml` before and after the second run; the two negative-control assertions; the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the counts 384, 17, 13, 510 and 117 against 267. Negative controls: the dropped seed row, the
orphan control, a renumbered NC, a word-only entry marked runnable. Determinism: class D0; `controls.toml` is
byte-identical on both platforms.

## Considerations

- An entry whose rule has no gate is a finding about the design, not a defect of the seeding; it is never
  silently attached to an unrelated gate.
- Counterexamples found in use are appended to the catalogue and become permanent controls (N38b-04).
- Each chapter owns its controls' substance; this bead owns identifiers, links and completeness.
- The risk register's detectors are exercised the same way: each has a planted instance registered here as a
  control so that its signal is known to fire (a dummy dependency that enables two conflicting library
  features; a second exact pin on one package; a sampler with correct marginals and a wrong joint law; the
  rare-tail arm of the tournament's pre-analysis). Their gate belongs to the risk chapter.
- The reserved numbers exist only because examples in the documents use them; no meaning attaches to a number.
