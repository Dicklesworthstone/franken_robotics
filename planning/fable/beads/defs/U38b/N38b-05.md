## Background

FrankenRobotics treats counterexamples as first-class: where a plausible shortcut is wrong, the design states
the smallest hand-checkable case that breaks it. The Gauntlet (verification subsystem, crate `frob-conformance`)
compiles them in a counterexample catalogue: 384 entries at plan time, CX-001 to CX-384, grouped by chapter
(FACT, counted). Each entry has the tempting shortcut, the smallest case with its numbers, the rule it protects
and where it is defined. The known-answer suite (835 rows) also states a negative control for 510 of its rows
(835 less the 325 printed `none stated`).

The negative-control registry (`crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml`; some documents call it
`registries/negative_controls.toml`, a logical alias) is seeded from both: each catalogue entry becomes at least
one control attached to the gates of the rule it protects, and each stated `Negative control` cell of the
known-answer suite becomes one. This bead is that seeding, with proof obligation PO-GAUNT-03: the seed is
complete, meaning every CX entry and every stated control cell maps to a control with at least one gate, checked
by a join over the three registries with no unmatched row.

DESIGN: seeding is a deterministic, repeatable tool with a committed map, not a hand transcription. Reason: 894
source items cannot be kept consistent by hand, and identifiers are append-only. Rejected: generating controls
at test time from the catalogue (identifiers would not be stable and a control could not carry its history).

## Scope

In: the catalogue data file schema; the mapping from kind of shortcut to kind of control; gate resolution; the
seeding tool; the seed map; the findings list; the join test; the seventeen word-only entries and the pre-slip
fixture as obligations. Out: writing each chapter's catalogue entries (that chapter's registry bead
`N<chapter>-CX`; for the contact chapter N18-CX, which enters CX-167 to CX-183 and four appended entries with
their plants, and depends on this bead); running controls (N38b-04); controls for the 325 rows that state none
(N38b-28 to N38b-34). The division is the same for every chapter: the chapter's bead writes
`registry/counterexamples.toml` entries and never a row of `controls.toml`; this bead's tool writes
`controls.toml` and never the substance of an entry.

## Technical approach

**Catalogue record (`registry/counterexamples.toml`).** `id` (CX-nnn, append-only), `shortcut`, `case`,
`numeric` (true, or false for "no numeric case is given"; it turns true when a chapter later supplies a case),
`plan_case` (`"words"` or `"numbers"`: the form of the case in the plan's text, immutable, so that the
plan-time count of word-only entries does not move when a case is supplied), `case_supplied_by` (empty, or the
bead and fixture, or a later CX entry that supplied the numeric case, whether or not it carries `same_as` this
one: CX-012's case comes from the reflex-untimely entry that is `same_as = "CX-012"`, CX-215's from CX-371, a
plan-time entry that is not marked ≡; the entry named must exist, be later and have `numeric = true`), `rule`
(text), `rule_ids` (list: GATE-…, KILL-…,
INT-…, INV-…, PO-…, ADR-…, FROB-REF-…, C-nn, D-nn, T-nn, BET-nn or a section), `defined_in`, `same_as` (an
earlier CX using the same fixture), `label` (DESIGN or FACT with source; a FACT case is re-run in this
repository before it is registered as a control), `rerun` (Boolean, present on FACT entries only: `false` until
the case has been reproduced in this repository, then `true` with the receipt hash in `rerun_receipt`),
`control_kind` (the kind of the entry's control by the table below; the default kind of each of its plants),
`plant` (the entry's planted defects; empty while the entry is an obligation with no plant named), `due`
(optional: the milestone at which the entry's controls fall due, copied to each control's optional `due` of
BD-025, so that a chapter states its due milestones in its rows and keeps no compiled-in table of them) and
`slug` (optional: the working name of an entry appended after plan time, which had no CX number when its
chapter first stated it).

`plant` is a list of plant records, one per planted defect, because one shortcut can need several plants (CX-358
and CX-360 two each and CX-362 three in the workspace chapter, CX-342 two in Foundry, four on the second Cortex
late entry): `{ id, kind, target, defect, expect, gates, feature, fixture, removed_check }`. `id` is required
and has the form `<planting bead>:<name>` (for example `SPEC-C-01:cx-019`, `N13-18:M2`,
`N34-18:writer-no-key-sort`); the other fields are optional, and the chapter fills them when its table states
them per plant (N34-CX, N31-CX, N23-CX do). `removed_check` names the check disabled in the build or input that
carries a planted input whose `expect` is the product's own refusal (BD-025's rule 5: without it such a control
is rejected `boundary-not-control`); N40-CX, N41-CX, N39-CX, N18-CX (CX-176) and N23-CX state it per plant. A plain string is accepted as the one-plant short form, `plant = "SPEC-C-01:cx-019"` reading as `[{ id =
"SPEC-C-01:cx-019" }]` (DESIGN: chapter beads that state one plant per row keep their form). Each plant becomes
one control (step 2), with its own kind, target, defect, expectation and gates.

**One parser, this bead's validation.** The file has one parser, `testkit::registry::Counterexamples` of
STD-TEST (one reader per file). This bead is the schema owner: its validation
`frob_conformance::counterexamples::validate` (the counts below, the `same_as` and `case_supplied_by` targets,
the FACT `rerun` check, the pilot agreement below) runs on the rows that parser returns, and the seeding tool and
the join of this bead read the same rows; the parser's type carries every field of this record, the optional
ones and the plant fields included. An unknown field is a load error naming the file and the entry. The
validation's errors are typed and name the entry (reason vocabulary of N38-REF): `cx-count-mismatch` (a
chapter's or the plan-time count differs from the table below), `same-as-invalid` (`same_as` names a missing or
later entry), `case-supplier-invalid` (`case_supplied_by` names a missing or earlier entry, or one with
`numeric = false`), `fact-without-rerun` (a FACT entry with no `rerun` field), `plant-missing` (a runnable entry
with an empty `plant`) and `rerun-disagrees-with-pilot` (below); the seeding join's are `unmatched-seed`,
`orphan-control` and `rule-without-gate`.

**Pilot-derived entries (DESIGN).** A FACT entry whose `label` names a pilot record of the pilot register
(`PILOT-…`, `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, N38a-09) must agree with that record: through the
register's status read (`pilots::rerun_status`, the mapping N38a-12 uses for pilot-derived known-answer rows),
`rerun = true` with `rerun_receipt` equal to the receipt of the record's result exactly when the status is
`reproduced`, and `rerun = false` otherwise. A disagreement is a load error, `rerun-disagrees-with-pilot`, naming
the entry and the record, so that the catalogue's pair can never say a case was reproduced while the register
says it was not run or differs. A FACT entry from a sibling source or a publication keeps its own pair.

**When the counts are checked.** The entries arrive chapter by chapter, with each chapter's registry bead, and
some of those beads belong to a later milestone than this one. The loader therefore checks a chapter's count
(the table below) once that chapter has any entry, lists a chapter with none as `pending: N<chapter>-CX`, and
checks the total of 384 when no chapter is pending; entries appended after CX-384 are counted separately as
`appended`. Every plan-time count (the 384, the 17 word-only entries and the 13 entries with `same_as`) is taken
over CX-001 to CX-384 only: appended entries may carry `same_as` (the overlaps of N33-CX, N34-CX, N39-CX and
N41-CX; an appended entry `same_as = "CX-002"`) and are reported in the `appended` count with their own
`same_as` total, never added to the 13. The tool, the schema, the join and this bead's own tests are complete at M0 on the chapters present
and on `synthetic` catalogues.

**Kind of shortcut → kind of control.**

| Kind of shortcut | Kind of control | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A wrong formula or implementation choice | `mutation`: a test build in which the shortcut replaces the rule | Explicit Euler that updates the current before the wheel speed; a pyramidal friction cone; a two-statement idempotency check |
| A wrong inference from a right computation | `planted-input`: a request that asks for the unsound conclusion and must be refused | A tie verdict at n = 64; a certificate offered with its quantifiers reordered; a scale-fitted estimator score |
| A wrong screen or prune | `planted-design`: a design the shortcut would accept or discard wrongly | A body the support-polygon screen accepts and the exact classifier rejects; the two-tick bus schedule that an additive peak rule prunes |
| A wrong assumption about worlds | `planted-world`: a world or world pair that separates the shortcut from the rule | The T-junction alias; the third docking gain 33/32; the doorway narrower than the body |
| A wrong experimental procedure | `a-a` or a scripted adversary | Peeking at a fixed-n test; resubmission to reused clusters; audit bits chosen after the outcomes |

**Gate resolution.** An entry is registered against the gates of the rule it protects. At plan time 117 entries
name a gate, kill gate or admission gate in their rule cell; 267 name only an invariant, a proof obligation, a
decision, a refusal code, a construction or a section. For those the tool resolves through
`registry/rule_gates.toml` (rule identifier → gate identifiers), filled from the gate file: an invariant or
obligation resolves to the gate whose test checks it; a refusal code to the gate that exercises it; a decision,
construction or section to the gates whose defining subsection is that section.

**Algorithm (`tests/seed_controls.rs --ignored seed`, idempotent).**
1. Load the catalogue, the known-answer registry and `gates.toml`; validate counts and `same_as` targets.
2. For each CX entry in order, and for each of its plants in list order (an entry with no plant yet gives one
   control, an obligation by step 4): if the pair (entry, plant `id`) is already in `registry/seed_map.toml`,
   keep its NC number; else allocate the next free number (NC-0412, NC-0398 and NC-0399 are reserved for the
   wheel-loop mutation of CX-262 and the two controls of GATE-ARENA-05). One control per plant: the same case
   stated in two chapters protects two rules and gets two controls, each caught by its own rule's gates. The
   control's fields of BD-025 are filled as follows, so that no control is written with a field BD-025's loader
   rejects (`defect-or-expect-empty`, `no-gate`, `feature-missing`, `fixture-missing`):
   | Control field | From the plant, when the chapter states it | Otherwise (the derivation rule) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `seed` | — | The entry's CX number |
   | `kind` | The plant's `kind` | The entry's `control_kind` |
   | `gate` | The plant's `gates` | The gates resolved from the entry's `rule_ids` (gate resolution, above) |
   | `target` | The plant's `target` (code path, verb, fixture or procedure) | The plant's `id`, which names the planting bead and the plant; with no plant yet, the entry's CX number |
   | `defect` | The plant's `defect` | The entry's `shortcut`: the plant puts the shortcut into the component |
   | `expect` | The plant's `expect` | The entry's `case`: the smallest case states the observable refutation (a value, a refusal, a failed row) |
   | `feature` (a `mutation`) | The plant's `feature` | The name after the colon of the plant's `id` (`SPEC-C-01:cx-019` → `cx-019`), the Cargo feature the planting bead declares in the target crate; with no plant yet, `cx-<nnn>` from the entry's CX number |
   | `fixture` (the other kinds) | The plant's `fixture` | The name after the colon of the plant's `id`, the fixture the planting bead writes with its `synthetic` header; with no plant yet, `cx-<nnn>` from the entry's CX number |
   | `removed_check` | The plant's `removed_check` | Absent |
   | `due` | — | The entry's `due`, when it has one |
   | `state` | — | `obligation` by step 4, else `runnable` |
   The tool writes `seed`, `kind` and `target` once and never rewrites them (BD-025 rule 1, `renumbered`), with
   one exception, applied only while the control is an obligation or its plant is unbuilt: a target that is
   still the placeholder (the plant's `id`, or the CX number of an entry with no plant) becomes the target the
   chapter or the planting bead later states for that plant. Any other change of a plant's kind or target after
   its control exists is written to the findings file (step 5) as `plant-changed` with the old and the new text;
   the chapter restores the text or appends a plant with a new `id`, which gets a new control. `feature`,
   `fixture`, `due` and `state` are refreshed on every seeding, and gates are only added. The `feature` written
   is the name the control runner builds (`--features gauntlet-mutations,<crate>/<feature>`), so a mutation
   feature is named by its plant (`cx-262` for NC-0412), not by its NC number.
3. For each known-answer row whose control cell is stated: a cell may name several plants (KA-243, KA-244 and
   KA-256 each name more than one of N13-18's mutation identifiers). Resolve each plant separately: if a control
   with the same plant and the same target already exists, whether its seed is a CX entry or an earlier KA row,
   append that NC to the row's `control.nc` (a list, in the order the cell names the plants); else allocate a
   new control with `seed = "KA-nnn"`. Each mutation therefore has exactly one control, however many rows name
   it. A KA-seeded control attaches to the gate that owns the row and to every gate the row's control cell
   names (for example GATE-MARROW-04 for KA-244, KA-247 and KA-249); a reused control gains those gates. Its
   other fields: `kind` by the table applied to the cell (`mutation` for a named mutant); `target` the plant
   identifier the cell names, or the row's `fixture` for a planted input or world; `defect` the cell's text for
   that plant; `expect` "KA-nnn fails: the observed value leaves the row's tolerance about its truth" (a failed
   row, the observable every known-answer control states); `feature` or `fixture` by the rule above;
   `removed_check` the cell's `removed_check` (the optional field of a stated control cell, N38a-12's row,
   beside `text` and `nc`; N30a-15 and BD-390 state it), otherwise absent; `due` the row's `due`.
4. An entry with `numeric = false` and an empty `case_supplied_by`, or a FACT entry with `rerun = false`, is
   written with `state = "obligation"`: it has an identifier and gates but is not runnable and does not make a
   gate eligible. An entry whose case is supplied later becomes runnable on the next seeding with the same NC
   number: either its chapter sets `numeric = true` and fills `case_supplied_by` (CX-243), or the tool fills
   `case_supplied_by` by the rule of obligation (b) below while `numeric` stays as the chapter wrote it (CX-012:
   its case is the supplying entry's). Filling `case_supplied_by` by that rule is the one write this tool makes
   into `registry/counterexamples.toml`.
5. An entry whose rule resolves to no gate is written to `registry/seed_findings.toml` with the entry, the rule
   and a required disposition: `gate-added` (which) or `withdrawn` (reason); a `plant-changed` finding (step 2)
   requires `restored` or `new-plant` (its `id`). Findings are reported at the next milestone exit.
6. Write `controls.toml`, `seed_map.toml` (keyed by entry and plant `id`) and the findings; never rewrite an
   existing identifier.

**The join (PO-GAUNT-03; `tests/seed_join.rs`).** Left-join catalogue → seed map → controls → gates, and stated
control cells → controls → gates (one join row per plant of a multi-plant cell); any unmatched row fails, and so
does a control whose `seed` names a missing entry. Each seed form is resolved in its own registry (CX in the
catalogue, KA in the known-answer registry, and the other forms BD-025's loader admits in theirs: `MR-nn` and
`MR-Cnn` in the relation registry, `CERT-nn` in the certifier register, `INV-…` and `PO-…` through
`registry/rule_gates.toml`, `RISK-nn` to a row `id` of the risk register `registries/risks.toml` (N41-01),
`SAFE-Xnn` in the safety catalogue, `GATE-…` in `gates.toml`). The tool writes records of the forms `CX-nnn`
and `KA-nnn` only (BD-025, "Who writes a record"); a record of any other form is appended by the bead that owns
what its seed names, and the join only resolves it. In particular a `RISK-nn` control is registered by the

only for a risk row that has no control of the detector's owner; the four late rows (RISK-56 to RISK-59) use the
controls this tool seeds from N41-CX's entries X41-06, X41-01, X41-07 and X41-08.

- [ ] Every seeded control has a non-empty `target`, `defect` and `expect`, at least one gate, and a `feature`

  and one new, yields `control.nc = [existing, new]`; a second row naming the new plant reuses it.
- `ka_control_gets_cell_gates`: KA-244's control carries GATE-MARROW-04 and the row's owner gate.
- `fact_entry_obligation_until_rerun`: a `synthetic` catalogue entry labelled FACT with `rerun = false` is an
  obligation and makes no gate eligible; with `rerun = true` and a `rerun_receipt` it is runnable with the same
  NC number; a FACT entry without the `rerun` field is rejected by the loader.
- `pilot_entry_agrees_with_register`: in a scratch register, a FACT entry naming `PILOT-PT-06` with `rerun = true`
  while the record is `registered` is refused `rerun-disagrees-with-pilot`; with the record `reproduced` and
  `rerun_receipt` equal to its result's receipt it loads; with the record `differs` and `rerun = true` it is
  refused.
- `seeded_fields_filled`: a one-plant entry with no per-plant texts (plant `SPEC-C-01:cx-019`, kind `mutation`)
  yields `target = "SPEC-C-01:cx-019"`, `defect` = its `shortcut`, `expect` = its `case`, `feature = "cx-019"`;
  a planted-world entry yields `fixture = "cx-nnn"`; a `synthetic` word-only `planted-input` entry with no plant
  yields an obligation whose `target` is its CX number and `fixture` is `cx-<nnn>`; the seeded file passes BD-025's `validate` with
  no `defect-or-expect-empty`, `no-gate`, `kind-not-closed`, `feature-missing` or `fixture-missing`.
- `plant_list_one_control_per_plant`: a synthetic entry shaped like CX-362 (three plants: two `planted-input`
  and one `mutation`, each with its own target, defect, expectation and gates) yields three controls whose fields
  equal the plant texts; re-seeding is a no-op; appending a fourth plant appends one control.
- `plant_string_short_form`: `plant = "N13-18:M2"` and `plant = [{ id = "N13-18:M2" }]` seed byte-identical
  controls.
- `entry_due_copied`: an entry with `due = "M2"` gives each of its controls `due = "M2"`.
- `target_written_once`: a placeholder target becomes the planting bead's code path on the next seeding; a
  second change of that target is not applied and appears in the findings as `plant-changed`.
- `case_supplied_without_same_as`: CX-215 with `case_supplied_by = "CX-371"` (no `same_as`) loads; naming an
  earlier entry, or one with `numeric = false`, is refused.
- `same_as_count_plan_time_only`: appended entries with `same_as` (one `same_as = "CX-002"`) leave the plan-time
  count at 13 and are reported under `appended`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/seed_controls.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-05 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet,registry
gates=GATE-GAUNT-01 plants=drop-seed-row,orphan-control,pilot-pair-disagrees`). It runs the seeding twice
(`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test seed_controls -- --ignored seed`; the second run must
be a no-op by digest), runs the join (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test seed_join`) and BD-025's
`registry_check` on the seeded `controls.toml` (exit 0: the loader accepts every seeded control), then in a
scratch copy deletes one seed-map row (`unmatched-seed`), adds one orphan control (`orphan-control`), and sets
`rerun = true` on a FACT entry whose pilot record is `registered` (`rerun-disagrees-with-pilot`); each must
fail with its reason. Log through the STD-E2E helper at `target/e2e-logs/seed_controls/<utc>.ndjson`: commands
with exit codes and wall time; `metric` lines for entries (plan-time and appended), pending chapters,
controls, obligations and findings (thresholds: unmatched = 0); the digest of `controls.toml` before and after
the second run; the three negative-control assertions with the reason printed; on failure the first unmatched
join row and the scratch path; the evidence block (claim `known-answer`, class `Tested`, determinism D0,
seed 0); the `summary` naming GATE-GAUNT-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the counts 384, 17, 13, 510 and 117 against 267. Negative controls: the dropped seed row, the
orphan control, a renumbered NC, a word-only entry marked runnable, a pilot-derived pair that disagrees with
its register record, a multi-plant entry with one plant's control removed. Determinism: class D0; `controls.toml` is

## Considerations

- An entry whose rule has no gate is a finding about the design, not a defect of the seeding; it is never
  silently attached to an unrelated gate.
- Counterexamples found in use are appended to the catalogue and become permanent controls (N38b-04).
- Each chapter owns its controls' substance; this bead owns identifiers, links and completeness.
- The risk register's detectors are exercised the same way: each has a planted instance registered as a
  control so that its signal is known to fire (a dummy dependency that enables two conflicting library
  features; a second exact pin on one package; a sampler with correct marginals and a wrong joint law; the
  rare-tail arm of the tournament's pre-analysis). Those records carry `seed = "RISK-nn"` and are written by the
  risk-detector beads N41-32 to N41-37, not by this tool; the join resolves them in `registries/risks.toml`.
- The reserved numbers exist only because examples in the documents use them; no meaning attaches to a number.
