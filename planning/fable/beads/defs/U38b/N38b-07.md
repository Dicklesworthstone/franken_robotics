## Background

FrankenRobotics lets tests and pilots use things that are not the real system: a *synthetic fixture* (data
constructed to have a known answer), a *stand-in* (a simulated device, a second simulator playing the part of
reality) and the *development oracle* (one incumbent simulator at a pinned release, used for small pilots
before the project's own trusted CPU tier exists). Each is useful and each is dangerous when its output is
later read as if it came from the real thing. The verification subsystem (the Gauntlet, crate
`frob-conformance`) states the rule twice: among the guards against false progress, "Synthetic is labeled:
output computed from a fixture or a stand-in prints `synthetic` in its evidence"; and as invariant
INV-GAUNT-04: evidence computed from a fixture, a stand-in or the development oracle is labelled `synthetic` or
`development-oracle`, the label is propagated through the claim product, and a planted unlabeled record is
refused.

The *claim product* is the typed summary every result carries (claim kind, evidence class, tier, determinism
class, reality scope, condition flags, inputs). Combination takes the union of condition flags, so a label
placed on one input reaches everything built on it without anyone remembering to copy it. DESIGN: the two
labels are condition flags of the claim product, set where the evidence is created and never removable.
Rejected: a naming convention on files or a field in test reports only (neither survives a combination).

## Scope

In: the two flags and where they are set; propagation; printing; the refusals; the fixture header check; the
planted unlabeled record. Out: the development-oracle lane itself (N38a-06,
which depends on this bead: its adapter creates the leaf with `origin-development-oracle` and its tests offer a
stripped copy of a lane record); the reality statuses (the status `synthetic`
of a Reality comparison is the calibration subsystem's; this flag is the general origin label).

## Technical approach

**Flags.** `origin-synthetic` and `origin-development-oracle` join the claim product's condition flag set
(beside `tier-sensitive`, `controller-sensitive`, `model-conditional`). They serialize in the envelope's
evidence block as `"origin": ["synthetic"]` or `["development-oracle"]` and print in reports as the word
`synthetic` or `development-oracle` beside the result.

**Where a flag is set (the only three places).**
1. The fixture reader: every fixture file begins with a kind header, `synthetic`, `oracle` (with pin, date and
   capture script) or `recorded`. Loading a `synthetic` fixture yields a leaf claim product with
   `origin-synthetic`. A fixture without a header does not load.
2. Stand-in devices and stand-in realities: the simulated device of the daemon tests and any simulator standing
   in for reality create leaves with `origin-synthetic`.
3. The development-oracle adapter: every result it returns is a leaf with `origin-development-oracle`.

**Propagation.** `ClaimProduct::combine` takes the union of flags; no function removes a flag; a compile-fail
test pins the absence of a public remover. A result re-run on the real tier is a new result with new inputs:
the label disappears only because the labelled input is no longer among the inputs.

**Consequences enforced.**
- A result carrying either flag cannot be the evidence of a gate whose claim kind is `reality`, and a
  development-oracle result supports no gate at all: it funds or kills a work package and nothing more.
- Pooling synthetic with real data is refused (existing refusal FROB-REF-814).
- A record offered to the gate recorder whose inputs include a labelled leaf but whose own evidence block lacks
  the label is refused: the recorder recomputes the product from the inputs and compares (existing refusal for
  a result presented above its claim product, FROB-REF-819).
- Where (DESIGN, as for the claim-kind check of N38a-05): this bead adds one function,
  `origin_labels_admissible(row, resolved_evidence)`, to the hook list of the gate recorder's step 7 (N38b-19, in
  this same crate). It recomputes the claim product of each evidence item from its inputs and returns the
  harness reason `origin-label-mismatch` (the recomputed product carries `origin-synthetic` or
  `origin-development-oracle` and the presented evidence block does not: the recorder's form of FROB-REF-819;
  repairs: cite the receipt as produced; re-run on the real tier) or `origin-not-admissible` (an evidence item
  carries `origin-development-oracle`, for any gate; or carries `origin-synthetic` for a gate whose claim is
  `reality`; repairs: re-run on the CPU referee; record a non-reality claim), process exit 3, nothing written.
  Both reasons are registered in the harness-reason registry (`registry/reasons.toml`, N38-REF).
- Known-answer gates may cite synthetic fixtures; that is what a known answer is. The label still prints.

**Lint (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/origin_labels.rs`).** Every file under any `fixtures/` or `corpora/`
directory has a kind header; every test that loads a `synthetic` fixture and emits an evidence line has
`synthetic: true` in that line; no report template omits the origin field.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A result computed from a synthetic fixture prints `synthetic` in its envelope and report; combined with
      unlabelled inputs, the product still carries the flag.
- [ ] A planted unlabeled record (inputs include a synthetic leaf, evidence block without the flag) is refused
      by the recorder.
- [ ] A development-oracle result is refused as evidence for any gate; a synthetic result is refused for a
      `reality` gate.
- [ ] No public function removes an origin flag (compile-fail test).
- [ ] A fixture without a kind header fails to load and fails the lint.
- [ ] Pooling a synthetic dataset with a real one is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-core/tests/claim_product_origin.rs` and `crates/frob-conformance/tests/origin_labels.rs`:
- `synthetic_leaf_from_fixture`: loading a fixture headed `synthetic` yields the flag.
- `flag_union_under_combine`: {synthetic, plain, plain} → synthetic; {development-oracle, synthetic} → both.
- `combine_order_independent_for_flags` (property test over random flag sets).
- `no_flag_remover` (compile-fail).
- `unlabeled_record_refused`: recomputed product ≠ presented evidence → the recorder exits 3 with
  `reason=origin-label-mismatch` and `gates.toml` is byte-identical.
- `dev_oracle_supports_no_gate` (`origin-not-admissible` for a known-answer gate);
  `synthetic_refused_for_reality_gate` (`origin-not-admissible`); a synthetic result for a known-answer gate is
  recorded with the label printed.
- `headerless_fixture_does_not_load`.
- `report_prints_origin`: the rendered line contains the word `synthetic`.
- `rerun_on_real_tier_drops_label`: a result whose inputs no longer include the labelled leaf has no flag.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/origin_labels.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-07 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet gates=GATE-GAUNT-01
plants=strip-label,headerless-fixture`). It runs a known-answer row on a synthetic fixture
(`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers -- <row>`) and checks the receipt's
evidence `origin`; offers that receipt for a scratch gate of claim `reality` (`cargo run -p frob-conformance
--example record_gate -- --gate <scratch gate> --receipt <hash>`: exit 3, `origin-not-admissible`); strips the
label from a copy of the receipt and offers it for the row's own gate (exit 3, `origin-label-mismatch`, and
`gates.toml` byte-identical); adds a headerless fixture (`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test origin_labels`
fails). Log at `target/e2e-logs/origin_labels/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time; the evidence
block of each envelope; refusal events with reasons; assertions; the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Negative controls: the stripped label; the headerless fixture; a hand-written combine that drops flags (must
fail the property test). Live-dependency proof: replacing the synthetic fixture by the real tier's output
removes the flag. Metamorphic relation: the claim product is order-independent and never raises a class; the
same holds for flags (a flag is never lost). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- The label is not a mark of low quality: exact known answers are synthetic by nature. It states origin so that
  no reader mistakes origin for measurement.
- When the same pilot is re-run on the project's own CPU tier and the verdict differs from the
  development-oracle run, the own-tier verdict stands and the difference counts toward the kill gate on the
  development oracle (N38b-24).
- Logs never contain private-scene data; a synthetic fixture derived from a private scene inherits the privacy
  mark as well as the origin flag.
