## Background

A training pipeline can run end to end, write checkpoints, export a policy and print a number while having
learned nothing. A test that only checks the pipeline ran is a *plumbing* test. The verification subsystem of
FrankenRobotics (the Gauntlet, crate `frob-conformance`) lists the answer among its guards against false
progress:

- **Behaviour, not plumbing.** A learning gate needs a learned behaviour and failing controls: zero-weight,
  random and shuffled-label policies must fail the same threshold.

The smallest case that shows the difference (carried in the counterexample catalogue): an exported all-zero
policy head passes a plumbing test; it must fail the return threshold. This bead makes the controls a
standing requirement of every gate that claims a learned behaviour, as a lint over the gate register, so that
such a gate cannot pass without them.

Which controls a gate needs is the learning chapter's rule, and this bead applies it unchanged: a training gate
record holds "a zero-weight policy and a random policy that both fail the same threshold, a shuffled-label
student where distillation is involved, and a replay of the exported checkpoint that reproduces the behaviour".
So the zero-weight and the random control are required of every learning gate, and the shuffled-label control
of a gate whose claim involves distillation. The register agrees: the M2 training gate GATE-CORTEX-01 names the
zero-weight and the random policy, and the shuffled-label control is the negative control of the distillation
gate GATE-CORTEX-14 (M4). A reinforcement-learning run has no teacher labels to shuffle. The record type
`TrainingGateRecord`, the builders of the controls, the policy forms that reach them, their registration and
the refusal FROB-REF-544 are N23b-15's; this bead adds the Gauntlet's side: the marking of learning gates, the
lint, the margin rule and the planted plumbing-only gate.

Rejected alternative: a single "untrained policy" control. Each of the three catches a different defect: a
zero policy catches thresholds that standing still can meet; a random policy catches thresholds any motion can
meet; a shuffled-label student catches a learner whose improvement does not come from the labels.

## Scope

In: the definition of a learning gate and its marking in the register; which controls each must list; the
lint; the margin rule; the planted plumbing-only gate. Out: the record, the builders and policy forms of the
controls, their registration in the negative-control registry and the refusal FROB-REF-544 (N23b-15, a
dependency); the shuffled-label run of a distillation (`frob train distill … --control shuffled-labels`, BD-323,
M4), which this bead's lint requires of GATE-CORTEX-14 and does not run; the training algorithm and its own
known answers; exploitation detectors; the return thresholds (the learning subsystem's).

## Technical approach

**Which gates.** A gate row carries `learned = true` when its threshold is a statement about a trained policy
(a return, a success rate, a tracking error, time to a threshold), and also `distilled = true` when that policy
is a student trained on a teacher's labels. The first learning gate is the M2 training gate on the reference
quadruped's flat velocity task, GATE-CORTEX-01; later ones include family policies, inheritance and
distillation (GATE-CORTEX-14, the first `distilled` row). DESIGN: the two are optional fields of `[[gate]]`,
which this bead adds to the register's one schema file (`frob.gates/1`, written by N38b-18 and extended by
N40-01, where an unknown field fails the load) and sets on the rows of the learning gates; a row without them
loads unchanged and is no learning gate.

**The controls** (NC records of kind `planted-design` with `seed = "CX-257"`, registered once per gate by
N23b-15; target the evaluation of the gate, expect "fails the gate's threshold"). Their `defect` fields are
exactly `zero-weight`, `random` and `shuffled-label`, which is how the lint finds them.

| Control | Required of | Construction (N23b-15's builders) | What it shows when it passes the threshold (a defect) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-weight | Every `learned` gate | The gate's network with every weight and bias zero and the observation normalizer kept, evaluated by the same evaluation as the trained policy: `frob eval run --policy zero:<spec>` | The threshold can be met by the null action, or the evaluation does not read the policy |
| Random | Every `learned` gate | The same network at its seeded random initialization, never updated, reproducible from `(spec, seed)`: `frob eval run --policy random:<spec>:<seed>` | The threshold can be met by arbitrary motion |
| Shuffled-label | Every `distilled` gate | A student trained by the gate's distillation procedure on teacher labels permuted across states by a keyed permutation; same budget, same seeds otherwise: `frob train distill … --control shuffled-labels` (BD-323, M4) | The improvement does not come from the signal: the metric rises for another reason (an exploit, a leak of the evaluation, a curriculum effect) |

**Rules.**
1. Every required control is evaluated on the same worlds, tier, horizon and threshold as the trained policy,
   in the same run (a control is run every time its gate is run).
2. Each must fail the threshold, with its measured value recorded beside the trained policy's. If any passes,
   the gate fails whatever the trained policy scored (N23b-15's verdict FAIL with reason
   `threshold-not-discriminating`).
3. The trained policy's margin over the best control is printed (difference with its interval); a gate whose
   margin interval contains zero is reported `behaviour-not-shown` even if the threshold is met.
4. A training gate record without its required controls, or without the replay of the exported checkpoint, is
   refused (FROB-REF-544, raised by N23b-15's checker, which names the missing element).
5. A policy trained under the labelled nondeterministic profile is re-evaluated under a stricter class before
   any of this is cited; the controls are evaluated under the same stricter class.

**Lint (`tests/learning_controls.rs`).** Every gate row with `learned = true` lists a control whose `defect`
field is exactly `zero-weight` and one whose `defect` is exactly `random`; a row with `distilled = true` lists
one whose `defect` is exactly `shuffled-label` as well; a missing one makes the gate `not-eligible`. A row with
`distilled = true` and no `learned = true` is a lint error.

**Planted plumbing-only gate (the negative control of this mechanism).** A fixture gate whose threshold is
"the exported policy file exists and evaluates without error"; the zero-weight control passes it, so the lint
and rule 2 must reject the gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every `learned` gate lists the zero-weight and the random control, and every `distilled` gate the
      shuffled-label control as well; a gate missing one is `not-eligible`. GATE-CORTEX-01, with its two
      controls and no shuffled-label one, is eligible.
- [ ] On the M2 training gate GATE-CORTEX-01 the zero-weight and the random control fail the return threshold
      and the trained policy passes it, all in one run; the margin over the best control is printed with its
      interval.
- [ ] The planted plumbing-only gate is rejected because the zero-weight control passes it.
- [ ] On a fixture row with `distilled = true`, a shuffled-label control whose record names a budget or a seed
      list other than the real run's is rejected, and so is one whose permutation is the identity. The first
      real `distilled` row, GATE-CORTEX-14, is measured at M4 by BD-323 under this lint.
- [ ] A training gate record submitted without its required controls is refused with FROB-REF-544.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/learning_controls.rs`:
- `zero_policy_is_all_zero`: every weight tensor of `zero:<spec>` is zero; its action is the null action.
- `random_policy_reproducible`: the same `random:<spec>:<seed>` gives identical weights on both machines.
- `shuffle_is_permutation`: on N23b-15's shuffled-label builder the multiset of teacher labels is unchanged,
  their assignment to states is not, and the permutation is a function of its key; the identity permutation
  is rejected.
- `controls_share_evaluation`: the evaluations of the trained policy and of each control name the same
  population identity, tier, horizon and threshold; a shuffled-label record with another budget or seed list
  than the real run's is rejected.
- `any_control_passing_fails_gate`.
- `margin_interval_contains_zero_is_flagged`.
- `lint_requires_controls`: a `learned` fixture row without `zero-weight`, or without `random`, is
  `not-eligible`; a `learned` row with those two and no `shuffled-label` is eligible; a `distilled` row with
  those two and no `shuffled-label` is `not-eligible`; a row with `distilled` and no `learned` is a lint error.
- `plumbing_only_gate_rejected`.
- `record_without_controls_refused` (FROB-REF-544, through N23b-15's checker).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/learning_controls.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-08 milestone=M2 labels=gauntlet,cortex
gates=GATE-GAUNT-01 plants=plumbing-only-gate`). Every run verb carries its own key from `e2e_key`. On
`batch-cpu` at a small budget it runs `frob train start quad-ref --task quad-flat-velocity --backend batch-cpu
--seed 1 --steps <small budget> --key <train> --json`, `frob job wait`, `frob train export`, and then one
evaluation each of the trained policy and the two controls on one population: `frob eval run --policy <id> |
zero:<id> | random:<id>:2 --task quad-flat-velocity --stream dev --tier batch-cpu --key <trained | zero |
random> --json`. It asserts that the three evaluations name one population, tier and threshold, that both
controls fail the threshold, and that the margin over the best control is printed with its interval and flagged
`behaviour-not-shown` when the interval contains zero (at this budget the trained policy need not reach the
threshold: the script tests the controls and the margin rule, and the full-budget measurement of GATE-CORTEX-01
is BD-198's); it runs the lint over the register; then the plant. Log at
`target/e2e-logs/learning_controls/<utc>.ndjson`: commands, exit codes, wall time; the three returns with units
against the threshold; the margin and its interval; the population identity; evidence blocks (tier,
determinism class, seed); the lint's result per learning gate; the plant's rejection; the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the zero policy's return on a velocity-tracking task equals the return of standing still, which
the task's reference scorecard states. Negative controls: the plumbing-only gate; a shuffled-label control
that accidentally keeps the true signal (the permutation equal to the identity must be detected by
`shuffle_is_permutation`); a `distilled` row that lists only the two controls of a plain learning gate. Determinism: evaluations under class D2 on one backend; control weights D0.

## Considerations

- Cost: the zero-weight and random controls cost one evaluation each; the shuffled-label control costs one
  extra distillation run per `distilled` gate at the gate's budget; runs above the default machine envelope
  name their budget and wait for approval, and may run at a reduced, printed budget.
- An honest zero policy can legitimately pass a badly chosen threshold; that is a finding about the threshold,
  and the gate fails until the threshold is corrected by a dated amendment made before a new measurement.
- A policy that wins by exploiting a contact-model artifact is the business of the exploitation detectors; the
  shuffled-label control is not a substitute for them.
