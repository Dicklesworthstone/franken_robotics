## Background

The verification subsystem of FrankenRobotics (the Gauntlet, crate `frob-conformance`) judges every other
subsystem's gates, and it has gates of its own. Its decision ADR-GAUNT-03 says every gate is registered with its
failing control before its passing test is written: the control (a planted defect the gate's test must catch)
is the specification of what the gate detects. This bead applies that rule to the Gauntlet itself, for the
Gauntlet's eleven gates, the adopted known-answer gate that its chapter defines for another family
(GATE-FID-09) and its kill gate. Their records in `registries/gates.toml` are created by the register beads
that own them (Scope names each), with claim kind, brief and milestone parts; this bead registers the negative
controls of the twelve gates and enters them in those records, so that each has a non-empty list of registered
controls before any of them is measured, and it holds the full threshold of each.

All thresholds are TARGETs until a gate records a measured value. All eleven Gauntlet gates and GATE-FID-09 are
of claim kind *known answer*: they show the harness behaves on planted inputs.

## Scope

This bead is the Gauntlet chapter's side of the gate register. It creates no gate record and no kill record:
each is written once, by the register bead that owns it.

In:

- For the twelve gates: the full threshold of each (the table below, which a recording session reads as the
  defining statement); the controls that must be registered first, and their identifiers in the `controls`
  field, with `tier`, of the records that N40-02 and N40-10 created; the chapter's owner declaration of each
  gate, with the binding to the beads that measure it.
- For the kill gate: its decision bead, and the check that it has its ledger record and one owner declaration.
- The meta-test and the end-to-end script below.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Creating the record of GATE-GAUNT-01 in `registries/gates.toml`, with identity, claim, section, the recurring
  mark and the brief: N40-02. The same, with the milestone parts, for GATE-GAUNT-02 to -11 and GATE-FID-09:
  N40-10.
- The kill record of KILL-DEVORACLE, with trigger, fallback, decision point (M1), mode and what the trigger
  reads: the fallback ledger, N40-29.
- The measurements; the record schema (N38b-18, extended by N40-01); the kill decision (N38b-24).

## Technical approach

**What this bead writes, and what it does not (DESIGN division, the same in every chapter's register bead;
reason: two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart).** `registries/gates.toml` holds
exactly one record per gate, in the format `frob.gates/1` (base record N38b-18, extended by N40-01). The record
of GATE-GAUNT-01 is created by N40-02 and those of GATE-GAUNT-02 to -11 and GATE-FID-09 by N40-10, with the
fields the register governs: `id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]`
list with its scopes, `recurring` and the brief in `threshold`. The kill record of KILL-DEVORACLE is created by
the fallback ledger (N40-29) with `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads`. This bead creates no
record and writes none of those fields. It writes three things:

1. The control records of every planted failure in the fourth column, in `registry/controls.toml`, and their
   identifiers in the `controls` field of each gate's existing record, with the gate's tiers in `tier`.
2. The chapter's owner declaration of each gate, in the form and place the agreement lint N40-09 reads: a
   `[[gate]]` block with `role = "owner"`, `claim`, `milestone_parts` and `threshold_numbers`, in the
   `contract.toml` of `frob-conformance` (`registries/gate_declarations.toml` until that block is agreed with
   the owner of `contract.toml`). The declaration also carries the chapter-side data that the register record
   has no field for, as fields the lint does not compare: `criterion`, the full threshold of the table below, of
   which the record's `threshold` is a brief; and `measured_by`, the last column.
3. Nothing in the kill gate's record. The owner declaration of KILL-DEVORACLE (`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`,
   `mode`, `reads`) is written by its decision bead N38b-24, carries the ledger's text and is compared with the
   ledger by N40-30.

The Earliest column and the kill row below are the chapter's statement of fields the register governs. They
were compared with N40-02, N40-10 and N40-29 when the beads were reconciled, and they agree. If the two ever
differ, the register governs (rule 4 of N40-01) and the difference is a finding of N40-09 or N40-30; it is never
settled by a second record. Two rows need a reading, which is the same here and in N40-10:

- GATE-GAUNT-11 (DESIGN; its defining row says only "each row lands at the milestone of its owner section" and
  names no milestone). The nine adopted rows land by owner section at M0 (the Cayley map), M1 (unresolved
  outcomes), M2 (the single-precision moment merge; the writable view), M3 (one edge of the extrinsic graph),
  M4 (the single-precision residual bound), M6 (the film front) and M8 (the certified root; one-sided tube
  erosion); the row beads are N38b-39 to N38b-45 and the script is N38b-46. The register record has one part,
  due at M1, with scope "first rows": the M0 row and the M1 row. A row that lands later is not a further part
  of this record: it is a row of the known-answer suite with its own due milestone, and the recurring gate
  GATE-GAUNT-02 demands it at the exit where it lands. Reason: the part counts of every exit review rest on the
  register's index, and one part with the later rows held by GATE-GAUNT-02 keeps every row enforced at its own
  exit without a part at seven exits.
- GATE-FID-09 has three parts: rows 1, 3 and 5 at M1 (N38b-40); row 2 at M4 (N38b-43); row 4 at M8 (N38b-45),
  because row 4 runs with the continuation lane and that lane lands at M8.

**Procedure.**
1. Write the control records (NC identifiers, kinds, targets, defects, expectations) for every planted failure
   in the fourth column into `registry/controls.toml`, each naming its gate. The kind follows how the failure
   is planted, because the control runner (N38b-04) builds a feature for a `mutation` and reads a fixture for
   the other kinds, and a record of the wrong kind reads `stale`. The two controls of GATE-GAUNT-01 are of kind
   `planted-input`: they are the two plants of N38b-09's script (a scratch registry in which a passing gate's
   only control is detached; one in which a control's mutation is replaced by a no-op), not branches of product
   code.
2. Fill `controls` and `tier` in the twelve gate records that N40-02 and N40-10 created, every part still
   `not-run`, and write the twelve owner declarations. Create no record. The records already mark GATE-GAUNT-01
   recurring and give a gate that closes in parts one part per milestone listed; those fields are the
   register's, and step 4 checks them.
3. Run the registry check; none of the twelve convergence rows may be `not-eligible`.
4. A meta-test compares this table with the file (identifier, claim kind, milestone list, control count) and
   asserts that each of the thirteen identifiers is the `id` of exactly one record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Thirteen records exist, one per identifier (created by N40-02, N40-10 and N40-29; none by this bead);
      twelve have non-empty control lists whose records exist and name the gate, and an owner declaration with
      the full threshold above; the kill gate's ledger record has trigger, fallback and decision milestone.
- [ ] In the commit history each gate's first control is no later than its first passing receipt.
- [ ] The meta-test fails when a threshold, a milestone or a control is removed from the file.
- [ ] Every row's claim kind is `known-answer` (the kill gate has none).
- [ ] Each gate is cited by the bead that measures it, and each measuring bead cites the gate.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/own_gates.rs`: `thirteen_rows_present`; `claims_are_known_answer`;
`milestones_match_table` (for example GATE-GAUNT-04 has parts M1 and M3; GATE-FID-09 has M1, M4 and M8;
GATE-GAUNT-11 has one part, at M1, with scope "first rows"); `one_record_per_identifier` (a planted second
record for GATE-GAUNT-10 fails the load with `duplicate-id`);
`controls_registered_first` (commit order on a scratch history); `gauntlet_01_is_recurring`;
`kill_gate_has_trigger_and_fallback`; `no_row_not_eligible`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/own_gates.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-23 milestone=M0 labels=gauntlet,registry gates=GATE-GAUNT-01
plants=drop-own-control`). It runs the registry check and the meta-test, then removes the controls of
GATE-GAUNT-10 in a scratch copy and expects `not-eligible`. Log at `target/e2e-logs/own_gates/<utc>.ndjson`:
commands with exit codes; per gate the number of controls (threshold at least 1) as `metric` lines; the plant's
assertion; the `summary` naming the thirteen identifiers.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the table itself. Negative control: the dropped control list; a gate registered after its first
receipt (history fixture). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- Where a subsystem's text and the register differ on a gate's identity, claim, milestone or number, the
  register governs; the record's `threshold` is a brief, and the full criterion is the row above together with
  the fixtures and tolerances stated in the measuring bead.
- GATE-FID-09 carries a fidelity-family name and a known-answer claim; it is entered once (by N40-10), under the Gauntlet
  chapter as owner.
- The kill gate's trigger, threshold or fallback changes only by a dated amendment made before the measurement
  it governs.
