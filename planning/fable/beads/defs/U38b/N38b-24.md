## Background

Every Frontier or Moonshot mechanism of FrankenRobotics needs a small pilot with a falsifier before its work
package is funded. Some pilots need rigid-body dynamics before the program's own trusted CPU tier (the
*referee*) exists. For those, the verification subsystem (the Gauntlet) admits a *development oracle*: one
incumbent simulator at a pinned release, run out of process and outside the product, with every result
labelled `development-oracle` and re-run on the referee when it exists. The rule that governs such results: a
result obtained on the development oracle funds or kills a work package and supports no gate; when the same
pilot is re-run on the referee and the verdict differs, the referee's verdict stands and the difference is
recorded against this kill gate.

KILL-DEVORACLE is the gate that asks whether that arrangement was sound. A kill gate names its trigger and its
fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped; firing is not a failure of the program, the fallback is the
plan.

- **Trigger (TARGET).** Of the first five pilots re-run on the CPU referee, two or more change verdict relative
  to their development-oracle run.
- **Fallback.** Development-oracle results lose standing as funding evidence; pilots for work packages not yet
  funded wait for the CPU referee; packages funded on a reversed pilot are re-decided.
- **Decided at.** The M1 exit, recorded as an architecture decision record whichever way it goes.

Rejected alternative: deciding on the fraction of all pilots at a later milestone (funding decisions made in
between would rest on unexamined evidence).

## Scope

In: the definition of "the first five", of "verdict" and of "change"; the measurement; the decision record; the
consequences downstream for each outcome. Out: running the pilots (their owners); the lane itself (N38a-06);
which incumbent is the development oracle (N38b-35).

## Technical approach

**Definitions (fixed before any re-run; a change needs a dated amendment made before the measurement).**
1. *The first five*: the first five pilot records in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`, ordered by
   the date of their development-oracle result, whose protocol the referee can execute at M1. A pilot the
   referee cannot yet run (it needs batched tiers or devices) is skipped and named.
2. *Verdict*: the pilot's pre-registered outcome class, one of `falsifier-holds` (the mechanism is not funded;
   its fallback ships), `falsifier-fails` (fundable) or `inconclusive` (as its frozen protocol defines).
3. *Change*: the two runs fall in different classes. A numeric difference inside one class is recorded and is
   not a change. `inconclusive` against a decisive class is a change.
4. The re-run uses the frozen protocol, the same seeds and world addresses, and differs only in the engine.

**Measurement.**
1. For each of the five, run the protocol on the referee; write the result into the pilot record beside the
   development-oracle result, with both pins.
2. Compute `changed_of_first_five` (an integer from 0 to 5) and list each pair of verdicts.
3. If fewer than five pilots are available at the M1 exit, the decision entry is `deferred` with `deferred_to`
   the next exit and the `reason` "too few verdicts: <k> of 5", and it is taken again at the first exit at which
   five exist; until then the fail-closed posture below holds for any new funding decision. If no pilot ever
   used the development oracle (none chosen, N38b-35), the outcome is `not-reached`.

**Decision (the one form of a kill-gate decision, N40-03).** The kill row of KILL-DEVORACLE in
`registries/gates.toml` carries no status and no part (a kill row measures nothing); the decision bead writes
its entry `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`, with `outcome` one of the four registered
outcomes and `receipts` the hashes of the receipts the count rests on: the `receipt` of each of the ten
`[[pilot.result]]` entries read (the five development-oracle results and their five referee re-runs), each
committed with its result at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09 rule 2a), which N40-03 resolves there. The
counting test is a plain Cargo test, not a tier run, and writes no receipt of its own; it recomputes
`changed_of_first_five` from those results.
- `changed_of_first_five ≥ 2`: outcome `fired`; the fallback is in force. Mark every pilot record whose only
  result is from the development oracle as `standing: none`; list the work packages funded on a reversed pilot
  and open a re-decision for each; unfunded packages with a development-oracle pilot wait for a referee run.
- `changed_of_first_five ≤ 1`: outcome `not-fired`. Development-oracle results keep their standing as funding
  evidence (and still support no gate); a single reversal is recorded with an explanation in a derivation file,
  and that package is re-decided.

**Fail-closed posture until decided.** No development oracle is assumed: if the incumbent release has not been
chosen, the pilots that need dynamics wait for the referee.

**Record.** The decision record `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DEVORACLE-M1.md` (or the exit of a deferred entry) with the
fields N40-03 requires: the kill gate; the milestone; the trigger as registered and any amendments; each measured
value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (the five pilots, both verdicts and both pins for each,
`changed_of_first_five` against 2); the outcome; the fallback now in force; the beads re-read (each one "stands
on the fallback", "superseded by this ADR" or "withdrawn before this decision"); what changes downstream (the
packages listed for re-decision, the records losing standing); the session that recorded it, which ran none of
the pilots.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The definitions above are committed before the first referee re-run.
- [ ] Five pilot records show two results each with pins, or the decision entry is `deferred` with the count in
      its `reason`.
- [ ] The count `changed_of_first_five` is computed by a test from the pilot register, not typed by hand.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DEVORACLE-M1.md` exists at the M1 exit for every outcome, with N40-03's required
      fields, and the kill row's `decision` entry names it with outcome `fired`, `not-fired`, `not-reached` or
      `deferred` and the receipts of the pilot results the count reads (resolving under `receipts/pilots/`);
      the kill row carries no status, part or measured field.
- [ ] If it fired: no pilot record with only a development-oracle result is accepted by the funding rule, and
      each package funded on a reversed pilot has an open re-decision.
- [ ] A development-oracle result is never cited by a gate in either outcome.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_devoracle.rs` (fixture registers labelled `synthetic`):
- `first_five_by_date_and_runnability`: seven records, two not runnable on the referee → the five chosen are
  the earliest runnable ones.
- `change_is_class_change`: (falsifier-holds, falsifier-holds, different numbers) → no change;
  (falsifier-fails, inconclusive) → change.
- `count_two_fires`, `count_one_does_not_fire`, `count_zero_does_not_fire`.
- `fewer_than_five_is_deferred`: four runnable records → a `deferred` entry with `deferred_to` the next exit
  and the reason naming 4 of 5; `decision_entry_form`: the written entry loads under N40-03's rules (outcome
  closed set, ADR path `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-DEVORACLE-M1.md`, at least one receipt for `not-fired`, each receipt
  a pilot result's hash present at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json`; a hash with no committed file fails the load).
- `fired_removes_standing`: the funding rule rejects a record with only a development-oracle result.
- `reversed_package_listed_for_redecision`.
- `edited_devoracle_verdict_detected` (the negative control below): on a scratch history in which a reversed
  pilot's development-oracle verdict is rewritten after its referee result was committed, the counting test
  reports the edit with the record and the commit, and refuses to compute `changed_of_first_five` from the
  edited register.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_devoracle.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-24 milestone=M1 labels=gauntlet,kill-gate
gates=KILL-DEVORACLE plants=two-reversals`). It reads the pilot register, runs the counting test (`cargo test
-p frob-conformance --test kill_devoracle`), prints the
five verdict pairs, and on a planted register with two reversals checks that the funding rule then refuses a
development-oracle-only record. Log at `target/e2e-logs/kill_devoracle/<utc>.ndjson`: commands with exit codes;
one `metric` line per pilot (both verdicts, both pins); `changed_of_first_five` against the trigger threshold
2; the plant's assertion; the evidence block (the development-oracle results labelled as such); the `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the fixture registers give counts 0, 1 and 2 as constructed. Negative control: a register in
which a reversed pilot's development-oracle verdict was edited after the fact to match (the record's hash chain
or its commit history must expose the edit). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- What changes downstream if it fires: funding decisions of every Frontier and Moonshot package whose pilot
  needs dynamics slip until the referee has run their pilots; the M1 exit review lists them.
- A reversal is also evidence about the incumbent or about the referee; two oracles that disagree are a filed
  finding with both pins, and neither is tuned toward the other until a derivation explains the difference.
- The comparison is between verdict classes because pilots are small; numeric agreement is recorded for the
  parity ledger, where it is Parity and never Reality.
