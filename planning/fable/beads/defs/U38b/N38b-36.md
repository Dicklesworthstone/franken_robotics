## Background

The verification subsystem of FrankenRobotics (the Gauntlet, crate `frob-conformance`) has an open question
with the performance program as owner, OPEN-GAUNT-5: **the Gauntlet's runtime budget per change, per night and
per release on each machine.** It blocks the schedule of tiers G1 to G5 (known answers, parity, metamorphic
relations, chaos, determinism). Its fail-closed default is in force until decided: the `smoke` and `fixtures`
batteries per change; everything else nightly.

The rule for open questions says evidence decides where evidence exists: a question whose answer is a
measurement is decided by that measurement and not by preference. This bead is the measurement and the
resulting decision record. The two machines are the Apple-silicon workstation (CPU tiers and the Metal device)
and the Linux host with two RTX 4090 cards (CPU tiers and CUDA), which is also heavily loaded, so timings are
noisy and must be taken interleaved and reported with their spread.

Rejected alternative: fixing budgets by guess at planning time (the first real suite would either overflow them
silently or be cut to fit without anyone recording what was cut).

## Scope

In: the timing protocol; the table of measured costs per selection and machine on every execution site
available at the M1 exit; the proposed budgets; the decision record; the resulting edit of the schedule file.
Out: making the tiers faster; the count question for referee relations (N38b-38); the timing of device sites
not yet available at M1, which N38b-38 takes at M2 with this bead's script and enters in this record as a dated
amendment.

## Technical approach

**Protocol.**
1. For each selection of the schedule file (trigger × tier target × filter × count) and each machine, run it
   three times interleaved with the other selections, cold cache once and warm twice, under a timeout of twice
   the default hour.
2. Record per run: wall time, CPU core-seconds, peak resident memory, device seconds and peak device memory,
   the host load average at start, the commit, toolchain and hardware fingerprint (a disclosure block).
3. Compute per selection the median and the range; compute the per-entry cost distribution so that the
   slowest ten entries of each tier are named.
4. Compute the largest count that fits each candidate budget, for the count-scaled selections (relations at
   32, 10³ and 10⁴ scenarios; chaos at 10³ and 10⁵ schedules; fuzzing at 10⁵ and 10⁷ executions).
5. What is timed is the schedule of N38b-25 with the `smoke` and `fixtures` batteries of N24b-01, on the tree
   as it stands when the bead runs (the commit is in each disclosure block). The M1 capstone BD-146, which adds
   the remaining rows and controls due at M1, is not a dependency, because no bead waits for it; so the
   selections are timed again on the M1 exit candidate, the decision cites that table, and a selection whose
   median grew beyond its first range between the two runs is named with the entries that grew.

**Proposed budgets to confirm or amend (DESIGN starting points, not measurements).** Per change: at most 10
minutes of wall time inside the default envelope. Per night: each selection inside one default-envelope hour,
the whole night inside 8 hours per machine. Per release: selections may use a named larger class with the
owner's approval recorded before they start. Per exit: as release, for the gates due.

**Decision.** A record `docs/decisions/OPEN-GAUNT-5.md` with the measured table and, for each trigger and
machine, the adopted budget and the selections that moved (from `nightly` to `release`, or to a reduced count).
The schedule file is edited in the same commit. Anything cut is listed with what it would have cost. The
decision itself is the record `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-GAUNT-5.md` (the one directory for decision records, N40-03),
which cites the measured table as its supporting file; the row OPEN-GAUNT-5 of `registries/open.toml` (decided by
measurement, N41-03 rule 4) moves to `decided` with `adr` naming it, or, under the default below, to `defaulted`
with the ADR written from the register's default template (N41-03).

**Default if not decided by the M1 exit.** The conservative schedule stays: `smoke` and `fixtures` per change,
everything else nightly, counts above the default envelope reduced and printed.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A table exists with median and range of wall time, core-seconds and peak memory for every selection on
      both machines, each row with its disclosure block.
- [ ] The ten slowest entries per tier are named.
- [ ] The decision record states the adopted budget per trigger and machine and lists every moved or reduced
      selection; the schedule file matches it.
- [ ] No selection in `change` exceeds the adopted per-change budget on the reference tree.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-GAUNT-5.md` exists at the M1 exit and the register row OPEN-GAUNT-5 names it, with
      status `decided`, or `defaulted` if undecided (the default recorded as the decision).
- [ ] Every site of the schedule that is unavailable at M1 (for example a device backend not yet built) appears
      in the table as `not measured` with its reason and a dated line `re-measure at M2 (N38b-38)`; no budget is
      adopted for it until it is measured (the default schedule holds for its selections).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/schedule.rs`:
- `budget_table_covers_schedule`: a schedule of three selections on two machines and a table missing the row of
  `nightly × G3 × referee` on the Linux host is rejected naming that row; a `not measured` row with a reason is
  accepted.
- `change_fits_budget`: medians 120 s, 240 s and 200 s for the `change` selections sum to 560 s and fit a
  600 s budget; adding a 60 s selection (620 s) fails, naming it.
- `median_and_range`: runs of 10.0, 12.0 and 11.0 s give median 11.0 s and range [10.0, 12.0] s; a row with a
  median and no range is rejected.
- `slowest_ten_named`: twelve entries with costs 1 to 12 s give the ten from 3 to 12 s, in descending order.
- `largest_count_in_budget`: at 0.5 s per scenario and a 600 s budget the largest of {32, 10³, 10⁴} that fits is
  10³ (500 s); at 0.01 s it is 10⁴ (100 s).
- `moved_selections_recorded`: a schedule-file change of a selection from `nightly` to `release` with no line in
  the decision record is rejected; with the line and its cost it is accepted.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gauntlet_budget.sh` (header: `bead=N38b-36 milestone=M1 labels=gauntlet,performance
plants=slow-entry,rangeless-row`; sources the STD-E2E helper). Steps: (1) `sleep-check`: the fixed 2-second
entry, timed three times, must measure within 10% of 2 s (`e2e_assert_close`); (2) `run`: each selection of the
schedule file, on each site available on this machine, under the helper's accounting (`e2e_cmd` wraps the
command with wall time, CPU core-seconds, peak resident memory, and device seconds and peak device memory where
a device runs), three times interleaved with the other selections, cold once and warm twice, under a timeout of
twice the default hour; an unavailable site logs `skip` with its reason and is entered as `not measured`; (3)
`table`: the medians, ranges, slowest ten per tier and largest count in each candidate budget written to
`target/gauntlet/budget_<machine>.json` and checked by `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test schedule`; (4)
`aa`: one selection timed in two interleaved series, the difference asserted inside its own range; (5) plants:
`slow-entry` (an entry with an injected 30-second sleep must appear in the slowest-ten list) and `rangeless-row`
(a table row with its range removed must be rejected). Log at `target/e2e-logs/gauntlet_budget/<utc>.ndjson`
with the transcript; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`; `cmd` lines the exact command, exit
code and wall time; one `metric` per run (wall seconds, core-seconds, peak GiB, device seconds; threshold = the
candidate budget of its trigger), the host load average at start, the disclosure block (commit, toolchain,
hardware fingerprint); failed assertions the selection, observed and expected values and the paths of the run's
artifacts; the `summary` with counts of selections measured, skipped and over budget per machine.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: an entry that sleeps for a fixed 2 seconds is measured within 10% of 2 seconds (the timing
instrument's own check). Negative controls: the slow planted entry; a selection reported without its range.
A/A check: the same selection timed in two interleaved series must not differ by more than its own range.
Determinism: timings are not deterministic; they are reported as distributions and never compared as bits.

## Considerations

- This is an experiment about cost, not a performance claim about the product; its figures still carry
  disclosure blocks.
- A budget is a cap that triggers a conversation, not a schedule; a tier that outgrows its budget is reported
  with the entries that grew.
- Mutant builds for negative controls dominate cost when many crates are touched; grouping controls by crate
  is the main lever and is measured separately.
