## Background

Three of the fourteen known answers adopted by FrankenRobotics' verification subsystem (the Gauntlet) belong
to Moonshot mechanisms that are delivered last, if at all. They are written now so that the mechanisms cannot
land without them.

*Reality of roots.* Mechanism synthesis by numerical continuation finds complex solutions of polynomial
systems and must say which are real, since only real solutions are mechanisms. A root is certified by Smale's
α-test in exact rational arithmetic: with β = ‖f′(z)⁻¹·f(z)‖, γ = sup over k ≥ 2 of
‖f′(z)⁻¹·f⁽ᵏ⁾(z)/k!‖^(1/(k−1)) and α = β·γ, the condition α(f, z) < (13 − 3√17)/4 ≈ 0.157671 proves that
Newton's iteration from z converges quadratically to a root within twice the Newton step. Distinctness
follows from disjoint balls; non-reality from disjointness with the conjugate ball; reality from the robust
test α(f, z) < 0.03 together with ‖z − Re z‖ < 1/(20·γ(f, z)), which shows that z and its conjugate converge to
one root. Two shortcuts are tempting: calling a root real when its imaginary part is small, and calling it
real because the α-test also passes at its real part.

*Tube erosion.* A shield for a small subsystem keeps a table of safe cells; a cell stays safe only if every
successor lies in a safe cell after allowing for sensing error. When cells hold *measured* states and the true
state is the measured state plus an error in a set E, the successor set must be eroded by −E, not by E; the two
agree only when E is centrally symmetric.

Gates: GATE-FID-09 for the first root row (counts exact), GATE-GAUNT-11 for the second root row (four
significant figures in exact rational evaluation of α and γ) and for the erosion row (rational rows exact).

## Technical approach

**Row 4 of the corrected table, reality of roots at saturation (KA-839; verb `mission.synthesize` with the
continuation strategy; checker `frob-verify`).** Two instances from the literature (FACT, to be re-read from
the primary sources before the gate cites them): a Stewart–Gough platform instance with 40 real assembly modes
(Dietmaier 1998) and a serial six-revolute instance with 16 real inverse-kinematics solutions (Manseur and
Doty 1989). Expected: the lane certifies 40 of 40 and 16 of 16 roots as real by the conjugate-ball test, and
the result is labelled complete only when the certified count equals an independent generic count with no
lost path. A generic instance of each family has the same complex count, and its real count is whatever the
test certifies. Control (`mutation` of the reality test): "real if |Im z| < tolerance". Fixture for the
control: a planted near-real conjugate pair (two distinct complex roots with imaginary parts ±10⁻⁹); the
tolerance test calls them one real root, the ball test finds two disjoint conjugate balls and calls neither
real.

**Row 4 of the late table, reality of a certified root (KA-844; host and `auditor`).** f(z) = z³ + z, whose
roots are 0, +i and −i; z = 0.001 + 1.001·i.
1. f′ = 3z² + 1, f″ = 6z, f‴ = 6. At z: β = 0.001412, γ = max(|6z/(2f′)|, |1/f′|^(1/2)) = 1.497,
   α = β·γ = 0.0021 < 0.157671: a root near z is certified, and it is +i.
2. At Re z = 0.001: α = 0.0010, certifying a root near 0.001, which is 0: a different root.
3. ‖z − Re z‖ = 1.001 exceeds 1/(20·γ(f, z)) = 0.033, so the robust test does not call the root real. Correct.
All quantities are evaluated in exact rational arithmetic on the real and imaginary parts (squared moduli
compared, square roots enclosed), and reported to four significant figures: 0.002114, 1.497, 0.03340,
0.0010000. Control (`mutation`): a test that infers reality from α(f, Re z) alone calls +i real.

**Row 6 of the late table, tube erosion with a one-sided error (KA-846; verb `shield.fit` on a one-dimensional
table).** A cell [0, 1] of measured states; true state = measured + e with e in E = [0, 0.1]; the erosion
A ⊖ B = {x : x + B ⊆ A}. A true successor x is admissible only if every measurement of it, x − e, lies in the
cell: x − 0.1 ≥ 0 and x ≤ 1, so the admissible set is [0.1, 1.0] = [0, 1] ⊖ (−E). Exact in rationals. Control
(`mutation` of the pruning condition): erosion by E gives [0, 0.9], which admits 0.05 (whose measurement can be
−0.05, outside the cell) and rejects 0.95 (whose measurements lie in [0.85, 0.95], inside it). The test
checks both witness points.

**Evidence.** Known answers, class `Tested`; the checker's verdict on an `alpha-root` certificate is
`Certified` for the root's existence only, with Smale's theorem as a named assumption; determinism D0.

Known answers: 40 and 16; α = 0.0021, γ = 1.497, 1/(20γ) = 0.033; [0.1, 1.0]. Negative controls: reality by

(exact arithmetic).

## Considerations

- The host rows KA-844 and KA-846 are pure arithmetic and can be written at any time; they are bound to the
  product only when the lanes exist, and the gates' parts are recorded then.
- A certified root proves a nearby nonsingular root and nothing about roots that were not found; a partial
  result can support inclusion of the designs it found and never an exclusion.
- Where cells hold true states and the whole belief set must stay in one cell, the condition uses erosion by
  E ⊕ (−E); the row covers the measured-state form only.
