## Background

FrankenRobotics keeps many scenes resident on a device at once (the world bank) and admits them under a memory
budget. On the Apple-silicon workstation the budget was first printed as "about 190 GB … a little under 85%"
of about 222 GiB. Recomputed: 85% of 222 GiB is 188.7 GiB, which is 202.6 GB; and 190 GB is 177.0 GiB, which
is 79.7% of 222 GiB. Gigabytes (10⁹ bytes) and gibibytes (2³⁰ bytes) had been mixed. The text was corrected:
the device budget is 85% of the measured working-set limit, in the scene chapter and in the performance
chapter alike.

This is one of eleven values that did not reconcile with their own formulas when the known-answer suite was
compiled (register record REC-05; status corrected). It matters because the number of resident scenes, and so
the scale of scene-diverse training, is read off this budget. The correction has not yet reached the dependent
row: KA-460's workstation column (about 2,500, 320, 320, 40, 4, 13 and 1) divides the number 189 (a budget in
GiB) by scene sizes in decimal GB, the same mixing again; the scene chapter's own table and its owners (N19-KA,
BD-236) give the consistent counts 2,700, 340, 340, 42, 5, 14 and 1.

## Scope

In: the record; one unit convention for memory budgets; the conformance crate's byte-figure parser; the
inputs and counts the dependent row takes from the record; a test. Out: the register (N38b-50); the row itself,
which N19-KA enters at M2; the scene byte model and its capacity table (N19c-16, N36-16), whose pinned figures
the counts below are; measuring the limit; the world bank.

## Technical approach

**Formula.** budget = 0.85 × working_set_limit, in bytes; 1 GiB = 2³⁰ bytes = 1.073741824 GB.

**Both values.** Printed: "about 190 GB, a little under 85% of about 222 GiB". Recomputed: 0.85 × 222 GiB =
188.7 GiB = 202.6 GB; 190 GB = 176.95 GiB = 79.7% of 222 GiB.

**Settlement.**
1. One convention: memory budgets, working-set limits and device memory are stated in binary units (GiB, MiB)
   with the byte count as the authority; card capacities marketed in decimal units ("a 24 GB card") are
   converted once, at the fingerprint, and printed with both.
2. The budget is a function of a measured input: the working-set limit comes from the hardware fingerprint
   (FACT to measure on the machine; the planning figure is KA-546's starting value, about 222.7 GiB, which
   Appendix M.38 rounds to 222, and is labelled as such). The budget is generated as 0.85 × limit and never
   typed.
3. The known-answer row on resident scene counts (KA-460) states its inputs as "12 to 16 GB of a 24 GB card"
   and "85% of the measured working-set limit, about 189 GiB (203 GB)" and its counts follow from them by
   whole scenes, ⌊budget/size⌋ with both in bytes; its derivation file shows the division for each scene size.
   The workstation budget of the table is the fixture the scene chapter pins, exactly 203 × 10⁹ bytes (85% of
   222.7 GiB is 189.295 GiB = 203.25 GB, printed 203 GB; N19c-16 and N36-16 pin it so that the beads printing
   the table cannot differ, and a run uses the measured budget). On the workstation, with the scene sizes of
   KA-459 in decimal units: home at 2 cm (75 MB) 2,706, printed "about 2,700"; home at 1 cm and warehouse at
   4 cm (0.6 GB) 338, "about 340"; warehouse at 2 cm (4.8 GB) 42; warehouse at 1 cm (38.4 GB) 5; building at
   2 cm (14.4 GB) 14; building at 1 cm (115.2 GB) 1. Appendix M.38's 188.7 GiB (202.6 GB, from the rounded
   222 GiB) would give 2,701 and 337: it is the record's `recomputed`, not the row's truth. The row's printed
   column (2,500, 320, 320, 40, 4, 13, 1: the number 189 divided by sizes in GB) is kept in its `printed` field
   and retired. The card column (160–210, 20–27, 20–27, 2–3, none, at most 1, none) is consistent in units; its
   upper ends are printed rounded (213.3 as 210, 26.7 as 27), and as whole scenes they are 213 and 26, which
   the derivation file prints beside the printed forms.
   The retired entries are `{ pattern = "190 ?GB", scope = "budget|working.set|85 ?%" }` and
   `{ pattern = "about 2,500", all_of = ["2 cm"], scope = "resident|home" }`.
4. The byte-figure parser of the conformance crate (`frob_conformance::bytes::parse`, written here; it reads
   the budgets, limits and scene sizes of registry records and derivation files, and no product crate has a
   byte quantity to reuse: the scene byte model takes and returns plain bytes, N19c-16) distinguishes decimal
   and binary prefixes, so that "190 GB" offered where GiB is declared is a unit error and not a silent 7%
   discrepancy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] REC-05 is settled in the register with the printed and recomputed values above.
- [ ] Both chapters' generated text state the budget as 85% of the measured working-set limit, with the limit's
      source; "190 GB" does not appear as the budget.
- [ ] REC-05's `settled` and the derivation file give the resident-scene-count row (KA-460) inputs in one unit
      system and a workstation column of 2,706, 338, 338, 42, 5, 14 and 1 (printed about 2,700, 340, 340, 42, 5,
      14, 1), the counts N19c-16's `fit_table` and N36-16 assert for 203 × 10⁹ bytes; the printed 2,500-column is
      retired. N19-KA enters the row from them at M2; nothing here waits on it.
- [ ] A budget typed in GB where GiB is declared is rejected by `bytes::parse`.
- [ ] When the fingerprint measures the limit, a run's budget and counts are generated from it; the table's
      fixture budget stays 203 × 10⁹ bytes.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/reconciliations.rs`:
- `rec_05_budget`: 0.85 × 222 = 188.7 GiB; × 1.073741824 = 202.6 GB.
- `rec_05_printed`: 190 GB / 1.073741824 = 176.95 GiB; / 222 = 0.797.
- `rec_05_counts_follow`: ⌊203 × 10⁹ / (75 × 10⁶)⌋ = 2,706; 338, 338, 42, 5, 14 and 1 for 0.6, 0.6, 4.8, 38.4,
  14.4 and 115.2 GB; 0.85 × 222.7 × 2³⁰ = 203.25 × 10⁹ bytes, which the fixture's 203 GB prints; the M.38
  reading ⌊0.85 × 222 × 2³⁰ / (75 × 10⁶)⌋ = 2,701 differs and is not the truth; the card column in whole
  scenes 160–213, 20–26, 20–26, 2–3, 0, 0–1 and 0 from 12–16 GB.
- `rec_05_unit_mixed_column_detected`: 189 / 0.075 = 2,520 and 189 / 38 = 4.97 (floor 4) reproduce the printed
  column and differ from the consistent counts, so the old column is the GiB-number-over-GB defect.
- `byte_prefix_mismatch_rejected`: "190 GB" offered where GiB is declared is a unit error.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/reconciliations.sh` (bead N38b-50, on which this bead depends) carries REC-05: the `register`
step logs its `metric` line (printed "about 190 GB", recomputed 188.7 GiB = 202.6 GB, settled 0.85 × the
measured limit, status corrected); the `rows` step runs KA-460 and logs, per scene size, the count on the card
and on the workstation against the settled values (`assert` lines with observed and expected counts and the
budget in bytes; until N19-KA has entered the row at M2, an `e2e_skip` naming N19-KA, never a pass); the `scan` step reports zero hits for REC-05's entries, and the `retired-string` plant is run
for it ("about 2,500 homes at 2 cm" resident on the workstation gives one hit naming REC-05). No script of its
own.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 188.7 GiB and 202.6 GB. Negative controls: the budget taken as 100% of memory (counts 18% too
high); decimal and binary prefixes mixed. Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- The planning limit of about 222.7 GiB on a 256 GB machine is itself to be measured; the settlement makes the budget
  follow the measurement.
- Scene sizes in the same table are printed in decimal megabytes; the derivation file must say so, or convert.
