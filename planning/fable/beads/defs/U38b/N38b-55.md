## Background

FrankenRobotics schedules sensors and controllers by a *clock contract*: every periodic process has a rational
period on a common lattice, so that a 30 Hz policy on a 400 Hz physics step fires at exact instants instead
of "every N steps". The tempting shortcut, running the policy every thirteenth physics step, is the registered
negative control of the contract: 400/13 is 30.77 Hz, not 30 Hz. The design first described that shortcut as
"2.6%" fast while also implying 615 decisions against 600 in 20 s, which is 2.5%.

Recomputed and corrected: the *rate* error is 30.77/30 − 1 = 2.6%; the *count* of decision instants in a
half-open 20 s window is 616 against 600 (decisions at steps 0, 13, 26, … below 8,000: ⌈8,000/13⌉ = 616), an
overpayment of 2.7%; 615 was a miscount. This is one of eleven values that did not reconcile with their own
formulas when the known-answer suite was compiled (register record REC-06; status corrected).

## Scope

In: the reconciliation record REC-06 of the known-answer suite; the exact figures and where each applies; the
control text of the two known-answer rows and of the metamorphic relation; the derivation file; a test whose
expected values are the hand formulas below. Out: the register (N38b-50); the clock contract itself (BD-078);
the product's one counting function `count_firings` in `frob-sense` with its window convention, the report lint
that forbids a rate times a duration printed as a count, and the settlement of item E7-6 of the worked-card
register (the register is NEG-03's; NEG-09 settles the same slip where the sensing chapter and the card GB-23
printed it, and E7-6 names REC-06 in its `same_as`). The two
beads carry the same figures (600 and 616 on [0, 20 s); 601 and 616 on [0, 20 s]; 2.564% in rate; 2.667% in
count); this one corrects the known-answer registry, that one the product code and the card.

## Technical approach

**Formulas.** Physics rate 400 Hz, step h = 1/400 s; nominal policy rate 30 Hz, period 1/30 s. Common lattice:
1,200 quanta per second; a physics step is 3 quanta and a policy period 40 quanta; the pattern repeats every
120 quanta (0.1 s) with decision intervals of 14, 13 and 13 steps, giving exactly 600 decisions in 20 s.
Shortcut: a decision every 13 steps.

**Values.**
- Rate of the shortcut: 400/13 = 30.769 Hz; relative error 30.769/30 − 1 = 2.564%, printed 2.6%.
- Decisions in the half-open window [0, 20 s): the contract gives 600; the shortcut gives ⌈8,000/13⌉ = 616.
- Relative overpayment on counts: 616/600 − 1 = 2.667%, printed 2.7%.
- Retired: 615 decisions and "2.5%" (615/600); 615.4 is 8,000/13 before the ceiling. Entries:
  `retired = [{ pattern = "615", scope = "decision|thirteenth|13 steps" }, { pattern = "2\\.5 ?%", all_of = ["thirteenth"] }]`.

**Settlement.**
1. The two rows that use the shortcut as their negative control state it as: "every-thirteenth-step
   scheduling gives 616 decisions on the same half-open window (400/13 = 30.77 Hz)"; the metamorphic relation
   on clock re-phasing registers the same mutation with the figure 30.77 Hz.
2. The derivation file `docs/derivations/clock-thirteenth-step.md` states the window convention (half-open,
   first decision at step 0), both percentages and what each measures.
3. The control's expected observation is the count 616 (an exact integer), not a percentage; the row's truth
   is 600 exactly.
4. Where a row's check prints a count of decisions from a run, the number comes from the product's
   `count_firings` (NEG-09); this bead adds no second counting rule, and its test compares that output with
   ⌈8,000/13⌉ computed in the test.

  in [0, 20 s); intervals 14, 13, 13 repeating; lateness 0, 2, 1 quanta.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 600 and 616; 30.77 Hz. Negative control: the every-thirteenth-step schedule itself (it is the
subject of the record). Determinism: D0 (integer schedules, identical on every tier).

## Considerations

- A 2.6% rate error looks small and accumulates: sixteen extra decisions in twenty seconds change every
  controller that integrates over decisions, and they make schedules differ between tiers that round
  differently. That is why the contract uses an integer lattice.
- The same arithmetic applies to any pair of rates whose ratio is not an integer; the derivation file gives
  the general count ⌈T·f_phys/k⌉ for a decision every k steps.
