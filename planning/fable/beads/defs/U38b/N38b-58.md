## Background

FrankenRobotics argues from a byte model that device memory does not limit the number of simulated worlds at
the planned batch sizes; it limits distinct scenes and camera frames. The opening survey of the design first
said that 8,192 quadruped worlds or 4,096 humanoid worlds use "about 1 to 1.5%" of a 24 GB card. Recomputed
from the per-world sizes it quotes (20 to 53 kB for a quadruped, 56 to 180 kB for a humanoid): 8,192 × 20–53 kB
is 0.7 to 1.8%, and 4,096 × 56–180 kB is 1.0 to 3.1%. The text was corrected to "about 1 to 3%", which the
per-world sizes of the performance chapter give.

This is one of eleven values that did not reconcile with their own formulas when the known-answer suite was
compiled (register record REC-09; status corrected). The conclusion (memory does not bind on world count) is
unchanged; the figure is quoted in three places and must be one figure.

## Scope

In: the record; the formula and its inputs; the rows that quote the share; a test. Out: the register
(N38b-50); the byte model's own row and its control; measured high-water marks.

## Technical approach

**Formula.** share = worlds × bytes_per_world / card_bytes, with the card at 24 GB (24 × 10⁹ bytes; the
marketed, decimal figure). Bytes per world in single precision with a dense Jacobian:
4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v), with n_b bodies, n_v degrees of freedom, C contact
capacity and R row capacity.

**Values.**
- From the quoted ranges: quadruped 8,192 × 20 kB = 0.164 GB (0.68%) to 8,192 × 53 kB = 0.434 GB (1.81%);
  humanoid 4,096 × 56 kB = 0.229 GB (0.96%) to 4,096 × 180 kB = 0.737 GB (3.07%).
- From the byte model's reference workloads: 8,192 quadruped worlds are 0.28 GB (1.2%) and 8,192 humanoid
  worlds 0.79 GB (3.3%); per world about 34 kB and 96 kB. (The dynamics chapter's own example with other
  capacities gives 27 kB and 72 kB.)
- Retired: "about 1 to 1.5%".

**Settlement.**
1. The share is generated from the byte model and the stated capacities; the survey text, the performance
   chapter and the memory counterexample all print "1 to 3% of a 24 GB card" for 8,192 worlds of the two
   reference workloads (0.28 GB and 0.79 GB).
2. The derivation file `docs/derivations/world-bytes-share.md` evaluates the formula for both workloads with
   their capacities, shows the ranges above, and states that the card figure is decimal.
3. The known-answer row on the consequences of the byte model (KA-816) carries 0.28 GB and 0.79 GB and cites
   this derivation file for them. Its tolerance is set by N38b-27's rule 2 for the whole row (half a unit of the
   last printed digit: 0.005 GB for these two values), not a second time here; the retired entry is
   `retired = [{ pattern = "1 to 1\\.5 ?%", scope = "24 ?GB|card|worlds" }]`.
4. When measured high-water marks exist, the estimator's own gate (memory within 5% of measurement) is the
   authority; the planning share is then regenerated from measurement.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/reconciliations.rs`:
- `rec_09_ranges`: 0.68%, 1.81%, 0.96%, 3.07%.
- `rec_09_reference_workloads`: 0.28/24 = 1.17%; 0.79/24 = 3.29%; bytes per world 34.2 kB and 96.4 kB.
- `byte_model_example`: n_v = 18, n_b = 13, C = 24, R = 128 → 26,668 bytes (27 kB); n_v = 35, n_b = 30, C = 40,
  R = 224 → 72 kB.
- `printed_range_covers`: [1.17%, 3.29%] rounds to "1 to 3%"; "1 to 1.5%" does not cover 3.29%.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/reconciliations.sh` (bead N38b-50, on which this bead depends) carries REC-09: the `register`
step logs its `metric` line (printed "about 1 to 1.5%", recomputed 0.7–1.8% and 1.0–3.1%, settled "1 to 3%"
from 0.28 GB and 0.79 GB, status corrected); the `rows` step runs KA-348 (26,668 bytes and 72 kB, exact) and
KA-816 (0.28 GB and 0.79 GB within 0.005 GB), with `assert` lines of observed and expected values; the `scan`
step reports zero hits for REC-09's entry, and the `retired-string` plant is run for it ("about 1 to 1.5% of a
24 GB card" in a generated page gives one hit naming REC-09). No script of its own.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 26,668 bytes for the quadruped example; 0.28 GB and 0.79 GB. Negative controls: the dense
Jacobian term omitted (17 kB); double-precision byte counts (twice the figures). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- Two cameras at 640 × 480 on 8,192 worlds are 37.5 GiB; memory does limit sensing and scenes, which is the
  point the corrected figure supports.
- The card figure is decimal and budgets elsewhere are binary; the derivation file says which is used where.
