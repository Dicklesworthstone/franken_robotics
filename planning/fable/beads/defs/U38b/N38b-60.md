## Background

FrankenRobotics certifies statements about families of designs on boxes in parameter space. One honest limit
is stated by the construction for region certificates: paving a whole design box isotropically is out of
reach, because 10⁴ boxes are only about 13 bisections, less than one cut per axis in 20 dimensions, "where a
uniform box keeps 0.64 of each side (0.41 in 10 dimensions)". The counterexample catalogue carries the same
sentence against the shortcut "a region certificate can be had by paving the whole design box".

Recomputed: 13 bisections (8,192 boxes) give 2^(−13/20) = 0.637 of each side; 10⁴ boxes exactly give
(10⁴)^(−1/20) = 0.631. The printed 0.64 is the rounded value for 13 bisections, quoted beside "10⁴ boxes". It
is one of eleven values recorded as not reconciling when the known-answer suite was compiled (register record
REC-11; status remark: rounding). Nothing depends on the second decimal; the settlement makes the sentence say
which count it computed.

## Scope

In: the record; the formula; the settled wording; a test. Out: the register (N38b-50); region certificates
themselves.

## Technical approach

**Formula.** N equal boxes that pave a d-dimensional box isotropically each keep a fraction N^(−1/d) of every
side. k bisections give N = 2ᵏ and a fraction 2^(−k/d).

**Values.**

| Boxes | d = 20 | d = 10 |
|---|---|---|
| 2¹³ = 8,192 (13 bisections) | 2^(−13/20) = 0.6373 | 2^(−13/10) = 0.4061 |
| 10⁴ exactly (13.29 bisections) | 10^(−1/5) = 0.6310 | 10^(−2/5) = 0.3981 |

So "0.64" and "0.41" belong to 13 bisections; for 10⁴ boxes the figures are 0.63 and 0.40.

**Settlement.**
1. The generated sentence reads: "13 bisections (8,192 boxes, of the order of 10⁴) are less than one cut per
   axis in 20 dimensions: a uniform box keeps 0.64 of each side (0.41 in 10 dimensions)."
2. The catalogue entry stores the inputs (k = 13; d = 20 and 10) and the exact expression 2^(−k/d); printed
   values are generated to two digits.
3. No known-answer row exists for this arithmetic and none is created: it is an order-of-magnitude argument.
   The register record carries the test below so that the sentence cannot drift again.
4. The practical rule the arithmetic supports is unchanged: certificates are issued per constraint on
   sub-boxes of about six parameters or fewer and assembled by products, monotonicity and witnesses (with
   d = 6, 10⁴ boxes keep 10^(−2/3) = 0.215 of each side, a useful refinement).

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/reconciliations.rs`:
- `rec_11_bisections`: 2^(−13/20) = 0.63728; 2^(−13/10) = 0.40613.
- `rec_11_ten_thousand`: 10^(−0.2) = 0.63096; 10^(−0.4) = 0.39811.
- `rec_11_rounding`: 0.63728 prints as 0.64 and 0.63096 as 0.63; the two differ at two digits, which is the
  recorded discrepancy.
- `rec_11_six_dimensions`: 10^(−4/6) = 0.21544.
- `bisections_of_ten_thousand`: log₂ 10⁴ = 13.288.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/reconciliations.sh` (bead N38b-50) prints REC-11; no script of its own.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the four fractions. Negative control: the catalogue's own shortcut, "pave the whole box": a
request for a region certificate over 20 parameters at once must be refused (the existing refusal for a
region certificate over more parameters than the admitted limit, or for a joint paving). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- The limit is about isotropic refinement; adaptive refinement along the few parameters that matter is what
  the sub-box rule exploits, and the pilot of region certificates measures whether it suffices.
- Higher-order enclosures move the barrier very little; the arithmetic of this record is independent of the
  enclosure method.
