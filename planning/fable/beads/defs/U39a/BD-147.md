## Background

An **exit review** records that a milestone's demonstrations ran and its gates have values. It is performed by a
**fresh-context session**: one that implemented none of the milestone's work. That session regenerates the gate
report from a clean checkout, reads every receipt, checks that every gate has a measured value and a caught
negative control, records a disposition for each, and records every kill decision due as an architecture
decision record (ADR). It implements nothing and repairs nothing. Reason: the sibling projects of this program
documented how agent swarms produce the appearance of progress (87 work items closed within 12 seconds with one
shared close reason); a gate's PASS is therefore never recorded by the session that built the capability.

Milestone M1 is the referee studio and first mission slice, on the CPU only: a typed design language and model
compiler, static analyses, a transactional referee step, baseline controllers, structured telemetry, both study
types, and the first mission queries answered over families of designs with certificates that an independent
auditor re-checks. It is the smallest product that is already useful and already honest, and it needs only three
foundations. Two program-level questions are also answered at this exit.

- **Is the rival's loop what the thesis assumed?** GATE-TENFOLD-02: the incumbent design loop with the common
  agent, end to end, on at least 12 development missions of the `legged-quad` and `wheeled` strata, with closed
  phase ledgers; the table of published phase figures is replaced by measured rows; a removable share and a
  ceiling are recorded for every lever of the thesis. And GATE-TENFOLD-09, ceiling before speed, runs for the
  first time here and at every exit after.
- **Is narrow purity affordable?** The schedule gate of BET-12 (the bet that a core built only on the program's
  own sibling libraries, with GPU annexes, reaches M2 without a foreign physics engine).

## Scope

In: the M1 exit record; a disposition for every gate part due; the kill decisions due; the schedule gate's
evaluation; the records of GATE-TENFOLD-02 and GATE-TENFOLD-09 and of KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER on the rival's ledger;
the risks and open questions due; the stop rules. Out: any repair or measurement; the shared review procedure,
exit record and runner (N40-05) and the exit as a query over the gate register with its load table (N40-04); the
gates' own beads.

## Technical approach

**1. Procedure (in brief; the shared runner implements it).** (1) Start a session with no context of M1 whose
identity appears as implementer of no due row. (2) Clean clone at the exit candidate commit; build on the pinned
toolchain. (3) Run every M1 demonstration through `frob --json` and, where the registry exposes the verb to
agents, through `frob-mcp`. (4) Run the recurring gates and the full negative-control sweep at this commit.
(5) Regenerate the gate report and the README status table; both must equal the committed files. (6) List kill
decisions with and without ADRs, shared close reasons, controls that have never failed, and parts that are
`not-run`, `blocked` or `fail`. (7) Record a disposition per part from its receipt: `accepted-pass`,
`accepted-kill: <fallback>`, `recorded-fail: blocks`, `not-run: <reason>`, `rejected: <reason>`. (8) Check each
kill decision against its registered trigger. (9) Read the risk register: per risk, whether its earliest signal
fired. (10) Answer or default the open questions due. (11) Demonstrate the exit line and apply the stop rules.
(12) Write `docs/delivery/exits/M1/EXIT.md`.

**2. The five exit conditions.** Every gate of the milestone has a recorded value (a failing value is a record; a
kill gate that fired is an ADR with the fallback in force). Every demonstration runs from a clean checkout.
GATE-GAUNT-01 holds: every gate that passed has a registered negative control that it catches. Every report
prints its `reality:` line (GATE-GROUND-01, from M1 onward). The risk register has been read and the open
questions due have been answered or have taken their defaults. A gate may pass before its milestone; it may not
move later without an ADR; where a list and the register differ, the register governs.

- *GATE-TENFOLD-09.* First run: every work package funded as a speed lever cites a removable share of at least

- *The schedule gate (BET-12).* Measurement: the elapsed time of M0 plus M1 against the planning estimate (2 + 5

  interim-posture checks (N33b-08), attached to the exit record; an item listed `posture: untested` cannot be
  counted as "an interim posture applied". Consequence: the program pauses for a written re-plan before M2
  starts. A slip above 10.5 weeks for other causes is recorded with its causes and does not trip the
  gate. The estimates exist to make the bet testable; they are not acceptance evidence.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/exit_m1.sh` wraps the shared runner `scripts/exit/review.sh M1`: the four demonstrations as
scripted sessions through the CLI and MCP; the ceiling check re-run on the rival's ledgers of record; the lever
shares recomputed from the ledgers and compared with the decision records; the recurring gates re-run at the
exit candidate's commit; `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example augur_slices -- --exit M1`; the negative-control
sweep; the ADR listing; the schedule computation from the milestone registry's dates. It sources the shared
helper `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes `target/e2e-logs/exit_m1/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript beside
it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`; logged: each command, exit code, wall time, the
envelope's evidence block, each recomputed share and ceiling beside the recorded one, elapsed weeks beside 10.5,
each recurring part with its receipt commit, the slice check's output, each disposition, and on failure the first
differing value with artifact paths; the summary counts dispositions by kind and names the gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the M1 load (4, 60, 12, and 7 recurring); 7 weeks and 10.5 weeks; 1/(1 − 0.10) = 1.11 for the lever rule; the
docking tolerance ε < (U + ½)/4 and the stance boundary 3/14 m shown by the demonstrations. Controls the review
must catch: a gate with a value and no caught control; a kill ADR that cites no receipt; a close reason shared by
several beads; a speed-lever work package with no cited share; a rival ledger that is `ledger-open` used for a
ceiling; a report without its `reality:` line; a PASS recorded by the implementing session.

## Considerations

- If the rival loop was not measured (compute not approved, or an incumbent could not be installed), M1 does not
  exit; the review records that state and stays open. It does not substitute published figures.
- The schedule gate asks about a cause, not only a duration; attributing blocked time to upstream work items is
