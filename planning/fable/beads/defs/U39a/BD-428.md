## Background

Milestone M7 is the ten-fold tournament: the thesis of FrankenRobotics (the time from a robot-design question to
an independently accepted answer is ten times shorter than with the strongest composed rival) is measured on a
**sealed suite** of missions and the result is published whatever it is. The suite is the experiment's sample.
It is written by a neutral adjudicator from public class descriptions, committed by hash before any arm is
frozen, opened only after every arm is frozen, and shown to an arm one mission card at a time when that arm's
clock starts. A sealed mission family serves one gate and is then retired: the tournament uses no family that the
partial gate at M3 used.

This epic covers every stratum that has a product to run. It is split into tasks when M7 opens; its seed
done-condition is that a short plan exists and its pilot has run, and that the suite's commitment hash is
published before either arm sees it. The mechanisms (strata registry and grid, family independence and rotation,
anti-shortcut controls, the sealing ledger, the warm arithmetic) were built for the partial gate; this epic uses
them at full scope.

"Externally authored" in the title is a requirement, not a hope: some sealed worlds are built with an evaluator
independent of every proposal engine, because a sampling run and a solver that share one mistranslated world
model agree on the wrong answer; and the missions themselves are written by a party that is neither builder nor
rival advocate.

## Scope

In: the short plan; the authoring pilot (N39ax-04); the tournament's strata, grid, sizes and regimes; the six
sealed mission types with their private changes (templates II for rebody, facility and throughput and the
warm-campaign rules, N39b-41, a sibling under WP-76); the enlarged development pool (N39ax-03); the commitment;
the warm cells' amendment sequences with standing assets and the crossover reuse count; the enlargement plans
carried over from M3. The suite's hygiene is measured by GATE-TENFOLD-12 (script N39b-42, gate row N40-21). Out:
the decision rule (BD-429); the arms (BD-430, BD-431); adjudication (BD-432); publication (BD-433).

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-428.md` when M7 opens) covers the following contract.**

1. **Strata and regimes.** Design strata `legged-quad`, `legged-biped`, `wheeled`, `tracked`, `hybrid` (answer
   kind: qualified design; cold); `envelope` (family answers and amendment verdicts; warm, with a cold base);
   `sensing` (qualified design or truthful unknown; cold); `rebody` (amendment verdicts; warm); `failure`
   (counterexample and repair; cold); `throughput` (a qualified design over a heterogeneous family at scale, the
   complete loop timed including certificates, divergence handling, cold compilation and the learner; cold);
   `facility` (amendment verdicts; warm; a Moonshot, run only if the facility co-design gate has passed,
   otherwise `not run`). A stratum that needs a mechanism which has not landed is declared `not run` in the
   report, with the reason.
2. **Grid.** Four robot classes (legged; wheeled; tracked; hybrid) on eight world types (open terrain and the
   seven interior archetypes: home, apartment, office, warehouse, manufacturing plant, retail store, commercial
   kitchen). Every cell holds at least two sealed mission families, or is declared `not applicable` with a
   written reason before the suite is sealed.
3. **Sizes.** 12 public development missions per stratum plus every family the partial gate retired; 24 sealed
   mission families per cell (a stratum and a regime) by default, never below 12, and at least 32 for a cell that
   declares a population result (46 where four confirmatory cells share the level); c = 1 campaign per family.
   The final counts are fixed by the pre-analysis before the suite is opened. At most four cells are named
   confirmatory. The family is the independent unit: missions that share a generator draw, a site, a material
   batch or a parent design are one family, and no sealed family is connected to anything revealed.
4. **The six sealed mission types, with what the adjudicator changes privately and the strongest control.**

| Sealed mission | Private changes | Strongest control |
|---|---|---|
| Adjustable stance under static load | Foot authority, a force floor, package dimensions, asymmetric payload, a missing foot, passive-spring compatibility | A lifted robust linear program; conventional projection; a force-law switch |
| Split-grip landing and the last usable observation | Mirror the strip; hide or delay the surface cue; shift the payload; change the contact order | Belief-state reachability; equal-budget reinforcement learning; geometric control |
| Gripping unfamiliar compliant stock | Geometry; an early-law alias; contact coupling | An expert probe; direct measurement at mission dwell; active system identification |
| Changing an accepted carrier or walker | Replace a gearbox, a wheel or a lower limb; perturb latency, friction or energy reserve | Retraining; a shared conditioned actor; warm-started control; ordinary graph caching |
| Recommissioning a facility | Move a door or a counter; perturb scan uncertainty; reduce a shared resource; alter a shift | Recomputation; incremental mixed-integer programming; standard motion planning; selective replay with cached bounds |
| A heterogeneous high-throughput family | Mix fixed and changed topology; grazing contacts; dense depth sensors; pressure to spill to the host | An optimized batched incumbent; this engine with family sharing switched off |

5. **Controls locked into every stratum.** At least one family in six on which a conventional method is expected
   to win or tie; at least one in twelve whose correct answer is `unknown` with a witness; unit, contact-order,
   causal-clock and passive-load mutations derived by the adjudicator; in amendment sequences the consequential
   edit concealed, with missed withdrawals and unnecessary invalidations both counted; the quantifier-order
   fixture permanently in `envelope`; at least one tier trap per design stratum.
6. **Warm cells.** Predeclared amendment sequences on an accepted cold answer; identical reusable inputs and
   equal cache budgets; failed amendments stay in the sequence and cost their deadline; one-time authoring and
   standing assets amortized at 1, 10 and 100 amendments: R_warm(N) = (S_B + N·b̄) ÷ (S_A + N·ā), with S each
   arm's standing-asset cost and ā, b̄ the pooled restricted mean times per amendment; the crossover reuse count
   N* is the smallest N with S_A + N·ā ≤ S_B + N·b̄; the warm verdict is evaluated at N = 10; a warm result is
   never quoted without S and N*.
7. **Order.** Publish the enlarged development pool; commit the suite and protocol hashes (the commitment is
   published before any arm is frozen and long before any arm sees a card); then tuning, freezes, the
   pre-analysis, and the opening, as the sealing ledger enforces.

**The pilot (registered before it runs; small enough for a day on a CPU).** *Suite-authoring pilot.* A
fresh-context adjudicator session authors six sealed-grade families (three `legged-quad`, three `envelope`) from
the public class descriptions alone, with all controls. Measured: adjudicator-hours per family; the share that
passes every suite check (kind admissibility, family connectivity, control quotas, card validation) after at
most one repair cycle; whether two independent authors given one cell brief produce families the connectivity
check keeps separate. *Falsifier:* fewer than four of six pass, or authoring exceeds four adjudicator-hours per
family (a tournament of eleven strata at 24 families is 264 families). *Fallback, named in advance:* fewer strata,
each declared `not run` with the reason. DESIGN (this epic's proposal for the order in which strata are dropped,
least-evidenced first; the protocol fixes only the fallback itself): `facility`, `throughput`, `rebody`,
`failure`, then `hybrid` and `tracked`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The short plan exists and states the seven parts; the pilot has run and its result is recorded, including
      a negative one.
- [ ] Seed done-condition: the suite's commitment hash is published before either arm sees the suite: its ledger
      entry precedes every freeze entry and every `card-shown` entry.
- [ ] No sealed family of the tournament was served at M3 or is connected to a revealed mission; the check's
      output is in the commitment.
- [ ] Every grid cell holds two sealed families or a dated `not applicable` reason; every stratum is listed as
      run or `not run` with its reason.
- [ ] Every stratum meets the control quotas; warm cards carry amendment sequences in which the consequential
      edits (at least one per sequence) are concealed among inconsequential ones, the card showing neither which
      nor how many (N39b-41).
- [ ] Cells from M3 with an enlargement plan have the planned family count or a recorded reason they do not.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the mechanism beads (strata, families, controls, sealing ledger, warm arithmetic). The epic adds
`epic_bd428_commitment_precedes_arms`: a query over the tournament's ledger that the step-2 entry's sequence
number is below every freeze and card entry; and `epic_bd428_no_family_reuse` over the union of gate registries.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_m7_suite.sh` (written by the suite task when the epic is split): runs every suite check on
the tournament's public parts (`frob-tenfold suite check --grid --strata --json`, `--families --json`,
`--controls --json`, `--commitment --json`) and the two epic tests above (`FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p
frob-conformance epic_bd428`), and, after the reveal, verifies that the published suite opens the commitment. It
plants a family reused from M3 and a suite committed after an arm's freeze on scratch copies, each asserted by
reason. Logged per STD-E2E (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code, wall
time). Before the reveal it logs only hashes, counts per stratum and cell, quota values and check outcomes; no
sealed content. The pilot has its own script and log (`scripts/e2e/tenfold_authoring_pilot.sh`, N39ax-04), and
the development pool its own (`scripts/e2e/tenfold_development_m7.sh`, N39ax-03).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 11 strata, 32 grid cells, at least 64 sealed families in the grid, quota minimums 4 and 2 per 24
families; the warm card (R_warm at 1, 10, 100: 0.272, 2.120, 6.596; limit 8.617; N* = 5). Negative controls: a
family reused from M3; a suite committed after an arm's freeze; a stratum silently absent; a warm table quoted
without S and N*; a sealed world generated by a proposal engine and marked independent.

## Considerations

- Cost: the tournament's default is 24 families per cell on both machines, above the default compute envelope; it
  needs the owner's approval of a campaign class per run, and of the pretraining class where an arm builds a
  standing asset. Without approval: fewer strata, each `not run` with the reason.
- The neutral party is an owner decision confirmed before the suite is sealed; under the fail-closed default (a
  fresh-context session under a named human) every ratio is `self-adjudicated` and the word waits for an external
  re-run of the kit.
- Expect `tracked`, `hybrid` and film-dependent cells to need cross-home adjudication.
- A leak retires the affected families at once; spare families per stratum are authored for that case.
