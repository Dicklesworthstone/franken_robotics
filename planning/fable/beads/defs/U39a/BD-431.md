## Background

In the ten-fold tournament at milestone M7, **arm A** is FrankenRobotics itself: the release under test at the
gate's commit, driven by the common agent (the same pinned model that drives the rival arms) through the
product's generated documentation and its public verbs, the same surface any user has. Arm A's restricted mean
time to an independently accepted answer is the denominator of every ratio the tournament reports.

Because the builders run this arm, its rules are the strictest. It is frozen like the rivals and cannot take a
fix while the suite is open. Its agents start from fresh contexts and no human may help. Every second of every
campaign must land in a phase of the ledger. And correctness is a gate, not a term in a score: **one false
certificate disqualifies the arm** from the scoped claim.

This epic is split into tasks when M7 opens. Seed done-condition: a short plan exists and its pilot has run;
every phase of every campaign is in the phase ledger, and one false certificate disqualifies the arm. The M3
version of this arm (fresh agents on the full MCP surface at the partial gate) is its predecessor; at M7 the
product has what M3 lacked: family policies, inheritance and rank-agreement allocation, where those passed
their gates at M4.

## Scope

In: the short plan; the dress-rehearsal pilot; arm A's freeze at the release; its tag map and ledger closure; its
standing assets; cold and warm campaigns on every stratum that has a product to run; the disqualification hooks;
the optional second-platform result. Out: the suite (BD-428); the decision rule (BD-429); rival arms (BD-430);
adjudication (BD-432); publication (BD-433); the disqualification procedure itself (see `needs`).

## Technical approach

**The short plan (to `docs/delivery/epics/BD-431.md` when M7 opens) covers the following contract.**

1. **What is frozen.** The product commit and the stack lock (every sibling pin and the toolchain, frozen for the
   length of the tournament, because a ratio of times measured across a pin move is not one measurement); the
   verb registry and generated documentation; the agent pin and caps (the gate's common agent); the kernel and
   scene caches in their declared state; standing assets with their measured build cost (a pretrained family
   policy per morphology class, compiled scene banks, kernel caches); the tag map; the builders' tuning log and
   development scores; the builders' signed statement of what more budget would have added. One manifest per
   stratum, published after the suite commitment and before the opening. A pin move or a toolchain change during
   the tournament voids the runs it spans.
2. **How a campaign runs.** The harness creates the workspace from the frozen pack, starts a fresh agent session
   behind the agent proxy with only the documentation, delivers the mission card (the clock starts), and enforces
   the caps (one RTX 4090 of `trj`, the gate's CPU cores, token and tool-call caps). The agent works through
   `frob-mcp` and `frob --json`; no private interface, no builder-written per-mission guidance. It submits through
   the adjudicator's submission protocol and receives only receipts. No human in the loop; any intervention is
   logged and flagged.
3. **Cold and warm, never pooled.** A cold campaign starts from the card and nothing specific to the mission:
   authoring the environment, the design domain, the requirement and the evaluation set is inside the clock. A
   warm campaign runs the card's amendment sequence on an accepted cold answer; reuse lives in the product's own
   stores (memo graph, caches, inherited policies) under the equal cache budget; a failed amendment stays in the
   sequence and costs its deadline.
4. **Every phase in the ledger.** Arm A's tag map assigns each verb and job to one of the thirteen phases
   (`author`, `propose`, `check`, `synthesize`, `compile`, `screen`, `train`, `evaluate`, `qualify`, `certify`,
   `diagnose`, `wait`, `adjudicate`); compilation ends at the first device step; while the agent is blocked on
   several jobs the one that finishes last owns the time; background work goes to the resource table. Closure:
   the exclusive phases sum to the clock within 2%; a run with more is `ledger-open`, stays in the ratio and
   leaves ceiling arithmetic. TARGET for this arm: no `ledger-open` run (the product reports its own timing, so a
   hole is a defect of the tag map).
5. **What is recorded.** Per campaign: outcome, clock, the ledger with its resource table, agent turns and
   tokens, designs decided, trainings, cost columns, receipts, the freeze hash; per edit, the edit-to-first-step
   interval; per accepted answer, whether a certificate changed the decision (the count is reported with the
   result).
6. **Disqualification hooks.** Every certificate arm A submits is replayed by the offline auditor and checked by
   the adjudicator's direct evaluation. A certificate its checker accepted and that direct evaluation or any
   later audit shows false within its stated scope is a false certificate: every cell of the gate in which arm A
   used that proof kind has no ratio for arm A and the verdict `disqualified`; the proof kind is suspended and its
   cells fall to `Tested`. A truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate and a certificate with a narrower scope
   than asked are not false certificates; they cost coverage and time. Also disqualifying: any read of sealed
   content by the arm's agent, tools or developers before the gate closes; a change to the frozen arm; use of the
   adjudicating simulator in development or tuning; an edited ledger or receipt. A breach of a resource cap is
   not: the run is censored and costs the deadline.
7. **A second platform.** Arm A may add a best-supported-platform result on the Apple M5 Ultra machine; it is
   labelled, never pooled with the primary result, and a gap that exists on one platform only is a portability
   observation, not the ratio.

**The pilot.** *Dress rehearsal.* During the tournament's two-week freeze and rehearsal, the frozen arm A runs
six development families per stratum (drawn from the families the partial gate retired) through the full harness
with fresh agents. Measured: the share of `ledger-open` runs; completion by the deadline; the unattributed share;
whether any campaign needed a human. *Falsifier:* more than 2% of rehearsal runs are `ledger-open`, or completion
on `legged-quad` or `wheeled` is below the same strata's completion at the partial gate, or any campaign stalls
on a documentation gap. *Consequence:* the freeze is redone once before the suite opens (the suite hash is
already committed and does not move); a second failure is recorded and the arm runs as it is.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The short plan exists with the seven parts; the dress rehearsal has run and its result is recorded.
- [ ] Seed done-condition: every campaign of arm A in the tournament has a ledger whose exclusive phases sum to
      its clock, and the share of `ledger-open` runs is reported per cell.
- [ ] Seed done-condition: a planted false certificate in a rehearsal cell yields `disqualified` for every cell
      using that proof kind, and no ratio for arm A there.
- [ ] Arm A's manifests are published before the opening; the workspace at every campaign start equals the
      frozen pack; no pin moved during the tournament.
- [ ] Cold and warm results are separate records; warm records carry S and feed N*.
- [ ] No campaign has an unflagged human intervention; token and tool-call counts are within caps or the run is
      censored.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

Carried by the mechanism beads. The epic adds `epic_bd431_all_phases_ledgered`: a query that every arm A
campaign record of the tournament has a ledger passing its check; `epic_bd431_pins_frozen`: the stack lock hash
is identical in the first and last campaign records; `epic_bd431_falsecert_scope`: the disqualification set
equals the set of cells in which the proof kind was used.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_m7_our_arm.sh`: on development families, freezes arm A on a rehearsal gate, runs one cold
and one warm campaign per stratum in scope with a fresh agent through `frob-mcp`, builds ledgers, and plants a
false certificate in one rehearsal submission. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence
block for product verbs, the clock and exclusive table per campaign, the unattributed share against 2%, cap
usage against caps, each receipt's decision and level, and the disqualification record.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: none of its own; the worked arm A ledger (600, 360, 20, 0, 12, 96, 150, 60, 40, 0, 30, 42, 90 s
summing to 1,500 s) is the shape a closed ledger has. Negative controls: a planted false certificate (the arm is
disqualified); a canary value from a sealed world in a transcript (the cell is void); a pin moved mid-tournament
(the runs it spans are void); a builder-written per-mission hint in the workspace (a foreign file at cold start;
the campaign is refused).

## Considerations

- M7 needs M4, not M5 or M6: a stratum that needs a mechanism of a later milestone which has not landed is
  declared `not run` with the reason.
- The result is conditional on the pinned agent; people, learning curves and usability for a person are outside
  the test.
- Pretraining a family policy exceeds the default envelope and needs the owner's approval of the pretraining
  class; without it arm A runs with specialists and inheritance only, and its standing-asset cost is small.
- The freeze applies to the builders as much as to the rivals: a defect found in arm A after the opening voids
  the affected cells and is not patched mid-gate.
