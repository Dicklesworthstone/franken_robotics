## Background

In the ten-fold test an *answer* is what a mission asks for, and the clock of an arm stops only when a neutral
adjudicator accepts one. The mission is handed to an arm's agent as a **mission card**, shown only at the instant
that arm's clock starts. The adjudicator writes each card from the public class descriptions; no arm and no
builder writes a sealed card.

A card names **exactly one answer kind** (DESIGN):

| Kind | What the arm submits | Accepted when |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified design | A design in the mission's family, a controller behind the standard observation and action interface, and the declared clocks | The acceptance battery passes in the adjudicating simulator: over n sealed world clusters, the exact one-sided lower confidence bound on the requirement pass rate is at least the mission's threshold |
| Family answer | Inner and outer design sets with witnesses, exclusions with impossibility witnesses, and the unresolved band | Every mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligation of the card is discharged; the unresolved band or optimality gap is within the card's maximum; every certificate checks; direct evaluation at the adjudicator's sealed probe points contradicts nothing. Strict and closed endpoints are part of acceptance where an exact boundary is asked for. A vacuous enclosure discharges nothing |
| Amendment verdict | For a changed mission: which earlier verdicts survive, which are withdrawn, and the repaired answer | The repaired answer passes as its own kind; no kept verdict is one the adjudicator's ground truth withdraws. A missed withdrawal fails the mission; unnecessary invalidations are counted and reported |
| Truthful unknown | A statement that the supplied evidence does not identify the choice, with the required witness: two admissible worlds or models under which the best choices differ, or the distinguishing experiment the card asks for | Only on missions whose question is whether the evidence identifies the choice. Everywhere else `unknown` is not an answer and the clock keeps running |
| Counterexample and repair | A replayable failing world inside the legality bounds, and a repaired design | The failure reproduces in the adjudicating simulator, and the repaired design passes the battery of the first row |

Terms: an *inner design set* is a set of designs every member of which meets the requirement over the whole world
family; an *outer set* contains every design that can; the *unresolved band* lies between them. A *world cluster*
is the unit that shares one generator draw (for example one building with its materials). The *legality bounds*
are the declared limits inside which an adversarial world must stay.

This bead is the card's data model. Reason for one record with a public part and a sealed part: a card must be
committable before any arm is frozen and revealable after the gate closes, and the part an arm may read must be
separable by construction from the adjudicator's ground truth.

## Scope

In: the record `frob.tenfold.mission/1` (public part), its sealed companion, validation, the card's commitment
hashes, the rules for showing it. Out: the acceptance procedures per kind (N39a-06, N39a-07); submissions and
receipts (N39a-09); the suite-level controls counted over cards (N39a-14); amendment sequences as run (N39a-16).

## Technical approach

**1. Public part (DESIGN; shown to an arm at clock start, to everyone at the gate's close).**
```
{ "schema": "frob.tenfold.mission/1", "gate": "M3-partial", "pool": "development" | "sealed",
  "stratum": "legged-quad", "family": "f:…", "regime": "cold" | "warm", "answer_kind": "qualified-design" | "family-answer" | "amendment-verdict" |
                                            "truthful-unknown" | "counterexample-and-repair",
  "robot_family": { "grammar": "b3:…", "mass_bounds_kg": ["…","…"], "actuator_catalogue": "b3:…",
                    "symmetry_knowledge": "b3:…" },
  "world_family": { "generator": "b3:…", "parameters": { … }, "legality": "b3:…" },
  "task": { "prose": "…", "requirement": "<text in the requirement language>", "monitor": "b3:…" },
  "obligations": { … per kind, below … },
  "battery": { "threshold": "0.9", "allowed_failures": 0, "n_first": 36, "tier": "adjudicator" },
  "deadline_s": "14400",
  "caps": { "devices": 1, "cpu_cores": 8, "memory_gib": "16", "tokens_in": "…", "tokens_out": "…", "tool_calls": "…" },
  "permitted_inputs": { "calibration_data": ["b3:…"], "prior_answers": ["b3:…"], "caches": ["b3:…"] },
  "amendments": [ { "seq": 1, "edit": "b3:…" } ],
  "sealed_commitment": "b3:…", "card_hash": "b3:…" }
```
- `gate` is one of the gate names of the harness skeleton (N39a-01); `pool` says whether the card belongs to
  that gate's public development suite or to its sealed suite, and equals the pool of its family in the family
  registry. That agreement is checked where the registry exists, by its suite check (N39a-13, which reads cards
  and depends on this bead), not by `card check`.
- `robot_family`: the grammar and bounds every arm receives equally, including the symmetry knowledge.
- `task.requirement` is stated twice, in prose and in the product's requirement language (unit-normalized
  predicates under temporal operators); the *adjudicator's own monitor* evaluates it, and its hash is on the card.
- `battery.n_first` is the cluster count of the first submission; later submissions use the level schedule of
  N39a-09 (for threshold 0.9 and no allowed failure: 36, 46, 53, … clusters).
- `amendments` is present only for `warm` cards and lists the edits in order. The card does **not** say which edit
  matters (hidden consequence, N39a-14).
- `obligations` by kind: *qualified-design*: the observation and action interface and the control clocks;
  *family-answer*: the design domain, the lists of mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations (each with
  the region it concerns and whether an endpoint is strict or closed), `max_unresolved_band` or
  `max_optimality_gap`, the proof kinds admitted; *amendment-verdict*: the prior accepted answer and its verdict
  list; *truthful-unknown*: the evidence supplied and the witness form required (a world pair or model pair, or a
  named distinguishing experiment); *counterexample-and-repair*: the design under test, the monitors, the legality
  bounds.

**2. Sealed part (`sealed/<mission-hash>.json`; adjudicator only until close).**
`{ card: "b3:…", salt: "<32 random bytes, hex>", world_key: "<sealed key for cluster derivation>",
probe_points: [exact rationals], ground_truth: { verdicts to withdraw; the consequential edit; whether the evidence
identifies the choice; the planted failure for failure missions }, controls: { easy_win, unknown_truth,
mutations: [unit | contact-order | causal-clock | passive-load], hidden_consequence, quantifier_order, tier_trap },
canaries: ["…"], independent_evaluator: bool }`.
`sealed_commitment` = BLAKE3 over the canonical sealed part including its salt: a hiding commitment, because
sealed content can be low-entropy and must not be guessable from its hash. `card_hash` covers the public part.

**3. Validation (`frob-tenfold card check`).** Numbered checks, each with a refusal reason:
1. exactly one answer kind, and `obligations` matches it (`card-kind-mismatch`);
2. the stratum admits the kind (N39a-12), for example `envelope` admits family answers and amendment verdicts
   (`card-kind-not-in-stratum`);
3. the requirement parses, is unit-consistent and has a finite reach no longer than the episode horizon; the
   monitor hash resolves in the adjudicator's acceptance tree (`card-requirement-invalid`);
4. `deadline_s` equals the stratum's H (the stratum record's `deadline_s`, N39a-12) on a sealed card, and on a
   development card the provisional development deadline published with the stratum's class description (the
   stratum record's `development_deadline_s`, 14,400 s by default) (the stratum's H is fixed only after the development suite has
   been run, so a development card carrying it is refused too); `caps` equal the gate's caps
   (`card-deadline-mismatch`, `card-caps-differ`);
5. a `truthful-unknown` card has ground truth "not identified" or "identified", and only then may `unknown` be an
   accepted answer on it (`card-unknown-without-truth`);
6. a family answer's obligations are not vacuous: at least one interior and one exterior obligation, each with a
   non-empty region (`card-vacuous-obligations`);
7. every permitted input resolves to an address that every arm of the gate receives (`card-unequal-inputs`);
8. the sealed part exists, its commitment matches, and it holds at least one canary (`card-sealed-missing`).

**4. Showing a card.** `frob-tenfold campaign start` is the only operation that copies a public part to an arm's
readable area, in the same atomic step that stamps `card_delivered`. Before that step the path does not exist for
the arm. The sealed part is never copied.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The record round-trips with identical bytes; each of the eight validation checks has a passing and a
      refused fixture.
- [ ] Changing one byte of the sealed part changes `sealed_commitment`; two sealed parts with equal content and
      different salts have different commitments.
- [ ] A card of each of the five kinds validates; a card naming two kinds, or none, is refused.
- [ ] No operation other than `campaign start` exposes a public part before the gate closes, and none exposes a
      sealed part (checked by enumerating the operator tool's subcommands against a planted sealed store).
- [ ] The twelve public development missions of a stratum use the same record with the gate whose development
      suite holds them, `pool: "development"`, and a sealed part (ground truth, controls, salt) that is published
      with them; a development card carrying the stratum's sealed deadline is refused (`card-deadline-mismatch`).
      A card whose `pool` differs from its family's pool is refused by the family registry's check (N39a-13).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- card::`
- `five_kinds_validate`: one fixture per kind → accepted.
- `kind_obligation_mismatch`: a qualified-design card carrying family obligations → `card-kind-mismatch`.
- `vacuous_family_obligations`: empty interior list → `card-vacuous-obligations`.
- `unknown_needs_truth`: a truthful-unknown card with no ground truth entry → refused.
- `commitment_hides`: equal content, different salts → different commitments; verify(commitment, sealed) holds.
- `deadline_must_match_stratum`: sealed card 14,400 s in a stratum with H = 7,200 s → refused; a development
  card at the provisional 14,400 s in the same stratum → accepted; a development card at 7,200 s → refused.
- `requirement_reach`: a requirement whose temporal window exceeds the episode horizon → refused.
- `card_show_only_at_start`: before `campaign start` the arm-readable path is absent; after it, present with the
  public part's exact bytes.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_cards.sh`: writes five development cards (one per kind) with `frob-tenfold card new`, checks
them, plants eight defects and expects each refusal, commits a rehearsal sealed part and verifies its opening.
It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/tenfold_cards/<utc>.ndjson`.
Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the card hash, the commitment, each refusal reason (`tenfold.card-…`)
with its JSON pointer and exit code, on a failed assertion the first differing field with the card path, and the
summary. No sealed material of a real gate is used.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: for threshold 0.9 with no allowed failure the first three battery sizes are 36, 46 and 53 clusters
(0.9³⁶ = 0.02253 ≤ 0.025 < 0.9³⁵ = 0.02503); for threshold 0.99 they are 368, 477 and 546. Negative controls: a
card that reveals which amendment matters in its public part (refused by a field allowlist); a card with a
requirement whose monitor is not the adjudicator's; a sealed part without a salt (the commitment check fails).
Metamorphic: key order of the JSON input does not change `card_hash`.

## Considerations

- The requirement is written by the adjudicator in the product's language because both arms must be told the same
  predicate; the adjudicator's monitor, not the product's evaluator, decides. A rival that cannot read the language
  reads the prose and the monitor's source, which are published with the development suite.
- Some worlds are built with an evaluator independent of every proposal engine, because a sampling run and a
  solver that share one mistranslated world model agree on the wrong answer; the sealed flag
  `independent_evaluator` marks them.
- Private scenes or datasets cannot appear on a card: a card with a private input cannot enter a replication kit.
- Revisit the field list when the first external adjudicator writes real cards; additions are a new schema version.
