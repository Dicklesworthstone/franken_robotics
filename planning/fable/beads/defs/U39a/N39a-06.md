## Background

In the ten-fold test the clock of an arm stops when a neutral adjudicator accepts its answer. Two of the five
answer kinds are decided by running the submitted design in the **adjudicating simulator** (a simulator neither
arm used for training, tuning or screening on that stratum):

- **Qualified design.** The arm submits a design in the mission's family, a controller behind the standard
  observation and action interface, and the declared clocks. It is accepted when, over n sealed world clusters,
  the exact one-sided lower confidence bound on the requirement pass rate is at least the mission's threshold.
- **Counterexample and repair.** The arm submits a replayable failing world inside the legality bounds and a
  repaired design. It is accepted when the failure reproduces in the adjudicating simulator and the repaired
  design passes the battery of the first kind.

A *world cluster* is the unit that shares one generator draw (one building with its materials, one terrain patch);
clusters are the independent units of the test. The *requirement* is the mission's pass predicate, evaluated by
the adjudicator's own monitor on each episode.

Reason for an exact test and not a point estimate: the denominator of the whole comparison is an accepted
decision, and its error rate must hold for every arm under retries. A 1% failure bound at 95% confidence costs 299
failure-free episodes per accepted design in every arm (0.99²⁹⁹ = 0.0495 ≤ 0.05 < 0.99²⁹⁸); under the retry
schedule of this protocol the first submission is tested at level 0.025 and needs 368. Rejected: a normal
approximation to the pass rate (wrong exactly where pass rates are near 1); accepting on a point estimate above
the threshold (about half of designs exactly at the threshold would be accepted).

## Scope

In: the battery rule (sizes and decision), cluster derivation for a submission, running episodes in the
adjudicating simulator, the cluster verdict, the reproduction test for a counterexample, the decision record
handed to the receipt writer. Out: the simulator adapter and controller process (N39a-08); commitment, levels and
receipts (N39a-09); family answers, amendment verdicts and unknowns (N39a-07).

## Technical approach

**1. The battery rule (exact; DESIGN for the size rule).** Inputs from the card: threshold p⋆ ∈ (0, 1),
`allowed_failures` f ≥ 0. Input from the submission protocol: the level α_i of submission i, with
α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)] and α = 0.05.
1. *Size.* n_i is the smallest n such that a result with at most f failures can be accepted:
   P(X ≥ n − f) ≤ α_i for X ~ Binomial(n, p⋆). With f = 0 this is n_i = ⌈ln α_i ÷ ln p⋆⌉.
2. *Decision.* With k passing clusters out of n_i, accept iff P(X ≥ k) ≤ α_i for X ~ Binomial(n_i, p⋆). This is
   the statement "the exact one-sided lower confidence bound at confidence 1 − α_i on the pass rate is at least
   p⋆" (the Clopper–Pearson bound), written as a test so that it is decided in exact rational arithmetic with no
   root finding.
3. *Reported bound.* The lower bound itself is reported to six places, rounded down, by bisection on the exact
   tail; it is descriptive and never decides.
The tail is computed as an exact rational: Σ_{j=k}^{n} C(n, j)·a^j·(b − a)^{n−j} ÷ bⁿ for p⋆ = a/b.

**2. Clusters for a submission.** For submission i on mission M the adjudicator derives n_i fresh clusters from
the mission's sealed key, the mission hash, the index i and a counter, after the submission has been committed.
The derivation key is (sealed key, mission, campaign index, submission index, counter) and does not include the
arm: no cluster serves two submission indices or two missions, and the two arms of a pair are scored on the same
sealed acceptance worlds at the same submission index, which is what makes the pair a pair (N39a-27). The
derivation is deterministic from the sealed key, so it can be replayed after the gate closes.

**3. One cluster, one verdict.** For each cluster: (a) instantiate the world from the generator at the derived
draw in the adjudicating simulator; (b) import the submitted design through the neutral exchange description;
(c) start the submitted controller as a separate process behind the observation and action interface with the
card's clocks; (d) run one episode of the card's horizon under the pinned seed; (e) evaluate the requirement with
the adjudicator's monitor. The cluster passes iff the monitor's verdict is satisfied. A controller that crashes,
exceeds its per-step deadline or emits a non-finite action fails the cluster. A simulator fault that is not caused
by the submission is a harness fault for the whole submission, never a pass or a fail.

**4. Counterexample and repair.** Acceptance has two parts, both required.
1. *Reproduction.* The submitted world is checked against the legality bounds of the card (an illegal world is
   rejected). It is then replayed in the adjudicating simulator with the design under test and the card's
   monitors; the failure reproduces iff the same monitor reports a violation. A world that fails only in the arm's
   home simulator does not reproduce.
2. *Repair.* The repaired design is tested by the battery rule above on fresh clusters, plus the submitted failing
   world itself, which the repaired design must pass.

**5. Output.** A decision record `{mission, submission_index, level, n, passes, failures, decision,
lower_bound_6dp, simulator_pin, clusters_hash}` goes to the receipt writer. Per-cluster outcomes are stored in the
sealed area and released only when every arm of the gate has closed.

**6. Determinism.** The adjudicating simulator runs deterministically on a CPU at a pinned revision; a battery
re-run with the same key, index and submission reproduces every cluster verdict. The decision arithmetic is D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sizes: for p⋆ = 0.99, f = 0: 368, 477, 546 at submissions 1, 2, 3 and 766 at submission 10; for p⋆ = 0.9:
      36, 46, 53 and 74; at a single test of level 0.05 the size is 299 for p⋆ = 0.99.
- [ ] The decision equals the exact tail test on a table of 10³ (n, k, p⋆, α_i) cases checked against big-integer
      arithmetic; no binary floating point decides.
- [ ] A planted design that passes exactly the threshold share in expectation is accepted in at most α_i of 2,000
      simulated first submissions (TARGET: at most 0.025 + 3 standard errors).
- [ ] A controller crash fails its cluster; a planted simulator fault yields a harness fault, not a failure.
- [ ] A counterexample that does not reproduce, or lies outside the legality bounds, is rejected; a repair that
      still fails the submitted world is rejected.
- [ ] No cluster identifier appears under two submission indices, two campaign indices or two missions (the arms
      of one pair share the clusters of a given submission index by design, which is what makes them a pair); a
      re-run reproduces every cluster verdict bit for bit.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- battery::`
- `size_zero_failure`: the sizes listed above, plus p⋆ = 0.9999 at level 0.05 → 29,956 and at α₁₀ = 0.05/110 →
  76,959.
- `size_ratio_to_single_test`: the cost of a retry in the large-p⋆ limit, ln α_i ÷ ln 0.05, for i = 1, 10, 100,
  500 is 1.2314, 2.5691, 4.0778 and 5.1496, each within 0.005 of the printed 1.23, 2.57, 4.08 and 5.15 (half a
  unit of the last printed digit, rule 2 of the tolerance rules N38b-27; this is registry row TF-24 of N39-KA,
  `same_as` KA-621, and the test is that row's binding). The ratios of the integer sizes at p⋆ = 0.99 (368, 766,
  1,216 and 1,535 over 299: 1.2308, 2.5619, 4.0669, 5.1338) are a different quantity: 2.56, 4.07 and 5.13 fail
  the same check, and the test asserts that they do.
- `size_with_allowed_failure`: p⋆ = 0.9, f = 1, level 0.025 → the smallest n with P(X ≥ n − 1) ≤ 0.025, checked
  against direct enumeration.
- `decision_exact_table`: 10³ random cases against a big-integer reference.
- `boundary_is_inclusive`: tail exactly equal to α_i accepts.
- `crash_fails_cluster`, `nonfinite_action_fails_cluster`, `deadline_overrun_fails_cluster`.
- `counterexample_must_reproduce`, `counterexample_must_be_legal`, `repair_must_pass_failing_world`.
- `cluster_freshness_property`: over 10³ random submission sequences, derived cluster sets of distinct
  (mission, campaign index, submission index) keys are pairwise disjoint, and arms A, B and C at the same key
  receive identical sets.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_battery.sh`: on a development mission with a `synthetic` planted pair of designs (one with a
true pass rate of 1.0, one with 0.8 against a threshold of 0.9), commits one submission of each, runs the battery
in the adjudicating simulator through `frob-tenfold adjudicate`, and asserts acceptance of the first and rejection
of the second; then submits a planted counterexample world and its repair. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`
(STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/tenfold_battery/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall
time, n, passes, the level, the decision, the reported lower bound against the threshold, the simulator pin, the
decision record's digest and, on failure, the first failing cluster's address (development clusters only, never
sealed ones) with the decision record path.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the size table (registry row TF-23 of N39-KA, bound to `size_zero_failure`) and the retry cost
(TF-24, bound to `size_ratio_to_single_test`); 0.99²⁹⁹ = 0.04954 and 0.99²⁹⁸ = 0.05004; 0.9³⁶ = 0.02253 and
0.9³⁵ = 0.02503.
Negative controls: a battery that reuses clusters across submissions at a constant level (false acceptance of a
no-margin design in more than 99% of 500-retry campaigns; the freshness property test fails it); a decision by
point estimate (accepts a design at exactly the threshold about half the time; the 2,000-submission test fails
it); a build that scores a cluster in the arm's home simulator. Metamorphic: permuting cluster order does not
change the decision.

## Considerations

- The battery tier is the adjudicating simulator, a third model and not the world; a pass is not a statement
  about hardware, and every receipt carries `reality: no-claim`.
- Battery cost grows with ln(1/α_i): retrying is allowed, costs clock time, and cannot buy acceptance by
  repetition. The adjudication time is inside the arm's clock, so n_i is itself a cost the arm weighs.
- If no neutral simulator can represent a stratum, batteries run in the highest-trust tier of both arms
  (cross-home adjudication), and an answer must pass in both; the decision record then holds two results.
- Sampling-controller episodes are acceptable evidence here only as the delivered controller's own episodes; the
  battery always runs the controller that was submitted.
