## Background

Three of the five answer kinds of the ten-fold test are not decided by running a battery of episodes. They are
statements about a *family* of designs or worlds, and the adjudicator must decide whether the statement is true.

- **Family answer.** Inner and outer design sets with witnesses, exclusions with impossibility witnesses, and the
  unresolved band. (An inner set contains only designs that meet the requirement over the whole world family; an
  outer set contains every design that can; the band between them is undecided.)
- **Amendment verdict.** For a changed mission: which earlier verdicts survive, which are withdrawn, and the
  repaired answer.
- **Truthful unknown.** A statement that the supplied evidence does not identify the choice, with a witness.

Every mathematical assertion an arm submits is replayed by `frob-verify`, the offline certificate auditor of the
product (a binary with no dependencies whose bundle schema is public and which any arm may target). DESIGN:
**because the builders wrote that checker, it is not the last word in this test.** The adjudicator also evaluates
each family answer directly against the mission, with tools of the adjudicator's choosing: exact rational
evaluation at sealed probe points, an SMT or interval tool on the raw mission statement, dense sampling in the
adjudicating simulator. A disagreement between an accepted certificate and the direct evaluation is a **false
certificate**, and one false certificate disqualifies the arm from the scoped claim.

Reason: two programs that share a solver, or a checker and a producer that share an author, have correlated error.
A quantifier written in the wrong order, a strict inequality checked as closed, or an enclosure so wide that it
says nothing would each pass a checker that trusts the statement it was handed. Rejected: accepting on
certificate replay alone; accepting a rival's family answer without the same checker (the rival's answer goes
through the same checker and the same direct evaluation).

## Scope

In: the obligations ledger of a family answer; certificate replay; direct evaluation; endpoint and vacuity rules;
the amendment-verdict comparison against ground truth; the truthful-unknown witness check; the false-certificate
signal. Out: battery-decided kinds (N39a-06); what happens after a false certificate (the disqualification
engine N39b-01 and the standing decision KILL-TENFOLD-CERT N39b-03; for arm A also the suspension of the proof
kind, its cells falling to `Tested`, and KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT, N25b-04); receipts and levels (N39a-09); the
revalidation engine arm A uses to produce amendment verdicts (the product's own). The SMT or interval tool of
check 5(b) is of the adjudicator's choosing, pinned by revision and run out of process as a comparator, never
linked into a product crate.

## Technical approach

**1. Family answer: acceptance is the conjunction of five checks, in this order.**
1. *Schema and scope.* The submission is a bundle in the public certificate format; its embedded statement
   (canonical bytes of the query: quantifier prefix, domain, assumptions) equals the card's statement. A different
   quantifier prefix, domain or assumption set is rejected, not reinterpreted.
2. *Obligations.* The card lists mandatory interior obligations (regions that must be inside the inner set),
   exterior obligations (regions that must be excluded, each with an impossibility witness) and boundary
   obligations (a boundary to locate within a tolerance). The adjudicator builds an obligations ledger: one row
   per obligation with the cells of the answer that claim to discharge it. Every mandatory row must be discharged.
   A scope narrower than asked discharges only what it covers and leaves rows open.
3. *Band.* The unresolved band, or the optimality gap, is at most the card's `max_unresolved_band` or
   `max_optimality_gap`, measured in the card's units by the adjudicator from the submitted sets.
4. *Certificates.* Every certificate in the bundle is replayed by `frob-verify` at a pinned build on the
   adjudicator's machine. A refused certificate discharges nothing; it is not a false certificate.
5. *Direct evaluation.* (a) At each sealed probe point (exact rationals chosen by the adjudicator inside claimed
   inner cells, inside claimed exclusions and on both sides of each claimed boundary) the mission statement is
   evaluated exactly by the adjudicator's own evaluator; a point in a claimed inner cell must satisfy the
   requirement over the world family, and a point in a claimed exclusion must not. (b) Where the card admits it, an
   SMT or interval tool is run on the raw mission statement over each claimed cell. (c) For claims about dynamics,
   dense sampling in the adjudicating simulator inside each claimed cell. *Nothing may contradict the answer.*

**2. Two rules that close the cheap exits.**
- *Strict and closed endpoints.* Where the card asks for an exact boundary, the answer must state for each endpoint
  whether it is included. Probe points are placed exactly on the boundary: an answer that includes a point the
  ground truth excludes, or the reverse, is contradicted. Known case: a boundary at exactly 3/14 m with the closed
  side on the inner set; an answer reporting 0.2143 as a decimal fails the exact probe at 3/14.
- *A vacuous enclosure discharges nothing.* An outer set equal to the whole design domain, an inner set of measure
  zero where an interior obligation asks for a region, or a band wider than the card's maximum: each leaves its
  obligation open.

**3. Outcomes of the five checks.**

| Situation | Decision | Further effect |
|---|---|---|
| All five pass | `accepted` | — |
| An obligation open, band too wide, a certificate refused, scope narrower than asked | `rejected` | Costs coverage and time; **not** a false certificate |
| A certificate that `frob-verify` (or the arm's declared checker) accepted is contradicted by direct evaluation or by a later audit, within its stated scope | `rejected` | A **false-certificate signal** is raised with the certificate hash, the proof kind, the probe point or tool output that contradicts it, and both verdicts |
| A truthful `unknown` on a mission of this kind | `rejected` | Not an answer here; the clock keeps running |

**4. Amendment verdict.** The submission lists, for each earlier verdict, `kept` or `withdrawn`, and a repaired
answer. Acceptance: (a) the repaired answer passes as its own kind (by this bead or by the battery); (b) no
`kept` verdict is one the adjudicator's sealed ground truth withdraws: one *missed withdrawal* fails the mission;
(c) *unnecessary invalidations* (verdicts withdrawn that ground truth keeps) do not fail it; they are counted per
arm and reported. The card does not say which edit of the sequence matters.

**5. Truthful unknown.** Admissible only on cards whose question is whether the evidence identifies the choice.
The witness is either two admissible worlds or models under which the best choices differ, or the distinguishing
experiment the card asks for. The adjudicator checks: each world or model lies in the card's world family and is
consistent with every supplied observation (exactly, by replay of the evidence); the best choice under each is
computed by the adjudicator's evaluator and the two differ. If the sealed ground truth says the evidence does
identify the choice, `unknown` is rejected however good the witness looks, and the witness is kept as a finding.

**6. Records.** The decision record lists each check with its result and, for a rejection, the first open
obligation or the contradicting point. Probe points and ground truth stay sealed until the gate closes; the arm's
receipt holds only the decision, the index, the level spent, the units used and a hash.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A correct family answer on the stance fixture (boundary b = 3/14 m, corner allocation (160, 130, 110, 0) N as
      witness, a dual vector as impossibility witness) is accepted; the same answer with the boundary's endpoint
      flipped is rejected at the exact probe.
- [ ] A bundle whose statement swaps the quantifier order is rejected at check 1 with both prefixes named.
- [ ] A vacuous outer set (the whole domain) leaves the exterior obligation open.
- [ ] A planted false certificate (accepted by the checker, contradicted at a probe point) raises exactly one
      false-certificate signal carrying the certificate hash and the contradicting point.
- [ ] A refused certificate, a narrower scope and a truthful `unknown` raise no false-certificate signal.
- [ ] One missed withdrawal fails an amendment mission; three unnecessary invalidations are reported and do not.
- [ ] An `unknown` with a valid witness is accepted on an unidentifiable mission and rejected on an identifiable one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- family_accept::`
- `obligations_ledger_complete`: 3 interior, 2 exterior, 1 boundary obligation; answer covering all → discharged;
  dropping one exclusion → that row open → `rejected`.
- `endpoint_exact`: ground truth "b ≤ 3/14 admitted"; answers `[.., 3/14]` accepted, `[.., 3/14)` rejected,
  `[.., 0.2143]` rejected (0.2143 > 3/14).
- `vacuity`: outer set = domain → open; band 0.3 with maximum 0.1 → rejected.
- `statement_mismatch`: ∀∃ submitted for an ∃∀ card → rejected at check 1.
- `false_certificate_signal`: a certificate with consistent hashes whose inner cell contains a probe point that
  violates the requirement → signal raised once, with proof kind and point.
- `refused_is_not_false`: checker refuses → rejected, no signal.
- `amendment_missed_withdrawal`, `amendment_unnecessary_counted`.
- `unknown_witness_checks`: witness world outside the family → rejected; inconsistent with one observation →
  rejected; best choices equal → rejected; valid on an unidentifiable card → accepted.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_family_accept.sh`: on development cards built from the stance, docking and quantifier-order
fixtures, submits one correct answer and six planted defective answers per card through `frob-tenfold submit` and
`frob-tenfold adjudicate`, replaying bundles with `frob-verify`. Asserts each decision and that exactly one
false-certificate signal exists. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_family_accept/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the checker build
and the direct-evaluation tool pins, per-check results, the first open obligation or contradicting point
(development probe points only) with the decision-record path, the false-certificate signal's certificate hash
and proof kind, and the summary.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the stance boundary 3/14 m; the docking tolerance ε < (U + ½)/4, with the strict endpoint (an
answer that admits ε = (U + ½)/4 is contradicted); the quantifier-order fixture, on which a per-build query and a
shared query return different answers. Negative controls (each must be caught): a planted false certificate; a
reordered quantifier with consistent hashes; a boundary opened or closed; a vacuous enclosure; a missed
withdrawal hidden among three inconsequential edits; an `unknown` offered where the evidence identifies the
choice. Determinism: decisions are exact (D0) and reproduce on a second machine.

## Considerations

- KILL-TENFOLD-CERT (standing from M3): one false certificate from an arm disqualifies that arm from the scoped
  claim; for arm A the proof kind is suspended as well. This bead only raises the signal with its evidence.
- Direct evaluation is itself fallible. A contradiction is confirmed by a second, independent evaluation (a
  different tool or exact arithmetic by hand-checkable steps) before the signal is raised; both are recorded.
- Probe points are the adjudicator's most sensitive sealed content: a leaked probe point lets an arm fit its
  boundary. They are placed per mission from the sealed key and never appear in a receipt.
- Rival arms may target the public bundle schema or submit answers in the card's plain obligation format; either
  way the same five checks apply, and "every certificate checks" covers only certificates actually submitted.
