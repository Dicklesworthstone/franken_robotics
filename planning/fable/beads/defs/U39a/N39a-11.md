## Background

Answers in the ten-fold test are accepted in a **neutral adjudicating simulator**: one that neither arm used for
training, tuning or screening on the stratum, whose contact model was derived independently of both arms, that
runs deterministically on a CPU, and that is pinned by revision. The reason for the rule: an arm scored in its
own engine is scored on its own modelling errors, and two programs that share a solver have correlated error. The
default candidate is a CPU multibody engine with its own convex contact solver at a pinned release. Whether such
a simulator exists for a given stratum is not known in advance. Legged briefs on rigid terrain are within reach
of a mature CPU multibody engine; briefs with liquid films, tracks with grousers, soft ground or moving surfaces
may not be, and an arm may already use the candidate as a tool. The plan therefore writes the outcome of that
uncertainty before it is measured.

**KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ.** *Trigger:* no neutral simulator passes GATE-TENFOLD-05 for a stratum. (GATE-TENFOLD-05 is
adjudicator acceptance: the simulator passes the known answers it can express inside its own stated tolerances;
of 20 planted answers per stratum it classifies 20 correctly; no arm's logs show it in training or tuning; the
discordance with each arm's home referee on a 5% sample is reported.) *Fallback, named in advance:* **cross-home
adjudication**, labelled: an answer must pass in the highest-trust tier of both arms (the product's CPU referee
and the rival's CPU reference), run by the adjudicator. If neither home referee can run the other arm's answers,
the stratum is `descriptive`: its numbers are reported and nothing is cited as passing or failing the ten-fold
bet. *Decided:* at M3 for the strata of the partial gate, and again at M7 for every stratum of the tournament.

This is a decision bead: it applies a rule that was fixed before any measurement, records the outcome either way
as an architecture decision record, and states what changes downstream.

## Scope

In: evaluating the trigger per stratum from the recorded values of GATE-TENFOLD-05; trying substitutes in a fixed
order; the fallback's conditions; the label; the decision record and its ADR at M3 (and their form for M7); the downstream
changes, including the effect on verdicts and on the word. Out: the gate's measurement (N39a-10); the cross-home
mechanism (N39a-08); choosing candidate engines (the simulator decision N39b-26, with the adjudicator named by
N39b-27). This is the only decision bead of KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ: it fixes the procedure for both readings and
records the M3 reading (strata of the partial gate), and it closes on the M3 records. The M7 reading (every
stratum of the tournament) applies the same procedure unchanged, before the tournament's suite hash is
committed, inside the tournament's adjudication epic (BD-432), which writes its ADR and register entries in the
form of step 3; the tournament (N39b-13) and the adjudication pilot (N38a-34) depend on this bead, so it cannot
wait for M7.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** For each stratum in the gate's scope: the list of candidate engines from the simulator decision
(N39b-26) in the adjudicator's order of preference (default candidate first; substitutes that meet the four
conditions next), and one GATE-TENFOLD-05 record per (stratum, candidate) that was tried. For each candidate the
record states the four conditions (not used by any arm on the stratum; independently derived contact model;
deterministic on a CPU; pinned by revision) and whether it can represent every mission of the stratum's
development pool through the neutral exchange description; a candidate that cannot represent the stratum is
recorded `fail: cannot-represent` and the next is tried.

**2. Decision procedure (per stratum; numbered).**
1. Take the candidates in order. The first whose GATE-TENFOLD-05 record is `pass` on clauses 1 and 2 (known
   answers with full coverage; 20 of 20 planted answers) and has zero arm-usage hits is the stratum's adjudicating
   simulator: `mode: neutral`. The kill gate **does not fire** for this stratum.
2. If a candidate fails only because an arm uses it as a tool on this stratum, it is skipped for the stratum
   (that is substitution, not failure of the engine).
3. If the list is exhausted, the kill gate **fires** for the stratum. Run the cross-home feasibility check, which
   has two parts and needs both. (a) *Can run.* Each arm's accepted development answers (at least 5 per arm) are
   exported through the neutral exchange description and run in the *other* arm's home referee: this system's
   referee imports and runs the rival's design and controller behind the standard observation and action
   interface, and the rival's CPU reference runs ours. This part holds iff both directions run every answer to a
   verdict (not iff the verdicts agree: the truth of these answers is unknown). A check in which only one
   direction was tested does not count. (b) *Classifies planted answers.* On the stratum's development missions
   each home referee is given the stratum's 20 planted answers (10 that must pass, 10 that must fail, whose
   truth is known by construction) and must classify all 20 correctly within its own tolerances; 19 of 20 fails.
4. Feasible → `mode: cross-home`. Acceptance for the stratum is the conjunction of the two home verdicts at the
   submission's level; every record, every number and every report line of the stratum prints
   `adjudication: cross-home`; the `adjudicate` phase of each ledger includes both runs.
5. Not feasible → `mode: descriptive`. The cell is opened as descriptive and says so before any sealed run; its
   cells cannot be confirmatory and cannot carry the word; it is enlarged or re-examined for the next gate.

A neutral simulator that passes is labelled `adjudication: neutral`.

**Effect on the word (DESIGN reading, fixed here so that it is fixed before sealing).** The verdict `ten-fold`
requires "the adjudication condition": a neutral simulator, or cross-home adjudication, operated by the
adjudicator. Under cross-home adjudication the sentence carries the label. A `descriptive` stratum cites nothing
for or against the headline bet.

**Bias note recorded with a cross-home outcome.** Passing in both homes is stricter for both arms than passing in
a third model, and the gap between an arm's home simulator and the other home is that arm's risk, equally. The
report prints, per arm, the share of submissions that passed at home and failed in the other home.

**Fail-closed posture.** Until the decision is recorded for a stratum, its sealed suite is not opened.

**3. The decision record (an ADR in the milestone in which it occurs).**
```
{ kill_gate: "KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ", instance: "M3-partial", stratum, fired: bool,
  candidates: [{sim: SimPin, gate05: "pass" | "fail: <clause>" | "skipped: used-by-arm <X>"}],
  cross_home: {feasible: bool, can_run: {a_in_b: bool, b_in_a: bool}, planted: {home_a: "20/20", home_b: "20/20"},
               evidence: [...]} | null,
  outcome: "neutral: <sim>" | "cross-home" | "descriptive",
  cells_changed: [{cell, from, to}],
  trigger_text, fallback_text, decided_at, decided_by (adjudicator role), hash }
```
The record states the measured values beside the trigger, whichever way it went: for each candidate the failed
condition or measurement, the fallback test results, the outcome and label, and the list of cells whose status
changed. It is written before the suite hash is committed, because the adjudication mode of each stratum is part
of the commitment.

The records of one gate instance are the sections, one per stratum, of one decision ADR in the one form of every
kill-gate decision (N40-03): `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ-M3.md` for the partial gate
(`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ-M7.md` for the tournament). The gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` (N40-28:
mode `exit`, `decided = "M3"`, `reread = ["M7"]`, `scope_kind = "cell"`) receives one decision entry per stratum,
`{exit = "M3", scope = "cell:<stratum>", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts}`: `not-fired` for `neutral: <sim>`;
`fired` with `fallback_form = "Cross-home adjudication, labelled"` for `cross-home`; `fired` with no
`fallback_form` (the form that withdraws the verdict) for `descriptive`. `receipts` are the GATE-TENFOLD-05
receipts under `receipts/gates/` that the outcome rests on (N39a-10), at least one in either case. The ADR carries
the required fields of that form: the kill gate and the milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendment;
each measured value with its receipt hash beside the trigger's number (planted answers classified against 20 of
20, arm-usage hits against 0); the outcome and the label; the fallback now in force; the beads re-read; what
changes downstream (step 4); and the recording session, which implemented none of the mechanisms. The
cross-check of N40-30 compares these entries with the row.

**4. What changes downstream if it fires.**
- The per-stratum adjudication record (N39a-08) carries the mode; the acceptance procedures (N39a-06) run two
  simulators and take the conjunction.
- The verdict rule's "adjudication condition" (N39a-32): a `cross-home` stratum may carry a verdict but its report
  line always shows the label; a `descriptive` stratum can carry no verdict.
- The pre-analysis (N39a-33) is recomputed for a cross-home stratum, because requiring acceptance in two
  simulators lowers both arms' completion rates and may move the cell below its reachability conditions.
- The rival's home referee becomes part of the acceptance path, so its revision is frozen with the suite, not only
  with the arm.
- A kill outcome is not re-tested on the same suite; the stratum may get a neutral simulator at the next gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every stratum in the gate's scope has a decision record with the candidates tried and their gate values.
- [ ] The procedure is a pure function of its inputs: the same records give the same outcome and the same hash.
- [ ] A planted stratum whose only candidate misclassifies 2 of 20 planted answers yields `fired: true` and, with
      a feasible cross-home check, `cross-home`; with an infeasible one, `descriptive`.
- [ ] A candidate used by an arm on the stratum is recorded as `skipped`, and the next candidate is tried; a
      planted candidate that an arm used during tuning is rejected at the first condition.
- [ ] For a `cross-home` outcome both home referees classified 20 of 20 planted answers and both directions ran
      every exported answer; the label appears on every number of the stratum in the report.
- [ ] For a `descriptive` outcome the stratum's cells are registered as `descriptive` and the pre-analysis names
      no confirmatory cell there.
- [ ] The records exist before sealing step 2 of the gate, and the suite commitment includes their hashes; no
      stratum's suite is opened without one of the three outcomes on record.
- [ ] The decision is recorded whether or not the gate fired, as `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ-M3.md` dated in
      M3 with one section per stratum of the partial gate, and the row holds one M3 entry per stratum with
      `scope = "cell:<stratum>"`, the outcome step 3 maps to and at least one receipt.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- kill_adj::`
- `first_passing_candidate_wins`: [fail, pass, pass] → the second candidate; `fired: false`.
- `skip_when_used_by_arm`: [used-by-arm B, pass] → second candidate; `fired: false`.
- `fires_to_cross_home`: [fail, fail], feasible → `cross-home`.
- `fires_to_descriptive`: [fail], infeasible (one direction cannot import the other's answers) → `descriptive`.
- `feasible_means_runs_not_agrees`: both directions run with opposite verdicts on 2 of 5 answers → still feasible.
- `nineteen_of_twenty_fails_acceptance`: a home referee that classifies 19 of 20 planted answers → not feasible;
  a neutral candidate with 19 of 20 → `fail`.
- `cross_home_needs_both_directions`: only one direction tested → not feasible.
- `candidate_used_by_arm_is_not_neutral`: the first condition rejects it before any measurement is read.
- `no_fallback_makes_descriptive`.
- `label_propagates_to_every_ratio`: every ratio object of a cross-home stratum carries the label.
- `record_before_seal`: a decision record dated after step 2 of the same gate is refused by the sealing ledger.
- `decision_entry_form`: `neutral: <sim>` → `not-fired` with one receipt; `cross-home` → `fired` with
  `fallback_form = "Cross-home adjudication, labelled"`; `descriptive` → `fired` with no `fallback_form`; each entry
  scoped `cell:<stratum>` with `adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ-M3.md"`, and a scratch copy of the register
  loads them (an entry naming another ADR path fails with `decision-adr-path`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_kill_adj.sh`: on a rehearsal gate with two synthetic strata, feeds one stratum a passing
candidate and the other a candidate planted to misclassify (the strata and plants are labelled `synthetic`),
runs the feasibility check with the product's CPU referee and the rival's real CPU reference launched out of
process from its recorded environment by the incumbent harness (BD-039, through N39a-08's adapter; the script
skips with code 77 where that environment is not installed, and never substitutes a stand-in), and writes both
decision records. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_kill_adj/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, per-candidate gate
values (known answers passed of expressible, planted answers classified against 20, arm-usage hits against 0),
the four conditions per candidate, the feasibility evidence (both directions, answers run to a verdict), the
outcome and the record hash, on a failed assertion the first differing record field with the record path; the
summary names KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ as decided for both strata.

At M7 the same procedure is exercised on development missions by the adjudication pilot (N38a-34, which depends
on this bead), with the neutral candidate disabled by a plant (the cross-home label is asserted) and with the
rival's reference also disabled (`descriptive` is asserted); that step is the pilot's, in its own script.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the three outcomes on the three constructed inputs above; 20 of 20 is required and 19 of 20 fails.
Negative controls: a decision procedure that falls back to the product's own referee alone when no neutral engine
passes (the test for the rejected alternative: the outcome must be `cross-home` or `descriptive`, never
`neutral: <product referee>`); a record written after sealing; a stratum silently dropped from the scope instead
of being recorded as `descriptive`; an arm that touched the candidate during tuning (registry entry TF-CX-15); a
cross-home run in which only one direction was tested (must not count as available).

## Considerations

- Fail-closed default while the owner's choice of simulator is open: a pinned incumbent CPU engine that no arm
  trains in; where it cannot represent the mission, cross-home adjudication, labelled.
- Cross-home adjudication is weaker than neutral adjudication in one specific way: an error shared by both home
  simulators (for example a common friction card) passes both. The label exists so that a reader weighs the
  stratum accordingly.
- Expect the trigger to be live for `tracked`, `hybrid` and any stratum with films at M7; expect it not to fire
  for `legged-quad` at M3 (HYPOTHESIS, settled by the gate's measurement).
- If every stratum of a gate ends `descriptive`, the gate records its ratios and no verdict; the milestone still
  exits on the record, and the adjudication question returns to the owner.
- Which simulator adjudicates each stratum, and whether it can represent terrain, films and tracks, is an open
  question owned by the adjudicator with the verification lead; this gate is its fallback.
- Under the fail-closed adjudicator (a project session), every ratio is also `self-adjudicated`; the two labels
  are independent and both print.
- Risk (pre-mortem): common-mode error, where every tier shares assumed card values and accepted decisions fail
  neutral adjudication; cross-home adjudication does not remove error shared by both homes.
