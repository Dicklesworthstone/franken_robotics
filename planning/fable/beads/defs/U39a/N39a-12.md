## Background

The ten-fold test does not produce one number. It produces one ratio per **stratum**: a set of mission families
that share a robot class or an answer kind, and a deadline. Each named stratum gets its own ratio, and a result on
one stratum earns that stratum alone. Reason: the evidence behind the thesis differs by mission class (the
expected range against per-design training is 3 to 10 for quadruped briefs, 5 to 20 for humanoid briefs, 1 to 3
per query for family queries, and is not estimated for facility missions), so a pooled number would let a strong
class carry a weak one.

The design strata are laid over a **coverage grid** (DESIGN): four robot classes on eight world types, the eight
being open terrain and the seven interior archetypes of the product's scene generator. The task families are the
product's task families, and the robots are drawn from its eighteen archetypes. At M7 every cell of the grid
holds at least two sealed mission families, or is declared `not applicable` with a written reason before the
suite is sealed. Rejected: a suite assembled from whatever missions the product does well (the grid forces the
unglamorous cells to be filled or explained); one global deadline (a deadline suited to humanoid training would
never censor a family query).

## Scope

In: the registry of the eleven strata as data; the grid as data with its 32 cells and their brief descriptions;
the admissible answer kinds, strongest control and first-run milestone per stratum; the checks on a suite against
the registry; the `not applicable` declarations. Out: sizes and independence of families (N39a-13); the
anti-shortcut controls counted per stratum (N39a-14); the deadline value per stratum (OPEN-TENFOLD-5, decided by
the adjudicator in N39b-30; this registry holds the field and the check); the rival's configuration per stratum
(N39a-20); the reason ids of the checks below (the registry N39-REF).

## Technical approach

**1. The coverage grid (`suite/grid.rs`; data file `tenfold/<gate>/grid.json`).** Rows are robot classes, columns
are world types; each cell names the brief it must contain.

| Robot class | Terrain | Home | Apartment | Office | Warehouse | Manufacturing plant | Retail store | Commercial kitchen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legged (quadruped, hexapod, biped, humanoid) | Stairs, gaps, stepping stones, rubble | Thresholds, rugs, a stair flight | A lift-lobby sill and tight turns | Castor chairs and glass doors | A ramp with payload | The split-grip landing; an oil patch | Aisles with movable carts | A wet galley crossing |
| Wheeled (differential, car-like, mecanum, omni, swerve, balancer, mobile manipulator base) | Slopes and curbs | Threshold delivery with a loaded cart | Timed docking under a hidden plant gain | Carpet rolling resistance; a door closer | Payload transport over a dock plate | Boarding and leaving a conveyor | Parallel shift and docking in an aisle | A greasy patch and a swing door |
| Tracked (tracks with flippers) | Stairs, rubble, soft ground | A stair flight with a carried load | Turning in place on a sill | Scrub energy on carpet | Ramp traction with payload | Floor grates and cable covers | Stopping distance in an aisle | A low-traction turn |
| Hybrid (wheel-leg, tri-wheel cluster, rocker-bogie) | Curbs and stairs on wheels | Stairs and thresholds, switching between rolling and stepping | A lift sill | Thresholds between carpet and tile | A ramp and a dock plate | Cable covers and grates | A curb at an entrance | Drains and mats |

Cell record: `{class, world_type, brief, families: [FamilyId], status: "filled" | "not-applicable", reason}`.

**2. The strata registry (`suite/strata.rs`; data file `tenfold/<gate>/strata.json`).**

| Stratum | Contents | Answer kind | Strongest control inside the rival arm | First run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `legged-quad` | Quadruped and hexapod design briefs with parametric and single-limb edits, across the first row of the grid | Qualified design | Per-design reinforcement learning with an outer-loop optimizer; a shared design-conditioned policy where one is published | M3 |
| `legged-biped` | Biped and humanoid briefs | Qualified design | The same, with the incumbent's humanoid training recipe | M7 |
| `wheeled` | The second row of the grid | Qualified design | The same, plus a model-based planner where the task has one | M3 |
| `tracked` | The third row | Qualified design | The same | M7 |
| `hybrid` | The fourth row | Qualified design | The same | M7 |
| `envelope` | Body-envelope family queries with amendments: adjustable stance and static load; timed docking; the quantifier-order fixture | Family answer; amendment verdict | A lifted robust linear program, a conventional projection, an SMT or interval tool, written by an expert | M3 |
| `sensing` | Sensing and material missions: the split-grip landing with a last usable observation; observation aliases; an unfamiliar contact law with a probe to choose | Qualified design or truthful unknown | Belief-state reachability, equal-budget reinforcement learning, an expert probe, direct measurement, active system identification | M3 |
| `rebody` | Change an accepted body under a frozen task interface (a gearbox, a wheel, a lower limb), then perturb latency, friction or energy reserve | Amendment verdict | Retraining, a shared conditioned actor, warm-started control, ordinary graph caching | M7 |
| `failure` | Find a planted failure inside the legality bounds and deliver an accepted repair | Counterexample and repair | Uniform randomization, a scenario-language generator with the same monitors, logs | M7 |
| `throughput` | A heterogeneous family at scale: fixed and changed topology, grazing contacts, dense depth sensors, memory pressure. The complete loop is timed, including certificates, divergence handling, cold compilation and the learner | Qualified design over a family | An optimized batched incumbent; and FrankenRobotics with family sharing switched off | M7 |
| `facility` (Moonshot) | Recommission a facility: move a door or a counter, perturb scan uncertainty, reduce a shared resource, alter a shift | Amendment verdict | Recomputation, an incremental mixed-integer program, standard motion planning, selective replay with cached bounds | M7, only if the facility co-design gate GATE-AUGUR-24 has passed; otherwise `not run` |

Stratum record: `{id, contents, answer_kinds: [..], strongest_control, first_run, deadline_s, grid_rows: [..] |
null, condition: null | "GATE-AUGUR-24"}`. The "strongest control" column is binding on the rival arm: the
advocates must include that control in arm B for the stratum or state in writing why it does not apply.

**3. Checks (`frob-tenfold suite check --grid --strata`), each with a harness refusal reason.**
1. The registry holds exactly the eleven names; an unknown stratum on a card is refused (`stratum-unknown`).
2. Every card's answer kind is admissible in its stratum (`card-kind-not-in-stratum`). The one exception is the
   companion kind of the unresolved-answer control: a truthful-unknown control card in a design stratum, admitted
   only under the committed extension and sealed flag that the anti-shortcut controls bead (N39a-14) defines and
   adds to this check.
3. For a tournament gate: every grid cell is `filled` with at least two sealed families, or `not-applicable` with
   a non-empty reason recorded **before** sealing step 2 (`grid-cell-empty`). The two-family rule is stated for
   M7 only. For the partial gate this check does not refuse: it prints, for each cell of the rows in scope, the
   families it holds or the adjudicator's reason it holds none (DESIGN: a report, so that thin coverage at M3 is
   visible and is not mistaken for a rule the plan did not set).
4. A stratum whose `first_run` is later than the gate is excluded and reported `not run: scheduled for M7`.
5. `facility` is admitted only if a pass of GATE-AUGUR-24 is on record; otherwise it is `not run` with that
   reason, and no card of it may be sealed.
6. Every stratum in scope has `deadline_s` set, equal on every card of the stratum (`stratum-deadline-missing`).
7. A stratum whose product mechanism has not landed (for example one that needs a mechanism of a later
   milestone) is declared `not run` in the report with the reason; it is never silently absent.

**4. What the partial gate and the tournament take from the registry.** Partial gate (M3): `legged-quad` and
`wheeled` cold as confirmatory cells; `envelope` warm and `sensing` cold as confirmatory if their pre-analyses
allow it, descriptive otherwise. Tournament (M7): every stratum that has a product to run, on families no earlier
gate used.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `strata.json` and `grid.json` are generated from the tables above and round-trip byte-identically; the
      registry has 11 strata and the grid 32 cells.
- [ ] Each of the seven checks has a passing fixture and a refused or reported fixture.
- [ ] A tournament suite with one empty, undeclared cell is refused; the same cell declared `not-applicable` with
      a reason dated before step 2 is accepted; declared after step 2 is refused.
- [ ] `facility` without a GATE-AUGUR-24 pass is `not run` and none of its cards can be sealed.
- [ ] The suite commitment (sealing step 2) includes the hashes of both files.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- strata::`
- `eleven_strata_exact`: names and first-run milestones as tabulated.
- `grid_has_32_cells`: 4 × 8; each cell's brief text non-empty.
- `kind_admissibility`: `envelope` admits family-answer and amendment-verdict, refuses qualified-design;
  `sensing` admits qualified-design and truthful-unknown; `failure` admits only counterexample-and-repair.
- `two_families_per_cell_at_m7`: 31 cells with two families and one with one → `grid-cell-empty` naming the cell.
- `not_applicable_needs_reason_before_seal`.
- `facility_conditional`: with and without the gate record.
- `stratum_rows`: `legged-quad` and `legged-biped` both draw from row 1; `wheeled`, `tracked`, `hybrid` from rows
  2, 3, 4; the other strata have no grid row.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_suite_grid.sh`: builds a rehearsal suite of development families covering the two M3 rows,
runs the checks, then plants an empty cell, a card of the wrong kind and a `facility` card, and expects the three
refusals. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_suite_grid/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the cell and stratum
counts against 32 and 11, each refusal reason (`tenfold.grid-cell-empty`, `tenfold.card-kind-not-in-stratum`,
the `facility` `not run` record) with the cell or card at fault, the hashes of the two data files, on a failed
assertion the first differing cell or stratum field with the data-file path, and the summary.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 11 strata; 32 cells; at M7 at least 64 sealed families in the grid if no cell is `not applicable`.
Negative controls: a suite that fills the grid only along the legged row and declares nothing (refused); a
`not-applicable` declaration backdated after sealing (the sealing ledger's times refute it); a stratum added
after the hash commitment (the commitment no longer verifies).

## Considerations

- Manipulation with hands, flight and soft bodies are outside the suite; the grid has no cell for them and the
  report says so.
- Tracked and hybrid briefs wait for the product's M4 mechanisms and are first measured at M7.
- The cell briefs are the adjudicator's starting points, not mission texts; the sealed missions are written from
  the public class descriptions and may vary the brief within the cell.
- The deadline of each stratum is an open decision of the adjudicator, taken when the suite is frozen; the default
  is three times the frozen rival's median development time, rounded up to half an hour, and raised until the
  horizon cap (deadline ÷ arm A's restricted mean time on the development suite) is at least 10.
