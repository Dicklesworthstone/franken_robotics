## Background

Every statistic of the ten-fold test treats something as an independent observation. If that something is the
mission, an adjudicator can write twenty-four missions on one warehouse site, the arms' times on them will rise
and fall together, and an interval computed as if there were twenty-four observations will be far too narrow.

DESIGN: the independent unit is the **mission family**. Missions that share a generator draw, a site, a material
batch or a parent design belong to one family and stay together in every split. Twenty-four missions on one site
are one replicate. The family is the unit that is resampled, counted by the exact guard, and listed in the
verdict.

Sizes (DESIGN): each stratum has 12 public development missions and an initial 24 sealed mission families. The
final sealed count is fixed by the pre-analysis before the suite is opened, and is never below 12. A stratum that
declares a population result needs at least 32 independent families at α = 0.05, and 46 where four strata share
the level; a stratum with fewer declares a finite-suite result (a statement about the sealed suite itself).
Arithmetic behind the two numbers: a procedure with one-sided error α that must be able to certify on the best
possible sample needs (10/11)ⁿ ≤ α, so n ≥ ln(1/α) ÷ ln(1.1): 32 at α = 0.05 and 46 at α = 0.0125.

A sealed family is also perishable. **INV-TENFOLD-06**: a sealed family serves one gate and is then retired.
When the gate closes, its missions, worlds and receipts are revealed and the family goes into the development
pool; it never serves as a sealed family again. The tournament at M7 uses no family that the partial gate at M3
used. A family touched by a leak is retired at once. Reason: a family that has been seen, by an arm or by the
public, can be tuned against.

Rejected: missions as units (pseudo-replication); splitting a site's missions between development and sealed
sets (the development half teaches the sealed half); reusing M3 families at M7 "because nobody looked".

## Scope

In: the family registry; the grouping rule and its connected-components computation; size rules and their checks;
the development and sealed split; rotation, reveal and retirement; the cross-gate reuse check. Out: the strata
and grid (N39a-12); control quotas counted over families (N39a-14); the sealing steps (N39a-15); the choice
between result types and the bounded paired contrast that a population claim rests on (see `needs`).

## Technical approach

**1. Family registry (`tenfold/<gate>/families.json`).**
```
{ "gate": "…", "families": [ { "id": "f:…", "stratum": "legged-quad", "cell": ["legged", "warehouse"],
    "pool": "development" | "sealed", "missions": ["b3:…"],
    "keys": { "generator_draw": "…", "site": "…", "material_batch": "…", "parent_design": "…" },
    "served_gate": null | "M3-partial", "status": "active" | "retired: <reason>", "result_type_cell": … } ],
  "hash": "b3:…" }
```
`id` is the BLAKE3 hash of the sorted key tuple, so two missions with equal keys cannot be registered as two
families.

**2. Grouping rule (connected components).** Build a graph whose vertices are missions; join two missions when
they share *any* of the four keys: the same generator draw (seed and parameters of the world generator), the same
originating site, the same material batch, or the same parent design (one is derived from the other, or both from
one ancestor by edits). A family is a connected component. Consequence: sharing is transitive (mission 1 shares a
site with 2, 2 shares a material batch with 3 → one family of three). `frob-tenfold suite check --families`
recomputes the components from the cards and refuses a registry that splits a component (`family-split`).

**3. Split rule.** A component is wholly in the development pool or wholly in the sealed pool. The check runs over
the union of both pools, including every earlier gate's revealed families: a sealed candidate that is connected
to any development or previously sealed mission is refused (`family-connected-to-known`). The public development
suite holds 12 missions per stratum, in at least 6 families (DESIGN: enough families that tuning on it is not
tuning on one site).

**4. Size rules (checked at sealing step 2 and again when the pre-analysis is recorded).**

| Rule | Check |
|---|---|
| Initial sealed count | 24 families per stratum unless the pre-analysis fixes another number |
| Floor | never fewer than 12 sealed families in a stratum that is run |
| Campaigns | c = 1 campaign per family by default; c = 2 where fewer than 24 families exist (the family remains the unit) |
| Reporting floor | no verdict of any kind on fewer than ten paired campaigns per arm |
| Confirmatory floor | a cell of four families is refused as confirmatory (its guard cannot be passed) |
| Population result | at least 32 independent families at α = 0.05; at least 46 where four confirmatory cells share the level; and never fewer than the count its registered bound requires |
| Finite-suite result | any cell below those counts declares a finite-suite result before sealing |

Each stratum's cell record carries `result_type: "finite-suite" | "population"`, declared before sealing; the
check refuses `population` below the counts (`suite-too-small-for-population`).

**5. Rotation (INV-TENFOLD-06).** State machine per family: `active(development)`; `active(sealed)` →
`served(<gate>)` when the gate opens → `revealed` when the gate closes (missions, worlds, keys and receipts
published) → `active(development)` in the enlarged development pool of the next gate. Transitions are ledger
entries. Forbidden transitions, each refused: `revealed → sealed`; `served(g1) → served(g2)`;
`development → sealed`. A leak signal moves the affected families straight to `retired: leak` and then to the
development pool.

**6. Cross-gate reuse check.** When a tournament suite is committed, every family id is compared against every
family that any earlier gate served, and the connected-components check of step 3 is run against all of them. One
hit refuses the commitment (`family-reused`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The components computed from the cards equal the registry's families; a registry that splits a component is
      refused, naming the two missions and the shared key.
- [ ] A sealed candidate sharing a site with a development mission is refused.
- [ ] A stratum sealed with 11 families is refused; with 12 it is accepted with `finite-suite`; `population` is
      refused at 31 families and accepted at 32; with four confirmatory cells sharing the level it needs 46.
- [ ] INV-TENFOLD-06 holds as a property over random transition sequences; each forbidden transition is refused.
- [ ] A tournament suite containing one family served at M3 is refused.
- [ ] Twenty-four missions with one site key register as one family.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- family::`
- `components_transitive`: missions {1: site a}, {2: site a, batch x}, {3: batch x}, {4: site b} → families
  {1, 2, 3} and {4}.
- `one_site_one_replicate`: 24 missions, one site → 1 family.
- `split_refused`: component {1, 2} with 1 in development and 2 sealed → refused.
- `size_table`: 11 → refused; 12 → finite-suite; 24 → finite-suite; 31 population → refused; 32 → accepted;
  45 with four shared cells → refused; 46 → accepted.
- `population_counts_formula`: ⌈ln(1/α) ÷ ln(1.1)⌉ = 32, 46 and 49 at α = 0.05, 0.0125 and 0.01.
- `inv_tenfold_06_property`: 10⁴ random transition sequences; no family is ever `served` by two gates; no
  revealed family returns to `sealed`.
- `cross_gate_reuse`: tournament registry ∩ M3-served ≠ ∅ → `family-reused`.
- `family_id_from_keys`: equal keys → equal id; a duplicate registration is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_families.sh`: generates a rehearsal pool of 60 development-grade missions with planted
shared keys, registers families, runs the checks, serves a rehearsal gate, closes it, and attempts to seal one of
its families again and to seal a mission connected to a revealed one. Logs each command, exit code, wall time,
the component count beside the expected count, each refusal with the missions and key at fault, each state
transition with its ledger sequence, and the summary.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the counts 32, 46 and 49; the floor 12; 24 missions on one site are one replicate. Negative
controls: a registry built per mission (the components test catches it); a pool split by mission instead of by
family; a reused family; a family revealed and resealed. Metamorphic: relabelling missions does not change the
partition into families.

## Considerations

- Independence is declared through four keys. Two families can still be dependent through something the keys do
  not capture (one author's habits, one robot grammar). The keys are the floor, and the adjudicator may add a key;
  adding one after sealing is refused.
- Retiring families makes sealed missions a consumable; an M7 tournament of 11 strata at 24 families needs 264
  fresh families that are connected to nothing revealed at M3.
- The development pool grows with every gate, which helps tuning of every arm equally.
- If the pre-analysis asks for more families than exist, the cell is opened as descriptive (the reachability kill
  rule), not padded with connected missions.
