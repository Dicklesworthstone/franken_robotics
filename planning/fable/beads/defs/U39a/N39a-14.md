## Background

A sealed suite can be honest about its contents and still flatter one arm. If every mission needs exactly the
mechanisms FrankenRobotics has, the comparison shows that the product does what it was built for. If no mission
has "the evidence does not identify the choice" as its right answer, an arm that always answers confidently is
never punished. If the checker's mutations were written by the builders, the checker is tested on the mistakes
its authors imagined.

DESIGN: six **anti-shortcut controls** are locked into every stratum. They are the sealed-suite form of the
control named in the program's ten-fold bet: routine easy wins for conventional methods locked into the suite,
and consequential edits hidden among inconsequential ones.

1. *Easy wins for conventional methods.* At least one family in six is a mission on which a conventional method
   is expected to win or tie. If the frozen rival wins none of them on the development suite, the rival is
   presumed under-built and the freeze is refused.
2. *Genuinely unresolved answers.* At least one family in twelve has `unknown` with a witness as its correct
   answer.
3. *Independent mutations.* Unit, contact-order, causal-clock and passive-load mutations are derived by the
   adjudicator, not by an arm.
4. *Hidden consequence.* In an amendment sequence the card does not say which edit matters. Missed withdrawals
   and unnecessary invalidations are both measured.
5. *The quantifier-order fixture* is in the `envelope` stratum permanently: every design has a command that works
   and no single command works for all, so a per-build query and a shared query must return different answers.
6. *Tier traps.* At least one family per design stratum has a candidate that passes on a fast tier and fails in
   the adjudicating simulator.

In addition, some worlds are built with an evaluator independent of every proposal engine, because a sampling run
and a solver that share one mistranslated world model agree on the wrong answer.

## Scope

In: the control flags on the sealed part of a card; the quotas and their checks over a stratum's families; how
each control is constructed and what is recorded about it; the easy-win check on the frozen rival; the counters
for missed withdrawals and unnecessary invalidations. Out: the card record (N39a-05); the acceptance procedures
that the controls exercise (N39a-06, N39a-07); the hygiene gate GATE-TENFOLD-12 as measured at M7 (the script
N39b-42, register row N40-21), which reads this bead's counters; the advocate's tuning (N39a-20); the reason ids
(N39-REF).

## Technical approach

**1. Flags (sealed part of each card).** `controls: { easy_win: bool, unknown_truth: bool, mutations:
[unit | contact-order | causal-clock | passive-load], hidden_consequence: {edit_seq} | null, quantifier_order:
bool, tier_trap: {candidate: b3:…} | null }` and `independent_evaluator: bool`. Flags are sealed: an arm that knew
which family is the easy win or the trap could special-case it. They are revealed at the gate's close.

**2. Quotas per stratum (checked by `frob-tenfold suite check --controls` at sealing step 2; m = sealed families).**

| Control | Quota | Applies to | Refusal reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy win | at least ⌈m/6⌉ families | every stratum | `controls-easy-win-quota` |
| Unresolved answer | at least ⌈m/12⌉ families whose correct answer is `unknown` with a witness | every stratum (the card's kind is then truthful-unknown; strata whose registered kinds exclude it carry the quota as cards of a declared companion kind, see step 3) | `controls-unknown-quota` |
| Independent mutations | all four mutation kinds present in the stratum; each mutated mission derived by the adjudicator | every stratum with certificate or requirement checking | `controls-mutation-missing` |
| Hidden consequence | every amendment sequence has exactly one consequential edit unknown to the arm, among at least two inconsequential ones | every warm card | `controls-consequence-exposed` |
| Quantifier order | present | `envelope` only, at every gate | `controls-quantifier-fixture-missing` |
| Tier trap | at least one family | design strata (`legged-quad`, `legged-biped`, `wheeled`, `tracked`, `hybrid`, `throughput`) | `controls-tier-trap-missing` |

With m = 24: at least 4 easy-win families and 2 unresolved families per stratum; with m = 12: 2 and 1.

**3. Constructing each control (the adjudicator's procedure, recorded per card).**
1. *Easy win.* A mission chosen so that a named conventional method has a short path: a flat-ground gait an
   incumbent's stock training recipe solves in its published time; a family query that is a small linear program.
   The sealed record names the method and the adjudicator's expected outcome (`rival wins` or `tie`).
2. *Unresolved answer.* The world family contains two admissible worlds consistent with all supplied evidence
   under which the best choices differ; the adjudicator holds the pair as ground truth. For a design stratum the
   quota is met by a card of kind truthful-unknown placed in that stratum's family list (the stratum's registry
   entry is extended with the companion kind for control cards only, and the extension is part of the commitment).
   This bead adds the matching exception to check 2 of the strata registry (N39a-12): a card of the companion kind
   in a design stratum is admitted only when its sealed `unknown_truth` flag is set and the stratum's extension is
   in the committed `strata.json`; any other card of that kind in the stratum is still `card-kind-not-in-stratum`.
3. *Mutations.* From a correct mission and its correct answer the adjudicator derives a mutated mission whose
   correct answer differs: a unit changed (millimetres for metres in one bound), the contact order changed (which
   foot or wheel lands first), a causal clock changed (an observation arrives after the decision that used it), a
   passive load added (a spring or payload the nominal analysis ignores). An answer that is correct for the
   unmutated mission must be rejected on the mutated one. The derivation script is adjudicator-authored.
4. *Hidden consequence.* The amendment list on the public card is unordered with respect to importance; the
   sealed record names the consequential edit and the verdicts it withdraws.
5. *Quantifier-order fixture.* One plant on which "for every design there exists a command that works" is true
   and "there exists a command that works for every design" is false; the mission asks one of the two and the
   sealed record holds both answers.
6. *Tier trap.* A candidate design, placed among the mission's permitted inputs or reachable in its family, that
   passes the requirement on a fast simulation tier and fails in the adjudicating simulator (for example a gait
   that exploits a penetration the fast contact model allows). The sealed record holds the candidate and both
   verdicts.
7. *Independent-evaluator worlds.* For flagged cards the world's ground truth is computed by an evaluator that
   shares no model translation with any arm's proposal engine (for example exact rational evaluation of the
   mission statement written by the adjudicator), and the record names that evaluator.

**4. The easy-win check on the frozen rival (part of the freeze-and-seal gate).** The development suite carries
the same control classes with public flags. Before an arm B freeze is accepted, the adjudicator reads the rival's
development scores: on the stratum's development easy-win families the rival must win at least one, a win being
a restricted time to acceptance strictly below arm A's logged time on the same development family at the same
deadline. Zero wins → the rival is presumed under-built and the freeze is refused (`freeze-rival-underbuilt`).
A tie does not count as a win here: the control expects a conventional method to win *or* tie on such a mission,
and the freeze rule asks that it actually win one.

**5. Counters produced at scoring time.** Per arm and stratum: `missed_withdrawals` (a kept verdict that ground
truth withdraws; one fails the mission), `unnecessary_invalidations` (reported), `mutations_rejected` of
`mutations_planted`, `traps_caught`, `unknowns_correct`, `easy_wins_by_arm`. These go to the report and to the
hygiene gate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each quota check has a passing and a refused fixture; with m = 24 the minimums are 4 and 2, with m = 12 they
      are 2 and 1.
- [ ] Control flags never appear in a public card part; a field allowlist test enforces it.
- [ ] A frozen rival with zero easy wins on the development suite cannot be frozen; with one it can.
- [ ] On the development suite every planted mutation of the four kinds is rejected by the acceptance procedures
      when answered with the unmutated mission's answer (TARGET: 100%).
- [ ] The quantifier-order fixture returns different answers to the per-build and the shared query.
- [ ] A tier-trap candidate submitted as an answer is rejected by the battery in the adjudicating simulator.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- controls::`
- `quota_arithmetic`: ⌈24/6⌉ = 4, ⌈24/12⌉ = 2, ⌈12/6⌉ = 2, ⌈12/12⌉ = 1, ⌈32/6⌉ = 6, ⌈32/12⌉ = 3.
- `quota_refusals`: a stratum of 24 with 3 easy wins → refused; with 4 → accepted; with 1 unknown → refused.
- `four_mutation_kinds_required`: three kinds present → `controls-mutation-missing` naming the absent kind.
- `consequence_hidden`: a public card whose amendment list is annotated with importance → refused.
- `quantifier_fixture_only_in_envelope`: absent from `envelope` → refused; absent elsewhere → no complaint.
- `trap_required_in_design_strata`.
- `companion_kind_only_for_flagged_controls`: a truthful-unknown card in `legged-quad` with `unknown_truth` set and
  the extension committed → admitted; the same card without the flag, or with the extension absent from the
  committed registry → `card-kind-not-in-stratum`.
- `underbuilt_rival`: development scores with 0 of 2 easy wins → `freeze-rival-underbuilt`; 1 of 2 → accepted.
- `counters`: a scored campaign with one missed withdrawal and three unnecessary invalidations → the mission
  fails and the counters read 1 and 3.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_controls.sh`: builds a development stratum with one card per control class (public flags),
runs `suite check --controls`, answers each mutated mission with the unmutated answer through the submission
protocol, submits the tier-trap candidate, runs both queries of the quantifier fixture, and checks the easy-win
rule on two planted rival score sheets. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_controls/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, quota values beside
their minimums, each rejected mutation with its kind and its receipt hash, the two fixture answers, the counters,
each refusal with its reason (`tenfold.controls-…`, `tenfold.freeze-rival-underbuilt`), on a failed assertion
the first differing counter or decision with the card path, and the summary naming GATE-TENFOLD-04 and
GATE-TENFOLD-12 as touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the quota minimums above; on the quantifier fixture the per-build answer is "feasible for every
design" and the shared answer is "infeasible". Negative controls: a suite with no easy win (refused); a rival
that wins no easy win (freeze refused); a mutation suite written by an arm (the authorship check of the roles
bead refuses it); an acceptance build that scores tier traps on the fast tier (the trap candidate is accepted;
the test fails it); a public card that marks the consequential edit.

## Considerations

- The easy-win rule is the suite's protection against a strawman rival and the suite's concession to the rival:
  it guarantees missions on which the product is not expected to shine, and their times are in the pooled ratio.
- Unresolved-answer missions punish over-confidence in both arms; on them a confident wrong answer is rejected
  and the clock runs to the deadline.
- Controls are sealed, so their presence cannot be audited from outside until the gate closes; the commitment
  hash covers them, and the reveal shows them.
- The companion-kind extension for control cards in design strata is this bead's reading of "at least one family
  in twelve has `unknown` … as its correct answer" applied to strata whose registered kind is a qualified design;
  if the adjudicator prefers, the same quota can be met by qualified-design missions whose correct outcome is
  "no admissible design is identified", which the card schema also expresses.
