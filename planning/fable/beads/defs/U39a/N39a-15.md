## Background

Two contaminations can ruin a sealed comparison, and they run in opposite directions. A mission can be written
*against a frozen arm*: an adjudicator who has seen the rival's final configuration can, even unconsciously,
write missions it handles badly. And an arm can be tuned *against a mission*: an advocate or builder who has seen
a sealed mission can fit to it. The ten-fold protocol removes both with an order of operations and makes the
order checkable.

**Sealing (DESIGN).** The order is fixed, and each step is a ledger entry with a hash and a time
(**INV-TENFOLD-02**: the six sealing steps appear in the ledger in order, each with a hash and a time):

1. class descriptions and the development suite are published;
2. the adjudicator commits the hash of the sealed suite and of the referee protocol;
3. arms are tuned on the development suite only;
4. each arm's freeze manifest is published;
5. the pre-analysis is recorded;
6. the suite is opened to the harness, and a mission card is shown to an arm only when that arm's clock starts.

Step 2 precedes step 4 so that no mission can be written against a frozen arm. Step 4 precedes step 6 so that no
arm can be tuned against a mission. The thresholds of the outcome rules (3 and 10, the guard floor 5, the tie
factor 1.5, the non-inferiority margin 0.05) are fixed when the first suite hash is committed, and none is changed
after any sealed run.

Rejected: freezing arms "about the same time" as sealing (leaves both directions open); trusting timestamps
alone (a hash chain makes the order a property of the data); one combined step for hashes and freezes.

## Scope

evidence ledger; what each step commits to; the order and precondition checks, with the registry through which
other beads add step preconditions; the `open` operation and its
refusals; verification by a third party; the continuation of the same chain after opening as the gate's event

thresholds (OPEN-TENFOLD-8, N39b-33, default as written); the reason ids (N39-REF).

## Technical approach

**1. The ledger (`tenfold/<gate>/seal.ndjson`).** One JSON line per entry:
`{seq, step, kind, payload: {…}, payload_hash, prev, entry_hash, utc, actor, role}` with
`entry_hash = BLAKE3(canonical(entry with entry_hash blanked))` and `prev` the previous `entry_hash` (the first
entry's `prev` is the gate identifier's hash). Each entry is also appended to the project's evidence ledger (the
crash-consistent store every gate record uses), which gives it a ledger sequence number and an independent time.
A step may consist of several entries (one per arm for step 4); a step is *complete* when its closing entry
(`kind: "step-complete"`) is present.

**The same chain is the gate's event log.** Other beads of the harness speak of "the gate ledger" and of "the
gate's event log (hash-chained entries with actor, role, time and payload hash)"; both mean this file. After the
`open` entry the chain continues, in the same entry format, with `step: 7` (the run) and `step: 8` (close and
reveal). `seal::append` accepts a `kind` only if it is in a small registry kept with the ledger; each kind is
registered by the bead that writes it, with its payload schema and the roles allowed to write it. Kinds known
when this bead was specified: `run-plan` (before `open`), `card-shown`, `campaign-start`, `campaign-close`,
`submission-commit`, `exclusion`, `supersession`, `kill-decision`, `prerun-notice`, `audit-record`,
`leak-signal`, `disqualification`, `void-cells`, `scorer-invocation`, `reveal`, `family-retired`. This bead supplies
the append operation, the kind registry, the role check on every entry and the verification of the whole chain;
the six-step order rules below apply to steps 1 to 6, and for steps 7 and 8 the only order rules are that
`open` precedes every step-7 entry and that `reveal` follows the `campaign-close` of every scheduled campaign.

**2. What each step commits (payloads).**

| Step | Entries | Payload |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `publish-classes`, `publish-development` | hashes of the public class descriptions (strata and grid), the mission grammar and schemas, the development suite (cards with public control flags, world generators), the role registry |
| 2 | `commit-suite`, `commit-protocol` | `suite_commitment` (below); the referee protocol's hash; the acceptance tree's hash; the final hash of the comment channel; the per-stratum adjudication records; the ratified outcome thresholds; the price table |
| 3 | `tuning-window` (open and close) | per arm: the tuning log's hash, device-hours spent, a statement that only development missions were read |
| 4 | `freeze` (one per arm and stratum) | the freeze manifest's hash and its publication location; the advocate's signed statement hash |
| 5 | `preanalysis` | the pre-analysis record's hash; the list of confirmatory cells (at most four); the result type of each cell |
| 6 | `open` | the adjudicator's opening statement; hashes of every earlier step-complete entry; the campaign schedule's hash |

`suite_commitment = BLAKE3(canonical({strata, grid, families, card_hashes (sorted), sealed_commitments (sorted),
deadlines, caps, thresholds, result_types, salt}))`. The salt (32 random bytes) is kept by the adjudicator and
revealed at close, so the commitment hides the suite and binds it.

**3. Order checks (`seal::append`), each a refusal with a harness reason.**
1. A step-n entry is refused unless step n − 1 is complete (`seal-out-of-order`). Step 3 may overlap nothing: its
   opening entry requires step 2 complete, and step 4 entries require the arm's tuning window closed. The
   converse holds too: once any entry of a later step exists, an entry of an earlier step (n ≤ 6) is refused. A
   step-4 `freeze` entry after `open` is refused with `freeze-after-open` (an arm frozen after the suite opened
   could have been tuned against a mission; the run is refused and the arm runs at the next gate); any other late
   entry with `seal-out-of-order`.
2. Times: each entry's evidence-ledger sequence number must exceed the previous entry's; a wall-clock time earlier
   than the previous entry's is refused (`seal-time-regression`).
3. Step 4 for an arm is refused if its manifest's `frozen_at` is earlier than the step-2 completion
   (`freeze-before-suite-commit`: such an arm was frozen before the missions were fixed, so the missions could
   have been written against it) or if any content hash in the manifest was first seen after its `frozen_at`.
4. Step 5 is refused until every arm of every stratum in scope has a step-4 entry (`freeze-missing`).
5. Step 6 (`open`) is refused unless steps 1 to 5 are complete, the role registry is unchanged since step 1, the
   suite files on disk still open the step-2 commitment, and the pre-opening conjunction of the gates that must
   precede a sealed run holds for this suite hash (`open-preconditions`).
6. After step 2, any change to strata, families, cards, thresholds, deadlines or the protocol is detected by
   recomputing the commitment at `open` and at every campaign start (`suite-changed-after-commit`).
7. Registered step preconditions. Beside the kind registry the ledger keeps a precondition registry: a bead that
   owns a condition a step must meet registers, for steps 1 to 6, a function that returns `ok` (optionally with a
   digest to record in the entry's payload) or a refusal with its reason and exit code. `seal::append` runs every
   precondition registered for the step, in registration order, before the order checks above; the first refusal
   refuses the entry with that reason and nothing is appended. Registered by later beads (each depends on this
   one): the non-inferiority decision default at step 2 (N39a-31); the owner-inputs check before every step and
   the adjudication-record check at step 2 (N39a-40, calling N41-15 and N41-06). This bead defines no
   precondition of its own beyond checks 1 to 6.

**4. Showing cards (step 6 continued).** Opening the suite gives the *harness* access. An arm sees a card only
through `campaign start`, at the instant its clock starts (N39a-05). The ledger records one `card-shown` entry
per campaign with the card hash, the arm and the stamp; a `card-shown` for an arm whose freeze entry is absent or
later is impossible by check 4 and is asserted again here (INV-TENFOLD-03 is enforced with the freeze manifest).

**5. Verification by anyone.** `frob-tenfold seal verify` (and the scorer in the replication kit) replays the
chain: recomputes every `entry_hash`, checks `prev` links, step order, times and, after the reveal, that the
published suite opens `suite_commitment`. Output: the six steps with their hashes and times, or the first
violation.

**5a. Commands (DESIGN; this bead's subcommands of the operator binary of N39a-01).** `frob-tenfold seal step <n>
--kind <kind> --payload <file> [--arm <a>] [--json]` appends one entry through `seal::append` under the caller's
identity in the role registry (N39a-02); `frob-tenfold seal step <n> --complete` appends the step's
`step-complete` entry; the `open` operation is `frob-tenfold seal step 6 --kind open`, which runs check 5 first;
`frob-tenfold seal verify [--json]` replays the chain. Each exits 0 on success and otherwise with the refusal's
exit code from N39-REF (3 for the order, time and precondition refusals; 9 for `suite-changed-after-commit`). An
entry whose kind is not in the kind registry is refused with `seal-kind-unregistered`, and one written by a role
the kind does not allow with `role-not-permitted` (both exit 3).

**6. Voiding.** A defect found in a frozen arm after step 6 voids the affected cells, which are re-run at the next
gate; it is never patched mid-gate. The ledger records `void-cells` entries; it never rewrites history.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Of the 720 orderings of the six step-complete entries, exactly one is accepted by `seal::append`.
- [ ] After `open` the chain accepts entries of the registered kinds for steps 7 and 8, refuses an unregistered
      kind and an entry from a role not allowed to write it, and `seal verify` covers the whole chain; a step-7
      entry before `open`, or a `reveal` before every scheduled campaign has closed, is refused.
- [ ] A manifest frozen before the suite commitment, a freeze entry after `open`, and a missing arm are each
      refused with the reason named above.
- [ ] Changing one byte of any committed suite file after step 2 makes `open` and `campaign start` refuse.
- [ ] A step precondition registered by another bead runs before its step; its refusal blocks the step with its
      own reason and leaves the chain unchanged.
- [ ] `seal verify` on a correct chain prints six steps with hashes and times; with one entry edited it names the
      first broken link.
- [ ] The ratified thresholds are inside the step-2 payload, and a scorer run with any other threshold set
      refuses to produce a verdict for this suite.
- [ ] The commitment is hiding: two suites differing only in salt have different commitments; the reveal verifies.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- seal::`
- `inv_tenfold_02_orderings`: all 720 permutations of the six steps; one accepted.
- `chain_integrity`: flipping one byte in entry 3 breaks `entry_hash` of 3 and `prev` of 4.
- `freeze_before_commit_refused`: `frozen_at` < step-2 time → `freeze-before-suite-commit`.
- `freeze_after_open_refused`: the planted manifest whose freeze time follows the suite opening → the `freeze`
  entry is refused with `freeze-after-open` and the run is refused; a `preanalysis` entry appended after `open`
  → `seal-out-of-order`.
- `preanalysis_needs_all_freezes`: one arm missing → `freeze-missing`.
- `open_detects_changed_suite`: a card edited after step 2 → `suite-changed-after-commit`.
- `time_regression`: an entry stamped earlier than its predecessor → refused.
- `kind_and_role_registry`: after `open`, an entry of an unregistered kind → `seal-kind-unregistered`; a
  registered kind written by a role its registration does not allow → `role-not-permitted`; both exit 3 and leave
  the chain unchanged.
- `commitment_binding_and_hiding`: reveal with a different card set fails; different salts give different hashes.
- `thresholds_bound`: the scorer reads thresholds from the step-2 payload hash; a mismatch refuses.
- `registered_precondition_runs`: a test precondition registered for step 3 that refuses with a planted reason →
  the step-3 entry is refused with that reason and the chain is unchanged; one that returns `ok` with a digest →
  the entry is appended and its payload carries the digest; two preconditions run in registration order and the
  first refusal wins.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_seal_order.sh`: on a rehearsal gate with two synthetic arms and a development-grade suite
(nothing sealed in the real sense), performs the six steps with `frob-tenfold seal step …`, verifies the chain,
then replays four ordering violations (2 after 4, 4 after 6, 5 before the last freeze, a suite edit after 2) and
expects each refusal. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_seal_order/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, each step's entry
hash, evidence-ledger sequence number and time, the commitment, each refusal with its reason
(in order of the four violations `tenfold.freeze-before-suite-commit`, `tenfold.freeze-after-open`,
`tenfold.freeze-missing`, `tenfold.suite-changed-after-commit`; `tenfold.seal-out-of-order` where a step is
attempted before its predecessor completes), the output of `seal verify` with the first broken link where one is
planted, and the summary naming GATE-TENFOLD-04.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: exactly one accepted ordering of 720; a six-entry chain's final hash is reproducible bit for bit
from its payloads. Negative controls (each must be caught): a manifest whose freeze time follows the suite
opening (the run is refused); a suite hash committed after an arm's freeze; a protocol edited after its hash; a
backdated entry; a threshold changed after a sealed run. Determinism: chain hashes are D0.

## Considerations

- OPEN (owner): ratification of the thresholds; it blocks the first suite hash; default as written.
- Wall-clock times can be forged by whoever controls the host; the binding evidence is the hash chain plus the
  independent sequence numbers of the evidence ledger, and, for an external adjudicator, publication of the step-2
  commitment somewhere the builders cannot rewrite.
- Step 3 cannot be proven from inside (an advocate can read whatever they have); the protocol's protection is that
  sealed content does not exist for them to read, not that they promise not to.
- The tournament repeats all six steps on a new suite; a pass at M3 does not carry to M7.
