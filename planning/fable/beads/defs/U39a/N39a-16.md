## Background

Two different questions hide inside "how long does it take to get an accepted answer". Starting from nothing but
the mission, how long? And when a mission you have already answered is changed, how long to bring the answer up
to date? The first charges every arm the cost of authoring its environment; the second rewards reuse. A system
built around caches, memoized analyses and inherited policies will look very different on the two, and a pooled
number would describe neither.

DESIGN: **cold and warm runs are separate results and are never pooled.** A ratio is always reported with its
regime: "cold", or "warm at N amendments".

- A **cold run** starts from the mission card and nothing else that is specific to the mission: authoring the
  environment, the design domain, the reward or requirement and the evaluation set is inside the clock for every
  arm.
- A **warm campaign** runs a declared sequence of amendments to an accepted cold answer, with identical reusable
  inputs and equal cache budgets for every arm. A fair rival also caches graphs, factors, compiled kernels and
  policies. A failed amendment stays in the sequence and costs its deadline. Accepted count, total time and time
  per acceptance are reported separately.

Rejected: reporting only warm numbers (hides authoring, where a new system may be slower than an expert with an
existing setup: the expected cold ratio for family queries may be below 1); letting an arm that failed an
amendment skip it (the sequence would then measure only the amendments that arm finds easy); unequal cache
budgets (a ratio of disk allowances).

## Scope

In: the definition and enforcement of a cold start; the warm campaign runner and its amendment sequence; the
rules on reusable inputs and cache budgets; the per-amendment records; what is reported per regime. Out: standing
assets built before any clock starts, their cost S and the crossover reuse count (N39a-17); acceptance of an
amendment verdict (N39a-07); the pooled statistics per regime and the scorer's own refusal of a mixed cell
(N39a-27, check 4, which reads the `regime` field this bead writes); the freeze manifest that names the frozen
configuration, cache states and standing assets (N39a-18); the cap enforcement that turns a cache-budget excess
into a cap breach (N39a-04); the fresh-session and documentation-budget rules of equal terms (N39a-19); the
reason ids (N39-REF).

## Technical approach

**1. Regime on every record.** `regime` ∈ {`cold`, `warm`} is a field of the mission card, the campaign record,
the ledger and every ratio; this bead writes it on the card, the campaign record and the ledger, and a warm
campaign record also carries `amendment_seq` and `base`. The scorer refuses to pool across regimes
(`regime-mixed`, check 4 of N39a-27): a cell is a stratum *and* a regime.

**2. Cold start: what may exist when the clock starts.** At `card_delivered` the arm's workspace contains exactly:
(a) the arm's frozen configuration and its *declared standing assets* (class-level, built before any clock; they
are named in the freeze manifest with their build cost and are charged through the warm arithmetic of N39a-17,
never hidden); (b) the card's permitted inputs (calibration data, prior answers, caches listed on the card,
identical for every arm); (c) nothing derived from this mission. Enforcement:
1. the harness creates the campaign workspace from the freeze manifest's content addresses and the card's
   permitted inputs only; any other file is absent by construction;
2. a manifest of the workspace (path, hash) is recorded at campaign start, before `card_delivered`, by a start
   precondition this bead registers with `frob-tenfold campaign start` (N39a-03); every hash must resolve to the
   freeze manifest or the card, otherwise the campaign is refused before its clock starts
   (`cold-start-foreign-file`);
3. caches are either empty or in the state the freeze manifest declares (`caches.compile: "warm"` means the
   class-level compile cache built during tuning; it may contain nothing keyed by a sealed mission, which is
   guaranteed by the fact that sealed missions did not exist for the arm before this instant);
4. the agent session is new: no memory, no prior transcript, the documentation budget of the equal-terms rules.
Consequently authoring (writing the environment, design domain, reward or requirement, evaluation set from the
card) happens after t₀ and is charged to the `author` phase for every arm.

**3. Warm campaign (numbered procedure).**
1. *Precondition.* An accepted cold answer of this arm on the family's base mission. If the arm's cold run was
   censored, the warm campaign for that arm on that family is not run and each of its amendments is charged its
   deadline (the arm has no answer to amend); the record says `warm: no-base`.
2. *Sequence.* The card's `amendments` list, in order: k = 1…K. Each amendment is its own clocked unit with its
   own deadline H_warm (the stratum's warm deadline) and its own `card_delivered` event that reveals edit k only.
   The card does not say which edit matters.
3. *State carried.* Between amendments the arm keeps its workspace: its own caches, compiled artifacts, policies,
   memo store, prior answers and transcripts summaries it wrote to disk, subject to the cache budget below. The
   agent session itself is fresh for each amendment (DESIGN: reuse must live in artifacts the system manages, not
   in an agent's context window, which is neither content-addressed nor equal across arms); it reads whatever the
   arm's tools left in the workspace.
4. *Submission.* For each amendment the arm submits an amendment verdict (which earlier verdicts survive, which
   are withdrawn, the repaired answer) through the submission protocol.
5. *Failure stays.* An amendment not accepted by its deadline is charged H_warm and remains in the sequence; the
   campaign proceeds to amendment k + 1 from whatever state the arm has. A missed withdrawal fails that amendment.
6. *Records.* One campaign record and one ledger per amendment, with `amendment_seq: k` and `base: <receipt hash>`.

**4. Equal reusable inputs and cache budgets.** The pre-registration states, per stratum: the reusable inputs
every arm receives (the same calibration data, prior answers and proof problems), and a cache budget in bytes of
persistent storage and of device memory that a campaign workspace may hold between amendments. The harness
measures the workspace after each amendment; exceeding the budget is a cap breach of that amendment (censored at
the breach instant, charged H_warm). An arm may spend its budget on whatever it likes (kernel caches, policies,
memo entries, solver factorizations).

**5. Reported per regime.**
- Cold cell: the pooled ratio of restricted times with its interval and verdict.
- Warm cell: for each arm the *accepted count* (of K amendments, over families), the *total time* (Σ restricted
  times over all amendments), and the *time per acceptance* (total ÷ accepted count), separately; the pooled
  restricted mean times per amendment ā (arm A) and b̄ (arm B); and the warm ratio at reuse counts 1, 10 and 100
  with the crossover reuse count (N39a-17). A warm result is never quoted without its standing-asset cost.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A workspace manifest at clock start resolves entirely to the freeze manifest and the card; a planted file
      derived from the mission is detected and the campaign is refused before its clock starts.
- [ ] The scorer refuses a cell mixing cold and warm records.
- [ ] In a warm campaign of K = 5 with amendment 3 censored, amendments 4 and 5 still run, the total time
      includes H_warm for amendment 3, and accepted count is at most 4.
- [ ] An arm with no accepted cold base is charged K × H_warm and marked `warm: no-base`.
- [ ] A workspace above the cache budget after an amendment is a cap breach of that amendment.
- [ ] Each amendment reveals only its own edit; edit k + 1 is unreadable during amendment k.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- regime::`
- `no_pooling`: records with regimes {cold, warm} in one cell → `regime-mixed`.
- `cold_manifest_closure`: workspace hashes ⊆ freeze ∪ card → accepted; one extra hash → `cold-start-foreign-file`.
- `failed_amendment_stays`: times (470, 470, censored, 470, 470) s at H_warm = 3,600 s → total 5,480 s, accepted
  4, time per acceptance 1,370 s.
- `no_base_charges_all`: K = 5, H_warm = 3,600 s → 18,000 s, accepted 0.
- `amendment_isolation`: reading edit k + 1 during amendment k → path absent.
- `cache_budget_breach`: 12 GiB against a 10 GiB budget → `cap_breach(cache)` on that amendment.
- `fresh_session_per_amendment`: the session identifier differs for each amendment; the workspace persists.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_regimes.sh`: a synthetic arm runs one cold development mission and a five-amendment warm
campaign with one planted failure and one planted foreign file in a second attempt, then hands the cold and
warm records together to `frob-tenfold-score` as one cell. Asserts the workspace manifest check
(`tenfold.cold-start-foreign-file`), the per-amendment outcomes, the three warm totals and the scorer's refusal to
pool (`tenfold.regime-mixed`). It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_regimes/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the regime, the
workspace manifest hash at clock start, each amendment's sequence number, outcome and charged time against its
deadline, the cache size against its budget, each refusal with its reason, on a failed assertion the first
differing total with the campaign record path, and the summary. The arm and the missions are labelled
`synthetic`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the totals above; for the warm worked card, one amendment taking arm B 4,050 s (six turns, six
trainings) and arm A 470 s (two turns, one inherited fine-tune) is a marginal ratio of 8.62. Negative controls: a
runner that drops a failed amendment from the sequence (total time shrinks; the test compares against the sum
with H_warm); a cold start that inherits a workspace from a previous campaign; a report that prints one pooled
number for a stratum (the report schema has no such field).

## Considerations

- Whether a fresh agent session per amendment is the right equal-terms choice is a judgement: it denies both arms
  conversational memory and makes reuse a property of the system. If the adjudicator registers continuing
  sessions instead, the rule must be the same for every arm and stated in the pre-registration.
- Warm deadlines differ from cold ones; both are part of the suite commitment.
- A defect found in a frozen arm mid-campaign voids the affected cells; an amendment sequence is not restarted
  with a patched arm.
- Cold authoring by an agent is a large and noisy phase for every arm; it is common to both and limits the cold
  ratio through the unremovable-cost ceiling, which is why warm cells exist at all.
