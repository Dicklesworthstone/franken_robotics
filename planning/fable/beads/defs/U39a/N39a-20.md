## Background

The numerator of the ten-fold ratio is the time of **arm B, the strongest composed rival**: a named incumbent
engine, per-design reinforcement learning, a standard outer-loop optimizer, analytic bounds and a verifier where
the stratum needs them, and the same agent that drives FrankenRobotics. A weak rival makes any ratio easy. The
protocol therefore gives the rival its own advocates (who have written no FrankenRobotics code), a tuning budget
that is never smaller than the builders', every capability the incumbents document, and a refusal to freeze a
rival that cannot win a mission a conventional method should win.

**Arm B, named (DESIGN).** The advocates choose, per stratum, the configuration that finishes the development
suite soonest; it is one frozen configuration per stratum, never a per-mission choice. The candidates are FACTs
about the releases read for the plan (MuJoCo and MuJoCo Warp 4.0.1, Newton 1.8.0, Isaac Lab 3.0.0, Genesis 1.4.7,
Drake 1.58.0, ManiSkill 3.0.1), re-pinned by revision at the rival audit before a gate cites them.

**Every capability an incumbent documents is available to arm B and is expected to be used where it helps**
(FACT about the documents read): per-world model parameters in one batch, state-preserving model edits, opt-in
run-to-run determinism, mixed topologies in one batch, electrical and thermal actuator models, sampling control
without training, interior generators, and agent access over MCP. A rival that leaves a documented capability
unused on the development suite is asked why before the freeze.

Rejected: a rival assembled by the builders (they know which rival features their product beats); a per-mission
choice of rival configuration (a best-of ensemble is a lower bound to report, not an arm); a rival restricted to
the incumbent's default recipe (the test is against the strongest composition, not the common one; the common one
is reported separately through the recovery ratio).

## Scope

In: the candidate registry per stratum group; the selection rule; the capability checklist and its "asked why"
record; the tuning protocol, its budget rule and its log; the builders' under-built objection and the
adjudicator's ruling; what the advocates receive. Out: the pinned incumbent harness and its recorded environment
(BD-039); the freeze manifest (N39a-18); running arm B at the partial gate (BD-315) and at the tournament
(BD-430); arm C (N39a-21); process tagging (N39a-25).

## Technical approach

**1. Candidate registry (`arms/advocate.rs`; data `tenfold/<gate>/arm_b_candidates.json`).**

| Strata | Engine and learner | Outer loop and tools |
|---|---|---|
| `legged-quad`, `legged-biped`, `wheeled`, `tracked`, `hybrid` | MuJoCo Warp under MuJoCo Playground or mjlab, or Isaac Lab on Newton or PhysX, each with its own PPO recipe, one policy per design; a published shared design-conditioned policy where one fits | CMA-ES or Bayesian optimization over the design parameters with successive halving; the engine's per-world model fields for parametric variants; warm compilation caches; sampling-based control with no training where it helps |
| `throughput` | Newton with a solver that batches different topologies, or ManiSkill3 | The same, with mixed-topology batches |
| `envelope`, `sensing` | An expert's program over Drake, a linear or mixed-integer solver, an SMT or interval tool | A scenario language for world generation; system identification from the same calibration data |
| `rebody`, `failure`, `facility` | The locomotion configuration above | Retraining with a warm start; uniform randomization with the same monitors; an incremental mixed-integer program and a standard motion planner |

Each candidate is a record `{stratum_group, engine, learner, outer_loop, tools, pins (from the comparator
registry of the incumbent harness), licence_ok}`. The registry is extensible by the advocates before the freeze:
a stronger candidate not on the list may be added with its pin.

**2. Selection rule (numbered).**
1. The advocates run every candidate they consider on the stratum's 12 public development missions, with the
   common agent, under the gate's caps.
2. For each candidate compute the restricted mean time to acceptance on the development suite at the stratum's
   provisional deadline (development acceptance uses the public development batteries).
3. The candidate with the smallest restricted mean time is arm B for the stratum. Ties are broken by higher
   completion, then by the advocates' choice with the reason recorded.
4. One configuration per stratum is frozen. The table of all candidates run, with their development times, is
   part of `development_scores` in the freeze manifest: it is the evidence that the frozen one was the fastest.
5. The stratum registry's "strongest control" (for example a shared design-conditioned policy where one is
   published; a model-based planner where the task has one) must be present in the frozen configuration or
   excluded in writing with a reason.

**3. Capability checklist (the "asked why" record).** For the chosen engine, one row per documented capability
from the incumbent capability register (the eight named above plus any the register lists for that engine):
`{capability, documented_at (source and pin), used_on_development: bool, where, reason_if_unused}`. An unused
capability with an empty reason blocks the freeze (`capability-unexplained`). Acceptable reasons are concrete
("mixed-topology batching is on a solver path without run-to-run determinism, which the stratum's replay needs";
"measured: no gain on development missions 3 and 7, log attached"). The checklist is published with the manifest.

**4. Tuning protocol.**
- *What the advocates receive:* the development suite, the common agent, the plan document itself, and a tuning
  budget.
- *Budget rule:* at least equal to the compute the builders logged on the same development suite, and never
  below 40 device-hours per stratum (DESIGN). The builders' figure is read from their own tuning log, published at
  step 3 of sealing; `advocate_budget_device_h = max(40, builders_logged_device_h)` per stratum.
- *What is allowed:* any improvement a competent engineer would make: hyperparameters, reward shaping, curricula,
  warm caches, policy reuse, batching, analytic pruning. On development missions only.
- *Tuning log (`tuning_log` in the manifest):* an append-only list of `{ts, change, run, device_s, result}`; the
  sum of `device_s` is compared with the budget. Under-spending is allowed and is stated; the statement's
  `would_add_with_more_budget` lists what was left undone.
- *Never touched:* the adjudicating simulator. Its use in development or tuning disqualifies the arm.

**5. The under-built objection.** Before the freeze the builders may object in writing that the rival is
under-built (for example "per-world model fields are documented and unused; halving is absent"). The objection
goes through the logged comment channel; the adjudicator rules `sustained` (the advocates must address it before
freezing) or `overruled` (with a reason), and the ruling is recorded with the freeze entry. Independent of any
objection, the easy-win rule applies: a frozen rival that wins none of the development easy-win families of the
stratum is presumed under-built and its freeze is refused.

**6. Home-task reproduction.** The frozen rival must reproduce its own home-task training time within 20% on the
Linux host (for a flat-ground quadruped policy the published figures are about 410 s on one RTX 4090 and about
325 s on two, with a compile region of about 45 s inside those times: FACT about the source, re-measured at the
parity baseline). A rival that runs 20% slower here than its own publication is mis-installed, and the freeze
waits until the difference is explained in writing.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The candidate registry holds the four rows above with pins from the comparator registry; every candidate has
      a licence status.
- [ ] `development_scores` lists every candidate run with its restricted mean time, and the frozen configuration
      is the minimum (or the tie-break reason is recorded).
- [ ] The capability checklist has no unused capability without a reason; a planted empty reason blocks the freeze.
- [ ] The tuning log's device-seconds are within the budget rule; the budget equals max(40, builders' logged)
      device-hours per stratum.
- [ ] A sustained objection without a recorded response blocks the freeze; an overruled one is recorded.
- [ ] The frozen rival's home-task time is within 20% of its published figure on `trj`, or the written explanation
      is attached and the measured figure replaces the published one.
- [ ] A per-mission configuration switch inside arm B is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- advocate::`
- `selection_is_min_rmt`: candidates with development restricted mean times (5,400, 4,900, 6,100) s → the second.
- `tie_break_recorded`: equal times → higher completion wins; equal again → a reason is required.
- `budget_rule`: builders logged 25 → budget 40; builders logged 63 → budget 63 (device-hours).
- `capability_unexplained_blocks`: one row unused with an empty reason → `capability-unexplained`.
- `strongest_control_present_or_excluded`.
- `home_task_within_20_percent`: published 410 s; measured 480 s → inside (492 is the limit); 500 s → outside.
- `objection_flow`: sustained + no response → freeze refused; sustained + response → accepted; overruled → accepted.
- `one_config_per_stratum`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_arm_b_protocol.sh`: with two `synthetic` candidate configurations on a development stratum
(scripted stand-ins with fixed times), runs the selection, writes the checklist with one planted unexplained row,
applies the budget rule to a planted builders' log, files an objection and a ruling, and attempts the freeze
before and after the gaps are closed. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, each candidate's restricted mean
time, the selected candidate, budget and spend in device-hours, checklist rows, the ruling, each refusal reason,
and the summary naming GATE-TENFOLD-04 and GATE-TENFOLD-11 as touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 1.2 × 410 s = 492 s and 1.2 × 325 s = 390 s as the home-task limits; the budget floor of 40
device-hours. Negative controls: a rival frozen with a documented capability silently unused; a rival tuned with
less than the builders' compute and no statement; a rival that wins no easy-win family (freeze refused); a
candidate table that omits a faster candidate (the adjudicator re-runs the fastest two on three development
missions and compares; a discrepancy above 20% blocks the freeze).

## Considerations

- FACT about the incumbent documents read: run-to-run determinism, heterogeneous worlds and the fastest solver
  sit on different solver paths, so each is available and their conjunction is not shown; an advocate's reason
  for leaving one unused may be exactly that, and it is recorded.
- The advocates read this plan. That is deliberate: the rival may implement any portable mechanism it describes.
- Under the default, advocates are agent sessions with no FrankenRobotics authorship, and every result prints
  `rival: internally advocated`.
- Licences: where an incumbent's licence forbids publishing a comparison, it is replaced by the next strongest and
  the replacement is printed.
