## Background

The program bets that its mechanisms *need this engine* (BET-14): that a short robust program over an incumbent
engine cannot reach the same accepted answers, with the same checking scope, in comparable time. If that bet is
wrong, the honest product is a layer over an incumbent engine, and the plan says so in advance. The ten-fold test
therefore carries an arm whose only job is to try to make the bet fail.

**Arm C, the ablation arm (DESIGN).** At most 2,000 lines of program text, counted by the adjudicator, over the
engine of arm B. Its authors (the rival advocates) have read the plan. It may implement any of the program's
eight levers that needs no engine internals: batched parametric variants through the incumbent's model fields, a
ladder of rungs with a published anytime-valid test, a sampling controller or a published shared policy as the
cheap rung, a standard solver for family queries, restorable state for forks, the incumbent's own caches. **Arm C
is the plan's test of itself.**

Its relation to the names used with the recovery ratio: there the arms are written F (this system, arm A), B (the
declared incumbent) and W (the composed incumbent). Arm C is the first form of W: W is the same engine with every
portable method layered on by a competent baseline engineer, built as one pipeline before arm A exists, and it is
not held to arm C's 2,000 lines where a portable method needs more. The tie test and the recovery ratio are both
computed on that arm.

Where the product's verbs allow it, a fourth arm D runs FrankenRobotics' own layer over the incumbent engine
through the same verbs; it is optional and is reported when run.

Reason for a line limit: "short" must be checkable, or the ablation arm silently grows into a second product and
stops answering the question "does a *short* program suffice". Rejected: no limit (answers a different question,
which W answers); a limit counted by the authors (a limit nobody else counts is a statement of intent).

## Scope

In: arm C's definition as a checkable object: the line count rule, what it may and may not use, the list of
portable mechanisms, its freeze, what is recorded for the tie test; arm D's definition and reporting rule; the
mapping to W. Out: the pinned incumbent harness (BD-039); arm B (N39a-20); the tie rule's consequences and the
recovery ratio (see `needs`); running the arms (BD-315 at M3, BD-430 at M7).

## Technical approach

**1. What arm C is made of.**
- The engine, learner and environment of arm B for the same stratum, at the same pins (it adds no second engine).
- A program of at most 2,000 lines written by the advocates, which the common agent drives through a tool layer.
- The same agent, caps, hardware, shared inputs and documentation budget as every arm.

**2. The line count (adjudicator's rule, `arms/arm_c.rs`; `frob-tenfold armc count <pack>`).**
1. Counted: every line of every file in arm C's pack that is executed or interpreted in a campaign and is not
   part of arm B's pack at the same hash: program source in any language, configuration that encodes logic
   (reward terms, curricula, schedules, solver models), shell and build scripts, prompt text given to the agent
   beyond the documentation budget's guide.
2. Not counted: blank lines; comment-only lines; arm B's pack; the incumbents' own code at their pins; third-party
   packages at pinned releases that are published independently of this test (a standard linear-program solver, a
   published shared policy checkpoint, a published sampling controller), each listed with its pin and its line
   count for information.
3. A third-party package first published after the plan's date by any party of the gate is counted in full
   (closes the exit of moving the program into a "library").
4. Generated code is counted as its generator plus its inputs, and the generated output must be reproducible from
   them in the pack.
5. Physical lines; a line longer than 200 characters counts as ⌈length ÷ 100⌉ lines (closes the exit of very long
   lines).
The count and the per-file table are part of arm C's freeze manifest (`advocate.statement` cites them). A count
above 2,000 refuses the freeze (`armc-over-limit`).

**3. Portable mechanisms arm C may implement (each maps to a lever of the program's thesis).**

| Mechanism in arm C | Lever it tests | Engine facility it stands on |
|---|---|---|
| Batched parametric variants | Decide many designs per turn | The incumbent's per-world model fields |
| A ladder of rungs with a published anytime-valid test | Spend where it matters | Rollouts and the published sequential test |
| A sampling controller or a published shared policy as the cheap rung | Train few | The engine's rollout interface; a published checkpoint |
| A standard solver for family queries | Answer over families | A linear, mixed-integer, SMT or interval solver |
| Restorable state for forks | Explain by intervention | The engine's state save and restore |
| The incumbent's own caches | Short setup; do not recompute | Compile and graph caches |

What arm C may *not* do: modify the engine's internals (it is "over" the engine); call FrankenRobotics in any
form; use the adjudicating simulator.

**4. Tuning and freeze.** Arm C is tuned on the development suite under the same budget rule as arm B
(max(40 device-hours, the builders' logged compute) per stratum; DESIGN: the budget is per arm, so the advocates
are not forced to trade arm B's strength against arm C's) and frozen with its own manifest per stratum. Its
checklist records, per mechanism above, `implemented | not implemented: <reason>`.

**5. What is recorded for the tie test.** Per stratum and regime: arm C's restricted mean time RMT_C on the same
sealed missions, pairs and deadline as arms A and B; its accepted scope (the set of missions accepted, and on
family strata the obligations discharged) for the "same accepted scope" comparison with arm A; the pooled ratio
RMT_C ÷ RMT_A with a descriptive interval. The scorer computes these; the decision and its consequences belong to
the tie rule. Because the word "ten-fold" is governed by arm A against the stronger of arm B and the composed
incumbent, arm C's ledger is also a denominator candidate: where RMT_C < RMT_B the cell's ratio is taken against
arm C (N39a-32).

**6. W and the 2,000 lines.** At a gate where the composed incumbent W is built beyond arm C's limit, both are
recorded: arm C (within 2,000 lines) and W (unbounded, with its line count printed). The freeze manifests name
which is which (`arm: "C"` with `variant: "ablation-2000" | "composed-W"`), and the report prints both rows.

**7. Arm D (optional).** FrankenRobotics' own layer (its design language, static analyses, ladder, memo graph and
mission queries) running over the incumbent engine through the product's verbs, where the verbs allow an
alternative engine backend. It is frozen like any arm, runs on the same missions, and is reported when run with
the label `arm D: product layer over <engine>`. It never enters the ratio R. Default: not built unless arm C ties
on a stratum.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `armc count` reproduces a hand count on three fixture packs (1,999, 2,000 and 2,001 counted lines): the
      first two freeze, the third is refused.
- [ ] Each exclusion and each closing rule (new "library", generated code, long lines) is exercised by a fixture.
- [ ] Arm C's manifest pins the same engine and learner revisions as arm B's for the stratum; a second engine is
      refused.
- [ ] A pack that imports any FrankenRobotics artifact, or names the adjudicating simulator, is refused.
- [ ] The scorer emits RMT_C, the accepted-scope comparison with arm A and RMT_C ÷ RMT_A for every cell run.
- [ ] When RMT_C < RMT_B in a cell, the cell's denominator is arm C and the report says so.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- arm_c::`
- `count_basic`: 10 code lines, 5 blank, 5 comment-only → 10.
- `count_long_line`: one line of 450 characters → 5.
- `count_new_library`: a package dated after the plan and authored by an advocate → counted in full.
- `count_generated`: generator 120 lines + input 30 lines, output 5,000 lines → 150, and the output reproduces.
- `limit_boundary`: 2,000 → accepted; 2,001 → `armc-over-limit`.
- `same_engine_as_b`: differing engine revision → refused.
- `forbidden_imports`: a reference to the product's crates or to the adjudicating engine → refused.
- `denominator_is_stronger_rival`: RMT_B = 5,400 s, RMT_C = 3,600 s, RMT_A = 600 s → ratio 6.0 against C, not 9.0.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_arm_c.sh`: counts three fixture packs, freezes the two admissible ones on a rehearsal gate,
runs a `synthetic` three-arm cell of six development families with scripted times, and prints RMT_C, the scope
comparison and the two candidate ratios. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, the per-file count table, the
total against 2,000, each refusal reason, the three restricted mean times, the chosen denominator, and the summary.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the count fixtures; the denominator example (6.0, not 9.0). Negative controls: an arm C whose
logic lives in a 30,000-line "utility package" written for the test (counted, refused); an arm C counted by its
authors at 1,900 lines that the adjudicator counts at 2,300; an arm C that quietly calls a product binary; a
report that awards the word against arm B when arm C is faster (the verdict input is the stronger rival).

## Considerations

- KILL-TENFOLD-TIE (decided at M3 and M7): if arm C reaches the same accepted scope within a factor of 1.5 of arm
  A's restricted mean time, the report says so and the layer ships over that engine for that stratum. Engine work
  then continues only for gaps internal to an engine; two are documented (a mixed-topology batch with run-to-run
  replay; the Metal tier) and two are unverified and checked before they are relied on (replay of one world
  outside its batch; solver-internal certificates).
- Ties are already on record from the plan-time pilots (a lifted linear program tied a projection; a dependency
  graph tied a semantic receipt), so a tie on `envelope` is a live outcome, not a remote one.
- 2,000 lines is a DESIGN number; it is part of the pre-registration and is not changed after the suite hash.
- The advocates who write arm C also tune arm B; the per-arm budget keeps the two from competing for compute.
