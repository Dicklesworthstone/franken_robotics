## Background

When FrankenRobotics trains a policy faster or slower than an incumbent stack, two things differ at once: the
simulation engine that produces experience and the learner that consumes it (the reinforcement-learning
implementation, its network code, its optimizer, its data path). The ten-fold test's phase ledger shows the
`train` phase of each arm, but a difference in that phase says nothing about which of the two causes it. The
funding question depends on the answer: an engine difference argues for engine work, a learner difference for
learner work, and the program's thesis holds that the engine's step is not where an order of magnitude lives.

DESIGN: **the learner-swap diagnostic.** The product ships a Gymnasium-compatible adapter (a binding that exposes
its batched environment through the vector-environment interface the incumbent learning stacks consume), which
lets a rival learning stack train in this environment. Of the four combinations of engine and learner, three can
be run: ours with ours, theirs with theirs, and our engine with their learner. The three split a difference in
the `train` phase into an engine part and a learner part. **The diagnostic never enters the ratio R.** If the
adapter fails its gate (KILL-GYMADAPT), the rival learner drives whole rollouts through the job interface and the
added latency is recorded in the ledger.

The fourth combination (their engine with our learner) is not run: the product's learner lives on the product's
device buffers and has no path into a foreign engine's process.

Rejected: inferring the split from published step rates (the learner's share is 13% of training wall-clock on
one incumbent stack and unmeasured on ours; shares do not transfer across stacks); letting the diagnostic's
numbers into the ratio (the ratio is arm against arm as frozen; a hybrid is neither arm).

## Scope

In: the three runs, their matched conditions and their records; the decomposition arithmetic; the fallback path
under KILL-GYMADAPT; where the result is reported. This bead is the one place where the learner-swap
diagnostic is run and recorded. Out: the adapter itself (BD-301); the phase ledger and its
resource table, where the learner's share of `train` is recorded for every run (N39a-24, N39a-26); the arms as
frozen (BD-315); the third clause of the adapter's gate GATE-CORTEX-16 and the final reading of KILL-GYMADAPT
(N23b-13, a dependency): one pinned external PPO trained through the adapter on the reference task on three
seeds, judged by the attainment rule with its controls. That bead reports wall-clock to attainment, steps per
second and the adapter's per-step overhead for its own run, labelled `adapter-driven`; those figures are not
this diagnostic's `OT` row. The `OT` run here is repeated on five seeds under the gate's caps with arm B's
frozen learner, and reuses from N23b-13 the pinned environment, the weights interchange and the kill-gate
reading that selects the path of step 4; the per-step overhead measured there is compared with the transfer
share of E_OT here, and a disagreement beyond the ledger's 2% closure is reported in the record.

## Technical approach

**1. The task.** One training task per design stratum, chosen from the development suite and fixed in the
pre-registration: for `legged-quad` the reference quadruped's flat-ground velocity task, trained to the task's
return threshold (the same threshold for all three runs). Hardware: one RTX 4090 of the Linux host, the gate's
core count, the gate's caps.

**2. The three runs (each repeated on 5 seeds, interleaved in time in randomized order).**

| Run | Engine | Learner | Path |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OO` | ours | ours | The product's on-device training |
| `TT` | theirs (arm B's frozen engine) | theirs (arm B's frozen learner) | Arm B's training recipe as frozen |
| `OT` | ours | theirs | Arm B's learner driving the product's environment through the Gymnasium-compatible adapter |

For each run record: wall time to the threshold T_x; environment steps to the threshold N_x; and, from the
resource table of the phase ledger, the split of `train` wall time into environment time E_x (stepping, reset,
observation and reward computation, transfer to the learner) and learner time L_x (inference, storage,
optimization), with T_x = E_x + L_x + residual, the residual reported. Observation and action spaces, reward,
termination and randomization are matched: `OT` uses the product's task definition; `TT` uses the incumbent's
closest home task, and the differences between the two task definitions are listed in the record (an unmatched
task is reported as a limit of the diagnostic, not hidden).

**3. Decomposition (exact arithmetic on the recorded times).**
- Total difference in the `train` phase: Δ = T_TT − T_OO.
- *Engine part* (same learner, different engine): Δ_engine = T_TT − T_OT.
- *Learner part* (same engine, different learner): Δ_learner = T_OT − T_OO.
- Identity: Δ = Δ_engine + Δ_learner, exactly.
- Reported also as ratios: T_TT ÷ T_OT (engine) and T_OT ÷ T_OO (learner), whose product is T_TT ÷ T_OO.
- Sample-efficiency check: N_OT against N_TT (the same learner should need a similar number of steps in both
  engines if the tasks match; a ratio outside [0.67, 1.5] flags a task mismatch and the decomposition is labelled
  `task-unmatched`).
- With 5 seeds per run, each figure is reported as a median with the minimum and maximum; no test is attached and
  no verdict follows (descriptive).

**4. The fallback under KILL-GYMADAPT.** If the adapter has failed its gate (the reading of N23b-14), `OT` runs
through the product's job interface, on the whole-rollout fallback that N23bp1-01 builds when the kill gate fires
(and closes unbuilt if it passes): the rival learner submits whole rollouts as jobs and reads back trajectories. The added latency per
rollout (submission, queueing, serialization, readback) is measured and charged to the `wait` phase in the ledger
of that run, and the record carries `path: job-interface` and the *lost factor* T_OT(job interface) ÷
T_OT(adapter estimate), where the estimate subtracts the measured per-rollout latency. The decomposition is then
labelled `engine part includes interface latency of <x> s`.

**5. Record and reporting.** `tenfold/<gate>/diagnostics/learner_swap/<stratum>.json` with the three runs, the
seeds, the decomposition, the task-difference list, and the label `diagnostic: not in R`. The report prints it in
a separate section. The scorer has no code path from this record to any ratio, interval or verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three runs exist for each design stratum of the gate with 5 seeds each, matched caps and hardware, and
      interleaved start times.
- [ ] Δ = Δ_engine + Δ_learner holds exactly in the record's arithmetic.
- [ ] E_x + L_x + residual = T_x for every run, with the residual at most 2% of T_x or the run flagged.
- [ ] A step-count ratio N_OT ÷ N_TT outside [0.67, 1.5] sets `task-unmatched`.
- [ ] Under the fallback, the per-rollout latency is in the `wait` phase and the lost factor is recorded.
- [ ] No ratio, interval or verdict in the gate's report changes when the diagnostic record is deleted.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- learner_swap::`
- `decomposition_identity`: (T_OO, T_OT, T_TT) = (300, 380, 410) s → Δ = 110, engine 30, learner 80; ratios
  1.079 and 1.267 with product 1.367.
- `negative_parts_allowed`: (450, 380, 410) → Δ = −40, engine 30, learner −70 (our learner is slower): reported
  with signs, no clamping.
- `task_unmatched_flag`: N_OT = 2.0 × 10⁸, N_TT = 1.0 × 10⁸ → flagged.
- `fallback_latency_to_wait`: 1,000 rollouts × 0.12 s interface latency → 120 s in `wait`; lost factor
  T ÷ (T − 120 s).
- `not_in_r`: the scorer's input set contains no diagnostic record (a type-level separation, tested by building
  the scorer input from a store that holds one).
- `median_min_max`: five seeds (298, 300, 305, 310, 340) → median 305, range [298, 340].

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_learner_swap.sh` (`needs=cuda`; skips with code 77 without a device): runs `OO` with
`frob train start <reference design> --task <reference task> --scenes <set> --target <reduced threshold> --seed
<s> --key "$(e2e_key oo-<s>)" --json` on the reference task at a reduced threshold (`E2E_SMALL=1`), `OT` with a pinned rival learner
through the adapter, and `TT` through the incumbent harness; builds the three ledgers and the record. Logs each
command, exit code, wall time, T, N, E and L per run with units, the decomposition, the flag, the disclosure
block fields of each training figure, the path of `OT` (`adapter` or `job-interface`, derived from the process
tags), and the summary. In small scale the log says `gates_claimable: false`. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh`
(STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/tenfold_learner_swap/<utc>.ndjson`; on a failed identity or
closure assertion it logs the first differing quantity with both values and the record path.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the identity; the worked numbers above. Published context (FACT about the sources, re-measured at
the parity baseline): about 410 s to the threshold on one RTX 4090 and about 325 s on two for a flat-ground
quadruped policy, with a compile region of about 45 s inside those times; on one incumbent stack inference and
learning are about 13% of training wall-clock. Negative controls: a planted record in which the three runs used
different return thresholds (refused: thresholds must match); a build that feeds `OT` into the pooled ratio (the
not-in-R test fails it); an `OT` run that silently used the job interface (the `path` field is derived from the
process tags, not declared).

## Considerations

- The diagnostic is only as good as the task match between `TT` and the other two; the list of task differences
  is the first thing a reader should look at.
- If the engine part is small and the learner part large, lever work on `train` is learner work (family policies,
  inheritance), which is where the thesis already puts it; if the engine part is large and negative, the parity
  kill rule for throughput is the relevant decision, not this diagnostic.
- Compute: 15 trainings to threshold per stratum, about 1.7 device-hours at the published time (15 × 410 s =
  6,150 s); above the default envelope only if thresholds are raised.
- The adapter is built and gated for this test and for conformance only; it is not a supported public binding
  unless the owner decides otherwise.
