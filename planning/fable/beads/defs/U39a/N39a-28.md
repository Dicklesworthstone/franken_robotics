## Background

Every ratio the ten-fold test reports comes with an interval: one-sided 95% bounds L and U on the pooled ratio R̂
of restricted times (rival over FrankenRobotics) for a cell (a stratum and a regime). DESIGN: the bounds come
from a bias-corrected bootstrap that resamples mission families with replacement and keeps each family's pairs
together, with 10,000 replicates, on ln R̂. The family is resampled because it is the independent unit; a
bootstrap over missions or pairs would treat correlated runs as independent and report an interval that is too
narrow.

The interval is **approximate and descriptive, and it does not decide the word**. Resampling cannot show a slow
tail that the sample does not contain: on a 24-family suite drawn from a population whose true ratio is 1.82 a
percentile bootstrap reports 20 in 79% of suites (the population has 1% of families on which the candidate takes
999 s instead of 1 s; a suite of 24 contains only fast families with probability 0.99²⁴ = 0.786, and then every
resample is the same). A population claim rests on a separate bounded paired contrast and never on this interval;
the word "ten-fold" is decided by the point estimate together with an exact guard. The interval is printed
beside the word because, between true ratios of 7 and 10, the word can be awarded by chance.

A second requirement comes from publication: the replication kit must reproduce every published interval **bit
for bit on a clean machine**. A bootstrap that uses a library random generator or the platform's mathematics
library cannot promise that. Hence the DESIGN decisions here: a fixed integer generator, exact rational replicate
values, and two normal-distribution functions implemented from IEEE-754 basic operations.

Rejected: a Student-t interval on log ratios (assumes a shape that censoring at H destroys); the accelerated
variant of the bootstrap (the registered procedure is the bias-corrected one; adding an acceleration constant
changes the registered procedure and would have to be ratified before the first suite hash); floating-point
replicate values (ordering near ties would differ by platform).

## Scope

In: the generator, the resampling scheme, replicate values, the bias correction, index selection, the two normal
functions, the output and its labels. Out: the pooled ratio and per-family sums it consumes (N39a-27); the
verdict (N39a-32); the bounded contrast for population claims (see `needs`).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** For a cell with m families: per-family sums (b_i, a_i) of restricted times in integer milliseconds
(b for the cell's rival arm, a for arm A), each family's pairs already summed, so a resampled family brings all
its pairs. The point estimate R̂ = Σ b_i ÷ Σ a_i. B = 10,000 replicates. Level 0.05 each side.

**2. The generator (`score/rng.rs`; fixed forever for schema version 1).** SplitMix64 on a 64-bit state:
```
next():  state ← state + 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15 (mod 2⁶⁴);  z ← state
         z ← (z xor (z >> 30)) × 0xBF58476D1CE4E5B9 (mod 2⁶⁴)
         z ← (z xor (z >> 27)) × 0x94D049BB133111EB (mod 2⁶⁴);  return z xor (z >> 31)
below(m): t ← (2⁶⁴ − m) mod m;  loop { x ← next(); p ← x × m (128-bit); if (p mod 2⁶⁴) ≥ t return p >> 64 }
```
`below` is unbiased on [0, m). The seed is the first eight bytes, little-endian, of
BLAKE3(suite commitment ‖ stratum ‖ regime ‖ "bootstrap/1"): it is fixed by committed data, so nobody chooses it.

**3. Replicates.** For r = 1…B, in order: draw m indices with `below(m)`, in order; the replicate is the exact
rational R*_r = Σ b_idx ÷ Σ a_idx (128-bit sums). Sort the B replicates ascending by exact comparison
(cross-multiplication). Sorting rationals is the same order as sorting their logarithms, so the procedure is "on
ln R̂" without evaluating a logarithm: bias-corrected percentile bounds are unchanged by any increasing
transformation.

**4. Bias correction.** p₀ = (#{R*_r < R̂} + ½·#{R*_r = R̂}) ÷ B, clamped to [1/(2B), 1 − 1/(2B)];
z₀ = Φ⁻¹(p₀). With z_lo = Φ⁻¹(0.05) = −1.6448536269514722:
α_L = Φ(2·z₀ + z_lo) and α_U = Φ(2·z₀ − z_lo).

**5. Bounds.** k_L = max(1, ⌊(B + 1)·α_L⌋) and L is the k_L-th smallest replicate; k_U = min(B, ⌈(B + 1)·α_U⌉) and
U is the k_U-th smallest. L and U are exact rationals; they are rendered with outward rounding (L down, U up) at
three decimals. Each is a one-sided 95% bound; the pair is printed as [L, U].

**6. The normal functions (`score/normal.rs`).** Φ and Φ⁻¹ in double precision, written with +, −, ×, ÷, square
root, comparisons and bit-level scaling only, in a fixed evaluation order, with their own `exp` and `ln` built the
same way (range reduction and fixed polynomials). No call into the platform mathematics library and no fused
multiply-add. Requirement: absolute error at most 10⁻¹² on [−8, 8] for Φ and on [10⁻⁶, 1 − 10⁻⁶] for Φ⁻¹
(TARGET; index selection needs only about 10⁻⁵), and identical bits on arm64 macOS and x86-64 Linux in debug and
release. The algorithms (for example a rational approximation of the complementary error function for Φ and a
piecewise rational approximation for Φ⁻¹) are chosen by the implementer and frozen with schema version 1; their
coefficient tables are part of the scorer source in the kit.

**7. Output.** `{B: 10000, seed: "0x…", p0: "9569/20000", z0, alpha_l, alpha_u, k_l, k_u, L: {"rational",
"decimal"}, U: {…}, method: "bc-family-bootstrap/1", label: "descriptive", degenerate: bool}`.
`degenerate` is true when all replicates are equal (every family has the same ratio, or m = 1): the interval then
has zero width and says nothing about the population, and the report prints `interval: degenerate` beside it.

**8. What the interval may and may not do.** It is printed with every ratio. It supplies the "at least ⟨L⟩ with
95% confidence" figure of the `measured` wording and the lower bound used by the engine-advantage wording for the
ablation arm. It is never an input to the `ten-fold` decision, and no population statement cites it.

- [ ] Reference known answer for the six-family card with seed 39 (decimal) and B = 10,000: p₀ = 9,569/20,000,
      k_L = 398, k_U = 9,380, L = 612/469 (rendered 1.304), U = 126/17 (rendered 7.412).
- [ ] The first three generator outputs from seed 39 are 0xce6a57a6e3ccdeec, 0xe60bb9617aa6ed70,
      0x5f8f570b5d4e3726; the first twelve values of `below(6)` are 4, 5, 2, 3, 4, 2, 1, 3, 1, 4, 4, 0.
- [ ] Constant-ratio families give L = U = that ratio and `degenerate: true`.
- [ ] Output bytes are identical on both platforms, in debug and release, and with the replicate loop run in one
      thread or split across threads (the stream is consumed in a fixed order regardless).
- [ ] Φ(0) = 0.5 exactly; Φ(1.6448536269514722) = 0.95 and Φ⁻¹(0.975) = 1.959963984540054 within 10⁻¹².
- [ ] Changing the seed changes replicates but, on the six-family card, moves L and U by less than 10% (a sanity
      bound on Monte Carlo error at B = 10,000; TARGET).
- [ ] No code path lets L or U reach the verdict function's `ten-fold` branch.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- score::bootstrap::`
- `splitmix_reference`: the three outputs and twelve bounded draws above.
- `below_unbiased`: 6 × 10⁶ draws of `below(6)`; each cell within 5 standard errors of 10⁶.
- `six_family_reference`: the reference values above, exact.
- `constant_ratio`: 24 families of (9,000, 1,500) s → L = U = 6, p₀ = ½, `degenerate`.
- `rare_tail_degenerate`: 24 families of (20, 1) s → L = U = 20, `degenerate`; the report line carries
  `interval: degenerate` (the case in which a percentile bootstrap would "certify" 20 for a population whose
  ratio is 1.8215).
- `transformation_invariance`: bounds computed on replicates and on their reciprocals (roles of arms swapped)
  satisfy L' = 1/U and U' = 1/L when the same index stream is used.
- `p0_clamp`: all replicates below R̂ → p₀ = 1 − 1/(2B), finite z₀.
- `normal_functions`: a table of 200 arguments against high-precision reference values, absolute error ≤ 10⁻¹²;
  bit patterns compared with a stored table (the cross-platform check).
- `order_exact`: two replicates differing by 1 part in 10¹⁸ are ordered correctly.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/tenfold_rehearsal.sh` (N39a-41): it runs `frob-tenfold-score cell` on the six-family card
with the reference seed and logs p₀, z₀, both α values, both indices, L and U as rationals and decimals beside
the reference values (tolerance: exact), then repeats on the second host and compares output digests.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the reference values above (FACT: computed while this bead was drafted, by a separate script that
follows steps 2 to 5 with a high-precision normal distribution; the implementation must reproduce them, and a
disagreement is a finding to resolve in the derivation file before the gate cites the bootstrap). Negative
controls: a bootstrap that resamples pairs instead of families on a fixture with c = 2 (the interval narrows; the
test compares against the family version and fails the mutant); a library generator (the reference stream
differs); floating-point replicates sorted with a tolerance; an implementation that feeds L into the `ten-fold`
decision (the verdict test with R̂ = 9.9 and L = 9.5 must not award the word). Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; one and eight worker threads; debug and release; both platforms: class D0.

## Considerations

- With m = 24 the bootstrap distribution is coarse and its bounds are optimistic for skewed data; that is why the
  word needs the exact guard and why a population claim needs the separate contrast with at least 32 families.
- A cell with many censored runs has per-family ratios piled at or below 1; the bias correction then matters and
  the interval is wide. It is reported as it is.
- The constants of the generator and the normal-function coefficient tables are part of what is frozen at the
  first suite hash; changing them changes published digits and needs a new method identifier.
- Thresholds on which the interval's use depends (the level 0.05, B = 10,000) are DESIGN choices in the
  pre-registration.
