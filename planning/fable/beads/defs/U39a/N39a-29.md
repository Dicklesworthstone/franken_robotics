## Background

In the ten-fold test a cell (a stratum and a regime) may be awarded the word "ten-fold" only if its pooled ratio
R̂ of restricted times is at least 10 **and** an exact guard rejects. The pooled ratio is a point estimate: a
true ratio of 10 lands above 10 half the time, and a true ratio of 8.5 lands above 10 in up to a fifth of gates.
One long mission can also carry a pooled ratio while most families show little. The guard asks a different
question of the same data: do *most families*, individually, show a clear advantage?

**The exact guard (DESIGN).** The count X of families with per-family ratio ρ_i > 5 is compared with a
binomial(m, ½) tail: the null hypothesis "the median per-family ratio is at most 5" is rejected at level α when
P(X ≥ x) ≤ α. The test is exact and distribution-free at fixed m. Its critical counts at α = 0.05 are 5 of 5,
6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12, 12 of 16, 17 of 24 and 22 of 32 (FACT: binomial arithmetic; for example
P(X ≥ 17) = 0.032 and P(X ≥ 16) = 0.076 at m = 24). At m = 4 even 4 of 4 has tail 1/16 = 0.0625, so the guard
cannot be passed: that verdict is unreachable, and a cell of four families is refused as a confirmatory cell.

Why the floor is 5: it comes from the first draft of the decision rule, whose lower bound had to exceed 5 and
which never confirmed a true ratio of 5. The pooled rule keeps that property: a true ratio of 5 is called
ten-fold with probability near zero in every design considered.

**Multiplicity (DESIGN).** At most four confirmatory cells are named per gate. The exact guard is applied with
Holm's step-down procedure at family level 0.05 across them, so the chance that any cell whose median per-family
ratio is at most 5 is awarded the word is at most 5% for the gate as a whole. With four cells the first step
needs 18 of 24. All other cells are reported with unadjusted intervals and cannot carry the word.

Rejected: a t-test on log ratios (a shape assumption that censoring breaks); a normal approximation to the
binomial (wrong at m = 24 near the 5% boundary); testing every cell at 0.05 (with eleven strata and two regimes
the gate-wide error would approach certainty of one false award somewhere).

## Scope

In: the tail in exact arithmetic; critical counts; reachability at a level; the per-cell p-value; Holm's procedure
over the named cells; the limit of four; refusals. Out: the pooled ratio and ρ_i (N39a-27); the other conditions
of the verdict (N39a-30, N39a-32); the pre-analysis that uses reachability (N39a-33).

## Technical approach

**1. The statistic.** For a cell with m families (the unit is the family, whatever the number of campaigns per
family), X = #{i : ρ_i > 5}, with ρ_i the per-family ratio against the cell's denominator arm, compared as exact
rationals (ρ_i = 5 exactly does not count). Censoring enters through ρ_i: a family where arm A was censored has
ρ_i ≤ 1 and does not count; a family where only the rival was censored has a lower bound ρ_i, which counts only if
the bound itself exceeds 5 (conservative for arm A). In a warm cell ρ_i is the per-family warm ratio at ten
reuses.

**2. The tail (`score/guard.rs`, exact).** tail(m, x) = Σ_{k=x}^{m} C(m, k) ÷ 2^m as a reduced rational, with
integer arithmetic wide enough for m ≤ 120 (128-bit suffices: C(120, 60) < 2¹¹⁷). The cell's guard p-value is
p = tail(m, X). The comparison p ≤ α is done on rationals (α as a/b: tail_num·b ≤ a·2^m).

**3. Critical count and reachability.** crit(m, α) = the smallest x with tail(m, x) ≤ α, or `none` when
tail(m, m) = 2^(−m) > α. The guard is *reachable* at level α iff crit(m, α) is not `none`, that is iff
m ≥ ⌈log₂(1/α)⌉: m ≥ 5 at α = 0.05 and m ≥ 7 at α = 0.0125.

| m | crit at 0.05 | tail there | crit at 0.0125 (four cells, first Holm step) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | none (1/16 = 0.0625) | — | none |
| 5 | 5 | 0.03125 | none |
| 6 | 6 | 0.015625 | none |
| 8 | 7 | 0.03516 | 8 |
| 12 | 10 | 0.01929 | 11 |
| 16 | 12 | 0.03841 | 13 |
| 24 | 17 | 536,155/16,777,216 = 0.03196 | 18 (190,051/16,777,216 = 0.01133) |
| 32 | 22 | 0.02505 | 23 |

**4. Holm's step-down over the confirmatory cells.** Let k ≤ 4 be the number of cells named confirmatory in the
pre-analysis record (fixed before the suite opens), with guard p-values p_1…p_k.
1. Sort ascending: p_(1) ≤ … ≤ p_(k) (ties broken by the cell's registered order).
2. For j = 1…k: if p_(j) ≤ 0.05 ÷ (k − j + 1), the guard *rejects* for that cell and j advances; otherwise stop,
   and this cell and all later ones do not reject.
3. Thresholds for k = 4: 0.0125, 0.016667, 0.025, 0.05; for k = 1: 0.05.
The output per cell is `{X, m, p: rational, holm_rank, holm_threshold, rejects: bool}`. A cell's *adjusted level*
for reachability purposes is the first-step level 0.05 ÷ k (DESIGN: the conservative reading; a cell must be able
to pass even if it is tested first), which is why four cells of 24 families each need 18 of 24 to be sure of
passing.
k counts every cell *named* confirmatory, including one later disqualified or descriptive for another reason
(its p-value is then set to 1): removing a cell after the fact would loosen the others' thresholds.

**5. Refusals (harness reasons; each maps to the product's study-protocol refusals where a study is registered).**
- A request to name a confirmatory cell with m such that the guard is unreachable at its adjusted level, in
  particular m = 4 at any level and m ≤ 6 with four cells: refused as unreachable (`cell-guard-unreachable`; the
  same rule as a fixed-n study that names a verdict it cannot reach).
- More than four confirmatory cells in one gate: `too-many-confirmatory-cells`.
- A guard computed on a cell that was not named before the suite opened is labelled `unadjusted` and its
  `rejects` field is `not-applicable`: such a cell cannot carry the word.

- [ ] Results are exact and identical across platforms.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- score::guard::`
- `critical_counts`: 5 of 5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12, 12 of 16, 17 of 24, 22 of 32 at 1/20; 18 of 24 at 1/80;
  none at m = 4.
- `tails_24`: tail(24, 17) = 536,155/16,777,216; tail(24, 16) = 1,271,626/16,777,216 (0.0758);
  tail(24, 18) = 190,051/16,777,216.
- `tail_identities`: tail(m, 0) = 1; tail(m, m) = 2^(−m); tail(m, x) + tail(m, m − x + 1) = 1 for all m ≤ 64.
- `boundary_inclusive`: p exactly equal to the threshold rejects.
- `strict_floor`: ρ = 5 exactly does not count; ρ = 5 + 1/10⁹ does.
- `lower_bound_family`: rival censored, bound 6.857 → counts; bound 4.2 → does not.
- `holm_cases`: the two cases above, plus (0.2, 0.001, 0.001, 0.001) → three reject and the 0.2 cell does not.
- `holm_k_fixed`: a disqualified named cell keeps k = 4 for the others.
- `reachability`: m = 6, k = 4 → unreachable (1/64 > 1/80); m = 7, k = 4 → reachable (1/128 ≤ 1/80).
- `null_error_rate`: 10⁵ simulated cells with ρ_i > 5 with probability exactly ½ and m = 24 → rejection rate
  within 3 standard errors of 0.03196 and never above 0.05.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/tenfold_rehearsal.sh` (N39a-41): it prints the critical-count table from
`frob-tenfold-score guard --table`, compares it with the expected values, runs the guard on the six-family card
and on a 24-family `synthetic` cell with 17 and with 16 families above 5, and requests a four-family confirmatory
cell, expecting the refusal. Log lines carry m, X, the tail as a rational and a decimal, the Holm threshold, the
decision, and the refusal reason.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer (the exact guard of the harness's known-answer set): critical counts 5 of 5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of
12, 12 of 16, 17 of 24, 22 of 32 at 0.05; 18 of 24 at 0.0125; no count at m = 4. It guards against an approximate
test and against a verdict that cannot be reached. Negative control from the harness's own list: **a request for
a confirmatory cell with four families is refused as unreachable.** Further controls: a guard using the normal
approximation without a continuity correction (at 21 of 32 it reports about 0.039 and would reject, where the
exact tail is 236,618,693/4,294,967,296 = 0.0551 and does not; the test uses exactly that case); a guard with
"≥ 5" instead of "> 5"; Holm replaced by unadjusted tests (the first Holm case above would then reject in all
four cells instead of one). Metamorphic: permuting families does not change X.

## Considerations

- The guard tests the *median* per-family ratio against 5, not the pooled ratio against 10; the two conditions
  together are the rule. A cell can have R̂ ≥ 10 and fail the guard (one dominant family) and is then `measured`.
- Because ρ_i for a rival-censored family is a lower bound, heavy censoring of the rival makes the guard harder
  for arm A to pass than the truth would warrant; this is accepted as conservative.
- OPEN (owner): the floor 5 and the limit of four cells are ratified before the first suite hash; default as
  written. They are never changed after any sealed run.
- The plan's planning table (one confirmatory cell, lognormal times) shows that six families give a rule no
  better than the rejected first draft and four give none; the reachability kill rule turns that into a refusal.
