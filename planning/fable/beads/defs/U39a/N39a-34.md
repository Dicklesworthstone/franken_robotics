## Background

The ten-fold test is expensive (a default cell of 24 mission families with three arms and a four-hour deadline is
up to 288 device-hours) and its sealed missions can be used once. Running a cell whose decision rule cannot
return the verdict the program hopes for, or cannot return the one it fears, spends both for nothing and invites
a result that is quoted as if it meant something. The protocol therefore decides, before any sealed run, which
cells are capable of a verdict.

The general discipline behind it: a frozen decision rule is enumerated before sampling. For every fixed-sample
rule, the set of verdicts it can return at that sample is listed first, and a verdict that cannot be reached must
not be promised. Its origin is a planning pilot in which a rule with 64 pairs could return three of its four
verdicts, the fourth being first reachable at 86 pairs. For the ten-fold test the same discipline is a kill gate.
At most four confirmatory cells are named per gate.

A cell (a stratum and a regime) is **confirmatory** only if its recorded pre-analysis shows all four
*reachability conditions*: P(`ten-fold`) at least 0.8 at a true ratio of 15; P(`withdrawn`) at most 0.05 at a
true ratio of 5; the exact guard reachable at its adjusted level; and the horizon cap H ÷ RMT_A on the
development suite at least 10 (H is the stratum's deadline and RMT_A arm A's restricted mean time; the measured
ratio can never exceed H ÷ RMT_A because the rival's restricted time cannot exceed H).

**KILL-TENFOLD-REACH.** *Trigger:* a cell's pre-analysis fails a reachability condition. *Fallback, named in
advance:* the cell is opened as `descriptive` and says so before any sealed run; it is enlarged for the next
gate. *Decided:* at M3 for the cells of the partial gate, and at M7 for the cells of the tournament.

A `descriptive` cell is still run and still reported (ratio, interval, ledgers); it simply cannot be cited as
passing or failing the ten-fold bet. This is a decision bead: it applies the written rule to recorded values and
writes the outcome as an architecture decision record whichever way it goes.

## Scope

In: evaluation of the trigger per cell from the pre-analysis record; the decision record; the "says so before any
sealed run" mechanism; the enlargement plan; downstream effects. Out: computing the pre-analysis (N39a-33 at M3;
the pilot N39b-16 simulates the tournament's rule at M7); the guard's reachability arithmetic (N39a-29); the
non-inferiority attainability question (N39a-31); the deadline rule and its decision (N39b-30); the ratified
thresholds (N39b-33); the population-size refusal of the pre-analysis verb (N39b-34). This is the only decision
bead of KILL-TENFOLD-REACH: it carries both the M3 reading and the M7 reading.

Order of work: the decision for a gate instance is taken at sealing step 5 of that instance, from the development
ledgers of the arms as frozen, so it happens inside the flow of that gate's arm beads and before its suite is
opened. This bead closes when the decision records, their ADR and register entries and the pre-run notice of the
M3 partial gate exist; the bead that opens the suite and records the gate (seed BD-317) must wait for it. The M7
instance is decided again, by this procedure unchanged, inside the tournament's decision-rule epic (BD-429), which
writes its ADR and register entries in the form of step 4.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** The pre-analysis record of the gate (hashed into sealing step 5): for each cell the four condition
results with their measured values, `ni_attainable`, the reachable-verdict set, the planned m, c, n = m·c and H,
arm A's restricted mean time RMT_A on the development suite, and the list of cells the adjudicator proposes to
name confirmatory (at most four). The number of confirmatory cells fixes the Holm-adjusted level of each: with
four cells the first step is 0.05 ÷ 4 = 0.0125, at which the guard needs 18 of 24 families.

**Reading the conditions.** Condition (iii) is read first, because it is arithmetic: the guard count X of
families with per-family ratio above 5 is compared with a binomial(m, ½) tail, and reachable means that some
x ≤ m has P(X ≥ x) at most the adjusted level. Critical counts at level 0.05: 5 of 5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12,
12 of 16, 17 of 24, 22 of 32. At m = 4 even 4 of 4 has tail 1/16 = 0.0625: not reachable, and a four-family cell
is refused as a confirmatory cell (product code FROB-REF-532 where the request comes through the pre-analysis
verb). At level 0.0125: m = 6 is not reachable (1/64 = 0.0156); m = 7 is (1/128 = 0.0078). Condition (iv) is the
cap H ÷ RMT_A. Conditions (i) and (ii) are read from the simulated table of the pre-analysis at the measured
per-family spread and censoring rates.

**2. Decision procedure (per proposed cell; numbered).**
1. Read the four conditions. If all hold and the reachable-verdict set contains both `ten-fold` and `withdrawn`,
   the kill gate **does not fire**: the cell is named confirmatory.
2. Otherwise it **fires** for this cell. The cell's status becomes `descriptive: unreachable`, with the list of
   failing conditions and their values (for example `P(ten-fold | 15) = 0.57 < 0.8`, `guard needs 6 of 6 at 0.05
   and is unreachable at 0.0125`, `horizon cap 9.6 < 10`, `ten-fold unreachable: completion non-inferiority
   cannot be met at m = 24`).
3. Before firing on condition (iv) alone, apply the one repair the protocol itself provides: under the default
   deadline rule H is raised in half-hour steps until the cap is at least 10, and the pre-analysis is rerun. If
   the adjudicator fixed H explicitly, no repair is applied.
4. Before firing on conditions (i) to (iii), check whether more sealed families exist for the stratum (connected
   to nothing revealed): if the smallest sufficient m is available, the cell may be enlarged *now*, the
   pre-analysis rerun, and the decision repeated once. No other repair (changing thresholds, the rule, the model)
   is permitted.
5. Cells never proposed as confirmatory are `descriptive: not-named` and are outside this kill gate.
6. After the last proposed cell is decided, the number of confirmatory cells is recounted and the adjusted levels
   of the remaining cells are fixed once, before sealing. They are not relaxed later if a cell is voided.

**Other ways a cell is descriptive** (not this gate; listed so that the statuses are not confused): n below 10;
more than 5% of pairs excluded; the cell was never named confirmatory; no adjudication is available for the
stratum (KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ, decided in N39a-11).

**Fail-closed posture.** A cell with no recorded pre-analysis is `descriptive`; the scorer refuses to issue any
other verdict for it.

**3. "Says so before any sealed run."** The decision records are part of the step-5 payload of the sealing
ledger. The harness writes a public pre-run notice (`tenfold/<gate>/prerun_notice.json`) listing every cell with
its status and reasons, publishes it with the freeze manifests, and refuses `open` (step 6) until the notice
exists and matches the pre-analysis record. The registration, the report's protocol section and the event log
all carry the same statuses before any sealed run of the cell. The final report copies the notice verbatim into
its first section, so a reader can see that the status preceded the data.

**4. The decision record (an ADR in the milestone in which it occurs).**
```
{ kill_gate: "KILL-TENFOLD-REACH", instance: "M3-partial", cell: {stratum, regime}, fired: bool,
  conditions: [{id: "i".."iv", value, threshold, holds}], ni_attainable, reachable_verdicts,
  repairs_tried: ["raise-H" | "enlarge-m"], outcome: "confirmatory" | "descriptive: unreachable",
  enlargement_plan: {next_gate, m_needed, families_to_author} | null, decided_by, decided_at, hash }
```
The records of one gate instance are the sections, one per proposed cell, of one decision ADR in the one form of
every kill-gate decision (N40-03): `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-REACH-M3.md` for the partial gate
(`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-REACH-M7.md` for the tournament), written at sealing step 5. The gate's row in
`registries/gates.toml` (N40-28: mode `exit`, `decided = "M3"`, `reread = ["M7"]`, `scope_kind = "cell"`)
receives one decision entry per proposed cell, `{exit = "M3", scope = "cell:<stratum>", outcome, adr, date,
session, receipts}`: `not-fired` for a cell named confirmatory, `fired` for `descriptive: unreachable`; a cell
never proposed (`descriptive: not-named`) is outside the gate and has no entry. `receipts` is the gate's
pre-analysis record (N39a-33) the reading rests on, committed byte-identical under `receipts/gates/` by this bead
so that its hash resolves (N40-01). The ADR carries the required fields of that form: the kill gate and the
milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendment; the four measured values per cell beside their
thresholds with the record's hash; the outcome; the fallback now in force (`descriptive`, with the enlargement
plan); the beads re-read; what changes downstream (step 6); and the recording session, which implemented none of
the mechanisms. The cross-check of N40-30 compares these entries with the row.

**5. The enlargement plan.** For a fired cell the record states the smallest m (and c) at which the conditions
would hold under the fitted model, the number of fresh sealed families that must be authored for the next gate,
and their estimated cost in device-hours (m·c·Σ over arms of the expected restricted time). The plan is an input
to the sealed-suite epic of the tournament.

**6. What changes downstream if it fires.** The verdict rule returns `descriptive` for the cell regardless of its
measured ratio. The cell does not count toward the limit of four confirmatory cells, so the Holm thresholds of
the remaining cells are computed with the smaller k. Nothing about the cell is cited for or against BET-13; the
bet can still be met on another stratum. If every proposed cell of a gate fires, the gate records ratios and no
verdict; the milestone still exits on the record, and the report states that the gate could not have confirmed
or withdrawn the claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every cell proposed as confirmatory has a decision record with the four measured values beside their
      thresholds, written before sealing step 6.
- [ ] `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-REACH-M3.md` exists with one section per proposed cell, and the row holds one M3
      entry per proposed cell with `scope = "cell:<stratum>"`, `not-fired` or `fired` as step 4 maps the
      outcome, and the pre-analysis record as a resolving receipt.
- [ ] A cell with P(`ten-fold` | 15) = 0.79 fires; 0.80 does not. A cell with horizon cap 9.9 fires unless the
      default deadline rule raises H; 10.0 does not.
- [ ] A four-family cell fires on condition (iii) and its record says the verdict is unreachable; a four-family
      confirmatory request made through the pre-analysis verb is refused with FROB-REF-532, and a six-family
      request at level 0.0125 is refused for the same reason.
- [ ] A cell with H ÷ RMT_A = 8 either has its deadline raised to reach 10 or opens as `descriptive`.
- [ ] `open` is refused without the pre-run notice, and the notice equals the pre-analysis record's statuses.
- [ ] The report's protocol section lists each `descriptive` cell with the failed condition, and that section's
      digest predates the first sealed run.
- [ ] The scorer returns `descriptive` for a fired cell whatever its data, and for a cell with no recorded
      pre-analysis.
- [ ] After a cell fires, k for the remaining cells is reduced and their Holm thresholds are recomputed in the
      record before the suite opens (never after).
- [ ] No repair other than the two listed is accepted; a record with changed thresholds is refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- kill_reach::`
- `boundary_i`: 0.79 → fired; 0.80 → not.
- `boundary_ii`: P(`withdrawn` | 5) = 0.05 → not fired; 0.051 → fired.
- `boundary_iv`: 9.9 with adjudicator-fixed H → fired; with the default rule → H raised by 1,800 s steps until
  ≥ 10, then not fired.
- `unreachable_guard`: m = 4 → fired, reason names the tail 1/16.
- `ni_unattainable`: m = 24 under the rule as written → fired, reason names the best bound −0.1174.
- `enlarge_once`: m = 12 fails (i), 24 families exist → rerun at 24, then decided; a second enlargement is refused.
- `k_recomputed_before_open`: four proposed, one fires → k = 3, thresholds 0.016667, 0.025, 0.05.
- `levels_fixed_after_recount`: a cell voided after sealing does not relax the remaining cells' levels.
- `guard_reachability_table`: the critical counts 5 of 5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12, 12 of 16, 17 of 24, 22 of 32
  at 0.05; none at m = 4; none at m = 6 for 0.0125; 18 of 24 at 0.0125.
- `horizon_cap_reading`: 14,400 ÷ 1,800 = 8 fails; 14,400 ÷ 1,200 = 12 passes.
- `fired_cell_scores_descriptive`: the scorer's verdict for a fired cell is `descriptive` on any data.
- `notice_required_for_open`.
- `decision_entry_form`: a confirmatory cell → `not-fired`, a `descriptive: unreachable` cell → `fired`, a
  not-named cell → no entry; each entry scoped `cell:<stratum>` with `adr = "docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-REACH-M3.md"`
  and the pre-analysis record's hash as its receipt, and a scratch copy of the register loads them.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_kill_reach.sh`: on a rehearsal gate with three proposed cells (`synthetic` development
ledgers arranged so that one passes, one has a horizon cap of 9.6 under a fixed H, one has four families), runs
the pre-analysis and this decision, writes the notice, and attempts `open` before and after
(`tenfold.open-preconditions` before). It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_kill_reach/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, per-cell condition
values with thresholds, repairs tried, outcomes, the recomputed Holm thresholds, the notice hash, each refusal
with its reason, on a failed assertion the first differing decision-record field with the record path, and the
summary naming KILL-TENFOLD-REACH as decided for all three cells.

The same decision is exercised in the partial gate's rehearsal `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12),
whose step `seal` records the pre-analysis before `open` and whose plant `four-family`, a sub-step of its step
`drills`, has the confirmatory cell refused as unreachable; and in the tournament rule's pilot
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_decision_rule_pilot.sh` (N39b-16), which logs each reachability condition as an assertion
with its threshold and refuses the four-family cell with `FROB-REF-532`. The pilot runs no campaign: that the
descriptive opening precedes the first sealed campaign is asserted by this bead's script (`open` refused until
the notice exists) and read from the gate's event log by the GATE-TENFOLD-04 record (N39a-40, clause 4).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the critical counts of the guard listed above; the planning table's rows as inputs: m = 24 with
spread 0.8 has P(`ten-fold` | 15) = 0.98 and P(`withdrawn` | 5) = 0.005 (does not fire on (i) or (ii)); m = 6 has
0.57 at 15 and 0.08 at 5 (fires on conditions (i) and (ii)); m = 12 has 0.89 at 15 and 0.03 at 5 (does not fire on (i) or (ii)); m = 4 is
unreachable. Negative controls: a four-family confirmatory cell (registry entry TF-CX-07); a status changed from
`descriptive` to confirmatory after the suite opened (the sealing ledger's order refutes it); a notice or a
descriptive announcement dated after the first sealed campaign (it must be a finding of the gate auditor); a cell
quietly dropped from the report instead of being marked `descriptive`.

## Considerations

- Expect this kill gate to fire often at M3: the `envelope` and `sensing` cells are confirmatory only "if their
  pre-analyses allow it", and development data on twelve missions gives a wide interval for the spread.
- With the completion non-inferiority condition as written, every cell under 59 families fires (N39a-31); the
  owner's decision there determines whether the partial gate has any confirmatory cell at all.
- Firing is not failure. It is the program declining to spend a sealed suite on a question the suite cannot
  answer, and saying so first.
- A kill outcome is not re-tested on the same suite.
- Risk (register): the tournament cannot confirm a true ratio of ten for lack of power. This gate does not create
  power; it stops the program from promising a verdict it cannot return.
- Raising H to satisfy the horizon cap raises the worst-case cost of the cell in proportion (m·c·arms·H).
- A cell opened as descriptive still costs its full compute; the owner may prefer to drop it, which is recorded
  as `not run` with the reason.
