## Background

One planning pilot behind this program measured a specialized solver against a composed one and found ratios of
1.345 warm and 1.088 cold, missing its speed gate. A later measurement showed why it could never have passed:
the faster arm's batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch (parsing, assembly, checking,
serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost could have reached about 1.46 at most
(842.8 ÷ 575.7). A ceiling computed first would have refused the test (FACT about the planning record).

The rule that followed (ADR-GAUNT-05, ceiling first): **a speed test is run only after its ceiling has been
computed from a measured ledger of the rival.** Each ceiling is arithmetic, and each exists because a tempting
comparison ignores it.

| # | Ceiling | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Removing a phase | If a phase holds the share p of the rival's restricted time and a mechanism removes it entirely, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − p). Stated on what remains, q = 1 − p, it is 1/q. Removing half the loop gives 2; removing 85% gives 6.7; ten needs p ≥ 0.9 |
| 2 | Improving a part | If the share f is improved by the factor r and the mechanism adds overhead h, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h). The three terms are measured on the rival's critical path |
| 3 | Unremovable cost | If arm A's time has a floor u that no mechanism removes (authoring, the common agent's turns, adjudication, checking, serialization), then R ≤ RMT_B ÷ u. When u exceeds one tenth of the rival's time, a ratio of ten needs another stage changed or the claim changed |
| 4 | Completion | If arm A misses the share q of deadlines, RMT_A ≥ q·H and RMT_B ≤ H, so R ≤ 1/q. With 12% unfinished the ratio cannot exceed 25/3 ≈ 8.33 |
| 5 | Common latency | With the same agent latency t per turn in both arms and a rival cost C per decision, deciding one design per turn gives at most (C + t)/t |
| 6 | The horizon | R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A, because the rival's restricted time cannot exceed H |

Here arm A is FrankenRobotics, arm B the strongest composed rival, R the ratio RMT_B ÷ RMT_A of restricted mean
times, and H a stratum's deadline. The phase ledger (thirteen exclusive phases per run, summing to the clock) is
the only input the ceilings accept, and only runs whose ledger is closed (at most 2% unattributed) may be used.

## Scope

In: the six calculators in exact arithmetic over ledger records; which arm's ledger each reads; the ceiling
record; the binding ceiling; the report command. Out: registration and refusal of speed comparisons, the funding
rule and the order rule (N39a-38); the kill decision per lever (N39a-39); removable shares per lever (N39a-26).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs (`score/ceiling.rs`).** A set of `ledger-closed` ledgers of the rival for one cell (a stratum and a
regime), and, where they exist, arm A's ledgers for the same cell. Shares are pooled: share(phase) =
Σ exclusive[phase] ÷ Σ clock over the rival's closed runs. A request that includes a `ledger-open` run, or a run
with a cap-breach remainder, is refused (`ceiling-needs-closed-ledger`); a request with no rival ledger is refused
(`ceiling-without-baseline`).

**2. The calculators (each returns an exact rational and the inputs it used).**
1. `remove_phases(P)`: p = Σ_{phase ∈ P} share_B(phase); ceiling = 1/(1 − p). P = every phase but the unremovable
   ones gives the outer bound of the cell.
2. `improve_part(f, r, h)`: 1/(1 − f + f/r + h), with f a share of the rival's ledger (a phase set, so f is read,
   not asserted), r > 1 the improvement factor measured on that part, and h ≥ 0 the added overhead as a share of
   the rival's time. r = ∞ reduces to 1/(1 − f + h).
3. `unremovable_floor(F)`: u = arm A's pooled restricted time spent in the floor phase set F (default
   F = {`author`, `propose`, `adjudicate`}), per run; ceiling = RMT_B ÷ u. Before arm A exists, u is an estimate
   built from the rival's ledger (its `author` time, its per-turn agent latency times a stated minimum number of
   turns, and its `adjudicate` time), labelled `estimated: from rival`. The record flags
   `floor_exceeds_tenth: true` when u > RMT_B ÷ 10.
4. `completion(q)`: 1/q with q arm A's share of missed deadlines in the cell (from campaign records); for q = 0
   the ceiling is `unbounded`, and the exact upper bound on q at zero misses is printed beside it. On given rival
   times the sharper form Σ_B ÷ (misses × H) is also returned.
5. `common_latency()`: t = the rival's pooled `propose` time ÷ agent turns; C = (the rival's pooled clock −
   `propose`) ÷ designs decided; ceiling = (C + t)/t. It applies to a candidate that still decides one design per
   agent turn.
6. `horizon()`: H ÷ RMT_A (arm A's restricted mean time on the cell; on the development suite before sealed
   runs exist).

**3. Which ledger.** Ceilings 1, 2 and 5 read the **rival's** ledger; ceilings 3, 4 and 6 need arm A's times
against the rival's clock. A ceiling for phases computed on arm A's own shares is a different number and is
refused under these names (`ceiling-wrong-arm`): on the worked pair, arm A's `train` share is 10%, which would
"prove" that removing training gains 1.11.

**4. The ceiling record.**
```
{ gate, cell, rival: "B" | "C", ledgers_used: ["b3:…"], ledgers_excluded: [{hash, reason}],
  shares: {phase → rational}, ceilings: [{id: 1..6, inputs: {…}, value: {"rational", "decimal"}, basis:
  "measured" | "estimated: from rival"}], binding: {id, value}, per_lever: [{lever, phases, p_max, ceiling}],
  hash }
```
Ceilings 1 and 2 bound the effect of one named mechanism and are computed per request. Ceilings 3 to 6 bound the
cell's ratio R itself. `binding` is the smallest of ceilings 3, 4 and 6, and of ceiling 5 only when its premise
holds for arm A (`one design per agent turn`, read from arm A's designs decided per turn); the report of a
`measured` cell names it. `per_lever` applies calculator 1 to each lever's phase set.

**5. Worked values (unit-test fixtures; all illustrative arithmetic).**
- *The single-pair ledger* (rival clock 9,000 s with `train` 4,920; arm A clock 1,500 s with `author` 600,
  `propose` 360, `adjudicate` 90; rival `propose` 1,080 over 12 turns, 12 designs decided): the pair's ratio is
  6.0. Removing training: 9,000 ÷ 4,080 = 2.206. Unremovable floor: u = 1,050 s, 11.7% of the rival's clock, so
  the cold ratio on this mission cannot exceed 9,000 ÷ 1,050 = 8.571 whatever the engine does. `check` is 2.67%
  of the rival's loop: removing only it is worth at most 1/0.9733 = 1.03. Common latency: t = 90 s,
  C = 7,920 ÷ 12 = 660 s, ceiling 750/90 = 8.33. Horizon: 14,400 ÷ 1,500 = 9.6.
- *The six-family card*: one miss in six, so R ≤ 1/q = 6 on any rival times and ≤ 54,000 ÷ 14,400 = 3.75 on these.
- *General*: removing half gives 2; 85% gives 6.67; f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02 gives 1/0.07425 = 13.47.

**6. Command.** `frob-tenfold-score ceiling --cell … [--floor-phases …]` writes the record;
`frob-tenfold ceiling report` renders it as a table with the binding ceiling first.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The worked values of step 5 are reproduced exactly as rationals (2.206 = 75/34; 8.571 = 60/7; 8.33 = 25/3;
      3.75 = 15/4; 13.47 = 4000/297).
- [ ] An open ledger in the input set is refused; a ceiling with no rival ledger is refused.
- [ ] A phase ceiling requested on arm A's shares under ceiling 1 is refused.
- [ ] `floor_exceeds_tenth` is true on the worked pair (11.7%).
- [ ] The binding ceiling of the worked pair is the unremovable floor, 60/7 = 8.571: ceiling 4 is unbounded (no
      miss), ceiling 6 is 9.6, and ceiling 5 (25/3) is listed with its premise and does not bind an arm that
      decides many designs per turn.
- [ ] Records are D0: identical bytes across platforms.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- score::ceiling::`
- `remove_phase`: p = 1/2 → 2; p = 17/20 → 20/3; p = 9/10 → 10; p = 41/75 → 75/34.
- `ten_needs_ninety_percent`: the smallest p with ceiling ≥ 10 is 9/10.
- `improve_part`: (0.97, 40, 0.02) → 4000/297 ≈ 13.47; (0.85, ∞, 0) → 20/3; with h = 0.02 no r reaches 10 unless
  f ≥ 0.92 (f = 0.91, r = ∞ → 1/0.11 = 9.09; f = 0.92 → 10).
- `unremovable_floor`: 9,000 ÷ 1,050 = 60/7; flag true.
- `completion`: q = 3/25 → 25/3; q = 1/6 → 6; sharper form 54,000 ÷ 14,400 = 15/4; q = 0 → `unbounded` with the
  zero-count bound printed.
- `common_latency`: C = 300, t = 60 → 6; t = 120 → 7/2; C = 410 → 47/6 and 53/12; the worked pair → 25/3.
- `horizon`: 14,400 ÷ 1,500 = 48/5.
- `pilot_residual`: full 842.8 µs against a residual of 575.7 µs → 8428/5757 ≈ 1.464; the measured 1.345 is below
  it and the goal the pilot set is above it.
- `wrong_arm_refused`, `open_ledger_refused`, `no_baseline_refused`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_ceilings.sh` (`bead=N39a-37 milestone=M1`): writes the worked pair and the six-family card as
`synthetic` closed ledgers plus one open ledger, runs the six calculators and the report, and compares every
value. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, each ceiling's inputs and value as a rational and a decimal
beside the expected value (tolerance: exact), the binding ceiling, each refusal, and the summary naming
GATE-TENFOLD-09 and GATE-TENFOLD-03 as touched. On the Linux host after the M1 baseline exists, a second step
runs the report on the measured rival ledgers and logs the shares and ceilings as metrics with no assertion.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers of the harness's own set: the single-pair ledger (6.0; training removed 2.206; unremovable floor
8.571), which guards against a ceiling computed on the wrong arm's ledger; the completion ceiling (12% unfinished
caps R at 25/3), which guards against a ratio claimed above its ceiling. Registry rows: KA-009 (completion cap),
KA-012 (one design per agent turn: (300 + t)/t is 3.5 to 6 for t between 60 and 120 s, and 4.4 to 7.8 against
410 s), KA-014 and KA-809 (the general bound: 13.5 at f = 0.97, r = 40, h = 0.02; infinite acceleration of 85%
cannot reach ten), KA-812 (the pilot residual). Counterexamples: CX-004 and CX-375 (a faster environment step:
at most 1/(1 − 0.87) ≈ 7.7 on one stack and 1.4 from physics on another), CX-005, CX-007, CX-008, CX-378.
Negative controls: a ceiling on arm A's ledger; a ceiling from an open ledger; ceiling 2 with f asserted above
the ledger's share.

## Considerations

- A ceiling is an upper bound under its stated premise. Ceiling 5 binds only a candidate that decides one design
  per turn (which is why the program's first lever is to decide many); ceiling 3's floor shrinks if a mechanism
  reduces the number of agent turns. The record states each premise beside each value.
- The M1 application uses the rival's ledger; the M2 re-read uses our own profile; a measured ledger in which
  training is a much smaller share than published lowers every ceiling, and the thesis is rewritten before work
  is funded against it if the measured ceilings are under 3 for every mission class.
- Risk named in the program register: the denominator of the ten-fold ratio is wrong (incumbents are faster, or
  their loop is shorter, than the composite of published figures); these calculators are how that shows up as
  numbers.
