## Background

The program's speed claim is a product of mechanisms, each of which shortens some part of a design loop. Two
habits make such a product dishonest. One is funding a mechanism as a speed lever when the part it touches is a
small share of the loop: if that share is under 10%, removing it entirely gains at most 1/0.9 = 1.11. The other
is multiplying factors that were measured on overlapping time: a pruning ratio multiplied by a speed-up measured
on work the pruning already removed. The ten-fold protocol answers both with rules applied to a measured ledger
of the rival, and with a gate.

**The funding rule.** A mechanism whose removable cost is under 10% of the measured loop cannot be funded as a
speed lever: by the phase-removal ceiling its whole effect is at most 1/0.9 = 1.11. It may still be funded for
what it does to trust, coverage or correctness, with that reason written in its work package and no speed figure
attached (KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER). The rule is applied to the rival's ledger at M1 and again with our own profile at
M2.

**The order rule.** A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work the pruning already
removed. Each factor in a product of levers is measured on the time that the earlier factors left.

**The refusal.** A registered speed comparison records p (a removable share) or (f, r, h) (a share, its
improvement factor and the added overhead) from a `ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline, and is refused when
its ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show.

**GATE-TENFOLD-09 (claim kind: Performance; TARGET; first at M1, then re-run at every milestone exit).** Ceiling
before speed, program-wide: every work package funded as a speed lever cites a removable share of at least 10% on
a `ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline, and every registered speed comparison records its ceiling and is
refused when the ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show. If the gate is not met, the speed comparison is
refused and the work package loses its speed justification.

## Technical approach

**1. The ceiling attachment (`tenfold/ceilings/<comparison-id>.json`).** Every registered speed comparison (an
experiment whose claimed quantity is a ratio of times or rates between a mechanism and a baseline) must carry:
```
{ comparison: "<id>", goal_ratio: "3/2", lever: 1..8 | "other", work_package: "WP-nn",
  baseline: {arm: "B" | "<named strong baseline>", ledgers: ["b3:…"], all_closed: true},
  basis: {kind: "remove", phases: [...], p: "…"} | {kind: "improve", phases: [...], f: "…", r: "…", h: "…"},
  ceiling: {"rational": "…", "decimal": "…"}, margin: ceiling ÷ goal_ratio,
  earlier_factors: ["<comparison-id>", …], time_left_after_earlier: "…", admitted: bool, hash }
```
- `baseline` is the *strong* baseline: the strongest warm or cached configuration, frozen after tuning on
  development fixtures only. Its ledgers must be `ledger-closed`.
- p and f are **read** from the baseline's pooled shares over the named phases; a registration that asserts a
  larger share than the ledger shows is refused (`lever-share-above-ledger`).
- `ceiling` is computed by the calculators: 1/(1 − p) for `remove`; 1/(1 − f + f/r + h) for `improve`. Where
  they apply, the attachment also records the other ceilings of the calculators (N39a-37) in an optional
  `other_ceilings` list: an unremovable floor u gives R ≤ RMT_baseline ÷ u; a missed-deadline share q gives
  R ≤ 1/q; the horizon H gives R ≤ H ÷ RMT_ours. The binding `ceiling` is the smallest of those recorded.
- `goal_ratio` is the ratio the comparison sets out to show (DESIGN: this is the one name of that field on a
  comparison; the gate bead N40-18 reads it under this name, and the funding record of N00-09 has its own field
  `claimed` for the ratio a work package sets out to show). `margin` is the quotient ceiling ÷ goal_ratio, at
  least 1 for an admitted comparison (DESIGN: a quotient and not a difference, because ceilings and goals are
  ratios; N40-18 reports the smallest margin in this sense).

**2. The refusal.** `frob-tenfold ceiling register` sets `admitted: false` and exits non-zero (exit 3) when: no
ceiling attachment can be computed, because the baseline ledger is `ledger-open`
(`ceiling-needs-closed-ledger`), there is no rival ledger (`ceiling-without-baseline`) or the shares were read
from arm A's ledger (`ceiling-wrong-arm`); or ceiling < goal_ratio (`ceiling-below-goal`). The reasons are those
of the ten-fold refusal registry (N39-REF), which also holds `lever-share-above-ledger` and
`order-rule-violation` used below; this bead and N40-18 use these names and no others. In the product's
vocabulary every one of them is FROB-REF-866 (a speed experiment requested without its computed ceiling, or with
a ceiling below its goal);
ranked repairs: (1) lower the goal to at most the ceiling and state it; (2) register a different mechanism that
acts on a larger share; (3) fund the work for trust, coverage or correctness with no speed figure. An experiment
without an admitted attachment cannot be cited by any gate.

**3. The funding-rule check (`frob-tenfold ceiling funding-check`).** Input: the admitted funding records of
N00-09 whose `justification` is `speed-lever`, each with its `lever`, its `ledger_phase`, its `baseline_record`
and its cited `removable_share`; the baseline's pooled shares. (N00-09's admission check applies the 10% rule to
one record when it is registered, against the share the record cites; this sweep re-reads every record against
the shares the closed ledgers actually show, which is what the gate's clause A measures.)
1. For each such package: the cited share p must come from a closed baseline ledger named by hash, must not
   exceed the ledger's share of the lever's phases, and must be at least 1/10.
2. A package with p < 1/10 is reported `not-fundable-as-speed-lever` with its ceiling 1/(1 − p); its record must
   then either drop the speed justification or state a non-speed reason and carry no speed figure. The non-speed
   justifications are those of the funding record: `trust`, `coverage` and `correctness`, which the kill rule
   names, and `parity`, which the record also has for work needed to match the incumbent.
3. A package marked `speed-lever` with no cited share fails the check.
Output: one row per package: `{work_package, lever, phases, p_cited, p_ledger, ceiling, status}`.

**4. The order-rule check (`frob-tenfold ceiling order-check <record>`).** Any record that multiplies lever
effects (a thesis table, a work-package
justification, a report sentence generated from attachments) must list its factors in order with, for each, the
time base it was measured on. The check recomputes: time_left_0 = 1; for factor i with share f_i *of the time
left by the earlier factors* and improvement r_i: time_left_i = time_left_{i−1}·(1 − f_i + f_i/r_i + h_i). The
product is 1 ÷ time_left_last. A factor whose f_i was measured on the original loop while an earlier factor
already removed part of that phase is refused (`order-rule-violation`), detected when the phases of factor i
intersect phases an earlier `remove` factor took, or when Σ of shares referred to the original loop exceeds 1.
The smallest case: 100 candidates of 10 s each; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors are the hard ones and
accelerate by 2, not by the 5 measured on the full set: the result is 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50.

**5. Measuring the gate.** At M1, and again at every milestone exit (the exit's run is started by the gate's
recurring part, N40-18, which then evaluates the record written here):
- *Clause A (work packages).* Run the funding check against the current funding record and the rival's closed
  ledgers of record (the M1 agent-driven baseline; from M2 also our own profile). Value: counts of speed-lever
  packages passing, failing and re-justified. Pass iff none fails.
- *Clause B (comparisons).* For every registered speed comparison in the experiment registry: an attachment
  exists, is admitted, and its ceiling ≥ its goal; and for every comparison that was refused, no result from it
  is cited anywhere. Value: counts of comparisons admitted, refused, and cited-while-refused (must be 0).
- *Controls.* The planted negative controls below, each caught, and the controls the control registry lists for
  GATE-TENFOLD-09, run through the control runner of N38b-04 (`FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p
  frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations --test <tier named by the gate's record> -- GATE-TENFOLD-09`),
  which writes each control's line (`caught`, `missed` or `stale`) in the run receipt; a control's `last_seen` is
  written by the gate recorder when the part is recorded (N38b-19), not here. They are the controls seeded from
  CX-375 (planted input `phase-ceiling-below-goal`: an experiment registered for 10× from removing the
  environment step, whose ceiling is 1/(1 − 0.87) ≈ 7.7, must be refused with FROB-REF-866, not admitted;
  plants by N36-04 and N36-05), CX-376 (mutation `pruning-times-speedup`: the bound must report 20, not 50, on the
  pruning example; N36-05), CX-377 (planted input `family-batch-no-switch-cost`: a mixed-batch experiment
  registered with no measured incumbent switch cost must be refused, its ceiling being uncomputable; N36-05),
  CX-378 (mutation `ceiling-recorded-after`: a ceiling recorded after the measurement it bounds must be rejected;
  N36-05 at M0) and TF-CX-03 (mutation `wrong_arm_ceiling`: a ceiling computed on arm A's ledger, 1.11 for
  removing training where the rival's ledger gives 75/34 = 2.206, must be refused with `ceiling-wrong-arm`;
  N39a-37). A control the runner reports `missed` or `stale` makes the measurement fail, whatever the clauses say
  (the runner's rule).
The measured record (schema `frob.tenfold.gate09/1`, one per measurement, written by `frob-tenfold ceiling measure
--at <milestone>` after it has run both clauses and read the control runner's receipt; DESIGN: this is the only
record of the gate's measured values, and the register row of N40-18 cites it by hash): `{gate: "GATE-TENFOLD-09", at: "M1" |
"<exit>", baseline_ledgers, clause_a: {checked, passing, failing, rejustified, rows: [...]}, clause_b: {checked,
admitted, refused, cited_while_refused, smallest_margin, attached_after_first_receipt}, controls: [...], verdict,
recorded_by, hash}`. `attached_after_first_receipt` counts comparisons whose attachment is later than their first
run receipt; it must be 0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A comparison whose ceiling is below its goal is refused with the three ranked repairs; the planted pilot
      case (goal above 1.46, ceiling 8428/5757 ≈ 1.464 for a zero-cost solver) is refused for any goal above its
      ceiling and admitted for a goal of 1.3.
- [ ] A work package citing a removable share of 2.67% (the `check` phase of the worked ledger) is reported
      `not-fundable-as-speed-lever` with ceiling 1.03; one citing 54.67% (`train`) passes.
- [ ] A cited share above the ledger's share is refused; a share read from an open ledger is refused.
- [ ] The order-rule check returns 20 for the pruning example and refuses the product 10 × 5.
- [ ] The measured record `frob.tenfold.gate09/1` exists at M1 with both clauses measured on the rival's ledgers
      of record, written by a session that did not implement this bead, and the measurement is callable by the
      exit reviews; this bead writes no row of `registries/gates.toml` (the parts are recorded under N40-18).
- [ ] No refused comparison's result is cited by any gate record (query over the registry).
- [ ] The measured record's `controls` lists the five registered controls of GATE-TENFOLD-09 (from CX-375, CX-376,
      CX-377, CX-378 and TF-CX-03) with `caught: true` from the control runner's receipt at the measurement's
      commit, beside the bead's own planted controls.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- ceiling_gate::`
- `refuse_below_goal`: ceiling 75/34 (remove `train` on the worked ledger), goal 3 → refused
  (`ceiling-below-goal`); goal 2 → admitted.
- `pilot_ceiling`: 842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.4640 (four places); goal 2.0 → refused; goal 1.3 → admitted.
- `remove_phase`: p = 0.10 → 1.111; p = 0.90 → 10. `improve_part`: (f, r, h) = (0.6, 3, 0.1) →
  1/(0.4 + 0.2 + 0.1) = 1.4286.
- `ceiling_before_first_receipt`: a comparison whose attachment is later than its first run receipt is counted
  in `attached_after_first_receipt` and clause B fails.
- `share_must_be_read`: registration asserts f = 0.9 for `train` where the ledger shows 41/75 → refused.
- `funding_threshold`: p = 1/10 → fundable; p = 99/1000 → not; ceiling at 1/10 is 10/9 = 1.11.
- `non_speed_reason`: a package with p = 0.04 and reason `trust` and no speed figure passes; with a speed figure
  fails.
- `order_rule_pruning`: 20, and the 50 refused.
- `order_rule_sequence`: remove 50% (factor 2), then improve 60% *of what is left* by 3 → time left
  0.5 × (0.4 + 0.2) = 0.3 → 3.33; the same second factor referred to the original loop is refused.
- `cited_while_refused`: a gate record citing a refused comparison → clause B fails.
- `open_baseline_refused`: a baseline ledger with 3% unattributed → `ceiling-needs-closed-ledger`;
  `missing_baseline_refused` → `ceiling-without-baseline`; `wrong_arm_refused` → `ceiling-wrong-arm`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_gate09_ceiling.sh` (header: `bead=N39a-38 milestone=M1 gates=GATE-TENFOLD-09`). Steps:
(1) load the worked rival ledger as a `synthetic` baseline (and, when present, the measured M1 rival ledgers);
(2) register five comparisons: two admissible, one with a goal above its ceiling, one with an asserted share, one
on an open ledger; (3) run the funding check on a planted funding record with eight packages, one per lever;
(4) run the order-rule check on the pruning example; (5) run the registered controls of GATE-TENFOLD-09 through
the control runner (the `cargo test` command of "Measuring the gate" above) and read its receipt; (6) write the
measured record with `frob-tenfold ceiling measure --at M1`. It sources
`scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/tenfold_gate09_ceiling/<utc>.ndjson` with a
transcript beside it; every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the exact command line, exit code
and wall time. Each control is logged with its NC number, its seed (CX-375 to CX-378, TF-CX-03), the expected
observation and `caught`. Each registration is logged with
its goal, basis, ceiling as a rational and a decimal, margin and admission; each refusal with its reason and
first repair; the funding table row by row with p beside the 10% threshold; the gate's two clause values; and a
summary naming GATE-TENFOLD-09 and KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER as touched. Exit non-zero if any planted control is missed.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 1/0.9 = 1.11; the worked ledger's lever bounds (levers 3, 4 and 7 under 10%: 4%, 6.67%, 6.67%);
the pruning example (KA-811: 20, with the product 50 as its control); the pilot (KA-812: 1.345 warm, 1.088 cold,
ceiling about 1.46). Counterexamples: "optimize the component first and measure the gain afterwards" (CX-378);
"a pruning ratio multiplies with a device speedup" (CX-376); "multiply a pruning ratio by a GPU speedup"
(CX-007). Negative controls the gate must catch: a comparison registered after its result was measured (the
attachment's ledger sequence follows the result's); a speed-lever package with no cited share; a ceiling computed
on arm A's ledger; a refused comparison quoted in a report; and the registered controls of the gate, seeded from
CX-375, CX-376, CX-377, CX-378 and TF-CX-03 (step 5).

## Considerations

- KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER is the kill rule this gate feeds (decision bead N39a-39): a lever under 10% is struck from
  the speed case before M2 begins.
- At M1 the only measured ledger is the rival's; our own profile exists from M2, and the gate is then read on
  both. A share can be above 10% on the rival's ledger and below it on ours (or the reverse); both are recorded.
- The 10% threshold is a DESIGN number. It does not say a small-share mechanism is worthless; it says its value
  must be argued on other grounds.
- This gate is program-wide and recurring. The register row, its part at each exit and the verdict over this
  bead's record are the program-gate bead N40-18's; the exit review of each milestone re-runs this bead's script
  through that part.
