## Background

The program's thesis names eight levers that together are supposed to shorten the time from a robot-design
question to an independently accepted answer: (1) decide many designs per agent turn; (2) train few; (3) spend
where it matters; (4) find failures instead of waiting for them; (5) answer over families instead of points;
(6) do not recompute or re-decide; (7) explain by intervention; (8) short setup. The ranges attached to them were
composed from published figures of different incumbent stacks, which is not a measurement of any loop. The first
two milestones therefore measure the rival's loop by phase on the program's own hardware, and this kill gate
applies the consequence.

**KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER.** *Trigger:* a mechanism's removable cost is under 10% of the measured loop. *Fallback,
named in advance:* it is not funded as a speed lever; it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with
that reason written and no speed figure. *Decided:* at M1 on the rival's ledger; at M2 on ours.

The arithmetic behind the number: if a phase holds the share p of the rival's restricted time and a mechanism
removes it entirely, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − p); at p = 0.10 that is 1.11. The baseline rule of the
protocol states the action: every lever receives its removable share p on the measured ledger, and a lever with p
under 0.10 is struck from the speed case before M2 begins.

A lever can only save time that is there: the reading is on the rival's ledger, because time the rival does not
spend cannot be saved. This decision is the funding half of the program rule "ceiling before speed"
(GATE-TENFOLD-09: every work package funded as a speed lever cites a removable share of at least 10% on a
`ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline, and every registered speed comparison records its ceiling and is
refused when the ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show). The rule's origin is a planning pilot in which
a specialized solver was measured at 1.345 times a composed one on a test whose ceiling, computed afterwards from
a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch, was about 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7): the test could not have
confirmed its claim.

What the baseline measurement is allowed to change: the ranking of the levers, the funding of work packages, the
deadlines of the strata, and the expected ranges per mission class. It is not allowed to change the estimand or
the outcome rules.

This is a decision bead. It evaluates a written trigger on recorded values, per lever and per mission class,
and records each outcome as an architecture decision record.

## Scope

In: the per-lever, per-class evaluation at M1; the decision records; the strike list and its effect on work
packages; the `justification` of each affected work package's funding record; the first application of
GATE-TENFOLD-09 to funding; the hand-off of the M2 re-read; what is and is not allowed to change. Out: measuring
the rival's ledger (seed BD-143 and the parity baseline seeds); computing shares and ceilings (N39a-26, N39a-37);
the funding record, its schema and admission check (N00-09); the gate that checks funding records against this
decision at every exit and refuses individual speed comparisons (N39a-38);
the re-read at M2 on our own profile with the thesis arithmetic redone (N39b-10). This is the only decision bead
of KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER at M1.

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** The closed ledgers of the agent-driven rival loop on at least 12 development missions of the
`legged-quad` and `wheeled` strata (the M1 baseline); the lever-to-phase table; the pooled shares and the bound
p_max(ℓ) for each lever ℓ (the share of the rival's time in the phases the lever acts on), per stratum class and
per regime (cold, warm), each with its interval, on closed ledgers only; each speed-lever work package's cited
share.

**2. Lever-to-phase table (the bound each lever is judged on; read from `lever_phases.toml` of N39a-26, the one
statement of it, and printed here).** 1 → `propose`, `screen`; 2 → `train`;
3 → `screen`, `evaluate`; 4 → `diagnose`; 5 → `certify`, `propose`; 6 → `check`, `compile`, `evaluate`;
7 → `diagnose`; 8 → `compile`, `author`.

**3. Decision procedure (per lever ℓ and per mission class with a measured ledger; numbered; the treatment of the
repeat range in steps 2 to 4 is a DESIGN reading of "p under 0.10" for a share that is measured with noise).**
1. Compute p_max(ℓ) on the class's pooled closed ledgers, with its repeat interval: the baseline repeats the
   round, and the share's range over repeats is recorded.
2. If the upper end of that range is below 1/10: the kill gate **fires** for (ℓ, class). The lever is struck from
   the speed case for that class.
3. If the lower end is at or above 1/10: it does not fire.
4. If the range straddles 1/10: it does not fire at M1 and is flagged `marginal`; it is re-read at M2 on our own
   profile, where a point value below 1/10 fires.
5. A lever is struck from the speed case *overall* only if it is struck for every class measured. A lever kept
   for one class keeps its work packages, with the speed justification restricted to that class.
6. For each struck (ℓ, class): every work package that cites lever ℓ as a speed justification for that class must,
   before M2 begins, either remove the speed figure and state a non-speed reason (`trust`, `coverage`,
   `correctness`) or be re-planned. The list of such packages is part of the record.

The status of a lever on a class is one of `funded-as-speed` (not fired), `struck` (fired) and `marginal`. A
share with a degenerate range is read at its point value: p = 0.10 exactly is funded; p = 0.0999 is struck. A
`marginal` lever keeps its speed funding until the M2 re-read and is flagged in every work package that cites it.

**Order rule.** A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work the pruning already removed:
in any product of levers each factor is measured on the time the earlier factors left. The record therefore
states, besides each lever's own share, the shares that overlap (`propose` under levers 1 and 5, `compile` under
levers 6 and 8, `screen` under 1 and 3, `evaluate` under 3 and 6, `diagnose` under 4 and 7) and forbids adding
them: levers 1 and 5 together remove at most `propose` + `screen` + `certify`, not twice `propose`.

**Funding reasons.** Every open work package has a funding record of N00-09 (`frob.funding/1`), whose field
`justification` is the funding reason and is read here under that name: either `speed-lever` with its `lever`,
its cited `removable_share`, its `ledger_phase` and its `baseline_record` (the ledger digest), or one of
`trust`, `coverage`, `correctness` (the values the kill rule names) or `parity` with a written `reason` and no
speed figure. A package citing a struck lever loses `speed-lever` for that class: when the lever is struck for
every class it is re-registered under a non-speed justification through N00-09's admission check; when it is
struck for some classes only, it is re-registered with the struck classes removed from the funding record's
`classes` field (N00-09's check g refuses a listed class on which this decision record struck the lever, quoting
the record's hash), and this record's `work_packages` action names the classes removed. A package with no
funding record is not funded.

**Fail-closed posture.** Until this decision is recorded, no work package is funded as a speed lever.

**4. The decision record (one per lever; one ADR at M1; the M2 re-read writes a new ADR beside it and leaves this
one as it is).**
```
{ kill_gate: "KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER", at: "M1", lever: 3, phases: ["screen", "evaluate"],
  by_class: [ { class: "legged-quad", ledgers: ["b3:…"], p_max: "1/25", range: ["…", "…"],
                ceiling: "25/24", fired: true | false, marginal: bool } ],
  struck_overall: bool, work_packages: [ { wp: "WP-nn", action: "speed figure removed; reason: trust" |
  "re-planned" | "kept" } ], trigger_text, fallback_text, decided_at, decided_by, hash }
```
The record states the measured values beside the trigger whichever way it went.

The eight records are sections of one decision ADR in the one form every kill-gate decision has (N40-03):
`docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER-M1.md`. The register row of this gate (N40-28: mode `exit`, decided M1,
`reread = ["M2"]`, no scope kind) takes one decision entry `{exit = "M1", outcome, adr, date, session, receipts}`,
the first of its `decision` array (the M2 re-read appends its own, N39b-10). DESIGN, for a
row whose trigger is evaluated on eight levers: the entry's `outcome` is `fired` when at least one (lever, class)
is struck and `not-fired` when none is; `marginal` levers do not make it `fired` and are carried to the row's M2
re-read, not written as `deferred`. `receipts` are the gate receipts of the M1 rival ledgers the shares were read
from, at least one in either case. The ADR carries the required fields of that form: the kill gate and the
milestone; the trigger as registered with any amendment; each measured share with its receipt hash beside 0.10;
the outcome; the fallback now in force; the beads re-read, each marked "stands on the fallback" or "superseded by
this ADR" (the work packages of step 3.6 and the beads under them); what changes downstream (step 7); and the
recording session, which implemented none of the mechanisms. The cross-check of N40-30 compares this entry with
the row. The M2 re-read writes its own entry and `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER-M2.md` (N39b-10).

Beyond those fields the ADR
also states: the table (p, interval, ceiling 1/(1 − p), status) per lever, class and regime; the strike
list; the packages whose reason changed; the re-ranked lever order; the overlapping shares; the binding ceiling
per class (for example the one-design-per-turn cap (C + t)/t with measured C and t); and the list of speed
comparisons that are now refused for lack of a ceiling above their goal.

**5. Illustration (the worked single-pair ledger; illustrative arithmetic, not a measurement and not a
forecast).** Rival seconds: `author` 900, `propose` 1,080, `check` 240, `compile` 540, `screen` 0, `train` 4,920,
`evaluate` 360, `diagnose` 600, `wait` 180, `adjudicate` 180; clock 9,000. Rival shares:
`author` 10%, `propose` 12%, `check` 2.67%, `compile` 6%, `screen` 0, `train` 54.67%, `evaluate` 4%, `diagnose`
6.67%. Bounds: lever 1 → 12%; 2 → 54.67%; 3 → 4%; 4 → 6.67%; 5 → 12%; 6 → 12.67%; 7 → 6.67%; 8 → 16%. On such a
ledger the gate would fire for levers 3, 4 and 7. That would not remove ladders, adversarial search or autopsy
from the product: their work packages would carry their non-speed reasons (error control on comparisons;
failures found and minimized; causes identified by intervention) and no speed figure.

**6. The M2 re-read.** At M2 our own profile exists. The same procedure is run on arm A's development ledgers
for a different question: does the lever's phase still hold at least 10% of *our* loop, so that further work on
it can pay? A lever kept at M1 and under 10% on our own profile at M2 gets no further speed funding (its job is
done or it never mattered). The re-read itself, with the thesis arithmetic redone on measured rates, is the bead
N39b-10; the M2 exit review records its decision entry.

**7. What changes downstream if it fires.** The thesis table's "surviving number" for the lever is replaced by
"not a speed lever on <class> (measured share p)". The expected range of each class is recomputed from the
levers that remain. Any registered speed comparison for the struck lever is refused by the ceiling gate. Nothing
in the estimand, the outcome rules or the thresholds changes. Struck levers stay in the plan for their other
value: their beads drop any speed threshold from their acceptance criteria. If lever 2 is struck for a class,
the M4 work on family policies is funded for that class on other grounds or not at all. If no lever reaches 0.10
for a class, the class's expected range is restated and the partial gate still measures it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Eight decision records exist at M1, each with a measured share and range per class measured, and each
      signed by the recording session.
- [ ] The procedure is a pure function of the ledgers and the table: the same inputs give the same records.
- [ ] On the illustration's ledger the gate fires for levers 3, 4 and 7 and not for 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8.
- [ ] Every work package citing a struck lever has an action recorded before M2 begins; one with a remaining speed
      figure fails the ceiling gate's funding check.
- [ ] A share whose repeat range straddles 10% is `marginal`, not fired, and appears on the M2 re-read list.
- [ ] The records cite only `ledger-closed` runs.
- [ ] The ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER-M1.md` exists, dated in M1, with the required fields of the
      kill-gate decision form (N40-03) and a status for each of the eight levers per class and regime; the
      register row's decision entry names it, with outcome `fired` or `not-fired` as step 4 defines and at least
      one receipt, and the kill-ledger cross-check (N40-30) passes on it.
- [ ] Every open work package has a funding record with a `justification`; none cites a struck lever for
      speed; a planted work package citing a 4% share as its speed justification is rejected; a package without a
      funding record is unfunded.
- [ ] A planted product of levers that adds overlapping shares is refused (the union bound of N39a-26,
      `lever-share-above-ledger`), and one that multiplies a pruning ratio by a speed-up measured on removed work
      is refused by the order rule of N39a-38 (`order-rule-violation`).
- [ ] The funding half of GATE-TENFOLD-09 is recorded at M1 with the measured shares.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- kill_lever::`
- `fires_below_tenth`: range [0.03, 0.05] → fired; ceiling at 0.04 is 25/24.
- `not_fired_above`: range [0.11, 0.14] → not fired.
- `marginal`: range [0.08, 0.12] → marginal; M2 point value 0.09 → fired at M2.
- `per_class`: lever 2 at 54.67% on `legged-quad` and 6% on a planted class → kept overall, struck for the
  planted class only.
- `illustration`: the eight bounds and the three firings.
- `work_package_actions_required`: a struck lever with a package lacking an action → the record is incomplete.
- `closed_only`: an open ledger among the inputs → refused.
- `ten_percent_is_funded`: p = 0.10 exactly is funded; 0.0999 is struck.
- `ceiling_of_ten_percent_is_ten_ninths`: 1/(1 − 1/10) = 10/9.
- `overlap_is_not_additive`: levers 1 and 5 together remove at most `propose` + `screen` + `certify`.
- `package_without_reason_unfunded`: an open work package with no funding record, or one whose `justification`
  is `speed-lever` on a struck lever for the class, is listed as unfunded.
- `row_outcome_from_levers`: three levers struck on a class → the row's entry is `fired`; none struck and two
  `marginal` → `not-fired` with both listed for the M2 re-read; an entry whose `adr` is not
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER-M1.md` fails the register load (`decision-adr-path`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_kill_lever.sh`: with the worked ledger and a second `synthetic` class ledger as baselines and
a planted funding record, runs the shares, the decision for all eight levers, and then the funding check of the
ceiling gate before and after the packages' actions are recorded. It sources `scripts/e2e/lib.sh` (STD-E2E) and
writes NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/tenfold_kill_lever/<utc>.ndjson`. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, each
lever's phases, share, range, ceiling and outcome per class, the strike list, the funding check's rows, on a
failed assertion the first differing record field with the record path, and the summary naming
KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER as decided for eight levers. When the measured M1 rival ledgers exist, the same
script runs on them and logs the real shares as metrics. The reading is also the step `lever-reading` of
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_incumbent_loop.sh` (the incumbent-loop script of seed BD-143): it reads the share table,
prints the status per lever, and asserts the strike list against the registry of work packages in the scratch
project, logging each p as a `metric` with threshold 0.10 and its interval, and the ADR draft digest.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 1/(1 − 0.10) = 1.11; the pilot ceiling 842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.464 against measured 1.345 and 1.088;
the illustration's bounds. Published context that the measurement replaces
(FACT about the sources): training is about 85% of a composite quadruped iteration of 5.9 minutes and over 90% of
a composite humanoid iteration of about 31 minutes; under 2% of morphology proposals fail validation before
training and 15.8% of trained candidates are degenerate after a full run with a strong language model, so even
pruning every such failure cuts trainings by at most 1/(1 − 0.16) ≈ 1.19 (KA-015). Negative controls: a decision
computed on arm A's shares at M1 (there is no arm A ledger at M1; the input check refuses); a lever kept because
its *published* share is large while the measured one is small; a struck lever whose work package keeps its
speed figure; a share computed on our own ledger instead of the rival's at M1; a speed comparison registered with
a ceiling below its goal (must be refused, FROB-REF-866).

## Considerations

- HYPOTHESIS from the published composite: lever 2 (`train`) dominates quadruped and humanoid briefs, and levers
  acting only on `check` or `diagnose` are small. If the measured ledger agrees, several mechanisms are funded on
  trust and correctness alone, which the program accepts in advance.
- The decision uses an upper bound on each lever's share (all time in its phases). A lever can fall under 10%
  only if its phases are small; it cannot be struck for being ineffective, which is a different question measured
  later.
- If the measured ceilings are under 3 for every mission class, the thesis itself is rewritten before any work is
  funded against it (the M0 stop rule); that is a larger decision than this gate and is the owner's.
- Priority is one step above the milestone's default because every M2 engine work package is funded against this
  record.
- The 10% line is a rule about speed funding only. Certificates, adversarial search and attribution may each hold
  little removable time and remain funded for what they do to trust and coverage.
- The shares are measured on development missions of two strata; other classes are read when first measured.
- Risk: agent turn latency and rival training time are both shorter than assumed, which lowers the ceilings of
  levers 1 and 2; the ADR states the measured values beside the planning values (60 to 120 s per agent turn;
  300 to 410 s per rival training).
