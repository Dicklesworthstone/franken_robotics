## Background

The program's thesis names eight levers that together are supposed to shorten the time from a robot-design
question to an independently accepted answer: (1) decide many designs per agent turn; (2) train few; (3) spend
where it matters; (4) find failures instead of waiting for them; (5) answer over families instead of points;
(6) do not recompute or re-decide; (7) explain by intervention; (8) short setup. The ranges attached to them were
composed from published figures of different incumbent stacks, which is not a measurement of any loop. The first
two milestones therefore measure the rival's loop by phase on the program's own hardware, and this kill gate
applies the consequence.

**KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER.** *Trigger:* a mechanism's removable cost is under 10% of the measured loop. *Fallback,
named in advance:* it is not funded as a speed lever; it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with
that reason written and no speed figure. *Decided:* at M1 on the rival's ledger; at M2 on ours.

The arithmetic behind the number: if a phase holds the share p of the rival's restricted time and a mechanism
removes it entirely, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − p); at p = 0.10 that is 1.11. The baseline rule of the
protocol states the action: every lever receives its removable share p on the measured ledger, and a lever with p
under 0.10 is struck from the speed case before M2 begins.

What the baseline measurement is allowed to change: the ranking of the levers, the funding of work packages, the
deadlines of the strata, and the expected ranges per mission class. It is not allowed to change the estimand or
the outcome rules.

This is a decision bead. It evaluates a written trigger on recorded values, per lever and per mission class,
and records each outcome as an architecture decision record.

## Scope

In: the per-lever, per-class evaluation at M1; the decision records; the strike list and its effect on work
packages; the hand-off of the M2 re-read; what is and is not allowed to change. Out: measuring the rival's ledger
(seed BD-143 and the parity baseline seeds); computing shares and ceilings (N39a-26, N39a-37); the gate that
checks funding records against this decision at every exit (N39a-38).

## Technical approach

**1. Inputs.** The closed ledgers of the agent-driven rival loop on at least 12 development missions of the
`legged-quad` and `wheeled` strata (the M1 baseline); the lever-to-phase table; the pooled shares and the bound
p_max(ℓ) for each lever ℓ (the share of the rival's time in the phases the lever acts on); each speed-lever work
package's cited share.

**2. Lever-to-phase table (the bound each lever is judged on).** 1 → `propose`, `screen`; 2 → `train`;
3 → `screen`, `evaluate`; 4 → `diagnose`; 5 → `certify`, `propose`; 6 → `check`, `compile`, `evaluate`;
7 → `diagnose`; 8 → `compile`, `author`.

**3. Decision procedure (per lever ℓ and per mission class with a measured ledger; numbered; the treatment of the
repeat range in steps 2 to 4 is a DESIGN reading of "p under 0.10" for a share that is measured with noise).**
1. Compute p_max(ℓ) on the class's pooled closed ledgers, with its repeat interval: the baseline repeats the
   round, and the share's range over repeats is recorded.
2. If the upper end of that range is below 1/10: the kill gate **fires** for (ℓ, class). The lever is struck from
   the speed case for that class.
3. If the lower end is at or above 1/10: it does not fire.
4. If the range straddles 1/10: it does not fire at M1 and is flagged `marginal`; it is re-read at M2 on our own
   profile, where a point value below 1/10 fires.
5. A lever is struck from the speed case *overall* only if it is struck for every class measured. A lever kept
   for one class keeps its work packages, with the speed justification restricted to that class.
6. For each struck (ℓ, class): every work package that cites lever ℓ as a speed justification for that class must,
   before M2 begins, either remove the speed figure and state a non-speed reason (`trust`, `coverage`,
   `correctness`) or be re-planned. The list of such packages is part of the record.

**4. The decision record (one per lever; an ADR at M1, amended at M2).**
```
{ kill_gate: "KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER", at: "M1", lever: 3, phases: ["screen", "evaluate"],
  by_class: [ { class: "legged-quad", ledgers: ["b3:…"], p_max: "1/25", range: ["…", "…"],
                ceiling: "25/24", fired: true | false, marginal: bool } ],
  struck_overall: bool, work_packages: [ { wp: "WP-nn", action: "speed figure removed; reason: trust" |
  "re-planned" | "kept" } ], trigger_text, fallback_text, decided_at, decided_by, hash }
```
The record states the measured values beside the trigger whichever way it went.

**5. Illustration (the worked single-pair ledger; illustrative arithmetic, not a measurement).** Rival shares:
`author` 10%, `propose` 12%, `check` 2.67%, `compile` 6%, `screen` 0, `train` 54.67%, `evaluate` 4%, `diagnose`
6.67%. Bounds: lever 1 → 12%; 2 → 54.67%; 3 → 4%; 4 → 6.67%; 5 → 12%; 6 → 12.67%; 7 → 6.67%; 8 → 16%. On such a
ledger the gate would fire for levers 3, 4 and 7. That would not remove ladders, adversarial search or autopsy
from the product: their work packages would carry their non-speed reasons (error control on comparisons;
failures found and minimized; causes identified by intervention) and no speed figure.

**6. The M2 re-read.** At M2 our own profile exists. The same procedure is run on arm A's development ledgers
for a different question: does the lever's phase still hold at least 10% of *our* loop, so that further work on
it can pay? A lever kept at M1 and under 10% on our own profile at M2 gets no further speed funding (its job is
done or it never mattered). The M2 exit review records the amendment.

**7. What changes downstream if it fires.** The thesis table's "surviving number" for the lever is replaced by
"not a speed lever on <class> (measured share p)". The expected range of each class is recomputed from the
levers that remain. Any registered speed comparison for the struck lever is refused by the ceiling gate. Nothing
in the estimand, the outcome rules or the thresholds changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Eight decision records exist at M1, each with a measured share and range per class measured, and each
      signed by the recording session.
- [ ] The procedure is a pure function of the ledgers and the table: the same inputs give the same records.
- [ ] On the illustration's ledger the gate fires for levers 3, 4 and 7 and not for 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8.
- [ ] Every work package citing a struck lever has an action recorded before M2 begins; one with a remaining speed
      figure fails the ceiling gate's funding check.
- [ ] A share whose repeat range straddles 10% is `marginal`, not fired, and appears on the M2 re-read list.
- [ ] The records cite only `ledger-closed` runs.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- kill_lever::`
- `fires_below_tenth`: range [0.03, 0.05] → fired; ceiling at 0.04 is 25/24.
- `not_fired_above`: range [0.11, 0.14] → not fired.
- `marginal`: range [0.08, 0.12] → marginal; M2 point value 0.09 → fired at M2.
- `per_class`: lever 2 at 54.67% on `legged-quad` and 6% on a planted class → kept overall, struck for the
  planted class only.
- `illustration`: the eight bounds and the three firings.
- `work_package_actions_required`: a struck lever with a package lacking an action → the record is incomplete.
- `closed_only`: an open ledger among the inputs → refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_kill_lever.sh`: with the worked ledger and a second `synthetic` class ledger as baselines and
a planted funding record, runs the shares, the decision for all eight levers, and then the funding check of the
ceiling gate before and after the packages' actions are recorded. Logs each command, exit code, wall time, each
lever's phases, share, range, ceiling and outcome per class, the strike list, the funding check's rows, and the
summary naming KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER as decided for eight levers. When the measured M1 rival ledgers exist, the same
script runs on them and logs the real shares as metrics.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 1/(1 − 0.10) = 1.11; the illustration's bounds. Published context that the measurement replaces
(FACT about the sources): training is about 85% of a composite quadruped iteration of 5.9 minutes and over 90% of
a composite humanoid iteration of about 31 minutes; under 2% of morphology proposals fail validation before
training, so pruning trivial failures cuts trainings by at most 1.19 (KA-015). Negative controls: a decision
computed on arm A's shares at M1 (there is no arm A ledger at M1; the input check refuses); a lever kept because
its *published* share is large while the measured one is small; a struck lever whose work package keeps its
speed figure.

## Considerations

- HYPOTHESIS from the published composite: lever 2 (`train`) dominates quadruped and humanoid briefs, and levers
  acting only on `check` or `diagnose` are small. If the measured ledger agrees, several mechanisms are funded on
  trust and correctness alone, which the program accepts in advance.
- The decision uses an upper bound on each lever's share (all time in its phases). A lever can fall under 10%
  only if its phases are small; it cannot be struck for being ineffective, which is a different question measured
  later.
- If the measured ceilings are under 3 for every mission class, the thesis itself is rewritten before any work is
  funded against it (the M0 stop rule); that is a larger decision than this gate and is the owner's.
- Priority is one step above the milestone's default because every M2 engine work package is funded against this
  record.
