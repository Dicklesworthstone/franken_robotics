## Background

The ten-fold test measures the time from a robot-design question to an independently accepted answer, for this
system against the strongest composed rival, on missions grouped in named strata. Each stratum has two pools of
missions: an initial 24 **sealed** mission families, written by the adjudicator, committed by hash and shown to an
arm one card at a time when its clock starts; and 12 **public development missions**, published with the class
descriptions as the first of six ordered sealing steps. The development suite is what every arm is tuned on, and it
is read by four later steps: the rival's median development time sets the default deadline of the stratum
(OPEN-TENFOLD-5, N39b-30: three times that median, rounded up to half an hour, raised until the horizon cap H ÷
RMT_A is at least 10, RMT_A being this system's restricted mean time on the development suite); the freeze of the
rival is refused if it wins none of the development missions on which a conventional method is expected to win (a
rival that loses its own easy cases is presumed under-built); the adjudication dry run and the tournament rehearsal
run on development missions; and the leak scanner is exercised on them.

The mechanisms around the suite all have beads: the mission card format, the strata registry and grid, families
as the independent unit with the split rule, the six anti-shortcut controls, the sealing ledger, mission
templates for the sealed types. The partial gate's protocol bead publishes the development suite at step 1. No
bead **wrote** it: forty-eight missions for the four strata first run at M3 (`legged-quad`, `wheeled`,
`envelope`, `sensing`), each with a card, a world family, a monitor, a reference answer and its controls. This
bead builds the development-pool tooling and publication package and authors the 24 missions of the two strata
whose answers are checked by direct evaluation (`envelope`, `sensing`); the 24 missions of the two design strata
are N39ax-02. Tier: Solid.

Who writes them. The adjudicator: the neutral party of the owner's decision on adjudication or, under its
fail-closed default, a fresh-context session under a named human (every ratio is then labelled
`self-adjudicated`). Never a session that built the product or advocates for the rival: role disjointness is
checked from the role registry. Rejected: letting the builders write development missions (they would be shaped
by what the product does well, and the rival's tuning would inherit that bias); reusing the plan's public
fixtures as they stand (they are the cases every arm has already seen solved; they may seed generators, not
serve as missions).

## Scope

In: the development-pool profile of the suite checks; the published form of a development card; the publication
package and its hash; 12 `envelope` and 12 `sensing` missions with reference answers and controls. Out: the
design strata (N39ax-02); development missions of the strata first run at M7 (N39ax-03); the card schema and its
validation (N39a-05); strata and grid (N39a-12); family grouping and the split rule (N39a-13); how each control
is constructed (N39a-14); sealed missions and their templates (N39b-40 and the suite beads); the ledger entry
`publish-development` (N39a-15); deadlines (N39b-30).

## Technical approach

**1. A development card.** The schema `frob.tenfold.mission/1` unchanged, with two differences of handling
(DESIGN): the part that is sealed for a sealed mission (ground truth, control flags, probe points, canary,
independent-evaluator mark) is **published** beside the card at `tenfold/<gate>/development/revealed/<mission-hash>.json`,
and the card's commitment opens it, so the same validation code runs on both pools; and the card's `deadline_s`
is the stratum record's `development_deadline_s` (N39a-12: the provisional development deadline published with
the class description, 14,400 s by default, TARGET), not the stratum's H, which is the stratum record's
`deadline_s`, `null` until N39b-30 decides it from the rival's development times measured on this suite. A
development card carries a canary of its own namespace (`dev-canary:`), so that the transcript scanner can be
tested without touching a sealed canary.

**2. Pool checks (`frob-tenfold suite check --development [--stratum <s>] [--json]`, added to the existing suite
check; `--stratum` limits the checks to one stratum, `--json` prints the per-stratum results or the refusal as
canonical JSON).**
1. At least 12 missions per stratum, in at least 6 families (families by the connected-components rule: shared
   generator draw, site, material batch or parent design). At the gate where a stratum is first run its pool is
   the 12 authored missions; at a later gate it also holds the families an earlier gate retired, counted with
   them (N39ax-03), so the rule is a floor, not an exact count.
2. Every card passes the card check; its revealed part opens its commitment.
3. Control coverage, so that every control can be rehearsed in public: at least 2 missions on which a named
   conventional method is expected to win or tie; at least 1 whose correct answer is `unknown` with a witness;
   all four mutation kinds (unit, contact order, causal clock, passive load) each on at least one mission; the
   quantifier-order fixture in `envelope`; in every warm card exactly one consequential edit among at least two
   inconsequential ones (public here, since the revealed part is published).
4. Every mission has a reference answer on file that the acceptance code accepts, and one planted wrong answer
   per mutation kind that it rejects.
5. Every asset a card names is public and redistributable: an object with a private or research-only mark
   fails publication.
6. The family keys are recorded, so that the sealed suite's commitment check can refuse any sealed family
   connected to a development mission.
Each failing check has a reason string (`dev-count`, `dev-families`, `dev-control-coverage`,
`dev-reference-missing`, `dev-asset-mark`, `dev-keys-missing`), exit 3, each a row of the ten-fold refusal
registry (N39-REF) under the `tenfold.` namespace, whose consistency test fails on a reason raised here and
missing there.

**3. The 24 missions of this bead (DESIGN allocation; the adjudicator may reallocate inside the checks).**

| Stratum | Missions | Answer kind | Reference answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `envelope` | 5 adjustable-stance queries under static load (foot authority, force floor, package dimensions, asymmetric payload, a missing foot varied by generator); 3 timed-docking queries over a gain interval; 1 docking query whose calibration data admit two plant gains under which the certified gain interval differs (the stratum's unresolved control); the quantifier-order fixture (every design has a command that works and no single command works for all); 2 warm amendment sequences on accepted cold answers | Family answer; amendment verdict; the unresolved control as a companion card of kind truthful-unknown (the stratum's registered kinds exclude it, so it enters under the companion-kind extension of the anti-shortcut controls, N39a-14, with its sealed-part `unknown_truth` flag set) | Exact: the certified region or verdict list computed in rational arithmetic by the adjudicator's direct evaluator; for the unresolved control, the two admissible plant gains and the differing intervals |
| `sensing` | 4 split-grip landings with a last usable observation; 4 observation-alias missions; 4 missions with an unfamiliar contact law and a probe to choose | Qualified design or truthful unknown | The world pair or model pair that makes `unknown` correct, or the distinguishing probe with its predicted readings |

Easy wins: one stance query that is a single small linear program and one docking query with a closed form (in
`envelope`); one landing where direct measurement at mission dwell decides and one probe mission whose first
probe separates the laws (in `sensing`). Unresolved answers: in `envelope`, the docking companion card above; in
`sensing`, at least one alias mission and one contact-law mission in which two admissible worlds consistent with
all supplied evidence have different best choices. Check 3 is applied per stratum, so each stratum carries its
own.

**4. Publication package.** `tenfold/M3-partial/development/`: `cards/`, `revealed/`, the development rows of
`families.json`, the world generators' identities, the reference answers, and `INDEX.json` listing every file
with its BLAKE3 hash. The hash of `INDEX.json` is what sealing step 1 records. The package builds twice to the
same bytes. Determinism class D0 for the package and the checks; reference answers are exact.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-tenfold suite check --development` passes for `envelope` and `sensing` with 12 missions each, at
      least 6 families each, and the control coverage of check 3.
- [ ] All 24 reference answers are accepted by the acceptance code; every planted wrong answer (one per
      mutation kind per stratum) is rejected.
- [ ] The quantifier-order mission returns different answers for the per-design query and the shared query.
- [ ] The package's `INDEX.json` hash is byte-identical across two builds, and a rehearsal sealing ledger
      (N39a-15) records it in its `publish-development` entry and refuses an entry with any other hash; the
      partial gate's own entry is written at step 1 by its protocol bead (BD-314) with the same value.
- [ ] The role check shows that no session that authored a mission is a builder or a rival advocate.
- [ ] A development card whose asset carries a private mark fails publication with `dev-asset-mark`.
- [ ] A sealed candidate that shares a generator draw with a development mission is refused by the family
      check, using the keys this bead records.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::development` (real cards and registries; plants are scratch copies
labelled `synthetic`):
- `dev_pool_counts`: 11 missions, or 12 missions in 5 families, each fail with their reason; 12 authored
  missions plus the missions of retired families of an earlier gate pass.
- `dev_control_coverage`: removing the only `unknown` mission, a mutation kind, or the quantifier-order fixture
  fails `dev-control-coverage` naming the missing control; removing the `envelope` docking companion card fails
  it for `envelope` while `sensing` still passes.
- `dev_card_opens_commitment`: a revealed part edited after commitment fails.
- `dev_reference_accepted`, `dev_mutants_rejected`: on every mission of the two strata.
- `dev_deadline_is_provisional`: a development card whose `deadline_s` differs from the stratum's
  `development_deadline_s` (for example one carrying a sealed H) fails with `card-deadline-mismatch` (card check 4
  of N39a-05).
- `dev_canary_namespace`: a development card carrying a sealed-namespace canary fails.
- `dev_package_deterministic`: two builds, one hash.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_development_suite.sh`: (1) `frob-tenfold suite check --development --stratum envelope` and
`--stratum sensing`; (2) for each mission, the acceptance run of its reference answer and of its mutants, each
committed with `frob-tenfold submit` and decided by `frob-tenfold adjudicate` under the adjudicator identity
(N39a-09, running the decision procedures of N39a-07); (3) a scratch copy with each of five plants (a missing mission, a merged
family, a missing control, a private-marked asset, an edited revealed part), asserting the reason; (4) the
package build twice and the hash comparison. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_development_suite/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit
code and wall time; `metric` lines per stratum (missions, families, easy wins, unknowns, mutation kinds); one
`assert` per check and per plant; the package hash; a `summary` line. Development missions are public; the
script reads nothing sealed and logs no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: 24 missions here of 48 at the gate; at least 6 families per stratum; minimum control counts 2
  and 1 per 12 missions; four mutation kinds.
- Negative controls: the five plants above; a reference answer correct for the unmutated mission offered on its
  mutant (must be rejected); a quantifier-order mission answered with the per-design result for the shared
  query (must be rejected).
- Metamorphic: permuting the missions inside a stratum changes no check and no hash of a card.
- Determinism lanes: the checks and the package twice on one machine and on Linux and macOS, class D0.

## Considerations

- Cost: authoring is instantiation of generators plus one exact reference evaluation per mission, about two
  adjudicator-hours per mission (TARGET), 48 hours for this bead. If the measured figure is more than twice
  that, the stratum allocation is cut to the minimum the checks allow and the shortfall is reported, not hidden.
- The development suite grows: every family the partial gate retires is revealed and joins the pool for M7.
- Tuning on the development suite is allowed for every arm equally; using the adjudicating simulator while
  tuning is disqualifying in the tournament, so reference runs are made by the adjudicating session only.
- If the owner's adjudicator is an outside party, this bead is done by that party with this tooling; the
  checks, the package and the hash are the same.
