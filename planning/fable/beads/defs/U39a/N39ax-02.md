## Background

The ten-fold test compares this system with the strongest composed rival on missions grouped in strata. Each
stratum has 12 public development missions, on which every arm is tuned and from which the stratum's deadline is
derived (OPEN-TENFOLD-5, N39b-30: three times the frozen rival's median development time, rounded up to half an
hour, raised until the horizon cap H ÷ RMT_A is at least 10, RMT_A being this system's restricted mean time on the
development suite), and a sealed pool of 24 mission families. The development suite is published with the class
descriptions as the first sealing step. The pool tooling, the publication package and the two strata answered by
direct evaluation are built by N39ax-01. This bead authors the development missions of the two **design strata**
first run at M3:

- `legged-quad`: quadruped and hexapod design briefs with parametric and single-limb edits, across the legged
  row of the coverage grid;
- `wheeled`: the wheeled row of the grid (differential, car-like, mecanum, omni, swerve, balancer and mobile
  manipulator bases).

The answer kind of both is a **qualified design**: a design with its controller that passes the mission's
acceptance battery in the adjudicating simulator (for threshold 0.9 with no allowed failure, 36 clusters at the
first submission). A design mission is worth publishing only if it can be solved inside its own bounds, and the
only proof of that is a design that does: a **solvability witness**. That makes these 24 missions more
expensive to author than the family queries, which is why they are a bead of their own. Tier: Solid.

The missions are written by the adjudicator (the neutral party, or under the fail-closed default a
fresh-context session under a named human), from the public class descriptions, never by a builder or a rival
advocate. Rejected: publishing design missions without witnesses (an unsolvable development mission wastes
every arm's tuning budget equally and makes the rival's median development time, and so the deadline,
meaningless); using the product's own optimizer to produce every witness (a mission would then be known
solvable only by the arm under test; a witness may come from any tool, and at least a third per stratum must
come from a source other than the product, such as an archetype with a hand-tuned or incumbent-trained
controller).

## Scope

In: 12 `legged-quad` and 12 `wheeled` development missions (cards, world families, requirements with monitors,
solvability witnesses, controls); their grid coverage; their rows in the development pool and package. Out:
the pool checks, the published form of a development card and the package (N39ax-01); the strata first run at
M7 (N39ax-03); acceptance of a qualified design (N39a-06); the adjudicating simulator adapter (N39a-08) and the
choice of simulator per stratum (N39b-26); the rival's tuning and its development times (BD-315); deadlines
(N39b-30); sealed missions.

## Technical approach

**1. Grid coverage (DESIGN allocation; the adjudicator may reallocate, keeping every world type covered).**
Eight world types: open terrain and the seven interior archetypes.

| World type | `legged-quad` (12) | `wheeled` (12) |
|---|---|---|
| Terrain | 3: stairs; gaps and stepping stones; rubble | 2: slopes; curbs |
| Home | 2: thresholds and rugs; a stair flight | 1: threshold delivery with a loaded cart |
| Apartment | 1: a lift-lobby sill and tight turns | 1: timed docking under a hidden plant gain |
| Office | 1: castor chairs and glass doors | 2: carpet rolling resistance; a door closer |
| Warehouse | 1: a ramp with payload | 1: payload transport over a dock plate |
| Manufacturing plant | 2: the split-grip landing; an oil patch | 2: boarding a conveyor; leaving it |
| Retail store | 1: aisles with movable carts | 1: parallel shift and docking in an aisle |
| Commercial kitchen | 1: a wet galley crossing | 2: a greasy patch; a swing door |

At least 6 families per stratum (missions that share a generator draw, a site, a material batch or a parent
design are one family); by DESIGN no two grid cells of one stratum share a site, so that the family count does
not collapse when a site is reused.

**2. A card.** Robot family: the grammar, mass bounds, actuator catalogue and the symmetry knowledge every arm
receives. World family: the generator identity and parameters, and the legality profile. Task: prose and a
requirement in the task language, with the adjudicator's own monitor. Obligations of a qualified design: the
observation and action interface and the control clocks. Battery: threshold, allowed failures, first cluster
count, tier `adjudicator`. Caps: the gate's. Deadline: the stratum's provisional development deadline.

**3. Solvability witness (DESIGN).** For each mission, a record `witness.json` beside the revealed part:
`{ design: b3:…, controller: b3:…, produced_by: "archetype" | "incumbent-recipe" | "product" | "hand", battery:
{ clusters, failures, tier, simulator }, receipt: b3:… }`. Rules: (a) the design lies inside the card's robot
family (grammar and bounds checked mechanically); (b) the battery is the card's own, run by the adjudicating
session in the adjudicating simulator, and passes; (c) at least 4 of the 12 witnesses of a stratum have
`produced_by` other than `product`; (d) a mission for which no witness is found inside the authoring budget is
replaced, and the replaced draft is kept in the authoring log with the reason. The witness is published: it is
a development mission, so nothing is leaked, and an arm may start from it.

**4. Controls in each stratum (public in the development pool).** At least 2 easy wins: missions on which a
named conventional method is expected to win or tie (a mild-terrain gait that an incumbent's stock training
recipe solves in its published time; a slope mission that a model-based planner solves directly). At least 1
unresolved answer, carried as a companion card of kind truthful-unknown in the stratum's family list. All four
mutation kinds (unit, contact order, causal clock, passive load), each as a mutated mission whose correct
answer differs from its parent's. One **tier trap**: a candidate design placed among the mission's permitted
inputs that passes the requirement on a fast simulation tier and fails in the adjudicating simulator; its
record holds both runs.

**5. Compute.** Each witness battery is one job inside the machine envelope (16 GiB of memory, 8 cores, one
hour) or runs under a compute class the owner approved before it started, as the child of `frob-launch` under
that class's request row, citing its meter row (N36-20: work above E0 started any other way is not metered and is
not evidence); a battery that fits neither is a reason to replace the mission. Witness runs are class D2 on one backend and are replayed once to identical
digests; the checks on cards and coverage are class D0.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `frob-tenfold suite check --development --stratum legged-quad` and `--stratum wheeled` pass: 12 missions
      and at least 6 families each, every world type covered in each stratum, control coverage complete.
- [ ] Each of the 24 missions has a witness whose battery passes in the adjudicating simulator at the card's
      size; each witness design lies inside its card's robot family; at least 4 per stratum are not produced by
      the product.
- [ ] Each tier-trap candidate passes on the fast tier and fails in the adjudicating simulator, with both
      receipts on file.
- [ ] Each mutated mission rejects its parent's witness.
- [ ] Each easy-win mission names its conventional method and the adjudicator's expected outcome (`rival wins`
      or `tie`).
- [ ] The 24 missions are in the publication package of N39ax-01, whose `INDEX.json` lists every card, revealed
      part and witness of this bead with its hash; that package hash is the one the partial gate records at
      sealing step 1.
- [ ] The authoring log lists every replaced draft with its reason.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::development_design` (real cards and witnesses; plants are scratch
copies labelled `synthetic`):
- `grid_row_covered`: removing the only kitchen mission of a stratum fails naming the cell.
- `witness_inside_family`: a witness whose mass exceeds the card's bound, or whose actuator is outside the
  catalogue, fails.
- `witness_quota_non_product`: 3 non-product witnesses of 12 fails; 4 passes.
- `tier_trap_has_two_receipts`: a trap with only the fast-tier receipt fails.
- `mutant_rejects_parent_witness`: for each mutation kind.
- `two_cells_one_site_is_one_family`: two missions sharing a site are grouped, and the family count drops.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_development_design.sh`: (1) the two suite checks; (2) for two missions per stratum chosen
by a logged seed, the witness battery re-run at the card's size (the witness committed with `frob-tenfold
submit` and decided by `frob-tenfold adjudicate` under the adjudicator identity, N39a-09, running the battery of
N39a-06), with the digests compared to the recorded receipt; (3) one tier trap per stratum re-run on both tiers; (4) scratch
copies with four plants (a witness outside its family, a missing cell, a trap that passes on both tiers, a
parent witness accepted on a mutant), each asserted by reason. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_development_design/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit
code and wall time; the evidence block of each acceptance envelope (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed); `metric` lines per stratum (missions, families, cells covered, witnesses by producer) and per
battery (clusters, failures, threshold); one `assert` per check and plant with digests where state is compared;
a `summary` line. Development missions are public; nothing sealed is read and no private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: 24 missions; 8 world types per stratum; 36 clusters with zero failures at threshold 0.9 for a
  first submission; minimum counts 2 easy wins, 1 unresolved answer, 4 mutation kinds, 1 tier trap per stratum.
- Negative controls: the four plants; a witness battery run on a fast tier and labelled `adjudicator` (must be
  refused by the tier check of acceptance).
- Metamorphic: relabelling the links of a witness design, or respelling its units, changes neither its
  identity nor its battery verdict.
- Determinism lanes: witness batteries twice on one backend to identical digests (class D2); card and coverage
  checks in debug and release, on Linux and macOS (class D0).

## Considerations

- Cost dominates: 24 witnesses, each a design, a controller and a battery. TARGET: four adjudicator-hours per
  mission including compute waits. Archetypes with their reference controllers are the cheapest source of
  witnesses and are used first.
- Cells that need film or track models may have no admitted adjudicating simulator; such a cell uses cross-home
  adjudication as the simulator decision records, and the witness names both homes.
- A development mission that proves too easy for both arms carries no information about the ratio; the easy-win
  quota is a floor for the rival's sanity check, not a target for the suite.
- If the legged or wheeled product slice is not ready at M3, the stratum is `not run` at the partial gate and
  its development missions still ship, because the rival's audit and its median development time need them.
