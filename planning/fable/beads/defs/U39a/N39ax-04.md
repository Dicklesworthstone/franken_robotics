## Background

The ten-fold tournament at M7 measures the thesis on a sealed suite of missions written by a neutral
adjudicator from public class descriptions, committed by hash before any arm is frozen. The suite is large by
construction: eleven strata at 24 sealed mission families each is 264 families, every one with a card, a world
family, a monitor, a reference answer and its sealed controls (easy wins, unresolved answers, planted
mutations, a concealed consequential edit in warm sequences, tier traps). The seed bead of the sealed suite is
an epic whose done-condition begins "a short plan exists and its pilot has run". The epic names the pilot and
its falsifier, and no task carried it. This bead is that pilot.

A pilot in this project is a paper-scale or day-scale run with a falsifier written before it executes; it
decides whether the full work is attempted as planned or on its fallback, and its record is entered in the
pilot register whatever the result. What this pilot decides: **how many strata the tournament can afford to
seal.** If authoring a sealed-grade family costs more than the budget assumes, or fresh authors cannot write
families that pass the suite checks, the tournament runs fewer strata and says so; it does not seal a suite of
lower grade. Tier: Solid (the mechanisms are built; the unknown is cost and reliability of authoring).

Rejected: estimating the cost from the partial gate's authoring alone (that suite was written while the
templates were being built, by sessions that knew them; the tournament's suite is written by a party who
did not); skipping the pilot because the checks are mechanical (the checks say whether a family is admissible,
not how long a competent stranger takes to produce one).

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; six sealed-grade families authored under it; the three measurements; the verdict
against the falsifier; the record; the consequence for the tournament's list of strata. Out: the sealed suite
itself (the epic's tasks; nothing authored here is used as a sealed family, because the pilot's families are
revealed in its record); the development pool (N39ax-01, N39ax-02, N39ax-03); the suite checks (the strata,
family, control and card beads); the decision-rule pilot (N39b-16).

## Technical approach

**1. Registration (before any authoring).** A row in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml`: mechanism
"sealed-suite authoring at tournament scope"; the protocol below, frozen by hash; the falsifier; the ceiling
(264 families at the budgeted hours); the fallback; the cost cap of the pilot (one day of adjudicator time and
one CPU day); the session identities by role.

**2. Protocol.**
1. Two fresh-context adjudicator sessions that authored nothing in the partial gate and are neither builders
   nor rival advocates (checked against the role registry). Each receives only the public material: class
   descriptions (strata and grid), the mission grammar and schemas, the development suite, the sealed-type
   templates' public operator list, and the suite checks as a tool.
2. Session 1 authors six sealed-grade families: three in `legged-quad` and three in `envelope`, with every
   control the stratum requires, each with a reference answer (for `legged-quad`, a solvability witness).
3. Session 2 is given the cell brief of one of the `legged-quad` families and one of the `envelope` families,
   and authors a family for each without seeing session 1's.
4. Every family is run through the suite checks (kind admissibility for its stratum, family connectivity,
   control quotas, card validation). A failing family gets at most one repair cycle by its author.
5. The clock for adjudicator-hours is the session's wall time from brief to check-passing (or to the end of the
   repair cycle), excluding compute waits, which are logged separately.

**3. Measurements.**

| Quantity | How | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Pass share | Families of session 1 that pass every suite check after at most one repair cycle | Fewer than 4 of 6 |
| Adjudicator-hours per family | Mean over session 1's six families | More than 4 hours |
| Independence | For each of the two shared briefs: does the connectivity check keep session 1's and session 2's family separate (no shared generator draw, site, material batch or parent design)? | Reported; not part of the falsifier |

**4. Verdict and consequence.** If neither falsifier fires, the tournament plans all strata that have a
product, at 24 families per cell. If either fires, the fallback named in advance applies: fewer strata, each
declared `not run` with the reason, dropped least-evidenced first (DESIGN order of the epic): `facility`,
`throughput`, `rebody`, `failure`, then `hybrid` and `tracked`, until 24 families per remaining cell fit the
measured hours inside the tournament's authoring window. If independence fails on either brief, the class
description of that cell is too narrow to yield unconnected families, and the cell is redescribed before
sealing. The arithmetic is recorded: strata kept × 24 sealed families × measured hours per family against the
window (the same count as the epic's 11 × 24 = 264 families).

**5. Record.** The pilot's row (schema `frob.gauntlet.pilot/1`) gains the result, including a negative one,
with verdict `falsifier-rejected` (all strata planned) or `falsifier-holds` (the fallback, with the kept list); the six families and the two
independent ones are published in the record (they are spent: connected to the record, they can never be
sealed). The record is cited by the epic's short plan. Determinism class D0 for the checks; the hours are
measurements with their session logs attached.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's row exists in the pilot register with protocol hash, falsifier, ceiling, fallback and cost
      cap, and its timestamp precedes the first authored card.
- [ ] Six families from session 1 and two from session 2 are on file with their check outputs and repair logs.
- [ ] Pass share, mean hours per family and the two independence verdicts are recorded with the logs they were
      computed from.
- [ ] The verdict is recorded against the falsifier, and the list of strata the tournament attempts follows
      from it by the stated arithmetic; a negative result is recorded as such.
- [ ] No family authored in the pilot appears in any sealed pool (the connectivity check refuses it).
- [ ] The role check shows both sessions disjoint from builders, rival advocates and the partial gate's authors.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::authoring_pilot`:
- `verdict_rule`: (6 of 6, 3.0 h) → proceed; (3 of 6, 2.0 h) → fallback; (5 of 6, 4.5 h) → fallback; (4 of 6,
  4.0 h) → proceed (the falsifier is "fewer than four" and "more than four hours").
- `drop_order_arithmetic`: with 6.0 hours per family and a window of 800 adjudicator-hours, strata are dropped
  in the stated order until kept strata × 24 × 6.0 ≤ 800: all six droppable strata go and the five kept are
  `legged-quad`, `wheeled`, `envelope`, `sensing` and `legged-biped` (5 × 24 × 6.0 = 720; six would need 864);
  with 4.5 hours and the same window, seven strata are kept (7 × 24 × 4.5 = 756; eight would need 864), and
  `facility`, `throughput`, `rebody` and `failure` are dropped.
- `pilot_family_cannot_be_sealed`: a pilot family offered to a sealed registry is refused as connected to a
  revealed mission.
- `registration_precedes_authoring`: a card timestamp earlier than the pilot row fails the record check.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_authoring_pilot.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N39ax-04 milestone=M7 labels=tenfold,gauntlet,pilot
seed=0 needs=cpu plants=hours-without-repair,builder-session,pilot-family-sealed`): (0) `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test
-p frob-conformance tenfold::authoring_pilot`; (1) reads the registration row of
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` read-only, verifies its protocol hash and that its timestamp precedes
the first authored card; (2) runs the suite checks on the eight families (`frob-tenfold suite check --families
--json`, `--controls --json`, and the card validation, each on the pilot's scratch registry) and compares the
outputs with the recorded ones; (3) recomputes pass share, hours and the verdict from the session logs, and the
kept-strata list by the drop-order arithmetic; (4) runs the connectivity check of the two pairs (`frob-tenfold
suite check --commitment --json` on a scratch commitment holding each pair); (5) the three plants of the header,
each asserted by reason (a record whose hours omit the repair cycle; a session listed as a builder; a pilot
family offered to a scratch sealed registry). It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_authoring_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit code
and wall time; `metric` lines for pass share, hours per family (mean, minimum, maximum), compute-wait hours and
the two independence results, each against its threshold; one `assert` per step; a `summary` line naming
GATE-GAUNT-08. The pilot's families are public once recorded; no sealed content and no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: 11 strata × 24 families = 264; the falsifier thresholds 4 of 6 and 4 hours; the verdict table
  of the unit test.
- Negative controls: a record whose hours omit the repair cycle (the recomputation from logs disagrees); a
  session listed in the role registry as a builder (the role check fails); a pilot family in a sealed pool.
- Determinism: the recomputation is a pure function of the logs and registries (class D0).

## Considerations

- Six families from one session is a small sample; the pilot can show that authoring is far too expensive or
  unreliable, not that it is cheap. The tournament's own authoring log keeps measuring, and the same arithmetic
  is re-run at the midpoint of the authoring window.
- Under the fail-closed default for adjudication (fresh-context sessions under a named human) the pilot
  measures those sessions; an outside adjudicator appointed later repeats it on one stratum before sealing.
- Compute waits for `legged-quad` witnesses are excluded from adjudicator-hours and reported, because they bind
  the calendar even when they do not bind the author.
