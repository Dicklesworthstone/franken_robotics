## Background

The ten-fold tournament ends in a publication: the measured ratio of every cell with its interval and its
phase ledgers, whatever the ratio is, together with a **replication kit** from which a stranger can recompute
every published number (level 1: bit for bit, from the kit's ledgers, with a standalone scorer that uses the
standard library only) and re-run one sampled pair per stratum on comparable hardware (level 2). The outcome
rules are written in advance: each verdict maps to a fixed sentence, a tie with a short robust program over an
incumbent is reported as a tie, and the word "ten-fold" may appear only where a cell earned it.

A publication fails in ways no scorer test finds: a kit that silently needs a file outside itself, a number in
the report that the kit's ledgers do not reproduce, a report a competent reader cannot answer simple questions
from. The seed bead for publication is an epic whose done-condition begins "a short plan exists and its pilot
has run"; the epic names the pilot, a **dry run on the partial gate's record**, and no task carried it. The
partial gate at M3 produced a complete, revealed record of the same shape as the tournament's, at smaller
size. Publishing it with the tournament's generator, before the tournament's freeze, is a full rehearsal of
publication with real data and nothing sealed at risk. Tier: Solid.

Rejected: rehearsing on synthetic campaigns only (the harness rehearsal already does; it cannot show that a
real record, with its censored runs, voided cells and descriptive strata, publishes cleanly); letting the
sessions that built the generator judge the report's clarity (they know what every table means).

## Scope

In: the registered protocol; the kit and report built from the M3 record with the M7 generator; level 1 on a
clean machine with no network; the cold-reader test with ten questions and a mechanical answer key; the verdict
against the falsifier; fixing the generator's version before the tournament's freeze. Out: the kit builder and
its refusals (N39b-18); the standalone scorer (N39b-19); the report record and its tables (N39a-36, N39b-14);
the outcome sentences (N39b-05) and the wording lint (N39b-15); level 2 re-runs (N39b-20); the publication of
the tournament itself (the epic's tasks).

## Technical approach

**1. Registration.** A row in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` before the build: mechanism
"publication of a ten-fold gate"; the protocol below by hash, including the ten questions and the rule that
produces their answer key; the falsifier; the fallback; the cost cap (one CPU day; no device); the sessions by
role.

**2. Protocol.**
1. *Build.* From the revealed record of the partial gate (cards, receipts, ledgers, freeze manifests, the
   sealing ledger, verdict records), build the kit and the report with the generator at the commit proposed for
   the tournament. Record the kit's manifest hash and the generator's commit.
2. *Level 1, clean and offline.* On a machine (or a fresh user account in a fresh directory) with no checkout
   of this repository and the network disabled, unpack the kit and run its own level-1 command: the standalone
   scorer recomputes every number of the report from the kit's ledgers. Compare with the report bit for bit.
   Record every file the run opened outside the kit's directory (there must be none beyond the operating
   system's own libraries).
3. *Cold reader.* A fresh-context session that took no part in the gate or the generator receives the kit and
   nothing else, and answers ten factual questions in writing, each with the path inside the kit where the
   answer is found.
4. *Scoring.* The answer key is computed from the report record by a script written before the reader starts;
   numeric answers must match to the printed digits, categorical ones exactly.

**3. The ten questions (DESIGN; fixed at registration, instantiated on named cells of the M3 record).**
1. The ratio of a named cell. 2. Its lower and upper bounds. 3. Why a named cell is descriptive (the failed
reachability condition, the ceiling below the goal, or the missing mechanism). 4. Which rival a named cell's ratio
is taken against, that is, whose restricted mean time is the numerator of R = RMT_rival ÷ RMT_A (the stronger of
the composed rival B and the ablation arm C; arm A is always the denominator). 5. The binding ceiling of a named
stratum and its value. 6. The number of families and campaigns in a named cell and its result type (finite-suite or
population). 7. The deadline of a named stratum and how many runs were censored at it. 8. The verdict of a named
cell and the sentence printed for it. 9. Which cells were voided or which arms disqualified, and on what ground (or
that none was). 10. How the gate was adjudicated (the adjudicator, or `self-adjudicated`) and one thing the limits
statement says the test does not measure.

**4. Falsifier and consequence.**

| Check | Falsifier | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Any published number not reproduced bit for bit | The generator or the scorer is corrected and the dry run repeated; the tournament's freeze waits |
| Self-containment | The kit needs anything outside itself (a file, a network fetch, a tool not listed in its manifest) | The same |
| Readability | Fewer than nine of ten answers correct from the kit alone | The report's tables or the kit's index are revised and the reader test repeated with a new fresh reader and new named cells |

If none fires, the generator's commit is recorded as the tournament's publication generator and enters the pin
freeze; a later change to it needs a repeat of steps 1 and 2. The result is recorded either way.

**5. Record.** The pilot's row gains the result with the kit hash, the generator commit, the level-1 comparison,
the file-access record, the questions, the reader's answers with paths, the key and the score. Level 1 is class
D0 and identical on Linux and macOS; nothing here enters a gate record of the tournament.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pilot's row exists in the pilot register, registered before the build, with the protocol hash, the
      ten questions, the falsifier and the fallback.
- [ ] The kit and report of the M3 record are built with the proposed generator; the kit's manifest hash is
      identical across two builds.
- [ ] Level 1 on the clean, offline machine reproduces every number of the report bit for bit, and the
      file-access record shows nothing opened outside the kit beyond operating-system libraries.
- [ ] The cold reader's ten answers are on file with paths; the score against the mechanical key is recorded;
      nine or more is a pass.
- [ ] A cell constructed as a tie in a scratch copy of the record is reported with the tie sentence, not as a
      win; a descriptive cell prints no verdict word; the wording lint passes on the report.
- [ ] The verdict against the falsifier is recorded, including a negative one, and on a pass the generator's
      commit is listed among the tournament's frozen pins.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::publication_pilot`:
- `answer_key_from_record`: on a synthetic report record with three cells (one confirmatory, one tie, one
  descriptive) the key script returns the ten answers stated in the test.
- `score_rule`: nine matches pass; eight fail; a numeric answer differing in the last printed digit is a miss.
- `question_cells_fixed_at_registration`: changing a named cell after registration fails the record check.
- `generator_commit_pinned_on_pass`: a pass without the commit in the freeze list fails the record check.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_publication_pilot.sh` (header `# e2e: bead=N39ax-06 milestone=M7 labels=tenfold,gauntlet,pilot
seed=0 needs=cpu plants=last-digit,removed-ledger,private-input,builder-reader`): (0) `FROB_TEST_SEED=0 cargo test -p
frob-conformance tenfold::publication_pilot`, and the registration row of
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` read-only (protocol hash and timestamp before the first build); (1)
builds the kit and the report from the M3 record twice (`frob-tenfold kit build --gate M3-partial --out
<scratch>/kit-<n>` and `frob-tenfold report build --gate M3-partial`, N39b-18 and N39b-14; the gate id is the
gate name of the harness records, the name of the store directory `tenfold/M3-partial/`) and compares the manifest
hashes; (2) unpacks the kit into a fresh directory under a scratch root with the
repository path removed from the environment and the network disabled for the process, runs the kit's level-1
command, and compares every number with the report; (3) traces the files the level-1 run opens (`dtruss` or
`fs_usage` on macOS, `strace -e trace=file` on Linux) and asserts none lies outside the kit except system
libraries; (4) runs the key script and scores the recorded reader answers; (5) on scratch copies, plants a
report number changed in its last digit, a kit with one ledger removed, a kit input marked private, and a reader
session listed as a builder, asserting that level 1 fails on the first, the kit's own self-check (`frob-tenfold
kit verify <dir>`) on the second, the kit builder's refusal on the third and the role check on the fourth. It logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_publication_pilot/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson`: `cmd` lines with exact command line, exit
code and wall time; `metric` lines for numbers recomputed, numbers matching, files opened outside the kit and
the reader's score against 9; the kit and report digests; on a mismatch the first differing number with its
table and cell; one `assert` per step; a `summary` line naming GATE-GAUNT-08. The M3 record is revealed; the
script refuses to run on a record with an unrevealed family, and logs no private-scene data.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: ten questions, pass mark nine; zero files outside the kit; bit-for-bit equality at level 1.
- Negative controls: the changed last digit; the removed ledger; a kit built with a private-marked input
  (refused by the kit builder); a reader session that is listed as a builder (the role check fails).
- Determinism lanes: the build twice on one machine; level 1 on Linux and on macOS with identical output
  (class D0).

## Considerations

- The dry run publishes nothing outside the program: its kit and report stay in the delivery log. Whether the
  partial gate's record is itself made public is the owner's decision on repository visibility.
- One reader is a small sample; the test can show that the report is unreadable, not that it is good. The
  tournament's exit review reads the real report with a second fresh reader.
- If the partial gate was recorded `not run` (no approved compute class), there is no record to publish; the
  pilot then runs on the rehearsal record of the tournament's dress rehearsal and says so, which tests the
  generator and the kit and leaves the real-data question open until the tournament.
