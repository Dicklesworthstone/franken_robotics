## Background

The ten-fold test scores arms on sealed missions and sealed acceptance worlds that no arm has seen. If any part
of an arm (its agent, its tools, or the people and sessions that built it) reads sealed content before the gate
closes, the arm may have been fitted to the test, and its times no longer describe held-out missions. The rule
is strict: any such read is a leak; the cells it touches are void for that arm, the families are retired at
once, and if the extent cannot be bounded the whole gate is void and re-run on a rotated suite. A leak is never
repaired by excluding the affected runs and keeping the rest. This bead builds the three detectors the rule
names: the firewall audit, access logs, and canary values that the adjudicator plants in sealed worlds and that
must never appear in an arm's transcript.

Sealed content is: a mission card before that arm's clock starts on it; a sealed world address; a per-world
outcome (a rejected submission returns none); a probe point of a family answer; another arm's transcript.
Roles: the *adjudicator* writes and holds the sealed suite; *builders* and *rival advocates* (and their agent
sessions) are the arms' side.

Rejected alternatives. Access control alone (rejected: it shows what the store served, not what reached a
transcript through a side channel such as a shared cache or a mis-scoped log). Canaries alone (rejected: a
canary shows that content travelled, not how much; the access log bounds the extent).

## Scope

In: canary derivation and planting; the scanner; the access-log audit and its continuity check; mapping a hit to
families and cells; the extent rule; the operational leak alarm of the monitor; the positive controls. Out: the
disqualification record and cell effects (N39b-01); the decision record (N39b-04); the firewall itself (the
qualification-lane beads).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/canary.rs` and `leak.rs` (DESIGN). All of it runs under the
adjudicator role; no arm-side session can list canaries.

1. **Derivation.** For each sealed object o (card, world cluster, probe set) of gate g:
   `token(o) = first 96 bits of BLAKE3-keyed(sealed_key, "tenfold-canary/1" ‖ g ‖ id(o))`.
   Two renderings are planted: a *string canary* (24 hex characters) in a free-text field the object carries
   (a material label, an object name, a comment field of the generator record); and a *numeric canary* (the
   token's low 40 bits as a 13-digit zero-padded decimal: 2⁴⁰ − 1 = 1,099,511,627,775 has 13 digits, so 12
   digits cannot hold 40 bits) in a field with no physical effect on acceptance (a decorative
   identifier, a texture seed). Canaries are a function of the sealed key, so they need not be stored and are
   revealed with the key at close.
2. **Decoys.** 1,000 tokens derived with a different label and planted nowhere. Any decoy hit measures the
   scanner's false-positive behaviour and must be zero.
3. **No behavioural effect.** Planting must not change any acceptance outcome: for each planted world the
   acceptance battery's digest with and without canaries is equal on a reference answer.
4. **Scanner.** Input: every byte an arm produced or received during the gate: agent messages, tool calls and
   results, files written under the arm's roots, process logs. The stream is normalized into several views
   (case-folded; separators and whitespace removed; base64- and hex-decoded spans; digits only) and each view is
   searched with one multi-pattern automaton over all canaries and decoys. A hit records the arm, the file and
   offset, the view, and the object.
5. **Access-log audit.** The sealed store logs every read: sequence number, time, session, role, object. The log
   is hash-chained with heartbeat entries at a fixed period (DESIGN: 10 s). The audit lists every read by a
   session whose role is not adjudicator, every read of a card before the event-log entry that starts that
   arm's clock on it, and every continuity failure (a missing sequence number, a broken hash link, a heartbeat
   gap above three periods).
6. **Firewall audit.** The product's own sealed-field audit is run for arm A's sessions: no verb returned a
   sealed address, outcome, trace or still to a session without the sealing capability before close; refused
   attempts (FROB-REF-840) are listed as attempts, each examined.
7. **From evidence to scope.** Each evidence item maps to objects, objects to families, families to cells.
   Touched families are retired immediately (they join the development pool only after the gate closes; they
   never serve as sealed families again). Extent is *bounded* when every evidence item resolves to an
   enumerable object set and the access log is continuous over the gate; otherwise *unbounded*, and the gate is
   void. The scope is handed to the disqualification engine (N39b-01).
8. **During the run.** The per-cell monitor carries a leak alarm: the scanner runs incrementally on closed
   campaigns; an alarm stops nothing by itself at the tournament and is reported to the adjudicator at once.
9. **Positive controls before opening.** In the rehearsal a canary is injected into a rehearsal transcript in
   each rendering and each encoded view; all must be found, and the decoys must not be.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every sealed object of a gate carries both canary renderings; planting changes no acceptance digest.
- [ ] The scanner finds a planted canary in plain, case-changed, separator-split, hex, base64 and digits-only
      forms, and reports zero decoy hits on the rehearsal corpus.
- [ ] A read of a card before its clock start, a read by a non-adjudicator session, and an access-log gap are
      each reported; a gap makes the extent unbounded.
- [ ] A hit yields the right families and cells, retires the families, and voids the cells for that arm only.
- [ ] Negative control caught: a canary value from a sealed world in a transcript makes the cell void.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::canary` and `tenfold::leak`: `token_is_keyed_and_stable`;
`canary_absent_without_key` (tokens under a different key do not match); `scanner_finds_all_views` (six
encodings); `decoys_never_hit_on_random_corpus` (10⁸ random bytes, seeded: zero hits for 96-bit strings; for
13-digit numerics the expected count is computed and asserted to be below 10⁻²);
`numeric_canary_round_trips` (token low bits 2⁴⁰ − 1 render as `1099511627775` and 0 as `0000000000000`);
`planting_preserves_acceptance_digest`; `early_card_read_is_leak`; `heartbeat_gap_unbounds_extent`;
`hit_maps_to_family_and_cell`; `retired_family_refused_for_sealing`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `leak-drill` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12): plants a canary into arm B's rehearsal
transcript, runs the scanner and audit, and asserts the void cell, the retired family and that arm A's numbers
are untouched. The log records counts of bytes scanned, canaries, decoys, hits by view, the access-log digest and
continuity result. Canary values are never written to a log: hits are logged by object id and offset only.

### Known answers and negative controls

Control TF-CX-12 (a canary in a transcript). A mutant scanner that searches only the plain view must miss the
base64 control and fail. A mutant audit that ignores heartbeats must fail the gap test. Determinism: the scan
result is a pure function of the transcripts and the key.

## Considerations

- A canary cannot detect a leak that an arm does not echo; the access log and the firewall audit cover reads.
  Neither covers information passed by a person outside any logged channel; that residual risk is stated in
  every report's protocol section.
- Numeric canaries are weaker than string canaries (40 bits); a numeric hit alone is an alarm to examine, a
  string hit is evidence.
- Privacy: transcripts may contain private scene content; the scanner reads them in place and logs offsets only.
- Revisit if an arm's toolchain rewrites identifiers (canonicalizes names) so that string canaries cannot
  survive a legitimate read; then the canary moves to a field the toolchain must preserve.
