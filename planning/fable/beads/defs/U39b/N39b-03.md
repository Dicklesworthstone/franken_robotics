## Background

A kill gate names in advance a trigger and what happens when it fires; it is never quietly re-scoped, and its
decision is recorded as an architecture decision record (ADR) in the milestone in which it occurs.
KILL-TENFOLD-CERT is a standing kill gate of the ten-fold test, in force from the partial gate at M3 through the
tournament at M7. Trigger: one false certificate from an arm. Fallback, named in advance: that arm is
disqualified from the scoped claim; for arm A (this system) the proof kind is also suspended and the mission
subsystem's own kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT applies.

Why one is enough. A certificate is the product's strongest evidence class: a checked mathematical statement
about a family of designs or worlds. Its value is that it cannot be wrong within its stated scope. A scoring rule
that tolerated a small rate of false certificates would let an arm buy speed with unsound proofs, and every other
certificate of that kind would lose its meaning. The time score is never improved by lowering what counts as
accepted.

A certificate is *false* when the arm submitted it, the arm's declared checker accepted it, and the adjudicator's
direct evaluation or any later audit shows it false within its stated scope (a concrete execution, allocation or
world inside the scope contradicts it). Not false: a truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate, a certificate
with a narrower scope than was asked; those cost coverage and time.

## Scope

In: the decision procedure each time the trigger is alleged; the record; what changes downstream. Out: detecting
the falsity (adjudication: N39a-07 and the gate's certificate audit in BD-317); computing the scope and recording
the disqualification event (N39b-01); the arm-A internal procedure (N25b-03, the false-certificate procedure)
and its cause decision (N25b-04, KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT), which step 4 invokes and does not re-implement.

## Technical approach

The procedure is the module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/killcert.rs` behind two subcommands of the
operator binary (DESIGN), adjudicator role only: `frob-tenfold kill-cert decide --gate <id> --allegation <file>
[--json]` (steps 1 to 5; exit 0 whether the allegation is closed `not false` or the disqualification is
recorded, since a recorded decision is not a refusal; 3 with `role-not-permitted`) and `frob-tenfold kill-cert
record --gate <id> --exit <Mn> [--json]` (steps 6 and 7).

1. **Allegation.** The adjudicator (or an auditor after publication) files the certificate's hash, its embedded
   statement and scope, the checker and version that accepted it, and the contradicting witness: a probe point with
   exact rational evaluation, an SMT or interval counter-model on the raw mission statement, or a replayable world
   from dense sampling.
2. **Verification, two checks.** (a) The witness lies inside the certificate's stated scope, read from the statement
   embedded in the bundle and never from the submission's description. (b) The contradiction is reproduced by a second
   method of the adjudicator's choosing (for a numeric witness, exact rational re-evaluation; for a sampled world,
   replay in the adjudicating simulator at its stated tolerance). If either check fails the allegation is closed as
   `not false`, with the reason.
3. **Decision.** If both hold, the reading's outcome is `fired` (printed "killed: fallback adopted") for that arm and
   scope: every cell of the gate in which the arm used that proof kind has no ratio for that arm and the cell verdict
   is `disqualified`. Nothing is awarded to any other arm. A cell whose rival is disqualified has no denominator and
   is re-run at the next gate.
4. **Arm A only.** The proof kind and checker version are disabled in the registry until the cause is found; every
   stored bundle that used them is marked withdrawn; dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to `Tested`; the
   counterexample becomes a permanent fixture of the checker's mutation corpus and a permanent negative control of the
   harness; KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT then decides whether the proof rule or its implementation was at fault.
5. **Late discovery.** A certificate shown false after the report is published: the published cell is amended to
   `disqualified`, the amendment is dated, and the report's digest changes; the previous version stays retrievable. An
   accepted answer later shown wrong within its scope, without a false certificate, scores that mission as unresolved
   at H for the arm that submitted it and does not trigger this gate.
6. **ADR content.** The allegation, both verification results, the scope (cells), the arm, the consequences applied,
   and the list of downstream changes: report wording for the affected cells; for arm A, the list of suspended proof
   kinds and the beads re-read against the suspension.

7. **Record, in the one form of kill-gate decisions (N40-03).** The row KILL-TENFOLD-CERT (`mode = "standing"`,
   from M3, no `scope_kind`: decided whole, N40-28) carries one decision entry per reading, `{exit, outcome,
   adr, date, session, receipts}`: `fired` when at least one allegation of the reading was verified by both
   checks, `not-fired` when none was. The arm, the proof kind and the cells are the ADR's content, not register
   state. The ADR of a reading is `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-CERT-<milestone>.md`, with the six parts of step 6
   for every allegation of the reading, or the statement that the gate's certificate audit filed none.
   `kill-cert record` writes the ADR draft and the decision-entry fragment into the gate directory
   (`tenfold/<gate>/kill/KILL-TENFOLD-CERT/`) when the gate's certificate audit closes. The session that enters
   the gate's row (N40-15 for the partial gate, N40-16 for the tournament) commits the ADR and enters the entry,
   citing in `receipts` the receipt of that row's part under `receipts/gates/` (a `not-fired` entry needs one).
   The row is standing, so an entry is due at every later exit as well, with its own ADR: at an exit in whose
   milestone no gate of the test ran it is `not-fired`, cites the latest gate's receipt and states that no
   allegation was filed since. An allegation verified inside a milestone whose exit is already decided (step 5)
   adds an entry with `reading = "reopened"` and the ADR suffix `-reopened` (N40-03, step 6). This bead also
   writes the gate's owner declaration, which N40-30 compares with the ledger, in the `contract.toml` of
   `frob-conformance`: `[[gate]]` with `role = "owner"` and the ledger's current `trigger`, `fallback`,
   `decided`, `mode` and `reads` copied, not restated; the `decision` block is added with each entry. Without
   the declaration `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` fails `owner-declaration-missing` from
   the M3 exit on.

Fail-closed posture while an allegation is open: the affected cells print `under audit` and carry no verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the planted false certificate of this bead's script the arm is disqualified in exactly the cells that
      used the proof kind, and the ADR draft is produced with all six parts.
- [ ] A planted narrower-scope certificate and a planted truthful `unknown` are closed as `not false`.
- [ ] A witness outside the stated scope is rejected at check (a).
- [ ] For arm A the suspension propagates: the proof kind is disabled and dependent answers are downgraded.
- [ ] `kill-cert record` writes `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-CERT-<milestone>.md` as a draft and a decision-entry
      fragment that loads on a scratch copy of the register: `fired` for the planted gate; `not-fired` with a
      receipt for a clean audit; a `not-fired` fragment with no receipt, and an entry with `scope =
      "cell:wheeled"`, are refused (`decision-scope-invalid` for the second, N40-28).
- [ ] The owner declaration of KILL-TENFOLD-CERT is in the `contract.toml` of `frob-conformance` with the
      ledger's text, and `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` finds exactly one.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::killcert`: `witness_outside_scope_not_false`;
`second_method_must_reproduce`; `late_discovery_amends_report_and_keeps_history`;
`open_allegation_prints_under_audit`; `reading_outcome` (no verified allegation gives `not-fired`, one gives
`fired`, an open allegation gives no entry); `reopened_reading_has_suffix` (a late discovery after the M3 entry
gives an M3 entry with `reading = "reopened"` and the ADR path `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-TENFOLD-CERT-M3-reopened.md`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_kill_cert.sh`, owned by this bead and built only on its dependencies (header `bead=N39b-03
milestone=M3 labels=tenfold,e2e,kill-gate gates=KILL-TENFOLD-CERT seed=390303 needs=cpu
plants=unverified-allegation,outside-scope,narrow-scope-as-false`), on a synthetic gate directory of three cells
with two synthetic arms: (1) `FROB_TEST_SEED=390303 cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::killcert`; (2) the
planted input of TF-CX-11 (N39a-07's fixture `crates/frob-conformance/fixtures/tenfold/controls/
false_certificate/`: a linear-program dual witness with one altered coefficient that its declared, deliberately
weakened checker accepts) through `frob-tenfold kill-cert decide --gate <id> --allegation <file> --json`,
asserting both check results, the scope and the verdict `disqualified` in exactly the cells that used the proof
kind (N39b-01's record); (3) a narrower-scope certificate and a truthful `unknown`, each closed `not false`; (4)
`frob-tenfold kill-cert record --gate <id> --exit M3 --json`, asserting the six parts of the ADR draft and the
fragment, which is appended to a scratch copy of the register under `work/` and loaded with `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file work/gates.toml` (N40-01); (5) the plants: a build under
`--features gauntlet-mutations` that disqualifies on an unverified allegation, a witness outside the stated
scope, and a narrower-scope certificate offered as false, each caught. Logged as NDJSON through the STD-E2E
helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, seed): the certificate
hash, both check results, cells before and after, the outcome of the reading, the digests of the ADR draft and
of the fragment, each plant as a `negative-control` assertion with `caught`; on failure the first differing
value and the gate directory's path; a final `summary` with `gates: [{id: "KILL-TENFOLD-CERT", measured_ok,
controls_caught}]` and `gates_claimable: false`. The partial-gate script `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh`
(N39b-12, which depends on this bead) runs this script as a stage and repeats the drill on its rehearsal gate
(its sub-step `false-cert-drill`).

### Known answers and negative controls

Control TF-CX-11. Negative control for the procedure itself: a build that disqualifies on an unverified
allegation must fail `second_method_must_reproduce`.

## Considerations

- The builders wrote the public checker `frob-verify`; this gate exists because that checker is not the last
  word in the test.
- A true certificate of an uninteresting statement is not false; it earns nothing because acceptance requires
  the card's mandatory obligations.
- What would change the design: nothing short of a dated amendment to the gate before a sealed run.
