## Background

A kill gate names in advance a trigger and what happens when it fires; it is never quietly re-scoped, and its
decision is recorded as an architecture decision record (ADR) in the milestone in which it occurs.
KILL-TENFOLD-CERT is a standing kill gate of the ten-fold test, in force from the partial gate at M3 through the
tournament at M7. Trigger: one false certificate from an arm. Fallback, named in advance: that arm is
disqualified from the scoped claim; for arm A (this system) the proof kind is also suspended and the mission
subsystem's own kill gate KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT applies.

Why one is enough. A certificate is the product's strongest evidence class: a checked mathematical statement
about a family of designs or worlds. Its value is that it cannot be wrong within its stated scope. A scoring rule
that tolerated a small rate of false certificates would let an arm buy speed with unsound proofs, and every other
certificate of that kind would lose its meaning. The time score is never improved by lowering what counts as
accepted.

A certificate is *false* when the arm submitted it, the arm's declared checker accepted it, and the adjudicator's
direct evaluation or any later audit shows it false within its stated scope (a concrete execution, allocation or
world inside the scope contradicts it). Not false: a truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate, a certificate
with a narrower scope than was asked; those cost coverage and time.

## Scope

In: the decision procedure each time the trigger is alleged; the record; what changes downstream. Out: detecting
the falsity (adjudication: N39a-07 and the gate's certificate audit in BD-317); computing the scope and recording
the disqualification event (N39b-01); the arm-A internal procedure (N25b-03, the false-certificate procedure)
and its cause decision (N25b-04, KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT), which step 4 invokes and does not re-implement.

## Technical approach

1. **Allegation.** The adjudicator (or an auditor after publication) files the certificate's hash, its embedded
   statement and scope, the checker and version that accepted it, and the contradicting witness: a probe point
   with exact rational evaluation, an SMT or interval counter-model on the raw mission statement, or a replayable
   world from dense sampling.
2. **Verification, two checks.** (a) The witness lies inside the certificate's stated scope, read from the
   statement embedded in the bundle and never from the submission's description. (b) The contradiction is
   reproduced by a second method of the adjudicator's choosing (for a numeric witness, exact rational
   re-evaluation; for a sampled world, replay in the adjudicating simulator at its stated tolerance). If either
   check fails the allegation is closed as `not false`, with the reason.
3. **Decision.** If both hold, the gate status is `killed: fallback adopted` for that arm and scope: every cell
   of the gate in which the arm used that proof kind has no ratio for that arm and the cell verdict is
   `disqualified`. Nothing is awarded to any other arm. A cell whose rival is disqualified has no denominator
   and is re-run at the next gate.
4. **Arm A only.** The proof kind and checker version are disabled in the registry until the cause is found;
   every stored bundle that used them is marked withdrawn; dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to
   `Tested`; the counterexample becomes a permanent fixture of the checker's mutation corpus and a permanent
   negative control of the harness; KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT then decides whether the proof rule or its
   implementation was at fault.
5. **Late discovery.** A certificate shown false after the report is published: the published cell is amended
   to `disqualified`, the amendment is dated, and the report's digest changes; the previous version stays
   retrievable. An accepted answer later shown wrong within its scope, without a false certificate, scores that
   mission as unresolved at H for the arm that submitted it and does not trigger this gate.
6. **ADR content.** The allegation, both verification results, the scope (cells), the arm, the consequences
   applied, and the list of downstream changes: report wording for the affected cells; for arm A, the list of
   suspended proof kinds and the beads re-read against the suspension.

Fail-closed posture while an allegation is open: the affected cells print `under audit` and carry no verdict.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The procedure is exercised in the rehearsal on a planted false certificate: the arm is disqualified in
      exactly the cells that used the proof kind, and the ADR draft is produced with all six parts.
- [ ] A planted narrower-scope certificate and a planted truthful `unknown` are closed as `not false`.
- [ ] A witness outside the stated scope is rejected at check (a).
- [ ] For arm A the suspension propagates: the proof kind is disabled and dependent answers are downgraded.
- [ ] Each real firing has an ADR dated in its milestone; the standing status is reviewed at M3 and M7 exits.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::killcert`: `witness_outside_scope_not_false`;
`second_method_must_reproduce`; `late_discovery_amends_report_and_keeps_history`;
`open_allegation_prints_under_audit`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `false-cert-drill` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12): submits a planted false certificate
for a synthetic arm (a linear-program dual witness with one altered coefficient that its declared, deliberately
weakened checker accepts), runs both verification checks, and asserts the scope, the verdict `disqualified` and
the ADR draft. Logged: the certificate hash, both check results, cells before and after.

### Known answers and negative controls

Control TF-CX-11. Negative control for the procedure itself: a build that disqualifies on an unverified
allegation must fail `second_method_must_reproduce`.

## Considerations

- The builders wrote the public checker `frob-verify`; this gate exists because that checker is not the last
  word in the test.
- A true certificate of an uninteresting statement is not false; it earns nothing because acceptance requires
  the card's mandatory obligations.
- What would change the design: nothing short of a dated amendment to the gate before a sealed run.
