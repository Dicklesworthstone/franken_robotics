## Background

Level 1 of the replication kit shows that the published numbers follow from the published records. It cannot
show that the records describe what the arms actually do. Level 2 does a sampled check of that: from the frozen
manifests, on hardware with a comparable fingerprint, one sampled pair per stratum is run again for every arm.
TARGET (part of GATE-TENFOLD-08): the acceptance outcome is the same and the time is within a factor of 1.5 in
at least 90% of the sampled pairs. An external party doing this is what lifts the label `self-adjudicated` far
enough for the words "independently accepted" and "ten-fold" to be used; until it has passed, a result is
labelled `unreplicated`.

Terms. *Pair*: the campaigns of the arms on one mission with one campaign index. *Frozen manifest*: an arm's
published, content-addressed configuration (engine, learner, outer loop, tool layer, agent guide, agent model
revision and caps, caches, standing assets). *Fingerprint*: the recorded description of a machine (devices,
driver, memory, cores, measured numerical properties).

## Scope

In: the sampling rule; the comparability rule; the re-run procedure for each arm; the comparison and its
statistic; the replication record; what the result changes. Out: the kit's contents (N39b-18); level 1
(N39b-19); who the external party is (an owner decision).

## Technical approach

1. **Sampling (fixed before the gate closes).** For each stratum run, one family is drawn by a public rule: the
   family f whose key BLAKE3(suite hash ‖ stratum ‖ family id f ‖ "level-2") is smallest, compared as an
   unsigned big-endian integer, among the stratum's families (the family id must enter the hash, or every
   family would get the same key); the pair is that family's first campaign index. The draw is entered in the event log with the suite hash, so it
   cannot be chosen after times are known. With S strata there are S sampled pairs and S × arms re-run campaigns.
2. **Comparable hardware (DESIGN; this bead owns the rule, and the gate check of N40-17 applies this same
   function to the logged fingerprints).** `comparable(gate_fp, replica_fp)` over the two `frob.fingerprint/1`
   records (BD-029) holds when: (i) per campaign, the same device `name` and `architecture` (an RTX 4090,
   `sm_89`) and the same device count as the gate's matched-device envelope, and the same CPU core count per
   campaign; (ii) `driver`, `runtime` and `compiler` in the same major release; (iii) the whole `arithmetic`
   block equal (IEEE probe digest and attainable class, subnormal mode, 64-bit integer and atomic support,
   canaries `pass`). The fingerprint's `identity` digest (N21c-05) is not compared: it includes the host and
   the device's stable identifier, which differ on any other machine by construction. Measured rates
   (bandwidth, launch cost) are recorded with their ratio to the gate machine's beside each time ratio, as
   description: rates agree only within 5% between runs on one machine, and the factor 1.5 of step 5 already
   absorbs the spread between two cards of one model, so a rate condition would refuse most third-party
   machines for no gain. Anything else is recorded as `hardware: not comparable` and the time comparison is
   reported but not counted toward the 90%; the acceptance-outcome comparison still counts.
3. **Re-run of an arm.** Rebuild the arm from its manifest: arm A at the gate's commit with the pinned stack
   lock; incumbent arms from their pins by the kit's fetch instructions (incumbent code is never in the kit);
   the same agent model revision and caps; a fresh context; the same mission card and permitted inputs; the
   same resource caps; the same campaign seed where the provider allows the agent's sampling seed to be fixed.
   Standing assets are either rebuilt (their rebuild time is compared with the declared S) or fetched by hash.
4. **Adjudication of the re-run.** The replicating party runs the released acceptance code with the revealed
   key, so that the same sealed acceptance worlds are drawn for the same submission index.
5. **Comparison.** Per re-run campaign: `outcome_same` (accepted against accepted, or censored against
   censored), and the time ratio q = max(T_rerun, T_orig) ÷ min(T_rerun, T_orig) using restricted times
   min(T, H). A sampled pair *replicates* when every arm's outcome is the same and every arm's q ≤ 1.5.
6. **Statistic.** The share of sampled pairs that replicate; TARGET at least 90%. With S strata the count needed
   is ⌈0.9·S⌉ (for S = 10: 9; for S = 4: 4). The record also prints, per arm, the geometric mean of q and the
   worst q, and the ratio of the re-run pair's times beside the original pair's, as description only.
7. **Record (`replication.json` in the kit's `report/` directory).** The replicating party's identity and role
   statement (no authorship in any arm, for an external replication), `party` (`external` or `internal`), the
   machine's fingerprint and comparability verdict, the sampled pairs, each campaign's outcome and time, the
   statistic, and the status: `replicated`, `not replicated` or `incomplete`. **The verdict rule's view.** The
   verdict rule (N39a-32) reads this record only through its input view `ReplicationView {external_rerun_passed:
   bool, hash}`, and the record satisfies it by one conversion `ReplicationRecord::view()` in
   `tenfold/level2.rs`: `external_rerun_passed` is true exactly when `status = replicated` and `party =
   external`; `not replicated`, `incomplete` and an internal `replicated` (printed `replicated: internal`) all
   give false; `hash` is the BLAKE3 hash of the record's canonical bytes, the same hash the kit's manifest lists
   for `report/replication.json`. A record without `party` is refused (`verdict-input-missing`), never defaulted.
8. **Effect.** `replicated` by an external party removes `unreplicated` and, under self-adjudication, allows the
   words that were waiting. `not replicated` keeps the label, and the differing pairs are investigated; a
   re-run that reveals a defect in a frozen arm voids the affected cells for a later gate. Level 2 never
   changes a published number.
9. **Command (DESIGN).** `frob-tenfold kit level2 <kit-dir> --party external|internal --out <dir> --json`, a
   subcommand of the operator binary (N39a-01) beside N39b-18's `kit build` and `kit verify`: it refuses a kit
   that fails `kit verify`, recomputes and logs the draw of step 1, applies step 2 to the replica's fingerprint,
   performs steps 3 to 5 (incumbent arms through `frob-tenfold incumbent build` and `incumbent run`, BD-039), and
   writes the record of step 7. Exit codes are N39-REF's: 0 when the record's status is `replicated`, 2 when it
   is `not replicated` or `incomplete` (a finding, not a refusal), 3 on a typed refusal, 9 on an integrity fault.
   The replicating party runs this command; the kit's `README.md` (N39b-18) names it.

Agent nondeterminism is the main source of time variation; the factor 1.5 and the 90% share are TARGETs chosen
to tolerate it while still catching an arm whose recorded times cannot be approached.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The sampled pairs are determined by the public rule and logged before the gate closes.
- [ ] A dry run of level 2 on the rehearsal gate, on a second host, completes for every arm and writes the
      record with a status.
- [ ] A re-run with a planted slowdown of an arm by a factor of 2 yields `not replicated` for that pair.
- [ ] A machine with a different device model is recorded `not comparable` and its times are not counted.
- [ ] The record carries the no-authorization flags, all false, and never alters the report's digest.
- [ ] The record converts to the verdict rule's `ReplicationView`: a passed external re-run gives
      `external_rerun_passed: true`, a failed, incomplete or internal one gives false, each with the record's hash.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::level2`: `sample_rule_is_deterministic`; `sample_logged_before_close`;
`q_is_symmetric` (600 s against 900 s and 900 s against 600 s both give 1.5: replicates; 600 against 901 does
not); `censored_matches_censored`; `needed_count_is_ceiling` (S = 10: 9; S = 4: 4; S = 11: 10);
`not_comparable_excluded_from_share` (a replica fingerprint with device name `RTX 3090`, or with a different
IEEE probe digest, or with driver major release one higher → `not comparable`; one differing from the gate's
only in host, device UUID and a bandwidth 4% lower → `comparable`); `record_converts_to_replication_view` (an external record with 9 of 10
pairs replicating, status `replicated` → `ReplicationView {external_rerun_passed: true, hash}` with `hash` equal
to BLAKE3 of the record's canonical bytes and to the manifest's entry for the file; the same record with 8 of 10,
status `not replicated` → `external_rerun_passed: false` and its own, different hash; an internal `replicated`
record → false; `incomplete` → false; a record with `party` removed → `verdict-input-missing`).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_level2.sh`, owned by this bead and built only on its dependencies (header `bead=N39b-20
milestone=M7 labels=tenfold,e2e gates=GATE-TENFOLD-08 seed=392001 needs=cpu
plants=slow-arm,outcome-flip,other-device,party-missing`), on a kit built with `frob-tenfold kit build` from a
synthetic gate directory with two synthetic CPU arms and four strata (labelled `synthetic`, never claimable):
(1) `FROB_TEST_SEED=392001 cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::level2`; (2) `frob-tenfold kit level2 <kit>
--party internal --out <scratch> --json`: assert that the four sampled pairs equal the public rule's draw
recomputed by the script from the suite hash and that the draw's event-log entry precedes the close; assert each
arm rebuilt from its manifest, its pair re-run and adjudicated with the released acceptance code, and the record
written with status `replicated: internal`, converting to `external_rerun_passed = false`; (3) the plants, each
through `e2e_expect_caught`: a factor-2 slowdown of one arm (q = 2 > 1.5, `not replicated`, exit 2); an outcome
flip at q = 1.0 (`not replicated`); a replica fingerprint fixture with device name `RTX 3090` (`hardware: not
comparable`, the time not counted, the outcome still counted); the record with `party` removed (the verdict's
view refuses `verdict-input-missing`). Logged as NDJSON through the STD-E2E helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, seed): the sample draw with its hash inputs, the
fingerprint comparison field by field, each q as a `metric` against 1.5, the share as a `metric` against 0.9 with
the count needed ⌈0.9·S⌉, the record's hash, each plant as a `negative-control` assertion with `caught`; on
failure the first differing campaign and the record's path; a final `summary` with assertion counts. The kit
script `scripts/e2e/tenfold_kit.sh` (N39b-21, which depends on this bead) runs this script as a stage and then
runs `kit level2` on the second host in its step `level2`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the count rule; q at the boundary. Negative controls: a planted factor-2 slowdown; a re-run whose
acceptance outcome flips (accepted originally, rejected on re-run) must fail the pair whatever its time.

## Considerations

- Cost: one pair per stratum per arm at up to H each; for ten strata, three arms and H = 4 h the worst case is
  120 device-hours on the replicating machine.
- If no external party is engaged, an internal fresh-context session may run level 2 as a rehearsal; the status
  is then `replicated: internal` and the labels do not change.
- A provider that retires the pinned agent model revision makes level 2 impossible later; the kit's README
  states the date after which that is expected, if known.
