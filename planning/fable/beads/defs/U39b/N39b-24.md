## Background

Answers in the ten-fold test are accepted in a simulator that neither arm used for training, tuning or
screening on that stratum, whose contact model was derived independently of both arms, that runs
deterministically on a CPU, and that is pinned by revision. The reason is that an arm scored in its own engine
is scored on its own modelling errors, and two programs that share a solver have correlated error. The default
candidate is a CPU multibody engine with its own convex contact solver at a pinned release. Such a simulator
may not exist for every stratum: it may be unable to represent terrain, liquid films or tracks, or an arm may
already use it as a tool. KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ is the kill gate for that case.

Trigger: no neutral simulator passes the adjudicator-acceptance gate (GATE-TENFOLD-05) for a stratum. Fallback,
named in advance: cross-home adjudication, labelled; if neither home referee can run the other arm's answers,
the stratum is `descriptive`. Decided at M3 for the strata of the partial gate and at M7 for the rest.
*Cross-home adjudication*: an answer must pass in the highest-trust tier of both arms (this system's referee
and the rival's CPU reference), run by the adjudicator, and the report labels the stratum
`adjudication: cross-home`.

## Scope

In: the reading per stratum; the fallback's conditions; the label; the ADR; downstream effects on verdicts. Out:
choosing candidates (N39b-26); running the acceptance measurement (the adjudication beads).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs per stratum.** The candidate list from the simulator decision (N39b-26), in preference order. For
   each candidate: the four conditions (not used by any arm on the stratum; independently derived contact
   model; deterministic on a CPU; pinned), whether it can represent every mission of the stratum's development
   pool through the neutral exchange description, and its GATE-TENFOLD-05 measurement.
2. **Reading.** The first candidate that meets the four conditions, represents the stratum and passes the
   acceptance gate is the stratum's adjudicating simulator; the gate does not fire. If none does, the gate fires
   for that stratum.
3. **Fallback test.** Cross-home adjudication is available when each home referee can run the other arm's
   answers: this system's referee can import and run the rival's design and controller behind the standard
   observation and action interface, and the rival's CPU reference can run ours. Both directions are tested on
   the stratum's development missions with the 20 planted answers (10 that must pass, 10 that must fail); each
   home referee must classify all 20 correctly within its own tolerances.
4. **Outcomes.** (a) Neutral simulator found: label `adjudication: neutral`. (b) Fired, cross-home available:
   an answer is accepted only if it passes in both homes; label `adjudication: cross-home` on every number of
   the stratum; the `adjudicate` phase of the ledger includes both runs. (c) Fired, cross-home unavailable: the
   stratum is `descriptive`; its cells cannot be confirmatory and cannot carry the word.
5. **Effect on the word.** The verdict `ten-fold` requires "the adjudication condition": DESIGN reading, stated
   here so that it is fixed before sealing: a neutral simulator, or cross-home adjudication, operated by the
   adjudicator; under cross-home the sentence carries the label. A `descriptive` stratum cites nothing for or
   against the headline bet.
6. **Bias note recorded with outcome (b).** Passing in both homes is stricter for both arms than passing in a
   third model, and the gap between an arm's home simulator and the other home is that arm's risk, equally;
   the report prints, per arm, the share of submissions that passed at home and failed in the other home.
7. **ADR content.** Per stratum: candidates tried, the failed condition or measurement for each, the fallback
   test results, the outcome and label, and the list of cells whose status changed.

Fail-closed posture until the decision is recorded for a stratum: its sealed suite is not opened.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every stratum of the gate has one of the three outcomes in the ADR before its suite is opened.
- [ ] For outcome (b) both home referees classified 20 of 20 planted answers; the label appears on every
      number of the stratum in the report.
- [ ] For outcome (c) the stratum's cells are registered as `descriptive` and the pre-analysis names no
      confirmatory cell there.
- [ ] A planted candidate that an arm used during tuning is rejected at the first condition.
- [ ] The decision is dated in M3 (strata of the partial gate) and again in M7 (all strata run).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::killadj`: `first_passing_candidate_wins`;
`candidate_used_by_arm_is_not_neutral`; `nineteen_of_twenty_fails_acceptance`; `cross_home_needs_both_directions`;
`no_fallback_makes_descriptive`; `label_propagates_to_every_ratio`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/adjudication_dry_run.sh` (the adjudication pilot's script) gains a step `adj-fallback` under this
bead: with the neutral candidate disabled by a plant it runs the cross-home test on development missions and
asserts the label; with the rival's reference also disabled it asserts `descriptive`. Logged: per candidate the
four conditions and the 20-answer count against 20, the outcome, the ADR draft digest.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: 20 of 20 is required; 19 of 20 fails. Negative controls: an arm that touched the candidate during
tuning (TF-CX-15); a cross-home run in which only one direction was tested (must not count as available).

## Considerations

- Which simulator adjudicates each stratum, and whether it can represent terrain, films and tracks, is an open
  question owned by the adjudicator with the verification lead; this gate is its fallback.
- Under the fail-closed adjudicator (a project session), every ratio is also `self-adjudicated`; the two labels
  are independent and both print.
- Risk (pre-mortem): common-mode error, where every tier shares assumed card values and accepted decisions fail
  neutral adjudication; cross-home adjudication does not remove error shared by both homes.
