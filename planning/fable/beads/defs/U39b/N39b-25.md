## Background

A frozen decision rule is enumerated before sampling: for every fixed-sample rule, the set of verdicts it can
return at that sample is listed first, and a verdict that cannot be reached must not be promised. The origin is
a planning pilot in which a rule with 64 pairs could return three of its four verdicts, the fourth being first
reachable at 86 pairs. For the ten-fold test the same discipline is a kill gate. Trigger: a cell's pre-analysis
fails a reachability condition. Fallback, named in advance: the cell is opened as `descriptive` and says so
before any sealed run; it is enlarged for the next gate. Decided at M3 and M7, before the sealed suite is opened.

A *cell* is a stratum and a regime. A cell is *confirmatory* only if its recorded pre-analysis shows all four:
(1) P(`ten-fold`) at least 0.8 at a true ratio of 15; (2) P(`withdrawn`) at most 0.05 at a true ratio of 5;
(3) the exact guard reachable at its adjusted level; (4) the horizon cap H ÷ RMT_A on the development suite at
least 10, since R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A whatever the rival does (the rival's restricted time cannot exceed H). At most four
confirmatory cells are named per gate.

## Scope

In: reading the four conditions per candidate cell; the descriptive opening and its announcement; the ADR; the
enlargement plan. Out: computing the pre-analysis (statistics beads; the pilot N39b-16 at M7); the deadline rule
(N39b-30); the population-size refusal of the pre-analysis verb (N39b-34).

## Technical approach

1. **Inputs.** For each cell proposed as confirmatory: the planned m, c, n = m·c and H; the number of
   confirmatory cells (which fixes the Holm-adjusted level: with four cells the first step is 0.0125 and needs
   18 of 24); the pre-analysis record with its hash; RMT_A on the development suite.
2. **Reading, condition by condition.**
   - (3) first, because it is arithmetic: the guard count X of families with per-family ratio above 5 is compared
     with a binomial(m, ½) tail. Reachable means some x ≤ m has P(X ≥ x) at most the adjusted level. At 0.05: 5 of
     5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12, 12 of 16, 17 of 24, 22 of 32. At m = 4 even 4 of 4 has tail 1/16 = 0.0625: not
     reachable, and a four-family cell is refused as a confirmatory cell. At 0.0125: m = 6 is not reachable
     (1/64 = 0.0156); m = 7 is (1/128 = 0.0078).
   - (4): cap = H ÷ RMT_A. Below 10: the deadline is raised by the deadline rule until the cap is at least 10,
     or the condition fails.
   - (1) and (2): read from the simulated table of the pre-analysis at the measured per-family spread and
     censoring rates.
3. **Decision.** All four hold: the cell is confirmatory. Any fails: the gate fires for the cell; it is opened
   as `descriptive`; the registration, the report's protocol section and the event log say so before any
   sealed run of the cell; nothing from it is cited as passing or failing the headline bet. The number of
   confirmatory cells is then recounted and the adjusted levels of the remaining cells are fixed once, before
   sealing (they are not relaxed later if a cell is voided).
4. **Enlargement plan.** For a fired cell the ADR states the smallest m at which the four conditions would hold
   under the measured spread (from the pre-analysis grid), the families that must be authored, and the gate at
   which the enlarged cell will run.
5. **Other ways a cell is descriptive** (not this gate, listed so the statuses are not confused): n below 10;
   more than 5% of pairs excluded; the cell was never named confirmatory; no adjudication is available for the
   stratum.
6. **ADR content.** Per candidate cell: the four readings with their numbers, the decision, the final list of
   confirmatory cells with adjusted levels, and the enlargement plans.

Fail-closed posture: a cell with no recorded pre-analysis is `descriptive`; the scorer refuses to issue any
other verdict for it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every candidate cell has four recorded readings and a decision, hashed into the event log before the
      suite-open entry.
- [ ] A four-family confirmatory request is refused (FROB-REF-532), and a six-family request at level 0.0125 is
      refused for the same reason.
- [ ] A cell with H ÷ RMT_A = 8 either has its deadline raised to reach 10 or opens as `descriptive`.
- [ ] The report's protocol section lists each `descriptive` cell with the failed condition, and that section's
      digest predates the first sealed run.
- [ ] The scorer returns `descriptive` for a fired cell whatever its data.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::killreach`: `guard_reachability_table` (the counts above; none at
m = 4; none at m = 6 for 0.0125); `horizon_cap_reading` (14,400 ÷ 1,800 = 8 fails; ÷ 1,200 = 12 passes);
`power_condition_read_from_table` (0.79 fails, 0.80 passes); `withdrawal_condition` (0.05 passes, 0.051 fails);
`levels_fixed_after_recount`; `fired_cell_scores_descriptive`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `preanalysis` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12) with the plant `four-family`, and of
`scripts/e2e/tenfold_decision_rule_pilot.sh` (N39b-16): each condition is logged as an assertion with its
threshold; the descriptive opening is asserted to precede the first campaign in the event log.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the critical counts; the illustrative row for six families (P(`ten-fold`) = 0.57 at a true ratio
of 15 with spread 0.8: fails condition 1). Negative controls: a four-family confirmatory cell (TF-CX-07); a
descriptive announcement dated after the first sealed run (must be a finding of the auditor).

## Considerations

- Risk (register): the tournament cannot confirm a true ratio of ten for lack of power. This gate does not
  create power; it stops the program from promising a verdict it cannot return.
- Raising H to satisfy the horizon cap raises the worst-case cost of the cell in proportion (m·c·arms·H).
- A cell opened as descriptive still costs its full compute; the owner may prefer to drop it, which is recorded
  as `not run` with the reason.
