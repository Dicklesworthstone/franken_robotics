## Background

A ratio against a rival is only as meaningful as the rival is strong. In the ten-fold test the rival arms (arm
B, the strongest composed rival; arm C, a short program over the rival's engine; and the composed incumbent W)
are built and tuned by *rival advocates*: people or agent sessions who have written no code of this system and
whose task is to make the rival finish the development missions as soon as possible. Who they are and how much
they may spend is an open question owned by the project owner. It blocks the freeze-and-seal gate
(GATE-TENFOLD-04), because no arm can be frozen before its advocates exist.

Fail-closed default: agent sessions that have contributed nothing to this system, 40 device-hours per stratum,
and the label `rival: internally advocated` on every result.

Why the budget has a floor. An under-tuned rival inflates the ratio. DESIGN: the advocates receive a tuning
budget at least equal to the compute the builders logged on the same development suite and never below 40
device-hours per stratum. They may improve the rival in any way a competent engineer would: hyperparameters,
reward shaping, curricula, warm caches, policy reuse, batching, analytic pruning.

## Scope

In: the content of the decision OPEN-TENFOLD-3 and its record (the ADR `ADR-PROG-15` and its supporting file
`decisions/OPEN-TENFOLD-3.md`); the options; what advocates are given; the budget rule and its accounting; the
eligibility check; the label; the under-built objection procedure.

Out, and where it is: the schedule on which the question is put to the owner (one page with the three other
owner inputs at the M1 exit), the generated configuration record `registries/tenfold/owner-inputs.toml` whose
`[advocates]` table is a projection of this decision, and the freeze precondition that all four owner rows are
`decided` or `defaulted` (N41-15, on which this bead depends for the record format); the arms' construction (the
rival-arm beads); the pre-sealing audit (N39b-37).

## Technical approach

1. **Options put to the owner.** (a) External engineers experienced with the incumbent stacks, with their
   names and affiliations on record. (b) Internal
   agent sessions with no contribution to this system (the default), optionally reviewed by an external
   engineer who signs the "strongest configuration" statement. (c) A mix by stratum.
2. **What advocates receive.** The development suite, the common agent (the same model at one pinned revision,
   the same token and tool-call caps), the design document of this system (arm C's authors must have read it),
   the same calibration data, reusable prior answers, proof problems and access to the public checker, the
   same hardware envelope, and a documentation budget: an advocate-written guide for the rival's agent no
   longer than this system's own agent documentation, frozen with the arm.
3. **Budget rule.** Per stratum: budget = max(40 device-hours, the builders' logged compute on that stratum's
   development missions). The builders' figure is read from the project's run ledger over the development
   period; the advocates' spend is logged the same way and both are printed in the freeze manifest's tuning log.
4. **Eligibility check for the default.** A session or person is eligible if no commit, review or bead closure
   of this system is attributed to it. The check is run against version-control history and the delivery log.
5. **The under-built objection.** Before the freeze the builders may object in writing that the rival is
   under-built; the adjudicator rules. Independently, a frozen rival that wins none of the easy-win development
   families of a stratum is presumed under-built and the freeze is refused. A documented incumbent capability
   left unused on the development suite is questioned before the freeze.
6. **Label.** `rival: internally advocated` is derived from the role registry (advocate kind) and printed on
   every result of the gate; it is independent of `self-adjudicated`.
7. **Record.** `decisions/OPEN-TENFOLD-3.md`: the option, the named parties per stratum, the budget per stratum
   with both compute figures, and the date; cited by every freeze manifest's advocate block.
   One decision has one record of authority (DESIGN, the same statement in N41-15 and in the four
   decision beads N39b-28, N39b-29, N39b-31 and N39b-33): the ADR that the open-question register requires, which
   the register row's `adr` field cites. The ADRs are `ADR-PROG-15` (OPEN-TENFOLD-3, advocates), `ADR-PROG-16`
   (OPEN-TENFOLD-4, envelope), `ADR-PROG-17` (OPEN-TENFOLD-6, agent) and `ADR-PROG-18` (OPEN-TENFOLD-8, thresholds),
   under `docs/adr/`. The file `decisions/OPEN-TENFOLD-<n>.md` is the ADR's supporting file: the request, the
   options, the figures and the evidence. The ADR cites it by path and digest, and it states no decision that the
   ADR lacks. Reason: the register's rule is that a decision or a default is an ADR, while the request and its
   arithmetic are too long for one and change until the decision is taken.
   This bead writes `ADR-PROG-15` when the owner decides; if the deadline is reached first, the register's default
   writer writes it with the default above. Either way the `[advocates]` table of the configuration record
   (`kind`, `budget_floor_device_hours_per_stratum = 40`, `label`) is regenerated from it by N41-15's generator.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The decision or the default is recorded before any arm is tuned for the partial gate.
- [ ] Each stratum's budget is at least 40 device-hours and at least the builders' logged compute, with both
      figures on record.
- [ ] Every advocate identity passes the eligibility check; a planted identity with one commit to this system
      is refused.
- [ ] The label appears on the rehearsal report when advocates are internal sessions.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::advocates`: `budget_is_max_of_floor_and_builders` (builders 25 h gives
40 h; builders 63 h gives 63 h); `eligibility_rejects_repo_author`; `advocate_kind_sets_label`;
`guide_longer_than_reference_refused` (a guide exceeding this system's agent documentation in length).

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `roles` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12): registers advocate sessions, asserts
eligibility and the label, and asserts that a tuning log exceeding its budget is flagged. Logged: budgets and
spends per stratum as metrics.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the budget rule's two cases. Negative controls: an ineligible advocate; a rival frozen with no
easy win on the development suite (freeze refused).

## Considerations

- Internal advocacy is the weakest point of the default: sessions of the same model family as the builders may
  share blind spots. The label says so, and the external replication does not cure it.
- 40 device-hours per stratum over four strata is 160 device-hours before any paired run; it is part of the
  compute request (N39b-29).
- An advocate may state in the manifest what more budget would have added; that list is published.
