## Background

The gates of the ten-fold test are campaigns of many long runs. By the program's machine rule nothing above the
default compute class (16 GiB of memory, 8 cores, one hour of wall time, one device) runs without the owner's
written approval. The partial gate and the tournament are far above it, so each needs an approved class: a
campaign class for each run, and the pretraining class where an arm builds a standing asset (for example a
family policy pretrained before any clock starts). The open question is owned by the project owner and blocks
both program gates. Fail-closed default: nothing above the default class runs, the gate is recorded as
`not run`, and M3 does not exit.

## Scope

In: the cost arithmetic; the request document; the decision record; what runs under each answer. Out: the
classes' definition (the performance program); the runs.

## Technical approach

1. **Cost of a cell.** A cell with m families, c campaigns per family, deadline H and arms X costs
   m·c·Σ_X E[min(T_X, H)] device-hours. Worst case, when every run is censored: m·c·|arms|·H. With m = 24,
   c = 1, three arms and H = 4 h: 24 × 3 × 4 = 288 device-hours per cell. HYPOTHESIS: about 70 device-hours per
   design cell at restricted mean times near 1.5 h, 1.0 h and 0.4 h for arms B, C and A (24 × 2.9 = 69.6).
2. **Other items of the request.** Advocate tuning: at least 40 device-hours per stratum. Acceptance batteries
   (the `adjudicate` phase) in the adjudicating simulator on CPU cores: n sealed world clusters per submission,
   estimated from development missions. Standing assets: each arm's declared build cost S, under the
   pretraining class where it is a pretrained policy. The rehearsal. Re-runs of pairs after a harness fault
   (at most one per pair). Level 2 of the replication kit at the tournament.
3. **The partial gate's request (four cells).** Expected and worst case per cell from the measured incumbent
   baseline (the rival's restricted mean times on development missions) and from our own estimates with their
   intervals; totals; the calendar time on one machine with two cards at one device per campaign (the primary
   result uses matched devices: one RTX 4090 and the same number of CPU cores per campaign, arms interleaved).
   Illustration with the planning values: two design cells at about 70 device-hours and two family cells
   (CPU-bound, counted in core-hours), plus 160 device-hours of advocate tuning, is about 300 device-hours
   expected and 4 × 288 + 160 = 1,312 in the worst case.
4. **The tournament's request.** The same arithmetic per stratum run, with 24 families per cell by default and at
   least 32 for a cell that declares a population result, on both machines, as a campaign class per campaign
   and a pretraining class where an arm pretrains.
5. **Options for the owner.** (a) Approve the request as computed. (b) Approve a cap; the adjudicator then
   chooses, before sealing, which cells run, and each omitted cell or stratum is declared `not run` with the
   reason. (c) Approve only CPU strata (`envelope`, `sensing`): the design cells are `not run`. (d) No approval:
   the default.
6. **What each answer means for the gate.** GATE-TENFOLD-06 passes when every cell *in scope* has its record;
   under (b) and (c) the scope is what was approved and the record lists the rest as `not run`. Under (d) the
   gate is `not run` and M3 does not exit. A run that would exceed the class in force is refused before it
   starts (FROB-REF-761); there is no partial credit for a truncated campaign.
7. **Record.** `decisions/OPEN-TENFOLD-4.md` with the request, the option chosen, the approved device-hours per
   gate and per class, any cap, and the date. The harness reads the approved totals and stops scheduling new
   campaigns at the cap (campaigns in progress run to their end or to H).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The request exists with expected and worst-case device-hours per cell and in total, derived from measured
      baseline times, before the partial gate's suite hash is committed.
- [ ] The decision or the default is recorded; the gate's scope in the registration equals what was approved.
- [ ] A campaign scheduled beyond the approved total is refused with FROB-REF-761.
- [ ] Omitted cells appear in the report as `not run` with the reason.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::envelope`: `worst_case_cell` (24, 1, 3 arms, 4 h: 288);
`expected_cell` (1.5, 1.0, 0.4 h: 69.6); `cap_stops_scheduling_not_running`; `unapproved_scope_is_not_run`;
`population_cell_cost_scales_with_m` (32 families: 384 worst case).

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `envelope` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12): with a planted approval of half the
rehearsal's cost, asserts that scheduling stops at the cap, that the excess campaign is refused, and that the
report lists the omitted cell as `not run`. Logged: estimated, approved and spent device-seconds as metrics.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 288; 69.6; 1,312. Negative control: a harness that starts a campaign whose worst case exceeds the
remaining approved budget must be caught by `cap_stops_scheduling_not_running`.

## Considerations

- The classes themselves are a proposal awaiting ratification; if their limits change, the request is restated.
- Raising a deadline H to satisfy the horizon cap raises the worst case in proportion.
- The estimate of our own arm's time before M3 is a planning value; its interval is printed, not a point.
