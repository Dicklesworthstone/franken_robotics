## Background

Every stratum of the ten-fold test has a common deadline H, fixed before the suite is sealed. A run not accepted
by H is censored and contributes H to its arm's restricted mean time RMT = E[min(T, H)]. The deadline shapes
everything: too short, and both arms are censored and the ratio collapses toward 1; too long, and the worst-case
cost of a cell (families × arms × H) grows without need. It also caps the ratio whatever the rival does: since
the rival's restricted time cannot exceed H, R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A (the horizon ceiling), so a cell whose horizon cap is
below 10 cannot return the verdict `ten-fold` at all. The deadline of each stratum is an open question owned by
the adjudicator and decided at the freeze-and-seal gate from the development suite. Default rule: three times
the frozen rival's median development time, rounded up to half an hour, and raised until the horizon cap is at
least 10.

## Scope

In: the rule as a computation; its inputs; the record; the interaction with cost and reachability. Out: the
provisional deadline used before this decision, which is the stratum record's field `development_deadline_s`
(N39a-12; 14,400 s by default, the value BD-143's baseline and the development cards use); the strata registry
itself, whose field `deadline_s` (the stratum's H, `null` until decided) this decision fills (N39a-12, a
dependency); the reachability kill gate (N39a-34); the compute decision (N39b-29).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/deadline.rs`; subcommand of the operator binary (DESIGN),
adjudicator role only: `frob-tenfold deadline set --gate <id> --stratum <s> [--dry-run] [--json]`, which
computes the rule from the development ledgers of the stratum, prints t_med, H₀, H, cap(H), the censoring share
and the worst-case cost, and (without `--dry-run`) writes H to the stratum record's `deadline_s`; exit 0, 2
when the result carries a flag of step 3 or the stratum is referred, 3 on a refusal.

1. **Inputs per stratum.** The frozen rival's (arm B's) clock times on the stratum's development missions, cold,
   with censored development runs marked; arm A's restricted mean time on the same development missions.
2. **The rule (default; the adjudicator may adopt it or record a different rule before the suite hash).**
   a. t_med = the median of arm B's development times. A development run censored at the provisional deadline
      (`development_deadline_s`) enters at that deadline; if more than half are censored the median is not
      identified, the provisional deadline is doubled and the rival's development runs are repeated (DESIGN: a
      median from censored values would set H too low).
   b. H₀ = 3·t_med, rounded up to a multiple of 1,800 s.
   c. cap(H) = H ÷ RMT_A(H), where RMT_A(H) is arm A's development mean of min(T, H). While cap(H) < 10, raise H
      by 1,800 s. Because RMT_A(H) ≤ mean T_A, the loop ends once H ≥ 10·mean T_A unless arm A itself is
      censored on development missions; if the loop has not ended at H = 8·H₀ (DESIGN stop), the stratum's cells
      are referred to the reachability kill gate (they open as `descriptive`).
   d. Warm cells use the same rule per amendment on the development amendment sequences.
3. **Checks recorded with the result.** The share of arm B's development runs that would be censored at H (if
   above one third, the rule's output is flagged: the rival often fails to finish, and the ratio will be
   dominated by H); the worst-case cost of each cell at H (m·c·arms·H) for the compute request.
4. **Allowed and forbidden changes.** The measured baseline may change H before sealing. After the suite hash is
   committed H is part of the registration and cannot change; a request to change it is refused
   (`tenfold.parameters-frozen`).
5. **Record.** The deadline this decision sets is written to the stratum record's `deadline_s` (N39a-12), which
   every sealed card of the stratum then carries; `development_deadline_s` is left as it was. The record of
   authority is the ADR `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-TENFOLD-5.md` (the one directory for decision records; it is the
   record the `adr` field of the open-question register's row OPEN-TENFOLD-5 cites, N41-OPEN8), with
   `decisions/OPEN-TENFOLD-5.md` as its supporting file: per stratum t_med, H₀, the final H, cap(H), the
   censoring share, and the rule's identity. The ADR cites the supporting file by path and digest, and both are
   hashed into the event log with the suite hash. Where the default rule is adopted unchanged, the ADR records it
   as the default taken.

Worked example (illustrative): arm B's development times in seconds 5,400, 6,300, 7,200, 9,000, 11,700 give
t_med = 7,200; H₀ = 21,600 (already a multiple of 1,800). Arm A's development mean is 1,200 s, so cap = 18 and
H = 21,600 s. With an arm A mean of 2,700 s the cap is 8; H is raised to 27,000 s (cap 10).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every stratum of the gate has H recorded with its inputs before the suite hash is committed: `deadline_s`
      set in the stratum record, and `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-TENFOLD-5.md` citing the supporting file's digest.
- [ ] The worked example reproduces: 21,600 s; and 27,000 s in the second case.
- [ ] A stratum whose cap cannot reach 10 by the stop is referred, and its cells are registered `descriptive`.
- [ ] A change of H after the suite hash is refused.
- [ ] The cost implication of each H is passed to the compute request.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the worked example; the horizon ceiling R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A. Negative control: a rule implementation
that computes the cap with arm B's mean instead of arm A's must fail `cap_uses_restricted_mean`'s companion
assertion on the example (arm B's mean is 39,600 ÷ 5 = 7,920 s, so it would give 21,600 ÷ 7,920 = 2.7 and raise
H in half hours to 79,200 s, where 79,200 ÷ 7,920 = 10: a deadline 3.7 times the correct 21,600 s, which the
test detects by the value of H, not by reaching the stop at 8·H₀ = 172,800 s).

## Considerations

- The horizon cap uses arm A's development performance, which the builders could depress to obtain a longer
  deadline; the adjudicator computes it from runs under the common agent and caps, and the rival benefits from
  a longer deadline at least as much.
- Three times the rival's median makes rival censoring uncommon but not rare; the completion ceiling 1/q then
  applies to the rival's side of the ratio symmetrically (a censored rival run gives a lower bound on that
  family's ratio, which is conservative for arm A).
