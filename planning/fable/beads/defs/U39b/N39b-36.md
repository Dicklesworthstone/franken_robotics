## Background

GATE-TENFOLD-10 (claim kind: Known answer; M3, before the partial gate) is the gate of the estimation rules of
the ten-fold test. Its threshold: the pre-analysis reproduces 10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568 and the counts 32,
46, 49 and 182; and in 2,000 simulated campaigns of 24 families with the rare-tail candidate, the contrast test
certifies ten-fold in none, while the percentile bootstrap, run as the negative control, certifies it in
78.6% ± 3 points. If the gate is not met, the suite is not opened and no population wording is used. This bead is
the script and test target that measure it, driving the product verb `eval.preanalyze` from the user's side and
the harness's scorer.

What the simulation shows. The rare-tail candidate finishes 99% of mission families in 1 s and takes 999 s on
1%; the rival always takes 20 s; the deadline is 1,000 s. The true ratio of restricted mean times is
20/10.98 = 1.8215: the candidate is less than twice as fast. Yet 78.568% of 24-family suites contain no slow
family, and on those a resampling interval sees a constant ratio of 20 and would certify "ten-fold". A valid
test must certify in none.

## Scope

In: the one measurement of GATE-TENFOLD-10: the script `scripts/e2e/tenfold_preanalysis.sh`, every run of the
product verb and of the scorer that the gate needs (the eight known values, the boundary plans, the refusal, the
MCP path), the 2,000-campaign simulation with its seed and its certification rules, the three plants, the test
target `tenfold_estimation`, and the receipt. Out: the gate's register row, the evaluation of this receipt
against the gate's expected values, the recording session and the consequence that the suite is not opened
(N40-19, the program-gate bead, which depends on this bead and runs no plan and no simulation of its own; its
earlier second script and second simulation are withdrawn in favour of the ones here); the verb (N39b-34); the
contrast module (N39b-35); the harness battery of the other known answers (N39b-22); the 100-seed row of
GATE-WS-07 on undersized plans (N41-31).

## Technical approach

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_preanalysis.sh`; header `bead=N39b-36 milestone=M3 labels=tenfold,e2e gates=GATE-TENFOLD-10
seed=392010 timeout_s=900 plants=count-rows,bootstrap-as-certifier,rate-as-time`. Test target
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold_estimation`.

Steps:

1. `two-estimands`: score the 25-mission fixture (H = 100 s; the rival 50 s on each: 1,250 s, 25 accepted; the
   candidate 24 at 1 s and one timeout: 124 s, 24 accepted). Assert R_time = 625/62 (prints 10.081) and
   R_rate = 300/31 (prints 9.677), each under its own name.
2. `population-ratio`: `frob eval preanalyze --plan rare_tail_24.json --json`. Assert from the envelope: the
   alternative's population ratio 1.8215 (to four places), the probability of an all-fast suite 0.78568 (to
   five places), `reachable_verdicts` for a finite-suite plan, and the evidence block.
3. `counts`: three plans at α = 0.05, 0.0125 (four confirmatory cells) and 0.01. Assert n_min = 32, 46, 49 and,
   for the registered bound at 0.05, n_bound = 182.
4. `refusal`: a population plan with 24 families: `e2e_expect_refusal` with the population-claim refusal;
   assert the ranked repairs (finite-suite result; enlarge; fewer confirmatory cells; a tighter registered
   bound). A plan of 24 missions on one site: assert n_effective = 1. The boundary of the necessary count, at
   α = 0.05 under the registered bound `bounded-paired-contrast-hoeffding/1`: a population plan with 31 families
   is refused with `required` showing 32 and 182; with 32 families the necessary count is reached (n_min = 32)
   and the plan is still refused, because the registered bound needs 182, and the refusal prints both counts;
   with 182 families the population result is admitted. (The verb admits population wording only at
   n ≥ max(n_min, n_bound), N39b-34.) The 24-family plan declared `finite-suite` is admitted, and its report
   text is searched for population wording: none may occur. For that plan the rare-failure interval in the
   output is the zero-count bound 1 − 0.05^(1/24) = 0.1174; a zero-width resampling interval of 24 zeros is
   never emitted.
5. `mcp`: repeat step 3's first plan through the MCP server; assert the same result bytes in `result`.
6. `simulate`: 2,000 campaigns. Campaign k draws 24 families independently: slow with probability 1/100
   (candidate 999 s), otherwise fast (1 s); rival 20 s; H = 1,000 s. Draws come from a counter-based generator
   keyed by (seed, k, family), so the result does not depend on thread count. For each campaign:
   (a) the bounded paired contrast test at α = 0.05: certifies when its lower bound is nonnegative;
   (b) the negative control: a percentile bootstrap (1,000 replicates, families resampled) "certifies" when the
   5th percentile of the resampled ratio is at least 10.
   Assert: count of (a) is 0 of 2,000. Assert: count of (b) lies in [1,512, 1,632] (78.6% ± 3 points of 2,000);
   the expected count is 2,000 × 0.78568 = 1,571. Also assert that (b) certifies exactly on the campaigns with
   no slow family (in a campaign with one slow family the ratio is 480/1,022 = 0.47).
7. `scaling`: every time of steps 1 and 2 and the deadline H are multiplied by one factor (3, then 1/4); assert
   that R_time, R_rate, the population ratio, the all-fast probability and all four counts are unchanged.
8. `plants`: with `count-rows` the one-site plan must be wrongly admitted by the mutant and the assertion of
   step 4 must fail (caught); with `bootstrap-as-certifier` the mutant scorer that grants population wording
   from the bootstrap must certify in step 6(a) and be caught; with `rate-as-time` the mutant prints 9.677 as
   the ratio and step 1 must fail.

**Logging.** Through the shared helper: for every `frob` command the exact command line, exit code, wall time
and the envelope's evidence block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); each known answer as an
`assert` with `--ka`, observed and expected as exact strings; the two simulation counts as `metric` lines with
thresholds (`== 0`; within [1,512, 1,632]), `n = 2000` and the seed; each refusal with its code or slug and
ranked repairs; each plant as a `negative-control` assertion with `caught`. The summary lists
`gates: [{id: "GATE-TENFOLD-10", measured_ok, controls_caught}]`.

**Receipt, and who records.** The script's last step writes the gate receipt: the eight observed values as
exact strings, the two campaign counts with `n = 2000` and the seed, each plant with `caught`, the commit, the
hardware fingerprint and the digest of the log. This bead ends there. The register row of GATE-TENFOLD-10 is
evaluated and entered from this receipt by N40-19 (by a fresh-context session), before the partial gate's suite
is opened. (An earlier draft of this bead said "before the suite hash is committed"; the plan's consequence for
this gate is that the suite is not opened, and the pre-opening check keys each pass to the committed suite hash,
so the row follows the hash and precedes the opening.)

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The script exits 0 with all eight known values reproduced (10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568, 32, 46, 49,
      182).
- [ ] The contrast test certifies in 0 of 2,000 campaigns; the bootstrap control certifies in 78.6% ± 3 points.
- [ ] All three plants are caught; with any one mechanism broken the script exits non-zero.
- [ ] The MCP path returns the same result as the command line.
- [ ] The simulation is identical across runs with the same seed and across thread counts.
- [ ] At α = 0.05 under the registered bound, population plans with 31 and with 32 families are refused (the
      second with n_min = 32 shown as met and n_bound = 182 as unmet) and one with 182 families is admitted; a
      24-family plan may declare only a finite-suite result and its report carries no population wording.
- [ ] A resampling interval of n zeros is never emitted; the zero-count bound 1 − α^(1/n) is (0.1174 at n = 24,
      α = 0.05).
- [ ] Scaling every time and H by one factor changes no ratio and no count.
- [ ] The receipt is written with the eight values, the two counts, the seed and the three plants caught, in the
      form N40-19 evaluates; the register row itself is N40-19's.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold_estimation`: `simulation_seed_stable`;
`all_fast_fraction_matches_binomial` (the count of all-fast campaigns equals the count of bootstrap
certifications); `one_slow_family_ratio` (480/1,022); `contrast_never_certifies_at_24`;
`expected_count_interval` ([1,512, 1,632] contains 1,571); `scaling_changes_no_ratio_and_no_count` (times and
H scaled by 3 and by 1/4); `boundary_31_32_182` (the three boundary plans of step 4 against the real verb);
`receipt_has_all_fields` (eight values, two counts, seed, three plants).

### End-to-end script and logging

This bead is the script. It runs in minutes inside the default compute class on CPU.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: rows TF-17 to TF-20 of the harness registry. Negative controls: the percentile bootstrap as a
certifier (TF-CX-16), the rate ratio as the time ratio (TF-CX-17), rows counted as families (TF-CX-18); a
pre-analysis that accepts a 24-family population claim (must be refused; step 4). Metamorphic relation: scaling
every time and H by one factor changes no ratio and no count (step 7).
Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; 1 and 36 worker threads; debug and release; two platforms (the
simulation is seeded and bit-reproducible on each).

## Considerations

- The ± 3 points band is about 3.3 standard errors of a binomial share at 2,000 campaigns (0.92 points), so a
  correct implementation fails by chance about once in a thousand runs with a fresh seed; the seed is fixed in
  the header and a failure under a new seed is investigated, not retried silently.
- The simulation tests the estimation rules, not any property of this system or of a rival.
- The simulation is cheap (CPU seconds to minutes), so the script is re-run at every later exit as part of the
  harness's known answers; each run writes a new receipt and N40-19 decides what a later differing receipt does
  to the row.
