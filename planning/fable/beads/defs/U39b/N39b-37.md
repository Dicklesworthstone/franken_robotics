## Background

The comparison in the ten-fold test is only fair to the rival if the rival is as strong as a competent engineer
can make it, and only credible if that strength was fixed before any sealed mission was opened. The rival audit
is the step that establishes both. Before any sealed mission is opened each rival arm is frozen: its
configuration, exact revisions, licence, wrappers, tolerances and optimizer scripts are committed to the
*experiment pack*, after a competent baseline engineer has improved the fastest rival on the development
fixtures. GATE-TENFOLD-11 (claim kind: Known answer; M3 for the partial gate, M7 for the tournament): the rival
audit is complete before sealing: every arm's frozen configuration reproduces its own development-fixture
figure within 10% (TARGET) on the tournament hardware; the baseline engineer's sign-off and the digest of the
committed pack are recorded; no arm's configuration changes afterwards, and a changed digest voids the arm. If
the gate is not met the suite is not sealed.

DESIGN: the most serious rival may be a short analytic program wrapped around a mature simulator. It is always
an arm.

## Scope

In: the audit as a procedure and its artifact: the register of rival classes and what must be earned against
each; the experiment pack, its contents, layout and digest; the reproduction run and the band function it
applies; the equal-inputs declaration; the sign-off and its entry before the suite hash; the void rule and the
two refusals; the refresh at M7. Module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/rival_audit.rs`.

Out, with the bead that has each: the program gate's own check and record, that is the function
`tenfold11(pack, ledger) -> Verdict`, which reads this pack and the sealing ledger and measures nothing itself,
the register row of GATE-TENFOLD-11 with its parts at M3 and M7, the rule that the M3 record does not carry over
to M7, and the record of the protocol controls of the comparison (pairing, randomization, the blind adjudicator,
the labelled best-of-rivals figure) (N40-20, which depends on this bead); building the arms (the rival-arm
beads); the M0 audit of incumbent pins (N39b-08), which this audit extends at each gate.

## Technical approach

**Rival classes (FACT from the source register; each named system and its revision is re-read and pinned at the
audit).** The audit must show, per stratum, that the frozen rival is drawn from the strongest class for that
problem.

| Problem | Strongest rival to compose | What the candidate must earn |
|---|---|---|
| GPU learning with sensing, control and transfer | The leading batched engines with their training stacks and system-identification workflows | More accepted designs per unit time, counting setup, training and independent evidence |
| Industrial and rich-world simulation | The industrial stacks and the batched manipulation engines | A checked family answer across several contact or sensing regimes at competitive total cost, at fixed scene diversity and sensor resolution |
| Joint synthesis of body and control | Grammar-based morphology search, differentiable co-design and learned design generators (RoboGrammar, DiffHand, N-LIMB, DiffuseBot) | A cheaper design under the same sealed constraints, or stricter checkable coverage at matched cost; the rival receives the same grammar, masses, symmetry knowledge and controller budget |
| Certified geometry, adversarial scenes, robust design | Certified configuration-space regions; a scenario language (Scenic); linear and mixed-integer programs; interval, reachability and satisfiability tools | More of the mission decided with correct quantifiers and interpretable counterexamples |
| Digital twins and systems engineering | A multibody systems-engineering tool with a skilled author; an agent-built twin | Less engineering time from a changed mission to a reconciled portable answer |

**The experiment pack** (one per gate, content-addressed; DESIGN layout `tenfold/<gate>/pack/`): for every arm
and stratum, the freeze manifest and every file it names: configuration, exact revisions and container digests,
licence reading, wrappers and tag maps, numerical tolerances, optimizer and outer-loop scripts, the agent guide,
the tuning log, the development scores; plus the equal-inputs declaration (below) and the sign-off. The pack
digest is the hash of its sorted manifest.

**Audit procedure.**
1. *Improve first.* The baseline engineer (a rival advocate) improves the fastest rival on the development
   fixtures within the tuning budget; the tuning log records what was tried.
2. *Class check.* For each stratum the frozen rival's class is named from the table, with a sentence on why no
   stronger class applies; the short-analytic-program arm is present on every stratum.
3. *Equal inputs.* Each arm received the same calibration data, hardware envelope, reusable prior answers,
   cache budget and access to the public checker. The declaration lists each item's digest per arm; a
   difference is a finding.
4. *Reproduction.* On the gate's hardware (the tournament hardware of the gate's row; at M3, the hardware of the
   partial gate) each frozen configuration re-runs its development fixtures (or a declared subset of at least
   three per stratum); the figure it declared in `development_scores` (median restricted time to acceptance on
   the fixtures) must be reproduced within 10%, on either side: |rerun − declared| ÷ declared ≤ 0.10 (a rerun
   faster than the tuned figure by more than 10% is also a miss). The band is one function,
   `within_band(declared, rerun)`, defined here and called by the gate's check. Both figures are recorded per
   arm. A miss is investigated: either the frozen configuration is not the one that was tuned (a packaging
   defect) or the hardware differs from the development hardware. DESIGN (one rule, stated identically in
   N40-20): a miss is never resolved by editing the declared figure. The configuration is corrected, its
   development fixtures are run again, and that run produces a new declaration with its own ledger entry,
   before sign-off and never after.
5. *Sign-off.* The baseline engineer signs the statement that this is the strongest configuration the budget
   allowed, with the list of what more budget would have added. The sign-off and the pack digest are entered
   in the event log, as an entry of the suite's sealing ledger (the ordered record of the sealing steps), before
   the suite hash, which is step 2 of sealing.
6. *After the audit.* The pack digest (BLAKE3 over the sorted manifest) is recomputed at the start of every run
   of a campaign and at close. A changed digest voids the arm (`tenfold.frozen-arm-changed`, exit 9; the alias
   `arm-changed` is never emitted, registry N39-REF) and the run is refused;
   sealing requested before the sign-off and digest exist is refused (`tenfold.audit-incomplete`).

**At M7** the audit is refreshed: pins re-read, licences re-read, configurations re-frozen, the reproduction
repeated on the tournament hardware.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The pack exists with every file each manifest names; its digest and the sign-off precede the suite-hash
      entry in the event log.
- [ ] Every frozen configuration reproduces its development-fixture figure within 10% on the gate's hardware,
      with the rerun and declared values recorded.
- [ ] The equal-inputs declaration shows identical digests across arms for calibration data, prior answers,
      cache budget and checker access.
- [ ] A planted change to a pack file after sign-off voids the arm; sealing without sign-off is refused.
- [ ] Each stratum names its rival class; the short-analytic-program arm is present.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::rival_audit`: `within_ten_percent` (declared 5,400 s, rerun 5,900 s:
9.3%, passes; rerun 6,000 s: 11.1%, fails; rerun 4,800 s: 11.1% below, fails);
`redeclaration_is_a_new_run` (a declared figure edited without a new development run is rejected); `pack_digest_changes_with_any_file`; `signoff_must_precede_suite_hash`;
`equal_inputs_difference_is_finding`; `missing_named_file_fails_pack`; `changed_digest_voids_arm`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_rival_audit.sh` (bead N39b-37; needs `cuda` for real arms; `E2E_SMALL=1` uses the synthetic
arms of the rehearsal): builds the pack, runs the reproduction, records the sign-off, then plants a late change
and an early sealing request. Header `bead=N39b-37 milestone=M3 labels=tenfold,e2e gates=GATE-TENFOLD-11
seed=390737 plants=late-change,early-seal,larger-cache,stale-dev-score`. Logged as NDJSON through the shared
helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command, exit code, wall time): per arm and stratum the
declared and rerun figures as `metric` lines against the 10% band with the fixtures named before the rerun; the
rival class named per stratum; the equal-inputs digests per item and arm; the pack digest before and after each
plant and the event-log sequence numbers of the sign-off and of the suite-hash entry; each refusal with its
reason id and exit code (`tenfold.frozen-arm-changed` 9, `tenfold.audit-incomplete` 3); on failure the first
differing value and the pack path. The `summary` gives `gates: [{id: "GATE-TENFOLD-11", measured_ok,
controls_caught}]`, with `gates_claimable: false` under `E2E_SMALL=1`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answer: the 10% band arithmetic. Negative controls: TF-CX-20 (an arm retuned after sign-off); a manifest
whose development scores were produced by a configuration different from the frozen one (the reproduction
fails); an arm given a larger cache budget (finding).

## Considerations

- 10% is tighter than the 20% used when reproducing published incumbent figures, because here the arm
  reproduces its own figure on the same hardware.
- Agent nondeterminism widens development-fixture times; the figure compared is a median over the fixtures, and
  the fixtures re-run are named before the rerun.
- Rivals are comparison instruments run out of process; nothing of theirs enters the product's dependencies.
