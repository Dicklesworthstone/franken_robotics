## Background

A ratio of wall-clock times is easy to bias without anyone intending it: one arm always runs first on a cool,
idle machine; one arm always gets the faster card; an adjudicator who knows whose answer it is scores it more
kindly; a transcript leaks from one arm to the other. The protocol controls of the ten-fold test remove these
paths by construction. Missions are paired across arms with isolated transcripts. Order, operator, site and
resource assignment are randomized and counterbalanced. Agent arms start from fresh contexts on the same model
revision, with the same caps on model calls and parallel workers; human operators, if any, are matched for
proficiency. An adjudicator who does not know which system proposed an answer releases paired receipts after
both arms close. Where a platform lacks a native path, a best-supported trial and a matched-device trial both
run, and a missing cell is published as `unavailable`. The fastest qualifying rival, all arms and the raw
pairings are published; a per-case best-of-rivals ensemble is reported only as a labelled extra lower bound on
the ratio. This bead implements those controls as a run-plan generator, isolation checks, a blinding layer and
the publication set.

## Scope

The protocol controls are stated twice in the protocol, once with the arms and the acceptance procedure and once
as a list of controls; several are therefore implemented by other beads, and this bead holds what they do not.

In (built here): the run plan as a recorded object of a gate (the command, `runplan.json`, its entry in the event
log before the suite opens, its balance metrics asserted, and the check that the executed order equals the
plan); the operator and site factor; isolation of arms (own roots, separate operating-system identities, the
cross-read refusal and its log); the verification at each campaign start that the session is fresh and the caps
equal; the key under which blind tokens are derived and the rule for formats that cannot be blinded; the
matched-device and best-supported trials as two labelled result sets; the publication set and the ensemble bound
as a check on what the report publishes.

Out (built elsewhere, used here): the schedule generator and the equal-terms record, including fresh context,
equal caps and the second-platform rule (N39a-19: there is one generator, `arms/schedule.rs`, seeded by the
`schedule_seed` committed at sealing step 2; `frob-tenfold plan` calls it, and the order, device and
interleaving rules written out below are that generator's properties, restated so that this bead's balance
assertions are self-contained); submissions, the opaque `arm_token`, receipts and the paired release of results
(N39a-09: this bead adds only how the token is derived, see step 4); the raw-pairings table and the ensemble
lower bound in the report record (N39a-36) and the report generator (N39b-14); acceptance itself; leak detection
(N39b-02); the pre-sealing audit (N39b-37).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/runplan.rs` and `blind.rs` (DESIGN); command `frob-tenfold plan
--gate <id>` writing `runplan.json`, entered in the event log before the suite opens. `runplan.rs` wraps the
schedule generator of `arms/schedule.rs` and adds the recorded plan, the metrics and the execution check; it
contains no second generator.

1. **Run plan.** Inputs: cells with their families and campaign indices; arms; devices (the two RTX 4090 cards
   of the Linux workstation) and core groups; the public plan seed committed with the suite hash (so the plan is
   reproducible and was not chosen after anything was seen). Construction:
   a. *Order.* For each family the order of arms is one of the |arms|! permutations; permutations are assigned so
      that each is used ⌊m/|arms|!⌋ or ⌈m/|arms|!⌉ times (with three arms and 24 families, each of the six
      orders exactly four times), then families are shuffled by the seed.
   b. *Device.* Each arm is assigned each card for half of its campaigns (difference at most one); within a
      family the arms rotate over cards so that no arm is confounded with a card.
   c. *Interleaving.* Campaigns are scheduled so that the arms of one pair run adjacent in time on the same
      card where sequential, or concurrently on the two cards with the assignment of step b, so that host load
      and temperature are shared; no arm runs more than two consecutive campaigns.
   d. *Operator and site.* With no human in the loop the operator factor is the agent session; each campaign has
      a fresh session. If a variant with human operators is ever run, operators are matched for proficiency
      and counterbalanced like devices, and the matching evidence is recorded. Site: all primary runs are on one
      site; a second site is a separate, labelled result.
   Balance metrics are computed and asserted: per arm the count per card; per arm the count per order position;
   the largest run of one arm.
2. **Fresh contexts and equal caps.** At each campaign start the harness verifies: a new agent session; the
   pinned model revision (through the one check of OPEN-TENFOLD-6, N39b-31's `agentpin`, refusing with
   `tenfold.agent-revision-differs`; no second revision check is written here); the same token, tool-call,
   model-call and parallel-worker caps for every arm (from
   the manifests); zero human minutes (any intervention is logged and reported).
3. **Isolation.** Each arm has its own root for work files and transcripts, owned by a separate operating-system
   identity; an arm's processes cannot read another arm's root (a read attempt is refused by the system and
   logged; a successful cross-read is a leak). Shared caches between arms are forbidden; the equal cache
   *budget* is a size, not a shared store.
4. **Blind scoring.** Where the submission format allows it, a submission reaches the adjudicator under a blind
   token: the opaque `arm_token` of the submission record. DESIGN, fixed here so that the two descriptions of the
   token agree: the token is random-looking to everyone without the harness's sealed key and is derived as a
   keyed hash of (gate, arm, mission, campaign index) under that key, so that it is reproducible by the harness
   and not derivable by an arm or by the decision procedure; the token-to-arm table is held in the sealed area
   and revealed at close. Formats that identify their producer (for example a certificate bundle naming its
   writer) are scored unblinded and the cell's record says `blind: false` with the reason.
5. **Paired release.** A receipt goes to its arm at once and holds only the decision, the index, the level
   spent, the units used and a hash. Paired results (times, per-world outcomes, transcripts) are released only
   after every arm of the gate has closed.
6. **Platform trials.** The primary result is the *matched-device trial*. Where an arm has a better-supported
   platform (this system may add the Apple workstation), a *best-supported trial* is also run and reported
   separately; a platform an arm cannot use is printed as `unavailable`, and a gap that exists on one platform
   only is a portability observation, not the ratio.
7. **Publication set.** For every cell: the fastest qualifying rival; all arms; the raw pairings (mission,
   campaign, arm, time, censored flag, device, order position). The *best-of-rivals ensemble*: per pair, the
   smallest restricted time among the rival arms; the ratio of sums against it is printed as
   `extra lower bound (ensemble)` and is never a verdict input, since no single frozen rival achieved it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] For three arms and 24 families the plan uses each of the six orders four times, gives each arm 12
      campaigns per card, and no arm more than two consecutive campaigns; the plan is identical for the same
      seed and differs for another.
- [ ] The plan's entry in the event log precedes the suite-open entry.
- [ ] A campaign started with a reused agent session, a different model revision or unequal caps is refused.
- [ ] A cross-arm read attempt is refused and logged; a planted successful cross-read is reported as a leak.
- [ ] Blind tokens hide the arm from the acceptance service's inputs; the table is revealed only at close.
- [ ] The report's publication set contains the raw pairings and labels the ensemble bound.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::runplan` and `tenfold::blind`: `orders_balanced_24_by_3`;
`devices_balanced_within_one`; `max_run_of_one_arm_is_two`; `plan_is_seeded`; `two_arms_alternate`
(with two arms each order 12 times); `fresh_session_required`; `caps_must_match`; `blind_token_is_keyed`
(not derivable without the key); `unblindable_format_recorded`; `ensemble_is_min_over_rivals_per_pair`
(rival times 100 and 60 on one pair, 40 and 90 on another: ensemble sum 100, labelled).

### End-to-end script and logging

Step `run` of `scripts/e2e/tenfold_partial_gate.sh` (N39b-12): generates the plan for the rehearsal, asserts the
balance metrics, executes it, and asserts from the event log that the executed order equals the plan. Logged:
the plan digest, the balance metrics as `metric` lines with their bounds, each campaign's device and order
position.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the balance counts above. Negative controls: a plan generator that always runs arm A first (the
order-position balance assertion must fail); a harness that releases per-world outcomes with a rejected
submission's receipt (must be caught by the receipt schema check). Metamorphic relation: relabelling the arms
permutes the plan's arm columns and changes no balance metric.

## Considerations

- Counterbalancing equalizes expected load, not realized load; host load and thermal state are recorded per
  campaign, and a pair whose two campaigns ran under very different load is flagged for the descriptive section.
- With two cards and three arms, concurrent execution of a whole pair is impossible; the plan pairs two arms
  concurrently and rotates which arm waits.
- Blinding is weaker for arms with recognizable answer styles; the record says where it could not be applied.
