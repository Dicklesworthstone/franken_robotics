## Background

The ten-fold test carries a second claim beside speed, with its own acceptance: that this system offers a *new
engineering operation*. Stated as a test: a fresh team receives a typed continuous design family, realistic
uncertainty, a causal controller contract and sealed changes, and must return answers about the whole family,
not about one design. The claim matters because the thesis's lever "answer over families instead of points"
changes what an answer is; its gain is in scope and reuse, not in solve speed (on small plants standard exact
methods tie a specialized solver). The claim is tested on the `envelope` stratum (family queries with
amendments) and is where the mission subsystem's bet (BET-19) meets the sealed suite. Tier: Frontier for the
mechanism under test; this bead, the acceptance contract in the harness, is Solid.

Terms. *Design family*: a set of designs given by typed parameters over continuous ranges. *Inner answer*: a set
of designs certified to satisfy the mission; *outer answer*: a set outside which no design satisfies it; the
*unresolved band* lies between. *Causal controller contract*: what the controller may observe and when, so that
no answer assumes information the controller cannot have. *Amendment*: a sealed change to the mission after an
answer was accepted. *Vacuous enclosure*: an inner set that is empty or an outer set that is everything.

## Scope

In: the acceptance contract (three parts), the card fields that predeclare obligations, the checks, the rule
for the rival's answer, the tie rule and its consequence. Out: producing family answers (the mission
subsystem); the certificate checker; the kill gates read on the record (N25a-18, N12-30, N39b-07).

## Technical approach

Module `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/opclaim.rs` (DESIGN): the adjudicator-side checker of the contract.

1. **What the team receives** (one mission card per family): the typed continuous design family with its
   bounds; the uncertainty (world family, tolerances, measurement error) in realistic ranges; the causal
   controller contract (observations, clocks, latencies, what may be remembered); and, sealed, the amendment
   sequence.
2. **Acceptance requires all three.**
   (i) A machine-checkable inner or outer answer with explicit scope: every assertion in a certificate bundle
       that the public offline auditor accepts, with the quantifier prefix of the query and the parameter box
       it covers stated in the bundle.
   (ii) A physically interpretable witness or a scope-preserving ambiguity report: for an inner cell, a design
        with a controller and a trace that shows the requirement met; for an excluded cell, the binding
        constraint or the pair of worlds that defeats every controller; where the evidence does not decide, a
        report that says which measurement would, without widening the claim's scope.
   (iii) Correct reuse or withdrawal after an amendment: which earlier verdicts survive and which are withdrawn,
         with the repaired answer; no kept verdict may be one the adjudicator's ground truth withdraws.
3. **Predeclared obligations** (fields of the mission card, fixed before sealing): the mandatory interior
   obligations (regions that must be shown feasible), exterior obligations (regions that must be shown
   infeasible) and boundary obligations (where the boundary must be located); the largest unresolved band or
   optimality gap allowed; and any mandatory distinguishing experiment. Strict and closed endpoints are part of
   acceptance where an exact boundary is asked for (an answer that returns ε ≤ ε* where the truth is ε < ε* fails
   that obligation). A vacuous enclosure fails: it discharges nothing.
4. **Checks, in order.** Bundle accepted by the auditor; scope of each certificate covers the obligation it is
   offered for (read from the embedded statement); every mandatory obligation discharged; band or gap within
   the card's maximum; endpoints as asked; witnesses replay; direct evaluation at the sealed probe points
   contradicts nothing (a contradiction of an accepted certificate is a false certificate and disqualifies);
   after each amendment, zero missed withdrawals (a missed withdrawal fails the mission) and a count of
   unnecessary invalidations (reported per arm).
5. **The rival's answer goes through the same checker.** The rival may produce bundles in the public schema by
   any means (a lifted robust linear program, a conventional projection, an SMT or interval tool, written by
   an expert). The same obligations, band and probe points apply.
6. **Tie rule.** If the rival's answer discharges the same obligations within the same band on a fixture, the
   claim of *exclusive expressivity* is retired on that fixture: no document may say that only this system can
   answer it. A tie can still justify shipping the simpler implementation behind the same interface. The
   reading is recorded per fixture in the gate record and feeds the mission subsystem's tie decision.
7. **Record per mission.** Obligations with status (discharged, not discharged, vacuous), band achieved against
   maximum, endpoint checks, witness replays, probe results, amendment table (kept, withdrawn, missed,
   unnecessary), for each arm.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The checker accepts a correct family answer on the static-stance development fixture (the adjustable
      stance under static load whose known minimum half-width is 3/14 m) and rejects each planted defect:
      a vacuous inner set; an open endpoint offered where a closed one is asked; a certificate whose scope is
      narrower than its obligation; an unresolved band above the card's maximum; a missed withdrawal.
- [ ] The rival's bundle for the same fixture, produced by a lifted robust linear program, passes through the
      same checker and its tie is recorded as retiring exclusive expressivity on that fixture.
- [ ] A contradiction at a probe point is routed to the disqualification engine as a false certificate.
- [ ] Unnecessary invalidations are counted and reported, and do not fail the mission.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::opclaim`: `vacuous_enclosure_discharges_nothing`;
`strict_versus_closed_endpoint` (truth ε < (U + ½)/4 on the docking fixture: an answer closed at the boundary
fails); `scope_must_cover_obligation`; `band_above_maximum_fails`; `missed_withdrawal_fails_mission`;
`unnecessary_invalidation_counted_not_failed`; `rival_through_same_checker`; `tie_retires_exclusive_claim`;
`probe_contradiction_is_false_certificate`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_opclaim.sh` (bead N39b-39): on two development fixtures, submits a correct answer and each
planted defect for a synthetic arm and for a rival-style bundle, asserting the per-obligation statuses. Logged:
the bundle digest, the auditor's verdict, each obligation with its status, band against maximum, the amendment
table, and for `frob prove verify` calls the envelope's evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (plan-time pilots, FACT to be re-run in the repository before a gate cites them): on the
static-stance family both the specialized projection and a lifted robust linear program return the half-width
3/14 m in one solve each, a tie; on the two-gain docking family feasibility holds exactly when
ε < (U + ½)/4. Negative controls: the five planted defects; a checker that accepts a vacuous enclosure.

## Considerations

- The pilots already show ties on small plants; the claim that survives is an interface and a discipline
  (scope, witnesses, amendment handling), and the tie rule makes the program say so fixture by fixture.
- "Fresh team" means sessions or people with no part in building either arm's method for that fixture; at the
  partial gate this is the arms' fresh agent contexts.
- Open question of the mission subsystem: a true certificate of an uninteresting statement still reads as
  evidence; mandatory obligations on the card are the defence.
