## Background

The sealed suite of the ten-fold test is written by the adjudicator from public class descriptions. For the
comparison to be hard on the candidate and fair to the rival, three things are fixed per mission type: what the
adjudicator changes privately when deriving sealed variants (so that no arm can prepare for the exact case),
which conventional method is the strongest control (so that the rival is not a straw man), and which mutations
are planted to test that acceptance catches the classic errors. This bead provides those three things as data
and tooling for the three mission types the M3 product can answer, which feed the `envelope` and `sensing`
strata of the partial gate. The remaining three types follow at M7 (N39b-41).

Terms. *Private change*: a typed edit the adjudicator applies to a public template to obtain a sealed mission.
*Strongest control*: the conventional method an expert would use, built into the rival arm for that mission
type. *Planted mutation*: a deliberate, independently derived error (in units, contact order, causal clocks or
passive load) whose presence acceptance must detect. *Easy win*: a mission on which a conventional method is
expected to win or tie. *Unresolved answer*: a mission whose correct answer is `unknown` with a witness.

## Scope

In: operator definitions and generators for three mission types; the strongest-control declarations; the
mutation generators; the per-stratum quotas this tooling must be able to fill; the independent-evaluator rule.
Out: writing the sealed missions (the adjudicator, under the suite beads); the hygiene measurement (N39b-42);
warm-campaign accounting and the other three types (N39b-41).

## Technical approach

Tooling for the adjudicator's side only (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/suitegen/` with one module
per mission type; each operator is a function from a template card and a sealed seed to a card, recording the
operator and its parameters in the sealed part of the card. Arms never see operator parameters.

| Sealed mission type | Private changes (operators) | Strongest control in the rival arm |
|---|---|---|
| Adjustable stance under static load | Foot authority; a force floor; package dimensions; asymmetric payload; a missing foot; passive-spring compatibility | A lifted robust linear program; conventional projection; a force-law switch |
| Split-grip landing and the last usable observation | Mirror the strip; hide or delay the surface cue; shift the payload; change the contact order | Belief-state reachability; equal-budget reinforcement learning; geometric control |
| Gripping unfamiliar compliant stock | Geometry; an early-law alias; contact coupling | An expert probe; direct measurement at mission dwell; active system identification |

**Operator rules.** Every operator states its admissible range and keeps the mission inside the public class
description (a sealed mission must be answerable from what the card gives). Operators compose; the generator
draws one to three per sealed family. Each generated family is checked by the adjudicator's reference solution
before it is admitted: it must have a known correct answer (including `unknown` with its witness where that is
the answer).

**Independent-evaluator worlds.** Some worlds are built with an evaluator independent of every proposal engine,
because a sampling run and a solver that share one mistranslated world model agree on the wrong answer. The
generator can emit a world description in two independently written forms (the exchange description and a
direct closed-form or exact-rational evaluator for these three types); the card records which form adjudicates.

**Planted mutations (derived by the adjudicator, never by an arm).** For each sealed family the tooling derives,
from the reference answer, one mutant answer per class, which acceptance must reject:
- *Unit*: a quantity re-expressed with a wrong unit (a half-width in millimetres read as metres; a force in
  newtons read as kilogram-force).
- *Contact order*: the order of contact events exchanged (the landing foot sequence; grip before settle).
- *Causal clock*: an observation used one sampling instant before it can arrive (the surface cue, the velocity
  packet).
- *Passive load*: an active allocation replaced by the passive one (equal springs under the same body: the
  plan-time stance falsifier gives leg forces (175, 115, 85, 25) N against a 160 N rating where the active
  allocation peaks at 155 N).
The mutants are stored sealed; the hygiene gate requires 100% rejection.

**Quotas the tooling must fill per stratum.** At least one family in six an easy win for a conventional method
(for stance: a case the lifted linear program solves in one solve; for compliant stock: a case where direct
measurement at mission dwell decides). At least one family in twelve with `unknown` and a witness as its
correct answer (for split-grip: two admissible worlds that are indistinguishable at the last usable observation
and under which the best choices differ). At least one tier trap per design stratum (a candidate that passes on
a fast tier and fails in the adjudicating simulator) is the suite beads' duty and is listed here only for the
count. In amendment sequences the card does not say which edit matters.

**Output.** For each generated family: the sealed card, the reference answer, the mutant answers, the
strongest-control declaration, the easy-win or unresolved flag, and the generator record (operators, seed);
all under the adjudicator's store.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each operator of the table exists with an admissible range and a test that the generated mission stays
      inside the public class description.
- [ ] For each of the three types the generator produces at least 24 distinct families from 24 seeds, each with
      a reference answer verified by an independent evaluator.
- [ ] Each family has four mutant answers, and the acceptance procedure rejects all of them on the development
      copies (100%).
- [ ] The generator can fill the quotas: at least 4 easy wins and 2 unresolved answers among 24 families.
- [ ] No operator parameter appears in the arm-visible part of a card.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::suitegen`: `stance_ops_stay_in_class`; `missing_foot_changes_answer`
(live dependency: the reference answer differs from the template's); `passive_spring_mutant_exceeds_rating`
(175 N > 160 N); `split_grip_mirror_preserves_difficulty` (the mirrored strip has the mirrored answer);
`delayed_cue_can_make_unknown_correct`; `stock_early_law_alias_pair_exists`; `unit_mutant_rejected`;
`clock_mutant_rejected`; `contact_order_mutant_rejected`; `sealed_fields_absent_from_arm_view`;
`quota_fill_24`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_suitegen.sh` (header `bead=N39b-40 milestone=M3 labels=tenfold,e2e
gates=GATE-TENFOLD-12,GATE-TENFOLD-04 seed=390740 plants=unit-blind-acceptance,operator-param-leak`): generates 24
development-pool families per type from public seeds, runs the reference solutions through the independent
evaluator and acceptance, runs the four mutants of every family through acceptance, and asserts counts and the
quota fill. Logged as NDJSON through the shared helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command
line, exit code, wall time, seed): families generated per type, operators drawn, reference-answer digests,
mutants rejected out of mutants planted per class as a `metric` against the threshold 100%, quota counts against 4
easy wins and 2 unresolved answers, each plant as a `negative-control` assertion with `caught`; on failure the
first mutant accepted (family, class, digest) and the paths needed to reproduce it; a final `summary` with
assertion counts and `gates_claimable: false`. Only development-pool seeds are used; sealed seeds and operator
parameters of sealed families never appear in a log.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers (plan-time pilots, FACT to be re-run in the repository before a gate cites them): the stance
family's minimum half-width 3/14 m from both the projection and the lifted program; the passive-spring forces
(175, 115, 85, 25) N; on the material probe a generator that is right on 675 of 675 in-class cases is wrong on
225 of 225 cases of a withheld law, while direct measurement at dwell is right on 900 of 900. Negative controls:
an acceptance procedure that ignores units (must accept the unit mutant and be caught); a generator that
writes operator parameters into the public part of the card.

## Considerations

- A generator shared between sealed and development missions is itself public knowledge; sealing rests on the
  seed and the operator draw, and families from one generator draw are one family for inference.
- The strongest controls named here have already tied the specialized methods in plan-time pilots; an honest
  suite will contain ties, and the easy-win quota makes sure they are visible.
