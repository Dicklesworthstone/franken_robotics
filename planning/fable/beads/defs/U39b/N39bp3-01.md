## Background

The tournament at M7 is voided and re-run for a breach of protocol, and for nothing else. Three mechanisms
decide what a breach is and what it voids: the fixed order of the tournament's ten steps, entered in the gate's
event log; the freeze record written when every arm is frozen, with the watcher that verifies it before every
campaign start; and the breach rules, which void the runs, cells or tournament that a breach's extent covers. A
fourth rule keeps the tournament from being stopped on an interim number: the per-cell monitor reports, and an
operator's request to stop a cell is refused.

These mechanisms must exist before the two-week freeze and dress rehearsal (N39b-17), which plants each breach
once and must see it caught by the mechanism that will guard the tournament, and before the tournament itself
(N39b-13), which depends on that rehearsal. They are therefore this bead's, and both of those beads depend on
it. (Reason for the split: when the mechanisms were part of the tournament bead, the rehearsal that must catch
their plants came before the bead that built them.)

Terms. *Event log*: the gate's hash-chained log of the sealing ledger (N39a-15). *Stack freeze*: the `[freeze]`
record of `franken-stack.lock`, opened and closed as recorded acts, and `check-stack` in frozen mode (N33b-03).
*Extent*: the set of runs, cells or arms a breach is shown to touch.

## Technical approach

1. **The ten steps** (the order of §39.13, as N39b-13 performs them): 1 refresh the rival audit; 2 publish the
   enlarged development pool; 3 commit the suite and protocol hashes with the parameter block's digest; 4 tune
   on the development pool; 5 freeze and publish every arm and complete the pre-sealing audit; 6 record the
   pre-analysis and name at most four confirmatory cells; 7 run with the arms interleaved; 8 close and reveal;
   9 publish the report and the kit; 10 the external re-run of the kit. `frob-tenfold tournament step <n> --gate
   <id> --evidence <file> --json` (DESIGN, a subcommand of the operator binary of N39a-01) enters step n in the
   gate's event log with its hash, its time and the evidence file's digest. It is refused, exit 3, when step n − 1
   is not entered or the time precedes the predecessor's (`tenfold.step-out-of-order`, N39-REF's row; repair:
   enter the missing step; the sealing steps keep their own `seal-out-of-order`).
2. **The freeze record.** `frob-tenfold tournament freeze-record --gate <id> --json` writes, at step 5, the
   canonical JSON record `tournament_freeze.json` in the gate directory and enters its BLAKE3 in the event log:
   for the stack, the campaign identifier and the frozen resolution receipt's hash exactly as the `[freeze]` table
   of `franken-stack.lock` holds them, and the stack lock's digest (every sibling commit and the toolchain); beyond
   the stack, every arm's manifest hash (N39a-18), the comparator registry's digest, the agent model revision and
   caps, and the hardware fingerprints. It is refused when the stack freeze is not open (N33b-03 opens it at this
   step; it is the only freeze of pins and of the toolchain, and this record cites it and holds no second copy of
   the pins).
3. **The watcher.** `frob-tenfold tournament watch --gate <id> --json` verifies the record: the stack items by
   running `check-stack` in frozen mode, which fails on any pin, toolchain, lockfile or receipt that moved; the
   other items by comparing digests. It is registered as a precondition of `frob-tenfold campaign start` for every
   campaign of a gate of type tournament and is run again at close. Exit 0 when nothing moved. An arm manifest that
   differs is N39b-01's ground: the start is refused with `tenfold.frozen-arm-changed`, exit 9. Any other moved
   item is a breach and is classified by step 4.
4. **Breach extents and voids** (`tenfold.protocol-breach`, recorded in the event log with its evidence; the row
   of N39-REF). (a) *A pin moved during the freeze*: while the stack freeze stays open on the frozen receipt and
   every affected run is identified by its receipt hash, the runs that span the move are void and their cells are
   `void: stack moved`, re-run on fresh families; if the freeze has to be closed as `aborted`, or a run cannot be
   attributed to one receipt, every run is void and the tournament is re-run under a new freeze (the rule of
   N33b-03, stated the same in N40-16). (b) *Sealed material read by an arm*: a bounded extent voids only the
   touched cells for that arm and retires their families at once (N39b-02's leak rule); an unbounded extent voids
   the tournament. (c) *An arm changed after its freeze*: N39b-01's scope rule: that arm's cells run after the
   change are void for that arm (all its cells if the time of the change is unknown) and re-run at the next gate;
   the other arms' records stand. A re-run, of a cell or of the tournament, uses a rotated suite of fresh
   families. Nothing else voids: not a low ratio, not a disqualification (which removes one arm from scoped
   cells), not a harness fault (the pair is re-run once; a second fault excludes the pair, counted).
5. **No interim stop.** At a gate of type tournament the monitor reports and raises operational alarms and its
   futility stop is disarmed (N39a-35). An operator's request `frob-tenfold tournament stop-cell --gate <id>
   --cell <cell> --reason <text>` is refused, exit 3 (`tenfold.interim-stop-refused`, N39-REF's row; repair:
   none, the cell runs to n), and the request is entered in the event log; the cell's campaigns keep starting. No
   monitor output is an input to a verdict.
6. **Exit codes** are N39-REF's: 0 done; 2 done with flags (a void recorded); 3 refused; 9 integrity.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ten steps can be entered only in order, each with a hash and a time; step 6 before step 5 is refused.
- [ ] The freeze record's stack entries equal the `[freeze]` table of the stack lock (campaign, receipt), and the
      watcher's stack verification is `check-stack` in frozen mode.
- [ ] A planted pin move is detected before the next campaign starts and voids the runs that span it and their
      cells; with the stack freeze closed as `aborted` it voids the tournament.
- [ ] A planted change to a frozen arm is refused at the next campaign start and voids only that arm's cells run
      after it; a bounded leak voids only the touched cells; a disqualification voids nothing.
- [ ] A stop request on N39a-35's planted input `tournament_futility_stop` (eight consecutive families below 3,
      E = 25.63, the monitor's `futility-crossing` recorded and no stop) is refused with
      `tenfold.interim-stop-refused`, exit 3, entered in the event log, and the cell runs to n.
- [ ] TF-CX-25 (`pin_moved_in_freeze`) and the stop-request side of TF-CX-26 (`tournament_futility_stop`) are
      planted and caught.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::tournament`: `steps_enforce_order` (step 6 before step 5 refused with
`tenfold.step-out-of-order`, exit 3);
`pin_move_voids` (one sibling commit changed in the stack lock: with the freeze open, the spanned runs and their
cells are void and the other cells stand; with the freeze aborted, the tournament is void);
`freeze_record_cites_stack_freeze` (the record's receipt equals `[freeze].receipt`); `interim_stop_is_disabled`
(on N39a-35's planted input `tournament_futility_stop`, eight consecutive families below 3 with E = 6,561/256 =
25.63 and the monitor's `futility-crossing` recorded: a stop request → `tenfold.interim-stop-refused`, exit 3,
the request entered in the event log, the cell keeps running to n);
`arm_change_voids_that_arms_later_cells` (one byte of arm B's agent guide changed before campaign 7 of 12: the
start is refused with `tenfold.frozen-arm-changed`, arm B's cells from campaign 7 on are void for arm B, cells 1
to 6 and arms A and C stand, the tournament is not void); `bounded_leak_voids_touched_cells_only` (one card read
before its clock in one cell → that cell void for that arm, its families retired, every other cell stands);
`disqualification_does_not_void` (a false certificate in one cell → that cell `disqualified`, the tournament
not void); `unattributed_run_voids_tournament` (a run whose receipt hash is missing after a pin move → every run
void); `harness_fault_reruns_once` (a second fault on one pair excludes it, counted, and voids nothing).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_tournament_conduct.sh`, owned by this bead and built only on its dependencies (header
`bead=N39bp3-01 milestone=M7 labels=tenfold,e2e gates=GATE-TENFOLD-07 seed=397001 needs=cpu
plants=step-out-of-order,pin-moved,pin-moved-aborted,arm-changed,bounded-leak,unbounded-leak,stop-request`), on a
synthetic tournament gate directory with three synthetic CPU arms, two strata of four families and second-scale
deadlines, and a scratch stack lock whose freeze is opened by N33b-03's procedure (labelled `synthetic`, never
claimable): (1) `FROB_TEST_SEED=397001 cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::tournament`; (2) enters the ten
steps with `frob-tenfold tournament step`, writing the freeze record at step 5 and asserting it against the
scratch `[freeze]` table, and runs `tournament watch` before every campaign start of step 7 and at close (exit 0
on the clean run); (3) the plants, each on a fresh copy of the gate directory and through `e2e_expect_refusal` or
`e2e_expect_caught`: step 6 entered before step 5 (refused); a sibling commit changed in the scratch stack lock
during step 7 (TF-CX-25: detected before the next start, the spanned runs and their cells `void: stack moved`,
the other cells standing), and again with the freeze closed as `aborted` (the tournament void); one byte of an
arm's agent guide changed before its fourth campaign (`tenfold.frozen-arm-changed`, exit 9, that arm's later
cells void); one card read before its clock (that cell void for that arm, its families retired) and a log gap
of unbounded extent (the tournament void); a stop request on a cell with eight consecutive families below 3
(TF-CX-26's stop-request side: refused, the cell's later campaigns start). Logged as NDJSON through the STD-E2E
helper (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, exact command line, exit code, wall time, seed): each step's
event-log entry hash, the freeze record's digest and its comparison field by field, each watcher run with what it
compared, each `tenfold.protocol-breach` record with its extent (runs, cells, arms) and evidence digests, each
refusal with its reason, exit code and first repair, each plant as a `negative-control` assertion with `caught`;
on failure the first differing value and the gate directory's path; a final `summary` with assertion counts and
controls caught. The dress rehearsal's script (N39b-17) and the tournament's (N39b-13), which depend on this
bead, run this script as a stage.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the extent rules on the unit-test cases above (campaign 7 of 12; cells 1 to 6 standing). Negative
controls: TF-CX-25 (a pin moved during the freeze) and the stop-request side of TF-CX-26, planted here under
N39-CX's plant identifiers; a watcher that compares pins itself instead of calling `check-stack` (caught by
`freeze_record_cites_stack_freeze` and by the scratch lock's moved receipt); a void rule that voids the whole
tournament on a bounded leak (caught by `bounded_leak_voids_touched_cells_only`).

## Considerations

- The stop request exists so that it can be refused and logged: an operator who asks is on record, and a
  tournament stopped on an interim number would be a fixed-n study that peeked.
- A void costs fresh families; the extent rules keep a local breach from spending the whole suite, and only an
  aborted freeze, an unattributable run or an unbounded leak spends it.
