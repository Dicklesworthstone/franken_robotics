## Background

An **exit review** records that a milestone's demonstrations ran and its gates have values. A **fresh-context
session**, one that implemented none of the milestone's work, regenerates the gate report from a clean checkout,
reads every receipt, records a disposition for each gate part, and records every kill decision due as an ADR. It
implements nothing.

M6 is a milestone of **epics**: frontier physics behind feature flags and off the critical path (advected films,
slosh, cable and rug proxies, rods, cloth and coupled islands, soft soil at scale, the hard-contact law on devices,
tactile sensing, the rotor port, RGB and splat scenes, moving people, gradients through contact, two mission
queries, lifecycle compression). Every item is Frontier or Moonshot, so the program's rule applies to each: no work
is funded before a paper-scale pilot with a falsifier has run, and each has a kill gate. The exit line is therefore
not that anything was built. It is that **every epic has its plan, its pilot result and its kill-gate decision on
record**, and every gate that was reached has a recorded value and a caught negative control. An epic whose pilot's
falsifier held and which was never built is a correct, complete outcome.

## Scope

In: the M6 exit record; per epic, the plan, the pilot record and the decision; dispositions for every gate
reached; the Moonshot cap; the feature-flag isolation check. Out: any repair; the pilots and measurements; the
shared procedure (N40-05) and query (N40-04).

## Technical approach

**1. Procedure (in brief).** (1) A session with no context of M6, whose identity is not an implementer of any due
row. (2) Clean clone at the exit candidate commit; build on the pinned toolchain with every M6 feature flag off,
then with each funded epic's flag on. (3) Every demonstration manifest of a funded epic through `frob --json` and
`frob-mcp`. (4) Recurring gates and the full negative-control sweep. (5) Regenerate the gate report and README
table; both equal the committed files. (6) List kill decisions with and without ADRs, shared close reasons,
never-failed controls, and `not-run`, `blocked`, `fail` parts. (7) A disposition per part: `accepted-pass`,
`accepted-kill: <fallback>`, `recorded-fail: blocks`, `not-run: <reason>`, `rejected: <reason>`. (8) Each kill
decision against its registered trigger and amendments. (9) The risk register read. (10) Open questions answered or
defaulted. (11) The Moonshot cap. (12) Demonstrate the exit line, write `docs/delivery/exits/M6/EXIT.md`.

**5. Checks particular to M6.**

- *The cap.* At no commit of the milestone were more than four Moonshot items active (an item is active from the
  funding of its pilot until its expiry gate). The review computes the active set over the milestone's history
  from the pilot ledger.
- *Pilots are pilots.* Each pilot record was registered before the pilot ran (its commit precedes the pilot's
  receipt), names a falsifier, and has an outcome that could have gone either way. A pilot that cannot fail is
  rejected as not a pilot.
- *Isolation.* With every M6 feature flag off, the standard battery's digests equal the M4 reference.
- *The pause rule.* Where M4 or M7 slipped during M6, the review records whether M6 work paused.

**6. Open questions due, with defaults.** OPEN-PROG-5 and OPEN-AUGUR-10: which epics are funded, in which order,
under the cap (default: none active before its pilot passes, then the default order of funding; no Moonshot mission
slice funded before its pilot passes).

**7. Stop rules to apply.** An epic stops when its pilot's falsifier holds or its kill gate fires.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every epic of M6 has its plan, its pilot result and its kill-gate decision on record (the seed's

- [ ] Every gate reached has a recorded value and a caught negative control (the seed's done-condition); every

- [ ] The nine kill gates of the compiled register and the four late ones have outcomes.
- [ ] The Moonshot cap held at every commit; every pilot record predates its pilot's receipt.
- [ ] Flags-off digests equal the M4 reference.
- [ ] `docs/delivery/exits/M6/EXIT.md` is written by the fresh-context session.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance exit::m6::`: `due_counts` (2, 7, 9; 4 late parts; 4 late kill decisions);
`unfunded_epic_does_not_block` (GATE-HAB-07 `not-run` with KILL-ADVECT `not-reached` → ready; with no decision →
`not-ready`); `cap_over_history` (a history with five simultaneously active items for one commit → violation naming
the commit); `pilot_must_precede_result` (pilot record committed after its receipt → rejected).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/exit_m6.sh` wraps `scripts/exit/review.sh M6`: re-runs every pilot from its registered record and
compares its result with the recorded one; runs each funded epic's acceptance fixture through `frob --json` with
its flag on; runs the standard battery with all flags off and compares digests with the M4 reference. Logs:
command, exit code, wall time, evidence block with the tier label, the falsifier's threshold beside the measured
value, digests, and on failure the first differing value with artifact paths.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the M6 column of the load table (2, 7, 9); fourteen epics. Controls the review must catch: an epic
with code and no pilot record; a pilot registered after it ran; a fifth active Moonshot item; a flag that changes
default digests; a `not-run` gate printed as passed.

## Considerations

- The seed's dependency list omitted the epics for advected films (BD-413) and articulated proxies (BD-415); both
  are M6 epics whose records this review reads, and both are added as dependencies here.
- The review may run more than once: epics are funded over time, and the exit is recorded when the last decision
  is on record.
- Nothing in M6 is needed by M7 or M8; this review never blocks the tournament.
