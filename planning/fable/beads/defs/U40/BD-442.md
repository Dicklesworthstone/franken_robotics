## Background

An **exit review** records that a milestone's demonstrations ran and its gates have values. A **fresh-context
session**, one that implemented none of the milestone's work, regenerates the gate report from a clean checkout,
reads every receipt, records a disposition for each gate part, and records every kill decision due as an ADR. It
implements nothing.

M8 delivers the Moonshot items that would make the product unlike anything else, each behind a flag and each with a
stop rule; the plan works without every one of them. Seven epics: facility co-design and multi-robot handoff; skill
transport with learned residuals; continuation-based synthesis; firmware export; geometric-algebra tree policies at
scale; reachable-set tables for shields; the fused per-world step kernel. A Moonshot is funded only after a
paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, at most four are active at once, and none is on the critical path. The exit
line is that **every epic has its plan, its pilot result and its stop rule applied**, and every gate reached has a
recorded value and a caught negative control. This is the last exit of the program.

## Scope

In: the M8 exit record; per epic, the plan, the pilot record and the stop rule's application; dispositions for
every gate reached; the Moonshot cap; flag isolation against the release measured at M7. Out: any repair; the
pilots and measurements; the shared procedure (N40-05) and query (N40-04).

## Technical approach

**1. Procedure (in brief).** (1) A session with no context of M8, whose identity is not an implementer of any due
row. (2) Clean clone at the exit candidate commit; build with every M8 flag off, then with each funded epic's flag
on. (3) Every demonstration manifest of a funded epic through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp`. (4) Recurring gates and
the full negative-control sweep. (5) Regenerate the gate report and README table; both equal the committed files.
(6) List kill decisions with and without ADRs, shared close reasons, never-failed controls, and `not-run`,
`blocked`, `fail` parts. (7) A disposition per part, from the six of the protocol: `accepted-pass`, `accepted-kill: <fallback>`,
`recorded-fail: blocks`, `no-claim` (the `reality:` line of the recurring GATE-GROUND-01 with no dataset),
`not-run: <reason>`, `rejected: <reason>`. (8) Each kill decision against its registered trigger and
amendments. (9) The risk register read. (10) Open questions answered or defaulted. (11) The Moonshot cap. (12)
Demonstrate the exit line, write `docs/delivery/exits/M8/EXIT.md`.

**How the review is run.** This is the exit protocol of N40-05 with the M8 gate list: the gate query is N40-04's,
the runner is `scripts/exit/review.sh M8`, and the exit-time checks that other beads attach to the runner
(`scripts/exit/checks.toml`, N40-05 section 5a) are run by it, each landing as a section of the exit record and
each of its blocks making the verdict `not exited`. Among them at M8: `firewall`, `risk-review`, `owner-calendar`
and `moonshot-cap` (the four every exit carries), the decision-record sweep of this exit (NAB-31, whose record
`docs/delivery/exits/M8/decision-records.json` is written before the verdict and read by it) and the Augur
delivery-order check `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example augur_slices -- --exit M8` (N25d-26; section 5). This bead writes no second query and no
second runner.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/exit_m8.sh` wraps `scripts/exit/review.sh M8`: re-runs every pilot from its registered record; runs
each funded epic's acceptance fixture through `frob --json` with its flag on; runs the standard battery with all
flags off and compares digests with the M7 release reference; runs `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example augur_slices -- --exit M8` on the register
and on a copy with slice 5's ADR removed (the copy must fail, exit 1, naming slice 5); and asserts that the exit
record holds a section from every attached check of `scripts/exit/checks.toml` in force at M8. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/exit_m8/<utc>.ndjson` (`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event` on every line): command, exit
code, wall time, evidence block
with the tier label Moonshot, the falsifier's threshold beside the measured value, digests, and on failure the
first differing value with artifact paths; per attached check its id, owner, `blocks` and `warnings`; for the
slices check the slice, its ADR path and disposition; the summary names the gates touched and the verdict.

## Considerations

- This review depends on the M7 review (seed dependency), not on M6's: the two frontier milestones are independent
  apart from the shared cap.
- The seed listed the seven M8 epics as dependencies. An epic carries no dependencies and nothing depends on one,
  so the seven are kept in `related`, and the dependencies are the pilot, gate-measurement and decision beads of
  the table in 2a and the other M8 beads of 2b. The decision-record sweep (NAB-31) reads the gate report and the
  ADRs at the exit candidate commit and does not wait for this review, so that this review can wait for it.
- Several epics may never be funded; a milestone that exits with seven `not-reached` decisions has exited
  correctly.
- If the owner funds an epic after this exit is recorded, the exit record gains an addendum by the same procedure.
- Upstream work items: none is among this review's dependencies, and none dated M8 exists. Through the chain of
  earlier beads its ancestry reaches one item's bead, N33bx-01 (FD-FSIM-42, at M4), by way of N12-29, which
  needs that bead's in-repository half (the admission record of `fs-checker` as an out-of-process oracle and
  the interim posture under which its arm is recorded `not run`) and not the change in FrankenSim. The tracker
  check lists the path as the warning `tracker-exit-waits-on-upstream` (N41-16 check 3(d)); the exit record
  carries it with this reason, and it blocks nothing.
