## Background

The gate register is the single list of every gate of the program. The subsystem chapters define their gates where
the mechanisms are described; the register collects them, gives each **one claim** and **one earliest milestone**,
lists every kill gate with its trigger and fallback, and says what each milestone exit must have on record. This bead
enters those rows into `registries/gates.toml` (record format: N40-01) and generates the printed register from the
file, so that the printed tables can never drift from the data that the exit reviews query.

Counts (FACT, counted from the compiled register tables): 32 admission gates and 5 sibling admission gates; 278
convergence gates in 23 subsystem tables plus the verification program's own gate GATE-GAUNT-01, 279 rows in all;
13 gates of the ten-fold test; 112 kill gates plus the 7 kill gates of the ten-fold test; the schedule gate. The
subsections added after the tables were compiled add 76 convergence gates and 25 kill gates (N40-10). All thresholds
are TARGETs until a gate records a measured value. An earlier count of 421 identifiers was taken before the late
subsections; the count is recomputed by the tooling and never typed by hand.

Why the register assigns claims and milestones itself: several chapters gave their gates no claim column, some rows
named two claims, and some rows named a milestone before their mechanism lands. One list with one reading each is
the precondition for a milestone exit being a query (N40-04).

## Scope

One record per gate, written once. This bead **creates** the record of every admission gate, every convergence
gate of the compiled tables, the program rows and GATE-GAUNT-01, and fills the fields the register governs: `id`,
`kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the `[[gate.part]]` list with its scopes, `recurring`,
`blocks_exit` and the brief in `threshold`. It leaves `controls` and `tier` empty and every part `not-run`.

In: those rows as data; the claim and milestone assignment rules below; the generated printout
`docs/delivery/register/REGISTER.md` (DESIGN path) and its freshness check.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Kill rows: the fallback ledger, N40-23 to N40-29. Late rows: N40-10.
- `controls`, `tier`, the full pass criterion and the chapter's declaration of each gate: the register bead of
  the gate's chapter (step 8 lists them). Such a bead creates no second record and restates no field of this
  bead with another value.
- Measured values: recording sessions.

**5. Milestones the register assigned.** Where a chapter's gate table has no milestone column, the earliest
milestone comes from the milestone skeleton (Marrow, Skeleton, Sinew, Tether, safety, workspace, GATE-QUAL-01 and
GATE-REFLEX-04) or from the milestone note printed under the chapter's own table (Atelier and Chronicle). Every other
milestone is the one in the gate's own row, except where that row disagrees with the skeleton: there the register
follows the skeleton (the eleven cases are carried by N40-09). Two rows have no milestone: GATE-HAB-09 ("before the
island work package", listed at the M6 exit) and GATE-GROUND-10 ("when data is admitted", tied to no exit).

One row gains a part the compiled table does not print (DESIGN). GATE-MARROW-01's row reads M0, and its earliest
milestone stays M0; but the milestone skeleton accepts it at M0 "for SO(3) and SE(3)", the two groups that exist
then, and the seed work of M1 "completes" it for every group (SE(2), SE₂(3), SE₂₊ₖ(3), product groups) and for
the rest of the law table. It is therefore entered with two `[[gate.part]]` entries: M0, scope "the laws for
SO(3) and SE(3)" (recorded by BD-011), and M1, scope "complete: every group and the whole law table" (recorded by
N13-19 with `record_gate … --part M1`). Reason: with one M0 part the gate would print `pass` on a fraction of
its threshold and the M1 exit would have nothing to demand. The M1 exit therefore has 60 convergence parts where
the plan's load table prints 59 (N40-04 states the reading), and EPIC-M0 and EPIC-M1 both list the gate.

**6. Program rows.** The schedule gate (identifier `GATE-PROG-SCHEDULE` in the file, DESIGN, because the register
prints it by name only) and GATE-TENFOLD-01 to -13, with `kind = "program"`; their beads are N40-11 to N40-22.

**7. Printout.** `print_register(&Register) -> String` writes the admission tables, one convergence table per
subsystem in chapter order, the kill tables, the program table and the gates-by-exit table, each row with its
current status. `xtask check-generated` fails when the committed printout differs from a fresh generation.

**8. The chapter side of each row (DESIGN division, so that no row is entered twice).** Fourteen chapters have a
register bead of their own. Those beads do not create rows. Each one holds its chapter's full pass criteria (it
is the "defining bead" that rule 4 of N40-01 sends a recording session to), fills `controls` and `tier` in the
rows this bead and N40-10 created, writes the chapter's declarations that the lint N40-09 compares with the
register, and names the bead that measures each gate. Reason for the division: the register governs identity,
claim and milestone, so those fields have one writer; the chapter owns its fixtures and negative controls, so
those fields have the other; two writers of one field is how two statements of one gate drift apart. Each of
these beads depends on this one, because the row must exist before its chapter fields are filled.

Where the chapter-side data lives (DESIGN, settled when the fourteen chapter beads were reconciled): the full
criterion, the measuring beads and each chapter's extra data (`criteria[]`, `fixture`, `script`, `kill`, `notes`,
`form_hash`, `strata_fixture`, `bet`) are not fields of `gates.toml`, whose schema admits only the fields of N40-01
(an unknown field fails the load). They are fields of the chapter's owner declaration, the `[[gate]]` block that
the lint N40-09 reads (`contract.toml` of the owning crate; `registries/gate_declarations.toml` until N34-08 settles
the contract format), which the lint carries but does not compare with the register.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The file holds 37 admission rows, 279 convergence rows, 13 ten-fold rows and the schedule gate, each valid
      under N40-01, each with at least one registered control or the status `not-run`.
- [ ] Row counts per table and claim counts equal the tables above; `INT-FSIM-12` is absent and rejected.
- [ ] Every one of the 23 listed rows, and GATE-AUGUR-07, has claim `design-comparison`; the nine two-claim rows
      carry the reading entered above.
- [ ] Every gate's set of part milestones equals the set of exits that list it (zero differences; the two
      milestone-free rows are the only exceptions); GATE-MARROW-01 has the two parts M0 and M1 of step 5.
- [ ] The five sibling admission gates have `blocks_exit = false`; all other rows block.
- [ ] The printout regenerates byte-identically, and a hand edit of it fails `cargo xtask check-generated`.
- [ ] Every identifier is the `id` of exactly one record: a second record for a gate, as a chapter bead that
      entered its own row would produce, fails the load with `duplicate-id` (N40-01).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::rows::`.

- `count_by_table`: the 27 counts of the table above, exactly.
- `count_by_claim`: {197, 34, 16, 13, 10, 7, 2}, sum 279.
- `design_comparison_list`: the 24 named rows are `design-comparison` (set inclusion; 34 in all).
- `two_claim_readings`: the nine rows carry exactly the claim of the "Reading entered" column.
- `parts_equal_exits`: for every row, part milestones = exits listing it; 36 convergence rows (the 35 of the
  compiled tables and GATE-MARROW-01, whose parts are M0 and M1) and 3 admission rows (INT-FSIM-07, INT-FMCP-01,
  INT-FDGR-01) have more than one part.
- `sibling_gates_do_not_block`: exactly five rows have `blocks_exit = false`.
- `reserved_identifier_rejected`: a row `INT-FSIM-12` fails the load.
- `every_row_has_owner_section`: `section` is non-empty and matches `^\d+(\.\d+)*$`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_register_rows.sh`: runs the unit target, regenerates the printout into a scratch directory,
diffs it against the committed file, then plants (a) a deleted row, (b) a row with claim `performance` changed to
`method`, (c) a part moved to a later milestone, (d) a second record for GATE-KIN-04 as a chapter bead would write
it, and (e) a field of a chapter declaration (`fixture`) written into `gates.toml`, and asserts each is caught with
the row identifier and by the check that owns it (`count_by_table`, the claim parse `claim-not-one-of-seven`,
`parts_equal_exits`, `duplicate-id`, the unknown-field load failure). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to
`target/e2e-logs/gate_register_rows/<utc>.ndjson`: per step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command,
exit code, wall time; `metric` lines with the 27 table counts and seven claim counts measured against the counts
expected; the BLAKE3 digests of the committed and the regenerated printout; the evidence block `{claim:
known-answer, class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`; on failure the first differing row and the
scratch paths; the `summary` line counts assertions and names GATE-GAUNT-10.

### Known answers and negative controls

N40-KA rows R-05 to R-09 (counts by table, by claim, parts, sibling gates, the reserved identifier). Negative
controls N40-CX RC-07 (a gate entered with two claims), RC-08 (a gate entered at a later milestone than the
register's), RC-09 (a row silently dropped), RC-60 (INT-FTUI-01 entered with `blocks_exit = true`, caught by
`sibling_gates_do_not_block`). Metamorphic relation: reordering rows in the file changes no count and
no exit report. Determinism: the printout is byte-identical twice on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The brief in `threshold` is not the criterion. A recording session reads the defining bead; a clause the brief
  omits is not waived (rule 4 of N40-01).
- Shared fixtures (one experiment recorded by several gates) are one fixture run once per milestone, each gate
  compared with the threshold in its own row; the list is N40-08.
- If a chapter unit's defining bead and this entry disagree on identity, claim or milestone, the register governs
  and the disagreement is a finding of the lint N40-09, not an edit made silently here.
- The fourteen chapter register beads of step 8 print their gates with claim and milestone columns of their own.
  Those columns are the chapter's declaration, compared with this entry by N40-09; they are not a second entry.
  They agree with this entry on every compiled-table gate (checked row by row when the two sets of beads were
  reconciled); a chapter's note such as "may be recorded at M4" is a note and adds no part.
- The load is concentrated: M2 and M3 carry half of the convergence parts. That is by design and is a schedule risk
  (N40-04 prints the load table at every exit).
