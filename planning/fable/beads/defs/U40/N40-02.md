## Background

The gate register is the single list of every gate of the program. The subsystem chapters define their gates where
the mechanisms are described; the register collects them, gives each **one claim** and **one earliest milestone**,
lists every kill gate with its trigger and fallback, and says what each milestone exit must have on record. This bead
enters those rows into `registries/gates.toml` (record format: N40-01) and generates the printed register from the
file, so that the printed tables can never drift from the data that the exit reviews query.

Counts (FACT, counted from the compiled register tables): 32 admission gates and 5 sibling admission gates; 278
convergence gates in 23 subsystem tables plus the verification program's own gate GATE-GAUNT-01, 279 rows in all;
13 gates of the ten-fold test; 112 kill gates plus the 7 kill gates of the ten-fold test; the schedule gate. The
subsections added after the tables were compiled add 76 convergence gates and 25 kill gates (N40-10). All thresholds
are TARGETs until a gate records a measured value. An earlier count of 421 identifiers was taken before the late
subsections; the count is recomputed by the tooling and never typed by hand.

Why the register assigns claims and milestones itself: several chapters gave their gates no claim column, some rows
named two claims, and some rows named a milestone before their mechanism lands. One list with one reading each is
the precondition for a milestone exit being a query (N40-04).

## Scope

In: the admission rows, the convergence rows, the program rows and GATE-GAUNT-01 as data; the claim and milestone
assignment rules below; the generated printout `docs/delivery/register/REGISTER.md` (DESIGN path) and its freshness
check. Out: kill rows (N40-23 to N40-29); the late rows (N40-10); full pass criteria (the defining beads); measured
values (entered by recording sessions).

## Technical approach

this exit": gate, claim, part due, pass criterion in brief, defining subsection. Their union is the row set. A gate
with parts at two exits appears in both epics and is one record with two `[[gate.part]]` entries.

**5. Milestones the register assigned.** Where a chapter's gate table has no milestone column, the earliest
milestone comes from the milestone skeleton (Marrow, Skeleton, Sinew, Tether, safety, workspace, GATE-QUAL-01 and
GATE-REFLEX-04) or from the milestone note printed under the chapter's own table (Atelier and Chronicle). Every other
milestone is the one in the gate's own row, except where that row disagrees with the skeleton: there the register
follows the skeleton (the eleven cases are carried by N40-09). Two rows have no milestone: GATE-HAB-09 ("before the
island work package", listed at the M6 exit) and GATE-GROUND-10 ("when data is admitted", tied to no exit).

**6. Program rows.** The schedule gate (identifier `GATE-PROG-SCHEDULE` in the file, DESIGN, because the register
prints it by name only) and GATE-TENFOLD-01 to -13, with `kind = "program"`; their beads are N40-11 to N40-22.

**7. Printout.** `print_register(&Register) -> String` writes the admission tables, one convergence table per
subsystem in chapter order, the kill tables, the program table and the gates-by-exit table, each row with its
current status. `xtask check-generated` fails when the committed printout differs from a fresh generation.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::rows::`.

- `count_by_table`: the 27 counts of the table above, exactly.
- `count_by_claim`: {197, 34, 16, 13, 10, 7, 2}, sum 279.
- `design_comparison_list`: the 24 named rows are `design-comparison` (set inclusion; 34 in all).
- `two_claim_readings`: the nine rows carry exactly the claim of the "Reading entered" column.
- `parts_equal_exits`: for every row, part milestones = exits listing it; 35 convergence rows and 3 admission rows
  (INT-FSIM-07, INT-FMCP-01, INT-FDGR-01) have more than one part.
- `sibling_gates_do_not_block`: exactly five rows have `blocks_exit = false`.
- `reserved_identifier_rejected`: a row `INT-FSIM-12` fails the load.
- `every_row_has_owner_section`: `section` is non-empty and matches `^\d+(\.\d+)*$`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/gate_register_rows.sh`: runs the unit target, regenerates the printout into a scratch directory,
diffs it against the committed file, then plants (a) a deleted row, (b) a row with claim `performance` changed to
`method`, (c) a part moved to a later milestone, and asserts each is caught with the row identifier. Logs per step
the command, exit code, wall time, the counts measured against the counts expected and, on failure, the first
differing row; the `summary` line names GATE-GAUNT-10.

### Known answers and negative controls

N40-KA rows R-05 to R-09 (counts by table, by claim, parts, sibling gates, the reserved identifier). Negative
controls N40-CX RC-07 (a gate entered with two claims), RC-08 (a gate entered at a later milestone than the
register's), RC-09 (a row silently dropped). Metamorphic relation: reordering rows in the file changes no count and
no exit report. Determinism: the printout is byte-identical twice on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- The brief in `threshold` is not the criterion. A recording session reads the defining bead; a clause the brief
  omits is not waived (rule 4 of N40-01).
- Shared fixtures (one experiment recorded by several gates) are one fixture run once per milestone, each gate
  compared with the threshold in its own row; the list is N40-08.
- If a chapter unit's defining bead and this entry disagree on identity, claim or milestone, the register governs
  and the disagreement is a finding of the lint N40-09, not an edit made silently here.
- The load is concentrated: M2 and M3 carry half of the convergence parts. That is by design and is a schedule risk
  (N40-04 prints the load table at every exit).
