## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet. It names, before any measurement, the *trigger* (a number or an event)
and the *fallback* (what ships instead). When the trigger is met the mechanism is dropped or narrowed, the fallback
becomes the plan, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from then on. A kill gate that fires is not
a failure of the program; a kill gate that is quietly re-scoped after a disappointing measurement is. The register
holds 112 kill gates in its compiled tables, 7 for the ten-fold test and 25 from later subsections (ledger beads
N40-23 to N40-29).

This bead builds the record-keeping that makes "never quietly re-scoped" checkable: the kill row, the dated
amendment, the decision record (an ADR: architecture decision record) and the renaming. DESIGN decisions:

- *A trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated amendment, with its reason, made before the
  measurement it governs.* Rejected: free editing with review, because the temptation to lower a bar is strongest
  exactly when the measurement is known, and a reviewer cannot see a number that was changed before they looked.
- *Every decision is an ADR at the milestone named in the ledger*, whether the gate fired or not. Rejected:
  recording only the gates that fired, because "not fired" without a cited measurement is indistinguishable from
  "not looked at".
- *The fallback ships under its own name.* Rejected: keeping the original capability name with a footnote, because
  release cells and reports would then describe more than the fixture qualified.

## Scope

In: the kill-row fields, and which gate fields a kill row does not carry; the decision entry and its per-outcome
fields; the closed table of scope kinds; the amendment record (the `introduce` amendment included) and its ordering
check; the decision ADR template, its path and naming (a second entry at one exit included) and its link to the
row; the four decision outcomes; standing and rolling gates; the meaning of "one repair cycle" and "one optimization
pass"; propagation to the gate statuses, the README table and the register printout (`apply_decisions`). Out: the
ledger rows (N40-23 to N40-29); the cross-check of chapter decision beads against the ledger (N40-30); the
measurement of any trigger (the chapter units' decision beads); the exit report (N40-04); the `killed` status of
`frob capabilities` (N00-02); typed closes of the mechanism bead and its dependents (N41-17); the end-to-end chain
on a real kill gate (N40-37).

## Technical approach

**1. Kill row** (extends the record of N40-01, `kind = "kill"`; the owner of a kill gate is its `section`, as for
every gate under rule 9, and the ledger beads' "Owner (consumer)" column fills `section` and `consumers`):

```toml
[[gate]]
id        = "KILL-METAL"
kind      = "kill"
section   = "21.24"                 # owner: governs trigger and fallback
consumers = []                      # subsections that state the same gate from their side
trigger   = "GATE-SPARK-04 fails after one optimization pass, or the Metal backend cannot hold run-to-run determinism"
fallback  = "Metal becomes a development and replay tier; CUDA is the training tier; the Mac trains on batch-cpu"
decided   = "M2"                    # the exit at which the decision is recorded as an ADR; stands for `milestone`
mode      = "exit"                  # exit | pilot-before-funding | standing | rolling
reads     = ["GATE-SPARK-04"]       # the gates or pilots whose records the trigger reads
# reread  = ["M1"]                  # later exits at which the decision is taken again (see step 2)
# scope_kind = "backend"            # only on a row decided per scope: one kind of the table below
decision  = { outcome = "", adr = "", date = "", session = "", receipts = [] }
# outcome: not-fired | fired | not-reached | deferred; a deferred entry adds deferred_to and reason;
# a fired entry of a row whose fallback names alternative forms may add fallback_form
```

A kill row carries none of the measurement fields of a gate (`claim`, `claim_id`, `threshold`, `tier`, `controls`,
`[[gate.part]]`): DESIGN, because §40.4 gives kill gates no claim column and a kill gate measures nothing; what it
decides is read from the gates in `reads`, which carry their own claims, thresholds and controls. A kill row with
one of those fields fails the load naming it (N40-01). Its derived state is `not-run` until a decision is in force
and then the outcome in force, per scope where it has a `scope_kind`; the register printout and the README table
print that outcome (step 7). The fields of a decision entry: `outcome`, `adr` (the ADR path of step 6), `date`
(ISO), `session` (the recording session) are always required; `receipts` lists the hashes of the receipts whose
measured values the outcome rests on, at least one for `not-fired`: harness receipts under `receipts/gates/`
(N40-01), and, for an entry that reads a pilot (mode `pilot-before-funding`: KILL-FLEET, KILL-SPECIALIZE,
KILL-MEGA), the `receipt` of the `[[pilot.result]]` it read, committed with the result at
`receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` (N38a-09); a hash resolves when it names a file in one of the two directories whose
bytes hash to it. A `deferred` entry adds `deferred_to` (an exit later than the entry's) and `reason` (non-empty).
A `fired` entry of a row whose `fallback` names alternative forms may add `fallback_form`, the form in force, which
must occur verbatim in the row's `fallback` text; a `fallback_form` that does not, or one on an entry of another
outcome, fails the load naming the field. A `fired` entry without it is read as the form that withdraws the
capability entirely (KILL-PROXY, whose forms are "articulated proxies in evaluation batches only" and "not at all":
read as "not at all"; its reader is the admission of N19b-03).

A row decided more than once (a `rolling` re-read; an `exit` row with `reread`, such as KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER at M1
and M2; a gate decided per scope, such as KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED per cell or KILL-ALTALGO per algorithm; a gate decided
in two stages at one exit; a decision re-opened by a new ADR) carries `decision` as an array of the same tables,
each adding `exit` (the milestone at which it was taken; default `decided`), `scope` where the row has a
`scope_kind`, and `reading` where it is a second entry for one scope at one exit (step 6). The latest entry per
scope is in force; earlier entries are kept, never edited. A single table is read as a one-entry array, so a
decision bead of a once-decided gate writes `decision = { outcome, adr, date, session, receipts }`.

**Scope kinds (DESIGN; a closed table, extended only by a change to this bead).** A row decided per scope names its
kind in `scope_kind`, filled by the ledger bead or the registering bead that enters the row; every entry's `scope` is
`<kind>:<value>` of that kind. A `scope` on a row with no `scope_kind`, or of another kind than the row's, fails the
load (`decision-scope-invalid`). The due list asks, at each exit a row with a `scope_kind` is due, for at least one
entry taken at that exit, and prints the scopes decided.

| `scope_kind` | Scope form and values | Gate (row entered by; decided by) |
|---|---|---|
| `cell` | `cell:<stratum>`, the strata of the ten-fold test | KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED, -TIE, -ADJ, -REACH (N40-28) |
| `algorithm` | `algorithm:<name>`, an alternative learning algorithm | KILL-ALTALGO (N40-25; N23b-23) |
| `half` | `half:<name>` (`cuts`, `quotient`) | KILL-AUGUR-CUTS (N40-26; N25b-17) |
| `clause` | `clause:<name>` (`finite`, `tray`; `screening`, `comparison`) | KILL-COUPLING (`finite`, `tray`; N40-29); KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR (`screening`, `comparison`; N40-29; N25e-22) |
| `backend` | `backend:<tier>`, a tier name: `batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal` | KILL-D1 per device family (N40-24; N21b-34); KILL-SHADOW (N40-24; N16b-19); KILL-HARDCONTACT (N40-24; N18a-23 for `batch-cpu` at M5, N18ax-03 for the device back ends at M6); KILL-RUT (N38a-37; N38a-38) |
| `item` | `item:<name>` (`assets`, `actors`) | KILL-DRESSING (N40-24; N19a-41 at M5, N19ax-05 at M6) |
| `resolution` | `resolution:<grid>` (`2x2`, `4x4`) | KILL-TACTILE (N38a-37; N38a-39) |
| `kind` | `kind:<island kind>` (`fluid`, `cloth`, `rod`) | KILL-LIVING (N40-24; N19b-20) |
| `family` | `family:<name>`, a column family of the response query (`counterfactual`, `adjoint`, `stencil`) | KILL-RESPONSE (N40-24; N18a-26) |
| `mechanism` | `mechanism:<name>`, an articulated proxy mechanism (`chain-cord`, `hinged-rug`) | KILL-PROXY (N40-24; N19b-19) |
| `proofkind` | `proofkind:<kind>`, a proof kind of the enabled registry of `frob-verify` (for example `lp-farkas`, `sos-gram`) | KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT (N40-26; N25b-04) |

DESIGN reason for one `backend` kind: a device family of the plan (CUDA, Metal) is exactly one GPU tier, and one name
per axis lets every view filter decisions by backend across gates, so KILL-D1's families are written
`backend:gpu-cuda` and `backend:gpu-metal`. The kind `family` never names a device family: its values are the
column families of the response query, and a `family:` scope on a `backend` row is refused like any other scope
of the wrong kind.

**2. What "decided" means, by mode.** `exit`: the milestone at whose exit the decision is recorded. For a gate whose
trigger is a pilot (`pilot-before-funding`), it is the earliest milestone at which the work package can be funded;
the decision is taken when funding is requested and may fall later. `standing`: the rule applies from the named
milestone on and is evaluated at every exit and whenever its event occurs (KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT from M1;
KILL-AUGUR-REUSE, KILL-TENFOLD-CERT and KILL-TENFOLD-LEAK from M3). `rolling`: decided at its milestone and re-read
at the exits its `reread` field names, or at every later exit when the field is omitted (KILL-FSIM: "M0, then
rolling", no `reread`, so M1 to M8; KILL-MCP: "M0; re-read at M1 with the M1 verbs", `reread = ["M1"]`, so it is
not in the due list after M1). An `exit` row may also carry `reread`: it is then decided again at each named exit
(KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER: decided M1 on the rival's ledger, `reread = ["M2"]` on our profile; the four per-cell gates of
the ten-fold test: decided M3, `reread = ["M7"]`; KILL-POOL, KILL-LOOPELIM and KILL-LIMBKERNEL re-read at M3,
KILL-AUGUR-REGION at M4, KILL-DRESSING and KILL-HARDCONTACT at M6); with no `reread` an `exit` row is decided once. A `reread` naming
an exit at or before `decided` fails the load. A kill gate of a later subsection with no milestone of its own is
decided at the milestone of the gate beside it. A `deferred` entry in force puts the row (for its scope) in the due
list of its `deferred_to` exit and takes it out of the due list of the exit at which it was recorded, which then
reports it as deferred, not missing.

**3. Outcomes.** `not-fired`: the trigger was evaluated against recorded measurements and is not met; the entry's
`receipts` and the ADR cite them. `fired`: the trigger is met; the fallback is in force. `not-reached`: the
mechanism was not funded (its pilot was not run, or the owner did not fund the epic); the capability does not exist
and is not described. `deferred`: an ADR moved the decision to the later exit named in `deferred_to`, with its
`reason` (a second repair cycle, a measurement not yet possible, too few verdicts); the reads stay `not-run` and the
capability stays partial.

**4. Amendments.**

```toml
[[amendment]]
id     = "AMD-0007"                 # append-only
gate   = "KILL-METAL"
field  = "trigger"                  # trigger | threshold | fallback | decided | reads | claim (a late row's confirmed claim reading, N40-10) | introduce
old    = "…"                        # byte-equal to the row before the amendment
new    = "…"
reason = "…"
date   = "2027-02-03T10:00:00Z"
commit = "…"
```

Check `amendment_precedes_measurement`: for every amendment, `date` is earlier than the creation time of the earliest
receipt cited by any part of any gate in `reads` at the governing milestone. An amendment later than such a receipt is
refused (`amended-after-measurement`). A kill row whose `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided` or `reads` differs from the
text reached by replaying its amendments from the seeded text is refused (`changed-without-amendment`). The same two
checks cover the threshold of any convergence gate named in `reads`. The seeded texts are those of the ledger beads.

A kill gate that no ledger bead seeds (one proposed after the ledgers were written, by a pilot or an epic that had
none: KILL-RUT, KILL-TACTILE and KILL-TAXEL, entered by N38a-37, with no `scope_kind` on KILL-TAXEL, decided by
N38ap3-01; and the rows the ledger beads add beyond the plan's tables: N40-23's KILL-SINEW-SEA, N40-24's KILL-HULL,
KILL-STRIBECK, KILL-TYRE and KILL-GRANULAR, N40-26's KILL-AUGUR-SOS and KILL-AUGUR-FAMILYKERNEL) is entered by an
amendment with `field = "introduce"`: `old =
""`, `new` = the row's `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode` and `reads` as one TOML inline table in that key
order, `reason` naming the pilot or epic that proposed it. Its date must precede every receipt cited by any gate in
`reads`, under the same `amendment_precedes_measurement` check; its `new` is the row's seeded text for every later
replay. A kill row with neither a ledger seed nor an `introduce` amendment is refused `changed-without-amendment`. A
pilot record that names `kill_gate = "to-register"` (N38a-09) is updated to the identifier in the commit of the
`introduce` amendment. A seeded row that gains a trigger clause after seeding carries one `trigger` amendment
(KILL-AUGUR-PROBE, N40-26; KILL-LOOPELIM, N40-29).

**5. "One repair cycle", "one optimization pass" (DESIGN reading, recorded because many triggers use the words).**
One bounded attempt to fix the cause of a failed measurement, opened by a log line naming the failed receipt and the
hypothesis (a `note` line of the measuring bead's log, `frob.beadlog/1` of N29a-09, with `cause =
"repair-cycle: <failed receipt hash>: <hypothesis>"`) and closed by a re-measurement with the same threshold on the
same fixture; a value still outside its bound is then recorded `fail` and the measuring bead closes `recorded`
(N41-17, rule 2), which makes the decision bead ready. Where the first measurement
exposed the fixture's instances (their labels, or per-instance or per-pair results that a fix could be tuned to),
the re-measurement runs on fresh instances drawn by the fixture's registered construction with a new seed, and the
opening log line records that reason and the seed or digest of the fresh instances (N24c-26 re-runs on fresh planted
tasks, N24c-34 on a newly recorded matrix). A cycle changes the mechanism, never the fixture's construction, its label
rule, the threshold or the control. A second cycle is a `deferred` decision and needs an ADR.

**6. The decision ADR** (`docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>.md`: the one directory for decision records,
architecture, bet, open-question and kill-gate decisions alike, so that there is one place to look; the former
`docs/delivery/adr/` is retired; an ADR that moves a gate later or records an open question's default is in the same
directory; one file per decision, never renumbered). Entries for several scopes taken at one exit are sections of
that one file, one per scope. A second entry for one scope at one exit (a gate decided in two stages, first at its
pilot and finally after its gate: KILL-SHORTGRAD, N23b-37 then N23b-39, and KILL-FARFIELD, N19cp1-02 then N19c-37,
both at M6) carries `reading = "final"`, and its ADR is `docs/adr/ADR-<gate-name>-<milestone>-final.md` (DESIGN, the
name those beads use); a re-opened decision at an exit already decided takes `reading = "reopened"` and the suffix
`-reopened`. Two entries for one scope at one exit with the same `reading` (absent counting as one value) fail the
load (`decision-entry-duplicate`), and an entry whose `adr` is not the path this rule gives, or names no committed
file, fails it too (`decision-adr-path`). Required fields: the kill gate;
the milestone; the trigger as registered and the amendments applied; each measured value with its receipt hash, set
beside the trigger's number; the outcome; the fallback now in force and the name the capability carries from now on;
the beads re-read (each one "stands on the fallback", "superseded by this ADR", or "withdrawn before this decision",
the last for a bead closed `withdrawn` citing the pilot result or envelope ruling that made the decision ready, N41-17
rule 5); what changes downstream (gates that become `killed` or `superseded`, release cells withdrawn, wording
removed from the README and reports); the session that recorded it, which must not be a session that implemented the
mechanism.

**7. Propagation.** When the decision in force (for a scope, in that scope) has outcome `fired`: every gate in `reads` that failed takes status `killed` with
`detail = "<kill gate>: <fallback>"`; the README status table and the register printout print the row under the
fallback's name. Built here: the status change and those two printouts (the function `apply_decisions(&Register)
-> Register` is the one place it happens; every generated view reads its result). Carried by later beads that
read the same function, so that this bead, on which 73 others depend, holds only the record and its checks:
the exit report (N40-04); `frob capabilities --json` listing the fallback and not the killed capability, status
`killed` (N00-02, whose dependency path reaches this bead through BD-022 and N38b-19, so it cannot be asserted
here); the bead that carried the mechanism closing with reason `fallback` and the fallback's name, and each
dependent closing with `superseded` and the ADR identifier or keeping its dependency if it can stand on the
fallback (the close rules of N41-17); the whole chain on a real kill gate (N40-37). A kill outcome of the ten-fold
test is not re-tested on the same sealed suite.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every kill row loads with the eight kill fields of step 1 (`section`, `consumers`, `trigger`, `fallback`,
      `decided`, `mode`, `reads`, `decision`) and, where they apply, `reread` and `scope_kind`; a kill row with an
      empty `fallback` or `trigger`, or with a measurement field (`claim`, `threshold`, `tier`, `controls`, a
      `[[gate.part]]`), fails.
- [ ] A `deferred` entry names `deferred_to` later than its exit and a non-empty `reason`, and a `not-fired` entry
      cites at least one receipt in `receipts` that resolves under `receipts/gates/` or, for a pilot-read entry,
      `receipts/pilots/`; either without them is refused; a `deferred` entry moves the row to the due list of
      `deferred_to`.
- [ ] Each of the eleven scope kinds loads on a row of that `scope_kind`; a `scope` of another kind (for example
      `family:cuda` on a `backend` row) or on a row with no `scope_kind` is refused `decision-scope-invalid`.
- [ ] A `fired` entry's `fallback_form` loads only when it occurs verbatim in the row's `fallback`; on another
      outcome, or not in the text, the load fails naming the field.
- [ ] A second entry for one scope at one exit loads only with a distinct `reading`, and its `adr` is
      `docs/adr/ADR-<gate>-<milestone>-<reading>.md`; a duplicate is refused `decision-entry-duplicate`; an `adr`
      outside `docs/adr/`, or naming no committed file, is refused `decision-adr-path`.
- [ ] An amendment dated after the earliest governing receipt is refused; a kill row edited with no amendment is
      refused; a valid amendment made before the measurement is accepted and replays to the row's text; a kill row
      in no ledger loads only with an `introduce` amendment dated before every receipt of its reads.
- [ ] At an exit, every kill gate with `decided` equal to that milestone and mode `exit` has an ADR and an outcome,
      or this bead's due list for that exit (the list the exit query of N40-04, which depends on this bead,
      reads) reports it missing.
- [ ] A fired gate is printed under its fallback's name in the README table and the register printout, both
      generated from `apply_decisions`; the killed capability's name appears in neither as passed or implemented.
      (`frob capabilities --json` is asserted by N00-02 and N40-37, which come after this bead.)
- [ ] An ADR recorded by a session listed as implementer of the mechanism is refused.
- [ ] The DESIGN reading of "one repair cycle" (step 5, fresh instances included) is recorded as an ADR in
      `docs/adr/` before the first kill decision of M0.
- [ ] `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <file>` exits 0 on the committed register when every listed
      identifier is one complete kill row whose seeded text hash matches, and non-zero naming the first
      identifier that is absent, duplicated, incomplete or whose text differs; it writes nothing.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::kill::` (synthetic fixtures).

- `kill_row_requires_trigger_and_fallback`: empty `fallback` → load error naming the row.
- `amendment_replay`: seeded trigger "below 0.5", amendment old "below 0.5" new "below 0.45" → row text "below 0.45"
  accepted; row text "below 0.4" with the same amendment → `changed-without-amendment`.
- `amendment_after_measurement_refused`: receipt at 2027-02-03T12:00Z, amendment at 13:00Z → refused; at 11:00Z →
  accepted.
- `amendment_old_must_match`: `old` differing by one character from the current text → refused.
- `outcome_closed_set`: the four outcomes load; `"waived"` and `"passed"` are rejected.
- `fired_propagates`: KILL-X fired with reads [GATE-Y failed] → GATE-Y status `killed`, detail names KILL-X and the
  fallback; the printed capability name is the fallback's.
- `standing_gate_evaluated_every_exit`: a `standing` row from M1 appears in the due list of M1 through M8.
- `late_kill_takes_neighbour_milestone`: a kill row with no `decided` and neighbour gate at M4 → due at M4.
- `adr_by_implementer_refused`: `decision.session` in the implementer set → refused.
- `deferred_needs_later_exit_and_reason`: `deferred` at M3 with `deferred_to = "M4"` and a `reason` → accepted;
  `deferred_to` M3 or M2, absent, or an empty `reason` → refused; the reads stay `not-run`.
- `not_fired_needs_receipt`: `not-fired` with `receipts = []` → refused (negative control RC-13); with a hash that
  names no file under `receipts/gates/` or `receipts/pilots/` → refused; a `pilot-before-funding` row's entry citing
  the hash of a pilot receipt committed at `receipts/pilots/<hash>.json` → accepted.
- `pilot_mode_due_at_funding`: a `pilot-before-funding` row decided "M4" appears in the M4 due list as
  "due when funding is requested" and is not missing at M4 while its work package is unfunded; a funding request
  recorded with no decision makes it missing.
- `rolling_reread_named_exits`: KILL-MCP (`rolling` from M0, `reread = ["M1"]`) is in the due lists of M0 and M1
  and in none after; a `reread` naming an exit at or before `decided` fails the load; an `exit` row decided M1
  with `reread = ["M2"]` is due at M1 and M2 only.
- `decision_array`: a single `decision` table loads as one entry with `exit = decided`; an array with entries
  `{exit = "M3", scope = "cell:wheeled", outcome = "fired"}` and `{exit = "M3", scope = "cell:tracked", outcome =
  "not-fired"}` puts the fallback in force for `wheeled` only; a later entry for `cell:wheeled` at M7 supersedes
  the M3 one, which stays in the file; an entry with a `scope` on a row not decided per scope fails the load; an
  earlier entry whose bytes changed since its commit (RC-57) fails `decision-entry-edited`.
- `rolling_reread_every_exit`: KILL-FSIM (`rolling` from M0) is in the due list of M0 to M8, each later exit
  needing a re-read line in its ADR.
- `kill_row_no_measurement_fields`: a kill row with `claim = "parity"`, or `controls = ["NC-0001"]`, or one
  `[[gate.part]]`, fails the load naming the field; the same row without them loads and prints `not-run`.
- `scope_kinds_closed`: for each of `cell`, `algorithm`, `half`, `clause`, `backend`, `item`, `resolution`, `kind`,
  `family`, `mechanism`, `proofkind`, a row with that `scope_kind` and one entry of that kind loads; `scope = "family:cuda"` on a `backend` row,
  `scope = "item:actors"` on a `cell` row, and `scope = "cell:wheeled"` on a row with no `scope_kind` are each
  refused `decision-scope-invalid`.
- `fallback_form_verbatim`: a KILL-PROXY entry `fired` for `mechanism:chain-cord` with `fallback_form =
  "articulated proxies in evaluation batches only"` loads and is read with that form; `fallback_form = "evaluation
  only"` (not in the row's text), or the field on a `not-fired` entry, fails the load naming `fallback_form`; a
  `fired` entry without it is read as "not at all".
- `backend_scope_per_family`: KILL-D1 entries `backend:gpu-cuda` fired and `backend:gpu-metal` not-fired at M2 put
  the fallback in force for `gpu-cuda` only; both sections live in `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-D1-M2.md`.
- `item_scope_reread`: KILL-DRESSING (`decided = "M5"`, `reread = ["M6"]`, `scope_kind = "item"`) with an M5 entry
  for `item:assets` and an M6 entry for `item:actors` keeps the assets entry in force at M6.
- `second_reading_named_final`: KILL-SHORTGRAD with an M6 entry (ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6.md`) and an M6
  entry with `reading = "final"` (ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6-final.md`) loads, the final entry in force; a
  second M6 entry with no `reading` → `decision-entry-duplicate`; a final entry whose `adr` is
  `docs/delivery/adr/ADR-KILL-SHORTGRAD-M6-final.md` → `decision-adr-path`.
- `deferred_moves_due`: a `deferred` entry at M3 with `deferred_to = "M4"` and `reason = "insufficient verdicts"`

- `introduce_seeds_row`: a kill row in no ledger seed, with an `introduce` amendment dated before every receipt of

## Considerations

- Clock trust: the ordering check compares an amendment's commit time with a receipt's creation time. Both come from
  the repository history and the ledger, not from wall clocks typed by hand; a rebased history is detected by the
  commit hash in the amendment.
- An amendment is legitimate and expected: thresholds are TARGETs. What is refused is the order, not the change.
- Risk: a trigger phrased qualitatively ("no route to less", "no consumer") cannot be checked by a number. The ADR
  must then cite the written argument; the exit review reads it (N40-05).
- The ADR path and naming are settled (DESIGN, the lead's decision that one place to look serves users better than
  two): `docs/adr/`, one file per decision, never renumbered, with the `-<reading>` suffix of step 6 for a second
  entry at one exit. Decision beads that still name `docs/delivery/adr/` are refused by `decision-adr-path` at load.
- The scope-kind table is closed so that a view can rely on it; a gate that needs a twelfth kind changes this bead
  (and its ledger row's `scope_kind`) first, and until then records one conservative whole-row entry, as N21b-34
  did before `backend` existed.
- A killed capability is never re-enabled by a feature flag without a new pilot, a new kill row and a new decision.
