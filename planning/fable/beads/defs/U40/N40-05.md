## Background

An **exit** is the recorded statement that a milestone's demonstrations ran and its gates have values. It is recorded
by a **fresh-context session**: an agent session that did not implement the milestone and has no memory of how the
work was done, so it can only believe what the repository shows. The program has nine exits (M0 to M8), each with its
own review bead. This bead builds what they share: the procedure, the exit record, and a runner script that performs
the mechanical steps, so that nine reviews are one procedure applied nine times and not nine improvisations.

The reasons are recorded failures of sibling projects: 87 beads closed within 12 seconds with one shared close reason
while serving figures from a stand-in (FACT, from the sibling's own review); status tables edited by hand; a gate
declared passed by the session that wrote the code. The rules here answer each: typed close reasons that cite a test
and its output; negative controls that must be caught; a generated status table; no self-certification.

DESIGN decisions: an exit review *implements nothing* (if it finds a defect it opens a bead and stays open);
a *failing value is a record* (the review records it and applies the kill gate or blocks; it never waits for a
better number); the exit is *the observable line of the milestone*, not a count of gates. Rejected: sign-off by the
implementing swarm with a reviewer's approval, because approval of a narrative is weaker than regeneration from a
clean checkout.

## Scope

In: the procedure (all twelve steps as a procedure), the exit record format, the runner, the disposition
vocabulary, the cross-milestone rules, the table of attached checks through which other beads add an exit-time
check to the runner, and the rule that a block returned by any called check makes the verdict `not exited`.

Out, with the bead that has it. Four checks of the procedure are carried out by mechanisms other beads own;
this bead calls them, places their sections in the exit record and propagates their blocks, and specifies and
tests none of their rules:

- step 9, the risk review (which rows are readable at an exit, the pre-filled readings, the three outcomes, the
  rule that a fired signal with no outcome blocks, the file `risk-review.json`): N41-01, through
  `scripts/exit/risk-review.sh`;
- step 10, the open questions due (the register and its loader, and the command that writes a default as an
  ADR: N41-03; the per-milestone list of questions due and the rule that a due question with neither an answer
  nor a default ADR blocks: N40-35, which attaches its check to this runner);
- step 11, the count of active Moonshot items (the predicate `moonshot_cap`: N10a-02, the doctrine check D-6;
  the queue it counts: NAB-21; the order of activation and the listing of the queue from M5 on: N41-09).

- the dependency firewall at the exit (`firewall(Mn)`: foundations reached outside the milestone's set, admission
  inherited from an older pin, a missing required foundation without its route, the M7 freeze window): N40-07,
  which this runner calls in step 5 and whose "Foundations" section and blocks it places in the exit record.

Also out: the content of each exit (the nine review beads and the epics), the gate query (N40-04).

## Technical approach

**1. What an exit requires (all of the following).**

- *Every gate of the milestone has a recorded value.* A failing value is a record. A kill gate that fired is
  recorded as an ADR with the fallback now in force, and the capability is described by the fallback's name from
  then on.
- *Every demonstration runs from a clean checkout*, through `frob --json` and, where the verb registry exposes the
  verb to agents, through `frob-mcp`.
- *GATE-GAUNT-01 holds*: every gate that passed has a registered negative control that it catches; a run over all
  controls shows each caught by every gate it names.
- *Every report prints its `reality:` line* (GATE-GROUND-01, from M1 onward); with no dataset it reads `no-claim`.
- *The risk register has been read*, and the open questions due have been answered or have taken their defaults.

**2. Rules that bound the review.** A gate may pass before its milestone. It may not move later without an ADR. The
register fixes each gate's milestone and threshold; where a milestone's own list and the register differ, the
register governs. A milestone does not exit on a count of gates: its exit line is the observable. An admission is
never inherited: a foundation or an airlock group first used at a milestone is admitted at that milestone, by its
own record, at the pin of that day. At most four Moonshot [M] items are active at once in any milestone (an item is
active from the funding of its pilot until its expiry gate; the others wait in the Moonshot queue, the
`[[moonshot_queue]]` records of `registries/ev_matrix.toml`, which belongs to the register of deferred and killed
ideas).

**3. The procedure (numbered; steps 1 to 6 are run by the runner, 7 to 12 are the reviewer's).**

1. Start a session with no prior context of the milestone; record its identity. Refuse to proceed if that identity
   appears as `implemented_by` in any row due at this exit or as author of any bead of the milestone.
2. Clone the repository at the exit candidate commit into an empty directory; build with the pinned toolchain.
3. Run every demonstration manifest of the milestone (`demos/<demo_id>/demo.yaml`), each command through
   `frob --json` and again through `frob-mcp` where exposed; each must finish in under 60 s on the CPU tier
   (TARGET) and its claim identifiers must exist in the register.
4. Re-run the recurring gates at this commit and the full control sweep.
5. Regenerate the gate report (N40-04) and the README status table; fail if either differs from the committed file.
   Run the dependency firewall `firewall(Mn)` of N40-07 over what the milestone's targets link; copy its
   "Foundations" section into the exit record and list each of its findings (`foundation-outside-firewall`,
   `unadmitted-use`, `admission-stale`, `admission-inherited`, `missing-foundation-without-route`, at M7
   `pin-moved-during-freeze`) among the blocking parts.
6. List: kill decisions due with and without ADRs; beads of the milestone whose close reason is shared with another
   bead; controls that have never failed their gate; `not-run`, `blocked` and `fail` parts.
7. For every part due, read its receipt and record a disposition: `accepted-pass`, `accepted-kill: <fallback>`,
   `recorded-fail: blocks`, `no-claim`, `not-run: <variant and reason>`, or `rejected: <reason>` (a void pass).
   A part measured on several tiers cites a receipt set (`frob.gauntlet.receipt-set/1`, N38b-19): the reviewer
   reads every member receipt, and a member that is absent from `receipts/gates/`, does not hash to its name, or
   names another gate or part makes the disposition `rejected: receipt-set-member`. A pilot part (scope `pilot:
   <id>`, N40-02 step 5) takes none of the six: the report prints it with its pilot's verdict and receipt
   (N40-04, step 4), and the reviewer checks that the receipt resolves under `receipts/pilots/`.
8. For every kill gate due, check the measured values against the registered trigger and its amendments, and that
   the ADR names the fallback, the renamed capability and the re-read dependents (N40-03).
9. Read the risk register. For each row record whether its earliest signal has fired. A fired signal ends in one of
   three recorded outcomes: a kill gate decided, an ADR that changes a mechanism or a scope, or a written reason why
   neither applies. A fired signal with no record is a defect of the exit review. (Carried out with
   `scripts/exit/risk-review.sh <Mn>` of N41-01, which pre-fills the mechanical readings, takes the reviewer's
   outcomes and returns the rows that block.)
10. List the open questions due. Each is answered, or takes its fail-closed default, recorded as an ADR and
    reported with the capability the default removed. (The list and its block come from the owner-input calendar's
    check, N40-35, over `registries/open.toml`; a default is written with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
    open_questions -- default <id> --exit <Mn>` of N41-03. There is no binary named `frob-conformance`: the
    register commands are example targets of that crate, declared by their owning beads, BD-025.)
11. Count active [M] items; more than four blocks the exit until one is parked. (The count is `moonshot_cap` of
    N10a-02 over the Moonshot queue; "parked" means released from `active` by a recorded transition of N41-09.)
12. Demonstrate the exit line; apply the milestone's stop-or-re-plan rule; write the exit record; append the
    review's own line to its bead log.

On every run the runner evaluates the verdicts behind steps 5 and 9 to 11 by calling those checks through the
table of section 5a, lists each returned block among the blocking parts with the name of the check that returned
it, and copies each check's section into the exit record. The runner holds no risk rule, no open-question rule,
no firewall rule and no count of its own.

**4. The exit record** `docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/EXIT.md` (generated skeleton, reviewer fills dispositions): commit;
session; the exit line and its receipts; the gate report digest; the table of dispositions; kill decisions with ADR
identifiers; variants in force; the "Foundations" section of the firewall check; risks with fired signals and
their outcomes; open questions and defaults taken; [M] items active; the section and the warnings of every
attached check (section 5a) under the check's identifier, with the list of checks run; the milestone's
stop-or-re-plan rule
(`stop_rule` of the milestone row, N40-06) with its reading (no trigger met, or the re-plan ADR); beads found
during the review; the verdict `exited` or `not exited: <blocking list>`.

**5. The runner** `scripts/exit/review.sh <Mn>` (plain shell, idempotent): performs the identity refusal of step 1
and steps 2 to 6, runs the attached checks (section 5a), writes `target/exit/<Mn>/<utc>/` with the transcripts,
and prints the skeleton of the exit record. It never writes to `registries/gates.toml`. A part whose register row
names the milestone's exit review as its recording bead (the parts of GATE-LEMMA-03 after M1, N40-02 step 5) is
recorded by the review session with the recorder of N38b-19 before it starts the runner, so that the clone of step
2 carries the recording; the session implemented nothing, so the recorder admits it, and recording a measured value
is not a repair.

**5a. Attached checks (DESIGN).** Many beads own a rule that must be read at every exit (the risk review, the
owner calendar, the tracker check, the principles check and the others listed below), and most of them depend on
this bead, so the runner cannot name them in its source. They are attached through one table,
`scripts/exit/checks.toml` (schema `frob.exitchecks/1`), one record per check:

```toml
[[check]]
id      = "risk-review"                   # unique, stable
owner   = "N41-01"                        # the bead that specifies and tests the rule
step    = 9                               # the step of the procedure whose section it fills
command = "scripts/exit/risk-review.sh"   # run as: <command> <Mn> --root <dir> --json
since   = "M0"                            # the first exit at which it must run
after   = ""                              # optional: a check of the same step whose output this one reads
```

Contract of a check: it prints one JSON object, `{check, milestone, section, blocks: [{id, reason}], warnings:
[{id, text}]}`, where `section` is the Markdown the exit record carries for it, and it exits 0 whenever it ran,
whatever it found; a non-zero exit means it could not run. The runner executes every record whose `since` is at or
before Mn, in step order, within a step a check after the one it names in `after`, and otherwise by `id` (an
`after` that names a check of another step, an unknown check, or closes a loop is a load error of the table);
copies each `section` into the exit record under the check's `id`;
lists every block among the blocking parts with the check's `id` and `owner`; lists warnings in the record's
"warnings" section (a warning never blocks); and prints the identifiers it ran. A record whose command is absent,
exits non-zero or prints no valid object is reported `not-run`, and the verdict is `not exited`. Four identifiers
are required at every exit, because the requirements of section 1 and the rules of section 2 name them: `firewall`, `risk-review`,
`owner-calendar` and `moonshot-cap`; if one of them has no record the runner reports its step `not-run`. Reason
for a table and not edits to the runner: each rule stays specified and tested in one bead, the order of work stays
"the runner first, its checks after", and a reviewer sees in one place which checks an exit ran.

The table as this bead writes it holds the checks whose owners it depends on: `firewall` (N40-07, step 5),
`risk-review` (N41-01, step 9: `scripts/exit/risk-review.sh`, which wraps `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
risks -- review <Mn> --root <dir> --json`), `risks-complete` (N41-01: `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example risks -- check
--complete --root <dir> --json`, then the same with `--cross`; step 9), `open-complete` (N41-03: `cargo run -p
frob-conformance --example open_questions -- check --complete`; step 10) and `moonshot-cap` (N10a-02, step 11). The two completeness commands are wrapped so that a failure is a block naming
the first missing or unresolved row. Beads that depend on this one attach their own record in their own change:
`owner-calendar` (N40-35, step 10); `suite-02-confirm` (N41-14, step 10, since M0: the recorded toolchain and
MCP decision confirmed at the exit); `premortem` (N41-PM, step 9, since M0, after `risk-review`: command
`scripts/exit/premortem.sh`, which wraps `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example premortem -- read <Mn>`);
`risk-plants` (N41-32, step 9, since M0, after `risk-review`: command `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh`, which wraps
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example risks -- plants --exit <Mn>`, with the flag `--all` added by N41-37 for
the full sweep from M5); `tracker-check` (N41-16, step 6); `principles` (N00-21, step 6); `visibility-guard`
(N41-05, step 10; its `unverified` reading is a warning at an exit); `decision-sweep` (NAB-22, step 8, since M0:
command `cargo run -p frob-conformance --bin decision-sweep -- check --exit`, run as `<command> <Mn> --root <dir>
--json`, over the sweep record `docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/decision-records.json` that the milestone's sweep bead
writes at the exit candidate commit; a missing record is the block `sweep.missing`); and from M1
`bead-lifecycle` (N41-17, step 6: command `scripts/bead-lifecycle-check.sh`) and `done-report` (N41-26, step 6:
command `scripts/exit/done-report.sh`), `gate-register-path` (N40-36, step 4), `kill-ledger` (N40-30, step 6)
and `kill-decision-path` (N40-37, step 6, after `kill-ledger`). An exit review bead depends on the beads whose checks its milestone needs,
so that the table is complete when the review starts; among them is its milestone's sweep bead, NAB-23 to NAB-31
for the exits M0 to M8 in order, whose record the `decision-sweep` check reads.

**6. Bead discipline.** A bead closes with a typed reason that cites its own test and that test's output: a log
line whose verdict is `pass`, whose claim identifiers resolve, and whose recording session is not the implementing
session. A closed bead is not acceptance; acceptance is the gate's row. A bead discovered during implementation
becomes a dependency of its milestone's exit review. An exit review closes when every gate of its milestone has a
recorded value and a caught negative control, every kill gate due has its ADR, and the status table regenerates.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `scripts/exit/review.sh M0` on a clean clone runs steps 2 to 6 and every attached check whose `since` is
      reached, and exits non-zero while any part blocks.
- [ ] The runner refuses a session identity that appears as an implementer of a due row.
- [ ] A demonstration that passes through the CLI and is not reachable through MCP although the registry exposes it
      fails step 3.
- [ ] A shared close reason across two beads of the milestone is listed in step 6.
- [ ] A fired risk signal with no recorded outcome makes the exit record invalid (`not exited`): the block is
      returned by the risk review of N41-01 and the runner propagates it.
- [ ] An open question at its deadline with neither an answer nor a default ADR blocks: the block is returned by
      the calendar check of N40-35 over the register of N41-03 and the runner propagates it.
- [ ] Five active [M] items block; four do not: the verdict is `moonshot_cap` of N10a-02 and the runner
      propagates it, printing the items that hold the slots.
- [ ] A firewall finding of N40-07 (a planted `cudarc` package linked by an M1 crate) is listed among the blocking
      parts and the verdict is `not exited`.
- [ ] With any of the four required checks (`firewall`, `risk-review`, `owner-calendar`, `moonshot-cap`) absent
      from the table, or any registered check failing to run, the runner reports that step as `not-run` and the
      verdict is `not exited`; it never treats a check it could not run as a pass.
- [ ] A check attached by a record in `scripts/exit/checks.toml` is run from its `since` exit on: its section
      appears in the exit record, a block it returns makes the verdict `not exited` and names the check and its
      owner, and a warning it returns is listed and does not block.
- [ ] The register completeness checks run at every exit: a scratch risk register with a gap in its series, and
      a scratch open-question register one row short of its count, each block the exit (through `risks-complete`
      and `open-complete`).
- [ ] The exit record lists a disposition for every part due and validates against its schema
      (`frob.exitrecord/1`, DESIGN).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

The runner's helpers live in `crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/review.rs`; `cargo test -p frob-conformance
exit::review::`.

- `disposition_closed_set`: the six dispositions parse; `"ok"` is rejected.
- `every_due_part_has_disposition`: a record missing one of 11 due parts → invalid, naming the part; a pilot
  part among them needs none and is listed with its pilot's verdict.
- `receipt_set_members_read`: a part citing a receipt set of three members, one removed from the scratch
  `receipts/gates/` → `rejected: receipt-set-member` naming that member; with all three present → the
  disposition the reviewer gives stands.
- `shared_close_reason_detected`: two bead logs with byte-equal close reasons → both listed.
- `called_check_block_propagates` (four cases on a scratch repository; the first three with the real check, whose
  bead this one depends on): a queue with 5 active items → the runner's blocking list holds the cap check's block
  and the five identifiers, with 4 → none; a fired risk signal with no outcome → the risk review's block is
  listed, with an outcome ∈ {kill decided, ADR, written reason} → none; a synthetic metadata graph in which an M1
  crate links `cudarc` → the firewall's `foundation-outside-firewall` is listed, without it → none; a due
  question with no answer and no ADR → the block of the check registered as `owner-calendar` is listed, with the
  default ADR → the question is listed with the removed capability. The calendar check belongs to N40-35, which
  depends on this bead, so this fourth case registers a `synthetic` check under that identifier which returns
  one block per `silent` row of N41-03's `due(Mn)`; N40-35 replays the case with its real check. The rules
  themselves are tested where they live (`d6_cap` in N10a-02; `fired_without_outcome_blocks` and
  `three_outcomes_each_unblock` in N41-01; `due_question_blocks` in N40-35; `outside_firewall_detected` in N40-07).
- `called_check_missing_is_not_run`: with `risk-review.sh` removed from the scratch repository the step is
  `not-run` and the verdict `not exited`; with the `owner-calendar` record removed from the table, the same.
- `attached_check_contract`: a `synthetic` check registered with `since = "M1"` is not run at M0 and is run at M1;
  its section is copied under its identifier; its warning is listed and does not block; its block does; a check
  that exits non-zero, or prints an object without `blocks`, is `not-run`, and a check that exits non-zero with
  no output never leaves its step reported as passed (negative control N40-CX RC-62).
- `attached_checks_order_is_stable`: two runs over one table list the checks in the same order (step, then
  `after`, then `id`): a `synthetic` check `aaa` of step 9 with `after = "risk-review"` runs after it; an
  `after` that names a check of another step, or two checks naming each other, fails the table load.
- `completeness_checks_block`: a scratch risk register missing RISK-07 blocks through `risks-complete`; a scratch
  open-question register with one row removed blocks through `open-complete`.
- `reviewer_not_implementer`: reviewer identity in the implementer set → refused.
- `stop_rule_recorded`: an exit record whose stop-or-re-plan reading is empty → invalid, naming the field.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/exit_protocol.sh`: builds a scratch repository holding a synthetic milestone with three gates, one
kill gate, one demo, one risk and one open question; runs `scripts/exit/review.sh` and asserts `not exited`;
supplies receipts, dispositions, the ADR and the default; asserts `exited` and that the record lists the
identifiers of the attached checks that ran; then replays the acceptance plants one at a time (every acceptance
criterion above that names a planted state) and asserts each returns `not exited` with its reason. Logs per step: `ts`, `script`, `bead`,
`step`, `event`, the command line, exit code, wall time, the evidence block of each demo envelope (claim, class,
tier, determinism, identities, seed), counts against thresholds, the digest of the gate report, and on failure the
first differing value and the scratch path. The `summary` line counts assertions and names GATE-GAUNT-01,
GATE-GAUNT-10 and GATE-GROUND-01.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: five requirements; twelve steps; six dispositions; cap 4 (N40-KA R-14). Negative controls (N40-CX):
RC-03 (self-certification), RC-15 (a demonstration run from a dirty tree), RC-16 (a shared close reason), RC-17 (a
fired risk signal with no outcome), RC-18 (a report with no `reality:` line), RC-19 (a fifth active Moonshot item),
RC-62 (an attached check that exits non-zero taken as a pass; caught by `attached_check_contract`).
RC-17 and RC-19 are each registered once and are caught by the rule's owner (the risk review of N41-01; the cap
check of N10a-02); this bead's script replays them to show that the runner propagates the block.
Determinism: the runner's listings are sorted and stable; two runs at one commit give equal bytes.

## Considerations

- The reviewer is an agent session; "fresh" is enforced by identity, not by trust. Session identities come from the
  bead logs and gate rows.
- Cost: step 4 (the control sweep) grows with the registry; at M3 it is the long pole. It may be sharded, never
  sampled.
- A review that finds a defect does not fix it. It opens a bead, adds it to its own dependencies and stays open.
- Clean checkout means no sibling checkout is dirty either: a dirty sibling is acceptable for development builds
  with the tree hash in the receipt, and no PASS is recorded from one.
- Interaction: the exit review of M1 also evaluates the schedule gate (N40-11); those of M3 and M7 read the
  ten-fold program gates (N40-14 to N40-22).
