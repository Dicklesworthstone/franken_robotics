## Background

Gates are defined twice by construction: once in the chapter where the mechanism is described (with fixtures,
tolerances and controls) and once in the register (identity, claim, earliest milestone, the brief). Two statements
of one thing drift. The rule is fixed: **where a chapter and the register differ, the register governs**, on a
gate's identity, its claim, its earliest milestone, a kill gate's trigger, fallback and decision point, and every
number the register states. A rule needs a detector, and this bead is the detector: a lint that compares what each
crate declares about its gates with the register and fails on a difference that is not on the list of recorded,
resolved discrepancies.

The list matters as much as the lint. When the register was compiled, a number of real disagreements were found
and resolved; each resolution is a small design decision with a reason. They are carried here in full so that an
implementer who meets the chapter's older wording knows which reading is in force and why. Alternative rejected:
correcting the chapter text and forgetting the history, because the next reader of an old bead, receipt or ADR
would meet the old number with no explanation.

## Scope

In: the lint; the four groups of recorded discrepancies; three more found when the milestone lists were compared
with the register. Out: related thresholds that are not conflicts (N40-08); the cross-check of
kill decisions against the ledger (N40-30); late gates (N40-10).

## Technical approach

**1. What a crate declares.** In `contract.toml`, one block per gate the crate's mechanism defines or consumes,
in the format of N34x-02 (schema, the one reader `frob_core::contract::read_gate_decls`, and the switch-over from
the interim file `registries/gate_declarations.toml`): `[[gate]] id, role = "owner" | "consumer", claim,
milestone_parts, threshold_numbers = [...]`, and for a kill gate `trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, `reads`.
The block also carries the chapter-side data of the fourteen chapter register beads (`criterion`, `criteria[]`,
`measured_by`, `fixture`, `script`, `kill`, `notes`, `form_hash`, `strata_fixture`, `bet`); the lint reads them,
prints `criterion` beside a difference, and compares none of them with the register (they are not register
fields; N40-02 step 8). The lint (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/register_agreement.rs`, run by
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test register_agreement`) joins the declarations with `registries/gates.toml`
and parses no block itself.

**2. Checks.**
1. Every register row has exactly one `owner` declaration, in the crate of its defining subsection, from the exit
   at which its first part (for a kill row, its decision) is due; before that exit it has at most one, and a row
   whose owning crate does not exist yet is reported `pending: <crate>` and does not fail (DESIGN: a declaration is
   written with the mechanism or by the deciding bead, many of which land at M3 to M8, so demanding it at M1
   would fail every later gate; the same reading as the kill cross-check N40-30).
2. Owner and register agree on claim, part milestones, and (kill rows) trigger, fallback and decision point; every
   number in the register's brief appears among `threshold_numbers`.
3. A `consumer` declaration may restate, never change: a consumer whose text differs from the owner's is compared
   with the resolutions below; an unlisted difference fails `consumer-diverges`.
4. Each milestone's own acceptance list (the epic) is a subset of the register's due set for that exit; an
   identifier in the epic and not in the register fails; one in the register and not in the epic is added to the
   exit report with the note "register governs".
5. A discrepancy on the recorded list is printed with its resolution and does not fail.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The lint passes on a tree whose declarations agree with the register and prints the recorded discrepancies
      (6 + 4 + 1 + 11 + 3) with their resolutions.
- [ ] A consumer declaration of KILL-DR that keeps the effective-sample-size clause as a trigger fails
      `consumer-diverges`; as a recorded diagnostic it passes.
- [ ] An owner declaration that states GATE-HAB-11 at M3 fails; at M4 it passes.
- [ ] A register row due at the reviewed exit with no owner declaration fails; a row with two owners fails at any
      time; a later row whose crate does not exist yet is printed `pending: <crate>` and does not fail.
- [ ] KILL-ADVECT and KILL-FILM are two rows with different owners; a declaration naming Habitat's trigger under
      KILL-FILM fails.
- [ ] The lint runs in under 5 s and is part of the M1 exit runner's step 5.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `one_owner_per_gate`: zero or two owners for a row due at the reviewed exit → failure naming the gate and
  crates; zero owners for a row due at a later exit whose crate does not exist → `pending: <crate>`, no failure;
  two owners at any time → failure.
- `chapter_side_fields_not_compared`: a declaration whose `criterion` and `fixture` differ from anything in the
  register passes; the same declaration with a changed `claim` fails.
- `owner_matches_register`: mismatch in claim, in a part milestone, in a trigger number → three failures.
- `consumer_restates_only`: consumer text equal to owner → pass; unlisted difference → `consumer-diverges`.
- `recorded_discrepancy_tolerated`: the six kill pairs, eleven skeleton rows and three extra rows load as
  `[[resolved]]` records and each suppresses exactly its own difference (a property test plants each difference on a
  neighbouring gate and expects failure).
- `skeleton_governs`: for each of the eleven rows the register's parts equal the "Register" column.
- `kill_film_is_tractions`: owner section of KILL-FILM is Traction's; KILL-ADVECT's is Habitat's.
- `epic_subset_of_register`: each epic's acceptance identifiers ⊆ due set of its exit.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/register_agreement.sh`: runs the lint on the clean tree (exit 0, 25 resolved rows printed); then for
each of five plants (wrong claim, wrong milestone, changed trigger, missing owner, a consumer that turns a
diagnostic into a trigger) applies it in a scratch copy and asserts a non-zero exit and the gate identifier in the
output. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/register_agreement/<utc>.ndjson`: per step `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, the command, exit code, wall time, `metric` lines with the counts of rows
compared, declarations read, rows `pending`, and differences found against the expected counts (25 resolved rows:
6, 4, 1, 11, 3), the evidence block `{claim: known-answer, class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`,
and on failure the first differing field with both texts and the file paths; the `summary` line counts assertions
and names GATE-GAUNT-10.

### Known answers and negative controls

N40-KA R-21 (25 resolved rows by group: 6, 4, 1, 11, 3). Negative controls N40-CX RC-28 to RC-32 (the five plants
above). Metamorphic: renaming a crate's contract file path changes nothing; reordering blocks changes nothing.

## Considerations

- The declaration format is DESIGN and must be agreed with the workspace unit that owns `contract.toml`; until
  then the lint reads a single hand-kept file `registries/gate_declarations.toml` with the same fields.
- A new discrepancy is never resolved by editing the list. It is resolved by an amendment or an ADR, and the list
  gains a row that cites it.
- The lint compares numbers extracted from text; it will miss a threshold restated in words. Reviews read rows.
- Two readings found by the Sensorium beads enter the list by that route when their rows are recorded, and the
  recorded count of 25 changes only then. (a) The brief of GATE-SENS-13 says the third-gain amendment "reverses
  every admitted cell"; exact enumeration on the 561-cell docking grid reverses the 327 admitted cells with
  positive error, and the 17 cells with zero error stay admitted (BD-190 tests 327 and 17). (b) The M1 part of
  GATE-SENS-08 is printed "docking rows", and the observability gate has none; its M1 scope is the linearized form
  on the CPU that the first mission slice needs (N20b-21). GATE-SENS-15 to -18 and KILL-VISIBILITY, absent from the
  Sensorium table, are among the late rows that N40-10 folds in.
- The `[[gate]]` block, its schema and its single reader are N34x-02's (on which this bead depends); the
  hand-kept file above is used only until N34x-02's switch-over, after which a row added to it fails
  `contract.gate-decl-stray-file`.
