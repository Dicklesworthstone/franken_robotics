## Background

The register's subsystem tables were compiled at one point in time. Subsections added to the chapters afterwards
define further gates and kill gates. They are **binding under the same nine rules** as every other gate (N40-01);
they simply have no row in the compiled tables yet. Until the tables are regenerated, each such gate has its row in
the subsection that defines it, and that row governs its threshold; the milestone is the one its defining row
states; and a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it.

Left as an index in prose, these gates are the ones most likely to be forgotten at an exit: they are in no
subsystem table, and one group of them is printed only in abridged form. This bead enters every one into
`registries/gates.toml` as a first-class row, so that the generated register and the exit reports include them and
the "until the tables are regenerated" state ends. Counted from the two indexes: 76 convergence gates (49 from the
first index, 27 more from the final-audit index) and 25 kill gates (22 and 3).

## Scope

One record per gate, written once. This bead **creates** the record of each of the 76 late convergence gates and
fills the fields the register governs (`id`, `kind`, `claim`, `section`, `consumers`, `milestone`, the
`[[gate.part]]` list, `recurring`, the brief in `threshold`), as N40-02 does for the compiled tables. It leaves
`controls` and `tier` empty and every part `not-run`.

In: those rows (identity, claim, part milestones, owner subsection, brief), the neighbour rule for kill gates, the
four amended rows, the two name collisions, the regenerated tables.

Out, and which bead has it:

- The full criteria, `controls`, `tier`, the chapter's declaration and the measuring bead of each late gate: the
  register bead of the gate's chapter, where the chapter has one (the table of step 8 in N40-02): N14b-26
  (GATE-SKEL-09 to -11), N16c-05 (GATE-KIN-08 to -13), N19c-29 (GATE-HAB-15, -16), N20b-21 (GATE-SENS-15 to -18),
  N21c-32 (GATE-SPARK-23), N22c-49 (GATE-REFLEX-10, -11; GATE-SHIELD-06, -07; GATE-GAIT-04, -05), N23b-16
  (GATE-CORTEX-18 to -22), N28c-25 (GATE-ATEL-17 to -19), N30b-22 (GATE-GROUND-11), N31b-12 (GATE-FOUNDRY-13 to
  -15), N32b-25 (GATE-TETHER-15, -16), N38b-23 (GATE-GAUNT-02 to -11, GATE-FID-09). Those beads create no second
  record. The late Augur gates (GATE-AUGUR-25 to -35) have no chapter register bead (N25d-41 covers the compiled
  Augur table only); their measuring beads fill `controls` and `tier`.
- The triggers and fallbacks of the 25 late kill gates as ledger text, and their kill records: N40-29. Step 3
  below fixes only their decision points.
- The two related-threshold rows printed beside the index: N40-08.

**2. Claims.** Each row takes the claim its defining row prints. A defining row that prints two (for example
"Known answer; Design comparison", "Performance; known answer (determinism)", "Known answer; Parity", "Known answer;
Fidelity") is entered with the claim of its governing part under rule 3 of N40-01, decided by this procedure: if the
row contains a comparison of two methods or arms with a threshold, `design-comparison`; else if it contains a figure
on named hardware with a threshold, `performance`; else if it compares with an oracle or another tier, `parity` or
`fidelity`; else `known-answer`. The reading entered is recorded per row with one sentence of reason, and the
defining bead's owner confirms it. Until confirmed each part of the row is `blocked` with
`detail = "claim-reading: <chapter register bead>"` (printed "blocked: claim reading"; the second blocking cause
N40-01 admits besides an open question), which is not a pass; the confirmation is a dated `[[amendment]]` of the
row's `claim` field (N40-03) citing the governing clause, after which the parts return to `not-run`.

Where the chapter's register bead already states the owner's reading, that reading is the one entered and the
procedure is not run against it (DESIGN: the owner knows which clause governs, and one row must not carry one
claim in the register and another in its chapter's declaration): GATE-REFLEX-11 `fidelity` (the promotion clause
governs and the cart is its known-answer precondition; N22c-49); GATE-ATEL-19 `known-answer` (an accounting
identity and two structural facts govern; the timing columns are reported under the Performance disclosure
rules; N28c-25); GATE-FOUNDRY-15 `known-answer` (the tube case and the planted under-built link govern; the
corpus share is a recorded figure; N31b-12). The two-claim rows whose chapter bead states no reading are entered
by the procedure and stay `blocked: claim reading` until their owner confirms: GATE-KIN-08, -10, -11, -12
(N16c-05) and GATE-HAB-15 (N19c-29).

## Considerations

- The count 550 is a snapshot of the seeded rows: the compiled tables and the two indexes. A kill gate that
  enters later by an `introduce` amendment (N40-03: three from N38a-37, one from N40-23, four from N40-24, two
  from N40-26 at plan time) is counted by `late_counts` in a separate figure, "introduced", printed beside 550,
  so that the pinned 550 stays the plan's count and each introduction is still a visible act: it changes the
  second figure and adds an amendment record. A convergence gate added to the plan's rows changes 550 itself,
  by the change that adds it.
- Several late gates are due at M0 (GATE-WS-05, parts of GATE-WS-06, -07, -09, GATE-GAUNT-02, -05, -08, -10): the
  M0 exit review must see them, which is why this bead is an M0 bead.
- Fail-closed default for an unconfirmed claim reading: `blocked`, shown as open.
- A chapter unit that discovers a gate in its text that neither index lists adds it by amendment; the lint N40-09
  reports any gate declared by a crate and absent from the register.
- The chapter register beads named in Scope print their late gates with a claim and a milestone of their own.
  Those columns are the chapter's declaration, which N40-09 compares with this index; they agree with it on every
  late gate (checked row by row when the two sets of beads were reconciled). Each of those beads depends on this
  one, or is related to it where it is the earlier bead's concern only.
