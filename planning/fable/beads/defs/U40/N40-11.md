## Background

The program bets that **narrow purity is affordable** (bet BET-12): a core built only on the suite's own sibling
libraries, with GPU backends in annex workspaces, reaches M2 without a foreign physics engine. The way that bet
fails is specific: the first two milestones overrun because a sibling library lacked a primitive that the work
needed, the upstream work item that would add it had not landed in the sibling's repository, and no interim
posture (a documented temporary route with a switch-over rule) covered the gap. If that happens, continuing into M2
would stack the engine on a foundation that has already shown it cannot be relied on at the pace assumed, so the
program **pauses for a written re-plan before M2 starts**.

This is a *program gate*: it decides whether the program continues as planned and belongs to no subsystem. It is
evaluated at the end of M1, in the M1 exit review (seed BD-147), which calls the computation this bead builds. The
planning estimates (2 weeks for M0, 5 for M1) exist only to make this bet testable; they are not acceptance
evidence and no other gate cites them. DESIGN decision: the gate fires on a *cause*, not on lateness alone.
Rejected alternative: a pure deadline. A milestone that is late because its own work was harder than expected says
nothing about purity, and a deadline gate would either punish that or be waived, and a gate that is waived
teaches the program to waive gates.

## Scope

In: the elapsed-time computation; the cause attribution of blocked time; the decision rule; the record in the
register; the pause mechanism; the content required of a re-plan. Out: the milestone registry (N40-06); the M1 exit
review that invokes this (seed BD-147); the upstream work items themselves (seed BD-037 and the suite units).
Also out: the bet's side. BET-12 depends on this bead, reads this gate's record and adds what belongs to the bet:
the pre-registration of the falsifier, the bet verdict by branch with its ADR (`docs/adr/bet-12-m1.md`), and the
refusal of funding records for M2 work packages until the re-plan ADR exists. This bead does not depend on
BET-12; the registered falsifier it implements is the row of the bet register.

## Technical approach

**1. Quantities.** E₀ = 2 weeks, E₁ = 5 weeks (planning estimates of M0 and M1, DESIGN). E = E₀ + E₁ = 7 weeks.
Threshold Θ = 1.5 · E = 10.5 weeks. T = elapsed time from the start of M0 (the first M0 bead moving to in-progress)
to the candidate exit of M1, in weeks: from `started` of M0 in `registries/milestones.toml` to the UTC commit time
of the M1 exit candidate commit (M1's own `exited` is written only after this review). Arithmetic is exact
(rationals; 21/2). The two events are one definition (DESIGN, the same in BET-12; the plan says "the elapsed time
of M0 plus M1" and names no event): the first M0 bead is the first bead of the first implementation session, so
`started` of M0 is the start the bet registers. It is written by the exit runner at the M0 exit, and before it is
used `times_supported` of N40-06 checks it, and `exited` of M0, against the tracker history and the M0 exit
record's commit; a value they do not support stops the computation with `exit-time-unsupported` (RC-21). E₀ and
E₁ are the estimates as registered: `estimate_weeks` of M0 and M1 must equal their values in the file at the
commit of M0's `started`, and a value changed since with no `[[amendment]]` record (N40-06) stops the computation
with `estimate-raised-silently` (RC-37). An amendment dated after the start does not change E: it is a re-plan's
new estimate, which applies after this gate.

**2. Attribution.** Every interval in which a bead of M0 or M1 was blocked is a `blocked`/`unblocked` pair of
lines in its per-bead log `frob.beadlog/1` (N29a-09), whose `cause` field carries a tag from the closed set below;
a `sibling-primitive-missing` line also names the sibling, the primitive and the upstream work item (`FD-…`). The
set is owned here (DESIGN: the gate must tell a missing primitive from a wrong one, which the coarser tags of an
earlier draft of the log format, `upstream` and `defect`, could not). A `blocked` line whose tag is outside the set
is refused like an untagged one, naming the bead (N29a-09's `append` already refuses it; the attribution refuses it
again on a log that reached it some other way). An interval's cause is the tag of its `blocked` line: a later line
that re-tags an open or closed interval (a retag is a new line, never an edit, N29a-09) does not change the
attribution, and is reported `blocked-events-retagged` with the bead, both tags and both line identifiers (RC-36),
listed in the record and the exit record for the reviewer:

| Cause tag | Meaning | Qualifies for BET-12? |
|---|---|---|
| `sibling-primitive-missing` | A sibling library lacked a primitive the bead needs; the event names the sibling, the primitive and the upstream work item | Only if both conditions of step 3 hold |
| `sibling-defect` | A sibling primitive exists and is wrong (a foundation-defect admission) | No; recorded |
| `owner-input` | Waiting for an owner decision that had not reached its deadline | No |
| `compute` | Waiting for an approved compute envelope or a machine | No |
| `own-work` | The bead's own work or a dependency inside this repository | No |
| `other` | With a written reason | No |

Blocked intervals of beads on the critical chain to the M1 exit are merged (union of intervals, so that two beads
blocked on the same cause in the same week count that week once) to give B_q, the weeks blocked for qualifying
causes, and B_n, the weeks blocked for non-qualifying causes.

**3. Qualifying.** A `sibling-primitive-missing` interval qualifies only while (a) its upstream work item had not
landed in the sibling's repository, and (b) no interim posture of the upstream registry applied to it (the item
had no interim posture, or the posture's own preconditions were not met). A posture with no standing check (an
item the staleness report of N33b-08 lists as `posture: untested`) does not count as applying. Time blocked after
the item landed, or while a checked interim posture was available and not used, is `own-work`.

**4. Decision rule (DESIGN reading of "took more than 1.5 times their estimate because …"; to be ratified by the
owner before M1 ends).**

1. If T ≤ Θ: the gate is **met**. Record T, B_q, B_n.
2. If T > Θ and T − B_q ≤ Θ: the qualifying cause is the but-for cause of crossing the threshold. The gate is
   **not met**; BET-12 is falsified; the pause of step 5 applies.
3. If T > Θ and T − B_q > Θ: the milestones would have overrun without the sibling cause. The gate is **met with a
   recorded slip**: BET-12 is not falsified by this evidence, the slip and its causes are entered in the exit
   record, and the risk "breadth starves the spine" (RISK-33) is read with its signal marked fired, which requires
   one of the three outcomes of the exit protocol (a kill gate decided, an ADR changing a mechanism or scope, or a
   written reason).

**5. If not met.** The program pauses for a written re-plan before M2 starts. Mechanically: the register row takes
status `fail`; the exit report of M1 is `not-ready`; no bead of M2 or later may move to in-progress until the ADR
"re-plan after the schedule gate", `docs/adr/replan-after-schedule-gate.md` (DESIGN path), is committed. The pause
is enforced at two points, both this bead's code: (a) the pre-check `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
schedule -- may-start --bead <key> --json`, run by a session before it claims a bead, which exits 3 with the
reason "schedule gate: re-plan pending" and the row's path for a bead labelled `m2` or later while the row is
`fail` and the ADR is absent, and exits 0 otherwise; (b) the check `schedule-gate` attached to the exit runner
through `scripts/exit/checks.toml` (N40-05 section 5a: `owner = "N40-11"`, `step = 12`, `since = "M1"`, command
`scripts/exit/schedule-gate.sh`, which wraps `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example schedule -- check <Mn>
--root <dir> --json`): at M1 it returns the computation of steps 1 to 4 as its section and, on branch 2, the block
`schedule-gate.not-met`; at every later exit it returns the block `started-during-schedule-pause` for each bead of
M2 or later whose first move to in-progress in the tracker history falls after the `fail` record and before the
ADR's commit. The re-plan states: each missing primitive and its sibling; for each,
the chosen route (wait for the upstream item with a date; more upstream capacity for it; an interim posture now
written; the worker-process fallback; a lift of the minimal code with provenance headers and golden tests; a
change of scope; or a relaxation of the Franken-only rule, which only the owner can take and which is recorded as
an ADR, never as a quiet dependency); the effect on the kill gate KILL-FSIM; new planning estimates as
`[[amendment]]` records of the milestone registry (N40-06); and which M2 work packages change. After the ADR the
row is re-recorded as `killed` with `detail = "BET-12: written re-plan adopted; <ADR path>"` (printed "killed:
re-plan adopted"; for a program row the bet whose written consequence is the fallback stands where a kill gate
would, as N40-01's `killed-without-decision` rule reads it) and M2 may start under the re-plan.

**6. Record.** Register row `GATE-PROG-SCHEDULE` (DESIGN identifier; the register prints it as "the schedule gate
(BET-12)"), kind `program`, claim `known-answer` (it is arithmetic on recorded times), milestone M1. `measured`
holds T, B_q, B_n and the branch taken. Evidence: the milestone registry digest and the list of blocked intervals
with their log line identifiers.

**7. Code.** `crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/schedule.rs`: `attribute(logs, upstream) -> Attribution`,
`decide(T, B_q, Θ) -> Outcome`, `pause_findings(register, history, adr_dir) -> Vec<Finding>`. Pure; called by the
example `crates/frob-conformance/examples/schedule.rs` (subcommands `check` and `may-start`, declared by this bead
in the crate's `CONTRACT.md` row of examples, BD-025's rule that the crate has no default binary), which the
exit runner reaches through the attached check of section 5. This bead adds the `schedule-gate` record to
`scripts/exit/checks.toml` in its own change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] T, B_q and B_n are computed from the logs and the milestone registry with no hand-entered number.
- [ ] The three branches of the rule return the stated outcomes on the fixtures of the test plan.
- [ ] A `sibling-primitive-missing` interval whose upstream item had landed, or whose interim posture applied, does
      not count toward B_q.
- [ ] Overlapping blocked intervals on one cause are counted once.
- [ ] On `not met`, the M1 exit report is `not-ready` and the attached check returns `schedule-gate.not-met`;
      `schedule -- may-start` exits 3 for an M2 bead until the re-plan ADR is committed and 0 after it; at a later
      exit, an M2 bead whose first move to in-progress fell inside the pause is returned as
      `started-during-schedule-pause`.
- [ ] A typed `started` or `exited` of M0 that the tracker history does not support stops the computation with
      `exit-time-unsupported` (RC-21); an estimate of M0 or M1 changed with no `[[amendment]]` record stops it with
      `estimate-raised-silently` (RC-37); a retag does not change the attribution and is listed as
      `blocked-events-retagged` (RC-36).
- [ ] The decision rule of step 4 is ratified by the owner, or its default (as written) is recorded as an ADR,
      before the M1 exit review runs.
- [ ] The row is recorded by the fresh-context session of the M1 exit review, with a caught negative control.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance exit::schedule::` (synthetic histories).

- `threshold_is_ten_and_a_half`: E = 7, Θ = 21/2.
- `on_time_met`: T = 7.0, B_q = 0 → met.
- `at_threshold_met`: T = 10.5 exactly → met ("more than" is strict).
- `sibling_cause_not_met`: T = 11.5, B_q = 3.0 (item not landed, no posture) → T − B_q = 8.5 ≤ 10.5 → not met.
- `overrun_without_sibling_met_with_slip`: T = 13.0, B_q = 1.0 → 12.0 > 10.5 → met with recorded slip; RISK-33
  signal fired.
- `posture_available_does_not_qualify`: the same history as `sibling_cause_not_met` but an interim posture applied
  → B_q = 0 → branch 3 → met with recorded slip.
- `landed_item_stops_the_clock`: item landed after 1.0 week of a 3.0-week block → B_q = 1.0.
- `union_of_intervals`: two beads blocked on one primitive over the same two weeks → B_q = 2.0, not 4.0.
- `untagged_block_rejected`: a `blocked` event with no cause tag, or with the tag `upstream` (outside the closed
  set) → the attribution refuses to run and names the bead.
- `untested_posture_does_not_apply`: the history of `sibling_cause_not_met` with an interim posture whose item is
  listed `posture: untested` → B_q = 3.0 → not met.
- `retag_does_not_move_time` (RC-36): the history of `sibling_cause_not_met` with a later line re-tagging the
  3.0-week interval `own-work` → B_q = 3.0 still, one `blocked-events-retagged` finding naming both lines.
- `estimate_raised_silently` (RC-37): M1's `estimate_weeks` 5 at the commit of M0's `started` and 6 at HEAD with
  no amendment → refused `estimate-raised-silently`; with an `[[amendment]]` dated after the start → E = 7 still.
- `typed_start_refused` (RC-21): `started` of M0 two days later than the first `in_progress` commit of an M0 bead
  in the tracker history → refused `exit-time-unsupported` naming `started`.
- `pause_findings`: a `fail` row, no ADR, an M2 bead first `in_progress` inside the pause →
  `started-during-schedule-pause`; the same bead after the ADR's commit → no finding; `may-start` on an `m1` bead
  → admitted.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/schedule_gate.sh`: builds three scratch repositories with synthetic bead logs and upstream registries
reproducing the three branches, each with a tracker history (`.beads/issues.jsonl` commits) and a
`scripts/exit/checks.toml` holding the `schedule-gate` record; runs `scripts/exit/schedule-gate.sh M1 --root
<scratch> --json` in each; asserts the outcome and the recorded `measured` string, and for branch 2 the block
`schedule-gate.not-met`, that `scripts/exit/review.sh M1` exits non-zero, and that `schedule -- may-start` on an
M2 bead exits 3; then commits `docs/adr/replan-after-schedule-gate.md`, asserts the row records `killed` with the
BET-12 detail and `may-start` exits 0; then plants RC-21, RC-36 and RC-37 in turn and asserts each reason.
Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/schedule_gate/<utc>.ndjson`: per step `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, T, B_q, B_n and Θ as `metric` lines with units
(weeks), the branch, the digests of the inputs, the evidence block `{claim: known-answer, class: Tested, tier:
G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`, and on failure the first differing interval and its log path. The `summary`
line counts assertions and names the schedule gate.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 2 + 5 = 7; 1.5 × 7 = 10.5; the outcomes of the nine fixtures of N40-KA (R-16, R-24) and of
`untested_posture_does_not_apply`. Negative controls
(N40-CX): RC-36, a history in which blocked events were retagged from `sibling-primitive-missing` to `own-work`
after the fact (caught because log lines are append-only and the retag is a new line, not an edit); RC-37, an
estimate edited upward in the milestone registry with no dated amendment; RC-21, an exit time typed by hand.
Determinism: a pure function of the logs and registries.

## Considerations

- The rule depends on honest cause tags at the time of blocking. The exit review samples blocked intervals and
  reads the surrounding log; a fresh-context reviewer has no stake in the tags.
- The per-bead log lands in M1 (N29a-09). A bead of M0 worked before it existed has no `blocked` lines, so its
  blocked time is in neither B_q nor B_n; the record lists such beads of the critical chain as `unlogged` with
  their count, so the reviewer sees how much of T the attribution could not read.
- A dirty or moving sibling is a different risk (the kill gate KILL-FSIM reads upstream breakage above 20% of
  working days in any four-week window); days lost to breakage are `sibling-defect`, not `primitive-missing`.
- OPEN (owner): ratify the but-for reading of step 4. Fail-closed default: as written, which fires the pause in
  the case the bet names and records every other overrun.
- The gate is evaluated once, at the end of M1. It is not re-run at later exits; later overruns are read through
  the risk register.
