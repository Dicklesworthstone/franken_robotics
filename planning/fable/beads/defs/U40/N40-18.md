## Background

**The ceiling is computed before any speed test.** Before a speed comparison is locked, the largest ratio it could
possibly show is computed from the work that both arms share or that the mechanism does not touch, and the test is
refused if that ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show. The rule comes from a recorded mistake (FACT, a
paper-scale pilot recorded in the program's review log): a specialized solver was compared with a composed one and measured 1.345 times
faster warm and 1.088 cold; the faster arm's batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch
(parsing, assembly, checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost could have
reached about 1.46 at most (842.8 ÷ 575.7). The experiment could not have confirmed the claim it was run to test,
and nobody had computed that beforehand.

GATE-TENFOLD-09 makes the rule program-wide and recurring. It is one of the four gates re-run at every milestone
exit after its first. It has two clauses: every work package funded as a speed lever cites a removable share of at
least 10% on a `ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline; and every registered speed comparison records its
ceiling and is refused when the ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show. If it is not met, the speed
comparison is refused and the work package loses its speed justification (kill gate KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER). Claim
kind: Performance. Decision of record: ADR-GAUNT-05, ceiling first.

## Scope

In: the two clauses as a check over the experiment registry and the work-package justifications; the refusal; the
recurring record at every exit; the kill rule's application. Out: the ceilings' arithmetic library and the rival's
ledger (N40-13, seed BD-143); the subsystem-level copy of the check for mission queries (seed BD-354).

## Technical approach

**1. What a registered speed comparison must carry (before it runs).** `claimed_ratio`: the ratio it sets out to
show. `baseline_ledger`: the identifier of a `ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline (exclusive phases sum to
the clock within 2%). And one of: `p` (the share of the baseline's restricted time the mechanism removes entirely),
giving the ceiling 1/(1 − p); or `(f, r, h)` (the share improved, the improvement factor, the added overhead, all
measured on the baseline's critical path), giving the ceiling 1/(1 − f + f/r + h). The other ceilings apply where
relevant: unremovable floor u gives R ≤ RMT_baseline ÷ u; missed-deadline share q gives R ≤ 1/q; the horizon gives
R ≤ H ÷ RMT_ours.

**2. Clause A: work packages.** For every work package whose recorded justification is `speed-lever`: a removable
share p ≥ 0.10 on a `ledger-closed` run of the strong baseline (the rival's ledger at M1; our own profile as well
from M2). Below 0.10 its whole effect is at most 1/0.9 = 1.11: the justification is changed to `trust`, `coverage`
or `correctness` with that reason written and no speed figure attached, or the package is not funded
(KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER; applied on the rival's ledger at M1 and re-read on ours at M2).

**3. Clause B: comparisons.** For every registered speed comparison: ceiling C computed from its recorded
quantities. If C < `claimed_ratio`, the comparison is **refused** before it runs (`ceiling-below-claim`), with the
ceiling printed. A comparison with no baseline ledger, or with a `ledger-open` baseline, is refused
(`no-closed-baseline`). **The order rule**: a pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work the
pruning already removed; in a product of levers each factor's quantities are measured on the time the earlier
factors left, and a registration that violates this is refused (`factor-on-removed-work`).

**4. The check** `tenfold09(registry, packages, ledgers) -> Verdict` in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/ceiling.rs`. The gate passes at an exit when: (i) no work package is
`speed-lever` with p < 0.10 or with no cited closed ledger; (ii) every speed comparison registered since the last
exit carries its ceiling; (iii) every comparison whose ceiling was below its claim was refused (none ran); (iv) the
refusal is live, shown by the negative control below being refused at this commit.

**5. Record.** Register row GATE-TENFOLD-09, kind `program`, claim `performance`, `recurring = true`, first part at
M1 and one part at every later exit (M2 to M8). `measured`: counts of packages checked, comparisons checked,
refused, and the smallest margin C − `claimed_ratio` among those that ran.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every `speed-lever` work package cites p ≥ 0.10 on a closed ledger; any below is re-justified without a
      speed figure or unfunded, with the kill decision recorded.
- [ ] Every registered speed comparison has its ceiling recorded before its first run receipt.
- [ ] A comparison with ceiling below its claimed ratio is refused before it runs, at every exit's commit.
- [ ] The recorded pilot's case is reproduced as a known answer: residual 575.7, total 842.8, ceiling 1.46 (to two
      places), claimed ratio above 1.46 → refused.
- [ ] The gate has a fresh part at each exit from M1 to M8, each recorded by the exit review's session.
- [ ] A speed figure in any report whose comparison is not in the registry is refused by the report generator.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::ceiling::`.

- `pilot_ceiling`: 842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.4640 (four places); claimed 2.0 → refused; claimed 1.3 → allowed.
- `remove_phase`: p = 0.10 → 1.111; p = 0.90 → 10.
- `improve_part`: (f, r, h) = (0.6, 3, 0.1) → 1/(0.4 + 0.2 + 0.1) = 1.4286.
- `lever_floor`: package with p = 0.099 → fails clause A; p = 0.100 → passes.
- `open_baseline_refused`: baseline ledger with 3% unattributed → `no-closed-baseline`.
- `order_rule`: second factor measured on the full ledger → `factor-on-removed-work`.
- `ceiling_before_first_receipt`: a comparison whose ceiling record is later than its first run receipt → fails
  clause B.
- `recurring_parts`: the row has parts at M1…M8.
- `unregistered_figure_refused`: a report line with a speed ratio and no registry entry → refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_09.sh`: registers three synthetic comparisons against a recorded closed ledger (one with
ceiling above its claim, one below, one with no baseline), attempts to run each through the conformance target,
and asserts one run and two refusals with their reasons; marks a synthetic work package `speed-lever` with p = 0.06
and asserts the check fails and names it; regenerates the exit report and asserts the gate's part for the current
milestone. Logs: command, exit code, wall time, per comparison the ceiling beside the claimed ratio, per package p
beside 0.10, the ledger closure residual beside 2%, and on failure the first offending entry with its path.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 1.46 from 842.8 and 575.7 with measured 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold; 1/0.9 = 1.11. Negative controls
(kept in the control registry and run at every exit): a comparison claiming 2.0 with a ceiling of 1.46 (must be
refused); a ceiling computed from the candidate's own ledger instead of the strong baseline's (must be refused); a
`speed-lever` package at p = 0.06 (must fail clause A).

## Considerations

- The gate judges whether a test *could* show its claim, not whether the mechanism is good. A mechanism refused as
  a speed lever may be the most valuable thing in its package for trust or coverage.
- "The strong baseline" is the strongest composed incumbent, warm; measuring the ceiling on a weak baseline
  inflates it.
- From M2 the check runs on two ledgers (the rival's and ours); a lever must clear 10% on the ledger that funds it.
