## Background

Before a sealed suite is opened, the rules by which its numbers will be turned into a claim are fixed: what is
estimated, which procedure is invalid, and how many independent mission families a claim about a *population* of
missions needs. GATE-TENFOLD-10 is the program gate that checks the **pre-analysis** implements those rules, by
making it reproduce a small set of hand-checkable numbers and by showing that a tempting invalid procedure (the
percentile bootstrap) would have certified a false ten-fold claim most of the time. If the gate is not met, the
suite is not opened and no population wording is used. It is due at M3, before the partial gate. Claim kind: Known
answer.

The risk it answers is specific (it is in the risk register): a rare slow mission is absent from the sealed sample,
and a resampling interval certifies a ratio the population does not have. Resampling cannot show a tail that the
sample does not contain.

## Scope

In: the known answers; the simulation with the rare-tail candidate; the refusal of an undersized population claim;
the two result types; the gate record. Out: the estimator and the guard for the sealed cells (the ten-fold
chapter's beads); the reachability conditions per cell (checked under GATE-TENFOLD-04, bead N40-14).

## Technical approach

**1. Two estimands, never interchanged.** With T the time to an accepted answer and H the deadline:
the restricted-time ratio R_time = Σᵢ min(T_rival,i, H) ÷ Σᵢ min(T_frob,i, H); the accepted-output rate ratio
R_rate = (accepted_frob ÷ makespan_frob) ÷ (accepted_rival ÷ makespan_rival). The word "ten-fold" attaches to
R_time only; a statement about decisions per hour cites R_rate. With parallel jobs the makespan is the measured
campaign makespan under matched resource caps. Each arm's independent acceptance is timestamped before the joint
reveal.

*Known answer 1.* 25 serial missions with H = 100 s; the rival takes 50 s on each (1,250 s, 25 accepted); the
candidate finishes 24 in 1 s each and times out once (124 s, 24 accepted). R_time = 1,250/124 = **10.081**;
R_rate = (24/124)/(25/1,250) = **9.677**.

**2. The unit of inference is the independent mission family.** Missions that share a family, an originating site
or a material batch form one cluster; 24 rows from one site are one site replicate. Connected groups stay together
across the development and sealed splits. The resampling unit, H and the cluster structure are registered with the
suite.

**3. A procedure that is invalid here.** *Known answer 2.* H = 1,000 s; the rival always takes 20 s; the candidate
takes 1 s on 99% of families and 999 s on 1%. Both always complete. Population ratio 20/(0.99·1 + 0.01·999) =
20/10.98 = **1.8215**. A suite of 24 families contains only fast missions with probability 0.99²⁴ = **0.78568**,
and then every percentile-bootstrap resample reports 20.

**4. The valid test.** For the claim R_time ≥ 10 form the bounded paired contrast
Xᵢ = min(T_rival,i, H) − 10·min(T_frob,i, H), which lies in [−10·H, H]; the claim is E[X] ≥ 0. For n independent
families a distribution-free one-sided lower bound is X̄ − 11·H·√(ln(1/α)/(2n)) (the range of X is 11·H). The
claim is made only when a registered valid bound is nonnegative.

**5. How many families a population claim needs** (DESIGN, derived, hand-checkable). If a fraction p of families
has X = −10·H and the rest have X = H, then E[X] = H·(1 − 11p), negative for p > 1/11, and a suite of n families
shows no slow family with probability (1 − p)ⁿ. A procedure with one-sided error α that reports a nonnegative
bound on the best possible sample must therefore have (10/11)ⁿ ≤ α, so n ≥ ln(1/α)/ln(1.1): **32** families at
α = 0.05, **46** at α = 0.0125 (four strata under a Bonferroni split), **49** at α = 0.01. The bound of step 4
needs n ≥ 121·ln(1/α)/2, which is **182** at α = 0.05, even on that best sample. With 24 families, zero slow
missions is compatible at the 5% level with a slow-family rate of 1 − 0.05^(1/24) = 11.7%, and 11 × 0.117 exceeds 1.

**6. Consequence: two result types, declared before sealing.** A *population result* has at least the family count
that its registered bound requires, and never fewer than ln(1/α)/ln(1.1). A *finite-suite result* states the ratio
on the sealed missions as an exact fact about that suite, with its completion table and its phase ledger, and
carries no population wording. Completion and useful coverage are tested as paired differences against
non-inferiority margins frozen with the suite. A rare binary failure is reported with an interval valid at zero
counts (0 of n gives the upper bound 1 − α^(1/n)); a bootstrap of n zeros has zero width and is not used.

**7. The verb.** `frob eval preanalyze` accepts a ten-fold study plan (n, cluster structure, H, α, result type)
and prints the reachable verdicts. It refuses a population result whose n is below the necessary count (refusal:
suite too small for a population claim).

**8. The gate.** The pre-analysis reproduces 10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568 and the counts 32, 46, 49 and 182. In
2,000 simulated campaigns of 24 families with the rare-tail candidate of step 3, the contrast test certifies
ten-fold in **none**, and the percentile bootstrap, run as the negative control, certifies it in **78.6% ± 3
points**. Check: `tenfold10()` in `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/preanalysis.rs`, run as
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test tenfold -- preanalysis`. Record: register row GATE-TENFOLD-10, kind
`program`, claim `known-answer`, milestone M3; `measured` holds the eight numbers and the two campaign shares.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The four ratios and probabilities are reproduced to the printed digits (10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568) and
      the four counts exactly (32, 46, 49, 182).
- [ ] Over 2,000 seeded rare-tail campaigns of 24 families the contrast test certifies ten-fold in zero; the
      percentile bootstrap certifies it in 78.6% ± 3 points.
- [ ] `frob eval preanalyze` refuses a population result with n = 31 at α = 0.05 and accepts n = 32 as reaching
      the necessary count (while printing that the registered Hoeffding bound needs 182).
- [ ] A plan with 24 families may declare only a finite-suite result; its report carries no population wording.
- [ ] A bootstrap interval of n zeros is never emitted; the zero-count bound 1 − α^(1/n) is.
- [ ] The row is recorded before the partial gate's suite is opened; `open` refuses without it.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::preanalysis::` (exact rational arithmetic where the inputs are rational).

- `r_time_and_r_rate`: 1250/124 and (24/124)/(25/1250) → 10.0806…, 9.6774….
- `rare_tail_population_ratio`: 20/10.98 → 1.82149….
- `all_fast_probability`: 0.99²⁴ → 0.785678….
- `necessary_counts`: ⌈ln(1/α)/ln(1.1)⌉ at α = 0.05, 0.0125, 0.01 → 32, 46, 49.
- `hoeffding_count`: ⌈121·ln(20)/2⌉ → 182.
- `zero_count_bound`: 1 − 0.05^(1/24) → 0.1174; 11 × 0.1174 > 1.
- `contrast_range`: X ∈ [−10·H, H] for all T in [0, ∞) under the cap.
- `population_refusal`: n = 31, α = 0.05, population → refused; finite-suite → accepted.
- `cluster_is_unit`: 24 missions from one site are one replicate (n = 1).

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_10.sh`: runs `frob eval preanalyze --json` on four study plans (24 finite-suite; 31
population; 32 population; 46 population at four cells) and asserts the reachable-verdict tables and the one
refusal; runs the 2,000-campaign simulation from a fixed seed and asserts the two shares. Logs: command, exit
code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block (claim Known answer, class, determinism, seed), each number beside
its expected value, the certify counts beside 0 and 1,572 ± 60 of 2,000, and on failure the first differing value.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the eight numbers above (the ten-fold chapter's registry numbers them). Negative control: the
percentile bootstrap itself, which must *certify* in 78.6% ± 3 points of the rare-tail campaigns (a control that
shows the invalid procedure fails as predicted); a pre-analysis that accepts a 24-family population claim (must be
refused). Metamorphic: scaling every time and H by one factor changes no ratio and no count. Determinism: the
simulation is seeded and bit-reproducible on one machine and across platforms.

## Considerations

- 182 families per cell is far above what the compute envelope allows; the practical consequence is that most
  cells declare finite-suite results, and a population result needs a tighter registered bound with its own
  necessary count, never below 32.
- The gate's simulation is cheap (CPU seconds); it runs at every exit thereafter as part of the harness's known
  answers.
- The detector of the corresponding risk is exercised by a planted instance (the rare-tail arm), so the signal is
  known to fire.
