## Background

Before a sealed suite is opened, the rules by which its numbers will be turned into a claim are fixed: what is
estimated, which procedure is invalid, and how many independent mission families a claim about a *population* of
missions needs. GATE-TENFOLD-10 is the program gate that checks the **pre-analysis** implements those rules, by
making it reproduce a small set of hand-checkable numbers and by showing that a tempting invalid procedure (the
percentile bootstrap) would have certified a false ten-fold claim most of the time. If the gate is not met, the
suite is not opened and no population wording is used. It is due at M3, before the partial gate. Claim kind: Known
answer.

The risk it answers is specific (it is in the risk register): a rare slow mission is absent from the sealed sample,
and a resampling interval certifies a ratio the population does not have. Resampling cannot show a tail that the
sample does not contain.

## Technical approach

**1. Two estimands, never interchanged.** With T the time to an accepted answer and H the deadline:
the restricted-time ratio R_time = Σᵢ min(T_rival,i, H) ÷ Σᵢ min(T_frob,i, H); the accepted-output rate ratio
R_rate = (accepted_frob ÷ makespan_frob) ÷ (accepted_rival ÷ makespan_rival). The word "ten-fold" attaches to
R_time only; a statement about decisions per hour cites R_rate. With parallel jobs the makespan is the measured
campaign makespan under matched resource caps. Each arm's independent acceptance is timestamped before the joint
reveal.

*Known answer 1.* 25 serial missions with H = 100 s; the rival takes 50 s on each (1,250 s, 25 accepted); the
candidate finishes 24 in 1 s each and times out once (124 s, 24 accepted). R_time = 1,250/124 = **10.081**;
R_rate = (24/124)/(25/1,250) = **9.677**.

**2. The unit of inference is the independent mission family.** Missions that share a family, an originating site
or a material batch form one cluster; 24 rows from one site are one site replicate. Connected groups stay together
across the development and sealed splits. The resampling unit, H and the cluster structure are registered with the
suite.

**3. A procedure that is invalid here.** *Known answer 2.* H = 1,000 s; the rival always takes 20 s; the candidate
takes 1 s on 99% of families and 999 s on 1%. Both always complete. Population ratio 20/(0.99·1 + 0.01·999) =
20/10.98 = **1.8215**. A suite of 24 families contains only fast missions with probability 0.99²⁴ = **0.78568**,
and then every percentile-bootstrap resample reports 20.

**4. The valid test.** For the claim R_time ≥ 10 form the bounded paired contrast
Xᵢ = min(T_rival,i, H) − 10·min(T_frob,i, H), which lies in [−10·H, H]; the claim is E[X] ≥ 0. For n independent
families a distribution-free one-sided lower bound is X̄ − 11·H·√(ln(1/α)/(2n)) (the range of X is 11·H). The
claim is made only when a registered valid bound is nonnegative.

**5. How many families a population claim needs** (DESIGN, derived, hand-checkable). If a fraction p of families
has X = −10·H and the rest have X = H, then E[X] = H·(1 − 11p), negative for p > 1/11, and a suite of n families
shows no slow family with probability (1 − p)ⁿ. A procedure with one-sided error α that reports a nonnegative
bound on the best possible sample must therefore have (10/11)ⁿ ≤ α, so n ≥ ln(1/α)/ln(1.1): **32** families at
α = 0.05, **46** at α = 0.0125 (four strata under a Bonferroni split), **49** at α = 0.01. The bound of step 4
needs n ≥ 121·ln(1/α)/2, which is **182** at α = 0.05, even on that best sample. With 24 families, zero slow
missions is compatible at the 5% level with a slow-family rate of 1 − 0.05^(1/24) = 11.7%, and 11 × 0.117 exceeds 1.

**6. Consequence: two result types, declared before sealing.** A *population result* has at least the family count
that its registered bound requires, and never fewer than ln(1/α)/ln(1.1). A *finite-suite result* states the ratio
on the sealed missions as an exact fact about that suite, with its completion table and its phase ledger, and
carries no population wording. Completion and useful coverage are tested as paired differences against
non-inferiority margins frozen with the suite. A rare binary failure is reported with an interval valid at zero
counts (0 of n gives the upper bound 1 − α^(1/n)); a bootstrap of n zeros has zero width and is not used.

**7. The verb.** `frob eval preanalyze` (N39b-34) accepts a ten-fold study plan (n, cluster structure, H, α,
result type) and prints the reachable verdicts. It refuses a population result whose n is below the count the
claim needs (refusal: suite too small for a population claim; slug `suite-too-small-for-population-claim`, exit
3). The count the claim needs is the larger of the necessary count of step 5 and the count its registered bound
requires (step 6: "at least the family count that its registered bound requires, and never fewer than
ln(1/α)/ln(1.1)"). Under the one registered bound, that of step 4, a population plan at α = 0.05 is therefore
refused at 31 families and at 32 (where the necessary count is reached and the bound's 182 is not) and admitted
at 182.

**8. The gate.** The pre-analysis reproduces 10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568 and the counts 32, 46, 49 and 182. In
2,000 simulated campaigns of 24 families with the rare-tail candidate of step 3, the contrast test certifies
ten-fold in **none**, and the percentile bootstrap, run as the negative control, certifies it in **78.6% ± 3
points** (a count in [1,512, 1,632] of 2,000; the expected count is 2,000 × 0.78568 = 1,571).

**9. Measurement, evaluation and record.** The measurement is N39b-36: its script `scripts/e2e/tenfold_preanalysis.sh`
and its module `tenfold_estimation` of the known-answer tier, run as `FROB_TEST_SEED=392010 cargo test -p
frob-conformance --test g1_known_answers --features gauntlet-mutations -- GATE-TENFOLD-10` (rows TF-17 to TF-20 of
N39-KA), drive the real verb and scorer, run the simulation and write a receipt (the eight observed values as exact
strings, the two campaign counts with n and seed, three plants with `caught`, commit, fingerprint, log digest). This
bead adds, in `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/preanalysis.rs` (DESIGN):

- `EXPECTED`: the gate's table of the eight values, held as exact rationals or integers (625/62, 300/31,
  2000/1098, (99/100)²⁴, 32, 46, 49, 182) with the printed digits each is compared at.
- `tenfold10(receipt) -> GateRow`: `pass` iff every observed value equals its expected value at the printed
  digits (the four counts exactly), the contrast count is 0 of 2,000, the bootstrap count lies in
  [1,512, 1,632], all three plants are `caught` in the same run, and the receipt's commit is the commit being
  recorded. Otherwise `fail`, with the first differing value. A receipt that lacks a field gives `incomplete`,
  never `pass`.

Record: register row GATE-TENFOLD-10, kind `program`, claim `known-answer`, milestone M3; `measured` holds the eight
numbers and the two campaign shares; the row cites the receipt's digest and the hash of the partial gate's suite it
precedes, so that the pre-opening check of N40-14 finds a pass for that suite. The row has one writer (N38b-19): the
run of `tenfold10()` writes its verdict as the gate's receipt (the gate line `pass` or `fail`, the `measured` string,
the digest of N39b-36's receipt it evaluated, the suite hash, and the three plants with `caught`), the receipt is
committed byte-identical at `receipts/gates/<hash>.json`, and a fresh-context session that did not implement the
pre-analysis enters the row from it with `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate
GATE-TENFOLD-10 --receipt <hash>`; `incomplete` records `fail`. The measurement is re-run at every later exit as part
of the harness's known answers; a later receipt that evaluates to `fail` reopens the row and blocks any opening that
has not yet happened.

DESIGN (one measurement): an earlier draft of this bead had its own script `scripts/e2e/tenfold_10.sh` with four
plans and a second 2,000-campaign simulation, and its own test target. Two simulations of one gate could differ
in seed, generator or certification rule and so give two counts for one row; the plans and the simulation are
therefore specified once, in N39b-36, and the checks this bead had beyond that script (the 31 and 32 family
boundary, the absence of population wording on a finite-suite report, the zero-count bound in place of a
zero-width interval, the scaling relation) were moved there.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The row's `measured` block, taken from the receipt of N39b-36, shows the four ratios and probabilities
      reproduced to the printed digits (10.081, 9.677, 1.8215, 0.78568) and the four counts exactly (32, 46, 49,
      182).
- [ ] It shows that over 2,000 seeded rare-tail campaigns of 24 families the contrast test certified ten-fold in
      zero and the percentile bootstrap certified it in 78.6% ± 3 points (a count in [1,512, 1,632]).
- [ ] The receipt evaluated shows the boundary plans decided as the verb specifies: a population result at
      α = 0.05 refused with n = 31 and with n = 32 (the necessary count reached, the registered Hoeffding
      bound's 182 printed as unmet) and admitted at n = 182; a plan with 24 families declaring only a
      finite-suite result, with no population wording in its report; the zero-count bound 1 − α^(1/n) emitted
      and no bootstrap interval of n zeros. (These assertions are made by the script of N39b-36; a receipt in
      which any of them failed evaluates to `fail`.)
- [ ] `tenfold10()` returns `pass` only for a complete receipt with every value matching, both counts in range
      and all three plants caught; each of the planted receipts of the test plan gives `fail` or `incomplete`.
- [ ] The row is recorded before the partial gate's suite is opened, by a session that did not implement the
      pre-analysis; `open` refuses without it (the refusal is `may_open` of N40-14, which reads this row).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::preanalysis::` (exact rational arithmetic where the inputs are rational).
The first seven check the gate's table of expected values by hand formulas, independently of the product verb;
the rest check the evaluator on real receipts and on planted ones.

- `r_time_and_r_rate`: 1250/124 and (24/124)/(25/1250) → 10.0806…, 9.6774….
- `rare_tail_population_ratio`: 20/10.98 → 1.82149….
- `all_fast_probability`: 0.99²⁴ → 0.785678….
- `necessary_counts`: ⌈ln(1/α)/ln(1.1)⌉ at α = 0.05, 0.0125, 0.01 → 32, 46, 49.
- `hoeffding_count`: ⌈121·ln(20)/2⌉ → 182.
- `zero_count_bound`: 1 − 0.05^(1/24) → 0.1174; 11 × 0.1174 > 1.
- `contrast_range`: X ∈ [−10·H, H] for all T in [0, ∞) under the cap.
- `count_band`: 78.6% ± 3 points of 2,000 is [1,512, 1,632] and contains 1,571.
- `evaluates_real_receipt`: the receipt written by `tenfold_preanalysis.sh` on this commit evaluates to `pass`.
- `fails_on_one_differing_value`: the same receipt with 9.677 replaced by 10.081 (the rate printed as the time
  ratio) → `fail`, naming R_rate.
- `fails_when_contrast_certifies`: contrast count 1 of 2,000 → `fail`.
- `fails_outside_count_band`: bootstrap counts 1,511 and 1,633 → `fail`; 1,512 and 1,632 → `pass`.
- `fails_when_a_plant_was_missed`: one plant with `caught: false` → `fail`.
- `incomplete_when_field_missing`: a receipt without the seed, or without one of the eight values → `incomplete`.
- `stale_commit_refused`: a receipt from another commit → not `pass`.
- The checks `population_refusal` (n = 31, α = 0.05, population → refused; finite-suite → accepted) and
  `cluster_is_unit` (24 missions from one site are one replicate, n = 1) of this bead's earlier draft run the
  real verb and are now steps `refusal` and `counts` of N39b-36's script and its test `boundary_31_32_182`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_10.sh` (the record script; it sources the helper of `STD-E2E` and writes
`target/e2e-logs/tenfold_10/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript): (1) runs `scripts/e2e/tenfold_preanalysis.sh` of N39b-36
unchanged and takes its receipt (the plans, 24 finite-suite, 31, 32 and 182 population, 46 at four cells, and the
2,000-campaign simulation are that script's; none is repeated here); (2) evaluates the receipt with `tenfold10()`
through the conformance driver, which writes the verdict receipt, and asserts `pass`; (3) offers that receipt to
`record_gate` against a scratch copy of the gate register (never the real one) and asserts that the row parses with
kind `program`, claim `known-answer`, milestone M3, the eight numbers and the two shares; (4) evaluates three planted
receipts (one value changed; a bootstrap count of 1,633; one plant not caught) and asserts `fail` for each. Logs:
command, exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block copied from the measurement's log (claim Known answer,
class, determinism, seed), each number beside its expected value, the certify counts beside 0 and [1,512, 1,632] of
2,000, the row as written, and on failure the first differing value. The `summary` names `gates: ["GATE-TENFOLD-10"]`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the eight numbers above (N39-KA TF-17 to TF-19; the simulation's counts are TF-20). Negative controls
of the gate, measured by N39b-36 and required `caught` by the evaluator: the percentile bootstrap itself, which must
*certify* in 78.6% ± 3 points of the rare-tail campaigns (a control that shows the invalid procedure fails as
predicted); a pre-analysis that accepts a 24-family population claim (must be refused). Negative controls of this
bead: the planted receipts of the unit tests (a changed value, a count outside the band, a missed plant, a missing
field, a stale commit). The metamorphic relation (scaling every time and H by one factor changes no ratio and no
count) and the determinism lanes of the simulation (seeded, bit-reproducible on one machine and across platforms) are
asserted by N39b-36; the evaluator is a pure function of the receipt and gives the same row twice on one machine and
across platforms.

## Considerations

- 182 families per cell is far above what the compute envelope allows; the practical consequence is that most
  cells declare finite-suite results, and a population result needs a tighter registered bound with its own
  necessary count, never below 32.
- The gate's simulation is cheap (CPU seconds); it runs at every exit thereafter as part of the harness's known
  answers (N39b-36's script), and each run's receipt is evaluated again by `tenfold10()`.
- The detector of the corresponding risk is exercised by a planted instance (the rare-tail arm), so the signal is
  known to fire.
