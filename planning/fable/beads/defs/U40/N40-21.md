## Background

A sealed suite can be honest in protocol and still be tilted in content: every mission might suit one arm's
strengths, or be solvable by anyone, or have answers that a checker would wave through. **Suite hygiene** is the
set of properties that make the suite fair to the rival and hard on the candidate. GATE-TENFOLD-12 is the program
gate that checks them at the tournament: every stratum holds at least one routine easy win for conventional
methods and one answer that is genuinely unresolved; the checker rejects 100% of the planted unit, contact, clock
and passive-load mutations; and in the amendment sequences an arm keeps its scoped claim only with zero missed
withdrawals, with unnecessary invalidations reported per arm. If the gate is not met for a stratum, **that stratum
is `descriptive`**: its numbers are published and nothing is cited as passing or failing the ten-fold bet. Claim
kind: Known answer. Due at M7.

Why each property. An *easy win* shows the rival is allowed to win where it should. An *unresolved answer* shows
that a truthful "unknown" is rewarded over a confident wrong answer. *Planted mutations* show the checker is
looking: a rollout and a solver that share one mistranslated model agree on the wrong answer, so independently
derived mutations are planted and must be rejected. *Missed withdrawals* matter because the claim includes correct
reuse under amendment: an answer that should have been withdrawn after a change, and was not, is a false claim.

## Scope

In: the hygiene properties as checks over the sealed suite and the run record; the per-stratum consequence; the
record. Out: authoring the suite (seed BD-428); the checker and the revalidation engine (their chapters); the
pre-opening gates (N40-14).

## Technical approach

**1. The six sealed mission types, what the verifier changes privately, and the strongest control.**

| Sealed mission | Private changes | Strongest control |
|---|---|---|
| Adjustable stance under static load | Foot authority, a force floor, package dimensions, asymmetric payload, a missing foot, passive-spring compatibility | A lifted robust linear program; conventional projection; a force-law switch |
| Split-grip landing and the last usable observation | Mirror the strip; hide or delay the surface cue; shift the payload; change the contact order | Belief-state reachability; equal-budget reinforcement learning; geometric control |
| Gripping unfamiliar compliant stock | Geometry; an early-law alias; contact coupling | An expert probe; direct measurement at mission dwell; active system identification |
| Changing an accepted carrier or walker | Replace a gearbox, a wheel or a lower limb; perturb latency, friction or energy reserve | Retraining; a shared conditioned actor; warm-started control; ordinary graph caching |
| Recommissioning a facility | Move a door or a counter; perturb scan uncertainty; reduce a shared resource; alter a shift | Recomputation; incremental mixed-integer programming; standard motion planning; selective replay with cached bounds |
| A heterogeneous high-throughput family | Mix fixed and changed topology; grazing contacts; dense depth sensors; pressure to spill to the host | An optimized batched incumbent; this engine with family sharing switched off |

**2. Properties checked.**

1. *Easy win and unresolved answer.* Every stratum's sealed set contains at least one case tagged, before sealing
   and by the adjudicator, `routine: conventional method suffices`, and at least one tagged `unresolved: no arm is
   expected to decide it`. After the reveal the tags are checked against outcomes: the easy win was solved by the
   rival arm; the unresolved case received no accepted verdict from any arm, and an arm that answered it with a
   truthful `unknown` lost coverage only.
2. *External worlds.* Some worlds in each design stratum come from an evaluator independent of the proposing
   engine; the count per stratum is recorded.
3. *Planted mutations.* Independently derived mutations of units, contact, causal clocks and passive load are
   planted among the answers submitted to the checker. The checker rejects 100% of them. One accepted mutation
   fails the property for the stratum.
4. *Amendments in warm campaigns.* Warm campaigns use predeclared amendment sequences; one-time authoring is
   amortized over 1, 10 and 100 amendments; failed amendments stay in the sequence; the consequential edit is
   concealed; missed withdrawals and unnecessary invalidations are both counted. An arm keeps its scoped claim only
   with **zero missed withdrawals**; unnecessary invalidations are reported per arm.
5. *The second claim's acceptance* (a new engineering operation: a typed continuous design family, realistic
   uncertainty, a causal controller contract and sealed changes). An accepted answer has (i) a machine-checkable
   inner or outer answer with explicit scope, (ii) a physically interpretable witness or a scope-preserving
   ambiguity report, and (iii) correct reuse or withdrawal after an amendment. Each mission predeclares its
   mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations, a largest unresolved band or optimality gap, and any
   mandatory distinguishing experiment. Strict and closed endpoints are part of acceptance, and a vacuous enclosure
   fails. The rival's answer goes through the same checker. A tie retires the claim of exclusive expressivity on
   that fixture, and can still justify shipping the simpler implementation behind the same interface.

**3. The check** `tenfold12(suite, record) -> Map<Stratum, Verdict>` in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/hygiene.rs`: per stratum, properties 1 to 4 (and 5 on family strata).
Register row GATE-TENFOLD-12, kind `program`, claim `known-answer`, milestone M7; `measured`: per stratum the
counts (easy wins, unresolved, external worlds, mutations planted and rejected, missed withdrawals and unnecessary
invalidations per arm).

**4. Consequence.** A stratum that fails any property is `descriptive` in the tournament report. The gate's row is
`pass` when every stratum run is either clean or marked `descriptive` with the failing property named: the
consequence, like the outcome rules, is applied, not negotiated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every stratum run has at least one pre-tagged easy win and one pre-tagged unresolved case, with tags
      committed before sealing.
- [ ] The checker rejected every planted unit, contact, clock and passive-load mutation (100%).
- [ ] For every arm keeping a scoped claim on a warm stratum, missed withdrawals are zero; unnecessary
      invalidations are reported per arm.
- [ ] Every stratum failing a property is printed `descriptive` with the property named, and no verdict of the
      ten-fold bet is cited for it.
- [ ] The count of externally authored worlds per design stratum is in the report.
- [ ] The amortization rows at 1, 10 and 100 amendments are present for every warm cell, with failed amendments
      kept in the sequence.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::hygiene::`.

- `needs_easy_win_and_unresolved`: a stratum with no `routine` case → `descriptive`; with no `unresolved` case →
  `descriptive`; with both → clean.
- `tags_before_sealing`: a tag committed after the suite hash → refused.
- `mutation_rejection_is_total`: 40 planted, 40 rejected → clean; 39 rejected → the stratum fails.
- `missed_withdrawal_forfeits`: one missed withdrawal for arm A in a 10-amendment sequence → A loses its scoped
  claim on that stratum; unnecessary invalidations do not forfeit and are counted.
- `failed_amendments_stay`: a sequence with a failed amendment removed → rejected.
- `vacuous_enclosure_fails`: an outer answer equal to the whole parameter box with no exclusion → not accepted.
- `truthful_unknown_costs_coverage_only`: an `unknown` on the unresolved case → no disqualification, coverage
  reduced.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_12.sh`: on the synthetic tournament of `tenfold_07.sh`, tags cases before sealing, plants
eight mutations (two of each kind) into the answer stream, runs an amendment sequence of 10 with one concealed
consequential edit, and runs the check; then removes the easy win from one stratum and asserts it is printed
`descriptive`. Logs: command, exit code, wall time, per stratum the six counts, the mutation identifiers with
their rejection codes, and on failure the first accepted mutation or missed withdrawal with its artifact path.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: mutation rejection 100%; missed withdrawals 0. Negative controls: a checker build that accepts a
unit-mutated answer (must be caught by the gate); a suite with every mission favouring the candidate (no easy win:
`descriptive`); an arm that skips revalidation and keeps a stale verdict (a missed withdrawal: forfeits).

## Considerations

- The tags "easy win" and "unresolved" are the adjudicator's, made before sealing; an arm's advocates may contest
  them only before the suite hash is committed.
- A stratum made `descriptive` here is enlarged and re-run at a later gate on fresh families.
- `facility` is run only if its Moonshot gate passed; otherwise it is `not run`, which is not `descriptive`.
