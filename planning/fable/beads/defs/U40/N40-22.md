## Background

One ratio cannot answer the two questions the ten-fold test is asked: *how far is this system ahead of the
incumbent pipeline as it is commonly run*, and *how much of that lead would an incumbent engine recover once every
portable method is layered over it*. The second question decides whether the program needs its own engine at all
(bet BET-14) or should ship its methods as a layer over someone else's. Every stratum is therefore run with **three
arms** on the same sealed missions, acceptance protocol, hardware and resource caps, and the **recovery ratio**
says what share of the saving the composed incumbent recovers. Separately, three **phase ablations** attribute a
gain to information, to controller acquisition or to stepping, and must respect bounds computed from the baseline
trace; an ablation that exceeds its bound is an accounting error.

GATE-TENFOLD-13 is the program gate that puts these on record: at M3 with the partial gate, and at M7 on the sealed
suite. Like the other ten-fold gates it is met by the record, whatever the values. Its consequence is about
funding: if it is not met, **no engine-internal work package beyond M2 is funded**, because without the recovery
ratio nobody knows whether engine work is what buys the gain. Claim kinds: Design comparison and Performance; the
governing part, the recovery ratio between arms, is a Design comparison.

## Scope

In: the definitions; the decision rules; the ablation bounds; the one-receipt rule for arm W; the completeness
check; the funding consequence. Out: building the arms and running them (seeds BD-315, BD-317, BD-430, BD-431);
the pooled ratio and its verdict (N40-15, N40-16).

## Technical approach

**1. The three arms.**

| Arm | What it is | Frozen by |
|---|---|---|
| B, the declared incumbent | A leading incumbent simulator with per-design reinforcement learning and a standard outer-loop optimizer | Its own advocates, on development fixtures |
| W, the composed incumbent | The same engine or engines with every portable method layered on: native state restore and forks, per-topology buckets or the engine's own heterogeneous solver, a sampling controller or a multi-embodiment prior with checkpoint resume, racing and stopping, importance-weighted failure search, a second engine as referee | A competent baseline engineer, on development fixtures |
| F, this system | The release under test | The release |

**2. The recovery ratio.** With T_X the restricted mean time of arm X on a stratum and C_X its GPU-hours:

    r_T = (T_B − T_W) / (T_B − T_F)        r_C = (C_B − C_W) / (C_B − C_F)

defined when F beats B on that measure. A value of 1 means the layer recovers the whole saving and the engine adds
nothing on that stratum; 0 means the layer recovers nothing. Known answer: if T_F = 0.10·T_B, then r_T ≥ 0.8
exactly when T_W ≤ 0.28·T_B. Three cases are fixed before any measurement: if T_F ≥ T_B the stratum is reported as
"no saving to recover" and r is undefined; if T_W < T_F then r exceeds 1 and the stratum is a loss to the composed
incumbent; a mission unresolved at the deadline H costs H in every arm. Each ratio is published with a 95%
interval (paired over mission families).

**3. Decision rules (TARGET thresholds, registered before the sealed suite opens).**

- r_T ≥ 0.8 and r_C ≥ 0.8 on a stratum: a thin layer over an incumbent is enough there. The report says so and the
  fallback of BET-14 applies to that stratum (KILL-TENFOLD-TIE: ship the layer over that engine).
- Both below 0.5: the engine is a source of the gain on that stratum.
- Otherwise the stratum is reported as mixed, with the phase the layer could not recover named from the ledger.
- The word "ten-fold" is governed by F against the stronger of B and W, never by F against B alone.

**4. One receipt first.** FACT (from the incumbent documents read at planning time): run-to-run determinism,
heterogeneous worlds and the fastest solver sit on different solver paths, so each is available and their
conjunction is not shown. Arm W is therefore built as **one pipeline**, on one fixed-topology brief and one
mixed-topology contact-rich brief, **before F exists**, and the property that binds when all are demanded at once
is recorded. One expensive incompatibility can sink the layer; several rarely exercised gaps may leave it
competitive. Feature counts decide neither.

**5. Phase ablations.** On two briefs (one narrow wheeled, one contact-rich legged), on arm B's engine, three
single-change replays run against the traced baseline:

1. *Information only.* Batch proposals and a sound cheap diagnostic filter; the same controller acquisition and
   the same solver.
2. *Controller acquisition only.* The same candidates, with per-design training replaced by a qualified cheaper
   controller: a prior with fine-tuning, inheritance, or a delivered sampling controller.
3. *Stepping only.* The same candidates and controllers on a faster solver at matched fidelity.

Each returns its own ratio and the share of the baseline's critical path that it touched. Two bounds from the
baseline trace: if training that an intervention does not remove is a share s of elapsed time, that intervention's
ratio is at most 1/s (1.43 at s = 0.7); if an intervention removes that share entirely its ratio is at most
1/(1 − s) (3.33 at s = 0.7). Illustration: 50 trained designs at 35 minutes each with 1.5 minutes of agent time
per design make training 1,750 of 1,825 minutes, 95.9%, and cap information alone at 1.04; with controller
acquisition at 45 s per design, agent time is 75 of 112.5 minutes, two thirds, and information becomes the larger
lever. Which regime a stratum is in is a measurement, not a premise. **A ratio above its 1/s or 1/(1 − s) bound
voids the stratum as an accounting error.**

**6. The verbs.** `frob report campaign --arms B,W,F` prints T, C, r_T and r_C with intervals;
`frob report campaign --ablate {information, controller, stepping}` prints an ablation with its bound.

**7. The check** `tenfold13(record) -> Verdict` in `crates/frob-conformance/src/tenfold/recovery.rs`: r_T and r_C
with 95% intervals for every stratum that has a ratio (or the fixed case label); the three ablations on both
briefs, each with its touched share and its bound, each at or below its bound; the one-receipt record for arm W
with its binding property, dated before any engine-internal work package beyond M2 was funded. Register row
GATE-TENFOLD-13, kind `program`, claim `design-comparison`, parts at M3 and at M7 (the sealed suite).

**8. Consequence if not met.** No engine-internal work package beyond M2 is funded: the exit runner's pre-check
refuses to start a bead of such a package while the row has no M3 record.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] r_T and r_C are published with 95% intervals for every stratum that has a ratio, whatever their values; the
      three fixed cases are labelled where they apply.
- [ ] The three ablations are run on both briefs; each is at or below its bound; a planted ratio above its bound
      voids the stratum as an accounting error.
- [ ] The one-receipt pipeline for arm W exists on both briefs and its binding property is recorded before any
      engine-internal work package beyond M2 is funded.
- [ ] The decision rule is applied per stratum and recorded as an ADR; a stratum with both ratios at or above 0.8
      ships the layer.
- [ ] `frob report campaign --arms B,W,F --json` and the three `--ablate` forms return the recorded numbers with
      their evidence blocks.
- [ ] The M3 part is recorded with the partial gate and the M7 part on the sealed suite.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance tenfold::recovery::`.

- `recovery_definition`: T_B = 10, T_W = 2.8, T_F = 1 → r_T = 7.2/9 = 0.8 exactly; T_W = 2.9 → 0.7889.
- `no_saving_case`: T_F = 11, T_B = 10 → "no saving to recover", r undefined (not negative, not infinite).
- `loss_case`: T_W = 0.5, T_F = 1, T_B = 10 → r_T = 9.5/9 > 1, labelled a loss to the composed incumbent.
- `unresolved_costs_h`: an unresolved mission contributes H to T_B, T_W and T_F alike.
- `decision_rule`: (0.85, 0.82) → layer suffices; (0.3, 0.4) → engine is a source; (0.85, 0.4) → mixed.
- `ablation_bounds`: s = 0.7 → 1/s = 1.4286 and 1/(1 − s) = 3.3333; s = 1750/1825 → 1/s = 1.0429.
- `ablation_above_bound_voids`: information-only ratio 1.2 with s = 0.959 (bound 1.043) → stratum void.
- `two_thirds_regime`: agent time 75 of 112.5 minutes → 0.6667.
- `word_against_stronger_rival`: F against B = 12, F against W = 6 → the governing ratio is 6.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/tenfold_13.sh`: on the synthetic three-arm record of `tenfold_06.sh`, runs
`frob report campaign --arms B,W,F --json` and the three ablation reports, compares with the scorer's recomputation
from ledgers, and runs the check; then inflates one ablation above its bound and asserts the void. Logs: command,
exit code, wall time, the envelope's evidence block, T and C per arm with units, r_T and r_C with intervals beside
0.8 and 0.5, each ablation's ratio beside its bound and touched share, and on failure the first differing value
with artifact paths.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 0.28·T_B at T_F = 0.10·T_B; 1.43 and 3.33 at s = 0.7; 95.9% and 1.04; two thirds. Negative
controls: an ablation above its bound (must void); a recovery ratio computed with B's unresolved missions dropped
instead of costed at H (must be refused); arm W assembled from three separate pipelines whose receipts are
combined (must be refused: one receipt).

## Considerations

- Arm W is the most expensive arm to build honestly and the one most tempting to under-build. Its freeze is by a
  baseline engineer, not by this system's implementers, and the audit gate (N40-20) covers it.
- A recovery ratio near 1 is a product decision, not a defeat: the layer ships, and engine work continues only for
  engine-internal gaps (a mixed-topology batch with run-to-run replay, and the Metal tier, are the two documented).
- Intervals on ratios of differences are wide at 24 families; the report prints them and does not round a mixed
  stratum into either camp.
