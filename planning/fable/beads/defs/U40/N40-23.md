## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet: a trigger and a fallback, named before the measurement, decided at a
named milestone and recorded as an ADR. The **fallback ledger** is the register's list of every kill gate, once,
with its trigger, its fallback, the milestone that decides it and its owner. The chapter units write one decision
bead per kill gate (the bead that takes the measurement and records the decision); this ledger is the independent
copy those beads are checked against (cross-check bead N40-30), so that a trigger cannot drift between the chapter
that hopes the mechanism survives and the register that governs.

This bead carries the first of four groups: the foundations and the mathematical spine. It includes the gate that
could change the architecture (KILL-FSIM: can the physics sibling be consumed by path at all) and is therefore an
M0 bead. Reading the columns: **Decided** is the milestone at whose exit the decision is recorded as an ADR; for a
gate whose trigger is a pilot, it is the earliest milestone at which the work package can be funded. **Owner** is
the subsection that governs the trigger and the fallback; a second subsection in parentheses is a consumer that
states the same gate from its side. A kill gate that fires is not a failure of the program: the fallback is the
plan.

## Scope

One record per kill gate, written once, and for these thirteen it is written here.

In: the thirteen rows as `kind = "kill"` records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03), each with
`trigger`, `fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, the owner in `section`, `consumers` and the gates or pilots its trigger
reads (`reads`); `decision` stays empty until the decision bead records it. Also in: one row the plan's table
does not hold, KILL-SINEW-SEA, entered by an `introduce` amendment (step 1a).

Out, and which bead has it:

- Taking any measurement or decision: the decision bead of each kill gate, written by the unit that owns its
  subsection.
- The other groups of the ledger: N40-24 to N40-29. The fifth Skeleton kill gate, KILL-SKEL-CELL, is a late one
  and its record is N40-29's.
- The chapter's side of the four Skeleton rows: N14b-26, the Skeleton chapter's register bead. It keeps the map
  from each Skeleton kill gate to its decision bead and checks that each has this ledger's record and exactly
  one owner declaration. It creates no kill record. Its table of triggers and fallbacks is the chapter's
  statement, which this ledger governs; it depends on this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::foundations::`: `thirteen_rows`; `text_equals_seed` (each row's
trigger and fallback hash equals the seeded hash); `decided_by_exit` (the counts of step 2);
`fsim_is_rolling`; `skel_port_follows_treeop` (synthetic: KILL-TREEOP `fired` → KILL-SKEL-PORT due and its
fallback "no limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path" expected);
`gate_fail_is_not_kill_fire` (GATE-LEMMA-01 at 65% → gate `fail`, KILL-LEMMA trigger not met);
`sea_row_introduced` (KILL-SINEW-SEA with its `introduce` amendment loads, is `pilot-before-funding` at M6 and
leaves `thirteen_rows` at thirteen seeded rows; the row without the amendment → `changed-without-amendment`; the
amendment dated after a receipt of the pilot → `amended-after-measurement`).

### End-to-end script and logging

At this bead's close: `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <this group's seed list>` (the live mode of
N40-03's script; the seed list is the thirteen identifiers with the hash of each seeded trigger and fallback text,
the list `text_equals_seed` reads, and KILL-SINEW-SEA with the hash of its `introduce` text), then `cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::foundations::`, then
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml` (N40-01) on the
committed register. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/kill_ledger_foundations/<utc>.ndjson`: per
step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block `{claim:
known-answer, class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`, per identifier the seeded hash, the mode
and the `decided` exit, the count per exit beside step 2, and on failure the first differing row; the `summary`
line counts assertions. Later, `scripts/e2e/kill_ledger.sh` (bead N40-30) loads all ledger groups, prints the
decisions due per exit and checks every decision bead's declaration against these rows.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 13 rows; the thresholds two weeks, 20%, 50%, 10⁻², 20%, 1.5 times and 20% as seeded. Negative
controls (N40-CX): RC-10 (a trigger lowered after its measurement), RC-11 (a fallback rewritten with no
amendment), RC-38 (a kill gate present in a chapter declaration and absent from the ledger).

## Considerations

- The gap between a gate's threshold and its kill trigger (KILL-LEMMA: 80% against 50%; KILL-MARROW-GUARD: 10⁻³
  against 10⁻²) is deliberate: inside the gap the capability is partial, not killed, and the README says partial.
- KILL-FSIM is the only kill gate whose fallback (b) is very expensive: a lift from a closure of about 1.5 million
  lines (FACT, measured in the sibling). The M0 stop rule re-plans before M1 if both INT-FSIM-01 and the worker
  process fail.
- "One repair cycle" is the bounded attempt defined in N40-03.
- N14b-26 prints the four Skeleton triggers and fallbacks with the identifiers spelled out (PO-SKEL-04 as the
  limb-port-packet proof obligation, INV-SKEL-04 as the two-view invariant, A-14 as sensor coverage). The
  numbers, conditions and decision points are the same as the rows above (compared when the two beads were
  reconciled). The text an owner declaration carries, and the text N40-30 compares, is this ledger's.
