## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet: a trigger and a fallback, named before the measurement, decided at a
named milestone and recorded as an ADR. The **fallback ledger** is the register's list of every kill gate, once;
the chapter units' decision beads are checked against it (N40-30).

This bead carries the second part of the register's third table: the 28 kill gates of mission queries and inverse
design (Augur, with its synthesis lanes), adversarial worlds (Nemesis), counterfactual debugging (Autopsy) and the
studio loop (Atelier). It contains the one kill gate that **stands from M1**: KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT, under which a
single certificate accepted by the independent auditor and shown false within its scope suspends its proof kind.
Because that gate is live from the first mission slice, this group is entered at M1 with a priority one step above
its milestone's default. Reading the columns: **Decided** is the milestone at whose exit the decision is recorded
as an ADR; "standing" means the rule is evaluated at every exit and whenever its event occurs. **Owner** governs
trigger and fallback; a subsection in parentheses is a consumer.

## Scope

One record per kill gate, written once, and for these twenty-eight it is written here.

In: the twenty-eight rows as kill records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03), each with `trigger`,
`fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, owner, consumers and `reads`, with the notes of step 3; the amendment that adds
one trigger to KILL-AUGUR-PROBE; the `scope_kind` of KILL-AUGUR-CUTS and of KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT and the `reread`
of KILL-AUGUR-REGION; two
rows the plan's table does not hold, entered by `introduce` amendments (step 3a).

Out, and which bead has it:

- Measurements and decisions: the decision bead of each kill gate, written by the unit that owns its subsection.
- The other groups of the ledger: N40-23 to N40-25, N40-27 to N40-29.
- The chapters' side of these rows, where a chapter has a register bead of its own: N25d-41 (Augur and
  synthesis: the eighteen rows from KILL-AUGUR-TIE to KILL-SYNTH-CONT), N26b-18 (Nemesis: KILL-NEMESIS,
  KILL-RARE, KILL-MCW) and N28c-25 (Atelier: KILL-STATIC, KILL-RANK, KILL-LADDER, KILL-VOI, KILL-REUSE,
  KILL-ATLAS). Each keeps the map from its chapter's kill gates to their decision beads and checks that each has
  this ledger's record and exactly one owner declaration; each depends on this bead. None creates a kill record.
  N16c-05 (Kinesis) states KILL-RANK as a consumer.

**3. Notes for the decisions.**

- **KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT (standing, from M1).** Trigger: one certificate accepted by `frob-verify` is shown false
  within its scope. Procedure: the proof kind is suspended at once; every cell certified by it falls to `Tested`;
  the cause is decided as *rule* or *implementation defect*. If the rule is at fault, the proof kind is removed and
  its cells stay `Tested`; if an implementation defect, it is fixed, the counterexample becomes a permanent
  negative control, and the kind is restored only after the mutation corpus passes again. In the ten-fold test the
  same event disqualifies the arm from the scoped claim (KILL-TENFOLD-CERT, ledger N40-28). It applies to both
  arms of the partial gate. Decides part of BET-19. The row carries `scope_kind = "proofkind"` (N40-03's closed
  table), because the fallback acts per proof kind and two kinds can be decided at one exit with different causes:
  at each exit its decision bead (N25b-04) writes one entry per proof kind in scope, `scope = "proofkind:<kind>"`,
  as sections of `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT-<milestone>.md`; a report confirmed at an exit already decided
  adds an entry with `reading = "reopened"` for the kind concerned (ADR suffix `-reopened`). A rule fault fires
  the row for that kind alone.
- **KILL-AUGUR-TIE (M3; first reading at M1).** Read on the strata of the partial ten-fold gate that use mission
  queries: if a short robust program over a conventional solver reaches the same checked scope at comparable
  turnaround, ship that program behind the same verbs, schema and checker, retire any claim of exclusive
  expressivity on those families, and spend the research budget on the first mission the program cannot resolve.
  A first reading is taken at M1 on the docking slice: if a conventional scenario-tree program gives the better
  answer per engineering hour, that program ships behind the same verbs. An owner question bears on it: whether a
  quantifier-elimination or SMT engine is a production dependency (default: comparator only).
- **KILL-AUGUR-REGION (M3, read again at M4) and GATE-AUGUR-09.** The pilot on the 12-parameter box is run at M3,
  where this kill gate is decided (falsifier: more than 90% undecided in the best arm, or more than 95% of decided
  volume from one monotone bound); the gate itself is due at M4 with region certificates. Decides BET-20. The
  trigger's second clause, "GATE-AUGUR-09 fails after one repair cycle", can only be read when the gate's M4 part
  is measured, so the row carries `decided = "M3"` and `reread = ["M4"]` (N40-03, step 2): the M3 entry reads the
  pilot, and a second entry with `exit = "M4"` and the ADR `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-AUGUR-REGION-M4.md` reads the gate
  (written with the gate's record by N25b-11). Without `reread` the M4 entry would not load and would not be due.
- **KILL-AUGUR-REUSE (standing, from M3) and KILL-REUSE (M4).** Two gates over one revalidation engine.
  KILL-AUGUR-REUSE removes a single implication rule that inherited a verdict incorrectly and falls back to the
  dependency-keyed memo graph. KILL-REUSE is Atelier's: any unsafe reuse in GATE-ATEL-11, or grade 3 saving less
  than a fifth of the recheck cost grade 2 leaves, restricts reuse to grades 1 and 2. Decide BET-33.
- **KILL-RANK (M4).** Atelier's row governs: GATE-ATEL-07 fails for every morphology class tested. The Kinesis
  chapter's older row (top-weighted τ below 0.5 in more than half the strata for the reference classes; fallback:
  tier pairs promote individual results only) concerns agreement between physics tiers, which GATE-FID-04
  measures; its consequence, that no population is ordered across a tier pair whose τ is below 0.5, is the
  per-stratum rule of GATE-FID-04 and holds without a kill gate. When KILL-RANK fires the ten-fold arithmetic
  records the lost factor. Decides BET-17.
- **KILL-STATIC (M3)** decides BET-05; **KILL-NEMESIS and KILL-MCW (M3)** decide BET-22; **KILL-RARE (M3)**
  decides BET-32 (three alternative triggers: gain below 10 times at 10⁻⁵; coverage of any interval offered as a
  bound below 85%; a planted mode missed in more than 20% of repetitions); **KILL-AUTOPSY (M3)** decides BET-23.

## Considerations

- The chapter register beads named in Scope print their kill gates with triggers, fallbacks and decision points
  of their own. Those tables are the chapters' statements; they were compared with the rows above when the two
  sets of beads were reconciled, and they agree on every number, condition and decision point. Several are
  worded more fully or spell out an identifier that a row here names by section. The text an owner declaration
  carries, and the text N40-30 compares, is this ledger's.
- KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT is the program's hardest promise: zero accepted false certificates. Its trigger is a single
  event, so its seeded-fault campaigns (at least 200 mutated witnesses per proof kind; a mutation corpus of at
  least 60 mutated queries and certificates) are the standing evidence that the auditor is looking.
- A tie under KILL-AUGUR-TIE is a product outcome with a planned shape (the simpler program behind the same
  interface), not a withdrawal of the mission-query surface.
- Where a trigger compares against "a conventional tool at similar cost" (KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE,
  KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE), the comparator and the cost measure are registered before the measurement.
