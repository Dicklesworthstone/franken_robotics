## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet: a trigger and a fallback, named before the measurement, decided at a
named milestone and recorded as an ADR. The **fallback ledger** is the register's list of every kill gate, once;
the chapter units' decision beads are checked against it (N40-30).

This bead carries the fourth group: the 19 kill gates of the evidence record (Chronicle), calibration against
reality (Ground), manufacture (Foundry) and the agent surface (Tether). It is an M0 bead because it contains
**KILL-MCP**, one of the two kill gates decided at M0: whether the MCP server can live in the core build graph.
Most of the rest are the lane-by-lane fallbacks that let M5 exit whatever happens: every Ground and Foundry lane
has a smaller capability that ships under its own name. Reading the columns: **Decided** is the milestone at whose
exit the decision is recorded as an ADR. **Owner** governs trigger and fallback; a subsection in parentheses is a
consumer.

## Scope

One record per kill gate, written once, and for these nineteen it is written here.

In: the nineteen rows as kill records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03), each with `trigger`,
`fallback`, `decided`, `mode`, owner, consumers and `reads`, with the notes of step 3.

Out, and which bead has it:

- Measurements and decisions: the decision bead of each kill gate, written by the unit that owns its subsection.
- The other groups of the ledger: N40-23 to N40-26, N40-28, N40-29.
- The chapters' side of these rows, where a chapter has a register bead of its own: N30b-22 (Ground:
  KILL-GROUND, KILL-BAND, KILL-CLOCK, KILL-ASBUILT, KILL-KIT), N31b-12 (Foundry: KILL-TOPOPT, KILL-LOADS,
  KILL-FAB, KILL-TOLERANCE, KILL-PACKET, KILL-FIRMWARE, KILL-LIFECYCLE) and N32b-25 (Tether: KILL-MCP,
  KILL-SLOT, KILL-PREEMPT, KILL-MCPTASKS, KILL-SOLVE, KILL-TUI). Each keeps the map from its chapter's kill
  gates to their decision beads and checks that each has this ledger's record and exactly one owner
  declaration; each depends on this bead. None creates a kill record. N16c-05 (Kinesis) states KILL-DR and
  N21c-32 (Spark) states KILL-SLOT as consumers.

**3. Resolutions and notes that govern rows of this group.**

- **KILL-MCP, stated once.** The design of record is `frob-mcp` as a member of the core graph on the one toolchain
  T, running verbs in process. The trigger is that the admission gate INT-FMCP-01 cannot pass within the M0 budget
  after the two P0 upstream work items have been attempted: FD-FMCP-01 (the MCP library brought onto T in its own
  repository) and FD-ASUP-01 (one async-runtime version across the consumed siblings). The fallbacks are ordered:
  first, `frob-mcp` as its own workspace reaching the daemon `frobd` over a local socket in newline-delimited JSON;
  second, agents use the CLI, which is complete on its own. The decision is taken between M0 and M1 and re-read at
  M1 with the M1 verbs (so it is counted in the decisions of both exits).
- **KILL-DR.** Chronicle governs: ρ_ep below 0.7 on 200 paired addresses in every stratum tested, or GATE-CHRON-09
  coverage fails after one repair cycle. The Kinesis chapter's clause on effective sample size (below 30% of the
  audited count in more than a tenth of promotions) is not a trigger; it stays a recorded diagnostic of the
  correction. The two chapters' fallbacks describe one state from two sides: the tier correction is off and
  promotion returns to the paired label; segment mode remains an option inside that fallback.
- **KILL-SLOT.** Tether governs: any digest difference under co-tenancy, or the throughput (at most 5% loss at
  8,192 identical worlds) or step-time (99th percentile at most 1.10 of solo) limits of GATE-TETHER-09 fail after
  one optimization pass. Spark's row (any slot-invariance failure at GATE-SPARK-15, or 99th-percentile step time
  more than 10% above solo) is contained in it. The fallback is the same: `frobd --dispatch=per-job`.
- **KILL-LIFECYCLE** is placed at M6 with lifecycle compression (its chapter's row said M8), and **KILL-LOADS** at
  M5 with Foundry (its row said M4; its gate may be recorded at M4).
- **KILL-BAND** fires on either GATE-GROUND-06 or GATE-GROUND-07; **KILL-CLOCK** on either GATE-GROUND-02 or
  GATE-GROUND-05; **KILL-PACKET** on either GATE-FOUNDRY-09 or GATE-FOUNDRY-10 after one repair cycle;
  **KILL-TOPOPT** on GATE-FOUNDRY-05 (saving under 7%, no convergence, or over budget; 15% is the target and 7% the
  kill threshold). They decide bets BET-28 and BET-29.
- **KILL-SOLVE** has no bet of its own: if the ceiling of the one-call pipeline is under 1.4-fold, or agents with
  `solve.run` do no better than agents driving the verbs, the fallback is the prompts.
- When Ground's kill gates fire together, what remains is: admission, frozen predictions, direct measurements and
  time-free metrics, with bands withdrawn and `reality: no-claim` printed.

**4. Use.** Seed text for amendment replay (N40-03); due sets for the exit query (N40-04); the reference for the
cross-check (N40-30).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::record::`: `nineteen_rows`; `text_equals_seed`;
`decided_by_exit` (1, 5, 1, 10, 1, 1, with KILL-MCP also in the M1 due set); `mcp_fallback_order` (a decision
that adopts the CLI-only fallback must state why the socket fallback was not available);
`mcp_needs_both_upstream_attempts` (INT-FMCP-01 failed with FD-ASUP-01 not attempted → trigger not met);
`dr_ess_clause_is_diagnostic` (effective sample size at 20% with ρ_ep = 0.8 → not fired);
`slot_contains_sparks_row` (99th-percentile step time 1.12 of solo → fired under either wording);
`topopt_thresholds` (saving 6% → fired; 9% → gate short of its 15% target, kill not fired);
`either_gate_fires` (GATE-GROUND-07 fails alone → KILL-BAND fired).

### End-to-end script and logging

At this bead's close: `scripts/e2e/kill_gate_records.sh --live --ids <this group's seed list>` (the live mode of
N40-03's script; the seed list is the nineteen identifiers with the hash of each seeded trigger and fallback text,
the list `text_equals_seed` reads), then `cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::record::`, then
`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example validate_register -- --file registries/gates.toml` (N40-01) on the
committed register. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/kill_ledger_record/<utc>.ndjson`: per step
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block `{claim: known-answer,
class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`, per identifier the seeded hash, the mode and the
`decided` exit, the count per exit beside step 2, KILL-MCP's due exits M0 and M1, and on failure the first differing row; the
`summary` line counts assertions. Later, `scripts/e2e/kill_ledger.sh` (bead N40-30) loads all ledger groups,
prints the decisions due per exit and checks every decision bead's declaration against these rows.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 19 rows; thresholds 0.7 on 200 paired addresses (KILL-DR), one fifth of features
(KILL-TOLERANCE), 7% (KILL-TOPOPT), 1.4-fold (KILL-SOLVE), 5% and 1.10 (KILL-SLOT). Negative controls: RC-10,
RC-11, RC-38, RC-42 (KILL-MCP recorded as fired before either upstream work item was attempted), and RC-61
(KILL-MCP entered with `reread = []`; `decided_by_exit` catches it, KILL-MCP missing from the M1 due set).

## Considerations

- The chapter register beads named in Scope print their kill gates with triggers, fallbacks and decision points
  of their own. Those tables are the chapters' statements; they were compared with the rows above when the two
  sets of beads were reconciled, and they agree on every number, condition and decision point. Several are
  worded more fully or spell out an identifier that a row here names by section. The text an owner declaration
  carries, and the text N40-30 compares, is this ledger's.
- KILL-MCP's second fallback (CLI only) removes the MCP surface but no capability: the CLI is complete on its own,
  and every later gate that reads "through `frob-mcp` where exposed" then reads the CLI alone.
- If KILL-TUI fires there is no dashboard; `job.list` and `session.show` carry the same numbers.
- The Foundry kill gates reduce the build packet step by step; if all fire, the packet is a parts list, mass
  properties and STL, and it still carries its four flags, all false.
