## Background

The ten-fold test compares design-loop time with this system against the strongest composed incumbent on sealed
missions. Its results have consequences that are easy to soften after the number is known: a low ratio can be
explained away, a tie with a simpler program can be reframed, a false certificate can be called an outlier. So the
test has seven **kill gates** of its own, each a rule with its trigger and its consequence written before any
sealed run, and none is changed after one. Each outcome is recorded as an ADR in the milestone in which it occurs,
and a kill outcome is not re-tested on the same suite.

This bead is their **fallback ledger**: the register's copy, against which the ten-fold chapter's decision beads
are checked (N40-30). It is entered at M1 because the first of them, KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER, is applied to the rival's
measured ledger at M1.

## Scope

In: the seven rows as kill records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03) with full triggers, fallbacks and
decision points. Out: the measurements and the decisions (the ten-fold chapter's beads; the program-gate beads
N40-13 to N40-22 read these rows).

## Technical approach

**1. The rows (seeded text).**

| Kill gate | Trigger | Fallback, named in advance | Decided |
|---|---|---|---|
| KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED | On a confirmatory cell R̂ is below 3, or at the partial gate the futility monitor stops the cell | The speed claim for that class is withdrawn from the thesis, the README and every report; speed-only work packages for the class are re-planned; the product is described for that class by the capabilities that do not depend on speed, and by its measured ratio | M3 for the classes run; M7 |
| KILL-TENFOLD-TIE | On a stratum, arm C reaches the same accepted scope with a restricted mean time within a factor of 1.5 of arm A's; or the composed incumbent's recovery ratio is at least 0.8 on elapsed time and on compute (bet BET-14) | Say so; ship the layer over that engine for that stratum behind the same verbs, schemas and checker; keep engine work for the engine-internal gaps only (two documented: a mixed-topology batch with run-to-run replay, and the Metal tier; two unverified and checked before they are relied on: replay of one world outside its batch, and solver-internal certificates); spend the research budget on the first mission the program cannot resolve | M3; M7 |
| KILL-TENFOLD-CERT | One false certificate from an arm: a certificate that the arm submitted, that its declared checker accepted, and that the adjudicator's direct evaluation or any later audit shows false within its stated scope | That arm is disqualified from the scoped claim: every cell of the gate in which it used that proof kind has no ratio for that arm, and the cell's verdict is `disqualified`. For arm A the proof kind is suspended, its cells fall to `Tested`, and KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT decides whether the rule or its implementation was at fault | Standing, from M3 |
| KILL-TENFOLD-LEAK | Any leak from sealed confirmation worlds: any read, by an arm's agent, tools or developers, of sealed content before the gate closes (a mission card before its clock starts, a sealed world address, a per-world outcome, a probe point, another arm's transcript) | The affected cells are void for that arm and their families retired at once; if the extent cannot be bounded the whole gate is void and is re-run on a rotated suite. A leak is never repaired by excluding the affected runs and keeping the rest of the cell | Standing, from M3 |
| KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER | A mechanism's removable cost is under 10% of the measured loop | It is not funded as a speed lever (by the removal ceiling its whole effect is at most 1/0.9 = 1.11); it may be funded for trust, coverage or correctness with that reason written in its work package and no speed figure | M1 on the rival's ledger; M2 on ours |
| KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ | No neutral simulator passes adjudicator acceptance (GATE-TENFOLD-05) for a stratum | Cross-home adjudication, labelled; if neither home referee can run the other arm's answers, the stratum is `descriptive` | M3; M7 |
| KILL-TENFOLD-REACH | A cell's pre-analysis fails a reachability condition: P(`ten-fold`) at least 0.8 at a true ratio of 15; P(`withdrawn`) at most 0.05 at a true ratio of 5; the guard reachable at its adjusted level; the horizon cap H ÷ RMT_A on the development suite at least 10 | The cell is opened as `descriptive` and says so before any sealed run; it is enlarged for the next gate | M3; M7 |

**2. Modes.** KILL-TENFOLD-CERT and KILL-TENFOLD-LEAK: `standing` from M3 (evaluated at every exit and on the
event). KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER: two parts, M1 and M2, and thereafter through the recurring gate GATE-TENFOLD-09.
KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED, -TIE, -ADJ, -REACH: one decision per cell at M3 for the classes run, and again at M7.

**3. What also disqualifies, and what does not** (carried with KILL-TENFOLD-CERT and -LEAK). Also disqualifying: a
change to a frozen arm after its freeze; use of the adjudicating simulator in development or tuning; an edited
ledger or receipt. Not disqualifying: a truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate, and a certificate with a
narrower scope than was asked (they cost coverage and time); a breach of a resource cap (the run is censored at
the instant of the breach and costs H). A disqualification awards nothing to the other arm: a cell whose rival is
disqualified has no denominator, is reported as such, and is re-run at the next gate. Leak detection uses the
firewall audit, access logs, and canary values that the adjudicator plants in sealed worlds and that must never
appear in an arm's transcript.

**4. Fixed numbers.** The thresholds 3 and 10, the guard floor 5, the tie factor 1.5, the recovery threshold 0.8,
the lever floor 10% and the completion margin 0.05 are fixed when the first suite hash is committed. A change
needs a dated amendment before any measurement they govern (N40-03); none is changed after a sealed run.

**5. Use.** Seed text for amendment replay; due sets for the exit query (N40-04): KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER at M1 and M2;
all seven at M3 and M7; the two standing gates at every exit from M3. The cross-check (N40-30) compares each
decision bead with its row.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::tenfold::`: `seven_rows`; `text_equals_seed`; `standing_two`
(due at M3…M8); `lever_two_parts` (M1, M2); `per_cell_decisions` (two cells at M3 → two records each for SPEED,
TIE, ADJ, REACH); `speed_threshold` (R̂ = 2.99 → fired; 3.00 → not fired); `tie_either_clause` (RMT_C ÷ RMT_A = 1.5
→ fired; r_T = 0.8 and r_C = 0.8 → fired; r_T = 0.8 and r_C = 0.7 → not fired by the second clause);
`not_disqualifying` (a truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate, a cap breach → no disqualification).

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/kill_ledger.sh` (bead N40-30); for this group it logs the seven identifiers, seeded
hashes, modes and due exits, and on failure the first differing row. The behaviour of each rule on a synthetic
suite is exercised by `scripts/e2e/tenfold_06.sh` (N40-15) and `tenfold_preopen.sh` (N40-14).

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 7 rows; 1/0.9 = 1.11; the four reachability numbers 0.8 at 15, 0.05 at 5, cap 10; the tie factor
1.5 and recovery 0.8. Negative controls: a planted false certificate (the arm must be disqualified); a canary
value in a transcript (the cell must be void); a leak "repaired" by dropping the affected runs (must be refused);
a threshold changed after a sealed run (RC-10).

## Considerations

- KILL-TENFOLD-SPEED firing on every class is a planned state: speed work is no longer funded, and the product is
  positioned on replay, qualification, certificates and the evidence ledger.
- KILL-TENFOLD-CERT is the same event as KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT seen from the test; one ADR may record both, and it
  must name the proof kind, the cells and the cause.
- An unbounded leak voids the gate and needs a rotated suite, which costs a full re-run; the firewall gates of the
  qualification lane are what make this unlikely.
