## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet: a trigger and a fallback, named before the measurement and recorded
as an ADR when decided. The register's four kill tables were compiled at one point in time; subsections added to
the chapters afterwards define 25 more kill gates. They are binding under the same rules. What they lack is a row
in the compiled tables and, in most cases, a stated decision point. The register's rule for them: until the tables
are regenerated, each has its row in the subsection that defines it, which governs its trigger and fallback; and
**a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it**.

This bead is their **fallback ledger**: the register's copy of all 25, with the decision point the neighbour rule
gives each, so that the chapter units' decision beads can be checked against it (N40-30) and so that the exit
reviews find them in their due sets. Without it these are the kill gates most likely to be skipped at an exit,
because no compiled table lists them.

## Scope

In: the twenty-five rows as kill records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03), each with its trigger, its
fallback, its decision point and the gate beside it. Out: the measurements and decisions (chapter units); the late
convergence gates themselves (N40-10).

**3. Notes for the decisions.**

- **KILL-DEVORACLE (M1)** governs the standing of evidence, not a mechanism: of the first five pilots re-run on the
  CPU referee, two or more change verdict relative to their development-oracle run. When it fires,
  development-oracle results lose standing as funding evidence, pilots for work packages not yet funded wait for
  the CPU referee, and packages funded on a reversed pilot are re-decided. Its consequence reaches every later
  funding decision, which is why it is decided as soon as the referee exists.
- **KILL-EXPODOMAIN (M3)** is Arena's kill gate for the exposure-domain coupling of random streams. It is not
  KILL-EXPOSURE, which is Sensorium's (camera exposure integration, decided at M2). Its neighbour GATE-ARENA-14 has
  parts at M2 and M3; the decision is entered at M3, the exit at which the gate's last part is due (a DESIGN
  reading of the neighbour rule, recorded as an ADR).
- **KILL-COUPLING** has two clauses with different decision points: one violated transported bound that survives a
  repair cycle (decided with the finite part of GATE-AUGUR-25 at M4, and standing afterwards), and no skill on the
  tray fixture with a budget below its slack (decided with the tray part at M8).
- **Pilot-triggered rows** (KILL-ROWQUEUE, KILL-ANCESTOR, KILL-LIMBKERNEL, KILL-STEPADJOINT, KILL-FARFIELD,
  KILL-SOILLIFT, KILL-SHORTGRAD, KILL-STRIDESEQ, KILL-BUDGETCURVE) name "the pilot's falsifier" among their
  triggers; their mode is `pilot-before-funding`, and the pilot record (falsifier, computed ceiling, fallback,
  result) must exist before the work package is funded.
- **Fallbacks that point at an earlier design.** Several fallbacks restore a decision "as first written"
  (KILL-WEAKVAR: a constraint that reads more than six parameters receives no region certificate and is checked
  pointwise; KILL-ENSEMBLE: attribution in double precision only; KILL-MPCRECORD: the sampling controller for
  screening and fallback only). The ledger row states the restored rule in full so that the ADR need not cite
  another document.
- **KILL-ANCESTOR and KILL-TREEOP.** KILL-ANCESTOR's fallback leaves the tree route "only where KILL-TREEOP leaves
  it": its decision reads the decision of KILL-TREEOP (ledger N40-24), and if that gate has not been decided when
  KILL-ANCESTOR is, the ancestor path is the dense path everywhere.
- **GATE-TRAC-11** is a late gate with no late kill gate: it is the gate that KILL-RESPONSE (ledger N40-24) reads.
  **GATE-FOUNDRY-13** leaves KILL-PACKET (ledger N40-27) unchanged, and its fail-closed default without a reviewer
  is `review: no-claim`.

**4. Use.** Seed text for amendment replay (N40-03); due sets for the exit query (N40-04); the reference for the
cross-check (N40-30).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Twenty-five kill rows are in the register with trigger, fallback, decided, owner, mode and reads; each
      trigger and fallback equals its defining subsection's text in full (no abridged statement is entered).
- [ ] No row has an empty decision point; each equals step 2.
- [ ] KILL-EXPODOMAIN's owner is Arena's subsection; a row confusing it with KILL-EXPOSURE fails the load.
- [ ] KILL-COUPLING has two parts (M4, M8), one per clause.
- [ ] The nine pilot-triggered rows have mode `pilot-before-funding`.
- [ ] The due sets contain these rows as in step 2 (0, 1, 4, 6, 9, 0, 4, 0, 2 at M0 to M8).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::ledger::late::`: `twenty_five_rows`; `text_equals_defining_row`;
`decided_by_exit` (0, 1, 4, 6, 9, 0, 4, 0, 2; total 26); `neighbour_rule_applied` (each row's decision point equals
the milestone of its `beside` gate, with the two documented exceptions KILL-EXPODOMAIN and KILL-COUPLING);
`devoracle_threshold` (1 of 5 pilots reversed → not fired; 2 of 5 → fired); `ancestor_reads_treeop`;
`expodomain_is_not_exposure`.

### End-to-end script and logging

Covered by `scripts/e2e/kill_ledger.sh` (bead N40-30); for this group it logs the twenty-five identifiers, the
hash of each seeded text, the decision exits and the neighbour gates, and on failure the first differing row.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 25 rows, 26 decisions; thresholds 1.15 (KILL-ROWQUEUE), 0.5 of robot-time (KILL-INTERACT), 0.5 at
K = 8 (KILL-STEPADJOINT), half of the closures (KILL-LOOPELIM), 1.15, 1.10 and 20% (KILL-ANCESTOR), 80% with 16
archetypes (KILL-LIMBKERNEL), a ≥ 0.8 (KILL-FARFIELD), one fifth (KILL-VISIBILITY), 1% (KILL-SOILLIFT), 10% and
99% (KILL-MPCRECORD), 60% and 30% (KILL-FOIL), factor 2 and 2 points (KILL-ITEMBANK), factor 2 (KILL-EXPODOMAIN),
3 times and a tenth (KILL-AUGUR-BRACKET), 15% (KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR), 90% (KILL-WEAKVAR), 90%, half and 3
(KILL-ENSEMBLE), 30% and half (KILL-BUDGETCURVE), 5% and a quarter (KILL-FREEPERIOD), 90% and 0.75
(KILL-SKEL-CELL), two of five (KILL-DEVORACLE). Negative controls: RC-33 to RC-35 (N40-10), RC-38, and RC-43 (a
late kill gate left with no decision point and so absent from every exit's due set).

## Considerations

- These rows were written later and reviewed less than the compiled tables; a discrepancy between a row here and
  its defining subsection is resolved in favour of the defining subsection until the tables are regenerated, and is
  then an amendment.
- When the register is regenerated (N40-10), each of these rows moves into the table of its subsystem and this
  bead's group ceases to be separate; the ledger rows and their history are unchanged.
- Nine of the 25 are decided at M4, on top of the 30 of the compiled tables: M4's decision load is 39.
