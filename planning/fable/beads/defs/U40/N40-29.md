## Background

A **kill gate** is the written exit of a bet: a trigger and a fallback, named before the measurement and recorded
as an ADR when decided. The register's four kill tables were compiled at one point in time; subsections added to
the chapters afterwards define 25 more kill gates. They are binding under the same rules. What they lack is a row
in the compiled tables and, in most cases, a stated decision point. The register's rule for them: until the tables
are regenerated, each has its row in the subsection that defines it, which governs its trigger and fallback; and
**a kill gate with no milestone of its own is decided at the milestone of the gate beside it**.

This bead is their **fallback ledger**: the register's copy of all 25, with the decision point the neighbour rule
gives each, so that the chapter units' decision beads can be checked against it (N40-30) and so that the exit
reviews find them in their due sets. Without it these are the kill gates most likely to be skipped at an exit,
because no compiled table lists them.

## Scope

One record per kill gate, written once, and for these twenty-five it is written here.

In: the twenty-five rows as kill records in `registries/gates.toml` (format: N40-03), each with its trigger, its
fallback, its decision point (`decided`), `mode`, `reads` and the gate beside it; the `scope_kind` and `reread`
fields of the rows decided per clause or read again; the one amendment that adds the pilot clause to
KILL-LOOPELIM (step 3).

Out, and which bead has it:

- The measurements and decisions: the decision bead of each kill gate, written by the unit that owns its
  subsection.
- The late convergence gates themselves: N40-10, which also states the neighbour rule that fixes these rows'
  decision points.
- The chapters' side of these rows, where a chapter has a register bead of its own: N14b-26 (KILL-SKEL-CELL),
  N16c-05 (KILL-ROWQUEUE, KILL-INTERACT, KILL-STEPADJOINT, KILL-LOOPELIM, KILL-ANCESTOR), N19c-29
  (KILL-FARFIELD), N20b-21 (KILL-VISIBILITY), N22c-49 (KILL-SOILLIFT, KILL-MPCRECORD, KILL-FREEPERIOD,
  KILL-STRIDESEQ), N23b-16 (KILL-SHORTGRAD), N28c-25 (KILL-BUDGETCURVE), N32b-25 (KILL-REPLAYPACK) and N38b-23
  (KILL-DEVORACLE). Each keeps the map from its chapter's kill gates to their decision beads and checks that
  each has this ledger's record and exactly one owner declaration. None creates a kill record. The other nine
  late kill gates (Phylum, Arena, Augur and Autopsy) have no chapter register bead.

**3. Notes for the decisions.**

- **KILL-DEVORACLE (M1)** governs the standing of evidence, not a mechanism: of the first five pilots re-run on the
  CPU referee, two or more change verdict relative to their development-oracle run. When it fires,
  development-oracle results lose standing as funding evidence, pilots for work packages not yet funded wait for
  the CPU referee, and packages funded on a reversed pilot are re-decided. Its consequence reaches every later
  funding decision, which is why it is decided as soon as the referee exists.
- **KILL-EXPODOMAIN (M3)** is Arena's kill gate for the exposure-domain coupling of random streams. It is not
  KILL-EXPOSURE, which is Sensorium's (camera exposure integration, decided at M2). Its neighbour GATE-ARENA-14 has
  parts at M2 and M3; the decision is entered at M3, the exit at which the gate's last part is due (a DESIGN
  reading of the neighbour rule, recorded as an ADR).
- **KILL-COUPLING** has two clauses with different decision points: one violated transported bound that survives a
  repair cycle (decided with the finite part of GATE-AUGUR-25 at M4, and standing afterwards), and no skill on the
  tray fixture with a budget below its slack (decided with the tray part at M8). The row carries
  `scope_kind = "clause"` (N40-03's closed table of scope kinds; values `finite` and `tray`), `decided = "M4"` and
  `reread = ["M8"]`.
- **KILL-SYNTH-REGRESSOR** is two-step, and its row carries `scope_kind = "clause"` with the values `screening`
  and `comparison`, both decided at M4 (N25e-22 writes the two entries): the screening clause (more than 15% of
  designs infeasible on every library motion pass the mission in closed loop) withdraws the motion-count
  screening signal first and leaves the lane; only the comparison clause (the design-comparison part of
  GATE-AUGUR-29 fails) removes the lane and puts the fallback in force. Its fallback reads "the mass-placement
  program of §25.21 and A-24 as they stand": while the mass-placement constructions are unfunded (their pilot
  and the owner's ruling, N40-26 step 3), what stands is the task-wrench analysis A-24 and the mass-support
  predicate FROB-REF-106, and the fallback is those; the row's own words cover it and no amendment is needed.
- **KILL-SKEL-CELL** also has two clauses with different consequences (no automatic discard; the exclusion rule
  suspended), but one fallback text, and is decided whole (DESIGN): its decision bead N14b-31 writes one entry
  whose ADR has a section per clause and states which consequence is in force. A `clause` scope was considered
  and not taken, because the row's `fired` state has one fallback to print either way.
- **Pilot-triggered rows** (KILL-ROWQUEUE, KILL-ANCESTOR, KILL-LIMBKERNEL, KILL-STEPADJOINT, KILL-FARFIELD,
  KILL-SOILLIFT, KILL-SHORTGRAD, KILL-STRIDESEQ, KILL-BUDGETCURVE) name "the pilot's falsifier" among their
  triggers; their mode is `pilot-before-funding`, and the pilot record (falsifier, computed ceiling, fallback,
  result) must exist before the work package is funded. Their `reads` names the pilot's record in
  `crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` beside the gate, since `reads` lists the gates or pilots whose
  records the trigger reads: for example KILL-BUDGETCURVE reads `PILOT-BUDGETCURVE-01` (N28c-32, whose result at
  M3 is the funding decision) and GATE-ATEL-18 (its one decision entry is taken at M4, N28c-41).
- **KILL-LOOPELIM is a tenth pilot-triggered row, by amendment (DESIGN).** Its defining row names two triggers,
  both read on the built mechanism, yet four-bar closure elimination has a pilot (N16c-29) whose falsifier ends
  the mechanism before it is built, and that decision (N16cp3-02, point A) must be a decision of this gate. The
  seeded row above stays the defining subsection's text. One `[[amendment]]` record of field `trigger` (N40-03),
  dated before the pilot's protocol is frozen, adds the clause "or the pilot's falsifier holds", so that the
  current text, which N40-30 compares and both decision beads apply, has three clauses; the row's mode is
  `pilot-before-funding` and its `reads` names the pilot's record beside GATE-KIN-11. The amendment adds a way
  to stop and removes none.
- **Rows read again at a later exit (`reread`, N40-03 step 2).** KILL-LOOPELIM and KILL-LIMBKERNEL are decided
  at M2 on the archetypes and the corpus that exist then, and each has a clause that is read again at M3:
  KILL-LOOPELIM's matched share over the archetype library once all eighteen archetypes have landed (the rule
  of N16c-34: eliminated closures ÷ all closures of the archetypes present; below one half fires; ADR
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LOOPELIM-M3.md`), and KILL-LIMBKERNEL's coverage on the frozen design corpus (N17bp1-01; ADR
  `docs/adr/ADR-KILL-LIMBKERNEL-M3.md`). Both rows carry `reread = ["M3"]`; the M2 entries are never edited.
  These two re-read entries are due at M3 beside the six decisions of step 2 and are not among the 26.
- **Kill gates in none of the ledgers' seeded rows.** A kill gate that a pilot or an epic proposed after the
  tables were written enters the register by an `introduce` amendment (N40-03): KILL-RUT, KILL-TACTILE and
  KILL-TAXEL (N38a-37, at M4; decided at M6 by N38a-38, N38a-39 and N38ap3-01), KILL-SINEW-SEA (N40-23), the four
  Traction rows of N40-24 and the two Augur rows of N40-26. They are not among these 25; the generated ledger
  prints them after the seeded rows of their subsystem, and N40-30 schedules them.
- **Fallbacks that point at an earlier design.** Several fallbacks restore a decision "as first written"
  (KILL-WEAKVAR: a constraint that reads more than six parameters receives no region certificate and is checked
  pointwise; KILL-ENSEMBLE: attribution in double precision only; KILL-MPCRECORD: the sampling controller for
  screening and fallback only). The ledger row states the restored rule in full so that the ADR need not cite
  another document.
- **KILL-ANCESTOR and KILL-TREEOP.** KILL-ANCESTOR's fallback leaves the tree route "only where KILL-TREEOP leaves
  it": its decision reads the decision of KILL-TREEOP (ledger N40-24), and if that gate has not been decided when
  KILL-ANCESTOR is, the ancestor path is the dense path everywhere.
- **GATE-TRAC-11** is a late gate with no late kill gate: it is the gate that KILL-RESPONSE (ledger N40-24) reads.
  **GATE-FOUNDRY-13** leaves KILL-PACKET (ledger N40-27) unchanged, and its fail-closed default without a reviewer
  is `review: no-claim`.

**4. Use.** Seed text for amendment replay (N40-03); due sets for the exit query (N40-04); the reference for the
cross-check (N40-30).

## Considerations

- The chapter register beads named in Scope print their kill gates with triggers, fallbacks and decision points
  of their own. Those tables are the chapters' statements; they were compared with the rows above when the two
  sets of beads were reconciled, and they agree on every number, condition and decision point. Several are
  worded more fully or spell out an identifier that a row here names by section. The text an owner declaration
  carries, and the text N40-30 compares, is this ledger's.
- The chapter register beads of M1 and later depend on this bead. N32b-25 and N38b-23 are M0 beads and this
  group lands at M1, so they are related to it and not ordered; KILL-REPLAYPACK is decided at M3 and
  KILL-DEVORACLE at M1, so the kill part of their checks is first required at the M1 exit.
- These rows were written later and reviewed less than the compiled tables; a discrepancy between a row here and
  its defining subsection is resolved in favour of the defining subsection until the tables are regenerated, and is
  then an amendment.
- When the register is regenerated (N40-10), each of these rows moves into the table of its subsystem and this
  bead's group ceases to be separate; the ledger rows and their history are unchanged.
