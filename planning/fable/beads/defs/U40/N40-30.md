## Background

The program has 144 kill gates: 112 in the register's four compiled tables, 7 for the ten-fold test and 25 from
later subsections (and, beside those seeded rows, the gates that pilots proposed and that enter by an
`introduce` amendment: ten at plan time). Each is carried twice by design. The **fallback ledger** (beads N40-23 to N40-29) holds the
register's row: trigger, fallback, decision point, owner. The **decision bead**, written by the unit that owns the
gate's subsection, takes the measurement and records the decision. Two copies exist so that one can check the
other: the chapter that built a mechanism is the party most tempted to restate its kill trigger a little more
kindly, and the register is the party with no stake.

This bead is the check. It compares what every decision bead declares with its ledger row, lists the kill
decisions due at each exit, and verifies after each exit that every decision has its ADR and that the tracker
reflects it (a bead whose kill gate fired closed as `fallback`; dependents re-read). It is the mechanism behind the
instruction that the ledger exists "so that the decision beads the chapter units write can be checked against it".

## Scope

In: the comparison; the by-exit schedule; the post-decision consistency checks; the end-to-end script shared by the
ledger beads. Out: the ledger rows (N40-23 to N40-29); the record format and amendments (N40-03); the lint for
convergence gates (N40-09).

## Technical approach

**1. What a decision bead declares (DESIGN; the same `contract.toml` block as N40-09 uses).**
`[[gate]] id = "KILL-…", role = "owner", trigger, fallback, decided, mode, reads = [...]`, plus, once decided,
`decision = { outcome, adr, measured = [{ name, value, unit, receipt }] }` (an array of such tables, each with
`exit`, `scope` and, for a second entry for one scope at one exit, `reading`, for a gate the ledger decides more
than once: N40-03). A consumer chapter declares `role = "consumer"` and restates only.

**2. The comparison** (`crates/frob-conformance/tests/kill_ledger.rs`, run as
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger`).

1. Every ledger row has at most one owner declaration before its decision is due by the schedule of step 3, and
   exactly one from that exit on (the declaration is written by the deciding bead, which for many gates lands at
   the decided milestone; a missing declaration at or after the due exit fails `owner-declaration-missing`, two
   fail `owner-declaration-duplicated`); every owner declaration has a ledger row (a kill gate that a chapter
   declares and the ledger lacks fails `kill-gate-not-in-ledger`).
2. Owner trigger and fallback, normalized (whitespace and inline code marks), equal the ledger's current text,
   that is, the seeded text with its amendments replayed. A difference fails `trigger-differs` or
   `fallback-differs` and prints both texts.
3. Every number in the ledger's trigger appears in the owner's trigger with the same comparison direction
   ("below 0.5" is not "at most 0.5").
4. The owner's decision point equals the ledger's; a later one fails `decided-later-without-adr` unless an ADR
   with outcome `deferred` exists.
5. For every decided gate: the ADR exists; each `measured` value cites a receipt that resolves; the outcome is
   consistent with the trigger evaluated on the measured values (a trigger that the measured values meet with
   outcome `not-fired` fails `outcome-contradicts-measurement`); the recording session is not an implementer; the
   declaration's outcome and ADR equal those of the ledger row's decision entry for the same exit, scope and
   reading (N40-03 step 6), or the check fails `decision-copies-differ` and prints both; and the set of receipt
   hashes cited by the declaration's `measured` values equals the entry's `receipts` field (N40-03 step 1), or
   it fails `decision-receipts-differ` (DESIGN reason string) naming the hashes on one side only. The reading is
   part of the key because a gate decided in two stages at one exit (KILL-FARFIELD: N19cp1-02 then N19c-37;
   KILL-SHORTGRAD: N23b-37 then N23b-39; both at M6) has a first entry and a `reading = "final"` entry: stage
   B's declaration is compared with the final entry and never with stage A's.
6. For every `fired` gate: the gates in `reads` that failed have status `killed`; the capability is printed under
   the fallback's name; the bead that carried the mechanism closed with reason `fallback`; each dependent either
   closed as `superseded` with the ADR identifier or still has an open path that stands on the fallback.
7. Beads that exist only on one branch of a decision depend on the decision bead (checked against the tracker's
   export: a bead labelled with the mechanism and not depending on its kill decision is listed). One exception, for
   a gate whose trigger has clauses read at two times (KILL-VISIBILITY: a pilot read before funding, then the
   gate's measurement, which the decision bead reads from a receipt that the branch-only beads themselves
   produce): a branch-only bead may depend instead on the pilot bead whose record the ledger row's `reads` names,
   since depending on the decision bead would make a cycle. The bead is then listed only if it depends on neither,
   and the decision bead must depend on it (the later clause is read before the decision is recorded).

**4. Output.** A section "Kill decisions" in each exit report (N40-04): per gate due, the ledger text, the
declared text, the measured values beside the trigger's numbers, the outcome, the ADR, and the consistency checks
of steps 5 to 7.

**5. Where it runs.** This bead adds `cargo test -p frob-conformance --test kill_ledger` to step 6 of the exit
runner `scripts/exit/review.sh` (N40-05, reached through N41-16), from M1 on; its failures are listed among the
blocking items of the exit record. At M0, before this bead exists, step 6 lists the two M0 decisions from N40-03's
due list alone.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On a tree in which every decision bead's declaration equals its ledger row, the test passes and prints 144
      seeded rows compared, and beside them every row entered by an `introduce` amendment (seven from the ledger
      beads N40-23, N40-24 and N40-26; ten once N38a-37 has entered its three at M4).
- [ ] The re-read entries and the introduced rows of step 3 are in the due lists of their exits and are printed
      apart from the two plan columns; none changes `schedule_counts`.
- [ ] Each of the plants of the test plan fails with its named reason and the gate identifier.
- [ ] The schedule computed from the register equals the table of step 3.
- [ ] After a synthetic `fired` decision, steps 6 and 7 list any dependent bead that neither closed as
      `superseded` nor stands on the fallback.
- [ ] A `not-fired` outcome whose measured values meet the trigger is rejected.
- [ ] The test runs in the exit runner's step 6 at every exit from M1, and a failure is a blocking item of the exit
      record.
- [ ] A gate whose decision is not yet due passes check 1 with no owner declaration; from its due exit it fails
      without one.
- [ ] A branch-only bead of a two-time gate that depends on the pilot named in `reads` is not listed; one that
      depends on neither the pilot nor the decision bead is.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `one_owner_per_kill_gate`: two at any exit → `owner-declaration-duplicated` naming crates; none at or after the
  decided exit → `owner-declaration-missing`; none before it → pass (`kill_declaration_not_required_before_due`).
- `trigger_text_matches_after_amendments`: ledger seed "below 0.5", amendment to "below 0.45", owner "below 0.45"
  → pass; owner "below 0.4" → `trigger-differs`.
- `direction_matters`: ledger "above 10⁻²", owner "at least 10⁻²" → failure.
- `decided_later_needs_adr`: owner M3, ledger M2, no ADR → failure; with a `deferred` ADR → pass and the gate
  appears in M3's due set.
- `outcome_consistent`: trigger "saving under 7%", measured 6.2%, outcome `not-fired` → rejected; outcome `fired`
  → accepted.
- `fired_renames_capability`: after `fired`, the generated capability list contains the fallback's name and not
  the killed capability.
- `dependents_reread`: three dependents: one `superseded`, one standing on the fallback, one untouched → the third
  is listed.
- `schedule_counts`: [2, 4, 14, 28, 30, 18, 9, 0, 8] and [0, 1, 4, 6, 9, 0, 4, 0, 2].
- `decision_copies_agree`: a declaration with outcome `fired` and a ledger decision entry `not-fired` for the same
  exit and scope → `decision-copies-differ`; equal → pass; per-cell entries are compared scope by scope.
- `two_stage_compared_by_reading`: KILL-SHORTGRAD with a first M6 entry `not-fired` and a `reading = "final"` M6
  entry `fired`; stage B's declaration (`fired`, `reading = "final"`) → pass; the same declaration with no
  `reading`, which would meet stage A's entry → `decision-copies-differ`.
- `decision_receipts_agree`: a declaration whose `measured` values cite receipts {a, b} against an entry with
  `receipts = [a, b]` → pass; against `[a]` or `[a, c]` → `decision-receipts-differ` naming b, or b and c.
- `reread_and_introduced_scheduled`: on the seeded register with its amendments, the re-read entries due are
  three at M3, one at M4 and two at M6, and the introduced rows the ledger beads enter are three with
  `decided = "M4"` and four with `decided = "M6"` (N38a-37 adds its three at M4, after this bead, and the same
  test then counts seven at M6 with no change here); KILL-AUGUR-REGION with no M4 entry at the M4 exit → listed missing; an introduced
  `pilot-before-funding` row with no funding request → listed "due when funding is requested", not missing.
- `branch_bead_on_pilot`: KILL-VISIBILITY's branch-only beads depending on the pilot N20a-35 named in `reads` →
  not listed; a branch-only bead depending on neither → listed.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/kill_ledger.sh`: loads all seven ledger groups from `registries/gates.toml`; prints the schedule; runs
the comparison against the declarations in the tree; then in a scratch copy applies six plants in turn (a softened
trigger; a fallback rewritten; a gate declared and not in the ledger; a decision point moved later; a contradicted
outcome; an untouched dependent after a fired gate) and asserts a non-zero exit with the reason and the gate;
then two plants that must pass (an owner declaration absent for a gate not yet due; a branch-only bead depending
on its gate's pilot) and two that must fail (a declaration and ledger decision entry that differ; a duplicated
owner declaration). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/kill_ledger/<utc>.ndjson`, per step:
`ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block `{claim: known-answer,
class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`, rows compared against 144, the schedule measured against
expected, the hashes of the ledger and declared texts for each difference, and on
failure the first differing gate with both texts and file paths; the summary names GATE-GAUNT-10 and the kill
gates touched.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 144 rows (13 + 35 + 17 + 28 + 19 + 7 + 25); 113 and 26 decisions; the schedule of step 3 (N40-KA
R-25, R-26); six re-read entries (3, 1, 2 at M3, M4, M6) and ten introduced rows (3 decided M4, 7 decided M6; seven of
the ten are in the register when this bead lands, the other three from M4), requested of N40-KA as a row
beside R-26. Negative controls: N40-CX RC-10, RC-11, RC-12, RC-13, RC-38, RC-44 (a softened comparison direction),
RC-45 (a fired gate whose dependents were left open on the killed capability), RC-58 (an owner declaration and
the ledger's decision entry that disagree).

## Considerations

- Text comparison is strict on numbers and direction and lenient on formatting. A trigger restated in different
  words with the same numbers passes the number check and is flagged for the exit reviewer to read.
- The declarations' format is shared with N40-09 and must be agreed with the unit that owns `contract.toml`; until
  then the check reads `registries/gate_declarations.toml`.
- A decision bead may legitimately carry *more* detail than the ledger (fixtures, the repair cycle's log). It may
  not carry a different trigger.
