## Background

The program is built on 37 **bets**: explicit hypotheses, each with a falsifier, about what will turn out to be
true (the algebra pays for itself; one source runs on three backends; a family policy walks unseen designs; the
sealed ten-fold ratio holds). A bet that nothing decides is a belief. The register therefore pairs every bet with
the **gates that decide it**, the **kill gate** that fires if it fails, and the **milestone** at which that happens.
A bet with no kill gate has its consequence written with the bet.

The pairing itself is data of the bet register (N00-28, M0), which enters the 37 rows once, with their falsifier
clauses, in `crates/frob-conformance/registry/bets.toml`. This bead is the gate register's side of those rows. It
checks the pairing against the gate register and the kill ledgers at every exit: a bet whose deciding gates are
due and recorded gets an exit state (`held`, `falsified`, `untested` and the others below); a bet whose deciding
gate has no record at its milestone is reported; and no gate is cited as deciding a bet unless the register says
so. It is the bridge between the thesis (the bets, owned by the unit that writes the bet beads) and the delivery
layer (the gates and kill gates).

## Scope

In: the resolution of every deciding gate and kill gate named by a bet row against `registries/gates.toml` and
the kill ledgers; the exit state of a bet, derived from the bet register's verdict and the decisions on record;
the exit-time checks, among them that an `open` bet blocks the exit; the count of bets due at each milestone;
the check that no bead or report cites a gate as deciding a bet unless the row lists it.

Out, with the bead that has it:
- the 37 rows themselves (`id`, `name`, `statement`, `falsifier`, `deciding_gates`, `kill_gates`,
  `no_kill_consequence`, `milestones` and the rest), the table of bets with their deciding gates, kill gates and
  milestones, the pre-registration check, the verdict function (`Untested`, `Held`, `Falsified`, `GateFailedShort`,
  `HeldInPart`) with its per-bet readings for BET-04, BET-12, BET-13, BET-14 and BET-34, the decision record of a bet,
  the owner's report and the generator of the README's bets table: N00-28. This bead depends on it, reads its file and
  enters no bet row in `registries/gates.toml` or anywhere else;
- the bets' own statements and falsifiers (their beads); the kill ledgers (N40-23 to N40-28); the fallback exits
  per milestone (N40-32).

## Technical approach

**1. The rows are read, not entered.** The 37 `[[bet]]` records are those of
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/bets.toml` (N00-28). This bead reads four of their fields: `deciding_gates`,
`kill_gates`, `no_kill_consequence` and `milestones` (the column "At" of the plan's table; an earlier draft of
this bead called the field `at` and kept its own copy of the rows inside `registries/gates.toml`). DESIGN: one
file holds the rows. Reason: the same table entered in two registries is two places to amend a decider, and the
bet register needs the rows a milestone earlier, beside the falsifier clauses, for its pre-registration check.
The gate register gains no `[[bet]]` record and no new field. What this bead adds is a reverse index, computed
from the bet rows whenever the register is loaded and printed in the exit report: for every gate and every
kill gate, the bets whose records name it (GATE-SPARK-04 → BET-09). It is generated, never typed, and is what
check (c) of section 3 is run against.

What the checks of this bead need from the rows, as a view by milestone (a bet is counted at every milestone
its row names; the view is generated from `milestones` and is the known answer of the due counts):

| M1 | BET-12, BET-19, BET-24 | 3 |
| M2 | BET-01, BET-02, BET-03, BET-08, BET-09, BET-18, BET-34 | 7 |
| M3 | BET-03, BET-05, BET-06, BET-10, BET-13, BET-14, BET-17, BET-19, BET-20, BET-21, BET-22, BET-23, BET-24, BET-25, BET-31, BET-32, BET-33, BET-34, BET-36, BET-37 | 20 |

BET-04 is due at M0 and is re-read at every later exit while KILL-FSIM is rolling. Rows that name more than one
kill gate, for which a partial outcome is possible: BET-14 (KILL-TENFOLD-TIE; KILL-AUGUR-TIE on the family

KILL-MCW), BET-25 (KILL-ENERGY, KILL-SHIELD), BET-27 (KILL-MPC, KILL-DISTILL), BET-29 (KILL-TOPOPT,
KILL-TOLERANCE, KILL-PACKET), BET-30 (KILL-EQUIV, KILL-MARROW-BLOCKS, KILL-GATREE), BET-33 (KILL-REUSE,
KILL-AUGUR-REUSE), BET-36 (KILL-FILM, KILL-ADVECT, KILL-LIVING).

Each bet is decided by a recorded gate. A bet with no kill gate (BET-08, BET-10, BET-12, BET-21) has its
consequence written with the bet and repeated in the row's `no_kill_consequence`.

**2. Exit state of a bet** (`bet_status(bet, register, exit) -> Status`). The exit state answers one
question the verdict does not: has the program acted on what the evidence shows? It is a function of two
inputs and computes nothing else from gate rows: the verdict of the bet register (`verdict(bet, gates)` of
N00-28, which reads the falsifier clauses, the measured values and whether each passing gate caught its
control) and the decisions on record (the kill ledgers and the ADRs).

| Exit state | Verdict of N00-28 | Decisions on record |
|---|---|---|
| `not-due` | `Untested` | No deciding gate of the row has a part due at or before this exit |
| `untested` | `Untested` | A deciding gate's part is due and is `not-run` (for example under a variant: BET-15 with no compute envelope); the bet is recorded as untested with the reason |
| `held` | `Held` | No kill gate of the row has fired |
| `falsified` | `Falsified` | A kill gate of the row is `fired` (the gate row then has status `killed`, printed "killed: fallback adopted"); or, for a bet with no kill gate, the written consequence has been applied by ADR |
| `partly-falsified` | `Falsified` or `HeldInPart` | Some kill gates of the row fired and others did not (the rows with several are listed in section 1); the state lists which |
| `failed-short` | `GateFailedShort` | The bet's decision record `docs/adr/bet-<nn>-<milestone>.md` (N00-28) is on file and states what is in force and why, since no kill gate installed a fallback. Non-blocking; printed `failed-short: <gate> <measured> (gate threshold <t_g>, falsifier <t_f>)` with the record's path, and the bet is read again at its next milestone |
| `open` | `Falsified`, `GateFailedShort`, or a deciding part is `fail` | Neither a kill decision nor the written consequence nor (for `GateFailedShort`) the bet's decision record is on record. This state blocks the exit |

For a bet decided per class or stratum the verdict is a map (`HeldInPart`), and the exit state is the same map
with each class taken through the table. The per-bet readings of the verdict (BET-13 needs a stratum with the
verdict `ten-fold`, and a `measured` ratio between 3 and 10 is neither held nor falsified; BET-14 where the
engine's advantage is not established; BET-34 per robot class below 90% referee coverage; BET-12 by the
schedule gate's three branches, N40-11; BET-04 re-read at every exit) are rules of N00-28's function and are
stated there; this function inherits them and has no second copy.
One rule (DESIGN). An earlier draft of this bead computed `held`, `falsified` and `untested` directly from gate
rows, by a rule that differed from the bet register's: a failed deciding gate with no kill decision was `open`
here and `Falsified` there, and a gate that passed without its control caught counted here and not there.
Both readings are kept, as two questions: the verdict says what the evidence shows, the exit state says
whether it has been acted on, and a pass without a caught control is not a pass for either.
`GateFailedShort` (a deciding gate `fail` while no falsifier clause is true and no kill gate fired: BET-18 at a
mixed-batch ratio between 0.5 and 0.70, where GATE-PHYLUM-03 needs 0.70 and KILL-PHYLUM fires below 0.5) is
`open` until the bet's decision record is written and `failed-short` after (DESIGN, agreed with N00-28: a missed
gate with an uncrossed falsifier must be answered once, in writing, before an exit passes, and must not block
every later exit once it has been answered; the next milestone's reading can still make it `held` or
`falsified`).

**3. Checks at every exit.** (a) Every bet whose `milestones` include this milestone has a status other than
`open`. (b) Every deciding gate named exists in the register, and every kill gate named exists in a ledger.
(c) No bet bead or report cites a gate as deciding a bet unless the bet's row lists it: every such citation is
looked up in the reverse index of section 1. (d) The README's bets table is generated from the rows and the
statuses by the bet register's generator (N00-28), which takes the exit-state column from this function; this
bead checks at each exit that the generated table is current and adds no second generator.

**4. Where it runs.** This bead adds a section "Bets" to the exit report of N40-04 (a dependency): per bet due at the
exit, its row's deciding gates and kill gates, the bet register's verdict, the exit state with its reason, and the
reverse index; checks (a) to (d) run whenever the report is regenerated, so they run in step 5 of every exit review
from M1 (N40-05), and an `open` bet, a dangling identifier, an unlisted citation or a README bets table that differs
from the generator's output is a blocking item of the report. The exit-review beads of M1 to M8 depend on this bead.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The 37 bet rows of the bet register load through this bead's reader; every identifier in `deciding_gates`
      and `kill_gates` resolves to a row of the gate register or of a kill ledger.
- [ ] `registries/gates.toml` holds no `[[bet]]` record; the reverse index from gates to bets is computed from
      the bet rows, printed in the exit report, and stored nowhere by hand.
- [ ] Exactly four rows have no kill gate, each with its written consequence.
- [ ] The exit state of every bet equals the table of section 2 applied to the bet register's verdict: on every
      fixture the two functions are called and no fixture has an exit state the verdict contradicts (a `held`
      state with a verdict other than `Held`).
- [ ] `bet_status` returns the stated status on each fixture of the test plan.
- [ ] An `open` bet blocks the exit report; a `falsified` bet prints its fallback's name; a `GateFailedShort`
      verdict is `open` until its decision record is on file and `failed-short` (non-blocking, printed with the
      gate's measured value, both thresholds and the record's path) after; the "Bets" section is in every exit
      report regenerated from M1 on.
- [ ] At each exit the README's bets table equals the output of N00-28's generator on the rows and the exit
      states; a hand-edited table is a blocking item of the "Bets" section (the `xtask check-generated` wiring of
      that table is N00-28's).
- [ ] At each exit the set of bets with a decision due equals the rows' `milestones` (M0: 2; M1: 3; M2: 7; M3: 20;
      M4: 14; M5: 3; M6: 1; M7: 2; M8: 1, counting a bet at every milestone its row names).

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance register::bets::`.

- `thirty_seven_rows` and `four_without_kill_gate` (BET-08, BET-10, BET-12, BET-21), read from the bet
  register's file; `no_bet_record_in_gate_register`; `reverse_index_generated`.
- `exit_state_follows_verdict`: for each of the seven exit states, a fixture whose verdict and decisions are
  those of its row in section 2; and the two cases the earlier rule got differently: a deciding gate `fail` with no
  kill decision (verdict `Falsified`, exit state `open`); a gate `pass` with `controls = []` (verdict not

- `all_identifiers_resolve`: over the rows, 0 dangling.
- `status_held`: BET-09 with GATE-SPARK-04 `pass`, KILL-METAL `not-fired` → `held`.
- `status_falsified`: KILL-METAL `fired` → `falsified`, fallback "Metal becomes a development and replay tier".
- `status_partly`: BET-29 with KILL-TOPOPT `fired`, KILL-TOLERANCE and KILL-PACKET `not-fired` →
  `partly-falsified`.
- `status_untested_under_variant`: BET-15 with GATE-CORTEX-11 `not-run` under the no-family-policy variant →

- `status_open_blocks`: BET-06 with GATE-REFLEX-02 `fail` and KILL-BASELINE undecided → `open`.
- `status_failed_short`: BET-18 with GATE-PHYLUM-03 `fail` at a measured ratio of 0.62, clause (c) false and

  `docs/adr/bet-18-M2.md`; with that record on file → `failed-short`, not blocking, printed with 0.62 against

  the state `falsified`.
- `bet13_exit_state`: all strata `measured` at ratio 6 → not `held`, not `falsified` (the verdict test
  `bet13_needs_ten_fold_verdict` is N00-28's; here only the mapping to the exit state is asserted).
- `due_by_milestone`: counts 2, 3, 7, 20, 14, 3, 1, 2, 1.
- `readme_table_current`: a bets table equal to the generator's output passes check (d); the same table with one

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/bets_registry.sh`: loads the rows, prints the status of every bet at each exit on a synthetic register
(first with no measurement: all `not-due` or `untested` or blocking where due; then with synthetic decisions,
among them BET-18 failed short at 0.62, logged `open` and blocking before its decision record is written and
`failed-short` after),
regenerates the README bets table and the exit report's "Bets" section, and asserts the due counts; then plants
the three negative controls below in a scratch copy (a `held` bet whose decider is `not-run`; a citation of a gate
the row does not list; a failed decider with no decision) and asserts each is caught with its reason
(`bet-held-on-not-run`, `bet-decider-not-listed`, `failed-bet-undecided`), the third as a blocking item of the
regenerated M3 report. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/bets_registry/<utc>.ndjson`:
per step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, command, exit code, wall time, the evidence block `{claim:
known-answer, class: Tested, tier: G0:host, determinism: D0, seed: 0}`, the status vector per exit, counts beside
expected, the digest of the bet register's file read, and on failure the first bet whose status differs with the
rows it read; the `summary` line counts assertions and names GATE-GAUNT-10.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: 37 rows; four without a kill gate; the due counts (N40-KA R-27). Negative controls: N40-CX RC-46 (a
bet declared `held` while its deciding gate is `not-run`: `bet-held-on-not-run`), RC-47 (a gate cited as deciding a
bet that the table does not list for it: `bet-decider-not-listed`), RC-48 (a bet with a failed decider and no
decision, passed over at an exit: `failed-bet-undecided`).

## Considerations

- The table is the register's and governs which gates decide which bet; a bet bead that wants a different decider
  proposes an amendment. The amendment is made in the one file of rows (the bet register's), dated, before the
  measurement it governs; the reverse index here follows on regeneration.
- Late gates may come to decide parts of bets (for example a late gate that measures a lever more directly); they
  are added to a row only by amendment, never implicitly.
- Bets are tested in an order that puts the cheapest decisive evidence first: BET-04 and BET-12 at M0 and M1.
