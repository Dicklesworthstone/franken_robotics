## Background

M2 puts the batched engine under the CPU studio on three backends: `batch-cpu`, CUDA and Metal. Two of those
backends depend on things the program does not control at M2: the owner's ratification of how GPU code is housed
(question OPEN-SPARK-1: GPU backends live in annex workspaces with registered foreign-function capsules, outside
the Franken-only core), and the admission of the CUDA and Metal gateways. DESIGN: **M2 can exit without a
device.** The no-GPU variant is that exit, written in advance so that a failed admission or an unanswered question
costs device claims and not the milestone.

The variant is not a waiver. Every device gate is recorded `not run` with its reason and is printed that way, the
demonstrations that need a device are recorded as not demonstrated, any device result that happens to exist is
labelled exploratory and supports no claim, and the lost capability is recorded. What still must hold is
everything the batch means on a CPU: the batched step in both profiles, the gym, PPO, variants and cohorts, the
ledgers, replay, forks and the sampled referee, with their Known answer, Parity and Fidelity gates passed on
`batch-cpu`. Alternative rejected: hold M2 open until a device is admitted, because M3's partial ten-fold gate
has strata that need no device, and the rival's measured ledger does not get more informative by waiting.

## Scope

In: the three conditions; the variant record; the rule that derives which gates are `not run`; the Performance
rows that apply; the undemonstrated scenarios; the consequences for M3; the way back; the outcome each kill
gate due at M2 takes under the variant. Out: the device backends and
their gates (Spark's beads); the exit review that applies the variant (seed BD-224); the rehearsal that the
variant still exits (N40-32).

## Technical approach

**1. When the variant applies (any one).** (a) OPEN-SPARK-1 is not ratified by the M2 exit: its default holds, GPU
results are labelled exploratory, and no Performance or Parity claim rests on a device result. (b) The admission
gates INT-CUDA-01 and INT-FT-01 both fail. (c) No device family holds a controlled determinism class (the IEEE
probe and the class tests show a family cannot hold run-to-run determinism), or no device family holds
batch-composition invariance. If exactly one device family is usable and ratified, the variant does not apply: M2
exits on that family, and the other family's gates follow their own kill gates (KILL-METAL; KILL-D1).

**2. The variant record** (an ADR plus a record in `registries/gates.toml`; `[[variant]]` is an additive top-level
table of schema `frob.gates/1` defined by this bead and also used by N40-34; within it an unknown field fails the
load):

```toml
[[variant]]
id         = "m2-no-gpu"
milestone  = "M2"
condition  = "a"                 # a | b | c, with the evidence: the question's default ADR, the failed
                                 # admission records, or the probe and class receipts
adr        = "…"
not_run    = [ { gate = "GATE-SPARK-03", part = "M2", reason = "no admitted, ratified device" }, … ]
not_shown  = ["NS-10", "NS-02 (device half)", "NS-14 (device half)"]
```

**3. Which gate parts are `not run`: derived, not listed by hand.** For each part due at M2, from its `tier` list:
(i) every tier is a CPU tier (`G1:host`, `referee`, `strict`, `batch-cpu`): the part runs and must pass as usual;
(ii) CPU and device tiers both: the CPU rows run and must pass, the device rows are recorded `not run (variant:
no-GPU)`, and the part is printed "CPU rows passed; device rows not run"; (iii) only device tiers: the part is
`not run (variant: no-GPU)`. The exit query (N40-04) treats (ii) and (iii) as non-blocking only when the variant
record lists them. Expected under rule (iii), from the gates' briefs (DESIGN list, to be confirmed against the
declared tiers): GATE-SPARK-02 (CPU against each device), GATE-SPARK-03 (CUDA throughput rows), GATE-SPARK-04
(Metal throughput), GATE-SPARK-06 (device faults and memory), GATE-SPARK-09 (strict placement of device kernels),
GATE-SPARK-10 (captured graphs and mixed batches on a device), GATE-SPARK-11 and -12 (device memory, graph
captures, snapshot reads), GATE-SPARK-16 (class D3 placement pairs), GATE-SPARK-20 (scaling on two cards),
GATE-CORTEX-05 (training time on one RTX 4090), GATE-CORTEX-08 (two-shard updates), GATE-FID-07's share of GPU
world-seconds, and the device rows of GATE-CHRON-03, -04, -11. Expected under rule (ii): GATE-KIN-05, -06;
GATE-PHYLUM-02; GATE-MARROW-05; GATE-SENS-02, -13; GATE-SPARK-15, -18, -22; GATE-CORTEX-01; GATE-CHRON-01, -07;
GATE-TRAC-01. GATE-HAB-01 is under neither: its device tiers are its M3 part (N19a-25; N40-02, step 5), so its M2
part (N19a-42) runs on `batch-cpu` only and is recorded as usual.

The `tier` lists the rule reads are filled by the register bead of each chapter that has a part due at M2 (N16c-05,
N19c-29, N20b-21, N21c-32, N22c-49, N23b-16, N28c-25, N32b-25, N38b-23: dependencies of this bead) and, for the
chapters without one (Marrow, Sinew, Phylum, Traction, Arena, Autopsy, Chronicle, safety), by the bead that
measures each gate (N40-02, step 8); those are read at the M2 exit by its review (BD-224), not when this bead
closes. Skeleton, Augur and Ground have no part due at M2, so their register beads are not prerequisites.

**4. What lands on `batch-cpu`, on the 36 cores of the M5 Ultra and on the CPU of `trj`.** The batched step in
both profiles (`lockstep` and `strict`), `VecEnv`, PPO, variants and cohorts, the ledgers, replay, forks and the
sampled referee. The Known answer, Parity and Fidelity gates run on `batch-cpu`.

**5. The Performance rows that apply.** Rows 13 to 15 of the performance table: (13) the reference humanoid,
physics only, `batch-cpu` in single precision on the M5 Ultra with 24 threads, in world-steps per second at
decimation 10: at least 0.5 of CPU MuJoCo on the same machine and threads (planning value 275 thousand; ambition
1.0, 550 thousand; measured context: CPU MuJoCo in double precision on this machine reaches 24 thousand
world-steps per second on one thread, 550 thousand on 24 and 576 thousand on 36); (14) the reference quadruped,
same tier, machine and threads: at least 0.5 of CPU MuJoCo measured on the same machine, and until that is
measured row 13's floor; (15) the `strict` profile: no tolerance, and its rate is reported with every strict
result.

**6. Not demonstrated.** NS-10 (the same task on a Mac through Metal) and the device halves of NS-02 (teleport a
world from a GPU batch) and NS-14 (256 designs in one batch on one RTX 4090). Their CPU halves run: a world of a
`batch-cpu` batch is replayed alone from its address; 256 designs step in one `batch-cpu` cohort with unchanged
digests.

**7. What M3 inherits.** The partial ten-fold gate at M3 charges the CPU engine's measured rate in full, and the
strata that need no device (family queries, amendments, failure discovery on small worlds) are run first.

**8. The way back.** When a device family is later ratified and admitted, its gates are run and recorded at the
exit then current, by their own records; the variant record is closed by an ADR; nothing recorded `not run` is
retroactively marked passed for M2.

**9. Kill decisions under the variant (DESIGN; the plan states the variant and not what it does to the kill
gates).** Every kill gate due at M2, the fourteen of the compiled register and the four of later subsections,
still gets its ADR. The outcome follows from the parts its record reads (the `reads` field of the kill record,
N40-03), by the rule of section 3:

- (i) every part it reads ran on a CPU tier: the decision is taken as usual. Expected: KILL-CONTACT,
  KILL-SINEW-ROWS, KILL-POOL, KILL-SCHED, KILL-EXPOSURE, KILL-LOOPELIM.
- (ii) every part it reads is `not run (variant: no-GPU)`: the outcome is `deferred`, by an ADR that cites the
  variant record and names M3, the next exit, as the exit at which the decision is taken again (a deferral must
  name a later exit; it is deferred again there if no device family has been ratified and admitted by then).
  Expected: KILL-PARITY, KILL-METAL, KILL-SHARD, KILL-LEARNKERN, KILL-D1, KILL-MARROW-GUARD.
- (iii) its trigger has a clause the CPU parts decide and a clause that needs a device part (expected: KILL-KIR,
  whose speed clause reads the CUDA throughput rows; KILL-BUCKET, whose rate clause reads a mixed batch on a
  device; KILL-PHYLUM where the head-to-head ran on `batch-cpu` alone; KILL-ROWQUEUE, KILL-ANCESTOR and
  KILL-LIMBKERNEL, whose gain clauses are device figures): if a clause decided on CPU parts is met the outcome
  is `fired`; otherwise it is `deferred` to M3, and the ADR lists the clauses evaluated with their values and
  the clauses left unevaluated.

Mechanical form of the rule: at the M2 exit under the variant, an outcome `not-fired` is refused for a kill gate
while any part named by its `reads` is `not run (variant: no-GPU)`; a trigger is never declared not met on half
of its clauses. `not-reached` keeps its meaning (the mechanism was not funded) and is not a way to record a
missing device. The exit line of M2, the kill decisions for the kernel toolchain, contact, Metal and mixed
batches recorded as ADRs, is met by these ADRs, the deferred ones included; the exit record lists every deferred
decision under "variants in force", and the M3 exit review finds each one due.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The variant record exists only with evidence of one of the three conditions; a variant record with none is
      refused.
- [ ] The `not_run` list equals the list derived from the declared tiers; a gate with a CPU tier that is listed
      whole as `not run` is refused (its CPU rows must run).
- [ ] Every listed part is printed `not run (variant: no-GPU)` in the exit report, the README table and
      `frob capabilities --json`; none is printed as passed.
- [ ] Rows 13 to 15 are measured and reported with their disclosure blocks.
- [ ] Any device result in the ledger carries the label exploratory and is cited by no gate.
- [ ] With the variant record, the M2 exit report is `exit-ready` when every CPU part passes and every kill
      decision is recorded; without the record the same register is `not-ready`.
- [ ] The partial gate's plan at M3 uses the CPU engine's measured rate and orders the device-free strata first.
- [ ] Under the variant every kill gate due at M2 has an ADR with an outcome; none is `not-fired` while a part
      its record reads is `not run (variant: no-GPU)`; every `deferred` one names M3 and cites the variant
      record; and the five gates of the exit line are among them.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`cargo test -p frob-conformance exit::variants::no_gpu::`.

- `condition_required`: record with no condition evidence → refused; with the default ADR of the ratification
  question → accepted; with two failed admission records → accepted.
- `derive_not_run`: tiers [`batch-cpu`] → runs; [`batch-cpu`, `gpu-cuda`, `gpu-metal`] → CPU rows run, device rows
  not run; [`gpu-cuda`] → not run.
- `cpu_rows_cannot_be_waived`: a hand-listed whole `not run` for a part with a CPU tier → refused.
- `one_family_is_not_the_variant`: CUDA admitted and ratified, Metal failed → variant refused; KILL-METAL expected.
- `not_shown_scenarios`: the record's `not_shown` equals {NS-10, device half of NS-02, device half of NS-14}.
- `exploratory_not_citable`: a gate part citing a receipt labelled exploratory → `evidence-unresolved` (the
  label and the recorder's check on it are N21c-34's, the decision bead of OPEN-SPARK-1, a dependency).
- `no_retroactive_pass`: after the variant is closed, the M2 parts remain `not run (variant)`.
- `kill_decisions_under_variant` (on a scratch register with the variant record): a kill gate whose `reads`
  parts are all `not run (variant: no-GPU)` with outcome `not-fired` → refused; with `deferred` naming M3 and
  the variant record → accepted; naming M2, or no exit → refused. A kill gate reading one CPU part that ran
  and one device part `not run`: `fired` with the CPU receipt → accepted; `not-fired` → refused; `deferred` to
  M3 listing the evaluated clause → accepted. A kill gate whose parts all ran on CPU tiers: `not-fired` with
  its receipt → accepted.
- `exit_line_met_by_deferred_adrs`: with KILL-METAL `deferred` and the other four gates of the exit line
  decided, the M2 report under the variant is `exit-ready`; with KILL-METAL's ADR absent it is `not-ready`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/m2_no_gpu_variant.sh`: on a machine with devices hidden from the process, runs the M2 demonstrations'
CPU halves (`frob train start <reference quadruped> --task flat-velocity --algo ppo --backend batch-cpu --key
nogpu-train --json` to the return threshold; `frob sim replay --run <that run> --world 1337 --tier batch-cpu
--key nogpu-replay --json` from a `batch-cpu` batch; `frob phylum sweep --designs <the 256-design workload>
--task <its task> --worlds-per-design 32 --tier batch-cpu --key nogpu-sweep --json` with the digest comparison;
the three are run verbs, so each call carries its own key, and `train.start` names its back end with
`--backend`, as their rows in the verb registries state), records the variant, and regenerates the exit report. Asserts: `exit-ready`; every device gate
printed `not run (variant: no-GPU)`; rows 13 to 15 present with disclosure blocks. Logs: command, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's evidence block (tier `batch-cpu`, determinism class, identities, seed), throughput with units
beside 0.5 of the CPU incumbent measured on the same machine, digests compared, and on failure the first
differing value with artifact paths. The script sources the STD-E2E helper and writes
`target/e2e-logs/m2_no_gpu_variant/<utc>.ndjson` (per step `ts`, `script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`); it then
replays two plants on copies of the register (a variant record with no condition evidence; a part with a
`batch-cpu` tier listed whole as `not run`) and asserts each refusal; the `summary` line counts assertions and names
the gates printed `not run (variant: no-GPU)`.

### Known answers and negative controls

Known answers: the three conditions; the two rule outcomes; rows 13 to 15 with their floors (275 thousand as the
planning value of row 13). Negative controls: a device-only gate printed as passed under the variant; the variant
claimed with one working device family; a device result cited as evidence while labelled exploratory; a gate with
a CPU tier skipped wholesale (N40-CX RC-49, RC-51); a kill gate recorded `not-fired` under the variant while
a part it reads was not run (N40-CX RC-65, due at M2; caught by this bead's test
`kill_decisions_under_variant`).

## Considerations

- The variant makes M2 a CPU milestone; PPO to the return threshold on 36 cores is slow but is the same test, and
  the zero-weight and random policies must still fail it.
- OPEN-SPARK-1 is asked at M0 and falls due at this exit; the variant is what its default costs.
- The kill gates that read device measurements are decided by the rule of section 9: deferred by ADR to M3,
  exit by exit, until a device family is admitted; never recorded `not-fired` on missing measurements. A long
  run of deferrals is itself a signal for the risk review: RISK-10 if the cause is the compute envelope, the
  admission risks if the cause is a failed gateway.
