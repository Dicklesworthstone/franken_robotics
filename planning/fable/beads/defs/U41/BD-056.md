## Background

Skeleton is the subsystem that owns what a robot is before anything moves; `frob-design` (layer 2, tier Solid) holds
the design term. A design is a term and not a file of numbers: its leaves are components (`Body`, `Joint`,
`Actuator`, `Transmission`, `Battery`, `Sensor`, `Wheel`, `Track`, `Foot`, `Payload`) and its internal nodes are
**constructors**. A constructor says how a limb is placed or repeated: attached at a port, chained, repeated in
series, mirrored across a plane, repeated around an axis, scaled, altered in one place, or closed into a loop.

Two decisions govern this bead.

- ADR-SKEL-01 (DESIGN): a design is a term and a file is a view of it; `DesignId` is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical
  bytes. Constructors, typed edits, lineage and incremental compilation all need a tree with stable subterms.
- Canonical-form rule 7 (DESIGN): constructor terms stay unexpanded in the canonical bytes; the expanded tree is a
  compile product and is never stored. Reason: two nested `mirror` constructors make the symmetry C₂ × C₂ a property
  of the term instead of something to rediscover from expanded numbers; an edit under a constructor changes all
  images at once and so preserves symmetry by construction; a subterm hash can serve as the memo key for all images.

Rejected alternatives. (1) Expand on write: four legs become four independent subtrees, an edit to one silently
breaks the symmetry, and the memo key of a limb is lost. (2) Expand on read but hash the expansion: the identity of a
design would then depend on the expansion code's version. (3) Write the mirrored joint axis as P·a (the polar image):
the mirrored joint then turns the opposite way for the same coordinate, so the coordinate, its limits and the
encoder and gear signs must all be negated on one side, and left and right limbs can no longer share kernels.

Terms. *Image*: one placed copy of a constructor's argument. *Parity*: +1, or −1 for an image under an odd number of
reflections. *Polar* vectors (positions, forces, linear velocities) map by P under a reflection x ↦ P·x + t; *axial*
vectors (angular velocities, torques, rotation axes) map by det(P)·P.

## Scope

In: the eight constructor node types, their canonical JSON spelling and field paths; the pure expansion rule of each
(the list of images with exact isometry, parity, scale and identifier suffix); the reflection rules `mirror` applies
to every component kind; the known answer "a design built with `mirror` equals its hand-expanded form"; refusals of
malformed constructors.

Out: the driver that walks a term, resolves parameters and builds the instance tree (N14a-07); `use` (BD-060); the
S-expression spelling (BD-057); typed edits that add or remove constructors (BD-058); symmetry computation (N13-15,
N14a-11); mobility of closures (the validate stage, FROB-REF-110).

## Technical approach

Layout (DESIGN): `crates/frob-design/src/constructor/{mod.rs, spell.rs, image.rs, reflect.rs}`.

**Canonical spelling** (DESIGN, fixed by the worked design of the format: `{"mirror": "xz", "of": {"mirror": "yz",
"of": {"attach": "torso.hip_mount", "chain": [ … ]}}}`; lever paths such as `/limbs/of/of/chain/1/body/…` rely on it).

| Constructor | Canonical object | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `attach(parent_port, child)` | `{"attach": "<body>.<port>", <child keys>}` | The child hangs on a named mechanical port of an existing body |
| `chain` | `{"chain": [link, …]}` | An ordered serial list; each link is `{joint, actuator?, transmission?, body}`; the last may be `foot`, `wheel` or `track` |
| `serial(k, link)` | `{"serial": k, "of": link}` | The same link k times in series, k ≥ 1 an integer |
| `mirror(plane, limb)` | `{"mirror": "xy"\|"xz"\|"yz"\|{"normal": […], "offset": qty}, "of": sub}` | Two images: the argument and its reflection across the plane, stated in the root body frame |
| `radial(n, axis, limb)` | `{"radial": n, "axis": "x"\|"y"\|"z"\|{"dir": […], "point": […]}, "of": sub}` | n images rotated by k/n turn, k = 0 … n − 1, n ≥ 2 |
| `scale(factor, sub)` | `{"scale": "<exact positive rational>", "of": sub}` | Every length in the subterm multiplied by the factor |
| `replace(path, component)` | `{"replace": "<path relative to of>", "with": component, "of": sub}` | The node at `path` is substituted before expansion |
| `closure(port_a, port_b)` | `{"closure": ["<inst>.<port>", "<inst>.<port>"], "joint": {class, screws or axis, limits}}` | A loop-closing joint between two ports; recorded as a pair, the tree stays a tree |

`use(module, at, bind, rename)` is reserved as a ninth tag and parsed by BD-060.

```rust
// DESIGN sketch: intent, not signatures.
pub enum Constructor { Attach{port: PortRef}, Chain, Serial{k: u32}, Mirror{plane: Plane},
                       Radial{n: u32, axis: AxisLine}, Scale{factor: PosRational},
                       Replace{path: Path}, Closure{a: PortRef, b: PortRef} }
pub struct Image { iso: ExactIsometry,   // (P or R, t), exact rationals or the cyclotomic field ℚ(ζ_n)
                   parity: i8, scale: PosRational, suffix: Suffix }
pub fn images(c: &Constructor) -> Vec<Image>;          // pure; order is the constructor index
pub fn reflect_component(node: &TypedNode, p: &Reflection) -> TypedNode;   // the table below
```

**Expansion rules** (pure functions; the driver composes images outermost first).

1. `attach`: one image, the port's `parent_from_port` transform. A port that does not exist or is not mechanical
   refuses (`FROB-REF-101`).
2. `chain`: link i is parented on the body of link i − 1 through its joint; `origin` is `parent_from_joint`.
3. `serial(k, link)`: k images; image j is parented on image j − 1 at the link's distal port (the port named `next`,
   or the single port if exactly one exists; otherwise refuse `FROB-REF-103`). Suffixes `0 … k−1`.
4. `mirror(plane, sub)`: with unit normal n and offset d (plane n·x = d), P = I − 2·n·nᵀ and t = 2·d·n, exact for the
   three named planes and rational for a written normal whose squared length is a rational square. Image 0 is the
   identity; image 1 applies the reflection table below and negates parity.
5. `radial(n, axis, sub)`: image k is the rotation by k/n turn about the axis line, exact in ℚ(ζ_n) (entries are
   cosines and sines of rational turns). Suffixes `0 … n−1`.
6. `scale(s, sub)`: one image with scale s. Every quantity of dimension length is multiplied by s (Region
   dimensions, port and mount offsets, joint origins' translations, prismatic limits, screw pitches, shell
   thicknesses, torsion radii). Angles, ratios, materials, cards and revolute limits are unchanged. Mass is not
   scaled: it is recomputed from the scaled Region, fill and material. A body with `declared` mass properties
   keeps them, the mass-support check then runs on the scaled Region, and the result carries the warning
   `scale-left-declared-mass`.
7. `replace(path, with, sub)`: substitution on the subterm, then expansion. The path must resolve inside `of` and the
   replaced node must be of the same component kind (`FROB-REF-103` if nothing is named; `FROB-REF-101` on a kind
   or port-signature mismatch).
8. `closure(a, b)`: no image; a `ClosurePair` with its joint. Both ports must be mechanical (`FROB-REF-101`).

**The reflection table** (what image 1 of a `mirror` does; x ↦ P·x + t, det P = −1).

| Quantity | Mapped to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A body frame (R, p) | (P·R·P, P·p + t) | A body point y maps to (P·R·P)·(P·y) + (P·p + t): the frame stays right-handed and the body-fixed geometry is reflected |
| Region geometry, ports, mounts in the body frame | y ↦ P·y; a mesh also flips triangle winding | Polar |
| A revolute or helical axis a | det(P)·P·a = −P·a, with the **same** coordinate, limits, encoder sign and gear sign | Axial; the image shares its original's kernels |
| A screw pitch h | −h | From v′ = P·(h·a + r × a) + t × ω′ with ω′ = −P·a: v′ = −h·ω′ + (P·r + t) × ω′ |
| A prismatic direction | P·d | Polar |
| A pseudo-inertia J (declared bodies) | G·J·Gᵀ, G = [[P, t], [0, 1]] | The second moment is polar: no parity sign |
| A twist (ω, v) | (det(P)·P·ω, P·v + t × det(P)·P·ω) | |
| A wrench (τ, f) | (det(P)·P·τ + t × P·f, P·f) | |
| Anisotropic friction axes, roller angles | P·d; a roller angle changes sign | A mecanum roller at +45° mirrors to −45° |

**Instance-identifier suffixes** (the rule the driver reads). A `mirror` contributes one letter chosen by the side of
the plane on which the image's attach port lies: plane `yz` gives `f` (x > 0) or `r` (x < 0); plane `xz` gives `l`
(y > 0) or `r` (y < 0); plane `xy` gives `u` or `d`; a written plane gives `a` or `b`. Letters of directly nested
mirrors are concatenated in the fixed order x-letter, y-letter, z-letter into one segment, so two nested mirrors of
`leg` give `leg.fl`, `leg.fr`, `leg.rl`, `leg.rr`. `serial` and `radial` contribute the image index as a segment.
`attach`, `chain`, `scale`, `replace` and `closure` contribute nothing. A limb whose attach port lies exactly on its
mirror plane would produce two coincident images and is refused (`FROB-REF-123`, repair: `move-mount` or remove the
`mirror`).

**Refusals** (exit code 3, raised when the constructor is read or expanded).

| Code | Meaning | Trigger here | Ranked repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-101` | Port type mismatch | `attach` or `closure` names a missing or non-mechanical port; `replace` changes a component kind | `move-mount` to an existing port; the list of ports |
| `FROB-REF-102` | Unit or dimension error | A `scale` factor that is zero, negative, not exact or not dimensionless; a plane normal of zero length | Write a positive exact factor |
| `FROB-REF-103` | Unknown, misspelt or duplicate field | An unknown constructor key; `serial` with k < 1; `radial` with n < 2; a `replace` path that names nothing; a missing `of` | The nearest valid spelling or path |
| `FROB-REF-123` | Interpenetration at the reference pose | A mirrored limb mounted on its mirror plane | `move-mount`; drop the `mirror` |

Determinism: exact arithmetic throughout, class D0; identical output on Linux and macOS. Evidence: structural facts
of class `Certified` by construction are not claimed here; the equality test below is `Tested`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The eight constructors parse from and write to the canonical objects above; an unknown key refuses
      (`FROB-REF-103`).
- [ ] Canonical bytes of the worked quadruped contain both `mirror` keys and one `chain`; no expanded limb appears
      in the bytes; canonicalizing twice gives equal bytes (seed condition: constructor terms stay unexpanded).
- [ ] The design built with two nested `mirror` constructors and its hand-expanded form (four explicit `attach`
      limbs written with the reflection table) elaborate to instance trees that are equal up to identifiers and
      source paths, and compile to models whose link placements, screw axes and inertias are bit-identical (seed
      condition: `mirror` equals its hand-expanded form). Their `DesignId`s differ.
- [ ] The three planted wrong reflections are each caught by that comparison (see controls).
- [ ] `radial(4, z, limb)` equals `mirror`-free hand expansion at 0, ¼, ½, ¾ turn exactly.
- [ ] `scale("2", sub)` multiplies a solid body's mass by exactly 8 and a 2 mm shell becomes 4 mm.
- [ ] Each refusal in the table is produced by its planted term with its code, the path and at least one repair.
- [ ] `crates/frob-design/CONTRACT.md` lists the constructors, the reflection table and the suffix rule.
- [ ] Clauses of the definition of implemented that stay open after this bead: reachability through `frob --json`
      (through `design.check` and `design.view`, other beads) and the gate record (GATE-SKEL-03 closes later).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-design/tests/constructors.rs`; real terms, no mocks.
- `spell_round_trip_all_eight`: each constructor parses and writes byte-identically.
- `mirror_images_xz`: P = diag(1, −1, 1), t = 0; port (0.19, 0.08, 0) m maps to (0.19, −0.08, 0) m.
- `mirror_axis_is_axial`: hip axis (1, 0, 0) under the `xz` mirror maps to −P·a = (−1, 0, 0); knee axis (0, 1, 0)
  maps to (0, 1, 0); coordinate, limits and gear sign unchanged.
- `mirror_pitch_negates`: a helical joint with pitch 2 mm maps to −2 mm.
- `mirror_pseudo_inertia_no_parity`: a declared inertia with product I_xy = 0.01 kg·m² maps to −0.01 under `xz`
  (from G·J·Gᵀ), and its eigenvalues are unchanged.
- `mirror_equals_hand_expanded_quad` (the seed known answer).
- `suffix_rule_nested_mirrors`: `fl`, `fr`, `rl`, `rr`; `serial(3)` gives `0`, `1`, `2`.
- `radial_exact_quarter_turns`; `radial_5_closes`: five images of a point at radius 1 sum to zero exactly in ℚ(ζ₅).
- `scale_lengths_only`: lengths doubled, angles and ratios unchanged, mass × 8, warning on a declared body.
- `replace_before_expand`: replacing the shank under two mirrors changes all four images.
- `refuse_{bad_port, zero_scale, serial_zero, radial_one, replace_nothing, mount_on_plane}`.
- Property test (10⁴ designs from the generator of N14b-01): expansion is deterministic; composing `mirror` twice
  across the same plane yields images pairwise coincident with the originals (P² = I).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/design_constructors.sh` (sources the shared helper): `frob design check fixtures/quad-a.fbot --json`
and the same for `quad-a-expanded.fbot`; `frob design view quad-a --as json` (asserts `mirror` present in the
bytes and the `DesignId` reproduced); compares the two compiled model digests of mechanical content; runs the six
refusal fixtures and asserts code and exit status 3. Log lines per the logging standard: `cmd`, exit code, wall
time, the envelope's evidence block, `assert` with both digests and, on mismatch, the first differing instance and
field, `refusal` with code and repair; a `summary` line with counts.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answer (registered in the Gauntlet's suite under construction C-12): a design built with `mirror` equals its
  hand-expanded form. Expected: equal mechanical model content, exact. Tier G1, host.
- Negative controls (planted mutants in `tests/support/constructor_mutants.rs`): (a) the axis mapped by P·a with the
  coordinate unchanged: forward kinematics of the right legs differs from the hand-expanded form; (b) a parity sign
  applied to the pseudo-inertia: products of inertia have the wrong sign; (c) a canonical writer that expands
  constructors: bytes and `DesignId` differ from the golden file.
- Metamorphic: mirroring twice is the identity on images; `radial(2, z, ·)` equals a half-turn, which differs from
  `mirror` by parity (the test asserts they are **not** equal for a chiral limb).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; debug and release; Linux and macOS, all byte-identical.

## Considerations

- The reflection table is the single place where chirality is handled; the symmetry code (N13-15) and the kinematic
  functor (BD-061) consume its output and must not re-derive it.
- `radial` with n above 12 is symmetric by construction; exact field arithmetic for symmetry *detection* is not
  extended past 12 (OPEN-MARROW-2, default no), which does not limit expansion.
- A `scale` under which a shell becomes thicker than half the Region's smallest dimension is caught by the fill
  rule (`FROB-REF-105`), not here.
- Expansion size is bounded by the driver (4,096 instances, TARGET); `serial` with a huge k must fail there, not
  allocate here: `images` is lazy over k.
- What would make us revisit: a kernel that needs the polar-axis form, or a module format that needs chirality
  flags on ports.
