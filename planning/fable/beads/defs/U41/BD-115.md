## Background

Three parts of the system pose small dense conic programs with tens to a few hundred variables: the contact solve
(a convex compliant problem over friction cones), the baseline controllers (force distribution over contacts,
whole-body inverse dynamics, short predictive programs) and the mission engine (linear and second-order relaxations
whose infeasibility excludes a design). The design decision (DESIGN, construction C-06) is one `Cone` abstraction
and **one cone solver**, provided by `frob-algebra` (Marrow, layer 1, tier Solid), instead of one solver per
consumer. Reason: the nonnegative orthant (limits), the second-order cone (friction; the four-dimensional contact
cone is a second-order cone after scaling the tangential and spin components by the friction coefficient and the
effective patch radius) and the semidefinite cone are all symmetric cones, so one interior-point method with one
scaling serves all, and one certificate format serves every consumer.

The second decision is about honesty of outcomes. A solver that returns "no solution" when it merely failed to
converge turns a numerical accident into a statement about the robot. The solver therefore ends in exactly one of
three states per program, and the caller must inspect which:

| State | Meaning | What is returned |
|---|---|---|
| `optimal` | Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity and stationarity all within tolerance | The solution and the four residuals |
| `infeasible` | A dual certificate was found and checked exactly | A rational vector y with Aᵀy in the dual cone K* and bᵀy < 0 |
| `unconverged` | Neither of the above within the iteration budget | The last iterate, marked unusable; the caller takes its fallback |

For "find x in K with A·x = b", a vector y with Aᵀy ∈ K* and bᵀy < 0 proves that no solution exists, because
0 ≤ ⟨Aᵀy, x⟩ = yᵀb < 0 would follow. The certificate is rounded to rationals and re-checked exactly (proof
obligation PO-REFLEX-03), which is what makes an infeasibility report class `Certified` for the stated program.

Rejected alternatives: projected Gauss–Seidel with softness knobs (no residual that bounds the error, no
certificate); a first-order splitting method (its infeasibility detection is asymptotic and its certificates are
too inexact to round); a general third-party solver linked into the product (the suite admits no such dependency,
and a reference optimizer is used out of process as an oracle only).

## Scope

In: the standard form and its lowering helpers; the interior-point iteration; the residual definitions; the
fixed-budget lockstep batch interface; the certificate search, rational rounding and exact check; the must-inspect
outcome type; the test corpus of 10³ programs. Out: the `Cone` trait and projections (BD-050); the control-side
program builder, fallbacks and the physical reading of a certificate (N22a-08); batched cone kernels of the contact
solve (BD-153, BD-156); exact basis reconstruction for linear programs of mission work (N25a-06) and the second

## Technical approach

Module (DESIGN): `crates/frob-algebra/src/conic/{form.rs, lower.rs, ipm.rs, residual.rs, certify.rs, batch.rs}`.

**Standard form.** minimize cᵀx subject to A·x = b, x ∈ K = K₁ × … × K_p, with A ∈ ℝ^{m×n} dense of full row rank
(a rank-deficient A is reduced by a rank-revealing elimination first, and a contradiction found there is itself an
infeasibility certificate), and each K_i a nonnegative orthant, a second-order cone {x₀ ≥ ‖x̄‖}, or a friction
cone 𝒦_μ = {‖x̄‖ ≤ μ·x₀} handled as a linear image of the second-order cone. The dual of 𝒦_μ is 𝒦_{1/μ}. Lowering
helpers turn inequalities, bounds, free variables and a convex quadratic objective ½·xᵀP·x (P = LᵀL, through one
rotated second-order cone) into this form; each helper returns the map back to the caller's variables.

**Iteration** (DESIGN; a primal–dual interior-point method on the homogeneous self-dual embedding).

1. Embedding: seek (x, y, s, τ, κ) with Aᵀy + s − c·τ = 0, A·x − b·τ = 0, −cᵀx + bᵀy − κ = 0, x ∈ K, s ∈ K*,
   τ ≥ 0, κ ≥ 0. Start at x = s = e (the identity element of the Jordan algebra of K), y = 0, τ = κ = 1.
2. Each iteration: compute the Nesterov–Todd scaling W with W·x = W⁻¹·s; form the m×m normal matrix
   A·W⁻²·Aᵀ and factor it by dense Cholesky after diagonal scaling; take a predictor step, choose the centering
   parameter σ = (1 − α_aff)³, take the corrector step; step length is the largest α ≤ 1 keeping (x, s) in the
   interior, times 0.99. All per-cone operations (Jordan product, spectral values, inverse, scaling) come from the
   `Cone` trait.
3. Fixed budget: N_it iterations (default 30 in `f64`, 20 in `f32`); no data-dependent exit. A program that meets
   its stopping test early is frozen under a `done` mask: later iterations are computed and discarded by a select.
4. Classification after the budget, from the final iterate:
   - in `f32`, the condition estimate of the diagonally scaled normal matrix (not the embedding's κ) times
     u = 2⁻²⁴ above the admission bound `Options::b_adm` (acceptance criteria): `unconverged` with reason
     `conditioning`, whatever the residuals.
   - τ > κ and the four residuals below are within tolerance: `optimal`, with (x, y, s)/τ.
   - κ > τ and bᵀy > 0: candidate primal infeasibility; the certificate in the convention above is ŷ = −y/(bᵀy), so
     that bᵀŷ = −1 and Aᵀŷ ≈ s/(bᵀy) ∈ K*. Go to certification.
   - κ > τ and cᵀx < 0 (a ray along which the objective falls without bound): `unconverged` with reason
     `unbounded-ray` and the ray; the three states are closed, and no consumer of this solver poses a program that
     is meant to be unbounded.
   - otherwise `unconverged` with reason `budget`.

**The four residuals** (returned with every `optimal`, each relative):
r_prim = max(‖A·x − b‖/(1 + ‖b‖), viol_K(x)); r_dual = viol_{K*}(s); r_comp = |xᵀs|/(1 + |cᵀx|);
r_stat = ‖Aᵀy + s − c‖/(1 + ‖c‖), where viol is the magnitude of the most negative spectral value relative to the
vector's norm. Tolerance (DESIGN defaults, recorded in every result): 10⁻⁸ in `f64`, 10⁻⁴ in `f32`.

**Certification.** (a) Read A and b as the exact rationals their floating values denote (every finite float is a
dyadic rational), so the certificate is about the stated program. (b) Round ŷ to rationals with denominators 2ᵏ
for k = 8, 12, 16, …, 40, after first moving it toward the interior of the feasible certificate set by the small
multiple of the embedding's central direction that keeps bᵀŷ < 0. (c) For each rounding check exactly: for an
orthant block every component of Aᵀy is ≥ 0; for a second-order block z = (z₀, z̄), z₀ ≥ 0 and z₀² ≥ z̄ᵀz̄; for a
friction block with coefficient μ (a rational), z₀ ≥ 0 and z₀² ≥ μ²·z̄ᵀz̄; and bᵀy < 0 strictly. (d) The first
rounding that passes is returned as `infeasible` with the rational y, the block list and the exact value of bᵀy.
(e) If none passes, the state is `unconverged` with reason `certificate-not-exact`. Only a strict, exactly checked
witness yields `infeasible`.

**Types** (DESIGN).

```rust
#[must_use]
pub enum Outcome<S> { Optimal { x: Vec<S>, y: Vec<S>, s: Vec<S>, residuals: Residuals<S> },
                      Infeasible { certificate: FarkasCert },          // rational y, exact bᵀy, cone blocks
                      Unconverged { last: Unusable<Iterate<S>>, reason: Why } }
pub fn solve<S: Real>(p: &Program<S>, o: &Options) -> Outcome<S>;
pub fn solve_batch<S: Real>(ps: &BatchProgram<S>, o: &Options) -> Vec<Outcome<S>>;   // one shape, lockstep
pub fn check_exact(p: &ProgramQ, c: &FarkasCert) -> bool;                             // rationals only
```

`Unusable<T>` has no accessor that yields numbers without the caller naming the fallback it is taking. The batch
form takes programs of one shape (m, n, cone layout) in structure-of-arrays layout and runs every program for the
same N_it, so that a kernel derived from it has a static launch graph and the iteration count is part of the
identity of every result. Determinism: D2 for the floating iteration (fixed operation order, no fused
multiply-add, no built-in transcendental; `sqrt` only); D0 for the exact check. The library is silent: it returns
values and typed errors and prints nothing. The verb-level refusal for a control program with no fallback
(`FROB-REF-424`) is raised by the control layer, which reads the certificate.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] On the corpus of 10³ programs every `optimal` outcome has its four residuals within tolerance (seed
      condition: KKT residuals), and status and optimal value agree with the pinned reference optimizer: equal
      status on all 10³, objective within 10⁻⁷ relative in `f64` (seed condition).
- [ ] The planted infeasible programs return `infeasible` with a certificate that `check_exact` accepts (seed
      condition), and none returns `unconverged` with reason `budget`.
- [ ] The hand case is reproduced: weight 50 N, required push 30 N, one contact with μ = 0.5, unknown (fₙ, fₜ) fixed
      at (50, 30): `infeasible`; the vector y = (0.5, −1) passes `check_exact` with bᵀy = −5 exactly.
- [ ] A certificate nudged outside the dual cone, (0.5 − 10⁻¹², −1), fails `check_exact`.
- [ ] A program forced to stop after two iterations returns `unconverged`, never `infeasible` and never a usable
      solution (counterexample CX-235).
- [ ] `solve_batch` on 4,096 programs of one shape equals 4,096 calls of `solve` bit for bit, and is bit-identical
      across worker counts.
- [ ] The `f32` path meets its 10⁻⁴ tolerance on the corpus programs whose scaled condition estimate is below the
      single-precision admission bound; the rest are reported `unconverged` with reason `conditioning`, not solved
      loosely. The bound is the one the contact solve uses: κ·u ≤ b_adm, with κ the condition estimate of the
      diagonally scaled normal matrix A·W⁻²·Aᵀ at the final iterate and u = 2⁻²⁴ the unit round-off of `f32`.
      b_adm is a field of `Options` that the caller supplies, never a literal in the solver: the corpus test passes
      0.05, the fail-closed default of OPEN-TRAC-4, and the contact layer passes the one registry constant that
      N18b-26 owns (a later bead that depends on this one; GATE-TRAC-10 decides its value).
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-algebra` documents the form, the three states, the residuals, tolerances, the
      iteration budget and the certificate format.
- [ ] The law suite of SPEC-C-06 (`crates/frob-algebra/tests/laws_c06.rs`) runs unchanged against this solver for
      the laws that name the cone program, bound through the suite's subject trait; the exact check of a dual
      certificate is the instance check stated by theorem card T-21 (CARD-T-21), whose statement this bead cites
      and does not restate differently.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-algebra/tests/conic.rs`; real arithmetic, no mocks; seeds explicit.
- `lp_two_var_known_optimum`: minimize −x₁ − x₂ subject to x₁ + 2x₂ + s₁ = 4, 3x₁ + x₂ + s₂ = 6, all ≥ 0: optimum at
  (1.6, 1.2), value −2.8.
- `soc_min_norm_known`: minimize t subject to ‖(x₁, x₂)‖ ≤ t, x₁ + x₂ = 2: t = √2 at (1, 1).
- `friction_force_distribution_two_contacts`: weight 100 N on two contacts at x = ±0.2 m about the centre of mass,
  μ = 0.5, force and moment balance, objective ½·‖f‖² through the quadratic lowering helper: the unique optimum is
  50 N normal at each contact and zero tangential (with a linear "minimal normal sum" objective every split of the
  100 N would be optimal and the expected value would not be determined).
- `friction_certificate_hand_case` (KA-556) and `nudged_certificate_rejected`.
- `false_certificate_mu_2_rejected`: friction block with μ = 2, b = (1, 1.5) (feasible: 1.5 ≤ 2·1); `check_exact`
  rejects y = (1, −1) (it needs 1 ≥ 2·|−1|) and `solve` returns `optimal`.
- `planted_farkas_100`: programs built from a random rational y and a matrix with Aᵀy in the interior of K*, b
  chosen with bᵀy = −1: all return `infeasible` and check exactly.
- `unconverged_is_not_infeasible`; `unusable_iterate_needs_named_fallback` (compile-fail test).
- `residual_definitions`: a hand-built point with a known violation of each of the four conditions reports it.
- `batch_equals_scalar_bitwise`; `rank_deficient_rows_contradiction_is_certificate`.
- Property tests: for feasible random programs strong duality gap ≤ tolerance; scaling (A, b) by a positive
  constant leaves the status unchanged; permuting cone blocks permutes the solution.
- `kkt_and_reference_agreement_corpus`: the 10³ programs (400 linear, 400 second-order force-distribution shapes
  with 2 to 8 contacts, 100 mixed with bounds, 100 planted infeasible; 4 to 200 variables) against the pinned
  fixture file of the reference optimizer.

### End-to-end script and logging
No verb is defined on the solver. `scripts/e2e/marrow_cone_solver.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper) runs `cargo
test -p frob-algebra --test conic` in debug and release, twice each, and compares the digests of all 10³ outcomes
(status, rational certificates, residuals as bit patterns) across the four runs. Log per the standard: per program
family a `metric` line with the worst of each residual against its tolerance, counts of the three states, the
agreement count against the reference with the first disagreeing program's digest on failure, `assert` lines for
each control, the outcome digests, and a `summary`. The offline audit of solver certificates through `frob-verify`
is not this bead's: control certificates are audited by BD-251 (kind `lp-farkas`, M3) and, once the `conic-farkas`
kind is admitted, by N12-16 (M4); both are later than this bead and depend on it, so no step here waits on them or
is skipped.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-556 (friction certificate, exact, class `Certified`; control: the nudged certificate). KA-083 (KKT residuals
  of every control program inside tolerance; tolerance set here). KA-056 is used through the cone laws.
- CX-235: an unconverged run proves nothing; the planted two-iteration run must come back `unconverged`.
- Mutants: a certificate check that accepts bᵀy ≤ 0 (caught by a program with bᵀy = 0 that is feasible); a dual
  cone taken as 𝒦_μ instead of 𝒦_{1/μ}: for μ = 0.5 it is stricter than the true dual and rejects the hand-case
  certificate (caught by `friction_certificate_hand_case`), and for μ = 2 it is looser and accepts a false
  certificate on a feasible fixture (b = (1, 1.5), which lies in 𝒦₂; y = (1, −1) has bᵀy = −0.5 and lies in 𝒦₂
  but not in the true dual 𝒦_{1/2}; caught by `false_certificate_mu_2_rejected`); a classification that maps
  `budget` to `infeasible`.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; `f32` and `f64` each against
  its tolerance; the exact check byte-identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- The reference optimizer is an oracle outside the product, run once by a capture script and pinned; its fixture is
  never regenerated from this solver.
- Semidefinite blocks are not in the M1 solver; the `Cone` trait has a small host instance, and adding it here is a
  scope conversation.
- Whether linear programs of mission work are solved here in floating point with rational reconstruction or
  exactly over ℚ is an open question decided from the reconstruction failure rate (OPEN-AUGUR-2; default: floating
  point here, exact reconstruction and check downstream; a failed reconstruction leaves the cell unresolved).
- The iteration budget and tolerances enter the identity of every result that used the solver; changing a default
  is a contract change.
- Performance envelope: one solve at n = 200 is a few hundred microseconds on one core (TARGET, to be measured);
  nothing here exceeds the default machine envelope.
