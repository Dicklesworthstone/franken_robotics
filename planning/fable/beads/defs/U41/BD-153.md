## Background

The batched step (tiers `batch-cpu` and the GPU tiers under the `lockstep` profile) runs ten stages in a fixed
order, each a named kernel or a short chain, batched over worlds with no data-dependent control flow across
worlds. Stage 7 builds constraint rows; stage 8 solves a strictly convex problem in generalized velocities by
Newton's method,

  v = argmin ½·(v − v*)ᵀ·A·(v − v*) + Σ_c ℓ_c(J_c·v),

where v* is the unconstrained velocity, A the mass matrix with implicit terms, J_c the Jacobian rows of contact c
and ℓ_c the convex potential of the contact law; minus the gradient of ℓ_c is the contact impulse γ_c. This bead
supplies the three kernels through which contacts enter that problem under the default law **CL-1** (linear
compliant normal with a dissipation time; regularized Coulomb friction with a stated stiction speed):

- `K-jac-rows` (stage 7): the Jacobian rows of every active contact;
- `K-cone-setup` (stage 7): the per-contact constants of the law for this step;
- `K-cone-eval` (stage 8, called inside every Newton and line-search iteration): impulse, potential value and
  Hessian block for the current velocity.

Tier Solid; crate `frob-kernels` (the kernels) with the law's reference in `frob-contact`. Why a convex compliant
law in this form (DESIGN, construction C-06): the solve has one solution, its gradient norm bounds the distance to
it (solver error is a recorded number per step), its parameters are physical (stiffness, dissipation time,
friction coefficient), and it runs in lockstep with a static launch graph. Its known artifacts (bounded creep of a
stuck contact, a separation offset when sliding, energy stored in compliance) are named and measured elsewhere.
Rejected: a pyramid approximation of the cone (it admits 41% of traction that does not exist along the diagonals)
and projected Gauss–Seidel with softness knobs.

## Scope

In: the three kernels for three rows per contact (normal and two tangential) in `f64` and `f32` on `batch-cpu`,
written once in the kernel IR and emitted as Rust and as dialect source; their manifests and stage signatures;
masks and padding; the KKT and projection tests. Out: the fourth (spin) row and anisotropic cones (BD-154); joint
rows (BD-155); scaling, Hessian assembly, Cholesky and line search (BD-156); the law's derivation and artifacts
(N18a-16); film friction (`K-film`); device parity runs (the Spark check).

## Technical approach

**Data per contact** (read from the compacted contact buffer; capacities fixed at environment creation, C contacts
and R rows per world): link indices (a, b) with b = none for the static scene; world contact point c; contact frame
(n, t₁, t₂) with n pointing from b to a; signed gap g₀ at the start of the step (negative in penetration);
interface parameters from the card of the material pair and condition: stiffness k (N/m), dissipation time τ_d (s),
friction coefficient μ, stiction speed v_s (m/s), nominal load N_nom (N); the last committed normal impulse γ̄ₙ; a
mask bit.

**`K-jac-rows`.** For each active contact and each degree of freedom j on the support path of link a (and of link b
with the opposite sign), with world-frame joint screw (ω_j, v_j) taken about the world origin from the kinematics
stage: the 3 × 1 column is Cᵀ·(v_j + ω_j × c), C = [n t₁ t₂]. The contact velocity is u = J_c·v = (uₙ, u_t1, u_t2),
positive uₙ separating. Storage is a parameter (`dense`: R × n_v; `path-table`: about R × (6 + depth)), which
changes the write footprint only. Entries off the support path are never computed as 0·x: they are written as the
canonical zero by the store, so a padded column cannot inject a signed zero into a sum.

**`K-cone-setup`** (once per step and contact, from committed state, so the potential stays convex inside the
solve):

1. R_n = 1/(h·k·(h + τ_d)); R_t = v_s/(μ·max(γ̄ₙ, h·N_nom)); both in (m/s)/(N·s).
2. Bias velocity v̂ₙ = −g₀/(h + τ_d).
3. μ̂ = μ·R_t/R_n and μ̃² = μ²·R_t/R_n.
A state-dependent μ (film, speed, temperature) is evaluated before this kernel and is constant within the solve.

**`K-cone-eval`** (for a given u):

1. Unprojected impulse y = −R⁻¹·(u − v̂), with normal part yₙ and tangential magnitude y_r = ‖y_T‖ (`sqrt` is
   correctly rounded and allowed; no reciprocal-square-root built-in).
2. Region, by comparisons that feed selects, never branches across worlds:
   stick when y_r ≤ μ·yₙ; open when yₙ + μ̂·y_r ≤ 0; slide otherwise.
3. Impulse: stick γ = y; open γ = 0; slide γₙ = (yₙ + μ̂·y_r)/(1 + μ̃²) and γ_T = μ·γₙ·y_T/y_r (the division is
   guarded by the select: in slide y_r > 0).
4. Potential ℓ_c = ½·γᵀ·R·γ. Hessian block G = −∂γ/∂u (3 × 3, symmetric positive semidefinite): R⁻¹ in stick, 0
   when open, and in slide the matrix derived at build time from step 3 (generated through the kernel IR with
   common subexpressions shared with γ).
5. A padded or masked contact outputs γ = 0, ℓ_c = 0, G = 0 by select. Arithmetic masks are forbidden (0·∞ is NaN).

γ is the projection of y onto 𝒦_μ = {‖γ_T‖ ≤ μ·γₙ} in the norm xᵀ·R·x. In stick γₙ = −h·k·(g₀ + (h + τ_d)·uₙ),
which is h times the Kelvin–Voigt force at the end-of-step gap.

**Manifests** (one per kernel; schema of the kernel library): class floating; determinism D2; inputs and outputs
with shapes in terms of C, R, n_v and physical dimensions (checked by the IR's dimension checker); read and write
footprints per buffer; no aliasing; neutral element for padding (−0.0 for sums; masks for everything else); the
symbol of the CPU reference (`frob_contact::cl1::reference`). The bans of the determinism rules apply and are
linted: no fast-math, no fused multiply-add, no built-in transcendental, no floating-point atomic, no strict
predicate decided in bare floating point where a decision crosses the agent boundary (region tests here select
between continuous branches, so a tie changes no value).

**Identity.** Kernel versions, the contact law and the Jacobian store enter the `StepPlanId`; any change to the
order of operations here changes kernel digests and is a breaking change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Projection tests (seed condition): on 10⁶ random (y, R, μ) per precision plus the boundary set (apex, the two
      region boundaries, y_T = 0, magnitudes from 10⁻²⁰ to 10²⁰) the output of `K-cone-eval` satisfies γ ∈ 𝒦_μ,
      R·(γ − y) ∈ 𝒦_{1/μ} and ⟨γ, R·(γ − y)⟩ = 0, within 64 ulp-equivalents in `f64` and 16 in `f32`; γ is
      continuous across both region boundaries.
- [ ] KKT tests (seed condition): on 10⁴ small worlds (1 to 8 contacts, 6 to 18 degrees of freedom) solved by a
      host Newton reference to 10⁻¹², the kernels' outputs give a stationarity residual
      ‖A·(v − v*) − Σ J_cᵀ·γ_c‖ ≤ 10⁻⁹·(1 + ‖A·v*‖) in `f64`; G equals a central difference of −γ in u within
      10⁻⁶ and has no eigenvalue below −10⁻¹²·‖G‖.
- [ ] `K-jac-rows` agrees with a finite difference of the contact-point velocity map to 10⁻⁷ on 10⁴ random
      configurations of the reference quadruped, and with the host reference bit for bit in `f64`.
- [ ] The kernels reproduce the CPU reference of the law bit for bit in `f32` and `f64` on `batch-cpu`.
- [ ] Hand cases hold (unit tests below), including the resting penetration of 98.1 µm for 1 kg on 10⁵ N/m.
- [ ] Bitwise equality across worker counts and under padding: adding padded contacts, raising C, or adding
      co-tenant worlds changes no output bit of a real contact.
- [ ] The three planted mutants are caught (controls below).
- [ ] Manifests validate; the dialect lint passes on the emitted sources; a manifest whose write footprint omits
      the Hessian block buffer is refused by the harness.
- [ ] Open clauses of the definition of implemented: the device differential check, reachability through a verb
      (through `sim.run` on a batched tier) and the gate records (GATE-TRAC-04 with the four-row cone, GATE-KIN-03).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-kernels/tests/cone_kernels.rs` and `crates/frob-contact/tests/cl1_reference.rs`; no mocks.
- `slide_euclidean_known`: R = I, μ = 0.5, y = (0, 1, 0): γ = (0.4, 0.2, 0).
- `slide_weighted_known`: R = diag(1, 4, 4), μ = 0.5, y = (1, 3, 0): μ̂ = 2, μ̃² = 1, γ = (3.5, 1.75, 0);
  R·(γ − y) = (2.5, −5, 0) lies on the boundary of 𝒦₂ and its pairing with γ is 8.75 − 8.75 = 0.
- `stick_is_identity`: y = (2, 0.5, 0.5), μ = 0.5: γ = y, G = R⁻¹.
- `open_is_zero`: y = (−1, 0.1, 0): γ = 0, G = 0.
- `setup_constants`: h = 2 ms, k = 10⁵ N/m, τ_d = 4 ms, g₀ = −60 µm, μ = 0.8, v_s = 1 mm/s, γ̄ₙ = 0.02 N·s:
  R_n = 1/(0.002 × 10⁵ × 0.006) = 0.8333…, v̂ₙ = 0.01 m/s, R_t = 0.001/0.016 = 0.0625.
- `resting_mass_penetration`: 1 kg point mass, k = 10⁵ N/m at equilibrium: g₀ = −98.1 µm and γₙ = h·9.81 N·s.
- `jac_rows_revolute_known`: one revolute joint about z through the origin, contact at (1, 0, 0) m with n = y:
  the point velocity per unit joint rate is (0, 1, 0) m/s, so the normal entry is 1 and both tangential entries
  (t₁ = x, t₂ = z) are 0.
- `masked_contact_outputs_zero_by_select`: an input with u = ∞ under mask 0 yields zeros, not NaN.
- Property tests: the three projection conditions; G positive semidefinite; ℓ_c convex along random segments;
  scale invariance γ(t·y) = t·γ(y) for t > 0 at fixed R.
- `kkt_small_worlds`; `padding_and_worker_count_bitwise`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/cone_kernels_cl1.sh`: runs the kernel tests in debug and release, twice; then, once the batched step
exists, `frob sim run --tier batch-cpu --profile lockstep --task fixtures/block-on-incline.ftask --seed 7 --json`
at 4, 8 and 16 workers and compares state digests (logged as skipped before the step lands). Logs: `metric` lines
with the worst projection-condition error, the worst KKT residual and their thresholds; digests per worker count;
`assert` lines per mutant naming the test that caught it; a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-056 (projection identities of the friction cone, here in the R metric), KA-055 (the pyramid point
  (μ·γₙ, μ·γₙ) is outside the cone: a pyramid mutant of `K-cone-eval` is caught by the membership clause).
- Mutants: (a) slide computed with μ in place of μ̂ when R_t ≠ R_n (fails orthogonality on the weighted case);
  (b) an arithmetic mask (multiplying by 0) instead of a select (NaN on the masked-∞ case); (c) the Jacobian sign
  of body b not negated (KKT residual nonzero on a two-body contact).
- Metamorphic: rotating the tangent basis about n rotates γ_T and leaves γₙ and ℓ_c unchanged; exchanging bodies a
  and b with n reversed leaves the impulse pair unchanged.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; `f32` and `f64`; device
  families later, each against its class.

## Considerations

- R_t depends on the last committed normal impulse, floored by h·N_nom; a first-touch contact uses the floor, which
  is why N_nom must be on the card (no silent default).
- In `f32` the product h·k·(h + τ_d) and the ratio R_t/R_n span many orders of magnitude for stiff contacts; the
  admission bound on scaled stiffness and the diagonal scaling of the solve (BD-156) keep this kernel inside its
  precision; rows over the bound mark the world `Degraded: ill-conditioned` rather than computing loosely.
- The region tests choose between branches that agree on the boundary, so no strict predicate decides a value;
  mode labels reported to the fidelity ledger (stick, slide, open) are diagnostics, not verdicts.
- What would make us revisit: CL-2 (Hunt–Crossley) entering as a second potential; it reuses `K-jac-rows` and
  replaces the other two.
