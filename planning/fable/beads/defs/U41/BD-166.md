## Background

Everything static in a scene is a signed-distance field, because a field answers "how far, and which way" in
constant time for any query point, which is what thousands of worlds need. Everything that moves is a union of
convex primitives carrying **sample spheres** (a foot has four to eight; a wheel has rim samples chosen by
curvature). Stage 4 of the batched step, the narrow phase, tests samples against the scene. This bead delivers its
two scene-facing kernels in `frob-kernels`, with their references in `frob-contact` (Traction, tier Solid):

- `K-np-sdf`: each sample sphere against the scene's distance field, read through the **pooled directory** and
  combined with **analytic overlays**;
- `K-np-thin`: each sample against **thin primitives**, the geometry a sampled field cannot hold.

Three design facts shape them. (1) One scene per world is impossible: 8,192 worlds each with its own 2 cm home is
614 GB. Bricks are therefore content-addressed and shared in a pool; a scene variant is a directory and a coarse
lattice, and a world holds a 16-bit variant index. (2) A feature smaller than a voxel is an analytic overlay, never
a voxel (ADR-HAB-02): thresholds, rug edges, grates and lips are exact primitives listed per index cell, and the
scene distance is the minimum of the interpolated brick value and the exact overlay distances. (3) A trilinear
field on a 2 cm grid cannot hold a 2 mm plate: the plate vanishes or is thickened to the grid, and an endpoint
test steps over it. Thin features are routed by the scene compiler to thin primitives (plates as boxes, cords and
wires as capsule chains, rails as cylinders) evaluated analytically on every tier.

Field sampling is manual trilinear interpolation in kernel source, not hardware texture filtering, because
fixed-point filter weights differ by vendor (ADR-HAB-01). Rejected: one dense field per world (memory); voxelizing
thin features at a finer level everywhere (a 1 cm warehouse is 38 GB); endpoint-only tests for thin geometry (the
counterexample below).

## Technical approach

**Field layout read by `K-np-sdf`** (DESIGN defaults of the scene pack). Coarse lattice of spacing S = 16 cm over
the whole scene; fine level of spacing s = S/8 = 2 cm in band bricks of 8³ samples for each coarse cell within the
truncation distance of a surface. Stored values are clamp(φ, −T, +T) quantized to 16 bits with T = 4 fine voxels
(8 cm), a step of 0.16 m/65,535 ≈ 2.4 µm. A per-variant directory holds one 32-bit pool slot per coarse cell, with
two sentinel slots for "far outside" and "deep inside", and one 16-bit coarse sample per cell. A band brick carries
a palette of at most four region identifiers and a two-bit selector per sample.

**`K-np-sdf`, per world and sample** (centre c in cell-relative anchored coordinates, radius r, margin m):

1. Map c to the scene's coarse cell (i, j, k) and the fine fractional position inside it, in integer arithmetic on
   the cell index plus the `f32` offset; a point outside the scene bounds reads the "far outside" sentinel.
2. Look up `slot = directory[variant][i, j, k]`. Sentinel: φ_b is the coarse-level trilinear value (far outside
   gives at least +T; deep inside at most −T) and the candidate is rejected or flagged by step 6.
3. Otherwise fetch the eight fine samples around c. Bricks carry no apron (OPEN-HAB-2, default no apron), so a query
   in a boundary cell of a brick reads up to eight directory entries and their bricks; a neighbour that is a
   sentinel contributes the clamped value ±T.
4. Dequantize and interpolate trilinearly with weights computed in the kernel: φ_b; the gradient ∇φ_b is the
   analytic derivative of the trilinear interpolant (differences of the eight samples weighted bilinearly, divided
   by s).
5. Overlays: for the 0.64 m index cell containing c, evaluate the exact distance d_o and gradient of each of the up
   to eight listed overlays (box, rounded box, wedge, cylinder; a periodic bar pattern folds the query point modulo
   its pitch and tests one bar). φ = min(φ_b, d_o …) and ∇φ is the gradient of the arg-min, chosen by select with
   ties broken by lowest slot index. The minimum of exact distances is exact outside the union and a lower bound
   on depth inside it, which is the side contact uses.
6. Candidate: gap g = φ − r. If g < m, emit a candidate with gap g, normal n = ∇φ/‖∇φ‖ (`sqrt` and one division per
   component), witness point c − φ·n on the surface, the region identifier (palette lookup at the nearest fine
   sample, or the overlay's region), and the error bound ε_g = ε_brick + half a quantization step + the sample's
   fitting error (overlay hits use the overlay's round-off bound). A degenerate gradient (‖∇φ‖ below 10⁻⁶) keeps
   the previous step's normal for that witness and sets the flag `normal-held`.
7. The margin on lockstep tiers is at least v·h + ε_g; it is always smaller than T, so contact never reads a clamped
   value. A sample whose margin would reach T is flagged, never silently clamped.

**`K-np-thin`, per world, sample and thin primitive in the sample's index cell.** Inputs: the sample's positions
A at the start and B at the end of the step (or sub-interval), its radius r and a dilation ρ supplied with the
swept bound (the sagitta of the sample's arc when its link rotates, so that the dilated segment encloses the true
path); the primitive (oriented box, capsule or cylinder) with its exact distance function.

1. Compute the minimum over t ∈ [0, 1] of the exact distance from A + t·(B − A) to the primitive, by the closed
   form for a segment against the primitive's convex shape (segment against box by slab clipping plus edge and
   vertex cases; segment against capsule by segment–segment distance).
2. If that minimum minus (r + ρ) is below the margin, emit a candidate at the earliest such t: gap, normal from the
   primitive to the path point, witness on the primitive, the time fraction t, and ε_g (round-off bound only).
3. On lockstep tiers the candidate enters the solve as a contact at the start of the step with gap measured along
   the normal; under transactional profiles t brackets the event for the event locator.
A sample that starts and ends clear on opposite sides of a plate is thereby detected.

**Manifests.** Class floating with exact-integer index arithmetic; determinism D2; buffers: sample poses (read),
directory, coarse lattice, one pool segment, overlay index and table, thin-primitive index and table (all read,
immutable and shared by every world that indexes the scene), candidate buffer (write, fixed capacity per world,
overflow flagged). A launch binds one pool segment and one directory; worlds are grouped by segment by the
placement planner. Physics reads only the collision field, the overlays and the maps, never the visual mesh
(INV-HAB-02).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Known answer KA-393 (seed condition, "the sphere-against-field known answer"): a spherical distance field
      (radius 0.5 m) compiled to bricks, queried with 10⁶ sample spheres and with the samples of a capsule: gap
      error against the analytic gap at most the brick's recorded interpolation bound; normals within the angle
      implied by that bound.
- [ ] Thin barrier (seed condition, the lockstep row of KA-408): a sample of radius 2 mm moving at 5 m/s with
      h = 2 ms toward a 2 mm plate 1 mm ahead is detected by `K-np-thin` in 10⁶ of 10⁶ randomized crossings
      (speed 1 to 10 m/s, angle of incidence up to 80°, plate thickness 0.5 to 5 mm), although both endpoints are
      clear; the endpoint-only mutant misses.
- [ ] The pooled path equals a dense per-variant reference bit for bit at 10⁶ points per scene, for two variants of
      one base scene that share bricks, and after a world changes variant at reset.
- [ ] Overlays: a threshold lip 12 mm high written as a box overlay gives gaps exact to round-off; a periodic bar
      overlay (slot 20 mm, bar 5 mm) equals the explicit union of 50 bars; an index cell with nine overlays is a
      compile refusal (`FROB-REF-306`), never a truncated list at run time.
- [ ] A query in a brick's boundary cell equals the same query on a field compiled with a one-sample apron.
- [ ] Quantization ripple: a 10 kg block resting on a brick floor with no overlay shows normal-force ripple at most
      1% of weight (TARGET, the scene gate's clause, measured here on `batch-cpu`).
- [ ] Bitwise equality across worker counts, batch sizes and co-tenant worlds; the three mutants are caught.
- [ ] Manifests validate; the dialect lint finds no hardware filtering and no banned built-in.
- [ ] Open clauses of the definition of implemented: device differential checks; the gate records (GATE-TRAC-03 and
      the batched rows of GATE-TRAC-06 in the contact battery bead; GATE-HAB-01 and GATE-HAB-13 in the scene
      beads).

scene compiler's fixtures (labelled `synthetic` where generated), no mocks.

- `sentinel_far_and_deep`: far outside returns no candidate; deep inside returns the flag, not a clamped gap.
- `boundary_cell_eight_lookups_equal_apron`.
- `overlay_min_composition`: a point 3 mm above a lip overlay and 9 mm above the brick floor reports 3 mm and the
  overlay's region; tie broken by lowest slot.
- `periodic_bars_fold`; `nine_overlays_refused_at_compile`.
- `thin_plate_swept_known`: A = (0, 0, 0), B = (10 mm, 0, 0), r = 2 mm, plate faces at x = 3 mm and x = 5 mm:
  minimum distance 0 at t = 0.3; candidate emitted with t = 0.1 (first touch at x = 1 mm).
- `thin_capsule_cord_swept`; `arc_dilation_used`: the 1 m arm turning from −60° to +60° with a 5 cm tip and a thin
  post at (1, 0) m is detected when ρ = 0.5 m (the sagitta) and missed with ρ = 0.
- `variant_switch_at_reset_bitwise`; `worker_count_bitwise`.
- Mutants: `endpoint_only_thin` (misses the plate); `hardware_style_weights` (weights rounded to 8 bits: fails the
  plane fixture); `overlay_ignored_inside_band` (fails the lip fixture).

Logs: `metric` lines for the worst gap error against the brick bound, the count of crossings and misses, the ripple
as a percentage of weight; digests across worker counts; `assert` lines per mutant; a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-393 (capsule and sphere against a spherical distance field; tolerance: the stated interpolation bound).
- KA-408 (thin barrier: the sphere ends 3 mm beyond the far face with both endpoints clear; the endpoint-only
  query misses) and counterexample CX-169.
- KA-426 clauses used here: overlays exact to round-off; a brick with its bound halved fails.
- Metamorphic: refining the scene field moves contact results by no more than the interpolation bound; translating
  scene and sample together by one cell changes no gap bit (cell-relative addressing).
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; debug and release; devices later against their
  class.

## Considerations

- Memory per launch: one pool segment and one directory are bound (the Metal gateway binds at most 31 buffers per
  kernel, FACT to re-read at admission), which is why the pool is segmented and worlds are grouped by segment.
- Dense bricks against a sparse brick tree at 1 cm is an open question (OPEN-TRAC-1, default dense bricks with a
  coarse outer level, decided from measured memory at M3); the kernel's addressing is behind one function so that
  a tree can replace the directory.
- If the pool's measured deduplication is below 1.5 (KILL-POOL), variants fall back to dense per-variant fields
  behind the same directory interface; this kernel is unchanged.
- The normal of a trilinear field is discontinuous across cell faces; the resulting force ripple is what the 1%
  clause bounds, and overlays are the remedy where an edge matters.
- What would make us revisit: thin features that deform (cords that move are rigid proxies, not thin primitives).
