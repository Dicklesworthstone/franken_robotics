## Background

A place changes because a robot is in it: a castor pushes a rug into a fold, a cord wraps an axle, a wheel shoves a
tote. A scene whose only state is fixed geometry and a friction map cannot produce these failures. The strong
response, coupled cloth and cable solvers in every training world, is refuted by arithmetic on memory and step
cost, so living matter is admitted in three tiers and only the first is on the critical path. This bead is one
half of that first tier (the other half is the film-friction law): **rigid proxies**, tier Solid, in `frob-scene`
with contact from `frob-contact`.

- A **rug** is a thin free rigid slab with its own rug-to-floor friction and an edge step.
- A **cord** is a fixed bevelled ridge where it is taped down and a free rigid rod where it is not.
- **Clutter** (toys, wet-floor signs, pallets, debris) is free rigid bodies.

A proxy uses no mechanism that the dynamics and contact subsystems lack: it is ordinary rigid bodies, primitives,
sample spheres, interface cards and overlays. Because of that it stays inside the determinism classes of the
batched tiers, and the evidence class of what it outputs is `Tested`.

Why a fixed step is not a sufficient proxy for a rug (the rejected alternative, with the hand-checkable case). To
push an undriven wheel of radius r carrying load W over an edge of height h takes a horizontal force W·tan α with
tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h). A castor with r = 25 mm at a 10 mm rug edge needs √(10 × 40)/15 = 4/3 of its load:
67 N for a 50 N castor. A 2 kg rug resists sliding with about 0.5 × 19.6 N ≈ 10 N (friction coefficient assumed).
The rug slides and folds long before the castor climbs it. A fixed step would simply be climbed or would block; a
free slab reproduces the push.

Capabilities of this kind are not new in the field (incumbent engines ship cables, cloth and particles coupled to
rigid robots; FACT from public documentation, to be re-read before a gate cites it). Nothing here is claimed as a
first. The claim is narrow: these proxies reproduce the named failure classes at negligible cost and
deterministically. Whether they reproduce what coupled models show is a separate pilot (the proxy-adequacy pilot on
a pinned incumbent), which decides the Moonshot tier and not this bead.

## Technical approach

Module (DESIGN): `crates/frob-scene/src/living/proxy.rs`; fixtures under `fixtures/scenes/proxies/`.

**Declarations** (in the living-world block of a scene pack, tier `solid`; every dimension carries provenance, and
generated scenes label them `assumed`):

| Kind | Fields | Lowered to |
|---|---|---|
| `rug` | footprint (length, width), thickness h (5 to 20 mm typical), mass or areal density, edge profile (`step` or `bevel` with its run), underside material and condition | One free rigid body: a box primitive of the footprint and thickness with its inertia from geometry and density; sample spheres on its underside corners and edges; an interface card for the pair (underside, floor) |
| `cord`, `mode = "taped"` | path (polyline), diameter, bevel run | Fixed geometry: one bevelled ridge overlay per segment (rounded box), exact to round-off; no body, no state |
| `cord`, `mode = "loose"` | length, diameter, mass per length, initial pose | One free rigid rod: a capsule primitive; interface cards against floor and wheel materials |
| `clutter` | shape (primitive or union of primitives), mass properties or material with fill, initial pose, behaviour | One free rigid body per item, through the movable-object path, tagged as a proxy |

Rules. (1) A free proxy is a movable rigid body of the world: it has per-world state, is restored at reset to its
declared pose (or to a randomized pose where the task declares one, drawn by address), and nothing about it
persists into another world or episode. (2) A rug's box is thin, and its collisions with the robot are primitive
pair tests (wheel or foot primitive against box), never samples against a field, so its edge is exact; its contact
with the floor uses its underside samples against the scene field and overlays. (3) No fill is defaulted: a rug
with neither mass nor areal density refuses at scene compile with the unknown-or-missing-field refusal of the
scene format, `FROB-REF-303` (exit 3, `observed: missing-member`, naming the rug and the two fields). (4) Added bodies and their worst-case contacts enter the static capacity estimate; a scene whose
proxies push a workload over its contact or row capacity is flagged at compile, and at run time an overflow marks
the world `Degraded`, never truncates silently. (5) Every result from a scene that contains proxies carries the
statement that deformable matter is represented by proxy; the proxy's own outputs are class `Tested`.

**Why the slab slides (what the fixture checks).** With the castor pressing on the rug's edge with horizontal force
F and the rug carrying only its own weight m·g on the floor, the rug slides when F > μ_rf·m·g. The castor climbs
only when F ≥ W·tan α. With the numbers above, 9.8 N against 66.7 N, sliding comes first by a factor of about 6.8.

**Surfaces.** `scene.compile` and `scene.gen` accept and emit the declarations; `scene.show` lists proxies with
their tier, lowering and assumed fields; `sim.run` treats them like any movable body. No new verb.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed condition (KA-440, the free-slab clause): on the referee, a castor of radius 25 mm under 50 N load pushed
      with a force ramped from 0 at 2 N/s toward a free 2 kg rug of thickness 10 mm with rug-to-floor friction 0.5
      makes the rug slide: the rug has moved at least 50 mm before the castor's axle has risen 1 mm, and sliding
      begins at a push of 9.8 N within 10% (tolerance set here; the verdict "slides" is exact). The onset of
      sliding is the push at which the rug has first moved more than 1 mm. These three numbers (onset at 1 mm of
      travel, 50 mm of travel, axle rise below 1 mm) are the pass rule of the castor case wherever it is
      measured (DESIGN; the plan fixes only the verdict); N19b-18 uses the same rule at M6.
- [ ] The same scene with the rug declared as a fixed 10 mm step does not slide: the castor either climbs when the
      push reaches 66.7 N within 10%, or is blocked below that; the two outcomes differ in the recorded failure
      class.
- [ ] The same verdicts hold on `batch-cpu` and on the batched tiers inside their determinism class, for 1,000
      seeded variants (radius 20 to 40 mm, edge 5 to 15 mm, rug mass 1 to 4 kg, friction 0.3 to 0.7, load 50 N),
      with the predicted ordering of the two thresholds deciding the expected verdict in every variant. A variant
      whose two thresholds lie within 10% of each other is reported as `near-tie` with both forces and is not
      scored (at the corner r = 40 mm, h = 5 mm, 4 kg, μ = 0.7 the climb needs 27.7 N and sliding 27.4 N); the
      count of near-ties is logged.
- [ ] A taped cord of diameter 8 mm lifts a rolling wheel by the ridge height within the overlay's exactness; a
      loose cord of the same size under a 5 N push moves with the wheel instead of being crossed.
- [ ] A clutter item pushed by the robot moves, and after reset is back at its declared pose; a proxy disturbed in
      world i is undisturbed in world j and in the next episode of world i.
- [ ] A rug with no mass and no areal density refuses at scene compile with `FROB-REF-303`, exit 3, naming the rug.
- [ ] `scene.show` lists every proxy with tier `solid` and its assumed fields; results carry the by-proxy
      statement.
- [ ] Digests of a proxy scene are identical for a world run alone and in a batch.
- [ ] Open clauses of the definition of implemented: the gate record. GATE-HAB-08 is an M6 gate whose slosh and
      articulated rows land with the Frontier beads, and its castor row is written then by N19b-18, which
      measures this fixture again. This bead writes no gate row: it records its own clause with measured
      values (onset force, rug travel, axle rise, the fixed-step control) as the known-answer record of KA-440,
      and N19b-18 cites that record.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-scene/tests/proxies.rs`, `crates/frob-contact/tests/proxy_contacts.rs`; real compiled scenes (labelled
`synthetic`), no mocks.
- `edge_force_formula`: r = 25 mm, h = 10 mm gives tan α = 4/3 exactly in rationals; r = 60 mm, h = 12 mm gives
  36/48 = 0.75 (the same law as the raised-threshold known answer).
- `rug_lowers_to_box_body`: areal density 4 kg/m² on a 1.0 × 0.5 m footprint, thickness 10 mm, gives 2 kg exactly;
  inertia from geometry: I_zz = m·(l² + w²)/12 = 0.208333… kg·m², I_xx = m·(w² + h²)/12 = 0.0416833… kg·m²,
  I_yy = m·(l² + h²)/12 = 0.1666833… kg·m², each to one unit in the last place.
- `rug_without_mass_refused`.
- `taped_cord_is_overlay_no_body`; `loose_cord_is_capsule_body`.
- `castor_slides_free_slab` and `castor_fixed_step_climbs_or_blocks` on the referee.
- `threshold_ordering_decides_verdict_1000_variants`.
- `proxy_state_resets_and_does_not_leak` (two worlds, two episodes).
- `capacity_estimate_includes_proxies`: 40 clutter boxes raise the static contact maximum by their rest contacts.
- Mutants: `rug_as_fixed_overlay` (the slide assertion fails: the control the known answer names);
  `rug_friction_from_floor_card_only` (the underside pair ignored: the sliding threshold is off by the ratio of
  coefficients); `proxy_pool_without_world_address` (the leak test fails).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/rigid_proxies.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper; NDJSON to `target/e2e-logs/rigid_proxies/<utc>.ndjson`
with a transcript): `frob scene compile fixtures/scenes/proxies/castor-rug.src --key rp-cmp-slab --json`, the
fixed-step variant `castor-step.src --key rp-cmp-step`, the variants pack `--key rp-cmp-variants` and the rug
without mass `castor-rug-nomass.src --key rp-cmp-nomass` (exit 3, `FROB-REF-303`; `scene.compile` is a `run`
verb, one key per call); `frob scene show <id> --json` (asserts the proxy list and labels); `e2e_scene_ready` on
each pack before it is run; `frob sim run fixtures/castor-probe.fbot --scene
<id> --task fixtures/castor-rug.ftask --tier referee --seed 4 --key rp-run-<slab|step> --json` (the probe, delivered here, is one
undriven castor of radius 25 mm under a 50 N load on a carriage pushed by the task's 2 N/s ramp) for the free slab
and for the fixed-step variant pack, asserting the two verdicts and reading push at onset, rug travel and axle rise
from telemetry; then the 1,000 seeded variants in one batch, `frob sim run --designs
fixtures/castor-variants.toml --scene <variants pack> --task fixtures/castor-rug.ftask --worlds 1000 --tier
batch-cpu --seed 4 --key rp-run-variants --json` (castor radius in the per-world design slots of BD-169; edge height,
rug mass and friction in per-world scene variants of the pack), asserting per world the verdict predicted by the
ordering of the two thresholds, and the digests of five worlds re-run alone equal to their in-batch digests.
Logs: `cmd`, exit code, wall time, evidence block (tier, class `Tested`, identities, seed), `metric`
lines for onset force against 9.8 N and climb force against 66.7 N with tolerances, counts of variants by verdict,
the first disagreeing variant with its parameters on failure, and a `summary` naming KA-440 and stating that
the castor row of GATE-HAB-08 is recorded at M6 by `scripts/e2e/proxy_gate.sh` (N19b-18), not here.

### Known answers and negative controls
- KA-440 (castor at a rug edge: horizontal force W·tan α with tan α = √(h·(2r − h))/(r − h); r = 25 mm, h = 10 mm
  gives 4/3 of the load, 67 N for a 50 N castor; a 2 kg free slab resists with about 10 N and slides first; control:
  a fixed step is climbed or blocks). Counterexample CX-190 (a fixed step is a sufficient proxy for a rug).
- KA-458 as a pattern for the leak test (no trace of a disturbed proxy outside its world and episode).
- Metamorphic: scaling r and h together leaves tan α unchanged; doubling the rug's mass doubles the onset force;
  mirroring the scene mirrors the outcome.
- Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; across worker counts; alone and in a batch; referee against batched
  tiers at the level of the verdict.

## Considerations

- The rug-to-floor friction is `assumed` on every generated card until measured; results that depend on it are
  `Estimated` for realism and the Reality line reads `no-claim`.
- A slab cannot fold. Folding and wrapping are what the Frontier articulated proxies add; if those fail their kill
  gate, the fallback named in advance is exactly this bead's proxies in training.
- A thin free slab resting on a compliant floor is a stiff contact problem; the fixture uses the default law and
  reports creep of the resting rug, which must stay below the stated stiction speed.
- Many small clutter bodies are a capacity question before they are a physics question; the capacity rule against
  biased comparisons applies (a design with more contacts must not be compared under a capacity that truncates it).
- What would make us revisit: the proxy-adequacy pilot showing a failure class the slab and ridge miss that
  matters for a mission family on the critical path.
