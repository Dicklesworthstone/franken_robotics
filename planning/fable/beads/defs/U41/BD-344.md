## Background

Before a run, an agent may register a prediction, for example `frob predict register --metric cot-elec --interval
0.42 0.50 --confidence 0.8 --for <planned-run>`. The prediction is hashed and frozen before the run starts and
scored afterwards with a proper scoring rule, and `frob predict report` shows each predictor's calibration. The
mechanism costs almost nothing and answers a question nothing else in the studio does: whether an agent
understands the robot it is designing or is searching blindly. (The idea is adapted from a forecast ledger used in
a sibling project; FACT, recorded in the source register.)

The studio registers its own predictions under the same rule: the intervals of `sim.estimate`, the outcome
intervals of `experiment.suggest`, and the saving a race or a ladder expects. Calibration is then put to work in
two places: an agent whose 80% intervals cover far from 80% of the time is told so in `next_actions`, with the
suggestion to buy evidence before the next edit; and the studio's own miscalibration is a fallback trigger for the
experiment-suggestion decision core (coverage of 80% intervals outside 65% to 92% over 30 experiments).

This bead is the foundation of that capability in `frob-opt` (Atelier, tier Solid): the prediction ledger, the
hash-and-freeze primitive, the scoring rule and the two verbs. Two properties make the ledger worth trusting, and
they are the seed's acceptance conditions:

- **A prediction registered after its outcome exists is refused.** Otherwise the ledger measures hindsight.
- **A report scores every registered prediction, the failed ones included.** Otherwise calibration is computed
  from survivors: a predictor could cancel the runs it expects to miss.

Rejected alternatives: timestamps as proof of order (a clock can be wrong or set); letting a refit overwrite earlier
predictions (it erases the evidence of miscalibration); scoring by hit rate alone (a predictor that always states
an enormous interval scores perfectly; a proper scoring rule charges for width).

## Scope

In: the prediction record and its canonical bytes; registration with the order proof; freezing at dispatch;
versions; outcome binding; the scoring rule; the handling of predictions whose run never produced the metric; the
report and its calibration table; the two verbs, their schemas and refusals. Out: the studio's automatic
registration hooks (N28c-14); the coverage monitor, the hint in `next_actions` and the decision-core trigger
(N28c-15); the capability's end-to-end script (N28c-18); Reality predictions frozen against sealed data (the
calibration subsystem, which has its own refusal for reading sealed data without a frozen prediction).

## Technical approach

Module (DESIGN): `crates/frob-opt/src/predict/{record.rs, ledger.rs, freeze.rs, score.rs, report.rs}`.

**Record** (schema `frob.prediction/1`, canonical JSON; `PredictionId` = BLAKE3 of the canonical bytes):
`predictor` (an agent identity from the session, or a studio predictor name), `version` (model name, integer
version, the ledger frontier at which it was fitted), `metric` (a registered metric name with its unit and
declared span), `interval` [lo, hi] with lo ≤ hi, `confidence` c ∈ (0, 1), `target` (the planned run, job,
experiment or campaign, bound to immutable identifiers: design, task, model, address set, seed), optional
`blocks` (one interval per mechanism block where a suggestion's mechanisms predict different outcomes), and
`registered_seq` (the ledger sequence number assigned at registration).

**Registration** (`predict.register`, kind `mutate`), as numbered steps in one ledger transaction:

1. Validate: the metric is registered and its unit matches; lo ≤ hi; c ∈ (0, 1); the target resolves to a planned
   run or campaign that the caller may read, or is `next`: the caller's next run or campaign dispatched in this
   session, whose immutable identifiers are written into the record's freeze set at that dispatch (a `next`
   record never bound by the end of the session receives `no-outcome: cancelled` with `reason: next-unbound`,
   listed and counted as unresolved).
2. Order check (the seed's first condition): look up outcomes for the target. If any result carrying that metric
   for that target identity exists in the ledger, or the target's job has committed its first chunk, refuse with
   `prediction-after-outcome`, returning the sequence number of the earliest such record. Order is ledger append
   order, never a clock.
3. Append the record; return `PredictionId` and `registered_seq`. The same idempotency key with the same content
   returns the same record; with different content it refuses.

**Freeze.** When a job for the target is dispatched, the job record embeds the sorted list of `PredictionId`s
registered for it (the freeze set) and their combined hash. From then on: a new registration for that target is
refused by step 2; an attempt to alter a frozen record (same predictor and target, different interval) is refused
with `prediction-changed-after-freeze`. A predictor who wants to predict again before the freeze registers a new
record; all records are kept and all are scored (the report shows each).

**Versions.** A refit after scoring opens a new prediction version for that predictor; the old version stays in
the ledger with its scores, and calibration windows are per version.

**Outcome binding and scoring.** When the target finishes (or ends in any terminal state), every prediction in its
freeze set receives exactly one disposition:

| Disposition | When | Score |
|---|---|---|
| `scored` | The run produced the metric value y | Covered = 1 if lo ≤ y ≤ hi else 0; interval score S = (hi − lo)/span + (2/(1 − c))·max(lo − y, 0, y − hi)/span |
| `no-outcome: cancelled` / `failed` / `refused` | The run ended without the metric | Not covered; listed and counted in the denominator as unresolved, never dropped |
| `no-outcome: pending` | The run has not ended | Listed as pending |

A `no-outcome` disposition carries a `reason`: the terminal state's own (`cancelled`, `failed`, `refused`),
`next-unbound` (above), or `target-replanned` when the target's immutable identifiers changed before it ran (for
example a job re-planned under a new kernel version), so the frozen target never produces an outcome. The report
breaks the unresolved count down by disposition and reason; the identity below is unchanged.

S is the interval score, a proper scoring rule for a central interval at confidence c: narrower is better, and a
miss costs its distance scaled by 2/(1 − c). Dividing by the metric's declared span makes scores comparable across
metrics. For a prediction with `blocks`, the block that applied (recorded with the outcome) is scored.

**Report** (`predict.report`, kind `read`). Per predictor and version, per confidence level: counts of registered,
scored, covered, missed, unresolved and pending; empirical coverage (covered over scored) with its exact binomial
interval; coverage counting unresolved as misses, printed beside it; mean interval score; the list of the most
recent n records with dispositions. Every registered prediction appears in the counts (the seed's second
condition): registered = scored + unresolved + pending, checked as an accounting identity in the output.

**Verbs.**

- `predict.register`: CLI as in the Background; input `frob.predict.register.in/1`, output
  `frob.predict.register.out/1` (`prediction_id`, `registered_seq`, the freeze status of the target). Capability
  `predict:write`.
- `predict.report`: `frob predict report [--predictor <id>] [--metric <m>] [--since <frontier>] --json`; output
  `frob.predict.report.out/1`. Capability `predict:read`. An agent sees its own predictions and the studio's; an
  operator scope sees all.
Both return the standard envelope; report numbers carry the claim product (evidence class `Tested`: they are
counts over ledger records; determinism D0 at a pinned ledger frontier).

**Refusals** (exit code 3), with the codes N28cx-01 decided: `prediction-changed-after-freeze` is raised as
`FROB-REF-883` (merged into that code; `pointer: prediction`; repair: none for this target; the record stands);
`prediction-after-outcome` is held for an `8xx` number and is raised on its interim form, `FROB-REF-883` with
`detail = "registration-after-outcome"` and `pointer: prediction.target`, behind one constant that switches when
the number is released (repair: predict a run that has not started; register the next version);
`FROB-REF-802` for arguments that fail the schema (inverted interval, confidence outside (0, 1), unknown metric).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Seed condition: registering for a target whose result already exists, or whose job has committed a chunk, is
      refused with `prediction-after-outcome` (interim `FROB-REF-883`, `detail = "registration-after-outcome"`)
      and the sequence number of the earliest outcome record; the same
      holds when the system clock is set back (order comes from the ledger).
- [ ] Seed condition: a report over a mixed set (predictions that covered, that missed, whose run was cancelled,
      whose run failed, and pending ones) lists every one, and registered = scored + unresolved + pending holds in
      the output; a cancelled run's prediction is never absent from the denominator.
- [ ] A frozen prediction cannot be altered; a second prediction before the freeze is kept alongside the first and
      both are scored.
- [ ] The interval score equals the hand values of the unit tests; a wider interval with the same outcome scores
      worse when both cover; a miss scores worse than a cover of equal width.
- [ ] A refit opens a new version; the old version's scores are unchanged byte for byte.
- [ ] `predict.register` and `predict.report` are reachable through `frob --json` and `frob-mcp` with documented
      schemas and exit codes; a repeated idempotency key returns the same `PredictionId`.
- [ ] Records contain metrics and identifiers only, never private-scene payloads; a prediction for a target the
      caller cannot read is refused by capability.
- [ ] `CONTRACT.md` of `frob-opt` documents the record, the order rule, the scoring rule and the dispositions.
- [ ] The report exposes, per predictor and version, the counts the prediction rows of GATE-ATEL-16 read
      (registered, scored, covered, unresolved, pending) through `predict.report --json`. Those rows are measured
      by N28c-18 and recorded with the cascade by BD-352; both depend on this bead, so neither is an acceptance
      condition here.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-opt/tests/predict.rs`; the real ledger (a temporary store), no mocks.
- `register_then_run_then_score`: interval [0.42, 0.50], c = 0.8, span 1.0, outcome 0.47: covered, S = 0.08.
- `miss_is_charged`: same prediction, outcome 0.53: not covered, S = 0.08 + (2/0.2) × 0.03 = 0.38.
- `wider_interval_scores_worse_when_covering`: [0.30, 0.60] at 0.47 gives S = 0.30 > 0.08.
- `after_outcome_refused`; `after_first_chunk_refused`; `clock_set_back_still_refused`.
- `frozen_record_cannot_change`; `second_prediction_before_freeze_both_scored`.
- `cancelled_run_is_unresolved_in_denominator`; `failed_run_listed`; `pending_listed`.
- `accounting_identity_holds` (property test over 10⁴ random ledgers of registrations and terminal states).
- `refit_opens_new_version_old_scores_fixed`.
- `block_prediction_scores_applied_block`.
- `coverage_binomial_interval_known`: 24 covered of 30 scored at c = 0.8 gives coverage 0.8 with the exact
  (Clopper–Pearson) 95% interval [0.6143, 0.9229] to four decimals.
- Mutants: `order_by_timestamp` (the clock test fails); `report_drops_unresolved` (the identity test fails: the
  survivor-calibration control); `refit_overwrites` (old scores change); `hit_rate_only_score` (the width test
  fails).

### End-to-end script and logging
The capability's script is `scripts/e2e/predict_calibration.sh` (its own bead). This bead adds
`scripts/e2e/predict_register_order.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): `frob predict register --metric cot-elec
--interval 0.42 0.50 --confidence 0.8 --for next --key pr-1 --json`, then `frob sim run fixtures/quad-a.fbot
--task fixtures/velocity-flat.ftask --worlds 64 --seconds 10 --tier batch-cpu --seed 3 --key <k> --json`
(asserts the prediction is in the job's freeze set); the same `--key pr-1` returns the same `prediction_id`;
once `frob job status` shows the first chunk committed (or the result exists), a second registration for that
run exits 3 with `FROB-REF-883`, `detail = "registration-after-outcome"` and the earliest outcome sequence
number; after the result, `frob predict report --json` shows one `scored` record with its interval score and the
accounting identity; then a registration for a second run, that run cancelled with `frob job cancel`, and the
prediction listed as `no-outcome: cancelled` in the denominator; `--interval 0.5 0.4` exits 3 with
`FROB-REF-802`; one report repeated over `frob-mcp` (`predict_report`) is byte-identical at the same frontier. Logs: `cmd`, exit code, wall
time, evidence block, `refusal` lines, `metric` lines (coverage, mean interval score, counts by disposition), and
a `summary` naming GATE-ATEL-16 (partial).

### Known answers and negative controls
- Hand values: S = 0.08 (cover), 0.38 (miss by 0.03 at c = 0.8), 0.30 (wide cover), all with span 1.
- The fallback trigger's window (coverage of 80% intervals outside 65% to 92% over 30 scored predictions) is a
  known answer of the monitor bead; here only the counts it reads are produced.
- Negative controls: the four mutants; a planted registration after the outcome; a planted report that omits a
  cancelled run.
- Metamorphic: reordering unrelated registrations changes no score; scaling the metric and its span by the same
  factor changes no score.
- Determinism lanes: the report at a pinned ledger frontier is byte-identical twice on one machine, in debug and
  release, and on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- A predictor can still avoid misses by never predicting; the report therefore shows the share of a predictor's
  runs that carried a prediction, and the monitor treats too few predictions as uninformative, not as calibrated.
- Predictions about sealed confirmation worlds are not available to agents (no verb of the studio reads a
  confirmation world); the calibration subsystem's frozen predictions against real data are a separate ledger with
  its own rule.
- The interval score is proper for central intervals; one-sided predictions are a later addition and would need
  their own rule (a quantile score).
- What would make us revisit: evidence that agents game the ledger with trivially wide intervals despite the
  score; the remedy is to rank by score, which already charges width.
