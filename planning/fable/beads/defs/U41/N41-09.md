## Background

Every mechanism in the design carries a tier: Solid, Frontier or Moonshot. A Moonshot item is one that would make
the product unlike anything else and may well not work; the design works without every one of them, and none is
on the critical path. The danger of Moonshots is not that they fail but that they are interesting: a swarm of
agents will happily spend its effort there while a milestone on the critical path slips (the risk "breadth starves
the spine"). Doctrine rule D-6 therefore caps the budget: **at most four Moonshot items are active at once.** An
item is active from the funding of its pilot until its expiry gate; the others wait in the Moonshot queue, which
is part of the register of deferred and killed ideas. Each has a falsifier and an expiry gate.

The cap needs an order, and the order is a matter of preference among things that are all optional, so it is the
owner's: OPEN-PROG-5, **the order in which admitted Moonshot items become active under the cap of four.**
Fail-closed default: **none is active before its pilot passes; then the order in which the frontier milestones
list their epics.** What the default costs: nothing on the critical path. Needed by M5, because the first
Moonshot epics open at M6.

The queue holds 21 items, `MQ-01` to `MQ-21`, with a plan-time order of entry (earlier consumers first, the
cheaper pilot first within a milestone, a dependent item after the one it waits for). The milestone plan governs
where its order differs, and the default of this question is that case. The frontier milestones list their
epics in this order. M6: robot-advected films, payload slosh, and cable and rug proxies (Frontier); Cosserat
rods, cloth and coupled multiphysics islands on the CPU referee for finalists only (Moonshot); soft soil at
scale; the hard-contact law on devices; the tree-operator route and the mesh path on devices; tactile sensing
(Moonshot); the rotor port (Moonshot); RGB and splat scenes; moving people; gradients through contact; the
geometric-phase and symmetry-at-contact queries, whose Moonshot halves (an atlas; an amortization) are separate
from their Frontier halves; lifecycle compression (Moonshot). M8: facility co-design and multi-robot handoff;
skill transport with learned residuals; continuation-based synthesis; firmware export; geometric-algebra tree
policies at scale; reachable-set tables for shields; the fused per-world step kernel. Frontier epics are not
counted against the cap.

## Scope

In: the decision record for OPEN-PROG-5; what is put to the owner and when; the default order and the rule that
computes it; the transition command that changes a queue record's `order`, `state`, `activated_at` and `adr`
(activation in order, release); the readiness rule for beads of items that are not active, with its hygiene
listing; the listing in the exit record from M5 on.

Out, with the bead that has it:

- the Moonshot queue as data (the 21 records in `registries/ev_matrix.toml`, their schema, the state
  vocabulary, the plan-time order, the slot-freeing rule, the function `next_entries`, the mapping of items to
  epics): NAB-21. This bead keeps no register of its own; an earlier draft specified `registries/moonshots.toml`,
  which does not exist (DESIGN: one file holds the queue);
- the cap of four as a check (`moonshot_cap`), the completeness rule of a record that is to become `active`,
  the rule that activation needs a registered pilot, and the rule that no Moonshot bead is on the critical
  path: the doctrine check D-6, N10a-02. The transition command of this bead calls those predicates and adds
  none of its own;
- the count of active items at every exit from M0 on (step 11 of the exit procedure): N40-05, through the same
  predicate;
- the pilots and their protocols; each epic's short plan, kill gate and fallback (the epic's own beads); compute
  envelopes for items that pretrain; the Augur slices' own envelope question (its decision bead reads this
  order).

## Technical approach

**The records this bead writes.** The queue is the `[[moonshot_queue]]` table of `registries/ev_matrix.toml`
(NAB-21). Of a record's fields this bead writes four, and only through its transition command: `order` (an
integer from the owner's ADR or from the default ADR), `state`, `activated_at` and `adr`. It reads `id`, `epic`
(the tracker key of the item's frontier epic), `pilot_id`, `falsifier`, `expiry_gate`, `kill_gate`, `fallback`,
`milestone`, `waits_for` and `frontier_part_first`. The state vocabulary is NAB-21's closed set: `queued`
(called "waiting" in prose), `active`, `passed` (the item was delivered), `killed`, `falsified`, `expired`,
`deferred`.

**What "active" means** (DESIGN; the same reading in N10a-02 and NAB-21). D-6 says an item is active from the
funding of its pilot until its expiry gate; the default of this question says none is active before its pilot
passes. Read together: an item holds a slot from the moment its pilot is funded (a pilot record registered in
the pilot ledger, status `running`), so a running Moonshot pilot counts against the cap, as the pilot ledger
also counts it; and the work behind the pilot (every bead of the item that is not its pilot, and its work
package) is not ready and not funded before the pilot has a recorded result whose falsifier did not hold. The
default therefore removes work, never a slot check.

**Transitions** (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example moonshot -- <enter|release|reorder> <id> --adr <path>
[--root <dir>] --json`: `frob-conformance` has no default binary (BD-025), so the command is an example target
this bead declares, `crates/frob-conformance/examples/moonshot.rs` (DESIGN name), in the form of the crate's
other registry commands such as `--example pilots` and `--example bets`; its logic is in
`crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/moonshots.rs`; exit 0 when the transition is written, and 3 when it is refused
(the unit's convention for a refusing command, N41-REF), with `reason=<id>` on the first line and the reason,
the rule and the items named in the JSON object: `moonshot-cap` (N38-REF; the cap of step 1, the same rule the
pilot register applies when a pilot is set `running`), and otherwise `moonshot-transition-refused` (N41-REF), with
`rule` one of `incomplete-record`, `not-next`, `queued-release-without-sweep` and `no-adr`; run by the exit
reviewer or the project lead, never automatically; every transition names an ADR or a gate record and writes
`adr`):

1. Enter (`queued → active`). Refused unless: `moonshot_cap` of N10a-02 would still hold after the change (the
   refusal names the items that hold the slots); the record passes N10a-02's completeness rule for an active
   record (a registered pilot whose protocol hash predates any result, a written falsifier, a non-empty
   `expiry_gate`); and the item is among `next_entries(queue, now_milestone, active_count)` of NAB-21, which
   returns items in `order` whose milestone is reached, whose `waits_for` items passed, and whose Frontier part
   passed where that is required. An item that cannot enter (no registered pilot, milestone not reached, waiting
   for another) does not hold up the queue: the next item by `order` that can enter does.
2. Work behind the pilot. When the pilot's result is recorded and its falsifier did not hold, the item's other
   beads become ready (rule 4); when the falsifier held, the transition is `release` to `falsified`.
3. Release (`active →` `passed`, `killed`, `falsified` or `expired`). At the item's expiry gate it becomes
   `passed` (the gate passed) or `expired` (no pass and no kill, or the pilot not passed within its planned
   weeks: it returns to the register and frees its slot); when its kill gate fires it becomes `killed`, with the
   fallback's name recorded; when its pilot's falsifier holds it becomes `falsified`. The slot then goes to the
   first item of `next_entries`.
4. Readiness and hygiene: a bead of an item that is not `active` is not ready, whatever its dependencies say,
   and a bead of an active item other than its pilot is not ready before the pilot's passing result; the tracker
   hygiene check lists such beads if they are in progress.
5. Reorder: `order` changes only by an ADR; the command rewrites all 21 values without gaps.
6. Pending proposals of earlier sweeps. The command lands at M5, but the sweeps of M2 to M4 may already have
   written an ADR that proposes a state change: NAB-25 (M2) writes one proposing MQ-01 `falsified` when the M2
   profile shows the step is not bound by what fusion removes, and lists the item under its sweep record's queue
   `freed` with the reason `falsified-before-pilot`. When the command first runs, and before any `enter` or
   `reorder`, the reviewer applies every such pending proposal: `release <id> --adr <that ADR>`, which is the one
   case of `release` from `queued` (allowed only to `falsified`, and only with an ADR whose sweep record lists
   the item as `freed: falsified-before-pilot`). The default order of the table is then written over all 21
   records as before (a `falsified` record keeps its row and its `order`, and `next_entries` never returns it).

**The default order** (taken at the M5 exit with no answer; DESIGN for the items the milestone lists do not
name). Sort key of an item: first, the position of its `epic` in the list "M6 epics in their listed order,
then M8 epics in their listed order"; an item with no listed epic takes the position just after the last
listed epic of M6 when its `milestone` field is M6 or earlier, and just after the last listed epic of M8
otherwise; ties are broken by the plan-time order. Reason: the milestone lists are silent on those items, and
the queue's own first rule (earlier consumers first) still applies to them. With NAB-21's mapping of items to
epics this gives:

| Default order | Item | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | MQ-02 coupled multiphysics islands | first Moonshot epic of the M6 list |
| 2 | MQ-05 persistent ruts and footprints | rides with the soft-soil epic, next in the M6 list |
| 3 | MQ-04 tactile hands | tactile sensing |
| 4 | MQ-03 rotor port | the rotor port |
| 5, 6 | MQ-09 shape-space atlas; MQ-10 symmetry at contact, amortized | the Moonshot halves of the two queries |
| 7 | MQ-12 lifecycle compression | last of the M6 list |
| 8 to 11 | MQ-06 link-chain tracks; MQ-07 hydraulic actuation; MQ-08 continuum arm; MQ-11 label minimization | no listed epic; placed at M6 or earlier by the queue; plan-time order |
| 12 | MQ-20 facility co-design and handoff | first of the M8 list |
| 13, 14 | MQ-16 skill transport through an interface; MQ-17 skill transport by a learned residual | the skill-transport epic; plan-time order within it |
| 15 | MQ-14 numerical continuation | continuation-based synthesis |
| 16 | MQ-13 firmware export | firmware export |
| 17 | MQ-19 geometric-algebra tree policy | tree policies at scale |
| 18 | MQ-18 reachable-set tables | shield tables |
| 19 | MQ-01 fused per-world step kernel | last of the M8 list |
| 20 | MQ-15 Study-quadric synthesis | no listed epic; M8; waits for MQ-14 |
| 21 | MQ-21 general Lean theorems | `deferred`; never entered |

The fused per-world step kernel is first in the plan-time order (its pilot may run after the M2 profile) and
last in the default order: this is the difference the rule "the milestone plan governs" settles. An owner who
wants it earlier says so in the ADR.

**What is put to the owner at the M4 exit:** the candidate list above with, for each, its pilot's state, its
ceiling stated before any measurement, its compute class if it needs one, and its fallback; and the request for an
order. The answer is an ADR `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-05-moonshot-order.md`; the queue's `order` fields are filled from
it by `reorder`. With no answer at the M5 exit the default ADR is written and `order` follows the table above.
The ADR may also lower the cap (a field `cap`, 1 to 4); N10a-02's predicate reads it and rejects a value above 4.

**At each exit from M5 on** the exit record prints the four active items, the queue, and any item whose state
changed, with the gate or ADR that changed it. (The count itself is checked at every exit from M0 by step 11 of
the exit procedure.)

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The transition command reads and writes the `[[moonshot_queue]]` records of `registries/ev_matrix.toml`
      and no other file; every change of `order` or `state` carries an `adr`.
- [ ] Entering a fifth item is refused by N10a-02's `moonshot_cap`, and the refusal names the four; entering an
      item with no registered pilot is refused; entering out of order is refused unless the earlier items cannot
      enter. An item that lacks a falsifier or an expiry gate cannot enter (N10a-02's completeness rule; a
      waiting record with a fallback and no kill gate is valid, as seven of the 21 are).
- [ ] No bead of an active item other than its pilot is ready before the pilot's passing result is recorded.
- [ ] A kill gate firing moves the item to `killed`, frees its slot and records the fallback's name; a pilot
      not passed within its planned weeks moves it to `expired` and frees its slot.
- [ ] The register row OPEN-PROG-5 is `decided` or `defaulted` with an ADR at the M5 exit, and `order` is filled;
      with the default it equals the table of the default order.
- [ ] The exit record from M5 on prints the active four and the queue.
- [ ] The hygiene listing reports an in-progress bead of an item that is not `active`.
- [ ] On the first run of the command, every pending proposal of the M2 to M4 sweeps is applied before any
      `enter`: if NAB-25's sweep record lists MQ-01 as `freed: falsified-before-pilot`, MQ-01 reads `falsified`
      with that ADR in `adr`; a `queued → falsified` release without such a sweep entry is refused.

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/moonshots.rs`: `fifth_enter_refused_names_the_four` (through N10a-02's
predicate); `no_pilot_no_activation` (no registered pilot record); `falsifier_fired_no_activation` (the item is

after MQ-12; MQ-15 after MQ-01); `entering_without_falsifier_or_expiry_refused` (N10a-02's completeness rule
reached through the command); `reorder_needs_adr_and_leaves_no_gap`; `pending_sweep_proposal_applied_first` (a scratch M2 sweep record
listing MQ-01 as `freed: falsified-before-pilot` with its ADR: the first run releases MQ-01 to `falsified` before
the default order is written, and `next_entries` never returns it); `queued_release_needs_sweep_entry` (a
`queued → falsified` release whose ADR no sweep record cites is refused); `cap_lowered_by_adr` (cap 3: a fourth
entry is refused); `hygiene_lists_in_progress_bead_of_waiting_item` (synthetic tracker export). Every refusal
test asserts exit 3 and the first line `reason=<id>`: `moonshot-cap` for the fifth entry and the lowered cap,
`moonshot-transition-refused` with its `rule` for the others.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/owner_prog5.sh`: on a scratch copy of the queue with seven items and synthetic pilot records,
enters four in order, attempts a fifth (refusal naming the four), fires one kill gate (slot freed, next item
entered), lets one pilot run past its planned weeks (`expired`); then, on a scratch copy of the full queue of 21
records with a scratch M2 sweep record proposing MQ-01 `falsified`, applies the pending proposal, writes the
default ADR and checks the 21 `order` values against the table and MQ-01's state, and regenerates the exit
listing. Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/owner_prog5/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a
transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`, `bead` ("N41-09"), `step`, `event`: `cmd` lines with the exact
command line, exit code and wall time; the active set after each step as a `metric` line; `refusal` lines with
the code and the items named; one `assert` per negative control with `caught`; on failure the first differing
`order` or `state` with the scratch file's path; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the cap (4); the default order (the table; 13 items by their listed epics, 2 riding with a
listed epic, 6 placed by the rule for unlisted items). Negative controls: the fifth activation (the registered
control is the exit protocol's "five items active at one commit"; N10a-02's `moonshot_cap` catches it);
activation without a registered pilot; work behind a pilot started before its result; an order changed without
an ADR. The Moonshot bead planted among the M3 exit's ancestors is the control of N10a-02's critical-path
check and is run there.

## Considerations

- "Active from the funding of its pilot" and "not active before its pilot passes" are read together as stated
  under "What active means": a funded pilot holds a slot, and the work behind it waits for the pilot's result.
  An earlier draft of this bead did not count a running pilot against the cap; that departed from the doctrine
  rule's own wording and from the pilot ledger's count, and is corrected. Because a pilot is a small,
  registered, day-scale activity, a slot held by a pilot alone is held briefly.
- An item with a Frontier half and a Moonshot half is two records in two registers (the Frontier half is a row
  of the Frontier and Moonshot table, the Moonshot half a queue record with `frontier_part_first`); only the
  Moonshot half is capped.
- The cap is enforced by a check and, in the end, by people: an agent that starts Moonshot work outside the
  queue produces beads the hygiene check reports, not beads the check can prevent.
- If the owner prefers fewer than four, the cap is a field of the ADR and may be lowered; it may not be raised
  without changing the doctrine rule.
- The mapping of the queue's items to the milestone lists' epics is a DESIGN reading (two items ride with an
  epic whose title does not name them; six have no listed epic). The owner's ADR overrides any of it.
