## Background

Every mechanism in the design carries a tier: Solid, Frontier or Moonshot. A Moonshot item is one that would make
the product unlike anything else and may well not work; the design works without every one of them, and none is
on the critical path. The danger of Moonshots is not that they fail but that they are interesting: a swarm of
agents will happily spend its effort there while a milestone on the critical path slips (the risk "breadth starves
the spine"). Doctrine rule D-6 therefore caps the budget: **at most four Moonshot items are active at once.** An
item is active from the funding of its pilot until its expiry gate; the others wait in the register of deferred
and killed ideas. Each has a falsifier and an expiry gate.

The cap needs an order, and the order is a matter of preference among things that are all optional, so it is the
owner's: OPEN-PROG-5, **the order in which admitted Moonshot items become active under the cap of four.**
Fail-closed default: **none is active before its pilot passes; then the order in which the frontier milestones
list their epics.** What the default costs: nothing on the critical path. Needed by M5, because the first
Moonshot epics open at M6.

The Moonshot candidates, in the default order (the M6 list first, then the M8 list): Cosserat rods, cloth and
coupled multiphysics islands on the CPU referee for finalists only; lifecycle compression; the Moonshot halves of
geometric phase for locomotion (an atlas) and of symmetry at the instant of contact (an amortization), whose
Frontier halves are separate; then facility co-design and multi-robot handoff; skill transport with learned
residuals; continuation-based synthesis; firmware export; geometric-algebra tree policies at scale; reachable-set
tables for shields; the fused per-world step kernel. Frontier epics are not counted against the cap.

## Scope

In: the decision record; the Moonshot register as data with item states; the cap check and the activation rule;
the default order; the listing at exits. Out: the pilots and their protocols; each epic's short plan, kill gate
and fallback (the epic's own beads); compute envelopes for items that pretrain; the Augur slices' own envelope
question (its decision bead reads this order).

## Technical approach

**Register** (DESIGN): `registries/moonshots.toml`, one record per Moonshot item: `id` (the epic's key), `title`,
`pilot` (identifier in the pilot ledger), `falsifier`, `expiry_gate`, `kill_gate`, `fallback`, `order` (an integer
from the owner's ADR or the default), `state` (`waiting`, `active`, `expired`, `killed`, `delivered`),
`activated_at`, `adr`.

**Rules** (in `crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/moonshots.rs`, run by the exit runner and by the tracker hygiene
check):

1. Cap: at most four records have `state = "active"`. A change that would make a fifth active is refused, naming
   the four.
2. Activation: an item may become active only if its pilot has a recorded result in the pilot ledger that is not
   its falsifier firing, and every item with a smaller `order` is active, expired, killed or delivered, or has no
   passing pilot (an item whose pilot has not passed does not hold up the queue).
3. Release: an item leaves `active` at its expiry gate (to `delivered` or `expired`) or when its kill gate fires
   (to `killed`, with the fallback's name recorded); the slot then goes to the next item by `order` that satisfies
   rule 2.
4. No Moonshot bead on the critical path: no bead of an M0 to M5 or M7 exit review's ancestor set carries the
   `moonshot` label unless it is a pilot (cross-check with the tracker export).
5. Labels: a bead of an item that is not `active` is not ready, whatever its dependencies say; the hygiene check
   lists such beads if they are in progress.

**What is put to the owner at the M4 exit:** the candidate list above with, for each, its pilot's state, its
ceiling stated before any measurement, its compute class if it needs one, and its fallback; and the request for an
order. The answer is an ADR `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-05-moonshot-order.md`; the register's `order` fields are filled from
it. With no answer at the M5 exit the default ADR is written and `order` follows the list above.

**At each exit from M5 on** the exit record prints the four active items, the queue, and any item whose state
changed, with the gate or ADR that changed it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The register lists every Moonshot item with pilot, falsifier, expiry gate, kill gate and fallback; a record
      missing one of them fails to load.
- [ ] Activating a fifth item is refused; activating an item with no passing pilot is refused; activating out of
      order is refused unless the earlier items cannot activate.
- [ ] A kill gate firing moves the item to `killed`, frees its slot and records the fallback's name.
- [ ] A bead labelled `moonshot` among the ancestors of a critical-path exit review is reported.
- [ ] The register row OPEN-PROG-5 is `decided` or `defaulted` with an ADR at the M5 exit, and `order` is filled.
- [ ] The exit record from M5 on prints the active four and the queue.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/moonshots.rs`: `fifth_active_refused`; `no_pilot_no_activation`;
`falsifier_fired_no_activation`; `order_respected`; `blocked_item_does_not_hold_queue`;
`kill_gate_frees_slot_and_names_fallback`; `expiry_moves_to_delivered_or_expired`;
`moonshot_bead_on_critical_path_reported` (synthetic tracker export); `default_order_is_m6_list_then_m8_list`;
`record_missing_falsifier_rejected`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/owner_prog5.sh`: on a scratch register with seven items and synthetic pilot results, activates four in
order, attempts a fifth (refusal), fires one kill gate (slot freed, next item activated), and regenerates the exit
listing. Logs: `cmd`, exit code, the active set after each step as a `metric` line, `refusal` lines, a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the cap (4); the default order. Negative controls: the fifth activation; activation without a
pilot; a Moonshot bead planted among the M3 exit's ancestors; an order changed without an ADR.

## Considerations

- "Active from the funding of its pilot" and "not active before its pilot passes" are read together: funding a
  pilot is a small, registered, day-scale activity and is not counted against the cap; the item's work is counted
  from the moment it is activated after a passing pilot.
- An item with a Frontier half and a Moonshot half is two records in two registers; only the Moonshot half is
  capped.
- The cap is enforced by a check and, in the end, by people: an agent that starts Moonshot work outside the
  register produces beads the hygiene check reports, not beads the check can prevent.
- If the owner prefers fewer than four, the cap is a field of the ADR and may be lowered; it may not be raised
  without changing the doctrine rule.
