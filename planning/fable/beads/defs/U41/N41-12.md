## Background

The program has two machines with different jobs. One is a large-memory Mac (36 CPU cores, an 80-core GPU, 256 GB
of unified memory): it holds the world bank of compiled scenes, runs the referee and replay, does derivation and
proof work, and serves the viewer. The other, `trj`, has two RTX 4090 cards and is the training tier. They are not
a fast GPU and a slow GPU. What crosses between them is deliberately small: seeds and scene recipes, design and
module differences, model-variant tables, policy weights, receipts, digests and telemetry summaries. Compiled
scene fields, world state and rollout buffers never cross; a scene is regenerated on the receiving machine from
its seed and must arrive at the same identity.

A **peer link** between the two daemons would let one queue assign jobs to either machine (the fleet scheduler, a
Frontier mechanism). It has upkeep: a second protocol surface to secure and test, failure modes when a machine
drops out, and a standing temptation to move bulk data. Its ceiling was stated before any test: adding the Mac's
GPU to training adds at most its share of the pair of cards (0.125 at a quarter of one card's throughput, 0.30 at
60%), so the gain cannot come from training throughput. It can come only from moving non-training work off the
training cards, and removing a fraction g of the work on the critical machine speeds a campaign by at most
1/(1 − g): a factor of 1.5 needs g ≥ 1/3.

OPEN-TETHER-5 asks the owner whether the link earns that upkeep. Fail-closed default: **standalone machines.**
What the default costs: the fleet scheduler assigns jobs by the role table only (applied through ordinary job
verbs on each machine), and no cross-machine queue exists. Needed by M4, when the fleet scheduler's gate is due.

## Scope

This bead is the one place where OPEN-TETHER-5 is decided and recorded. Two measurements come before it and are
made elsewhere: the peer link's own paper pilot (N32a-34, a dependency: a week of both queue traces, the
overlap of idle and queued time against its 10% falsifier, the recoverable device time, and the memo for the
owner), and the fleet scheduler's pilot with KILL-FLEET (g, the share of wall-clock that is not training).

In: the decision record; the evidence page (both pilots' numbers and the kill gate's state); the rule that
connects the evidence to the ruling; what each answer switches, including, on `build`, the entry of
KILL-PEERLINK's kill row and of the pilot record's kill gate; the `--machine` input (variation 12 of NJL-01's
generator) and its refusal; the checks that the ruling, and by default the standalone posture, is in force. Out: the peer-link pilot, its metric, its verdict and its memo (N32a-34, which
records no decision); the link itself (N32a-35, which depends on this bead and is built only on `build`); the
fleet scheduler, its pilot and KILL-FLEET (their own beads); the daemon; the seeds-only transfer rule and its
refusal (the device layer).

## Technical approach

**Evidence comes first.** This is an owner question about upkeep, asked after a measurement: the pilot records a
phase ledger of one design campaign on `trj` alone and reports g, the share of wall-clock that is not training.
Kill gate KILL-FLEET fires if g < 1/3, or if after one repair cycle the paired lower bound of campaign speedup
is still below 1.3 against the better single machine; its fallback is exactly the default here (standalone
machines, jobs assigned by the role table through ordinary job verbs, the seeds-only rule kept). So:

- if KILL-FLEET has fired, the question is closed by the kill-gate ADR and this bead records the default as
  following from it;
- if the pilot shows g ≥ 1/3 and the gate's speedup holds, the owner is asked whether to build and run the link.

The second measurement is the peer link's own pilot (N32a-34): one week of both daemons' queue traces replayed
on paper, with the overlap of idle and queued time in two readings, the recoverable device-hours R, the ceiling
R over the device-hours used, the share of queued work that could not have crossed and why, and what the
no-cost alternative (submitting to the idle machine by the role table) would already have captured. Its
pre-stated rule binds this bead: if either overlap is under 10%, or the week was `insufficient`, the ruling is
`standalone` without further argument; a ruling of `build` in that case is accepted only with the owner's
explicit statement and reason in the record, and the record's check rejects it otherwise. At or over the
threshold, and with KILL-FLEET not fired, the question goes to the owner with the memo.

**What is put to the owner, when both records exist and no later than the M4 exit review:** g from the fleet
pilot with its ledger; the measured or projected campaign speedup with its interval; from the peer-link pilot,
both overlaps with their intervals, R and its ceiling and the memo; the link's upkeep (the protocol surface, the
authentication between the machines, the chaos tests it adds, the per-campaign transfer budget of at most 1 GB
per campaign-hour); and the two answers. The ruling is one of two values, the same in the pilot's memo, the ADR
and the tests: `standalone` or `build`.

| Answer | Effect |
|---|---|
| `standalone` (the default, and the result of a failed or insufficient pilot or of KILL-FLEET) | `fleet.peer_link = false`. Each machine runs its own daemon and queue. An operator or agent submits to the machine the role table names; an agent that wants work on the other machine opens a session there. `fleet.plan` prints the role-table assignment as advice and dispatches nothing across machines. The peer listener is not started. The seeds-only rule for what may cross stays as a rule for people and scripts. Shared views (archive, leaderboard) still merge between machines by exchanging receipts as files, because that needs no link. `frob capabilities` reports the peer link `not-in-build` with the fallback "standalone machines". The link's mechanism bead (N32a-35) is closed `withdrawn` citing this ADR (N41-17's typed close); KILL-PEERLINK's row is entered only with a `build` ruling, so none is entered, PILOT-PEER-01's `fallback.kill_gate` stays `to-register`, and in N40-03's terms the gate is `not-reached` (the mechanism was not funded). |
| `build` | The mechanism bead N32a-35 is unblocked and funded as Frontier work with its own acceptance (every forwarded job has exactly one owner daemon; one job and one charge per key across both daemons; nothing private crosses, checked on the wire; a dropped link leaves both ledgers consistent) and its kill gate KILL-PEERLINK. In the same change as the ADR this bead enters KILL-PEERLINK's kill row in `registries/gates.toml` in N40-03's format, with N32a-35's trigger (clauses (a) to (c)) and fallback, `decided = "M4"`, `mode = "standing"`, its `reads` and an empty `decision`, and sets PILOT-PEER-01's `fallback.kill_gate` (N32a-34) from `to-register` to `KILL-PEERLINK`. The row cannot wait for N32a-35: until it exists N38a-09's `fundable_bead(N32a-35)` stays `Blocked{kill-gate-to-register}`. N32a-35 checks the row before its first forwarded job and owns its measurements and decision. A kill record has no pass (N32b-25, N40-03). `fleet.peer_link = true` is accepted once KILL-PEERLINK's record holds its first measurement (N32a-35's 1,000-schedule campaign), entered with clauses (a) and (b) not triggered, and no `fired` entry; until then the configuration stays standalone even under `build`. The `not-fired` or `fired` decision comes when clause (c) is read at four weeks, and a `fired` entry on any clause sets the key back to `false` (the fallback). With the link on: the daemons authenticate to each other; `fleet.plan` assigns by role affinity, then shortest estimated work with ageing, and may forward jobs over the link; a machine that drops out has its jobs re-queued on the other only where role and class allow; transfers are metered per class and a field or state slab is refused; the dashboard's device pane gains the peer's queue. |

**Recording.** ADR `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-12-peer-link.md`, the one decision record of the question, with the ruling
(`standalone` or `build`), the date and the owner's ruling verbatim, and the consequences applied; a
configuration key `fleet.peer_link = false | true` in the daemon's configuration, generated from the ADR; the
register row moves to `decided` or `defaulted`. Absent a decision by the M4 exit review the record states
`standalone` by default.

**What this bead builds** (DESIGN: the enforcement of the ruling has one home, here): the generator of the key
`fleet.peer_link` from the ADR; the configuration check that reads it at daemon start (below); and the one
job-admission rule that reads it, applied by the daemon to every job verb: under `false`, a job whose `--machine`
names a machine other than the local one is refused (exit 6) with the repair "submit on that machine" and that
machine's name from the role table (N21c-07). `--machine mac|trj` is an optional input of every `run`-kind
verb: variation 12 of the verb registry's generator (NJL-01), which lands with this bead. This bead writes that
arm of `crates/frob-verbs/src/registry/derive.rs` (the field `machine`, string `mac` or `trj`, CLI `--machine`,
on every `run` entry, appending the refusal) and its test, in the same change that receives the refusal's
number; until then no entry has the field and no surface offers `--machine`. The refusal `machine-not-local` is
in the execution-site family (`6xx`, exit 6; N35x-02's DESIGN, beside 618, which this bead accepts), with the
number the refusal allocation ledger (N35-33) assigns, cited by the constant `MACHINE_NOT_LOCAL` that holds it,
so that the number changes in one place; the slug is never raised as a code. The scheduler's own reading of the key (`fleet.plan` advises and
forwards nothing under `false`) is BD-370's, which depends on this bead through N32a-35.

**Checks.** With `peer_link = false`: no daemon listens on the peer port (a socket scan in the test); a job
submitted with a target on the other machine is refused with the repair "submit on that machine"; `fleet.status`
(N21c-15) reports the other machine's `link` as `standalone`. With `true`: KILL-PEERLINK's row (N32a-35) in
`registries/gates.toml` must hold its first measurement entered with clauses (a) and (b) not triggered and no
`fired` entry; otherwise the configuration is refused (a link cannot be switched on by decision alone).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The register row is `decided`, `defaulted` or closed by the KILL-FLEET ADR at the M4 exit; the exit record
      says which, with g.
- [ ] Under the default no peer listener exists, cross-machine submission is refused with its repair, and
      `fleet.status` reports `standalone`.
- [ ] `peer_link = true` is refused, under either ruling, when KILL-PEERLINK (N32a-35) has no record, a record
      without its first measurement, a first measurement with clause (a) or (b) triggered, or any `fired` entry;
      with a first measurement entered with (a) and (b) not triggered, planted in a scratch gate register, and the
      ruling `build`, it is accepted.
- [ ] Under `build`, the change that records the ruling enters KILL-PEERLINK's kill row (N40-03's format, N32a-35's
      trigger and fallback, `decided = "M4"`, `mode = "standing"`) and sets PILOT-PEER-01's `fallback.kill_gate`
      to `KILL-PEERLINK`, after which N38a-09's `fundable_bead(N32a-35)` no longer reads
      `Blocked{kill-gate-to-register}`; under `standalone` neither is written.
- [ ] Variation 12 of `derive.rs` lands in the change that receives `MACHINE_NOT_LOCAL`'s number: every `run`
      entry gains `machine` (`mac` or `trj`) and appends that code (exit 6); no entry of another kind has it, and
      before the number is issued no entry has the field.
- [ ] A comparison whose arms ran on different device families is still reported as unpaired unless bit equality
      across backends holds, under either answer.
- [ ] The evidence page is generated from the pilot's ledger, not typed, and carries the peer-link pilot's
      record (N32a-34) beside it.
- [ ] A ruling of `build` while the peer-link pilot's overlap is under 10%, with no statement and reason from
      the owner in the record, is rejected by the record's check; with them it is accepted and the pilot's
      verdict stays on record.
- [ ] The daemon's configuration carries the ruling as generated from the ADR, `frob capabilities` shows the
      link's status (`not-in-build` with the fallback "standalone machines" under `standalone`), and the state of
      the mechanism bead N32a-35 matches the decision (closed `withdrawn` citing the ADR, or unblocked). That `fleet.plan`
      forwards nothing under `standalone` is an acceptance criterion of BD-370, which depends on this bead.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_tether5.rs`: `default_is_standalone`; `no_peer_listener_when_false`;
`cross_machine_submit_refused_with_repair`; `link_on_requires_first_measurement` (no KILL-PEERLINK record, a
record with no measurement, a first measurement with clause (a) or (b) triggered, or a `fired` entry on any
clause: refused under either ruling; a first measurement with (a) and (b) not triggered, no `fired` entry and
the ruling `build`: accepted; a row carrying a `pass` is rejected as outside N40-03's outcomes);
`kill_fleet_fired_closes_question`;
`speedup_ceiling_formula` (g = 1/3 gives 1.5; g = 0.2 gives 1.25); `build_below_threshold_needs_owner_reason`
(a record with ruling `build`, pilot overlap 9%, and no owner statement is rejected; the same with a statement
and a reason is accepted); `pilot_insufficient_defaults_standalone`; `ruling_values_are_standalone_or_build`;
`build_ruling_enters_kill_row_and_pilot_gate` (on scratch copies of the gate register and the pilot register: the
row in N40-03's format with clauses (a) to (c) and `mode = "standing"`, PILOT-PEER-01's kill gate
`KILL-PEERLINK`, and N38a-09's `fundable_bead(N32a-35)` no longer `Blocked{kill-gate-to-register}`);
`standalone_enters_no_kill_row`. In `cargo test -p frob-verbs --test registry_schema` (NJL-01's test target):
`variation_12_machine_on_run_entries` (every `run` entry of a `synthetic` registry gains `machine` with the two
values and appends the number `MACHINE_NOT_LOCAL` holds; a `read` entry does not).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/owner_tether5.sh --seed <s>`: starts one daemon with the default configuration, asserts the absence
of the peer listener (socket scan) and the `standalone` status, submits a job naming the other machine (`frob sim
run --workload Q --worlds 64 --seconds 1 --tier batch-cpu --machine <other> --key ot5-cross-<run-id> --json`) and
asserts exit 6 with the code `MACHINE_NOT_LOCAL` holds and the repair naming that machine; then sets `peer_link = true`
on a scratch configuration with no KILL-PEERLINK record and asserts the configuration refusal; then, with a
scratch ADR recording `standalone`, checks it from the user's side (`frob fleet status --json` shows the other
machine's `link` as `standalone` and exits 0; `frob capabilities --json` shows the link `not-in-build` with its
fallback, and the scratch gate register holds no KILL-PEERLINK row); then, with a scratch ADR recording `build`,
asserts that its change entered KILL-PEERLINK's kill row and set PILOT-PEER-01's kill gate, and plants in a
scratch gate register a KILL-PEERLINK row whose
first measurement has clause (a) triggered (asserts the refusal), the same row with (a) and (b) not triggered
(asserts that `peer_link = true` is accepted), and that row with a `fired` entry added (asserts the refusal
again). Logs through the STD-E2E helper to `target/e2e-logs/owner_tether5/<utc>.ndjson` with a transcript: `ts`,
`script`, `bead`, `step`, `event`, `cmd` with exit code and wall time, the configuration digest, the planted
register state per step, `refusal` lines with code and repair, and a `summary`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: 1/(1 − g) at g = 1/3 is 1.5; the training-share ceilings 0.125 and 0.30; the transfer target of 1 GB
per campaign-hour. Negative controls: a peer listener found under the default; a link switched on without its
gate; a state slab offered for transfer (refused by the device layer whatever the answer).

## Considerations

- Two nodes do not need a clever scheduler; the question is whether a link is worth securing and testing at all.
  The default is cheap and loses nothing that the role table does not already give.
- Security: the peer link is the only network surface between machines; under the default there is none to attack.
- If a third machine is ever added the question reopens with a new identifier; this one is tombstoned.
