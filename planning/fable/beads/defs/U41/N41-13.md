## Background

A design carries budgets, and one of them is cost: `cost_max` with a currency, checked at stage 6 of the preflight
by summing the prices on the cards the design references (actuators, batteries, sensors, materials). A build
packet, the output of the fabrication chapter, lists parts. Both need **prices and part identities from
somewhere**, and nothing in a simulator can supply them. Four data-source questions are put to the owner as a
group: one cited source each for dimensional norms, for retrofit prices and legality, for card prices, and for the
parts catalogue. The first two have decision beads in the scene chapter. This bead carries the other two.

- **OPEN-SKEL-2.** Where card prices come from, and under what licence. Fail-closed default: **prices are
  `assumed`, the cost budget is advisory, and `--strict-data` refuses a cost verdict.**
- **OPEN-FOUNDRY-2.** Which parts catalogue and price data, under what licence. Fail-closed default: **card
  references and no prices.**

What the defaults cost: no claim about cost in a published report; a cost comparison between two designs is
printed with every price's provenance and the label `Estimated`; under `--strict-data` a cost verdict is refused
rather than computed from assumed prices. Neither blocks a gate before the build-packet work; both are needed by
M5.

The rule behind the default (every open question obeys it): a default removes a claim or a capability and never
adds one. "Assume the catalogue price" is the textbook example of what a default may not be: it would let a
plausible number stand in for a fact.

## Scope

In: putting the two questions to the owner as one input; the two decision records; what a source must provide to
be admitted; the provenance and licence rules for price fields; the behaviour of budgets and reports under the
default and under an admitted source (the card-price question OPEN-SKEL-2). This bead is the only one that
records either decision.

Out, with the bead that has it: the Foundry side of OPEN-FOUNDRY-2, that is the schema of a pinned, licensed
parts catalogue with its permitted uses, the three options put to the owner (A no prices, B an owner-supplied
catalogue, C user-supplied catalogues only) with their consequences, the refusal FROB-REF-818 of a priced
export without such a catalogue, and their tests (N31b-25, a dependency; it writes no record of its own). Also
out: the card schema and catalogue (the actuator chapter's beads); which real actuator parts ship in the catalogue (a
separate owner question with its own bead); norms and retrofit prices (the scene chapter's beads); the build
packet itself.

## Technical approach

**What is put to the owner at the M4 exit.** A table of candidate sources gathered by the project lead (supplier
catalogues, distributor listings, the owner's own purchase records), each with: licence or terms of use (may the
data be stored, may it be redistributed in a pack or a kit, may it be quoted in a report); coverage of the generic
catalogue's card classes; currency and date; whether part identities are stable. The owner names one source for
card prices and one for the parts catalogue (they may be the same), or none. For the parts catalogue the choice
is presented as the three options prepared by N31b-25: A, no prices (the default made permanent); B, an
owner-supplied catalogue, pinned and licensed; C, user-supplied catalogues only. Naming no source is option A.

**Admission conditions for a price** (enforced by the card loader whatever the answer): a price field has a value,
a currency, a date, a provenance (`measured` for a recorded purchase, `datasheet` for a cited catalogue entry with
its citation, `generic`, or `assumed`) and an uncertainty (a quoted price has `exact` at its date; an estimate has
an interval). A price with provenance `datasheet` and no citation is refused. A source whose terms forbid
redistribution yields card fields marked `no-redistribute`, which the pack builder and the kit builder exclude.

**Behaviour, default in force.**

1. Stage 6 of `design check` computes the cost sum and reports it as advisory: an exceeded `cost_max` is a finding
   with the share of the sum that is `assumed`, and it refuses only when no summed price is `assumed`.
2. With `--strict-data` a cost verdict (within budget, or exceeded) is refused if any summed price is `assumed`,
   with `FROB-REF-113` at stage 6 naming the cards; the repair is to cite or measure those prices.
3. Reports print cost with its provenance share and the label `Estimated`; the release wording lint refuses a cost
   claim in release documentation.
4. A build packet's bill lists card references and no prices; a priced export is refused with FROB-REF-818
   (the Foundry behaviour, specified and tested in N31b-25).

**Behaviour with an admitted source.** Cards updated from the source are new card versions (cards are immutable
and content-addressed) with the source cited; designs that reference them by class resolve to the new versions on
their next edit; cost verdicts over fully cited prices are ordinary verdicts of class `Tested` as of the price
date; the packet's bill carries prices with their date and source.

**Recording.** ADRs `docs/adr/ADR-PROG-13-card-prices.md` and `ADR-PROG-14-parts-catalogue.md`, each with the
option chosen, the source and licence if any, the permitted uses and the owner's signature; a `sources`
registry entry for each admitted source with its terms; register rows OPEN-SKEL-2 and OPEN-FOUNDRY-2 move to
`decided` or `defaulted`. These are the only records of the two decisions: the Foundry bead N31b-25 writes
none.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Both register rows are `decided` or `defaulted` with ADRs at the M5 exit.
- [ ] Under the default: a design over its cost budget on assumed prices gets an advisory finding, not a refusal;
      `--strict-data` refuses the cost verdict and names the cards; a report prints the provenance share; a packet
      lists no prices.
- [ ] A price with provenance `datasheet` and no citation is refused; a `no-redistribute` field is excluded from a
      pack and from a kit.
- [ ] With a synthetic admitted source (labelled `synthetic`), a design whose every price is cited gets an ordinary
      budget verdict, and the card versions that changed are new identities.
- [ ] A release note containing a cost claim under the default is refused by the wording lint.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/open_prices.rs`: `assumed_prices_make_budget_advisory`;
`strict_data_refuses_cost_verdict`; `datasheet_price_without_citation_refused`; `no_redistribute_excluded_from_pack`;
`cited_prices_give_ordinary_verdict`; `price_update_is_new_card_version`; `packet_bill_has_no_prices_by_default`;
`cost_sum_exact`: three cards at 120.00, 45.50 and 9.99 in one currency sum to 175.49 in exact decimal arithmetic.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/owner_prices.sh` (sources the STD-E2E helper): `frob design check fixtures/quad-a.fbot --json` with
`cost_max` set below the assumed sum (expects the advisory cost finding with its `assumed` share and exit 2, the
exit of `design.check` for reported findings; never exit 3), the same with `--strict-data` (expects exit 3 with
`FROB-REF-113` naming the cards whose price is `assumed`), then `frob design check fixtures/quad-a-cited.fbot
--json`, a copy whose card references resolve to new card versions with synthetic cited prices (labelled
`synthetic`; expects exit 3 with `FROB-REF-115`, the exceeded cost budget, its value, limit and largest
contributors); a card with a `datasheet` price and no citation is refused at load. Logs through the STD-E2E helper
to `target/e2e-logs/owner_prices/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a transcript; every line carries `ts`, `script`,
`bead` ("N41-13"), `step`, `event`: `cmd` lines with the exact command line, exit code and wall time; the evidence
block with the provenance share; the cost sum against `cost_max` with currency; `refusal` lines with the code and
the cards named; one `assert` per negative control with `caught`; on failure the first differing value and the
fixture path; a `summary` with assertions passed and failed.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the two default texts; the exact sum above. Negative controls: a cost verdict computed from assumed
prices under `--strict-data`; a planted "assume the catalogue price" default (rejected by the register loader); a
cost claim in release documentation.

## Considerations

- Prices age; a cost verdict is true as of its price date and says so. A stale price is not an error, but a report
  prints the date.
- Currency conversion is not performed: a design's cost budget and its cards' prices must be in one currency, or
  the sum is refused.
- The answer does not change any physics result; it changes only what may be said about cost.
