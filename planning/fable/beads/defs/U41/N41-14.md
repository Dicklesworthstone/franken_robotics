## Background

Two open questions were decided by the project owner on 2026-11-22, before implementation began, and are kept in
the register as a recorded decision rather than deleted (a closed question is tombstoned, never reused).

- **OPEN-SUITE-2** asked whether the core moves to a nightly at or above 1.100 so that the MCP server crate
  `frob-mcp` may join the core build graph. The background: as found, the sibling fastmcp_rust declares a minimum
  compiler version of 1.100 while FrankenSim pins a 1.99 nightly; Cargo refuses such a build unless told to ignore
  the declared minimum, and the design does not build on an ignored constraint.
- **OPEN-TETHER-1** asked whether `frob-mcp` can link the verb handler crates in process, or must talk to a
  separate process.

**The decision.** The whole build graph uses **one toolchain T**, chosen at the admission gate INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 in a
fixed order of preference: (a) the suite's common nightly, the one most siblings already pin
(`nightly-2026-08-31` on the day of writing), which requires advancing FrankenSim's pin from `nightly-2026-07-06`
in FrankenSim's own repository and moving FrankenSymPy and fastmcp_rust; (b) FrankenSim's present pin, if
FrankenSim cannot yet build on (a), with the train to (a) scheduled. fastmcp_rust is brought onto T by the upstream
work item FD-FMCP-01 (priority P0, at M0), done by this program in fastmcp_rust's repository under its own rules,
and complete when fastmcp_rust's own test suite and its MCP conformance checks pass on T. **`frob-mcp` is then an
ordinary member of the core workspace: it links the verb crate, runs verb implementations in process, and shares
the one toolchain and the one asupersync package with the rest of the graph.**

What the decision costs: the upstream work of FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01 at M0. If the admission INT-FMCP-01 cannot
pass within the M0 budget after both have been attempted, kill gate KILL-MCP names the fallbacks in order.

Rejected alternatives (recorded so that nobody re-proposes them without new facts): building with the declared
minimum version ignored (an ignored constraint is not a foundation); a second toolchain for an MCP annex with the
server talking to the core over a process boundary (two toolchains to move, two copies of shared types, and verbs
no longer run in process); forking fastmcp_rust (siblings are changed in their own repositories, never forked
without a recorded kill decision).

## Scope

In: the ADR that records the decision with its date and reasons; the register state for both identifiers; the
confirmation step at M0 that reads the two admission gates; the link to KILL-MCP if confirmation fails. Out: the
toolchain matrix and the choice of T (BD-031); the upstream changes (FD-FMCP-01, FD-ASUP-01, BD-023, BD-008); the
toolchain train (the suite unit's train beads).

## Technical approach

1. **ADR.** `docs/adr/ADR-OPEN-SUITE-2.md` (DESIGN: the register's usual name for an open question's record; the
   identifier ADR-SUITE-02 is taken by the plan's §33.14 decision that the referee moves behind a process
   boundary, and BD-031's two ADRs follow it in that series) with: the two questions as asked; the decision text above;
   the date 2026-11-22; the owner as decider; the candidates (a) and (b); the costs; the rejected alternatives; and
   a "confirmed by" section left open for step 3.
2. **Register.** The two rows are written by the register's parts, OPEN-TETHER-1 by N41-OPEN6 and OPEN-SUITE-2 by
   N41-OPEN7 (both land before this bead), with `status = "decided"`, `adr` pointing at the ADR's path and
   `decided_by.kind = "owner"`; this bead writes no second copy of them and no field of either. Their `blocks`
   are the parts' (INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 and INT-FMCP-01 for OPEN-SUITE-2; INT-FMCP-01 for OPEN-TETHER-1), and this
   bead tests that both rows and the ADR agree (path, date, decider). The loader refuses a
   later row that reuses either identifier.
3. **Confirmation at the M0 exit** (`frob_conformance::exit::confirm_suite_02`, run by the exit runner as an
   attached check of N40-05: this bead adds the record `id = "suite-02-confirm"`, `owner = "N41-14"`, `step = 10`,
   `command = "scripts/exit/confirm-suite-02.sh"`, `since = "M0"` to `scripts/exit/checks.toml`; the command prints
   the check object of N40-05's contract, with each failing condition as a block), which reads recorded admission
   results and never re-decides anything:
   - INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 has a recorded result with every cell of the matrix filled and every row passing on T, and
     the chosen T is written in both `rust-toolchain.toml` and the stack lock;
   - INT-FMCP-01 has a recorded result: fastmcp_rust's own suite and conformance checks green on T at the pinned
     commit; `frob-mcp` builds in the core graph on T with exactly one asupersync package (`cargo tree -d` shows no
     duplicate); it lists the tools generated from the verb registry; it passes the protocol smoke test over stdio
     and HTTP;
   - no annex or crate carries a toolchain file of its own, apart from the stated exceptions (the Lean directory
     is not Rust; the auditor crate builds on T in the graph and also alone on a stable release).
   When all three hold, the ADR's "confirmed by" section is filled with the two admission record identifiers and
   the commit.
4. **If confirmation fails.** The exit runner does not edit the decision. It reports which condition failed and
   requires the KILL-MCP decision bead's ADR: KILL-MCP fires only if INT-FMCP-01 cannot pass within the M0 budget
   after FD-FMCP-01 and FD-ASUP-01 have been attempted, and its fallbacks are taken in the order its record names
   (the first is the one that applies when a surface still brings a second asupersync package). Agents meanwhile
   use the command-line interface, which is complete on its own. The owner's decision stands as the target state
   and the ADR gains a dated note that a fallback is in force.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The ADR exists with the decision, date, candidates, costs and rejected alternatives.
- [ ] Both identifiers are in the register as `decided`, tombstoned against reuse, and listed among the four
      closed or decided questions.
- [ ] The check is attached in `scripts/exit/checks.toml` (`suite-02-confirm`, step 10, since M0) and the M0 exit
      record carries its section.
- [ ] At the M0 exit the confirmation check passes on recorded admission results and fills "confirmed by"; or it
      fails with the failing condition named and the KILL-MCP ADR required.
- [ ] A planted second toolchain file in an annex, and a planted duplicate asupersync package, each make the
      confirmation fail.
- [ ] The check reads records only: with the admission records removed it fails as "not yet confirmed", never
      passes by default.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/confirm_suite_02.rs` on scratch records: `confirmed_when_both_admissions_pass`;
`not_confirmed_without_records`; `second_toolchain_file_fails`; `duplicate_asupersync_fails`;
`t_mismatch_between_lock_and_toolchain_file_fails`; `failure_requires_kill_mcp_adr`;
`identifiers_cannot_be_reused`; `rows_and_adr_agree` (both rows point at the ADR's path; a scratch row with
another date or decider fails); `attached_check_contract` (the command prints one valid check object and exits 0
whether or not it found a block; with the duplicate asupersync plant its `blocks` name that condition).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/confirm_one_toolchain.sh`: on the real workspace after the M0 admissions, runs the confirmation check
and `cargo tree -d` for the `frob-mcp` target through the offload shim, asserting one asupersync package and one
toolchain; on a scratch copy plants the two violations and asserts the failures. Logs through the STD-E2E helper
to `target/e2e-logs/confirm_one_toolchain/<utc>.ndjson`: `cmd`, exit codes, T as
read from both files, the package count, the admission record identifiers, `assert` lines, and a `summary` naming
INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 and INT-FMCP-01 as read.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the decision date; the candidates' nightly names as recorded FACTs to be re-read at M0. Negative
controls: a second toolchain file; a duplicate asupersync package; a confirmation attempted with no admission
records.

## Considerations

- A recorded decision still needs a bead because it has a cost at M0 and a confirmation that can fail; without
  one, "decided" would silently mean "done".
- T moves after M0 only by a toolchain train: one coordinated change here and in every consumed sibling that needs
  it, followed by the admission and parity gates. The ADR is amended by a dated note at each train, not rewritten.
- The nightly names are facts of one day; the ADR records them as such and the matrix re-reads them.
