## Background

A closed bead is not acceptance. A sibling project found 87 work items closed within 12 seconds with one shared
close reason, while the product served figures from a stand-in (FACT, recorded in that project's review). The
tracker made progress visible and false at the same time. This program's answer is a set of rules for how a bead
is read, when it is ready, how it closes and what happens to its dependents when a kill gate fires. This bead
turns those rules into checks that run on the tracker export at every exit and on every close.

**How a bead's done-condition is read** (DESIGN, binding on every bead).

- A gate identifier in a done-condition means the gate's recorded value together with its caught negative control,
  as the owning chapter defines the gate and the gate register lists it. Phrases such as "rows of", "on the
  referee" or "for the M2 verbs" restrict the bead to the part of the gate that can be measured when it closes.
  The gate's PASS is recorded when the last bead that cites it closes. Admission gates are read the same way, with
  the admission record as the value.
- "Decided" after a kill gate means that its trigger was evaluated on measured values and that the outcome, either
  way, is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate that fires replaces the bead's capability by the fallback named in
  advance; the bead closes on the fallback, and its dependents are re-read against it.
- Numbers in a done-condition are TARGET thresholds or exact known answers. None is a measurement.
- A bead sits in the milestone the plan assigns to its mechanism; where a gate names a different earliest
  milestone, the value is recorded when the bead closes.
- Upstream work is bead work: a change to a sibling is a bead of this graph with the label `upstream`, done in the
  sibling's repository under its own rules and mirrored as a bead in the sibling's tracker; every bead that stands
  on a sibling has an interim posture that holds until the upstream change lands.

## Scope

In: the lifecycle rules below as machine checks over the tracker export, the bead logs and the registries; the
closed set of close reasons; the readiness rules; the split and add rules; the exit-review close rule. Out: the
structural hygiene of the graph (N41-16); the bead log format and the artifact graph (their own bead); kill-gate
records and decisions; the per-capability checklist of "implemented" (its own bead); the tracker tool itself.

## Technical approach

Files (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/src/tracker/lifecycle.rs`; wrapper `scripts/bead-lifecycle-check.sh`;
report `target/tracker-check/lifecycle.json` (schema `frob.beadlifecycle/1`). Inputs: the tracker's JSONL export;
`docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson`; `registries/gates.toml`; the pilot ledger; envelope approvals;
`registries/upstream.toml`.

**Rule 1: what a bead inherits.** Every capability bead inherits the eight conditions of "implemented" and the
guards against false progress: typed close reasons, negative controls, live-dependency proofs, the `synthetic`
label on synthetic fixtures, behaviour and not plumbing, no self-certification. Check: a bead that delivers part
of a capability lists in its acceptance criteria which of the eight conditions stay open; the capability's record
stays `partial` until the bead that closes its gate is closed (cross-check with the capability registry).

**Rule 2: how a bead closes.** The close reason is typed, from the closed set `pass`, `fallback`, `superseded`,
`duplicate`, `withdrawn`. For `pass` the reason cites the bead's own test and that test's output: a line in
`docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson` whose verdict is `pass`, whose claim identifiers resolve in the artifact graph,
and whose recording session is not the session that implemented the bead. Checks: the log line exists and
parses; the verdict is `pass`; every claim identifier resolves; the recording session differs from every
implementing session of the bead; the cited test names appear in the bead's test plan.

**Rule 3: shared close reasons are a failure signal.** Two or more beads closed with byte-identical reason text,
or citing the same log line, are listed and the exit review must reject them. Bursts are listed too: more than
five beads closed by one session within 60 seconds (DESIGN threshold, from the incident above).

**Rule 4: a closed bead is not acceptance.** Acceptance is the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml`. Check: no
status table, report or release manifest derives "accepted" or "implemented" from bead states; the generators read
the gate register (a source scan of the generators plus a planted table built from bead states).

**Rule 5: kill gates.** A bead whose kill gate fires closes with reason `fallback` and the name of the fallback,
and its close record names the ADR. Its dependents are then re-read: one that needs the killed capability closes
with reason `superseded` and the identifier of the ADR; one that can stand on the fallback keeps its dependency.
Checks: a `fallback` close names an ADR that exists and a fallback that matches the kill gate's record; every
dependent of a `fallback`-closed bead carries a re-read note dated after the ADR (kept, or `superseded` with the
ADR); a `superseded` close names an ADR.

**Rule 6: work packages.** A bead belongs to exactly one work package: the package that owns its crate at its
milestone or, where a bead touches several crates, the package that owns the crate in which its gate is measured.
Exit reviews belong to none. (Checked by the work-package script; this check only confirms it ran.)

**Rule 7: pilots and compute (readiness).** A bead for a Frontier or Moonshot mechanism is not ready until the
pilot of its chapter has a recorded result. A bead whose run exceeds the machine envelope (parity baselines,
training, the head-to-head, the rank-agreement experiment, the partial gate, the tournament) names its budget and
is not ready until the owner's approval is recorded. Checks: no bead labelled `frontier` or `moonshot` (other than
pilots) is in progress or closed `pass` without a pilot record dated before its first log line; no over-envelope
bead has a run recorded without an approval it cites.

**Rule 8: splitting and adding.** A bead too large for one session is split into children that carry its key with
a numeric suffix (for example BD-123.1, BD-123.2); the children inherit its dependencies and the parent closes
when its last child closes. A bead discovered during implementation takes the next free key, depends only on
existing beads, and the exit review of its milestone gains it as a dependency. Checks: suffixed children have a
parent with the stem key and a superset of its dependencies; a parent is not closed while a child is open; a bead
created after conversion is an ancestor of its milestone's exit review.

**Rule 9: exit reviews.** An exit review implements nothing. It closes when every gate of its milestone has a
recorded value and a caught negative control in the gate register, every kill gate due at the milestone has its
ADR, and the honest-status table regenerates from the logs. Checks: an exit-review bead has no implementation log
lines of its own; its close cites the exit record; the three conditions are read from the gate register.

**Rule 10: upstream beads.** A bead labelled `upstream` has a record in `registries/upstream.toml` with its
fixture, interim posture, switch-over rule and the identifier of its mirrored bead in the sibling's tracker; it
closes `pass` only with evidence that the change landed through the sibling's own gates.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the ten rules has a planted violation on a scratch export that the check reports with the bead key.
- [ ] A `pass` close with no log line, with a failing verdict, with an unresolved claim identifier, or recorded by
      the implementing session is each rejected.
- [ ] Two beads sharing one close reason text are listed; six closes by one session in 60 seconds are listed.
- [ ] A `fallback` close without an ADR is rejected; a dependent with no re-read note is listed.
- [ ] A Frontier bead in progress with no pilot result, and an over-envelope run with no approval, are each listed.
- [ ] A parent closed before its last child is rejected; a discovered bead that is not an ancestor of its exit
      review is listed.
- [ ] The exit runner calls the check and blocks on any failing list; the report is deterministic.

`exit_review_has_no_implementation_lines`; `upstream_bead_needs_mirror`; `status_table_not_from_bead_states`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/bead_lifecycle.sh`: builds a scratch tracker export of twelve beads with real log files produced by
running two trivial tests through the test kit, closes them in the right and the wrong ways, and runs the check;
then fires a scratch kill gate and walks the re-read of three dependents. Logs: `cmd`, exit codes, one `assert`
line per rule with the offending bead and the observed close record, `metric` lines for counts by close reason,
and a `summary` naming GATE-GAUNT-01 as supported.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the closed set of five close reasons; the incident's numbers (87 beads, 12 seconds, one reason) as
the burst fixture. Negative controls: the planted violations of the ten rules. Determinism: reports byte-identical
across platforms.

## Considerations

- The check cannot tell whether a test is meaningful; it can tell whether a close cites a test that ran, by
  somebody else, with claims that resolve. Substance is the exit reviewer's judgement.
- Session identity comes from the tracker's audit trail and the log's recording field; a swarm that shares one
  identity defeats the self-certification rule, so the exit runner refuses logs whose recording identity is the
  shared swarm account.
- Close reasons `duplicate` and `withdrawn` exist because drafting produced some duplicated decision beads; they
  need a pointer to the surviving bead or an ADR, and are never counted as work done.
- If the bead log format changes, this check changes with it in the same commit.
