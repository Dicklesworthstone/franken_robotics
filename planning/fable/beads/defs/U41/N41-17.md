## Background

A closed bead is not acceptance. A sibling project found 87 work items closed within 12 seconds with one shared
close reason, while the product served figures from a stand-in (FACT, recorded in that project's review). The
tracker made progress visible and false at the same time. This program's answer is a set of rules for how a bead
is read, when it is ready, how it closes and what happens to its dependents when a kill gate fires. This bead
turns those rules into checks that run on the tracker export at every exit and on every close.

**How a bead's done-condition is read** (DESIGN, binding on every bead).

- A gate identifier in a done-condition means the gate's recorded value together with its caught negative control,
  as the owning chapter defines the gate and the gate register lists it. Phrases such as "rows of", "on the
  referee" or "for the M2 verbs" restrict the bead to the part of the gate that can be measured when it closes.
  The gate's PASS is recorded when the last bead that cites it closes. Admission gates are read the same way, with
  the admission record as the value.
- "Decided" after a kill gate means that its trigger was evaluated on measured values and that the outcome, either
  way, is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate that fires replaces the bead's capability by the fallback named in
  advance; the bead closes on the fallback, and its dependents are re-read against it.
- A bead whose done-condition is the measurement of a gate that a kill gate's trigger reads is done when the
  gate's value is recorded with its controls caught, whichever side of the threshold it falls: after the one repair
  cycle a missed value is recorded `fail` and the bead closes `recorded` on that record (rule 2), so that the
  decision bead that depends on it becomes ready in exactly the case the kill gate exists for. A failed gate that
  no kill gate reads keeps its bead open: the failure blocks the exit.
- Numbers in a done-condition are TARGET thresholds or exact known answers. None is a measurement.
- A bead sits in the milestone the plan assigns to its mechanism; where a gate names a different earliest
  milestone, the value is recorded when the bead closes.
- Upstream work is bead work: a change to a sibling is a bead of this graph with the label `upstream`, done in the
  sibling's repository under its own rules and mirrored as a bead in the sibling's tracker; every bead that stands
  on a sibling has an interim posture that holds until the upstream change lands.

## Scope

export, the bead logs and the registries for rules 1 and 4 to 10: inheritance, acceptance read from the gate
register, kill-gate closes and the re-reading of dependents, work-package membership, the readiness rules, the

of a closing log line resolves in the graph. It also calls the close audit from the lifecycle check and merges
that audit's findings into the lifecycle report.

Out, with the bead that has it: the typed-close check itself (that a reason is one of the six types of rule 2 and
carries that type's content, the cited test bound to its bead, the cited output, the recording session differing
from the implementing sessions, shared reasons after normalization, shared outputs, bursts of closes, the
thresholds, and the replay of the 87-closure incident) is the close audit of N38b-03 (M0), the one implementation
of rules 2 and 3 over the tracker export; this bead states the six types as the binding reading and checks only
what the audit cannot (claims in the artifact graph, decisions behind `withdrawn`, the kill read behind

`gate-record` lines and its closing rule (N29a-09) and the artifact graph (BD-095), both read here; kill-gate records and
decisions; the per-capability checklist of "implemented" (its own bead); the tracker tool itself.

## Technical approach

Files (DESIGN): `crates/frob-conformance/src/tracker/lifecycle.rs`; wrapper `scripts/bead-lifecycle-check.sh`;
report `target/tracker-check/lifecycle.json` (schema `frob.beadlifecycle/1`). Inputs: the tracker's JSONL export;
`docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson`; `registries/gates.toml`; the pilot register
`crates/frob-conformance/registry/pilots.toml` through `pilots::fundable` (N38a-09); envelope approvals (the
`[[request]]` rows of `registries/compute_envelope.toml`) and the envelope meter rows (`envelope_meter`, N36-20);
the decision records under `docs/adr/`; `registries/upstream.toml`; the close audit's report
`target/gauntlet/close_audit.json` (N38b-03).

**Rule 1: what a bead inherits.** Every capability bead inherits the eight conditions of "implemented" and the
guards against false progress: typed close reasons, negative controls, live-dependency proofs, the `synthetic`
label on synthetic fixtures, behaviour and not plumbing, no self-certification. Check: a bead that delivers part
of a capability lists in its acceptance criteria which of the eight conditions stay open; the capability's record
stays `partial` until the bead that closes its gate is closed (cross-check with the capability registry
`registries/capabilities.toml` of N00-02: a record printed `implemented` while a bead that cites its gate is open
is listed).

**Rule 2: how a bead closes.** The close reason is typed, from the closed set `pass`, `recorded`, `fallback`,
`superseded`, `duplicate`, `withdrawn` (six; the plan names `pass`, `fallback` and `superseded`, and the other three
are DESIGN of this program):

| Type | Meaning | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| `pass` | The bead's observable holds | Its own test and that test's output, as below |
| `recorded` | The bead measured a gate that a kill gate's trigger reads, and the part is recorded `fail` after the one repair cycle (or after the one measurement, for a trigger that names no cycle); no capability is claimed | Its own test; the gate-record line with verdict `fail`, recorded by a session that did not implement the bead, whose receipt shows every listed control `caught`; the kill gate; and, where the trigger's text names a repair cycle or an optimization pass, the `note` line that opened the cycle (`cause = "repair-cycle: <failed receipt>: <hypothesis>"`, N40-03 step 5) |
| `fallback` | The bead's kill gate fired; the fallback delivers the capability under its own name | The kill gate, the fallback's name and the ADR |
| `superseded` | The bead needed a killed capability | The ADR |
| `duplicate` | Another bead carries the work | The surviving bead |
| `withdrawn` | The bead is not needed; no capability is claimed | The recorded reason; when the reason is a decision, the decision record: the ADR of a kill gate whose outcome left this bead's branch untaken (`withdrawn: not needed: <kill gate> not fired, docs/adr/ADR-<gate>-<Mn>.md`), the `[[pilot.result]]` whose falsifier held (rule 7), or the envelope row that was not approved (`withdrawn: not run: no envelope, R<n>`, N44-01) |

DESIGN reasons. `recorded` exists because otherwise a measurement bead whose acceptance is the gate's row could
not close when the gate fails, and the decision bead that depends on it could never become ready in the one case
it must decide; it is a type of its own, not a `pass` on a failing line, so that `pass` keeps meaning "the
observable holds" and no table counts a `recorded` bead as work toward the gate. Untaken branches and unrunnable
beads close `withdrawn` with the decision cited, not with a seventh type, because they claim nothing, which is what
`withdrawn` means, and citing the decision is the check that matters. For `pass` the reason cites the bead's own test and that test's output: a line in
`docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson` whose verdict is `pass`, whose claim identifiers resolve in the artifact graph,
and whose recording session is not the session that implemented the bead. Enforcement is split in two. The close
audit of N38b-03 checks, for every closed bead, that the reason is typed and carries its type's content, that the
cited test exists and is bound to the bead (by its header or name, or by the bead's test plan naming it), that the
cited output exists with verdict `pass` and names the bead, and that the recording session differs from every
implementing session. The check built here adds the part that audit cannot do at M0: every identifier in the
closing line's `claim_ids` resolves to a node of the artifact graph (BD-095) whose kind is a claim and whose
evidence closure contains the cited test's receipt; an identifier that does not resolve, or resolves to a claim of
another bead, is reported `unresolved-claim` with the bead and the identifier.

**Rule 3: shared close reasons are a failure signal.** A reason shared by several beads, or one output cited by
several beads, is listed and the exit review must reject it; bursts of closes by one session are listed for the
exit reviewer to disposition. The detection (normalization of reason text, the exception for a receipt that names
every bead of a legitimate batch) and the thresholds (two or more beads; more than 5 closes by one session in
60 seconds, DESIGN) are those of the close audit of N38b-03 and are set nowhere else. The lifecycle check runs
that audit on the same export, copies its findings (`untyped`, `foreign-test`, `no-output`, `self-closed`,
`shared-reason`, `shared-output`, `burst`) into `lifecycle.json` under `close_audit`, and fails when the audit
fails.

**Rule 4: a closed bead is not acceptance.** Acceptance is the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml`. Check: no
status table, report or release manifest derives "accepted" or "implemented" from bead states; the generators read
the gate register (a source scan of the generators plus a planted table built from bead states).

**Rule 5: kill gates.** A bead whose kill gate fires closes with reason `fallback` and the name of the fallback,
and its close record names the ADR. Its dependents are then re-read: one that needs the killed capability closes
with reason `superseded` and the identifier of the ADR; one that can stand on the fallback keeps its dependency.
A bead that exists only on the branch a decision did not take (the fallback's own work when the gate did not fire,
or the reverse) closes `withdrawn`, citing the decision's ADR. A bead that measures a gate a kill gate reads closes
`recorded` when the part is recorded `fail` after the repair cycle (rule 2). Checks: a `fallback` close names an ADR
that exists under `docs/adr/` and a fallback that matches the kill gate's record (`fallback-without-adr`); every
dependent of a `fallback`-closed bead carries a re-read note dated after the ADR, kept or `superseded` with the ADR
(`dependent-not-reread`); a `superseded` close names an ADR that exists (`superseded-without-adr`); a `withdrawn`
close that cites a decision cites one that exists and says what the close says: an ADR whose entry in force (for
the bead's scope) has the outcome named, a `[[pilot.result]]` whose verdict is `falsifier-holds`, or an envelope
row whose status was `requested` or `declined` on the close date (`withdrawn-without-decision`); a `recorded` close
names a gate that is in the `reads` of a kill row, cites a gate-record line of that gate with verdict `fail`,
recorded by a session outside the bead's implementing sessions, whose receipt resolves and lists every control of
the row `caught` (for a receipt set of N38b-19, its members together), and, where the trigger's text contains "repair cycle" or "optimization pass", an earlier `note`
line opening the cycle with a failed receipt older than the cited one (`recorded-close-invalid`).

**Rule 6: work packages.** A bead belongs to exactly one work package: the package that owns its crate at its
milestone or, where a bead touches several crates, the package that owns the crate in which its gate is measured.
Exit reviews belong to none. (Checked by the work-package script of N44-04; this check only confirms its report
exists for the export it reads, `wp-check-not-run` otherwise.)

**Rule 7: pilots and compute (readiness).** A bead for a Frontier or Moonshot mechanism is not ready until the
pilot of its chapter has a recorded result. A bead whose run exceeds the machine envelope (parity baselines,
training, the head-to-head, the rank-agreement experiment, the partial gate, the tournament) names its budget and
is not ready until the owner's approval is recorded. This bead is the one statement of the readiness rule and
its one machine check. The envelope half reads the request rows of `registries/compute_envelope.toml`, which
N44-01 registers and whose rulings the owner-decision beads fill: "names its budget" means the bead cites a
`[[request]]` row by its `id`; "approval recorded" means that row has status `approved` or `amended` with
`approved_by`, `date`, `decision` and `token` filled. N44-01 states what a row's status means and builds no
check of the rule. The pilot half reads the pilot register's funding function, `pilots::fundable(work_package)`
of N38a-09: "has a recorded result" means the function returns `Fundable` (every upper-tier mechanism of the
package has a result `falsifier-rejected` or `reproduced`); a result `falsifier-holds` blocks the package and its
fallback ships. **When a pilot's falsifier holds** the mechanism is never funded, so the beads that exist only for
the funded mechanism (the mechanism, its gate-measurement and end-to-end beads) can never become ready, and a kill
decision that depends on them would wait for ever. The session that records the funding decision on the pilot's
result (the work package's funding decision point, which calls `pilots::fundable`) therefore closes each of them
`withdrawn`, citing the pilot and its `[[pilot.result]]`; the kill-decision bead then becomes ready, records its
outcome on the pilot's numbers (`fired` for a `pilot-before-funding` row, N40-03), and its ADR lists each of those
beads as "withdrawn before this decision". The same holds for an envelope row that is not approved: the beads that
exist only under approval close `withdrawn` citing the row (N44-01, step 5). Checks: no bead labelled `frontier`
or `moonshot` (other than pilots) is in progress or closed `pass` while `pilots::fundable` for its work package was
not `Fundable` at its first log line (`not-ready-no-pilot`); no over-envelope run lacks an approval: every row of
the envelope meter (`envelope_meter`, N36-20) whose usage exceeds E0 (more than one device, 8 cores, 16 GiB or one
hour) has its scope in the `admits` of a row of `registries/compute_envelope.toml` that is `approved` or `amended`,
whose `date` is not later than the run's start, and every bead whose `gates` field names the gate of a `gate:`
scope so metered cites that row's `id` (`not-ready-no-approval`).

**Rule 8: splitting and adding.** A bead too large for one session is split into children that carry its key with
a numeric suffix (for example BD-123.1, BD-123.2); the children inherit its dependencies and the parent closes
when its last child closes. A bead discovered during implementation takes the next free key, depends only on
existing beads, and the exit review of its milestone gains it as a dependency. Checks: suffixed children have a
parent with the stem key and a superset of its dependencies; a parent is not closed while a child is open; a bead
created after conversion is an ancestor of its milestone's exit review, unless check 3(b) of N41-16 exempts it
(`split-rule-broken` for each).

**Rule 9: exit reviews.** An exit review implements nothing. It closes when every gate of its milestone has a
recorded value and a caught negative control in the gate register, every kill gate due at the milestone has its
ADR, and the honest-status table regenerates from the logs. Checks: an exit-review bead has no implementation log
lines of its own (`exit-review-implements`); its close cites the exit record; the three conditions are read from
the gate register.

**Rule 10: upstream beads.** A bead labelled `upstream` has a record in `registries/upstream.toml` (schema
`frob.upstream/1`) with its fixture, interim posture, switch-over rule and the identifier of its mirrored bead in
the sibling's tracker (fields `fixture`, `interim_posture`, `switch_over`, `mirror`); it closes `pass` only with
evidence that the change landed through the sibling's own gates. The presence of the fields is hygiene check H-5
of N00-08. The check here is the close: an `upstream` bead closed `pass` has a record whose `status` is `landed`
or `adopted` and whose closing log line cites the sibling commit and the sibling gate run that accepted it
(`upstream-pass-not-landed`).

**Harness reasons and the exit runner.** Each finding carries its harness reason from N41-REF: rule 1
`partial-clauses-unstated` and `implemented-while-gate-open`; rule 2 `unresolved-claim`; rules 2 and 3 the close
audit's own reasons, reused (`untyped`, `foreign-test`, `no-output`, `self-closed`, `shared-reason`, `shared-output`,
`burst`); rule 4 `status-from-bead-states`; rule 5 `fallback-without-adr`, `superseded-without-adr`,
`dependent-not-reread`, `withdrawn-without-decision`, `recorded-close-invalid`; rule 6 `wp-check-not-run`; rule 7
`not-ready-no-pilot`, `not-ready-no-approval`; rule 8 `split-rule-broken`; rule 9 `exit-review-implements`; rule 10
`upstream-pass-not-landed`. The check is an attached check of the exit runner of N40-05, registered by this bead in
`scripts/exit/checks.toml`: `id = "bead-lifecycle"`, `owner = "N41-17"`, `step = 6` (the step that lists shared
close reasons and kill decisions due), `command = "scripts/bead-lifecycle-check.sh"`, `since = "M1"`, called as
`<command> <Mn> --root <dir> --json`; every finding is a block with its reason, and the `burst` list is a warning
for the reviewer to disposition (rule 3).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the ten rules has a planted violation on a scratch export that the check reports with the bead key.
- [ ] A `pass` close with an unresolved claim identifier, or with one that resolves to another bead's claim, is
      rejected as `unresolved-claim` by this bead's check.
- [ ] A `pass` close with no log line, with a failing verdict, or recorded by the implementing session; two beads
      sharing one close reason text; and six closes by one session in 60 seconds each appear in `lifecycle.json`
      under `close_audit`, produced by the close audit of N38b-03 that the lifecycle check calls, and the first
      four fail the lifecycle check.
- [ ] An `upstream` bead closed `pass` whose record is still `open`, or whose closing line cites no sibling gate
      run, is rejected.
- [ ] A `fallback` close without an ADR is rejected; a dependent with no re-read note is listed.
- [ ] A `recorded` close is accepted on a gate in a kill row's `reads` whose part is `fail` after one repair cycle
      with every control caught, and rejected (`recorded-close-invalid`) on a gate no kill row reads, on a `pass`
      line, on a line recorded by an implementing session, or with no cycle-opening line where the trigger names a
      repair cycle.
- [ ] A `withdrawn` close citing a kill gate's ADR whose outcome in force differs from the one the close names, a
      pilot result whose verdict is not `falsifier-holds`, or an envelope row that is `approved`, is rejected
      (`withdrawn-without-decision`); the same closes citing a matching decision are accepted.
- [ ] A Frontier bead in progress while `pilots::fundable` for its package is not `Fundable` (no result, or a
      result `falsifier-holds`), and an over-envelope meter row with no approved row admitting its scope, are each
      listed.
- [ ] `scripts/exit/checks.toml` holds the record `bead-lifecycle` (owner N41-17, step 6, since M1); at the M0 exit
      the runner does not run it, at M1 it does, and a planted violation is a block naming `bead-lifecycle`.
- [ ] A parent closed before its last child is rejected; a discovered bead that is not an ancestor of its exit
      review is listed.
- [ ] The exit runner calls the check and blocks on any failing list; the report is deterministic.

## Considerations

- The check cannot tell whether a test is meaningful; it can tell whether a close cites a test that ran, by
  somebody else, with claims that resolve. Substance is the exit reviewer's judgement.
- Session identity comes from the tracker's audit trail and the log's recording field; a swarm that shares one
  identity defeats the self-certification rule, so the exit runner refuses logs whose recording identity is the
  shared swarm account.
- Close reasons `duplicate` and `withdrawn` exist because drafting produced some duplicated decision beads and
  because decisions leave branches untaken; they need a pointer to the surviving bead or to the decision, and are
  never counted as work done. `recorded` is never counted as work toward a gate's pass and never makes a capability
  `implemented`; it is the record a kill decision reads.
- The type table is the one statement of the close types; the close audit (N38b-03) implements what each must
  carry and the bead log's evaluator (N29a-09) accepts the closing line each type cites (a `fail` gate-record line
  for `recorded`).
- If the bead log format changes, this check changes with it in the same commit.
