## Background

Every row of the risk register names an **earliest signal**: a quantity the program already records, chosen so
that it moves while the failure is still cheap. A signal that has never been seen to fire is a hope, and the
register's rule is that no risk is mitigated by a hope. The plan therefore requires a **planted instance** for
each row: a deliberately constructed case in which the hazard is present, registered in the negative-control
registry, on which the row's detector must report the signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 records it:

> Known answer. Each of the four planted instances raises its named signal in 100 of 100 runs; `xtask check-deps`
> resolves the graph with every feature group enabled and fails on the planted second pin; no row of the register
> lacks a planted instance. M0 for the two build rows, M2 for the sampler row, M3 for the suite row.

The four late rows (RISK-56 to RISK-59) and their 100-variant runs are carried by N41-02, N41-38 and N41-31. The last
clause, **no row of the register lacks a planted instance**, covers the other 55 rows as well. The four register
beads state a planted instance for each of them in words; nothing yet builds them, binds them to the registry or
fires them. This bead supplies that layer for the whole register and builds the 18 instances whose detectors exist
at M0. Five later beads (N41-33 to N41-37) add the instances that fall due at M1 to M5.

Three facts shape the design.

1. **The detectors belong to other beads.** A row's detector is a workspace check, a gate, an admission test, a
   kill-gate trigger or a listing of the exit runner. Most of those beads already register negative controls of
   their own. A second fixture for the same defect would drift from the first, so binding is *reuse first*.
2. **Detectors arrive over six milestones.** A plant can be fired only against the real detector (no mocks: a
   plant that fires a stub proves nothing). Each plant therefore has a `due` milestone, the earliest exit at which
   its mechanical detector exists, which is never earlier than the row's `first_readable` milestone (the exit at
   which the reviewer can first read the signal, possibly from a listing). Ten rows have a second observation
   whose detector arrives later than the first.
3. **The control registry refuses a boundary dressed as a control.** A record whose defect only restates a limit
   of the product ("the request is refused at the limit") is rejected as `boundary-not-control`; the valid form
   names the guard whose removal would let the hazard through (`removed_check`).

Rejected alternatives: plants kept as prose in the register rows (nothing fires, and the gate's clause is paper);
all 55 fixtures built at M0 against stand-in detectors (forbidden: tests run the real component); a separate
plant registry beside the control registry (two files that drift, and the control runner already keeps outcomes,
history and a suspect review).

## Scope

In: the `planted` fields of a risk row; the binding rule; the staging rule; the command that fires plants and its
exit-time check; the staged counts; the 18 plants due at M0; the `risk-plants` section of the exit record. Out: the
gate's own parts and their records (GATE-WS-07 has three, at M0, M2 and M3: N41-02, N41-38, N41-31); the
rows themselves (N41-RISK1 to N41-RISK4); the two build plants and their 100 variants (N41-02); the sampler and
suite plants (N41-38, N41-31); plants due later (N41-33 at M1, N41-34 at M2, N41-35 at M3, N41-36 at M4, N41-37 at M5 with
the full sweep); the detectors and the control runner (their own beads, named per row).

## Technical approach

Files (DESIGN): `registries/risks.toml` (fields below); `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (records
seeded from risk rows); fixtures `tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, each beginning with the kind header `synthetic`;
`crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/risk_plants.rs`; the subcommand `plants` of the risk register's example target
(below); wrapper `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh <Mn>` called by the exit runner after the risk review; report
`docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/risk-plants.json` (schema `frob.riskplants/1`).

**Fields of a risk row.**

```toml
planted = { slug = "risk-23-dirty-tree", text = "one modified file in a sibling during a gate run",
            due = "M0", state = "runnable", control = "NC-0731" }
planted_more = []   # further observations of the same row: same fields, a later `due`
```

`state` is `declared` (slug, text and due only) or `runnable` (`control` names a record of the control registry,
and the plant command has reported it `caught` at the commit that binds it). A control that this layer registers
(binding rule 2) has the registry's own form: `id` (`NC-` and four digits, append-only), `seed = "RISK-nn"`,
`gate = ["GATE-GAUNT-01"]`, `kind` (`planted-input`, `planted-design`, `planted-world` or `a-a`; this layer plants
no `mutation`), `target` (the detector), `defect` (the hazard), `expect` (the signal as an observable: a listed
row, a refusal, a failed entry, a trigger that evaluates to "fires"), `fixture`, `due`, and `removed_check` where
the expectation is a refusal.

**Staging rule.** `due` = the later of the row's `first_readable` and the earliest milestone at which the
detecting gate has a part in the gate register (for a check or listing: the milestone of the bead that builds it).
A plant may be `declared` only while its `due` exit has not been reached.

**Binding rule (reuse first), applied per plant.**

1. Search the control registry for a control whose `target` is the row's detector and whose `defect` is the same
   hazard, registered by the bead that owns the detector or seeded from a catalogue entry or a known-answer row
   (N38b-05). If one exists, the row names it in `planted.control`. No second record is written, and the reused
   record is not changed: no gate is added to its `gate` list. The four late rows are bound this way to the
   controls seeded from N41-CX's entries: RISK-56 to X41-06, RISK-57 to X41-01, RISK-58 to X41-07 and RISK-59 to
   X41-08, which are the registered controls of GATE-WS-07's three parts.
2. Otherwise register a new control with `seed = "RISK-nn"` and build its fixture here. A fixture is a scratch
   record, a scratch manifest overlay, a scratch crate or a scratch clone; it is never the real registry, the
   real ledger or a sibling's real checkout. The record is appended to `controls.toml` under the registry's own
   rules (BD-025: the next free number in file order; the seed form `RISK-nn`, which the seeding join of N38b-05
   resolves in the risk register), with `gate = ["GATE-GAUNT-01"]` and the plant's `due`. This bead never writes
   or edits a control seeded from a catalogue entry or a known-answer row: those are the seeding tool's.
3. A plant whose expectation is a refusal carries `removed_check`, naming the guard. Where the detector's owner
   registers a `mutation` control that removes that guard, the row links it as `guard_control` and the report
   shows both outcomes: hazard with guard, signal fires; hazard without guard, nothing fires. This bead adds no
   mutation features to crates it does not own.
4. A plant that makes a kill-gate trigger evaluate to "fires" is a synthetic measurement record offered to the
   trigger evaluator on a scratch copy of the gate register. It never writes a decision.

Why binding adds GATE-WS-07 to no control (DESIGN). An earlier text of this rule added GATE-WS-07 to the `gate`
list of every bound control; only the four late rows' controls name it, from their own catalogue entries. The
control runner runs every control that names a gate whenever that gate is run, and a gate's line is `pass` only if
each is caught in that run (N38b-04); the registry check requires a gate and the controls that name it to list
each other (BD-025; the reason `one-way-link` of N38-REF). With some sixty foreign controls on its list, among them device plants that exist on one machine only and
mutations whose effect no entry of GATE-WS-07 can observe, no part of that gate could be recorded. The tie between a row and its plant is therefore the row's `planted.control`, which the
register-wide clause of N41-02 reads; a reused control keeps the gates of its owner; and a control registered
here names GATE-GAUNT-01, the gate re-run at every exit, under which planted cases of the program's own registers
are run (as N41-CX's entries are), so that its outcomes accumulate in the control history exit by exit.

**Command.** The crate `frob-conformance` has no binary of its own name (BD-025, ADR-GAUNT-01). The risk
register's commands are subcommands of one example target, `crates/frob-conformance/examples/risks.rs`, which
N41-01 declares with `check` and `review`; this bead adds the subcommand `plants` to it (the dispatch there, the
code in `src/exit/risk_plants.rs`): `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example risks -- plants --exit <Mn> [--id
RISK-nn] [--root <dir>] --json`. It prints the report as one JSON object and exits 0 when every selected plant is
`caught`, 3 otherwise with `reason=<id>` of the first failure on its first line.

1. Load the risk register and the control registry; select every plant, primary or further, with `due` ≤ Mn.
2. Build a scratch root `target/risk-plants/<utc>/` holding copies of the registries and the fixtures; record the
   digests of the real registries before and after (they must not change).
3. For each selected plant call the control runner on its control and record the outcome (`caught`, `missed`,
   `stale`, or `caught-unexpected` when the detector fails for a reason other than `expect`), the observed signal
   and the detector's exit status.
4. List every plant with a later `due` as `declared`, with its milestone.
5. A plant whose detector needs a device carries `machine` (`mac` for the Metal plants, `trj` for the CUDA plants;
   absent means either machine). Run on the other machine, it is reported `not-run-here`; the exit check merges
   the two machines' reports for the same commit.
6. Write the report. The command writes nothing in `controls.toml`: a control's `last_seen` and its history line
   are written by the gate recorder (N38b-19) when a gate that names the control is recorded, by a session that
   did not build the plant.

**Exit-time check.** `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh <Mn>` fails the exit (exit 3, first line `reason=<id>`, the
harness reasons of N41-REF) when a plant with `due` ≤ Mn is `declared` (`plant-declared-past-due`), `missed`
(`plant-missed`), `stale` (`plant-stale`) or `caught-unexpected` (`plant-unexpected`); when its detector's
live-dependency proof fails because it was fired against a stand-in (`plant-stub-detector`); or when a due plant was
recorded on neither machine in the merged reports (`plant-not-run`). A row with no `planted` field at all is refused
by the register's loader (`risk-without-plant`). The exit runner of N40-05 reaches this check through its table
of attached checks: this bead adds the record `id = "risk-plants"`, `owner = "N41-32"`, `step = 9`, `since =
"M0"`, `after = "risk-review"`, command `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh` (run as `<Mn> --root <dir> --json` under the
runner's check contract, in which each failure above is a block carrying its reason and the report is the check's
section). The beads N41-33 to N41-37 extend the same command and add no record of their own.

**Where the result is recorded (DESIGN).** This bead writes no row of `registries/gates.toml`. GATE-WS-07 has one
part per milestone at which a part is due, at M0, M2 and M3 (N40-10; one part per milestone, N40-01), and none of
them can hold a count that is taken again at every exit; an earlier text of this bead had a row `register-plants`
re-recorded at M0 to M5, which the register cannot hold. The gate's structural clause ("no row of the register
lacks a planted instance": every open row names a registered control, or a declared plant whose due exit has not
been reached) is checked by N41-02's harness over the whole register and held in the receipt of the M0 part. The
count taken at each exit (runnable primary plants out of 59, all `caught`, the declared rows with their due
milestones, the further observations) is this command's report, `docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/risk-plants.json`,
which the attached check `risk-plants` puts into the exit record as its section, directly after the risk review
of N41-01. A plant that is not caught therefore blocks the exit at which it is due, and every later exit, through
that check. Evidence class of the report `Tested`; determinism class D0 (every plant is exact).

## Considerations

- A plant proves that a detector fires on one constructed case; it does not prove that the detector fires on every
  case. Where the hazard can enter by several routes (as in the two build rows) the variants belong to the gate
  that owns the detector.
- Plants for process observations (RISK-20, RISK-53, RISK-55) exercise listings, not numeric gates. Their value is
  that the listing is run by a program at every exit.
- If a detector's owner changes its message or its observable, the plant becomes `caught-unexpected`, which counts
  as missed until `expect` is corrected in the same change. That is deliberate: a control must fail for the stated
  reason.
- Sibling repositories are never edited by a plant; RISK-01 and RISK-55 use scratch clones.
- The control identifiers are assigned by the control registry (append-only); the slug is the stable name used in
  the risk register and in logs.
