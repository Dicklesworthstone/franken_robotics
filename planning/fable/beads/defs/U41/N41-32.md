## Background

Every row of the risk register names an **earliest signal**: a quantity the program already records, chosen so
that it moves while the failure is still cheap. A signal that has never been seen to fire is a hope, and the
register's rule is that no risk is mitigated by a hope. The plan therefore requires a **planted instance** for
each row: a deliberately constructed case in which the hazard is present, registered in the negative-control
registry, on which the row's detector must report the signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 records it:

> Known answer. Each of the four planted instances raises its named signal in 100 of 100 runs; `xtask check-deps`
> resolves the graph with every feature group enabled and fails on the planted second pin; no row of the register
> lacks a planted instance. M0 for the two build rows, M2 for the sampler row, M3 for the suite row.

The four late rows (RISK-56 to RISK-59) and their 100-variant runs are carried by N41-02 and N41-31. The last
clause, **no row of the register lacks a planted instance**, covers the other 55 rows as well. The four register
beads state a planted instance for each of them in words; nothing yet builds them, binds them to the registry or
fires them. This bead supplies that layer for the whole register and builds the 18 instances whose detectors exist
at M0. Five later beads (N41-33 to N41-37) add the instances that fall due at M1 to M5.

Three facts shape the design.

1. **The detectors belong to other beads.** A row's detector is a workspace check, a gate, an admission test, a
   kill-gate trigger or a listing of the exit runner. Most of those beads already register negative controls of
   their own. A second fixture for the same defect would drift from the first, so binding is *reuse first*.
2. **Detectors arrive over six milestones.** A plant can be fired only against the real detector (no mocks: a
   plant that fires a stub proves nothing). Each plant therefore has a `due` milestone, the earliest exit at which
   its mechanical detector exists, which is never earlier than the row's `first_readable` milestone (the exit at
   which the reviewer can first read the signal, possibly from a listing). Ten rows have a second observation
   whose detector arrives later than the first.
3. **The control registry refuses a boundary dressed as a control.** A record whose defect only restates a limit
   of the product ("the request is refused at the limit") is rejected as `boundary-not-control`; the valid form
   names the guard whose removal would let the hazard through (`removed_check`).

Rejected alternatives: plants kept as prose in the register rows (nothing fires, and the gate's clause is paper);
all 55 fixtures built at M0 against stand-in detectors (forbidden: tests run the real component); a separate
plant registry beside the control registry (two files that drift, and the control runner already keeps outcomes,
history and a suspect review).

## Scope

In: the `planted` fields of a risk row; the binding rule; the staging rule; the command that fires plants and its
exit-time check; the staged counts; the 18 plants due at M0; the register-wide row of GATE-WS-07 at M0. Out: the
rows themselves (N41-RISK1 to N41-RISK4); the two build plants and their 100 variants (N41-02); the sampler and
suite plants (N41-31); plants due later (N41-33 at M1, N41-34 at M2, N41-35 at M3, N41-36 at M4, N41-37 at M5 with
the full sweep); the detectors and the control runner (their own beads, named per row).

## Technical approach

Files (DESIGN): `registries/risks.toml` (fields below); `crates/frob-conformance/registry/controls.toml` (records
seeded from risk rows); fixtures `tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, each beginning with the kind header `synthetic`;
`crates/frob-conformance/src/exit/risk_plants.rs`; command `frob-conformance risk plants`; wrapper
`scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh <Mn>` called by the exit runner after the risk review; report
`docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/risk-plants.json` (schema `frob.riskplants/1`).

**Fields of a risk row.**

```toml
planted = { slug = "risk-23-dirty-tree", text = "one modified file in a sibling during a gate run",
            due = "M0", state = "runnable", control = "NC-0731" }
planted_more = []   # further observations of the same row: same fields, a later `due`
```

`state` is `declared` (slug, text and due only) or `runnable` (`control` names a record of the control registry
whose last recorded outcome is `caught`). The control record has the registry's own form: `id` (`NC-` and four
digits, append-only), `seed = "RISK-nn"`, `gate` (the row's detecting gates and always GATE-WS-07), `kind`
(`mutation`, `planted-input`, `planted-design`, `planted-world` or `a-a`), `target` (the detector), `defect` (the
hazard), `expect` (the signal as an observable: a listed row, a refusal, a failed entry, a trigger that evaluates
to "fires"), `fixture`, `due`, and `removed_check` where the expectation is a refusal.

**Staging rule.** `due` = the later of the row's `first_readable` and the earliest milestone at which the
detecting gate has a part in the gate register (for a check or listing: the milestone of the bead that builds it).
A plant may be `declared` only while its `due` exit has not been reached.

**Binding rule (reuse first), applied per plant.**

1. Search the control registry for a control whose `target` is the row's detector and whose `defect` is the same
   hazard, registered by the bead that owns the detector. If one exists, the row names it, and GATE-WS-07 is
   appended to that control's `gate` list. No second record is written.
2. Otherwise register a new control with `seed = "RISK-nn"` and build its fixture here. A fixture is a scratch
   record, a scratch manifest overlay, a scratch crate or a scratch clone; it is never the real registry, the
   real ledger or a sibling's real checkout.
3. A plant whose expectation is a refusal carries `removed_check`, naming the guard. Where the detector's owner
   registers a `mutation` control that removes that guard, the row links it as `guard_control` and the report
   shows both outcomes: hazard with guard, signal fires; hazard without guard, nothing fires. This bead adds no
   mutation features to crates it does not own.
4. A plant that makes a kill-gate trigger evaluate to "fires" is a synthetic measurement record offered to the
   trigger evaluator on a scratch copy of the gate register. It never writes a decision.

**Command.** `frob-conformance risk plants --exit <Mn> [--id RISK-nn] [--root <dir>] --json`:

1. Load the risk register and the control registry; select every plant, primary or further, with `due` ≤ Mn.
2. Build a scratch root `target/risk-plants/<utc>/` holding copies of the registries and the fixtures; record the
   digests of the real registries before and after (they must not change).
3. For each selected plant call the control runner on its control and record the outcome (`caught`, `missed`,
   `stale`, or `caught-unexpected` when the detector fails for a reason other than `expect`), the observed signal
   and the detector's exit status.
4. List every plant with a later `due` as `declared`, with its milestone.
5. Write the report; `last_seen` is written by the recording session, which is not the session that built the
   plant.

**Exit-time check.** `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh <Mn>` fails the exit when a plant with `due` ≤ Mn is `declared`
(`plant-declared-past-due`), `missed` (`plant-missed`), `stale` (`plant-stale`) or `caught-unexpected`
(`plant-unexpected`). A row with no `planted` field at all is refused by the register's loader
(`risk-without-plant`).

**Staged counts** (a known answer of the command). Primary plants runnable by each exit: M0 20 (the 18 below and
the two build rows), M1 30, M2 39 (8 and the sampler row), M3 54 (14 and the suite row), M4 57, M5 59. Further
observations due: M1 1, M2 5, M3 3, M4 1; ten in all.

- `every_row_has_plant_fields`; `due_not_before_first_readable`; `declared_past_due_rejected`.

- One test per plant, named `plant_risk_<nn>_fires`, asserting the observable in the table (for RISK-03 the two
  versions and the path; for RISK-08 the limit 31; for RISK-22 "fires" at 3,700 s against 3,600 s).
- `real_registries_untouched` (digests before and after); `stub_detector_rejected`.
- `unexpected_reason_counts_as_missed`: a plant whose detector fails for another reason is `caught-unexpected`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M0 [--seed 1]`: builds the scratch root, runs
`frob-conformance risk plants --exit M0 --json`, then the exit-time check on the four planted bad states. Log
(shared helper, `target/e2e-logs/risk_plants/<utc>.ndjson` and a transcript): `cmd` lines with exit code and wall
time; one `assert` per plant with row, slug, control, detector, expected and observed signal, outcome; `metric`
lines for runnable against 20 and declared against 39; `refusal` lines with the reason names; on failure the first
plant whose outcome differed and the scratch path. `summary`: assertions, gates GATE-WS-07 (`register-plants`) and
GATE-GAUNT-01. The later beads extend the same script with `--exit M1` to `--exit M5`.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: the staged counts; 18 rows here; the limits each plant sits beyond (31 arguments; one hour, 16 GiB;
115% of a cap; two milestones without change). Negative controls: a stand-in detector; a plant with its fixture
header removed (`stale`); a plant with `expect` edited to another observable (`caught-unexpected`); a declared
plant past its due exit; the guard-off forms linked as `guard_control`. Determinism lanes: twice on one machine;
Linux and macOS; identical reports apart from timestamps and scratch paths.

## Considerations

- A plant proves that a detector fires on one constructed case; it does not prove that the detector fires on every
  case. Where the hazard can enter by several routes (as in the two build rows) the variants belong to the gate
  that owns the detector.
- Plants for process observations (RISK-20, RISK-53, RISK-55) exercise listings, not numeric gates. Their value is
  that the listing is run by a program at every exit.
- If a detector's owner changes its message or its observable, the plant becomes `caught-unexpected`, which counts
  as missed until `expect` is corrected in the same change. That is deliberate: a control must fail for the stated
  reason.
- Sibling repositories are never edited by a plant; RISK-01 and RISK-55 use scratch clones.
- The control identifiers are assigned by the control registry (append-only); the slug is the stable name used in
  the risk register and in logs.
