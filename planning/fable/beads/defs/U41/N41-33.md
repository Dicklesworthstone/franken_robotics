## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 requires that no row of the register lacks one. The layer that binds a row to a registered
control, stages it by a `due` milestone and fires it at each exit is N41-32, which also carries the rows due at
M0. This bead carries the ten rows whose mechanical detector first exists at M1, and one further observation of an
M0 row.

How a plant works, in brief. A row's `planted` field holds a slug, a text, a `due` milestone and a state. It is
`declared` until its detector exists and `runnable` once it names a control of the registry whose last outcome is
`caught`. Binding is reuse first: if the bead that owns the detector has registered a control for the same hazard,
the row names that control and GATE-WS-07 is appended to its gate list; otherwise a new control seeded
`RISK-nn` is registered with a fixture under `tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, labelled `synthetic`. A plant whose
expected observation is a refusal names the guard whose removal would let the hazard through (`removed_check`).
Plants run on a scratch root only. `frob-conformance risk plants --exit M1` fires every plant due by M1, and the
exit runner fails the M1 exit if one of them is still declared, is missed, or is stale.

Why these ten matter at M1: M1 is the first milestone at which a robot is designed, imported, simulated on the
referee and answered with a checked certificate. The hazards that can enter there are a lossy import, an
estimator that hides its own error, a speed lever funded above its ceiling, a referee that silently truncates,
cards that make every tier agree and be wrong together, breadth, a certificate for another statement, a stale
memoized verdict and a Reality statement with no data.

## Scope

In: the ten plants and the one further observation below, bound and runnable; their fixtures where no control
exists; the M1 row of the register-wide record. Out: the layer (N41-32); the detectors (named per row); the second
observations of RISK-17, RISK-29, RISK-31 and RISK-32, whose detectors arrive at M2 and M3 (N41-34, N41-35).

Known-answer detail for RISK-25: the planted ledger has a batch step of 842.8 µs of which 575.7 µs lies outside
the lever, so the lever's ceiling is 842.8 ÷ 575.7 = 1.46 however fast the lever itself becomes (the figures are
those of the plan-time solver pilot, used here as a synthetic ledger); a claimed ratio of 2 cannot be reached, and
the record must be refused before any work is funded.

**Record.** GATE-WS-07, row `register-plants`, re-recorded at the M1 exit: 30 of 59 primary plants runnable and
`caught`; 29 declared with their milestones; one further observation added.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each of the ten rows is `runnable` at the M1 commit: bound to a control whose `gate` list contains
      GATE-WS-07, and `caught` with the observable in the table.
- [ ] The further observation of RISK-54 is runnable and caught.
- [ ] `frob-conformance risk plants --exit M1` reports 30 runnable and 29 declared primary plants.
- [ ] RISK-25's plant is refused with the ceiling 1.46 printed; RISK-31's with the limit 21; RISK-35's with
      `FROB-REF-826`; RISK-43's with `FROB-REF-889` and the `no-claim` line.
- [ ] Where a control of the detector's owner already existed, no second record was written (the binding log names
      the reused identifier).
- [ ] The M1 exit is blocked when any one of the ten is set back to `declared` on a scratch copy.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/risk_plants_m1.rs`, one test per row named `plant_risk_<nn>_fires` asserting the
observable above, and: `ceiling_known_answer` (842.8 ÷ 575.7 rounds to 1.46; a claimed ratio of 1.4 is accepted);
`contact_limit_is_21` (21 accepted, 22 refused); `fifth_moonshot_refused` (four accepted);
`m1_counts` (30 and 29); `reused_controls_have_no_duplicate`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M1`: fires the 30 plants due by M1 on a scratch root, then sets one M1 plant
back to `declared` and asserts that the exit-time check fails with `plant-declared-past-due`. Log lines as in
N41-32: `cmd`, one `assert` per plant with expected and observed signal, `metric` lines for runnable against 30
and declared against 29, `refusal` lines carrying the refusal codes above, a `summary` naming GATE-WS-07.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: 1.46; 21 contacts; four active Moonshot items; 25% padding waste and eight buckets; the counts 30
and 29. Negative controls: the ten plants; a plant whose detector was replaced by a stand-in (rejected); RISK-35's
bundle with inconsistent hashes (rejected for the wrong reason, so it must be reported `caught-unexpected`, which
shows why the hashes are recomputed). Determinism: exact; identical reports on Linux and macOS.

## Considerations

- RISK-32's plant shows the limit of what a simulator can detect by itself: nothing in the tiers disagrees. The
  only signal is provenance, which is why the share line is printed on every report.
- RISK-38's fixture is owned by the mission slice; this bead binds to it and does not copy it.
- RISK-17's second observation (a design that fails only under the baseline controller is reported
  `controller-sensitive`) needs the baseline validation on the design corpus and is bound at M3.
- If the M1 pilot revises the bucket set, RISK-29's fixture is regenerated against the revised set in the same
  change.
