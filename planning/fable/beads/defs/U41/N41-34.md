## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 requires that no row of the register lacks one. N41-32 supplies the layer (binding to a
registered control, staging by a `due` milestone, the command that fires plants, the exit-time check) and the rows
due at M0; N41-33 the rows due at M1. This bead carries what falls due at M2: eight rows whose detector first
exists with the batched engine, the device backends and the performance harness, and the second observation of
five earlier rows whose first half was fired at M0 or M1.

How a plant works, in brief. A row's `planted` field holds a slug, a text, a `due` milestone and a state
(`declared`, then `runnable` once it names a control whose last outcome is `caught`); further observations of the
same row sit in `planted_more`. Binding is reuse first: a control already registered by the detector's owner for
the same hazard is named and left unchanged; otherwise a control seeded `RISK-nn`, with the gate list
`["GATE-GAUNT-01"]`, is registered with a fixture under `tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, labelled `synthetic`. A plant that makes a
kill-gate trigger evaluate to "fires" is a synthetic measurement record offered to the trigger evaluator on a
scratch copy of the gate register; it never writes a decision. Plants run on a scratch root only.

Why M2: this is the milestone at which speed is first measured against an incumbent and at which results come
from device backends in single precision. The hazards that enter are a contact model whose artifacts decide a
ranking, a kernel toolchain that is slower than hand-written code, a noisy shared machine, an engine below the
parity band, retention that deletes evidence, contact capacity that biases a comparison, an "optimization" that
quietly does less work, and scaling that vanishes.

## Scope

In: the eight plants and five further observations below, bound and runnable; fixtures where no control exists;
the counts of the `risk-plants` section of the M2 exit record. Out: the layer (N41-32); the detectors (named per row); the sampler row
RISK-58 and its 100 runs (N41-38); the two privacy and licence rows RISK-11 and RISK-12, whose detector (the
private root and licence joins) is built at M3 (N41-35).

## Technical approach

For each row apply the binding rule of N41-32, then fire the plant through the control runner on the scratch
root. Plant values are DESIGN choices placed beyond a threshold; every threshold is a TARGET repeated from the
gate named, and the gate register governs.

- [ ] The three trigger plants evaluate to "fires" at 1.4 against 1.3 (KILL-KIR), 0.30 against 0.33

      asserts so that the two numbers are never read as one; no decision record is written by a plant.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/risk_plants_m2.rs`, one test per row and per further observation named
`plant_risk_<nn>_fires`, and: `trigger_plants_both_sides` (the six values above); `scaling_tolerance_is_not_the_kill_threshold`
(strong scaling 1.1: gate fails, KILL-SHARD fires; 1.3: gate passes, KILL-SHARD fires; 1.7: gate passes, it
does not fire; the reading table of N36-10); `pyramid_cone_ratio` (√2 =
1.41421 to five places as the admitted diagonal ratio of the pyramidal cone); `coverage_signal_and_adjudicator`
(70%: the RISK-31 signal fires and an adjudicator is named; 85%: the signal does not fire and an adjudicator is
still named, because the verb names one under 90%; 92%: neither); `template_arm_outcome_rule` (94% with a win does not fire; 95% and 96% with a win
fire; 96% with a loss on the first measure does not);
`plants_write_no_decision`; `m2_counts` (39 and 20).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M2`: fires the 39 primary plants and six further observations due by M2 on a
scratch root; the device-dependent plants (RISK-04, RISK-08) run on the machine that has the device and are
recorded as `not-run-here` with the reason on the other, never as caught. Logged with the shared helper of STD-E2E
as in N41-32 (every line with `ts`, `script`, `bead` `N41-34`, `step`, `event`): `start`; `cmd` lines with the exact
command line, exit code and wall time; one `assert` per plant with row, slug, control, detector, machine, expected
and observed signal, outcome and the evidence block of any `frob` command; `metric` lines for each trigger value
against its threshold with units, and for runnable against 39 and declared against 20; `refusal` lines with the
codes and their exit statuses (813 and 822 exit 3, 761 exit 4, 533 exit 3); the digests of the real registries and
of the gate register before and after (equal: no decision written); on failure the first plant whose outcome
differed, its fixture path and the scratch root; `end`; `summary` with assertions passed and failed naming
GATE-WS-07.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: √2·μ against μ; thresholds 1.3 (KILL-KIR), 0.33 (KILL-PARITY), 1.25 (training time), 1.25 (the
scaling gate's tolerance) and 1.6 (KILL-SHARD), 80% (the RISK-31 signal) and 90% (an adjudicator is named), 95%
(the template arm); 31 buffer arguments; counts 39 and 20. Negative controls: the plants; each trigger plant on the safe side of its threshold
(must not fire); a decision record found after a plant run (must not exist). Determinism lanes: twice on one
machine; across worker counts for the batched plants; Linux and macOS for the registry plants.

## Considerations

- Trigger plants test the evaluator, not the engine: they say nothing about whether the real profile will fire a
  kill gate. Their purpose is that a real firing cannot pass unnoticed.
- RISK-05's second observation (the finalist typed `model-conditional` by the model-form rank check, and the run
  with that stage skipped failing its control) is bound at M3 with the model-form verb.
- RISK-09's background load is generated by a seeded busy loop on a fixed number of cores; the plant records the
  host load it produced in its disclosure block.
- A plant that needs a device is never run on the CPU and reported as caught; a CPU run establishes nothing about
  a device path.
