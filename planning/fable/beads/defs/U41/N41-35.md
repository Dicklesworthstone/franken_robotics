## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 requires that no row of the register lacks one. N41-32 supplies the layer (binding to a
registered control, staging by a `due` milestone, the command that fires plants, the exit-time check); N41-33 and
N41-34 carry the rows due at M1 and M2. This bead carries what falls due at M3: fourteen rows whose detectors
arrive with scenes, tasks, qualification, the adversary, attribution and the partial ten-fold gate, and the second
observation of three earlier rows.

How a plant works, in brief. A row's `planted` field holds a slug, a text, a `due` milestone and a state
(`declared`, then `runnable` once it names a control whose last outcome is `caught`); further observations sit in
`planted_more`. Binding is reuse first: a control already registered by the detector's owner for the same hazard
is named and left unchanged; otherwise a control seeded `RISK-nn`, with the gate list `["GATE-GAUNT-01"]`, is
registered with a fixture under `tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, labelled `synthetic`. A plant whose expected observation is a refusal
names the guard whose removal would let the hazard through. Plants run on a scratch root only; none touches a
sealed world family, a private scene or a real qualification campaign.

Why M3: it is the first milestone at which a result can be called qualified and at which the ten-fold claim is
first tested. The hazards that enter are the ones that corrupt evidence: a private place that leaks, a corpus
tuned to its analyses, a scorecard with a shrinking denominator, a gamed qualification, a leak from training into
evaluation, hostile imported text, an attribution with no stable cause, a tie with a composed rival, low rank
agreement, an unreachable verdict, and wording that claims independence nobody provided.

## Scope

In: the fourteen plants and three further observations below, bound and runnable; fixtures where no control
exists; the counts of the `risk-plants` section of the M3 exit record. Out: the layer (N41-32); the detectors (named per row); the suite
row RISK-59 and its 100 runs (N41-31); RISK-37 and RISK-47, whose detectors (the screening audit; derivative
validity labels) are built at M4, and the agent arm of RISK-49 (N41-36).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/risk_plants_m3.rs`, one test per row and per further observation named
`plant_risk_<nn>_fires`, and: `tie_factor_both_sides` (1.3 fires, 1.6 does not); `rung_modes_by_tau` (0.4, 0.55,
0.75); `scorecard_columns_must_sum` (seven columns summing to the battery size pass; one short is refused);
`null_design_500_retries_within_alpha`; `stripped_mark_still_blocked` (refused with `FROB-REF-731`, exit 5, and
never with `FROB-REF-310`, which is reserved to scene packs); `pack_with_restricted_asset_refused_310`;
`m3_counts` (54 and 5); `no_real_sealed_access`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M3`: fires the 54 primary plants and nine further observations due by M3 on a
scratch root, through the real `frob` verbs where the detector is a verb (`scene.accept`, `scene.split`,
`eval.scorecard`, `eval.qualify`, `fidelity.modelform`, `autopsy.attribute`; every call of a `run` or `mutate`
verb among them with its own `--key`, `risk-<row>-<part>`, so that no plant is answered from another's stored
receipt) and through the control runner otherwise. Logged with the shared helper of STD-E2E as in N41-32 (every
line with `ts`, `script`, `bead` `N41-35`, `step`, `event`): `start`; `cmd` lines with the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the evidence
block of each verb call; one `assert` per plant with row, slug, control, detector, expected and observed signal and
outcome; `refusal` lines with codes and exit statuses; `metric` lines for the tie factor, τ and the false-promotion
frequency against α, and for runnable against 54 and declared against 5; the digests of the real registries and
of the firewall's audit log before and after; on failure the first plant whose outcome differed, its fixture path
and the scratch root; `end`; `summary` with assertions passed and failed naming GATE-WS-07. Logs never contain
private-scene data: RISK-12's scene is a scratch scene marked private, and only identifiers are logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: tie factor 1.5; τ thresholds 0.5 and 0.7; 500 retries; seven outcome columns; counts 54 and 5.
Negative controls: the plants; each threshold plant on its safe side; a plant run that reads a real sealed
cluster (must be impossible: the session has no administrative scope, `FROB-REF-840`, exit 5); RISK-52's plant is
X41-23 of N41-CX, and the same text with a neutral party on record must be accepted, showing the lint is
conditioned on evidence and not on the phrase. Determinism lanes:
twice on one machine; the statistical plant (RISK-34) is seed-explicit and judged against its declared class.

## Considerations

- Several of these plants are existing controls of other gates. Binding them here adds no test; it adds the
  guarantee that the risk register cannot list a signal whose detector has silently stopped firing.
- RISK-34's plant is the most expensive (500 adaptive retries); it reuses the qualification gate's campaign
  fixture at a reduced campaign count and records the reduced count as such.
- RISK-49's second observation, a fresh agent that ignores the poisoned import, needs the agent mission session
  and is bound at M4.
- If KILL-AUGUR-TIE has already fired for a stratum, RISK-26's plant still runs: it tests the rule, not the state
  of the program.
