## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 requires that no row of the register lacks one. N41-32 supplies the layer (binding to a
registered control, staging by a `due` milestone, the command that fires plants, the exit-time check); N41-33 to
N41-35 carry the rows due at M1 to M3. This bead carries the three rows whose detectors arrive at M4 and one
further observation.

How a plant works, in brief. A row's `planted` field holds a slug, a text, a `due` milestone and a state
(`declared`, then `runnable` once it names a control whose last outcome is `caught`). Binding is reuse first: a
control already registered by the detector's owner for the same hazard is named, and GATE-WS-07 is appended to
its gate list; otherwise a control seeded `RISK-nn` is registered with a fixture under
`tests/controls/risk/<slug>/`, labelled `synthetic`. Plants run on a scratch root only.

Why M4: this is the milestone at which designs are decided without training each one. Three hazards decide
whether that is sound. A family policy may not transfer, or may lose plasticity along a lineage of edits. A cheap
rung may discard the design that would have won. A search or a gradient may exploit an artifact of contact order.
Each has a signal that the M4 mechanisms produce; this bead makes each signal fire once on purpose.

## Scope

In: the three plants and one further observation below, bound and runnable; the M4 row of the register-wide
record. Out: the layer (N41-32); the detectors (named per row); the last two rows, due at M5, and the full sweep
(N41-37).

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M4`: fires the 57 primary plants and ten further observations on a scratch
root, calling `train.family`, `train.inherit`, `opt.cascade`, `phylum.gradient` and the agent session harness for
this bead's rows. The two training plants use the smallest registered fixture and stay inside the default
machine envelope (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device); if a plant would exceed it the script stops with the
estimate and records `not-run: over-envelope`, never a pass. Log lines as in N41-32, with `metric` lines for the
decline along the chain, the audit twin's gap against 5%, and whether the audit lane reported the removed winner;
`summary` naming GATE-WS-07.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: ten edits in the chain and the 5% band of its audit twin; counts 57 and 2; ten further
observations. Negative controls: the plants;
the two positive controls (a kept winner, a smooth gradient) which must not fire; a plant recorded as caught from
a CPU run where its row names a device (refused). Determinism lanes: twice on one machine; the training plants
are judged against their declared determinism class, not bit for bit.

## Considerations

- These three rows are the thesis failing lever by lever; their fallbacks ship under their own names. A plant
  that fires says the detector works, and says nothing about whether the lever does.
- RISK-28's chain plant is the costliest in the register. It is kept small on purpose: its job is to show that
  disabling resets is detected, and the gate's own run measures the effect.
- Compute: no plant here is admitted above the default envelope. A plant that cannot run inside it is redesigned
  as a smaller fixture; an approval is not requested for a control.
