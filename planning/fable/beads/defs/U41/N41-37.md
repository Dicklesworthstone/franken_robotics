## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 ends with the clause "no row of the register lacks a planted instance". N41-32 supplies
the layer (binding to a registered control, staging by a `due` milestone, the command that fires plants, the
exit-time check) and N41-33 to N41-36 bind the rows as their detectors arrive. At the M4 exit 57 of 59 primary
plants are runnable. This bead binds the last two, whose detectors are the proof sibling's re-check and the
calibration subsystem's identifiability analysis, and then runs the **full sweep**: every row of the register
fired in one run at one commit. Only then does an exit record show the clause complete.

Why a sweep and not the sum of the earlier records: each earlier record was taken at a different commit. A
detector can stop firing after its plant was last run (a refactoring that moves a check, a message that changes,
a guard that becomes unreachable). The sweep is the standing answer: from M5 on, the exit runner fires all 59 at
every exit.

## Scope

In: the two plants below; the sweep mode of the command; the `risk-plants` section of the M5 exit record, which
shows the register-wide clause complete; the sweep as a step of every later exit. Out: the layer (N41-32); the earlier rows (N41-33 to N41-36); the 100-variant
runs of the four late rows (N41-02, N41-38, N41-31), which the sweep invokes once each and does not repeat.

## Technical approach

**The two plants due at M5.**

|---|---|---|---|---|

**The sweep.** The flag `--all`, which this bead adds to N41-32's subcommand of the risk register's example target
(the crate `frob-conformance` has no binary of its own name, BD-025): `cargo run -p frob-conformance --example
risks -- plants --exit <Mn> --all --json`:

1. Select every primary plant and every further observation of every open row, whatever its `due`. A row that has
   been retired is listed with the gate or decision that retired it and is not fired.
2. Fire each on one scratch root at the current commit, in identifier order. The four late rows are fired once
   each through their own harnesses (one variant, seed 1).
3. Device plants run on the machine that has the device. The sweep is therefore two runs, one per machine, whose
   reports the exit-time check of N41-32 merges for the one commit; a plant reported on neither machine is
   `not-run` and fails the sweep.
4. Outcomes as in the layer: `caught`, `missed`, `stale`, `caught-unexpected`. The sweep passes only if every
   fired plant is `caught` with its stated observable.
5. Write `docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/risk-plants.json` with one entry per plant: row, slug, control, detector,
   expected and observed signal, outcome, machine, wall time.

**Known answer of the sweep at M5.** 59 primary plants (55 older rows and the four late rows) and 10 further
observations: 69 firings, all `caught`; zero declared; zero retired unless an exit record says otherwise. By
severity of the rows: 28 High, 29 Medium, 2 Low.

**Record.** The `risk-plants` section of the M5 exit record: "59 of 59 runnable, 69 of 69 caught", the commit and
the digests of both machines' reports. No gate row is written: GATE-WS-07's parts are those of M0, M2 and M3
(N40-10; one part per milestone, N40-01), recorded from the receipts of N41-02, N41-38 and N41-31, and its
structural clause is held in the M0 part's receipt. With those three parts `pass` and this section in the exit
record, the gate's last clause holds in its strongest reading: every row's plant is runnable and caught at one
commit. From M5 the wrapper `scripts/exit/risk-plants.sh` passes `--all`, so the attached check `risk-plants` of
N41-32 runs the sweep at this and every later exit; an exit at which any plant is not caught is blocked by that
check with the plant's reason.

**The mutant of X41-17 (DESIGN).** This bead declares the mutation feature `detector-disabled-after-plant` of
`frob-conformance`, the plant of N41-CX's row X41-17 ("a detector disabled by a later change, after its plant last
ran"), reachable only under `gauntlet-mutations`; as every mutation feature it implies the crate's `conformance`
feature, whose guard makes it a compile error under the release profile (N38b-04). It is one cfg branch in the
detector of RISK-53, the due-question check that the exit runner calls (N40-35's rule over the register of N41-03,
both in this crate): under the feature the past-deadline test is skipped and no question is reported, as a later
change that made the guard unreachable would leave it, while the real detector is still the one called (so the
live-dependency proof passes and the outcome is `missed`, not `plant-stub-detector`). The control build is
`cargo test -p frob-conformance --features gauntlet-mutations,detector-disabled-after-plant --test risk_plants_m5
-- sweep_counts_69` (debug only): RISK-53's plant is `missed`, 68 of 69 are caught and the test fails, which is
how the control is caught. No other crate gains a feature.

**Controls never caught.** The sweep writes nothing in the control registry or its history (those are written by
the gate recorder when a gate that names a control is recorded). A plant's control with no `caught` line in that
history among the gates due, or a `stale` one, is listed by the control runner's suspect review (N38b-04) and
needs a typed disposition (`replanted`, `gate-strengthened`, or `withdrawn` with a reason) before the exit can
pass; the sweep's report names the same plants with their outcome at this commit, so the reviewer reads the two
side by side.

## Considerations

- The sweep is cheap because plants are small. If it grows past its budget, the remedy is smaller fixtures, never
  a sampled sweep: a row that is not fired is a row whose detector may have stopped working.
- A risk retired by an exit record leaves the sweep but stays in the register; its plant and control are kept so
  that a reopened risk can be fired again.
- A new risk added after M5 must arrive with a runnable plant if its detector exists, or a declared one with a
  due milestone; the sweep's count rises with it, and the known answers here change in the same commit.
- Privacy: plants use scratch scenes and synthetic logs; the report holds identifiers and values, never
  private-scene data.
