## Background

Each row of the risk register names an earliest signal and a **planted instance**: a constructed case in which the
hazard is present, registered in the negative-control registry, on which the row's detector must report the
signal. Gate GATE-WS-07 ends with the clause "no row of the register lacks a planted instance". N41-32 supplies
the layer (binding to a registered control, staging by a `due` milestone, the command that fires plants, the
exit-time check) and N41-33 to N41-36 bind the rows as their detectors arrive. At the M4 exit 57 of 59 primary
plants are runnable. This bead binds the last two, whose detectors are the proof sibling's re-check and the
calibration subsystem's identifiability analysis, and then runs the **full sweep**: every row of the register
fired in one run at one commit. Only then is the clause recorded complete.

Why a sweep and not the sum of the earlier records: each earlier record was taken at a different commit. A
detector can stop firing after its plant was last run (a refactoring that moves a check, a message that changes,
a guard that becomes unreachable). The sweep is the standing answer: from M5 on, the exit runner fires all 59 at
every exit.

## Scope

In: the two plants below; the sweep mode of the command; the complete record of the register-wide clause; the
sweep as a step of every later exit. Out: the layer (N41-32); the earlier rows (N41-33 to N41-36); the 100-variant
runs of the four late rows (N41-02, N41-31), which the sweep invokes once each and does not repeat.

## Technical approach

**The two plants due at M5.**

| Row | Plant (kind) | Detector and its owner | The signal fires as | Guard named |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RISK-16 | A re-check job for one theorem's closure run under a memory cap of 1 GiB (`planted-input`) | The re-check runner of the proof sibling at its admission INT-FLN-01 (BD-380; the measurement N12-23) | The job ends as "budget exceeded", with the peak memory reached; it is never recorded as a pass, and the admission record states the measured closure size | The budget accounting |
| RISK-42 | The identifiability fixture. Drawbar force F = min(k_t·I/r, μ·N) with torque constant k_t = 0.2 N·m/A and wheel radius r = 0.06 m. Runs at current I = 1 A and 2 A under normal loads N = 30 N and 40 N give 3.33 N and 6.67 N whether the friction coefficient μ is 0.4 or 0.8 (both are torque-limited); a run at 3 A under 10 N gives 4 N for μ = 0.4 and 8 N for μ = 0.8. The plant is a fit that reports μ from the first two runs alone (`planted-input`) | The Fisher-information identifiability analysis (BD-390) | The fit is flagged: zero sensitivity to μ in those runs, so the parameter is not identifiable (`FROB-REF-879`); a model that matches speed while current draw and contact work disagree carries the label `wrong-reason`; the excitation plan asks for a run in the traction-limited regime | The rank check |

**The sweep.** `frob-conformance risk plants --exit <Mn> --all --json`:

1. Select every primary plant and every further observation of every open row, whatever its `due`. A row that has
   been retired is listed with the gate or decision that retired it and is not fired.
2. Fire each on one scratch root at the current commit, in identifier order. The four late rows are fired once
   each through their own harnesses (one variant, seed 1).
3. Device plants run on the machine that has the device. The sweep is therefore two runs, one per machine, merged
   by the recorder; a plant recorded on neither machine is `not-run` and fails the sweep.
4. Outcomes as in the layer: `caught`, `missed`, `stale`, `caught-unexpected`. The sweep passes only if every
   fired plant is `caught` with its stated observable.
5. Write `docs/delivery/exits/M<n>/risk-plants.json` with one entry per plant: row, slug, control, detector,
   expected and observed signal, outcome, machine, wall time.

**Known answer of the sweep at M5.** 59 primary plants (55 older rows and the four late rows) and 10 further
observations: 69 firings, all `caught`; zero declared; zero retired unless an exit record says otherwise. By
severity of the rows: 28 High, 29 Medium, 2 Low.

**Record.** GATE-WS-07, row `register-plants`, at the M5 exit: "59 of 59 runnable, 69 of 69 caught", the commit,
both machines' receipts. With the M0, M2 and M3 rows recorded by N41-02 and N41-31, the gate's last clause is
complete. From M6 the exit runner re-runs the sweep and appends a dated value; a later exit with any plant not
caught sets the row back to failing.

**Controls never caught.** The sweep feeds the control runner's suspect review: a plant with no `caught` line in
the history at the gates due, or a `stale` plant, needs a typed disposition (`replanted`, `gate-strengthened`,
or `withdrawn` with a reason) before the exit can pass.

- [ ] RISK-16 and RISK-42 are `runnable` at the M5 commit, bound to controls whose `gate` lists contain

- [ ] The sweep stays inside the default machine envelope on each machine (TARGET: under 30 minutes each).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
`crates/frob-conformance/tests/risk_plants_m5.rs`: `plant_risk_16_budget_exceeded_never_pass` (a 1 GiB cap;
the outcome is "budget exceeded"); `plant_risk_42_unidentifiable_fit_flagged` (3.33 N and 6.67 N for both values
of μ at 1 A and 2 A; 4 N against 8 N at 3 A under 10 N; the two-run fit is refused, the three-run fit is accepted);
`sweep_selects_all_open_rows`; `sweep_counts_69`; `retired_row_listed_not_fired`; `merge_of_two_machines`;
`missing_machine_is_not_run`; `severity_tally_28_29_2`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/risk_plants.sh --exit M5 --all`: runs the sweep on the current machine, merges the other machine's
receipt if present, then repeats the sweep on a scratch build with one detector disabled and on a scratch root
with one fixture removed, asserting the two failures. Log (shared helper, `target/e2e-logs/risk_plants/`): `cmd`
lines with exit code and wall time; one `assert` per plant with expected and observed signal and outcome;
`metric` lines "fired" against 69, "caught" against 69, "declared" against 0, wall time against 30 minutes;
`refusal` lines with reason names; on failure the first plant not caught, its detector and the scratch path.
`summary`: assertions passed and failed; gates GATE-WS-07 and GATE-GAUNT-01.

### Known answers and negative controls
Known answers: 59, 10, 69; 28 High, 29 Medium, 2 Low; the fixture numbers of RISK-42; the 1 GiB cap. Negative
controls: a disabled detector (`missed`); a removed fixture (`stale`); a device plant on the CPU; a sweep that
skips retired rows silently (the retired row must be listed with its retiring gate). Determinism lanes: twice on
one machine; the merged report is identical whichever machine merges.

## Considerations

- The sweep is cheap because plants are small. If it grows past its budget, the remedy is smaller fixtures, never
  a sampled sweep: a row that is not fired is a row whose detector may have stopped working.
- A risk retired by an exit record leaves the sweep but stays in the register; its plant and control are kept so
  that a reopened risk can be fired again.
- A new risk added after M5 must arrive with a runnable plant if its detector exists, or a declared one with a
  due milestone; the sweep's count rises with it, and the known answers here change in the same commit.
- Privacy: plants use scratch scenes and synthetic logs; the report holds identifiers and values, never
  private-scene data.
