## Background

**The row.** WP-01: Bootstrap and stack: the one toolchain T and the stack lock. Crates: workspace, `xtask`,
`frob-core`. Milestone: M0. Key gates: INT-TOOLCHAIN-01; GATE-WS-01, -04. Waits for: no other package.

This is the first package of the program: every other package builds inside the workspace it creates. It delivers the
repository layout, one `rust-toolchain.toml` for the whole build graph (it names the suite's common nightly as the
provisional toolchain until the admission gate records the choice), `franken-stack.lock` with its first resolution
receipt, `.cargo/config.toml` with the build-identity setting that path consumption of FrankenSim needs, the companion
documents (`AGENTS.md`, `README.md` with a generated honest-status table, `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`), the registries
in schema-valid form, and `xtask` with exactly six checks: `check-layers`, `check-deps`, `check-unsafe`,
`check-generated`, `check-contracts`, `check-stack`. It also chooses T, the one Rust toolchain of the build graph,
from a recorded matrix over the four nightlies in play, in the order of preference (a) the suite's common nightly, (b)
FrankenSim's present pin.

It is one unit of work because the three things are meaningless apart: a lock that no check compares with the sibling
heads drifts silently, a toolchain choice that is not recorded in the lock cannot be reproduced, and layer and
dependency rules that no tool refuses are prose. The purpose is reviewability: a reader finds the crate for any
mechanism, and a tool refuses a dependency the crate map forbids.

**Order.** It waits for nothing: it is the root of the package order. (The order between packages is recorded in
`related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope review) and role:
- `xtask` (layer 7; Solid; cap 3): Six checks (§34.6).
- `frob-core` (layer 0; Solid; cap 8): Identities and hashing; evidence classes and the claim-product type; refusals;
  schema versions and format-registry types; artifact-graph identifiers; the `qty` re-export; the no-authorization
  flags.
- The workspace: six Cargo workspaces (the core, which includes `surface/frob-mcp`; the two GPU annexes; `apps/`;
  `derive/`; `bindings/`) and `worker/` as a seventh that only re-targets an existing crate, all on one
  `rust-toolchain.toml`; `franken-stack.lock` and its resolution receipt; `.cargo/config.toml`; `registries/`,
  `schemas/`, `receipts/`; the companion documents.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M0.** The workspace skeleton with `cargo check` green on the empty workspace through the build-offload shim; the
  receipt whose hash recomputes from the lock; the six checks, with a test that the subcommand count is six; the
  toolchain matrix, the choice of T and its record in `franken-stack.lock` and `rust-toolchain.toml`, and the decision
  on the conditional crate `frob-units`. One bead lands at M1 because its subject does not exist earlier: the
  format-registry rows and round trips of the seven formats that exist then (`.fbot`, `.fmodule`, `.fcard`, `.ftask`,
  `.fmission`, `.frun`, `.frobcert`).

Out of scope: the claim-product type, the evidence classes and the no-authorization flags in `frob-core` (WP-36); the
airlock crate and the admission of FrankenSim by path (WP-02); the file-by-file admission tests and the worker
fallback (WP-37); format rows added after M1, which complete with the build packet (WP-71).

acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every child bead is closed with a typed reason citing its own test and that test's output; no close reason is shared; no child is closed by the session that implemented it.
- [ ] Every gate below has a row in `registries/gates.toml` (commit, command, output, receipt hash) with status `pass`, or `killed: fallback adopted` with its ADR. `not run` is not a pass. Thresholds are TARGETs until measured.
- [ ] INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 (admission of The one Rust toolchain T of the build graph; first needed M0): T is chosen and recorded in `franken-stack.lock` and `rust-toolchain.toml`, in the order of preference of §33.13: (a) the suite's common nightly, (b) FrankenSim's present pin. The whole build graph (the core, the GPU annexes, `derive/frob-derive` and `surface/frob-mcp`) builds and tests on T; the matrix of §33.12 is recorded: which of the four nightlies in play compile `fs-qty`, the bedrock-direct list, the M1 closure and each of the other build sets. After this gate T moves only by a toolchain train.
- [ ] GATE-WS-01 (Known answer; earliest M0): Seven plants in a scratch copy (an upward edge, an intra-layer edge against rank, a second asupersync package, an unregistered capsule, an edited generated file, a contract missing a section, a stale stack lock): each caught by the check that owns it and by no other; no failure on the clean tree.
- [ ] GATE-WS-04 (Performance; earliest M0): The six checks together finish in at most 60 seconds on a warm tree, so that they are run and not skipped.
- [ ] `cargo check` is green on the empty workspace through the offload shim and the receipt hash recomputes from `franken-stack.lock`; an unknown subcommand count in `xtask` fails its own test.
- [ ] T is recorded once, in `franken-stack.lock` and in `rust-toolchain.toml`, and no annex carries a toolchain file of its own.
- [ ] Each crate above ships `CONTRACT.md` with its ten sections and `contract.toml`; `xtask check-contracts` reports its line count under 115% of its cap.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **Process growth.** A sibling project's `xtask` reached about 27% of its code (FACT recorded by that project). Here
  `xtask` is capped at 3 thousand lines and six checks; a seventh needs a named defect class it would have caught and
  the owner's approval. `xtask` runs no physics, records no gate and holds no policy. The package-table tooling of
  N44-03 is deliberately not an `xtask` subcommand.
- **The choice of T can be forced.** `fs-qty` depends on an unstable compiler feature, so the recorded matrix decides
  whether the conditional layer-0 crate `frob-units` (cap 3) must exist; the upstream work item FD-FSIM-15
  (macro-generated dimension products behind a feature) removes the need. After the gate, T moves only by a toolchain
  train: move the pin, write a new receipt, re-run the admission and parity gates, relock.
- **BET-04 (FrankenSim by path)** is decided by INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 together with INT-FSIM-01; its kill gate KILL-FSIM is
  carried by WP-02.
- **Working rules that bind every run of every package.** Cargo goes through the build-offload shim and is never
  invoked by absolute path; a disk preflight precedes heavy lanes; no file is deleted without permission; no branches
  and no worktrees; source that was never compiled does not land.
