## Background

**The row.** WP-23: Interiors: seven archetypes and hazard lists. Crates: `frob-scene`. Milestone: M3. Key gates:
GATE-HAB-02. Waits for: WP-17.

Robots are judged in places, and a place is generated, tagged and reproducible. The package delivers the interior
grammar and the structural proxies of seven archetypes (warehouse, commercial kitchen, home, apartment, office,
manufacturing plant, retail store) with doors, ramps, thresholds, stairs, and glass and mirror regions; the hazard
lists `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml` with dimension ranges, mechanisms, provoked failures and provenance
(`scene.hazards`); and feasibility tags for generated scenes.

It is one unit of work because an archetype is accepted as a generator, not as a scene: per archetype 1,000 seeds must
yield valid scenes, each tagged `witnessed`, `impossible` or `unresolved`, with no resampling.

**Order.** It waits for WP-17: generated interiors are compiled to scene packs by the scene compiler. (The order
between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block every child, so the child beads
carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

review) and role:
- `frob-scene` (layer 2; Solid, Frontier; cap 28): Scene packs, the scene compiler, terrain and interior generators,

  state.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M3.** The grammar, the seven archetypes, the hazard lists and the feasibility tags; GATE-HAB-02.

Out of scope: scene acceptance, optical and friction maps, fixtures and movable objects, clearance complexes, the
world bank, train and test hygiene, and retrofits (WP-53); real places and asset-populated interiors (WP-32); the use
of hazard registries as the scene-edit vocabulary of adversarial search (WP-55).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M3: BD-225 The interior grammar mechanism and the structural proxies of the warehouse and the commercial …; BD-226
  Structural proxies of the home, apartment, office, manufacturing plant and retail store; BD-227 Hazard lists:
  `registries/hazards/<archetype>.toml` with dimension ranges, mechanisms, provoked …; BD-234 Feasibility tags for
  generated scenes, from the clearance complex
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** The mechanisms here are Solid; the
crate's Frontier content belongs to WP-53, WP-32 and WP-73.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **OPEN-HAB-1**: which published building and workplace norms supply the dimension distributions. Default: generic
  ranges labelled `assumed`, and claims of realism are refused. The gate tests validity, not realism.
- **A dependency against the package order.** The feasibility tags are computed from the clearance complex, which
  belongs to WP-53, a package that itself waits for this one. The bead dependency is real and the package order is
  coarse: the package table is therefore regenerated per milestone part (N44-03).
- At most 5% of scenes may be `unresolved`, and `impossible: intended` must lie within one percentage point of its
  quota; a generator that resamples to hide impossible scenes fails the gate.
