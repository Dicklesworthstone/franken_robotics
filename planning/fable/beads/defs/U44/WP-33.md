## Background

**The row.** WP-33: Frontier epics not named below. Crates: —. Milestone: M6. Key gates: Per-epic plans. Waits for: no
other package.

This is the container for the Frontier and Moonshot items of M6 that the named packages (living worlds II in WP-73,
frontier physics and sensing in WP-74, lifecycle compression in WP-75) do not hold. The row names no crate and no
gate: each item is admitted as an epic with its own short plan, its paper-scale pilot with a falsifier, and its kill
gate, before any work starts. As drafted from the chapters, the items parented here are: deferred importers (SDF
behind a feature matrix, and OpenUSD only after its pilot); series-elastic drives; a hydraulic cylinder through an
orifice; the battery ageing law; a lifted linear planning model for a tracked base on one soil card; and short-horizon
gradient training.

It is one package only in the sense of a queue: its members share a funding rule, not a mechanism. At most four
Moonshot items are active at once in any milestone, and none becomes active before its pilot passes.

**Order.** The row waits for no package; each item waits for the package that owns the mechanism it extends, as its
own beads state. (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block
every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- None. The row names no crate: this package owns no code of its own.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M6.** For each funded item: the plan, the pilot record, the kill-gate decision, and then the mechanism behind a
  feature flag (`frontier-*` or `moonshot-*`, off in every default build).

Out of scope: every M6 item named in WP-73, WP-74 and WP-75; the M8 items (WP-78 to WP-80).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads: none. Its children are the frontier beads that the chapter drafters parent here.
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** Everything here is Frontier or
Moonshot. The pilots are those of the items: a two-mass resonance of a series-elastic drive against the stepped model;
one hydraulic cylinder through an orifice against the closed form; the battery ageing law against one published
capacity-fade curve (falsifier: error above 10%); band width against horizon for the lifted soil model; short-horizon
gradient training against derivative-free and paired-stencil PPO arms; and OpenUSD import on paper, hand-mapping the
schema of one published robot asset.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every child bead is closed with a typed reason citing its own test and that test's output; no close reason is shared; no child is closed by the session that implemented it.
- [ ] Every gate below has a row in `registries/gates.toml` (commit, command, output, receipt hash) with status `pass`, or `killed: fallback adopted` with its ADR. `not run` is not a pass. Thresholds are TARGETs until measured.
- [ ] Each item has its per-epic plan on file, with its pilot's falsifier, the computed ceiling, the fallback and the result recorded before any mechanism bead of the item is started.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-SINEW-AGE** (decided M6, before the lifecycle work package). Trigger: The ageing law fails its pilot, or no
  card with ageing coefficients better than `assumed` exists when the lifecycle work of §31 is funded. Fallback, named
  in advance: f_Q and f_R are user-set constants per scenario, with no evolution law.
- **Kill gates from later subsections, decided inside this package.** KILL-SOILLIFT (keep the lifted planning model
  for one soil regime, or plan on the full model) and KILL-SHORTGRAD (the pilot's falsifier fires or its gate fails
  after one repair cycle; fallback: PPO with the paired stencil where that passes its own gate, otherwise
  derivative-free search).
- **OPEN-PROG-5**: the order in which admitted Moonshot items become active under the cap of four. Default: none
  before its pilot passes, then the default order of funding.
- **The whole milestone pauses whenever M4 or M7 is slipping**, since nothing here is on their path.
- An item that has not passed its pilot within its planned weeks returns to the killed-idea register and frees its
  slot.
