## Background

**The row.** WP-43: Chunk store, job protocol, job engine and artifact graph. Crates: `frob-chronicle`, `frob-verbs`.
Milestone: M1, M2. Key gates: GATE-CHRON-04, -10, -11; INT-ASUP-02; GATE-TETHER-04, -05, -06, -08. Waits for: WP-13,
WP-06.

A long job must survive a crash, a cancel and a duplicate submission, and every number it produces must trace back to
what produced it. The package delivers the chunk protocol (leases, ranges, the committed frontier), first on the CPU
and then on devices with chunk publish, cancel and restart (`job.list`, `job.status`, `job.wait`, `job.find`,
`job.cancel`); atomic idempotency binding, pagination pinned to a committed frontier and integers above 2⁵³ as
strings; the artifact graph behind `evidence.trace` and the per-bead log `frob.beadlog/1`; the artifact and run verbs
(`artifact.read`, `artifact.list`, `artifact.export`, `run.show`, `run.list`); simulation testing of the ledger and
the submit path under asupersync's lab runtime; and the M2 verb set.

It is one unit of work because the same fault schedules test all of it: a late lease, overlapping ranges, a reset that
reuses tick zero, a kill between chunk write and publish, a daemon restart.

**Order.** It waits for WP-13 (the ledger the chunks are committed to) and WP-06 (the verbs and the envelope through
which jobs are submitted). (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would
block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-chronicle` (layer 6; Solid; cap 18): Telemetry, episode files, replay and forks, digests, the energy ledger,
  the job and chunk store, the memo store, summaries, stills.
- `frob-verbs` (layer 6; Solid; cap 22): The verb registry, envelope, schemas, verb implementations, the daemon's job
  engine, the memo graph in front of executors.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M1.** The chunk protocol on the CPU, the artifact graph, the artifact and run verbs, and lab-runtime testing of
  the publish and submit paths; GATE-CHRON-10; the CPU rows of GATE-CHRON-04 and -11.
- **M2.** The job protocol on devices and the daemon's job engine with device ownership; idempotency and pagination;
  the device rows of GATE-CHRON-04 and -11; INT-ASUP-02; GATE-TETHER-05, -06 and -08; the M2 verbs; where its bead is
  carried here, memoized episodes under class D2 on one fingerprint, with INT-FSQL-02. GATE-TETHER-04 (the three
  failure classes on both surfaces) is closed by a bead of WP-26, because it needs the full MCP surface.

Out of scope: slot dispatch, scheduling and estimates (WP-26); replay, forks and retention (WP-22, WP-51); the reuse
rules that sit on the store (the memo graph's invalidation, quotienting and revalidation; WP-63); capability scopes
and the path jail (WP-58).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):

  `run.list`

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every child bead is closed with a typed reason citing its own test and that test's output; no close reason is shared; no child is closed by the session that implemented it.
- [ ] Every gate below has a row in `registries/gates.toml` (commit, command, output, receipt hash) with status `pass`, or `killed: fallback adopted` with its ADR. `not run` is not a pass. Thresholds are TARGETs until measured.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-04 (Known answer; earliest M1 (CPU); M2 (device)): The chunk protocol under 10⁵ seeded fault schedules: INV-CHRON-01 to -04 hold in every one; each planted violation (late lease, overlap, conflict, tick-zero reuse) is refused, 100 of 100.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-10 (Known answer; earliest M1): The artifact graph: every row of `registries/gates.toml` with a PASS resolves; a dangling identifier fails the build; `evidence.trace` on 100 random claims returns closures whose hashes recompute.
- [ ] GATE-CHRON-11 (Known answer; earliest M1 (CPU); M2 (device)): Simulation testing: full await-point coverage of the publish and submit paths; every failure minimized to a regression that replays in under one second; dual-run agreement on 100 small jobs.
- [ ] INT-ASUP-02 (admission of asupersync lab runtime; first needed M2): The job protocol runs under the lab runtime: 100 of 100 repeats of one seed give identical traces; a planted lost-chunk bug is found within 10,000 explored schedules and replays from its minimized fixture.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-04 (Known answer; earliest M2): Each of the three failure classes is exercised on both surfaces; every refusal code maps to exactly one exit code; a killed connection during a wait leaves the job recoverable by key in 100 of 100 trials.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-05 (Known answer; earliest M2): Idempotency: 1,000 races of two identical submissions and 1,000 with a changed payload each leave exactly one job and one charge; a crash injected at every commit point leaves one job, one charge and a replayed receipt. Negative control: the two-statement variant fails.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-06 (Known answer; earliest M2): Pagination: over 10⁴ seeded schedules of concurrent appends, concatenated pages equal the frontier snapshot, including rows with equal timestamps; a changed filter with an old cursor is refused.
- [ ] GATE-TETHER-08 (Known answer; earliest M2): Job protocol under seeded faults (late lease, overlapping ranges, a reset reusing tick zero, a kill between chunk write and publish, a daemon restart): contiguous frontier; a duplicate chunk reuses its receipt; a conflicting chunk is flagged; terminal counts sum to planned; a cancel names the last committed tick; no device leak.
- [ ] Each crate above ships `CONTRACT.md` with its ten sections and `contract.toml`; `xtask check-contracts` reports its line count under 115% of its cap.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **Acceptance lives in the artifact graph.** A gate's row in `registries/gates.toml` with a pass must resolve there,
  a dangling identifier fails the build, and a bead closes by a line in `docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson` whose claim
  identifiers resolve.
- **The idempotency negative control**: the variant that binds key and job in two statements must fail the race test
  (1,000 races of identical submissions and 1,000 with a changed payload each leave exactly one job and one charge).
- The lab runtime must find a planted lost-chunk bug within 10,000 explored schedules and replay it from its minimized
  fixture; 100 of 100 repeats of one seed give identical traces.
