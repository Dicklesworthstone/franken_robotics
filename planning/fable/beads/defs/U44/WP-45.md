## Background

**The row.** WP-45: Contact cone, joint rows and scaling. Crates: `frob-contact`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-kernels`.
Milestone: M2. Key gates: GATE-TRAC-04, -10; GATE-SINEW-03, -04, -09; GATE-SPARK-18. Waits for: WP-16.

On the batched tiers contact and joint friction are rows of one convex solve, and single precision is safe only if
that solve is scaled. The package delivers the four-dimensional contact cone (normal, two tangential, spin) in the
`Cone` trait and the cone kernels, rolling and torsional rows with interface cards by material pair and condition
(`contact.laws`, `contact.probe`), `K-joint-rows` for stiction, backlash and hard stops, the actuator kernels
`K-act-l1`, `K-act-l2`, `K-passive` and the shared-bus reduction `K-act-bus`, and the solve itself: `K-scale-diag`,
`K-hess`, batched dense `K-chol`, exact `K-linesearch` and `K-refine`.

It is one unit of work because the rows are accepted through the solve they share: the factorization with twelve
backlash joints must have the size it has with none, and the scaled and unscaled systems must give bit-identical
solutions.

**Order.** It waits for WP-16: the CL-1 rows, the broad and narrow phases and the contact battery on batched tiers.
(The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block every child, so the
child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-contact` (layer 3; Solid, Frontier; cap 22): Collision geometry, detection, contact laws (convex compliant,
  the four-dimensional cone, joint rows, film friction, the hard-contact law), wheels, tracks, terrain mechanics.
- `frob-actuate` (layer 2; Solid; cap 12): Actuator, transmission, battery, bus and thermal models; joint stiction and
  backlash parameters; cards.
- `frob-kernels` (layer 3; Solid; cap 26): The kernel library: manifests, Rust skeletons, generated arithmetic, exact
  and guarded predicates.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M2.** The cone, the rows, the actuator kernels and the scaled solve; GATE-TRAC-04 and -10; GATE-SINEW-03, -04
  (batched rows) and -09; the single-precision row of GATE-SINEW-02; GATE-SPARK-18; the decision KILL-SINEW-ROWS.

Out of scope: the same rows on the referee, which land at M1 (WP-09, WP-10); film friction as a cone row (WP-53);
tyre, track and soil laws (WP-30); the hard-contact law (WP-72).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-SINEW-ROWS** (decided M2). Trigger: With stiction rows on, the fast-tier solve needs more than 1.5 times the
  Newton iterations of the smooth-friction model on the contact battery, or throughput falls by more than 20%, after
  one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance: Throughput tiers apply regularized friction as a force; the rows stay
  on the referee and `strict`; smooth-model results carry a flag and the fidelity ledger measures the gap.
- **The pyramid and independent-row mutations must fail** (GATE-TRAC-04): coupled and diagonal friction forces are
  within 0.5% of μ·N/√2 and μ·N, which a pyramid cone or independent tangential rows do not reproduce.
- **Conditioning is certified, not assumed.** On 10⁴ contact states per reference robot the relative error of the
  `f32` Newton step against `f64` is at most 10·κ₂·u, the estimate κ̂ never exceeds the κ₂ computed in `f64`, and
  cards over the bound are refused.
- Model-form exploits live here: a policy can win by exploiting a box cone or penetration energy, which is why the
  model-form check (WP-50) replays finalists under a second law.
