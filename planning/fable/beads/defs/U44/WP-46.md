## Background

**The row.** WP-46: Determinism, placement and device services; the fleet scheduler and survivor specialization [F].
Crates: `frob-kernels`, `frob-kir`, the Spark annexes, `frob-cli`. Milestone: M2, M4. Key gates: GATE-SPARK-09, -11,
-12, -15, -16; GATE-MARROW-05; GATE-SPARK-13, -14. Waits for: WP-18, WP-19, WP-35.

A world's digest must be a function of its address and its identities alone, on every back end, whoever shares the
batch. The package delivers the services that make that true and observable: randomness by address (`K-x-philox` and
addressed draws), the batch-composition invariance test (co-tenancy, slot permutation, bucket capacity, shards), the
decision-level equivalence test under class D3, `spark.check` and the divergence ledger, the placement planner with
requested-versus-actual receipts per stage and strict backends (`spark.place`, schema `frob.placement/1`), the device
arena, leases and the binary kernel cache (`spark.cache`, `spark.precompile`), and guarded predicates on devices over
the contact battery. At M4 it adds two Frontier items: the fleet scheduler across the two machines (`fleet.status`,
`fleet.plan`) and specialized kernels for surviving designs.

It is one unit of work because these are the cross-cutting services of both device backends, and one decision sits on
top of them: the determinism class each device family can hold (BET-02).

**Order.** It waits for WP-18 and WP-19 (the backends the services run on) and WP-35 (the fingerprint, and the exact
and guarded kernel classes). (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic
would block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-kernels` (layer 3; Solid; cap 26): The kernel library: manifests, Rust skeletons, generated arithmetic, exact
  and guarded predicates.
- `frob-kir` (layer 1; Solid, Frontier; cap 14): Straight-line programs with bounded loops and resource metadata,
  dimension checking, differentiation, emitters; the exact-integer kernel class.
- `spark/frob-spark-cuda` (annex workspace; Solid; cap 8): CUDA backend, arena lease, binary kernel cache.
- `spark/frob-spark-metal` (annex workspace; Solid; cap 7): Metal backend.
- `frob-cli` (layer 7; Solid; cap 8): The `frob` and `frobd` binaries.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M2.** All the services; GATE-SPARK-09, -11, -12, -15 and -16; GATE-MARROW-05; the decisions KILL-D1 and
  KILL-MARROW-GUARD. Wide counts on devices: the plan-time no-wrap bound for 32-bit bins, the exactly-once drain into
  64-bit ledger totals and the gating of 64-bit arithmetic, with GATE-SPARK-23 (§21.26, defined after the package
  table; measured by N21c-43).
- **M4.** The fleet scheduler (GATE-SPARK-13) and survivor specialization (GATE-SPARK-14), each only after its pilot;
  the decisions KILL-FLEET and KILL-SPECIALIZE.

Out of scope: the backends themselves (WP-18, WP-19); slot dispatch in the daemon and KILL-SLOT (WP-26); the
determinism-class licences and the labelled D4 training profile (WP-58); the fused per-world step kernel (WP-80).

- [ ] GATE-SPARK-12 (Known answer; earliest M2): Zero graph captures after warm-up and zero synchronizations inside a chunk; no pool memory leaked after 10³ cancel cycles; 10⁵ snapshot reads each match the digest emitted for their epoch; the commit-fence known answer holds under 10³ injected faults.

(The controls of these rows: a kernel with a batch-wide floating reduction feeding state must break the co-tenancy digests; padding with +0.0 must break the −0.0 sum of the one active element at two capacities; and a sequential random stream, which gives two designs differing by one leg the draws r₀ and r₁ where addressed streams give both the same push, must break address-only draws.)

## Test plan

None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** The M2 services need nothing above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device). The M4
  fleet gate does: row R12 of the envelope register is reserved for it and is filed by BD-370 before its first run
  above E0 (N44-01). Request R12: the fleet gate's campaign over both machines (GATE-SPARK-13): fleet runs holding
  three devices, arm B and the single-machine runs. Class expected: E1 for `gate:GATE-SPARK-13`, with runs of at
  most 13.2 minutes (arm B on `trj`) or 16.3 minutes (arm B on the Mac) by BD-370's rule T_run = (12 − 0.33) h ÷
  (10·(3 + d_B) + 3), d_B being the number of devices of arm B's machine; or E2 for longer runs. If not approved:
  GATE-SPARK-13 is recorded `not run` with the envelope reason, KILL-FLEET stays at its first reading and the
  machines are scheduled by hand, which costs a capability and no milestone. Nothing above E0 starts before the
  owner's written approval is recorded on row R12 of `registries/compute_envelope.toml`; any other run that needs
  more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved.
- **KILL-D1** (decided M2). Trigger: GATE-SPARK-01 or GATE-SPARK-02 shows that a device family cannot hold bit
  equality with `batch-cpu` for the floating kernels of the step. Fallback, named in advance: That family is class D2
  for itself and D3 against others, with the decision-level test of §35.4; a teleported world is a tolerance replay;
  cross-family comparisons are unpaired; the replay claim of §6 and §29 is reworded. (The decision-level test is the
  D3 equivalence test of GATE-SPARK-16 above; the replay claim reworded is that a world replays bit for bit on any
  device family, which then holds within a family only.)
- **KILL-MARROW-GUARD** (decided M2). Trigger: Fallback rate above 10⁻² on the contact battery on either GPU backend,
  or the exact path cannot be made bit-identical across backends. Fallback, named in advance: Strict predicates are
  evaluated exactly only on `strict` and the referee; GPU tiers mark worlds that came within E of a boundary and the
  sampled referee re-runs them.
- **KILL-FLEET** (decided M4). Trigger: The pilot shows g < 1/3, or after one repair cycle the paired lower bound of
  campaign speedup at GATE-SPARK-13 is below 1.3 against the better single machine. Fallback, named in advance: The
  machines run standalone; jobs are assigned by the role table; the seeds-only rule stays.
- **KILL-SPECIALIZE** (decided M4). Trigger: In the pilot the specialized kernel is below 1.2 times the data-driven
  one on the whole step, or takes more than 60 s to compile; or the M2 profile shows a specializable share under one
  third. Fallback, named in advance: Data-driven kernels only; derivation stays for correctness, certificates and the
  derivative kernels of CPU solvers.
- **Kill gates from later subsections, decided inside this package.** KILL-PEERLINK (the peer link between the two
  machines; a DESIGN name, since the plan names no gate for the link; registered and decided by N32a-35 once the
  owner rules `build` on OPEN-TETHER-5, standing from M4): one key admitted or charged twice across the two daemons,
  any byte of a forbidden transfer class or of private-marked data on the wire, or, read once after four weeks of
  operation, recovered device time below 10% of the device-hours used on both machines. Fallback: standalone
  machines (`fleet.peer_link = false`); forwarded jobs in flight finish on the peer and are found by key.
- **Stop decision of M2**: if no device family holds batch-composition invariance, M2 falls back to its no-GPU
  variant.
- **A planted bias of one decision in fifty must fail** the decision-level test (GATE-SPARK-16); a test that cannot
  fail it validates nothing.
- **Between the machines only seeds cross**: at most 1 GB per campaign-hour and no forbidden transfer class
  (GATE-SPARK-13). OPEN-TETHER-5 asks whether the peer link earns its upkeep; default, standalone machines.
- A warm start must load every kernel with zero compiler invocations, and a cache entry with one flipped byte is
  refused.
