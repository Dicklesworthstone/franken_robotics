## Background

**The row.** WP-58: Safety, privacy and supply chain. Crates: `frob-verbs`, `frob-io`, `frob-chronicle`. Milestone:
M2, M3. Key gates: GATE-SAFE-02, -04, -05, -06, -07, -09, -10; GATE-SPARK-17. Waits for: WP-06, WP-43.

The system reads files written by strangers, holds scans of private places, and emits artifacts someone might build
from. The package delivers the checks that keep each of those from becoming a harm: capabilities, namespaces and
session budgets with the path jail; the no-authorization flags on every exporting verb; imported text as data, with
the `x-frob-untrusted` mark carried through every verb that echoes it; private places and licences (the private mark,
licence joins, `scene.purge`); supply-chain checks (one asupersync package per workspace, committed lockfiles,
kernel-cache integrity); importer fuzz targets and limits; the claim product at every verb boundary; and the
determinism-class licences with the labelled D4 training profile.

It is one unit of work because these are properties of every verb, tested by corpora that sweep the whole surface, and
they can only be closed once the surface exists.

**Order.** It waits for WP-06 (the verbs and the envelope) and WP-43 (the job protocol and the artifact store that
marks and purges act on). (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would
block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-verbs` (layer 6; Solid; cap 22): The verb registry, envelope, schemas, verb implementations, the daemon's job
  engine, the memo graph in front of executors.
- `frob-io` (layer 2; Solid; cap 10): URDF and MJCF import and export, loss reports, the XML reader, fuzz targets.
- `frob-chronicle` (layer 6; Solid; cap 18): Telemetry, episode files, replay and forks, digests, the energy ledger,
  the job and chunk store, the memo store, summaries, stills.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M2.** Supply-chain checks and importer fuzzing; GATE-SAFE-06 and -07.
- **M3.** Capabilities and the path jail, the flags over the full surface, untrusted text, privacy and licences, the
  D4 licences; GATE-SAFE-02, -04, -05, -09 and -10; GATE-SPARK-17.

Out of scope: the flag and claim-product types and their first gates at M1 (WP-36); confirmation isolation,
GATE-SAFE-03 (WP-54); sandboxed custom reward terms, which are not admitted (OPEN-SAFE-3: the closed expression
language only).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M3: BD-258 Capabilities, namespaces and many agents: scopes, session budgets, the path jail; BD-269
  Determinism-class licences and the labeled D4 training profile; BD-303 The no-authorization flags on every exporting
  verb; BD-304 Imported text as data: the `x-frob-untrusted` mark through every verb that echoes imported text; BD-305
  Private places and licences: the private mark, licence joins, `scene.purge`; BD-306 Supply-chain checks: one
  asupersync package per workspace, committed lockfiles, kernel-cache …; BD-307 Importer fuzz targets and limits;
  BD-308 The claim product at the boundary
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** None. Every mechanism of this package

- [ ] Every gate below has a row in `registries/gates.toml` (commit, command, output, receipt hash) with status `pass`, or `killed: fallback adopted` with its ADR. `not run` is not a pass. Thresholds are TARGETs until measured.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-02 (Known answer; earliest M3): Injection: at least 200 hostile strings pass through every verb that echoes imported text; none appears outside an untrusted-marked field, and none appears in any tool description, prompt or `next_actions` command.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-04 (Known answer; earliest M3): Privacy: for a private scene, each derived artifact kind carries the mark; exports without the flag are refused; after `scene.purge` no digest of the closure remains in the store.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-05 (Known answer; earliest M3): Licences: over random derivation graphs the licence set equals the join of the inputs; a research-only asset cannot be made to enter an exported report or pack.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-06 (Known answer; earliest M2): Supply chain: one `asupersync` package per workspace; all lockfiles committed; a kernel cache entry with one flipped byte is refused; `frob-verify` builds with no dependency outside the standard library.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-07 (Known answer; earliest M2): Importers: at least 10⁷ fuzz executions per importer with no panic; each limit is reached by a crafted file and refused.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-09 (Known answer; earliest M3): D4: a result produced under the nondeterministic training profile is refused on each of the five paths of INV-SAFE-07; the same policy re-evaluated under D2 is accepted.
- [ ] GATE-SAFE-10 (Known answer; earliest M1): Path jail: a corpus of traversal, absolute-path and swapped-link attempts is refused on every path-taking argument of every verb.
- [ ] GATE-SPARK-17 (Known answer; earliest M3): Each licence refusal of §35.3 fires on its planted case (a D4 arm in a paired comparison; attribution below D2; a cited training return; a cross-family pair demanded as paired), and a policy trained under `fast-training` re-evaluates to the same digest twice.
- [ ] Each crate above ships `CONTRACT.md` with its ten sections and `contract.toml`; `xtask check-contracts` reports its line count under 115% of its cap.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **A date that needs resolving.** The gate register dates the path jail (GATE-SAFE-10) to M1, while the seed bead
  that names it lands at M3 behind the device job protocol. Until the lead rules, the M1 part (the traversal,
  absolute-path and swapped-link corpus on every path-taking argument of the M1 verbs) is owed at the M1 exit by a
  bead of the safety chapter.
- **RISK-11** (asset licences restrict what can ship) and **RISK-12** (a private scan leaks through a derived
  artifact) are both mitigated here: licence sets are the join of their inputs, and the private mark is inherited by
  every derived artifact kind, policy weights included by default.
- OPEN-SAFE-4: retention of job logs, idempotency keys and retired confirmation clusters. Default: 30 days for logs
  and keys; retired clusters sealed indefinitely.
