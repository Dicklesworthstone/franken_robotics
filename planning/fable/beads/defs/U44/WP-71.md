## Background

**The row.** WP-71: Foundry II: manufacturability, tolerance and the build packet [F]. Crates: `frob-foundry`,
`frob-cert`. Milestone: M5. Key gates: GATE-FOUNDRY-07 to -10; GATE-WS-02. Waits for: WP-70, WP-54.

The last artifact of the pipeline is a packet someone could build from, and it must say exactly what it is and is not.
The package delivers manufacturability checks (`foundry.check`: wall, corner radius, tool access, overhang, undercut,
each with location and repair), tolerance allocation driven by sensitivities (`foundry.tolerance`), bounded geometry
export, the build packet `.fbuild` with its four flags (`foundry.export`), and the completion of the format registry
for all thirteen formats.

It is one unit of work because the packet is the sum of its checks: it is schema-valid, every hash matches, parts,
geometry, nets and schedule are cross-consistent, and its four flags are present and false.

**Order.** It waits for WP-70 (the sized and optimized structure) and WP-54 (the qualification lane: a packet cites
qualified results, and the auditor that rejects a mutated flag replays them). (The order between packages is recorded
in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block every child, so the child beads carry the real
dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-foundry` (layer 5; Frontier; cap 12): Structure, fatigue, tolerance allocation, build packets.
- `frob-cert` (layer 6; Solid; cap 12): The certificate bundle reader and checkers; the `frob-verify` binary; standard
  library only.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M5.** The checks, the allocation, the export and the packet; GATE-FOUNDRY-07 to -10; GATE-WS-02 complete; the
  decisions KILL-FAB, KILL-TOLERANCE and KILL-PACKET.

Out of scope: the structure loop (WP-70); firmware export (WP-80); format rows that landed at M1 (WP-01).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M5: BD-405 Manufacturability checks (`foundry.check`); BD-406 Tolerance allocation driven by sensitivities
  (`foundry.tolerance`); BD-407 Bounded geometry export; BD-408 The build packet `.fbuild` with its four flags
  (`foundry.export`); BD-410 The format registry complete for all thirteen formats
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** The package is Frontier. Its mechanisms
are scoped by the structure pilot of WP-70 and by a tolerance pilot whose band-extreme checks decide KILL-TOLERANCE:
if they fail on more than a fifth of features, allocation falls back to worst-case boxes per interface.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-FAB** (decided M5). Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-07 fails. Fallback, named in advance: Manufacturability findings
  become advisory lints; a packet records no manufacturability pass.
- **KILL-TOLERANCE** (decided M5). Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-08 fails, or band-extreme checks fail on more than a fifth of
  features in the pilot. Fallback, named in advance: Worst-case boxes per interface.
- **KILL-PACKET** (decided M5). Trigger: GATE-FOUNDRY-09 or -10 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in
  advance: Parts list, mass properties and STL only; STEP and harness deferred.
- **A packet authorizes nothing.** It carries the three no-authorization flags and a fourth, `fabrication_released`,
  all false; a mutated flag is rejected by `frob-verify`.
- **A writer killed at any file boundary leaves no packet**: the packet is published atomically or not at all.
- OPEN-FOUNDRY-2 and OPEN-SKEL-2: parts catalogue and price data. Default: card references and no prices; the cost
  budget is advisory.
- A STEP file above the stated bound is quarantined with a receipt, not parsed (INT-FSIM-15, in WP-70).
