## Background

**The row.** WP-72: Reflection pilot, variational integrator, hard-contact batch law, the tree-operator pilot [F].
Crates: `frob-lemma`, `lean/`, `frob-dyn`, `frob-contact`. Milestone: M5. Key gates: GATE-LEMMA-05; GATE-TRAC-09.
Waits for: WP-31, WP-39, WP-45.

Four Frontier items of M5 strengthen the engine and its proofs, and each is small enough to be decided by its own
pilot. The package delivers the reflection pilot (Farkas and rational LDLᵀ reflectors, `prove.reflect`), the
articulated variational integrator on the referee and the `strict` profile, the hard-contact law CL-H as a batch law
on `batch-cpu`, and the tree-operator route for larger mechanisms with limb port packets, as a pilot.

It is one package because the four share a status, not a mechanism: each has a kill gate decided at M5, each falls
back to something that already ships, and none is on the path to the tournament.

**Order.** It waits for WP-31 (the Lean project and capsules that reflection builds on), WP-39 (the transactional step
the variational integrator and the hard-contact law run under) and WP-45 (the cone and the scaled solve that the
hard-contact law replaces). (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic
would block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M5: BD-382 The reflection pilot: Farkas and rational LDLᵀ reflectors (`prove.reflect`); BD-384 The articulated
  variational integrator on the referee and the `strict` profile; BD-385 The tree-operator route for larger mechanisms
  and limb port packets, as a pilot; BD-386 The hard-contact law CL-H as a batch law on `batch-cpu`
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** Everything here is Frontier, and each
item is its own pilot or has one on record first: the reflectors' closure against the blocked library modules and the
cost of kernel evaluation on exact rationals; the variational step on a free body and a heavy top against the Cayley
semi-implicit step; hard-contact fixed-point passes to a residual of 10⁻⁴ on 10⁴ battery contact states; the limb port
interface by hand, the packet from the recursion against the reaction from the whole model; and the hard-contact batch
law with factor reuse on the contact known answers (construction C-06).

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-REFLECT** (decided M5). Trigger: GATE-LEMMA-05 fails after one repair cycle, or no reflector's closure avoids
  the blocked modules. Fallback, named in advance: Instance certificates stay `Certified`; reflection is parked in
  Appendix C.
- **KILL-VARIATIONAL** (decided M5). Trigger: The implicit solve costs more than twice a semi-implicit step on
  `batch-cpu`, or the articulated form fails its momentum row after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance: The
  free-body step is kept as a known-answer oracle; every rollout uses the default integrator.
- **KILL-HARDCONTACT** (decided M5). Trigger: GATE-TRAC-09 fails after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance:
  Fixed-iteration island solvers on `batch-cpu` with a residual flag; CL-H remains the referee's specification and a
  model-form arm under `strict`.
- **KILL-TREEOP** (decided M5). Trigger: Whole-step gain below 1.15 at 64 degrees of freedom on the M2 profile.
  Fallback, named in advance: The dense factorization everywhere and a cap of 64 degrees of freedom.
- **KILL-SKEL-PORT** (decided M5). Trigger: PO-SKEL-04 fails, or the tree-operator route of §16 is not funded
  (KILL-TREEOP). Fallback, named in advance: No limb port packets; all dynamics go through the whole-model path.
- **The cap of 64 degrees of freedom** stands if KILL-TREEOP fires: the dense factorization everywhere, and no limb
  port packets.
- The hard-contact rows may be recorded from M3 on `batch-cpu`; the register dates the gate to M5 because the batch
  law lands there.
- Each reflector must prove its specimen by kernel evaluation within 60 s, or reflection is parked and instance
  certificates stay `Certified`.
