## Background

**The row.** WP-74: Frontier physics and sensing: soil at scale, the tree-operator route, meshes on devices, tactile
sensing, the rotor port, RGB and splat scenes, moving people, gradients through contact [F], [M]. Crates:
`frob-contact`, `frob-dyn`, `frob-sense`, `frob-actuate`, `frob-scene`, `frob-mission`, `viewer/`. Milestone: M6. Key
gates: GATE-AUGUR-20, -21; per-epic known answers. Waits for: WP-30, WP-49.

This package is the queue of physics and sensing that would make more failures visible, admitted behind feature flags
and off the critical path. Its epics are: soft soil at scale; the hard-contact law on GPU tiers; the tree-operator
route and the mesh path on devices, where their earlier pilots kept them; tactile sensing and compliant pads; the
rotor port for flight; RGB and splat scenes; moving people as kinematic actors on route graphs; gradients through
contact with their validity labels; geometric phase for locomotion (the holonomy query); and symmetry at the instant
of contact.

It is one package because its members share the rule of M6, not a mechanism: each needs its own short plan, its
paper-scale pilot and its kill gate before work starts, and each closes with the known answers its chapter lists.

**Order.** It waits for WP-30 (the wheel, track and soil laws that soil at scale and link-chain tracks extend) and
WP-49 (the sensor cards, timing and analyses that tactile and pixel sensing extend). (The order between packages is
recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a blocked epic would block every child, so the child beads carry the real
dependencies.)

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M6: BD-417 Epic: soft soil at scale; BD-418 Epic: tactile sensing and compliant pads; BD-419 Epic: the rotor port
  for flight; BD-420 Epic: RGB and splat scenes; BD-421 Epic: moving people as kinematic actors on route graphs;
  BD-422 Epic: gradients through contact; BD-424 Epic: the hard-contact law on GPU tiers; BD-425 Epic: geometric phase
  for locomotion; BD-426 Epic: symmetry at the instant of contact
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** Frontier and Moonshot throughout.
Pilots on record before each epic is funded, as drafted from the chapters: holonomy of the differential-drive loop and
of a three-link kinematic crawler on the CPU; the symmetry–event identity on a mirrored four-foot landing; rotor-port

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-SINEW-ROTOR** (decided M6). Trigger: The hover known answer or the port balance fails, or the hybrid variant
  has no consumer when frontier physics is funded. Fallback, named in advance: The rotor port and the variant are
  withdrawn.
- **KILL-AUGUR-PHASE** (decided M6). Trigger: No proposed gait is admitted by a full rollout, or trajectory
  optimization finds every proposed gait at lower total cost. Fallback, named in advance: A-09 remains a forward
  diagnostic; the inverse query and the atlas are parked.
- **KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT** (decided M6). Trigger: The identity cannot separate the symmetric fixture from its
  mutations, or amortization shows no reduction in decision time on either GPU. Fallback, named in advance: Per-event
  finite differences; symmetry is used only on smooth kernels.
- **Kill gates from later subsections, decided inside this package.** KILL-STEPADJOINT (step adjoints through the
  converged contact solve): the pilot's falsifier fires, or its gate fails after one repair cycle, or the median
  cosine on the design corpus is below 0.5 at K = 8. Fallback: the paired stencil where its own gate holds, otherwise
  rank-based search.
- **OPEN-PROG-5 and OPEN-AUGUR-10**: which epics are funded, in which order, under the cap of four active Moonshot
  items. Default: none before its pilot passes.
- **KILL-MESHGPU** was decided at M3 (WP-53) and **KILL-TREEOP** at M5 (WP-72); an epic whose earlier pilot was killed
  is not opened here.
- The whole milestone pauses whenever M4 or M7 is slipping.
