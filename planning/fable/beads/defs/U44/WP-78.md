## Background

**The row.** WP-78: Facility co-design and multi-robot handoff [M]. Crates: `frob-mission`, `frob-scene`. Milestone:
M8. Key gates: GATE-AUGUR-24. Waits for: WP-66, WP-53.

The furthest reach of mission queries is a facility: several robots with handoffs between them, and a building whose
priced edits are also variables. The epic delivers facility co-design and multi-robot handoff as the last slice of the
mission chapter.

It is one unit of work, a single Moonshot epic, because it is accepted on one fixture: at least two feasible
engineering strategies discriminated, each of four seeded failures recovered with a withdrawal and a minimized
scenario, and the optimality gap reported for every unresolved continuous subproblem.

**Order.** It waits for WP-66 (mission queries at scale) and WP-53 (clearance complexes and the priced retrofit
catalogue, which supply the world edits). (The order between packages is recorded in `related`, never in `deps`: a
blocked epic would block every child, so the child beads carry the real dependencies.)

## Scope

Crates and other units, with layer, tier of contents, scope cap (thousands of lines, DESIGN; 115% opens a scope
review) and role:
- `frob-mission` (layer 5; Frontier; cap 14): Mission queries, inner and outer design sets, region certificates, probe
  and experiment suggestion.
- `frob-scene` (layer 2; Solid, Frontier; cap 28): Scene packs, the scene compiler, terrain and interior generators,
  import and acceptance; optical and fixture properties; clearance complexes; the retrofit catalog; living-world
  state.

What lands at each milestone:
- **M8.** The plan, the pilot, the kill decision and then the mechanism; GATE-AUGUR-24.

Out of scope: single-robot missions with priced world edits, which is the fallback and already ships (WP-53, WP-66).

## Technical approach

An epic implements nothing: it contains its child beads, which name it in `parent`, and it is where the package's
acceptance is read. Each bead has one package (the owner of its crate at its milestone, or of the crate where its gate
is measured); exit reviews have none.

Seed beads (the table of contents, assigned by crate, title and gate; a seed's own `parent` governs where it differs,
and N44-03 regenerates this list from the graph):
- M8: BD-435 Epic: facility co-design and multi-robot handoff
- Beads drafted from the chapters for what no seed covers (`N<chapter>-<nn>`: mechanism parts, registries, end-to-end
  tests, pilots, kill-gate decisions) attach through their own `parent` field.

**Frontier and Moonshot content, and the pilot that must be on record first.** The package is Moonshot. Pilot on
record first: facility co-design at paper scale, with the handoff bit, the shared bus and the two-carrier fixture
decided by enumeration. A further pilot from the dynamics chapter is parented here: decoupled stepping of separated
robots, the certified share of robot-time on warehouse traces.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
None of its own: the children's tests run beside the code with `cargo test -p <crate>`, without mocks. The package's
structural tests are in N44-04 (`wp_children_have_one_parent`, `wp_gate_ids_exist`, `wp_tier_tag_has_pilot`).
### End-to-end script and logging
The children's scripts under `scripts/e2e/` demonstrate the package through the real `frob` binary, with the
structured logs of STD-E2E. `scripts/e2e/wp_table.sh` (N44-05) prints this row with its children, the open count and
each gate's status, and fails if a gate above has no record when the package is declared complete.
### Known answers and negative controls
Carried by the children and their chapter registries. A gate above counts only when its registered negative control
has been shown to fail (GATE-GAUNT-01).

## Considerations

- **Envelope.** No request above the default E0 (16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour, one device) is registered. A run that
  needs more is refused (`FROB-REF-761`) until a request with its class and its not-approved fallback is registered
  and approved (N44-01).
- **KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY** (decided M8). Trigger: The acceptance fixture fails to discriminate two strategies, or
  misses a seeded failure after one repair cycle. Fallback, named in advance: Single-robot missions with priced world
  edits from §19; multi-robot composition is parked.
- **Kill gates from later subsections, decided inside this package.** KILL-INTERACT (decoupled stepping of separated
  robots): one missed interaction survives a repair cycle, or the certified share is below 0.5 of robot-time on the
  warehouse fixture. Fallback: a multi-robot world is one coupled world under the `strict` profile with island-wise
  solves and fixed decimation.
- Funding is per epic, by its stop rule; an epic that has not passed its pilot within its planned weeks returns to the
  killed-idea register and frees its slot under the cap of four.
- The plan works without this epic: no North-Star scenario is tagged M8.
