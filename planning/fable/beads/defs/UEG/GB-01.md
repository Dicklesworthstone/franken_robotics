## Background

Rigid-body orientation is advanced on the rotation group SO(3) by a retraction: a map from a small rotation
vector to a rotation. The exact one is the exponential. The batched kernels use the Cayley map
cay(x̂) = (I − x̂/2)⁻¹·(I + x̂/2) instead, because it is rational: no sine, cosine or square root appears in a
kernel that must produce the same bits on CUDA, Metal and the CPU (decision ADR-MARROW-02: group-valued states
with a rational retraction on throughput tiers, the exponential where a tier has it; the rejected alternative
is the exponential everywhere, which puts transcendental functions, whose rounding differs between backends,
inside the determinism contract). The price is that a Cayley step with rotation vector of length ωh rotates by
2·atan(ωh/2), not by ωh. This card quantifies that price and fixes the bound beyond which the step is refused
(FROB-REF-212: a Cayley update with ‖ω‖·h above 0.2 in one substep; repairs: substep, or use the exponential on
a tier that has it). Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Marrow.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-01.toml`; the test `gb_01` in both lanes; the planted defects. Out: the retraction
itself and its laws (BD-011); the guarded predicate that enforces the bound on devices (N13-11).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** With x = ω·h (rad), the Cayley step rotates by θ_c = 2·atan(x/2). The relative shortfall is
ε(x) = (x − θ_c)/x = x²/12 − x⁴/80 + …, so ε ≈ x²/12 to leading order. The angular-rate lag is ω·ε.

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.**

| Output | Inputs | Golden (`f64`) | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| x | ω = 20 rad/s, h = 2 ms | 0.04 | 0.04 | exact |
| θ_c | x = 0.04 | 0.03999466794630107 rad | — | rel 10⁻¹² |
| ε(x) exact | x = 0.04 | 1.333013424732743 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.33 × 10⁻⁴ | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| x²/12 | x = 0.04 | 1.3333333333333334 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.33 × 10⁻⁴ | rel 10⁻¹² |
| lag ω·ε | — | 0.002666026849465 rad/s | 0.0027 rad/s | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| ε at the bound | x = 0.2 | 3.313475088379675 × 10⁻³ | 0.33% | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| x for ε = 10⁻⁵ (leading order) | — | √(12 × 10⁻⁵) = 0.010954451150103 | 0.011 | rel 10⁻¹² |
| x for ε = 10⁻⁵ (exact root) | bisection on ε(x) | 0.010954549741167 | 0.011 | rel 10⁻⁹ |

**Intuition.** The Cayley map costs a second-order integrator's worth of phase error, paid for rational
arithmetic with no transcendental function in a batched kernel. The update stays on the group, so the error is a
lag in angle and not a drift off SO(3).

**Assumptions.** One body-fixed axis over the step; ω constant within the step; the error is in rotation angle,
not in energy or momentum.

**Flip.** A requirement below 10⁻⁵ relative needs ωh < 0.011: substep, or use the exponential on a tier that has
it.

**Lane A (reference).** Evaluate the table in `f64` from the inputs (`atan` from the standard library; the exact
root by 100 bisections on [10⁻⁶, 1]).

**Lane B (mechanism).** Build cay(x̂) with `frob-lie`'s public Cayley retraction for x̂ = (0, 0, x); recover the
angle as atan2(R₁₀, R₀₀); assert it equals θ_c within 4 ulp in `f64`; assert RᵀR = I within 64 ulp (the bound of
the retraction-law gate) so that "stays on the group" is checked, not assumed. Then call `frob-lie`'s admitting
step `cay_step(g, ξ, h)` (BD-011), whose test is the exact sign of (ω·ω)·h² − κ on the stored operands, κ the
stored `f64` nearest 0.04 (never a rounded comparison and never a square root). Because 0.2 is not a binary
fraction, "x = 0.2" is not an input; the cases use binary operands at h = 2⁻⁹ s: ω = 96 rad/s (x = 3/16)
admitted; ω = 128 rad/s (x = 1/4) refused with FROB-REF-212, returned substep count ⌈x/0.2⌉ = 2 and both repairs
listed; and the boundary pair ω = fl(102.4) (x² − κ = +3.6 × 10⁻¹⁸, refused) and the next `f64` below it
(x² − κ = −7.5 × 10⁻¹⁸, admitted), each decision compared with the sign computed in exact rationals.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_01_reference`: the eight goldens within their tolerances.
- `gb_01_mechanism`: angle of `cay` at x = 0.04 equals 0.03999466794630107 rad; orthogonality within 64 ulp;
  `cay_step` admits x = 3/16, refuses x = 1/4 with substep count 2, and on the boundary pair agrees with the
  exact rational sign (fl(102.4) refused, its predecessor admitted).
- `gb_01_series_order`: ε(x)/x² → 1/12 as x halves from 0.2 to 0.0125 (successive ratios within 10⁻³ of 1/12 at
  the smallest x): the error is second order.
- `gb_01_controls`: (a) "mislabelled retraction": the exponential reported as Cayley gives θ = x and ε = 0,
  which must miss the golden 1.333 × 10⁻⁴; (b) the first-order update I + x̂ has determinant 1 + x² ≠ 1 and
  must fail the orthogonality assertion; (c) the rounded comparison √(ω·ω)·h ≤ 0.2 in `f64` admits
  ω = fl(102.4) at h = 2⁻⁹ (fl(102.4)/512 equals fl(0.2) exactly), where the exact sign refuses it, and must be
  caught by the boundary-pair assertion.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-237 (rotation angle of one Cayley step is 2·atan(ω·h/2); control: a mislabelled retraction); KA-052 (the
Cayley result lies exactly on the group); CX-025 (tempting shortcut: use the Cayley update as if it were the
exponential at any step size; rule: bounded by ‖ω‖·h ≤ 0.2 per substep, refused beyond). Determinism: lane A is
D0 on one machine; `atan` may differ in the last ulp across platforms, which the relative tolerances absorb.

## Considerations

- The card speaks of angle only. Energy and momentum behaviour of the full step belong to the dynamics
  known answers, not here.
- If a tier ever needs ε below 10⁻⁵ at its nominal step, that is a design change (substeps or the exponential),
  not a tolerance to loosen.
