## Background

A robot whose closed loop has a finite symmetry group Γ (for a quadruped with no forward sensor, the two mirror
planes generate C₂ × C₂ of order 4) admits policies that commute with the group action. Such an equivariant
layer has fewer free weights, and every recorded trajectory yields |Γ| trajectories under the action. Bet BET-07
is that this dividend is worth building equivariant networks for; kill gate KILL-EQUIV decides at M4 whether
equivariant networks stay or symmetry is used for augmentation and testing only. This card is the arithmetic
both rest on: it says how large the dividend can be before any experiment runs. The group that counts is the
observation-preserving subgroup Γ_obs of the robot with its sensors and task, never the mechanical group alone:
a forward lidar on a quadruped leaves Γ_obs = C₂. Tier: Solid arithmetic for a Frontier mechanism. Subsystem:
Marrow.

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Between n and m copies of the regular representation of a finite group Γ, the space of
equivariant linear maps has dimension n·m·|Γ|, against (n·|Γ|)·(m·|Γ|) = n·m·|Γ|² unconstrained. In general,
dim Hom_Γ(V_in, V_out) = Σᵢ cᵢ·mᵢ^out·mᵢ^in over irreducibles i with multiplicities mᵢ and cᵢ = 1 for a real-type
irreducible, 2 for complex type, 4 for quaternionic type; the regular representation contains each real-type
irreducible dim(i) times. Each trajectory also yields |Γ| trajectories under the group action.

**Substituted (illustrative; the counts are exact).** Γ = C₂ × C₂ (order 4, four one-dimensional real-type
irreducibles); layer width 256 = 64 copies of the regular representation in and out.

| Output | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equivariant weights per layer, 64·64·4 | 16,384 | 16,384 | count |
| Unconstrained weights, 256² | 65,536 | 65,536 | count |
| Ratio | 4 | "divides by its order" | exact |
| Check by irreducibles: 4 × (1·64·64) | 16,384 | — | count |
| Trajectories per recorded trajectory | 4 | four | count |
| Γ_obs with a forward lidar: C₂, weights 64·64·… at width 256 = 128 copies | 128·128·2 = 32,768 | "dividend shrinks" | count |
| Trivial group at width 256 | 65,536 (dividend 1) | "nil" | count |

**Intuition.** The group divides both the parameters to learn and the data needed to learn them by its order.
For the bodies of this project the order is between 2 and about 12, so the dividend is real and small.

**Assumptions.** Hidden features carry the regular representation (on which pointwise nonlinearities commute
with the group). The group is Γ_obs, never Γ_mech alone.

**Flip.** A payload mounted on one side, or a forward-facing lidar, breaks the group to a subgroup and the
dividend shrinks with it; at the trivial group it is nil.

**Lane A.** Integer arithmetic from |Γ|, n, m; the irreducible-sum form from the character table of C₂ × C₂
written in the test (four characters with entries ±1).

**Lane B.** Build C₂ × C₂ from two commuting involutions with `frob-algebra`'s group constructor; form 64 copies
of the regular representation; ask the commutant routine for the free-parameter count of equivariant maps
(64 in, 64 out) and assert 16,384; assert the dimension of the image of the Reynolds (group-averaging) operator
on a random 8 × 8 block (2 copies in, 2 out) is 2·2·4 = 16. Surface, in the verb's own argument form (group as
the positional family, channel spaces `regular*k`, N13-14): `frob algebra irreps C2xC2 --in regular*64 --out
regular*64 --json` returns `free_parameters = 16384`, `unconstrained_parameters = 65536` and
`reynolds_rank = 16384` (the verb's always-on cross-check), all constituents of type `real`; `frob algebra
irreps C2 --in regular*128 --out regular*128 --json` returns `free_parameters = 32768` (the forward-lidar row).
The general formula admits quaternionic type (cᵢ = 4), but the verb refuses a quaternionic constituent with
FROB-REF-214; none occurs in the card's groups.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_02_reference`: 16,384; 65,536; ratio 4; irreducible sum 16,384; C₂ case 32,768; trivial case 65,536.
- `gb_02_mechanism`: commutant count 16,384; Reynolds image dimension 16 on the 8 × 8 block.
- `gb_02_complex_type_c3`: C₃ with one copy of its regular representation in and out (the cyclic permutation of
  three points) has commutant count 3 = 1·1·1 (trivial, real type) + 2·1·1 (the conjugate pair, complex type),
  equal to n·m·|Γ| = 3 and to the Reynolds rank; the "every irreducible real" miscount gives 2 and must fail.
- `gb_02_orbit_of_trajectory`: one `synthetic` 3-step observation sequence produces exactly 4 distinct images
  under C₂ × C₂ when its entries are generic, and fewer than 4 only when it lies in a fixed subspace.
- `gb_02_controls`: (a) a miscounted commutant that treats every irreducible as complex type gives 32,768 and
  must fail; (b) the mechanical group used where a forward lidar is mounted (C₂ × C₂ in place of C₂) gives
  16,384 where 32,768 is right, and the test asserts the two differ.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/worked_cards.sh` (NEG-02): runs the verb above, compares `free_parameters` with the golden, logs
command, exit code, evidence block and both values.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-232: regular representation of C₂ × C₂, 64 copies in and out: 16,384 free weights from
dim Hom = Σᵢ cᵢ·mᵢ^out·mᵢ^in with four real-type irreducibles of multiplicity 64; tolerance exact; control: a
miscounted commutant. Metamorphic: doubling the copies on one side doubles the count. Determinism: integer
arithmetic, D0 everywhere.

## Considerations

- The card bounds parameters and data, not learning speed; whether the dividend shows up in sample efficiency is
  the experiment behind KILL-EQUIV, and the card must not be cited as its result.
- Groups with complex-type irreducibles (C₃, C₆ on a handed hexapod) have cᵢ = 2 blocks; the regular-
  representation count n·m·|Γ| still holds, and a test on C₃ with one copy (count 3) guards the general formula.
