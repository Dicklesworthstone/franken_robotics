## Background

The GPU tiers compute in single precision (bet BET-08: `f32` is adequate on the GPU given anchoring, group
updates, scaling and guarded predicates). Single precision has 24 significant bits, so the gap between adjacent
representable numbers grows with magnitude. Compliant contact turns a position error into a force error at the
contact stiffness, so absolute `f32` coordinates fail long before a building is large. Decision ADR-NUM-06
therefore stores a world position as an integer cell index per axis plus an `f32` offset from the centre of an
8 m cell, with the offset in [−4, 4) m, and re-anchors exactly when a body crosses a cell face (proof obligation
PO-NUM-01: re-anchoring is exact, because the shift is a multiple of the cell side that is representable at the
offset's exponent). Rejected alternative: an uncentred offset in [0, 8): its worst spacing is twice as large and
its re-anchoring shift is inexact. Gate GATE-SPARK-05 measures the precision battery out to 1 km. This card is
the arithmetic of the decision. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Spark (numerics).

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-04.toml`; test `gb_04`; controls. Out: the anchored representation and `K-anchor`
(N21b-37); the precision battery over 10⁶ steps (BD-179); the record of the corrected figure (NEG-14).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** For a magnitude in [2^e, 2^(e+1)) the spacing of `f32` is 2^(e−23). A compliant contact of
stiffness k carrying force F penetrates δ = F/k, and a position error u produces a relative force error u/δ.

**Substituted (exact arithmetic for the spacings; the contact values are illustrative).**

| Output | Inputs | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spacing near 100 m | e = 6, [64, 128) | 2⁻¹⁷ m = 7.62939453125 µm | 7.6 µm | bits |
| Spacing near 1 km | e = 9, [512, 1024) | 2⁻¹⁴ m = 61.03515625 µm | 61 µm | bits |
| Worst spacing, centred offset | magnitude in [2, 4), e = 1 | 2⁻²² m = 0.2384185791015625 µm | 0.24 µm | bits |
| Worst spacing, uncentred offset | magnitude in [4, 8), e = 2 | 2⁻²¹ m = 0.476837158203125 µm | 0.48 µm | bits |
| Penetration | F = 75 N, k = 10⁵ N/m | 0.75 mm | 0.75 mm | exact |
| Force noise at 1 km | 2⁻¹⁴/0.00075 | 0.0813802083… | 8% | rel 10⁻¹² |
| Force noise in a centred cell | 2⁻²²/0.00075 | 3.18 × 10⁻⁴ | — | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Spacing below 32 m | e = 4, [16, 32) | 2⁻¹⁹ m = 1.9073486328125 µm (0.25% force noise) | "about 30 m … would work" | bits |

**Intuition.** Compliant contact turns position error into force error at the contact stiffness, so absolute
single-precision coordinates fail long before a building is large.

**Assumptions.** Penalty-like compliance at the stated stiffness; in the unanchored case, positions stored as
absolute `f32` and gaps formed by subtracting them.

**Flip.** Scenes under about 30 m with soft contacts would work without anchoring. Anchoring costs one integer
per axis and an exact re-anchoring step, so it is unconditional. An uncentred offset in [0, 8) is not an
alternative: its worst spacing is 0.48 µm and its re-anchoring shift is inexact.

**Lane A.** Compute each spacing as `f32::from_bits(x.to_bits() + 1) − x` at x = 100, 1000, 3.999, 7.999 and 31
and compare bit for bit with the powers of two; the force-noise ratios in `f64`.

**Lane B.** With the anchored-position type of `frob-kernels` (N21b-37): (1) a body at cell (125, 0, 0),
offset 0.3 m (world x = 1000.3 m, cell centres at 8·i m): adding 2⁻²² m changes the stored offset (the local
spacing at 0.3 m is 2⁻²⁵ m, and 2⁻²² m is the worst anywhere in the cell), where the same addition to an
absolute `f32` 1000.3 leaves it unchanged (spacing 2⁻¹⁴ m); (2) re-anchoring a body whose offset reaches +4 m moves it to −4 m in the next cell and the world
position, reconstructed in exact rationals before and after, is identical (PO-NUM-01); (3) the gap between two
bodies 0.75 mm apart at world x = 1 km is computed from offsets with error below 2⁻²² m, where subtracting
absolute `f32` coordinates has error up to 2⁻¹⁴ m.

- [ ] The card file validates with the outputs above; the corrected 0.24 µm is the golden and 0.48 µm appears

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_04_reference`: the eight goldens (five by bit equality).
- `gb_04_mechanism_resolution`, `gb_04_mechanism_reanchor_exact`, `gb_04_mechanism_gap_error`: as in lane B.
- `gb_04_offset_range`: an offset of exactly +4.0 m is not representable in the range [−4, 4) and is
  re-anchored; −4.0 m is kept.
- `gb_04_controls`: (a) an uncentred offset in [0, 8) (CX-369): worst spacing 2⁻²¹ m, and a body that drifts
  to −2⁻²⁵ m must add 8; 8 − 2⁻²⁵ is not an `f32` (the spacing just below 8 is 2⁻²¹), so the sum rounds to
  8.0 exactly: the reconstructed world position moves by 2⁻²⁵ m and the offset leaves [0, 8), both asserted,
  while the centred rule keeps −2⁻²⁵ m unchanged; (b) absolute `f32` coordinates at 1 km: gap error up to
  61 µm.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs the card's tests and logs each spacing as hexadecimal bits beside its golden. No verb takes these
inputs; `frob spark probe --device <d> --key <k> --json` (the IEEE probe in the verb's registry form: kind `run`,
so it takes an idempotency key) is called once per available device to record that the device under test uses
round-to-nearest single precision, which the card assumes; the command line and exit code are logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-792: single precision resolves about 7.6 µm at 100 m from the origin; an offset in [−4, 4) m resolves at
worst 2⁻²² m, about 0.24 µm (tolerance exact). The earlier text printed 0.48 µm, the value for an offset in
[4, 8); that figure is the planted wrong golden here. CX-369 (offsets kept in [0, 8): adding the cell side to
−2⁻²⁵ m rounds to 8.0) is control (a). Determinism: bit-exact on every platform with IEEE-754
binary32.

## Considerations

- The card's stiffness is illustrative; a stiffer contact (10⁶ N/m) makes the unanchored case ten times worse
  and leaves the anchored case at 0.3%.
- A step that moves an offset out of range in one step is refused at run time (FROB-REF-249); that refusal is
  tested by the mechanism's own bead.
