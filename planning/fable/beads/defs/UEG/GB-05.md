## Background

A differential drive cannot move sideways directly: its two rolling constraints leave a two-dimensional set of
admissible velocities (forward and turn). The sideways direction is reached only by combining them, as the Lie
bracket of the two motions. Construction C-07 measures this with the growth vector of the rolling distribution,
which is (2, 3) for a differential drive: two directions directly, the third at bracket depth two. The
consequence (the ball-box estimate) is that a displacement ε in a direction first reached at depth w costs path
length of order ε^(1/w). Analysis A-07 (maneuverability) reports this cost so that an agent choosing a base for a
tight interior sees it before simulating; the `diff-drive` archetype carries it as a known answer. This card is
the explicit manoeuvre and its cost. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Atelier.

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Four moves: rotate in place by α, drive forward a distance s, rotate back by −α, reverse along the
original heading by s·cos α. The net displacement is purely lateral, d = s·sin α. Wheel travel (per wheel,
half-track b): each rotation costs α·b, the forward leg d/sin α, the reverse leg d·cos α/sin α, so
cost(α) = 2·α·b + d·(1 + cos α)/sin α = 2·α·b + d·cot(α/2). For small α this is 2·α·b + 2·d/α, least at
α = √(d/b) with cost 4·√(d·b).

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.** b = 0.20 m.

The exact optimum solves d(cost)/dα = 2·b − d/(1 − cos α) = 0, that is cos α = 1 − d/(2·b): α = acos(0.975) and
acos(0.75) for the two shifts (the test checks the numerical minimum against this closed form).

**Intuition.** This is the ball-box estimate for growth vector (2, 3): cost scales as the square root of the
displacement, so small corrections are relatively the most expensive.

**Assumptions.** Ideal rolling with no lateral slip; the four-move manoeuvre, which is one admissible path and
therefore an upper bound on the optimum; wheel travel as the cost.

**Flip.** An omnidirectional base has growth vector (3) and pays d for d. A skid-steer base does not obey the
rolling model at all, since it turns only by slipping.

**Lane A.** Closed forms in `f64`; the exact optimum from acos(1 − d/(2b)) and independently by golden-section
search on [10⁻⁴, 3].

**Lane B.** A-07 on a `synthetic` differential drive with half-track 0.20 m: growth vector (2, 3); the
sideways-shift cost for d = 0.01 m and 0.10 m equals the exact goldens, and the manoeuvre it returns, integrated
with the closed-form chord step Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2), sin(θ + δ/2)) with δ = ω·Δt, ends at
(0, d, 0) within 10⁻¹² m. Surface: `frob design check --stages 4 --json` reports the growth vector, and the
maneuverability analysis reports `sideways_cost`.

- `gb_05_reference`: the goldens above.

- `gb_05_manoeuvre_closes`: integrate the four moves; lateral d, forward 0, heading 0.
- `gb_05_sqrt_scaling`: cost(d/2)/cost(d) at d = 0.01 m within 2% of 0.7071.
- `gb_05_mechanism`: A-07 values and growth vector (2, 3).
- `gb_05_controls`: (a) an omnidirectional `synthetic` base returns cost d (ratio 1), which must differ from
  17.9; (b) the small-angle form reported as exact at d = 0.10 m (0.5657 against 0.5537) must fail the exact
  golden; (c) a cost that counts the rotation once (α·b) must fail.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs the analysis verb on the `synthetic` base, compares `sideways_cost` for both shifts with the exact
goldens and logs α*, the cost, the growth vector and the evidence block.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-267 (differential drive: the chord step, growth vector (2, 3) and this sideways cost; control: a planar-
drive routine with a reported defect near straight-line motion used as the oracle). Metamorphic: scaling b and
d by the same factor scales the cost by that factor and leaves α* unchanged. Determinism: D0; `acos` may differ
in the last ulp across platforms.

## Considerations

- The manoeuvre is an upper bound on the optimal cost; a smoother path may be cheaper, never more than the
  ball-box order allows. A-07 reports it as a bound.
- A bracket direction is infinitesimal and assumes reversible inputs; where the drive slips the kinematic
  prediction is withdrawn.
