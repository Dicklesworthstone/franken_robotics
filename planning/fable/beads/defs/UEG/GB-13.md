## Background

All actuators of a robot draw from one power bus fed by a battery. Current-controlled drives draw power, not
current: when the bus voltage sags they draw more current to deliver the same power, and the bus sags further.
A resistive source therefore has a finite power ceiling above which there is no operating point at all. The
bus model records a brownout event when demand exceeds the ceiling and never completes a step at the requested
power (run time), and preflight stage 6 refuses a design whose peak demand exceeds the ceiling at the checked
corner (FROB-REF-133; ranked repairs of its registry row, N15-REF: lower `harness_resistance`; a battery card
with lower R₀ or a higher voltage; stagger the peaks; reduce the peak demand). A battery asked
for power when exhausted, or driven outside its state-of-charge bounds, is FROB-REF-135. Because every member's
demand is summed at one instant, the bus couples limbs that the kinematic tree separates. GATE-SINEW-07
measures the ceiling and operating point against these closed forms. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Sinew.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-13.toml`; test `gb_13`; controls. Out: the shared bus and its brownout event (BD-075);
the two-RC battery element (N15b-03); ageing states that grow R₀ (N15b-14).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Battery: V = OCV(z) − R₀·I − V₁ − V₂ with dVₖ/dt = −Vₖ/(Rₖ·Cₖ) + I/Cₖ (two RC pairs) and
dz/dt = −I/Q (state of charge z, capacity Q). A source E = OCV − V₁ − V₂ behind series resistance R delivers
power P only if P ≤ E²/(4·R), and then at V = [E + √(E² − 4·R·P)]/2 (the high-voltage root) with current P/V.
Under a constant current I from rest, Vₖ(t) = I·Rₖ·(1 − e^(−t/(Rₖ·Cₖ))).

**Intuition.** Current-controlled actuators draw power, not current, so a sagging bus draws more current and
sags further. Above the ceiling there is no operating point: the step records a brownout and never completes at
the requested power. The two pairs make the ceiling depend on the last seconds and the last minutes of load.

**Assumptions.** Constant-power loads; a resistive source; the demand of every member summed at one instant,
which is why the bus couples limbs that the tree separates.

**Flip.** The checked case is end-of-discharge voltage at cold-temperature resistance (stage 6). A 20% rise in
R lowers the ceiling by 17%, and an ageing state that grows R₀ can move a passing design to a refusal.

**Lane A.** Closed forms in `f64`; the RC states also by exact exponential update over 20 s in 2,000 steps
(V ← V·e^(−h/τ) + I·R·(1 − e^(−h/τ))), which must equal the closed form to 10⁻¹².

**Lane B.** `frob-actuate`'s bus with a `synthetic` battery card (OCV flat at 50 V, R₀ = 0.08 Ω, the two
pairs, large Q): (1) static solve at E = 48 V and 1,800 W returns the high root 44.78 V, never the low root
3.215 V; (2) demand 7,200 W returns V = 24 V exactly (the double root); demand 7,201 W raises the brownout event
and the step reports `delivered < requested`; (3) 20 s at a constant 40 A current load: E = 49.124 V; (4) two
members each demanding 3,700 W at the same tick brown out (7,400 > 7,200) although each alone passes; (5) the
same bus on a `synthetic` battery of small capacity, discharged at 40 A to its lower state-of-charge bound: the
battery fires `battery-exhausted`, the next request for power refuses with FROB-REF-135 (returned: the step and
the energy short in joules), and the state of charge is never clipped.
Surface, in the verb's own form (BD-075; design positional, demand with its unit): `frob actuator bus <design>
--demand 1800W --source-voltage 48V --json` returns 44.78 V, 40.19 A and the ceiling 7,200 W, with `condition:
source-voltage-pinned`; `--demand 7201W --source-voltage 48V` is refused with FROB-REF-133, exit code 3.
`--source-voltage` (BD-075) is required for these rows: without it the verb evaluates E from the battery state
at its default condition `end-of-discharge-cold`, which is not the card's 48 V, and neither the 7,200 W ceiling
nor the refusal at 7,201 W follows.
Preflight (`frob design check <design> --stages 6 --json`) on the 7,400 W design refuses with FROB-REF-133:
stage 6 always evaluates E itself, with polarization at its steady value for the mean current, so the
fixture's nominal profile carries a mean current of at least 40 A (E ≤ 48 V, ceiling at most 7,200 W).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_13_reference`: the eleven goldens.
- `gb_13_mechanism_static`, `gb_13_mechanism_double_root`, `gb_13_mechanism_brownout`,
  `gb_13_mechanism_rc_20s`, `gb_13_mechanism_two_members`, `gb_13_mechanism_exhaustion` (lane B items 1 to 5;
  the last asserts FROB-REF-135 with the step and the energy short, and fails on a battery that keeps
  supplying power after exhaustion).
- `gb_13_energy_balance`: at 1,800 W, source power E·I = 1,929.23 W equals delivered power plus R·I² = 129.23 W
  to 10⁻⁹.
- `gb_13_controls`: (a) a constant-current load model (I = P/E = 37.5 A, V = 45 V) never browns out at any
  demand and must fail the brownout test; (b) peak demands added without regard to time for two members that
  draw at different ticks wrongly refuses a feasible schedule: the test asserts the time-resolved sum admits
  it; (c) selecting the low root (3.215 V, 559.8 A) fails the golden.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-324: bus with source E and series resistance R under a power demand P: deliverable only if P ≤ E²/(4·R);
then V = [E + √(E² − 4·R·P)]/2; with 48 V and 0.08 Ω the ceiling is 7,200 W and at 1,800 W the bus sits at
44.8 V. Determinism: D0 for the static solve; the integrated case within its class per tier.

## Considerations

- The checked corner for a design is end-of-discharge OCV at cold-temperature resistance, not the card's
