## Background

A result computed on a fast simulation tier is `provisional` until the trusted tier (the CPU referee) has
audited it. The promotion rule (decision core DC-01, verb `eval.promote`) audits pairs of episodes, one per
tier on the same world address, and reduces each pair to one bit: did the tiers disagree beyond an envelope
frozen (hashed) before sampling? Two rates are fixed in advance, an acceptable exceedance rate p₀ and an
unacceptable one p₁, and a pair of e-processes decides: one promotes, its mirror rejects. By Ville's inequality
both error rates hold at any stopping time, so the audit may stop the moment either threshold is crossed.
Because the evidence depends only on the counts (n, k), the whole rule is an integer table that CI regenerates
and compares; the alternative, a fixed audit of 30 pairs, is kept as the forced fallback. Gates GATE-FID-02 and
GATE-ATEL-13 check the table. This card is the table's arithmetic. Tier: Solid. Subsystems: Kinesis (tiers),
Atelier (decision cores).

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-14.toml`; test `gb_14`; controls. Out: the e-process pair compiled to a table
(N16b-05); `eval.promote` and the trust ladder (BD-263); the decision-core record DC-01 (BD-309).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** After n pairs with k exceedances, ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁); promote
when E⁺ ≥ 1/α⁺. The mirror process E⁻ = (p₁/p₀)ᵏ·((1 − p₁)/(1 − p₀))^(n−k); reject when E⁻ ≥ 1/α⁻. Budget: 32
pairs; neither threshold by then leaves the result `provisional`.

**Substituted (illustrative; the table is exact), with goldens.** p₀ = 0.05, p₁ = 0.30, α⁺ = 0.01, α⁻ = 0.05.

| Output | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multiplier of a clean pair | 0.95/0.70 = 19/14 = 1.357142… | 1.36 | exact |
| Divisor of an exceedance | 0.30/0.05 = 6 | 6 | exact |
| Clean pairs to promote | smallest n with (19/14)ⁿ ≥ 100: 16 (15.08 by logarithms) | 16 | count |
| Pairs to promote with k = 1, 2 | 22; 29 | 22; 29 | count |
| Pairs to promote with k = 3 | 36 (beyond the 32-pair budget: `provisional`) | — | count |
| Reject at (n, k) = (3, 2) | E⁻ = 36·(14/19) = 26.526… ≥ 20 | 26.5 ≥ 20 | exact |
| Clean-pair cost of one exceedance | ln 6/ln(19/14) = 5.867 | about six | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Clean pairs at α⁺ = 0.1 | 8 (7.54) | eight | count |
| Clean pairs at p₁ = 0.5, α⁺ = 0.01 | 8 (7.17) | eight | count |
| Fixed fallback, 30 pairs, promote on k ≤ 2: wrong promotion at p₁ | 0.0021131781488865 | 0.0021 | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Fixed fallback: promotion at p₀ | 0.8121788131469594 | 0.81 | rel 10⁻⁹ |

**Intuition.** Clear cases promote after a handful of referee episodes, and each exceedance costs about six
clean pairs. The table (n, k) → promote, reject or continue is integer data that CI regenerates and compares.

**Assumptions.** The envelope is hashed before sampling; episodes are chosen by address before outcomes exist.
Independence between episodes is not needed.

**Flip.** A stratum whose true exceedance rate lies between p₀ and p₁ reaches the 32-pair budget and stays
`provisional`. An envelope tuned after outcomes voids the bound.

**Lane A.** Exact rational arithmetic: E⁺ = (19/14)^(n−k)·(1/6)ᵏ compared with 100, E⁻ compared with 20, for
all 0 ≤ k ≤ n ≤ 32 (561 cells); the fallback probabilities as exact binomial sums in rationals.

**Lane B.** The compiled table of `frob-opt` for these parameters equals lane A's cell for cell (verdict per
(n, k), first-crossing semantics: a path that has crossed stays decided); `frob cores show DC-01 --json`
prints the same table with its digest; `frob eval promote` on a `synthetic` stream of 16 clean bits promotes at
pair 16, and on bits (1, 0, 1) rejects at pair 3.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The card file validates with all five fields; the full 561-cell table is stored as a digest plus the

- [ ] `gb_14` passes both lanes; the table regenerates bit-identically on every platform.
- [ ] A changed envelope after the first outcome is refused by `eval.promote` (the control of KA-695).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_14_reference_table`: 561 cells in rationals; promotion boundary n = 16, 22, 29 for k = 0, 1, 2.
- `gb_14_reject_cells`: (3, 2) rejects; (2, 2) rejects (E⁻ = 36); (1, 1) continues (E⁻ = 6).
- `gb_14_fallback_probabilities`: 0.0021131781 and 0.8121788131 as exact rationals.
- `gb_14_mechanism_table_equal`, `gb_14_mechanism_streams`.
- `gb_14_error_rates`: seeded Monte Carlo, 10⁵ streams at p₁: promotion frequency ≤ 0.01; at p₀: rejection
  frequency ≤ 0.05; each with its binomial interval.
- `gb_14_controls`: (a) the table built from floating-point logarithms with a truncated threshold promotes at
  n = 15 (15.08 rounded down) and must miss 16; (b) an envelope re-hashed after pair 1 is refused; (c) a rule
  that restarts counting after a rejection (optional stopping across restarts) exceeds 5% false promotion in
  the Monte Carlo and is caught.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs `frob cores show DC-01 --json`, checks the table digest against the card, then drives
`frob eval promote` on the two `synthetic` streams; logs n, k, E⁺, E⁻, the verdict and the envelope hash.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-089 and KA-350 (integer promotion table: a clean pair multiplies the evidence by 0.95/0.70 and an exceedance
divides it by 6); KA-695 (DC-01: 16 clean pairs promote, or 22 with one exceedance; control: an envelope changed
after the first outcome). Determinism: integer and rational arithmetic, D0.

## Considerations

- The loss constants behind p₀, p₁ and the two levels are an owner decision (OPEN-ATEL-1); the card's values are
  illustrative and the table regenerates from whatever is registered.
- The card says nothing about the size of a disagreement, only its rate; the envelope carries the size.
