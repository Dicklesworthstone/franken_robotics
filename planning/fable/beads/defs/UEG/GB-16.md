## Background

While a GPU batch runs, a background referee on CPU cores re-simulates a sample of its worlds from snapshots
(verb `fidelity.shadow`). Bet BET-34 is that this sampled shadow catches kernel defects and tier disagreement
early enough to matter; kill gate KILL-SHADOW falls back to running the referee at promotion time only. How
much can be shadowed is arithmetic: the device produces simulated time far faster than CPU cores can re-produce
it, so the referee is a sampler by construction, and a faster engine makes the shadowed share smaller. Every
receipt therefore prints the coverage figure, and GATE-FID-07 checks it. This card computes the share and the
time needed to see an outcome gap. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Kinesis.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-16.toml`; test `gb_16`; controls. Out: the background referee (BD-214); the coverage

figure (NEG-07).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** A device at T world-steps/s with step h produces T·h world-seconds per second. A referee on c
cores at real-time factor ρ_rt per core covers c·ρ_rt of them: share = c·ρ_rt/(T·h). Detecting a gap δ in a
success rate near p (two-sided 5%, power 80%) takes about 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² episodes per tier.

**Substituted (illustrative, on planning TARGETs: 1.7 M and 2.0 M world-steps/s, h = 2 ms, c = 24,
ρ_rt = 0.2 to 1.0), with goldens.**

| Snapshot traffic, 256 humanoid worlds at 50 Hz | 6.9 MB/s | 539 bytes per snapshot (derived) | 6.9 MB/s | rel 10⁻³ |
| `batch-cpu` tier at 275 k world-steps/s | 550 world-s/s; 550/3,400 | 16.176…% | 16% | exact |

**Intuition.** The referee is a sampler by arithmetic, and a faster engine makes the shadowed share smaller.
One-step defects from a wrong kernel show in seconds; gaps in outcome take minutes to hours.

**Assumptions.** Referee speed per core is unmeasured (the range is a TARGET); sampled worlds are chosen by
address with a known probability; trajectories are not compared beyond a few steps.

**Flip.** A `batch-cpu` tier at 275 k humanoid world-steps/s covers 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400,
and becomes the tier that shadows a tenth or more. That comparison checks kernels and arithmetic, not the
contact law.

**Lane A.** Exact rationals.

**Lane B.** `sim.estimate`'s coverage model on the same inputs returns the share as a rational and the
expected time to detect a stated gap; `fidelity.shadow`'s sampler, configured for a share of 24/3,400, selects
worlds by address hash with realized frequency inside the binomial 99.9% interval over 10⁶ addresses, and each
receipt carries `coverage = selected world-seconds / produced world-seconds`. Surface: `frob sim estimate
--workload H --referee-cores 24 --json` reports `referee_share` in [0.0014, 0.0071].

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_16_reference`: every row above.
- `gb_16_mechanism_estimate`, `gb_16_mechanism_sampler_frequency`.
- `gb_16_faster_engine_smaller_share`: share is strictly decreasing in T.
- `gb_16_gap_formula`: 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² at (0.9, 0.05) and (0.5, 0.05).
- `gb_16_controls`: (a) share computed against world-steps instead of world-seconds (24/1.7 M) is 500 times
  too small and must miss; (b) the 0.5 M rate gives 0.48 to 2.4% and must miss 0.14 to 0.71%; (c) a sampler
  that picks the first worlds of the batch instead of hashing addresses fails the frequency-by-stratum check.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 calls `frob sim estimate` for workloads H and Q and compares `referee_share`; logs T, h, cores, the
real-time factor range, the share and the time-to-detect figures.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-361 (referee coverage arithmetic: 3,400 and 4,000 world-seconds per second); KA-821 (24 cores at 0.2 to 1.0
cover 4.8 to 24, 0.14 to 0.7% for H). Determinism: rational arithmetic D0; the sampler is a pure function of
address and key.

## Considerations

- Every rate here is a planning value; the receipts print the measured share, and this card's illustration is
  replaced, not contradicted, by a measurement.
- Coverage of world-seconds is not coverage of failure modes; the outcome-gap times are the honest figure for
  what the shadow can and cannot see.
