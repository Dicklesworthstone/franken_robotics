## Background

A foot or wheel touches the ground over a patch, not a point. A patch resists sliding and also resists
twisting about the contact normal, and the two share one friction budget: a foot that is pivoting has less grip
left for sliding. Construction C-37 therefore models a finite contact with a four-dimensional cone (normal
force, two tangential components, spin moment scaled by an effective patch radius r_eff), an ellipsoidal
approximation to the limit surface of the patch. The rejected alternative, a three-dimensional cone plus an
independent torsional row, grants full sliding friction and full spin moment at once, and lets a foot hold
where it should slip; policies learn to exploit it. GATE-TRAC-04 checks the combined law within 0.5%.
OPEN-TRAC-2 (how r_eff is obtained: from sample geometry, or from the interface card; fail-closed default:
Estimated from geometry, labelled as such) is still open. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Traction.

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Cone: √(λ_t1² + λ_t2² + (λ_s/r_eff)²) ≤ μ·λ_n, with tangential forces λ_t, spin moment λ_s and
normal force λ_n. Under maximum dissipation with sliding speed v and spin rate ω, the force lies on the
boundary opposite the generalized velocity (v, r_eff·ω): tangential force F_t = μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²), spin
moment M_s = μ·N·r_eff·(r_eff·ω)/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²). Effective radius: r_eff = 2a/3 for uniform pressure on a
disc of radius a (∫r·p dA/∫p dA) and 3π·a/16 for a Hertzian pressure.

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.** μ = 0.6, N = 100 N, a = 30 mm, uniform pressure.

| Output | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| r_eff, uniform | 0.020 m | 20 mm | exact |
| Pure sliding force | 60 N | 60 N | exact |
| Pure spin moment μ·N·r_eff | 1.2 N·m | 1.2 N·m | exact |
| Force at v = r_eff·ω | 42.426406871192846 N | 42.4 N | rel 10⁻¹² |
| Moment at v = r_eff·ω | 0.8485281374238569 N·m | 0.85 N·m | rel 10⁻¹² |
| Each as a fraction of its limit | 1/√2 = 0.7071067811865476 | 1/√2 | rel 10⁻¹² |
| Excess granted by independent rows | √2 − 1 = 0.41421356237 | 41% more | rel 10⁻¹² |
| r_eff, Hertzian | 3π·0.030/16 = 0.017671458676442587 m | 17.7 mm | rel 10⁻¹² |
| Pyramid cone along its diagonal | √2·μ (0.8485 for μ = 0.6) | √2·μ | rel 10⁻¹² |

**Intuition.** Sliding and spin share one budget. A pivoting foot resists sliding less, and a model with a
separate torsional row holds where the foot should slip.

**Assumptions.** An ellipsoidal approximation to the limit surface of a finite patch; r_eff from the sample
geometry or the interface card, Estimated unless calibrated; rolling resistance as a separate row.

**Flip.** A point contact (r_eff = 0) masks the spin row and returns the three-dimensional cone. A pyramid cone
of the same μ grants √2·μ along its diagonals, the same 41% in another direction, which the model-form check
plants as an exploit.

**Lane A.** Closed forms in `f64`; r_eff for the uniform disc also by numerical quadrature of ∫r·p dA/∫p dA on
a 2,000-ring grid (within 10⁻⁶ of 2a/3), and for p ∝ √(1 − r²/a²) (within 10⁻⁵ of 3πa/16).

**Lane B.** `frob-contact`: one contact with prescribed sliding v = 0.1 m/s and spin ω = 5 rad/s (so
v = r_eff·ω) solved through the four-dimensional cone returns F_t = 42.43 N and M_s = 0.8485 N·m within 0.5%
(the gate's bound), with the force anti-parallel to the generalized velocity; ω = 0 returns 60 N; v = 0 returns
1.2 N·m; r_eff = 0 masks the spin row and the solve equals the three-dimensional cone bit for bit. Surface:
`frob contact probe --law cone4 --mu 0.6 --normal 100 --reff 0.02 --slide 0.1 --spin 5 --json`.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_20_reference`: the nine goldens; both quadratures.
- `gb_20_mechanism_combined`, `gb_20_mechanism_pure_slide`, `gb_20_mechanism_pure_spin`,
  `gb_20_mechanism_point_contact_masks_spin`.
- `gb_20_dissipation_maximal`: among 10³ random admissible (F_t, M_s) the returned pair maximizes
  F_t·v + M_s·ω.
- `gb_20_sweep`: for ratios v/(r_eff·ω) ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 10} the pair lies on the ellipse
  (F_t/60)² + (M_s/1.2)² = 1 within 10⁻⁹.
- `gb_20_controls`: (a) independent tangential and torsional rows (magnitude √2 times the budget); (b) a
  pyramid cone, which grants 84.85 N along a diagonal where 60 N is the limit; (c) r_eff taken as the full
  radius a (30 mm) gives a pure spin moment of 1.8 N·m and must miss 1.2 N·m.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 calls `frob contact probe` at the three states and compares force, moment and the reported regime;
logs μ, N, r_eff with its provenance (Estimated), v, ω and both outputs against the 0.5% threshold.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-399: sliding at speed v while spinning at ω on the four-dimensional cone: tangential force
μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²); at v = r_eff·ω it is μ·N/√2; within 0.5% of μ·N/√2 (GATE-TRAC-04). Metamorphic:
scaling N scales both outputs; rotating the sliding direction in the tangent plane rotates the force with it.
Determinism: closed forms D0; the solve within its tier's class.
