## Background

GPUs have fast half-precision (16-bit) matrix hardware, and a tempting optimization is to factor the per-world
contact system in half precision and recover accuracy by iterative refinement. The project rejects it and does
not build it: a request for a half-precision solve or state buffer is refused (FROB-REF-245). The reason is
two-fold and is this card. First, refinement on a low-precision factor contracts only if the condition number
times the unit round-off is below one, and contact operators are too ill-conditioned for that at 16 bits.
Second, the systems are small (18 to 64 unknowns), so the refinement steps cost more than the factorization
they were meant to cheapen. Recording a rejected mechanism with its refuting calculation as a known answer
keeps it from being re-proposed. Matrix accelerators stay available to policy networks. Tier: Solid (a
rejection). Subsystems: Traction, Spark.

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Iterative refinement with a factor computed at unit round-off u_low contracts the error by about
κ·u_low per step, so it converges only if κ·u_low < 1. A Cholesky factorization of an n × n system costs n³/3
flops and one refinement step (residual plus two triangular solves) about 3n², so k steps cost
(3n²·k)/(n³/3) = 9k/n times the factor.

**Substituted (illustrative, with one FACT), with goldens.**

| Output | Inputs | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| u₁₆ | 2⁻¹¹ | 4.8828125 × 10⁻⁴ | 4.9 × 10⁻⁴ | exact |
| κ of a rank-one contact operator with proximal shift | W = w·wᵀ, shift ρ = 10⁻²·tr(W)/48: κ = (tr(W) + ρ)/ρ = 1 + 48/0.01 | 4,801 | 4,801 | exact |
| κ·u₁₆ | 4,801/2,048 | 2.34423828125 | 2.34 | exact |
| Cost ratio 9k/n | n = 27, k = 7 | 7/3 = 2.3333 | 2.3 | exact |
| — | n = 35, k = 6 | 54/35 = 1.542857 | 1.5 | exact |
| — | n = 18, k = 6 | 3 | 3.0 | exact |
| n above which six steps cost under a quarter | 9·6/n < ¼ | n > 216 | 216 unknowns | exact |
| FACT (emulated on three reference models, plan-time check): share of sampled contact states with κ·u₁₆ ≥ 1 after unit-diagonal scaling | — | 37% to 64% | 37 to 64% | as recorded |

**Intuition.** The matrices are too ill-conditioned for half precision and too small for refinement to pay. The
repair costs more than the factorization it was meant to cheapen.

**Assumptions.** Dense per-world systems of 18 to 64 unknowns; a single-precision baseline, against which the
theory promises at most a factor of two.

**Flip.** Systems above about 200 unknowns with κ below 10³ would change both terms, and none is planned for
the dense per-world solve. Matrix accelerators stay available to policy networks.

**Lane A.** Exact rationals for the table. Plus the numerical demonstration, on the emulated half-precision
type and the rank-one fixture of N18b-27 (one type and one fixture for that bead, this card and N21c-25): the
whole Cholesky factorization is run operation by operation in a software binary16 type (each result rounded to
nearest-even at 11 significand bits); the matrix is W + ρ·I with W = w·wᵀ, a seeded random direction
w ∈ ℝ⁴⁸, in either of its two equivalent scalings (tr(W) = 48 with ρ = 0.01, or tr(W) = 1 with ρ = 10⁻²/48);
ten refinement steps with residuals in `f64`. The assertion is the fixture's statement of non-contraction
(DESIGN, the same in N18b-27 and N21c-25), taken over the 200 seeds 0 to 199 because κ·u₁₆ ≥ 1 removes the
guarantee of convergence and does not make every step of every instance fail: in at least 90% of seeds the
error after ten steps is at least 10⁻² of its initial value, and in at least half it is at least its initial
value; while the same procedure with a single-precision factor (κ·u₃₂ = 4,801·2⁻²⁴ = 2.9 × 10⁻⁴) contracts by
at least 10³ at the first step on every seed and is below 10⁻⁶ within two steps. (Plan-time check with a small
program, FACT to re-run: 99% and 68.5% for the two shares.) Rounding only the entries of an exactly computed
factor to 11 bits is not this demonstration: that factor is a small relative perturbation of the true one and
refinement on it contracts on every seed, so the card's emulation must be per operation.

**Lane B.** `frob-contact` and the backends: half precision is refused with FROB-REF-245, exit code 3,
`retry: never` and an empty repair list at the two entry points of N18b-27 that can carry the request (a step
plan cannot: its width is `f32 | f64`). For the solve, a request whose `--precision` is `f16` (or `half`, `fp16`,
`mixed`, `mixed-f16`: the recognized-and-refused values) is refused at admission step 1; for a state buffer, a
kernel manifest whose element type for a solve or state slab is a 16-bit float is refused at registration with
the same code. The capability is not offered; the refusal's message names what is used instead, single
precision in scaled variables, but that is not a repair of the request. The registry of rejected mechanisms
(`registries/rejected_mechanisms.toml`, N21c-25) lists "half-precision contact factor with refinement" with
this card as its refuting calculation; the single-precision solve with `K-refine` on the same W reaches a
relative residual below 10⁻⁶ in at most two steps.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_22_reference`: the seven exact goldens.
- `gb_22_emulated_half_no_contraction` (the two shares over seeds 0 to 199, at least 90% and at least 50%),
  `gb_22_single_contracts` (every seed; at least 10³ at the first step; below 10⁻⁶ within two steps).
- `gb_22_kappa_of_shifted_rank_one`: eigenvalues of W are tr(W) + ρ (once) and ρ (47 times), so κ = 4,801
  exactly, computed by an exact rational LDLᵀ on a small integer instance.
- `gb_22_mechanism_245_solve` (the five refused `--precision` values at admission), `gb_22_mechanism_245_buffer`
  (a manifest with a 16-bit state slab at registration), `gb_22_mechanism_registry_entry`.
- `gb_22_controls`: (a) a build flag that silently downgrades to `f16` instead of refusing is caught by the
  admission test; (b) the cost model with a refinement step counted as n² (not 3n²) reports 0.78 at n = 27 and
  must miss 2.33; (c) κ computed without the shift (rank-one, singular) must be reported as unbounded, never as
  a small number; (d) a refusal that offers "use f32 with scaled variables" as a repair must fail the
  empty-repair-list assertion (the plan's refusal row says "None; not offered"); (e) an emulation that rounds
  an exactly computed factor instead of computing in half precision contracts on every seed and must fail
  `gb_22_emulated_half_no_contraction`.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 submits `frob sim run <design> --tier batch-cpu --precision f16 --key <k> --json` (`f16` is on the
recognized-and-refused list of N35-04's `--precision`, entered by N18b-27; a step plan cannot express a 16-bit
width at all) and expects FROB-REF-245 with exit code 3,
`retry: never` and an empty repair list (all three asserted; a non-empty list fails the step); logs the exact
command line, exit code, the refusal, κ, u, κ·u and the cost ratios from the card tests.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-423: half-precision refuting calculation: rank-one contact operator with proximal shift
ρ = 10⁻²·tr(W)/48 for 16 contacts: κ = 4,801 and κ·u₁₆ = 2.34, so refinement does not converge. Determinism:
exact rows D0; the emulation is seed-explicit.

## Considerations

- 48 is three rows per contact for 16 contacts; with the four-dimensional cone it is 64 rows and κ rises to
  6,401 under the same rule, which strengthens the rejection.
- The FACT share (37 to 64%) is about three reference models at default contact impedance; the humanoid's
  measured scaled condition numbers are 2 × 10³ to 9 × 10³ over 201 poses, consistent with it.
