## Background

Training one policy per candidate design is the dominant cost of a design campaign. Bet BET-15 is that one
family policy per morphology class, pretrained once on a generator of designs, decides most designs without
per-design training (it scores a new design with no adaptation, or reaches specialist return after a short
fine-tune). That is an investment: pretraining is paid before any design benefits, so the family policy pays
only when a campaign is large enough. Kill gate KILL-FAMILY returns the plan to per-design training with
inheritance along parameter edits if zero-shot return is below 70% of specialists on more than half the
held-out set together with fine-tuning above 20% of specialist steps. GATE-CORTEX-11 measures both. Pretraining
needs compute above the default envelope and is approved per run with its ceiling and kill rule written first.
This card is the break-even arithmetic. Tier: Frontier (its pilot is carried by the family-policy bead).
Subsystem: Cortex.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-28.toml`; test `gb_28`; controls. Out: family policies and `train.family` (BD-320);
the held-out experiment (BD-321); the KILL-FAMILY decision (N23a-26); the M4 variant with no family policy
(N40-34).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Pretraining cost P, specialist cost s per design, fine-tuning fraction f (cost f·s per design).
Campaign of N designs: specialists cost N·s; the family route costs P + N·f·s. The family policy pays when
N > P/(s·(1 − f)). Its gain on the training phase is N·s/(P + N·f·s), at most 1/f as N grows.

**Substituted (illustrative, on FACT denominators), with goldens.** P = 60 GPU-hours = 216,000 GPU-s. FACT
(source register, `reread = pending`): a published quadruped specialist at s = 410 s; humanoid specialists at
0.5 to 1 GPU-hour.

**Intuition.** A family policy is an investment that only a campaign amortizes. One design, or ten, should
train a specialist or inherit from a parent.

**Assumptions.** Scoring with no adaptation costs nothing beside training; fine-tuning reaches specialist
return at fraction f; pretraining is paid once per morphology class.

**Flip.** Return with no adaptation below 70% of specialists on more than half the held-out set, together with
fine-tuning above 20% of steps, fires KILL-FAMILY and returns the plan to per-design training with inheritance
for parameter edits. A campaign of fewer than about 600 quadruped designs never reaches break-even at these
values.

**Lane A.** Exact rationals.

**Lane B.** `frob-learn`'s campaign cost model (the function `train.family` and the cascade's planner call
before spending on pretraining): given P, s, f and N it returns `breakeven_designs`, `gain` and a verdict
`pretrain` or `specialists`; at N = 500 quadrupeds it returns `specialists`, at N = 700 `pretrain`; with f
unknown it returns the range over f ∈ [0.05, 0.2] and refuses to recommend pretraining unless the whole range
pays. A pretraining request with no written ceiling and kill rule is refused at admission (the compute rule
for runs above the default envelope): `train.family` without an owner-approved envelope token returns
FROB-REF-761 with exit code 4 and the estimate attached (BD-320). That is the code for the card's request: its
handler runs in process, and its 16-design job is below the `campaign` class. A `campaign`-class request that
the daemon queues without an approved envelope, which the full 60 GPU-hour pretraining is, is refused earlier,
at submission, by the size classifier with FROB-REF-704, also exit code 4 (BD-320; the order of the envelope
refusals is the registries').

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_28_reference`: the six goldens.
- `gb_28_gain_limit`: gain(N) is increasing in N and tends to 1/f; gain = 1 exactly at the break-even N.
- `gb_28_mechanism_verdicts`: P = 216,000 s, s = 410 s, f = 0.1 → `breakeven_designs` 585.3658; N = 500 →
  `specialists`; N = 700 → `pretrain`, gain 287,000/244,700 = 1.1729.
- `gb_28_mechanism_unknown_f_range`: f unknown over [0.05, 0.2] → break-even 554.5571 to 658.5366; N = 600 →
  no pretraining recommendation (the range does not pay throughout); N = 700 → `pretrain`.
- `gb_28_mechanism_admission_refusal`: `train.family` without an approval token → FROB-REF-761, exit code 4, the
  estimate attached, zero device-seconds.
- `gb_28_controls`: (a) break-even computed without the fine-tuning term (P/s = 526.8) must miss 585.4;
  (b) a gain quoted as 1/f = 10 for a 600-design campaign must be rejected (actual 1.02); (c) pretraining
  counted once per design instead of once per class never pays and is caught by the monotonicity test.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs the card's tests and reads `breakeven_designs`, `gain` and the verdict at N = 500 and 700 from the
test binary's JSON status line (the cost model is a function of `frob-learn`; `train.family` has no planning
flag in its registry entry, and its `--dry-run` emits tokens, not costs). From M4 it submits `frob train family
--class quadruped --generator <g> --split <s> --task-family velocity --designs 16 --budget <small> --key <k>
--json` without an approval token and expects FROB-REF-761 with exit code 4 (BD-320's form); logs the exact
command line, exit code, P, s, f, N, gain and the `citable` flag.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-589: family break-even N > P/(s·(1 − f)) at P = 60 GPU-hours, f = 0.1, s = 410 s: about 590 quadruped
designs; about 70 to 130 humanoid designs at s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour; ceiling on the training phase 1/f.
Determinism: rational arithmetic, D0.

## Considerations

- The denominators are published figures about other stacks; the binding s is the specialist time measured on
  the project's own training gate, and P is a request, not a measurement.
- The card ignores the value of zero-shot scoring as a ranking rung, which can pay before break-even on
  training cost; that benefit is measured by the rank-agreement experiment, not assumed here.
