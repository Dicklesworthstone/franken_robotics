## Background

Besides the anytime-valid study there is a second study type: a fixed number n of paired episodes with an exact
decision rule frozen before the first episode. Its advantage is that it has a finite set of outcomes, so the
whole rule can be enumerated before sampling. The enumeration answers a question people rarely ask in time:
can this study return the verdict it was commissioned for? At n = 64 with a five-point margin it cannot return
"practical tie" at all. The verb `eval.preanalyze` regenerates the table and CI compares it; a fixed-n study
with no pre-analysis, or one that names a verdict it cannot reach, is refused (FROB-REF-532; repairs: raise n
to the first reachable value, or register a different inference). Gates GATE-ARENA-05 and GATE-ATEL-06 check
the enumeration. The card also quantifies why a fixed-n test must not be peeked at. Tier: Solid. Subsystem:
Arena.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-30.toml`; test `gb_30`; controls. Out: the fixed-n exact engine (BD-124); the verbs

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Each of n pairs is B-only success, A-only success, or concordant. With counts (b, a), b + a ≤ n,
form two-sided 97.5% exact Clopper–Pearson intervals [L_b, U_b] and [L_a, U_a] on the two discordance
probabilities (each tail 0.0125: L solves P(X ≥ k | n, L) = 0.0125 with L = 0 at k = 0; U solves
P(X ≤ k | n, U) = 0.0125 with U = 1 at k = n). Then [L_b − U_a, U_b − L_a] covers the difference with
probability at least 95%. Verdicts with margin 0.05: prefer B if L_b − U_a > +0.05; B inferior if
U_b − L_a < −0.05; practical tie if the interval lies inside [−0.05, +0.05]; inconclusive otherwise. With no
discordant pair the interval is ±(1 − 0.0125^(1/n)).

**Intuition.** A frozen rule has a finite set of outcomes, and a wanted verdict may not be among them.
Enumerating the rule before sampling costs nothing and prevents a study that cannot return the answer it was
commissioned for.

**Assumptions.** Complete pairs; a rule frozen before the first episode; no stop on an interim look.

**Flip.** A claim of equivalence may instead register the one-sided bound on total discordance: no discordant
pair in 64 gives 0.0457, inside the five-point band. That is a different registered inference and not a rescue
of the first.

**Lane A.** Exact binomial tail sums in rationals; interval endpoints by bisection to 10⁻¹⁵ and each verdict
decided by evaluating the binomial tail at the margin-shifted endpoint (so no verdict depends on a rounded
root); the peeking probabilities by dynamic programming over (looks, successes) in rationals.

**Lane B.** `frob-arena`'s pre-analysis for the same rule returns the four rows above with a table digest, the
list of reachable verdicts, and `first_n_for_tie = 86`; `frob study plan --kind fixed --n 64 --margin 0.05
--expect tie --json` is refused with FROB-REF-532 naming 86 as the repair; the same with `--expect prefer`
is admitted; at the level of qualification request i the pre-analysis uses tail α_i/4.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_30_reference_enumeration`: four rows; symmetry (prefer = inferior at every n).
- `gb_30_reference_tie`: none at 64 and 85; exactly (0, 0) at 86; 99 and 178 at the two qualification levels.
- `gb_30_reference_peeking`: 13/256 after 8 looks (the first possible rejection is at look 5 with 5 of 5,
  probability 1/32); 0.17418 after 64.
- `gb_30_reference_one_sided`: 0.0457297.
- `gb_30_mechanism_table_digest`, `gb_30_mechanism_532`, `gb_30_mechanism_level_scaling`.
- `gb_30_controls`: (a) intervals at 95% each (tail 0.025) put the first tie at n = 72 and the n = 64
  half-width at 0.0560, which must miss 86 and 0.0662 (the level must be the registered 97.5%); (b) a Wald
  interval in place of the exact one reaches a "tie" at (0, 0) for every n (zero width) and is rejected;
  (c) a study that stops at the first look where the rule would prefer B has a false-preference rate above 5%
  in the peeking computation and is refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 calls `frob eval preanalyze --n 64 --margin 0.05 --json` (and 86, 128, 256), compares the counts and the
digest, then submits the two `study plan` requests; logs counts per verdict, the first tie, and the refusal
payload.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-612 and KA-693 (rule enumeration at n = 64, 86, 128, 256: 2,145 count pairs, 521, 521, 1,103, 0; first tie
at n = 86 for (0, 0) only; 15 B-only pairs for a preference); CX-265 (tempting shortcut: a fixed-n study can
return any of its verdicts). FACT: the n = 64 enumeration was executed at plan time and agrees. Determinism:
rational arithmetic, D0.

## Considerations

- The first-tie result depends on the registered level: with 95% intervals it would be n = 72. The card and the
  engine both carry the level explicitly so that the two are never mixed.
- Power is a separate question: at n = 64 the chance of "prefer B" is about 0.12 at discordance (0.17, 0) and
  0.96 at (0.40, 0.02); the study plan prints the power row for the declared alternative.
