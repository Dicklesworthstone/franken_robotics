## Background

An evaluation battery is a small set of scenes chosen to expose as many failure modes as possible. The verb
`nemesis.cover` selects one greedily from a scene-by-failure-mode incidence matrix (whose rows come from the
hazard registries) and returns the selection with a certificate of how close it is to the best battery of the
same size (decision core DC-10; proof obligation PO-NEMESIS-03). The general guarantee for greedy selection of
a monotone submodular function is 1 − 1/e ≈ 63% of the optimum, but the instance usually admits a much tighter
bound computed from numbers the greedy trace already holds. GATE-NEMESIS-08 checks certificates on 1,000
instances. A covering battery overfits to known failures, so one slot in five is drawn from held-out scenes,
and a rare-failure bound refuses battery-selected episodes. Tier: Solid. Subsystem: Nemesis.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-33.toml`; test `gb_33`; controls. Out: `nemesis.cover` (BD-278); battery assembly with
held-out slots and the fallback to the standard battery (N26b-11); the incidence export (N19a-38).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** f(S) is the total weight of failure modes exposed by at least one scene of S; f is monotone and
submodular (marginal gains shrink as S grows). For a greedy selection S of k scenes, OPT_k ≤ f(S) + (the sum
of the k largest marginal gains of the remaining scenes with respect to S), and f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k.

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.** Forty modes of total weight 100; k = 6.

| Output | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greedy gains | 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4 | as stated | exact |
| Covered weight f(S) | 86 | 86 | exact |
| Six largest remaining gains, summed | 12 | 12 | exact |
| Bound on the optimum | OPT₆ ≤ 98 | 98 | exact |
| Certified ratio | 86/98 = 43/49 = 0.877551… | within 88% | exact |
| Worst-case constant | 1 − 1/e = 0.6321205588 | 63% | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Trivial bound | OPT₆ ≤ 100 (total weight), ratio 0.86 | — | exact |

**Intuition.** The certificate of the instance is usually far tighter than the constant, and it is computed from
numbers the greedy trace already holds.

**Assumptions.** Mode weights declared in advance; an incidence matrix built under the identities in force;
equal scene costs, since unequal costs use the cost-benefit variant and its weaker constant.

**Flip.** A change of model class, tier, kernel version or taxonomy voids the matrix, and the standard battery
returns. A battery of past failures alone overfits, so one slot in five is drawn from held-out scenes, and a
rare-failure bound refuses battery-selected episodes.

**Lane A.** Integer arithmetic on a `synthetic` instance stored in the card file: 40 modes with integer
weights summing to 100 and 30 scenes whose incidence is constructed so that greedy gains are exactly
(31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4) and the six largest remaining gains sum to 12. The test runs its own greedy, recomputes
the certificate, and for a reduced copy of the instance (12 scenes, k = 3) finds OPT₃ by exhaustive search
over the 220 subsets to confirm f(S) ≤ OPT ≤ bound.

**Lane B.** `frob-nemesis` on the same instance: `nemesis.cover --k 6` returns the same six scenes in the same
order (ties broken by scene identifier), gains, f(S) = 86, bound 98 and ratio 43/49 as a rational; the
certificate verifies in the checker by recomputing marginal gains from the matrix; with the incidence matrix's
identity changed (a different kernel version in its header) the verb returns the fallback `standard` battery
and the DC-10 record says why; the assembled battery of five slots contains exactly one held-out scene.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The card file validates with all five fields; the instance is stored with it (or by digest).
- [ ] `gb_33` passes both lanes; the certificate satisfies f(S) ≤ OPT ≤ bound on the exhaustively solved
      reduced instance.
- [ ] A stale matrix triggers the fallback; a rare-failure bound requested on battery-selected episodes is
      refused.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_33_reference_greedy`: gains, 86, 12, 98, 43/49.
- `gb_33_reference_exhaustive_small`: OPT₃ on the reduced instance lies between f(S) and the bound.
- `gb_33_submodularity_property`: on 10³ random weighted-coverage instances, marginal gains are non-increasing
  along the greedy trace and the bound holds against exhaustive search where k ≤ 3 and scenes ≤ 14.
- `gb_33_mechanism_selection_and_certificate`, `gb_33_mechanism_stale_matrix_fallback`,
  `gb_33_mechanism_heldout_slot`.
- `gb_33_controls`: (a) a "certificate" that reports f(S)/total weight (0.86) as the ratio to the optimum
  understates what is proven and is rejected for not being the registered bound; (b) a certificate whose top-k
  remaining gains are taken before the last greedy pick (so include the picked scene's gain) overstates the
  bound's tightness and fails the exhaustive check on a planted instance; (c) unequal scene costs passed to
  the equal-cost rule are refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 calls `frob nemesis cover --k 6 --matrix <synthetic> --json`, compares the selection, the gains and the
certificate; logs matrix identity, weights, gains, bound, ratio and the held-out slot.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-669: coverage certificate, worked instance: 40 modes of total weight 100; greedy gains 31, 22, 14, 9, 6, 4;
the six largest remaining gains sum to 12; 86 covered; OPT₆ ≤ 98; the selection is within 88% of the best
six-scene battery. Determinism: integer arithmetic and identifier tie-breaks, D0.

## Considerations

- The certificate bounds coverage of declared modes with declared weights; it says nothing about modes missing
  from the taxonomy, which is why held-out slots exist.
- The incidence matrix is evidence with an identity; any change of model class, tier, kernel version or
  taxonomy voids it.
