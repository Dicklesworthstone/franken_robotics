## Background

The design ladder evaluates candidates on rungs of rising cost (seconds of analysis, minutes of baseline
control, tens of minutes of training). A cheap rung may discard a design only if its score predicts the
expensive rung's, and it cannot separate designs closer than its own error. Construction C-26 measures that
error on an audit set (designs drawn by key before any score exists, scored on both rungs, never cancelled)
and turns it into a survivor margin by split-conformal calibration: every design within twice the margin of
the k-th best cheap score advances (decision cores DC-02 and DC-03). Kill gate KILL-LADDER keeps the fixed
halving table if conformal survivor sets do not pay; GATE-ATEL-08 measures the layer. The same finite-sample
law governs the reality band of the calibration subsystem (GATE-GROUND-06) and the floor of 19 battery points
in DC-08. The layer is Frontier and its pilot is carried by the ladder beads. This card holds the index
arithmetic, the coverage law and the ladder cost. Subsystems: Atelier, Ground.

## Scope

## Technical approach

**Equation.** On n audit designs with residuals rᵢ = |y_hi − ĝ(y_lo)| (ĝ a monotone map fitted on a separate
split), the margin q̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest residual; every design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best
lower-rung score advances. Conditional on the calibration set, coverage is distributed Beta(n + 1 − l, l) with
l = ⌊(n + 1)·α⌋. A finite margin needs n ≥ (1 − α)/α.

**Intuition.** A rung cannot separate designs closer than twice its own error, so the size of q̂ decides whether
the ladder saves a factor of three over halving or nothing. Coverage holds on average over calibration draws
and moves by several points at small n.

**Assumptions.** Audit designs drawn by key before any score exists and never cancelled; exchangeability with
the designs being scored; calibration never taken from survivors.

**Flip.** q̂ = 0.10 lets about twenty designs through, more than 3k = 9, and the layer gives back most of its
advantage. Calibrating on promoted designs under-covers wherever the residual correlates with the cheap score.

**Lane A.** Integer index arithmetic; the Beta distribution functions as exact binomial sums in rationals
(for integer parameters, I_x(a, b) = Σ_{j=a}^{a+b−1} C(a + b − 1, j)·xʲ·(1 − x)^(a+b−1−j)); the ladder costs in
exact minutes. A seeded simulation checks the law: 10⁴ calibration sets of 39 exchangeable residuals, coverage
of each computed against the known residual distribution; the empirical mean lies within 0.002 of 0.95 and
the empirical 5% quantile within 0.01 of 0.884.

**Lane B.** `frob-opt`'s calibration on a `synthetic` rung pair with a planted residual law: index 38 of 39;
with n = 18 at α = 0.05 the margin is reported infinite and the layer falls back to the halving table (the
forced fallback of DC-02); the survivor set for the card's scores cuts at 0.66; a calibration set that
contains a promoted design is refused; the cost model returns 40.5 h, 6.075 h, 2.025 h and the audit lane as a
separate 57 min. The reality-band routine reports the coverage law Beta(19, 2) with its mean and the 0.069
probability beside the nominal 90%.

### Unit tests

- `gb_35_reference_index`, `gb_35_reference_beta_38_2`, `gb_35_reference_beta_19_2`,
  `gb_35_reference_floor_19`, `gb_35_reference_ladder_costs`.

- `gb_35_mechanism_index_and_cut`, `gb_35_mechanism_infinite_margin_fallback`,
  `gb_35_mechanism_survivor_in_calibration_refused`, `gb_35_mechanism_cost_model`.
- `gb_35_controls`: (a) the index ⌈n·(1 − α)⌉ = 38 for n = 39 happens to agree, so the control uses n = 40
  (⌈41 × 0.95⌉ = 39 against ⌈40 × 0.95⌉ = 38) and the off-by-one rule must under-cover in the simulation;
  (b) calibrating on survivors (the top third by cheap score, with residuals planted to grow with the score)
  under-covers by more than 5 points and is caught; (c) a margin of q̂ in place of 2·q̂ cuts at 0.70 and
  discards a planted winner.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs the card's tests and, where the cascade's planner exists, `frob opt cascade --plan-only` on 81
`synthetic` designs; compares the three ladder costs and the audit-lane line; logs n, α, the index, q̂, the cut,
the survivor count and the coverage law.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-136: conformal index on an audit set: n = 39 and α = 0.05 select the 38th smallest residual; fewer than 19
audit designs cannot support α = 0.05. Determinism: integer and rational arithmetic D0; simulations
seed-explicit.

## Considerations

- Coverage is marginal over calibration draws; at n = 39 one draw in twenty has true coverage below 88.4%. The
  report prints the law, not just the nominal level.
- The ladder cost is a serial sum of rung times; device parallelism changes wall-clock, not the count.
