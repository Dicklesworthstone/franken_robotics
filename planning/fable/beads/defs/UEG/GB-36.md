## Background

When two finalist designs are close, an agent can spend on more fast-tier episodes, on referee episodes, or on
real trials. They are not interchangeable: fast-tier episodes shrink sampling noise but not tier bias, only
the referee shrinks tier bias, and only real data shrinks the reality gap. The verb `experiment.suggest` ranks
candidate experiments by expected value of sample information per unit cost and says STOP when nothing is
worth buying (decision core DC-05, construction C-39). The variance components are read from the ledgers; the
exchange rate between core-seconds, GPU-seconds and real episodes is declared (owner decision OPEN-ATEL-1). The
model ranks experiments and never issues a verdict: a wrong variance model costs efficiency, never validity.
Kill gate KILL-VOI reduces the mechanism to a stop rule with unranked proposals if its stated intervals are
miscalibrated; GATE-ATEL-15 measures it. The mechanism is Frontier and its paper-scale pilot (PILOT-C-39:
thirty registered experiments on the mission slice, N10c-11) precedes it. Subsystems: Atelier, Ground.

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Posterior difference Δ ~ N(m, s²) between two finalists. An experiment that removes variance s̃²
has EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃, φ and Φ the standard normal density and distribution. A
measurement of variance v on a component of prior variance w removes w²/(w + v).

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.** m = 0.03; sampling variance 0.03² = 0.0009; tier-bias variance
0.04² = 0.0016; noise per pair 0.3 on the fast tier and 0.1 on the referee.

**Intuition.** Cheap episodes are bought until only tier bias is left, which only the referee shrinks, and real
episodes are worth buying where the reality gap is the largest unknown. Calibration does not make simulation
faster; it changes which experiment is worth buying.

**Assumptions.** A Gaussian posterior whose variance components are read from the ledgers; a declared exchange
rate between core-seconds, GPU-seconds and real episodes. The model ranks experiments and never issues a
verdict.

**Flip.** A wrong variance model costs efficiency and never validity. If stated 80% intervals cover fewer than
65% or more than 92% of thirty registered experiments, the core falls back to the fixed order of check,
simulate, train, referee.

**Lane A.** Closed forms in `f64` (`erf` for Φ); cross-check EVSI by quadrature of
E[max(Δ', 0)] − max(m, 0) with Δ' ~ N(m, s̃²) on a 20,001-point grid over ±8·s̃ (agreement to 10⁻⁷).

**Lane B.** `frob-opt`'s value-of-information table for these variance components and costs ranks: fast batch
first (value per cost 1.306 × 10⁻³), then the referee batch (2.04 × 10⁻⁴ per unit), and after the first fast
batch it ranks the referee first; at m = 0.15 it returns STOP with the perfect-information value; with the
reality-gap term 0.10 it ranks the real batch above all simulation; with every candidate's value below the
registered floor it returns STOP and the zero-value reason. The forced fallback fires when a `synthetic`
history of thirty experiments has interval coverage 60%.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_36_reference_evsi`: the EVSI rows; `gb_36_reference_variance_removed`: w²/(w + v) rows.
- `gb_36_reference_quadrature`.
- `gb_36_properties`: EVSI is 0 at s̃ = 0, increasing in s̃, decreasing in |m|, and at most s̃/√(2π).
- `gb_36_mechanism_ranking_sequence`, `gb_36_mechanism_stop`, `gb_36_mechanism_reality_gap`,
  `gb_36_mechanism_calibration_fallback`.
- `gb_36_controls`: (a) fast-tier pairs credited with removing tier bias (w = 0.0025) inflates the first
  batch's s̃ to 0.046 and keeps buying fast episodes; the test asserts the second fast batch is ranked below
  the referee; (b) EVSI computed with z = m/s (prior width) instead of s̃ gives a value independent of the
  experiment and is rejected; (c) costs omitted (ranking by raw EVSI) puts the referee first at step one and
  must miss the registered ranking.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 calls `frob experiment suggest --study <plan> --json` (the verb takes the study whose finalists are
compared; registry form `--study plan:…`) on `synthetic` studies whose ledgers carry these components at the
three states (initial, after one fast batch, m = 0.15) and compares the ranking and values (fast batch first;
then the referee, at 1.29 × 10⁻⁴ per unit against 1.02 × 10⁻⁵; then STOP); logs the exact command lines, exit
codes, m, the components, s̃, EVSI, cost and value per cost for every candidate.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-187: expected value of sample information EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃: 0 when s̃ = 0;
increasing in s̃; decreasing in |m|; a campaign in which no experiment's ratio clears the floor returns STOP.
Determinism: closed forms D0 up to `erf` rounding (tolerances absorb it).

## Considerations

- The exchange rate and loss constants are owner decisions; the card's 27 GPU-second equivalents is an
  illustration and the table regenerates from the registry.
- Real pairs are only purchasable where bench data exist; elsewhere the proposal is listed as unavailable with
  its value shown, so that the cost of having no calibration is visible.
