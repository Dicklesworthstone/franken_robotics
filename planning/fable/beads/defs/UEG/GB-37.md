## Background

Foundry turns a simulated design into something buildable. Two of its checks rest on small formulas with large
consequences. Fatigue: link life is estimated from the stress histories of simulated campaigns by cycle
counting (rainflow, with residues carried between windows) and a linear damage rule; because life falls with a
power of stress amplitude, rare overloads dominate, so a campaign that never contains them certifies nothing,
and their rates must come from the adversarial subsystem. Tolerance: dimensional tolerances are allocated so
that the statistical stack meets a limit at least cost, using first-order sensitivities from the simulation;
the optimum loosens tolerances where sensitivity is low and cost is high. A check whose material card lacks
the field or provenance it needs is refused (FROB-REF-163); an allocation with a zero or unknown sensitivity,
or a failed band-extreme check, is refused (FROB-REF-165). Kill gate KILL-TOLERANCE withdraws sensitivity-
driven allocation if it does not beat equal tolerances; gates GATE-FOUNDRY-03 and GATE-FOUNDRY-08 measure both.
Every life figure is Estimated. Tier: Frontier (pilot of C-35 in the construction's specification beads).
Subsystem: Foundry.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-37.toml`; test `gb_37`; controls. Out: cycle counting and damage (BD-402, N10c-06);
tolerance allocation and `foundry.tolerance` (BD-406, N10c-07); load extraction from telemetry (BD-400).

## Technical approach

**Equation.** Linear damage D = Σ nᵢ/N(Sᵢ), with Basquin's life N(S) = (S/S₀)⁻ᵐ, so a load at c times the
amplitude has life c⁻ᵐ times as long. Statistical stack: (Σ aᵢ²·tᵢ²)^½ ≤ T for sensitivities aᵢ and tolerances
tᵢ. Minimizing the cost Σ kᵢ/tᵢ under it (Lagrange multipliers) gives tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓.

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.**

| Output | Inputs | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Damage of the base load | m = 3; 10⁶ cycles at an amplitude of life 2 × 10⁶ | 0.5 | 0.5 | exact |
| Life at five times the amplitude | 2 × 10⁶/5³ | 16,000 cycles | 16,000 | exact |
| Damage of 10³ overload cycles | 10³/16,000 | 0.0625 | 0.0625 | exact |
| Overload share of damage | 0.0625/0.5625 | 1/9 = 0.1111… | 11% | exact |
| Overload share of cycles | 10³/(10⁶ + 10³) | 0.000999 | 0.1% | exact |
| Allocation, equal sensitivity, cost weights 1 and 8, T = 0.1 mm | t ∝ k^⅓ = 1 : 2 | 0.0447213595 mm; 0.0894427191 mm | 0.0447; 0.0894 | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Allocation, sensitivities 1 and 4, equal cost, stack limit 10 µm at coverage factor 3 (T = 30 µm) | t₂/t₁ = 16^(−⅓) | 15.9904123798 µm; 6.3457993583 µm | 16.0; 6.35 | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Equal tolerances meeting the same limit | 30/√17 | 7.2760687511 µm | 7.28 | rel 10⁻⁹ |
| Cost saving of the allocation | 1 − (1/t₁ + 1/t₂)/(2/t_eq) | 0.1991884730 | 19.9% | rel 10⁻⁸ |

**Intuition.** Rare overloads dominate life, so a campaign that never contains them certifies nothing; their
rates come from Nemesis. Tolerance is cheapest to loosen where sensitivity is low and cost is high, and the
cube root keeps the allocation from being extreme.

**Assumptions.** Signed stress histories with residues carried between windows; a stress–life curve valid for
the material state, process and finish; nonzero first-order sensitivities; independent feature deviations.

**Flip.** A zero first-order sensitivity is not insensitivity (q = x² at a nominal of zero): the feature is
refused and goes to the band-extreme check. A damage figure that moves by more than 5% when the window is
halved fails the pilot of C-35. Every life figure is Estimated.

**Lane A.** Rational arithmetic for the damage rows; `f64` for the allocations, with a check that the
allocation satisfies the stack with equality and the stationarity condition kᵢ/(aᵢ²·tᵢ³) equal across
features to 10⁻¹²; a brute-force scan of t₁ over 10⁵ points confirms the minimum.

**Lane B.** `frob-foundry`: a `synthetic` stress history of 10⁶ constant-amplitude cycles followed by 10³
cycles at five times the amplitude, counted by rainflow, gives D = 0.5625 with the overload contributing
0.0625; the same history split into two windows with residues carried gives the same D, and without carried
residues it changes (the control); the allocation of `frob-foundry` (N31a-32, the function behind
`foundry.tolerance`) on the two two-feature stacks (sensitivities 1 and 4 at equal cost; equal sensitivity at
cost weights 1 and 8) returns the goldens and the 19.9% saving against equal tolerances; a feature with
sensitivity 0 at nominal (q = x²/L at nominal zero, the `quadratic` fixture of N31a-33) is taken off the
first-order allocation and routed to the band-extreme check: a purely statistical allocation (`--method
statistical`) is refused with FROB-REF-165, exit code 3, naming the feature, and the default method `auto`
completes with exit code 2, the feature on the robust path with reason `zero-sensitivity` and a worst-case box
in place of a tolerance loosened without limit; a material card without a stress–life curve for the stated
finish is refused with FROB-REF-163 (exit code 3).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_37_reference_damage`, `gb_37_reference_allocation_weights`, `gb_37_reference_allocation_sensitivities`,
  `gb_37_reference_stationarity`, `gb_37_reference_bruteforce_minimum`.
- `gb_37_mechanism_rainflow_damage`, `gb_37_mechanism_window_residues`, `gb_37_mechanism_tolerance`,
  `gb_37_mechanism_165`, `gb_37_mechanism_163`.
- `gb_37_halved_window`: D moves by less than 5% when the counting window is halved, with residues carried.
- `gb_37_controls`: (a) a campaign with the overloads removed reports D = 0.5 and a life 12.5% too long; the
  test asserts the overload term is present whenever the history contains it; (b) a worst-case (linear) stack
  Σ aᵢ·tᵢ ≤ T used in place of the statistical one gives a different allocation (tᵢ ∝ √(kᵢ/aᵢ): t₁ = 10 µm,
  t₂ = 5 µm) and must miss; (c) q = x² at nominal zero treated as insensitive (infinite tolerance)
  is the planted defect of KA-745 and must be refused.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs `frob foundry tolerance --fixture worked-case --json` (the cluster's named-fixture input, N31a-51,
in place of the positional design: a design's sensitivities come from its model, so only a fixture carries the
card's 1 and 4; the verb is a `read` and takes no key) and compares the two tolerances, the equal-tolerance
value and the saving; then `frob foundry tolerance --fixture quadratic --method statistical --json` (expected
FROB-REF-165, exit code 3: the default method does not refuse) and the same call without `--method` (exit code
2, the feature boxed with reason `zero-sensitivity`). Both fixtures are in the index when BD-406 closes; the
case of cost weights 1 and 8 is checked in lane B through the library, its verb fixture `cost-1-and-8` being
N31a-56's. The script logs the exact command lines, exit codes, sensitivities, cost weights, the stack value,
each tolerance and the evidence class (Estimated). The damage rows are checked by the card's tests; their verb (`foundry.check`'s
fatigue check) is not called here because it needs a geometry and a load campaign.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-169 (linear damage with Basquin exponent m = 3: D = 0.5 plus 0.0625; 0.1% of the cycles carry 11% of the
damage); KA-171 (equal sensitivity, cost weights 1 and 8, statistical limit 0.1 mm: tᵢ ∝ (kᵢ/aᵢ²)^⅓ gives
0.0447 mm and 0.0894 mm); KA-745 (tolerance allocation: 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm; 19.9% cheaper than equal
tolerances under the 1/t cost model; control: q = x² at nominal zero is refused). Determinism: D0.

## Considerations

- "Budget of 10 µm at k = 3" is read here as a limit of 10 µm on the stack's standard deviation with tolerances
  as ±3σ bands, that is T = 30 µm in tolerance units; this reading reproduces all four printed figures and is
  recorded in the card file.
- Linear damage ignores sequence effects; that is why life is Estimated and never Tested.
