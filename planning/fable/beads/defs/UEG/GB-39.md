## Background

An agent's turn waits on short interactive jobs (a preflight, a ten-second evaluation rung), while the same
device also runs half-hour training jobs. On one device the queueing discipline decides whether the agent
waits a fraction of a second or half an hour. The daemon therefore sorts work into size classes, serves the
shortest estimated work first and preempts long jobs at snapshot boundaries, resuming them later with their
digests intact (decision core DC-09). Two kill gates guard it: KILL-SLOT (slot-level dispatch does not pay)
and KILL-PREEMPT (a resume costs several seconds or changes a digest, in which case running to completion is
the right policy). GATE-TETHER-10 measures waits against this table and a discrete-event simulation of the
dispatcher. This card is the single-server arithmetic behind the decision. Tier: Solid. Subsystems: Tether,
Atelier.

## Scope

In: `registries/cards/GB-39.toml`; test `gb_39`; controls. Out: size classes, shortest-estimated-work and

## Technical approach

**Equation.** One server, Poisson arrivals at rate λ, service time S, utilization ρ = λ·E[S]. First come,
first served (Pollaczek–Khinchine): W = λ·E[S²]/(2·(1 − ρ)). Non-preemptive priority for the short class:
W₁ = λ·E[S²]/(2·(1 − ρ₁)), ρ₁ the short class's own utilization. Preempt and resume: the short class sees only
itself, W₁ = λ₁·E[S₁²]/(2·(1 − ρ₁)), and long jobs slow by 1/(1 − ρ₁).

**Substituted (illustrative), with goldens.** One device at utilization 0.7; 90% of jobs take 10 s and 10%
take 30 min (1,800 s).

| Output | Golden | Printed | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| E[S] | 189 s | 189 s | exact |
| λ = 0.7/189 | 1/270 s⁻¹ | 1/270 s⁻¹ | exact |
| E[S²] = 0.9·100 + 0.1·1,800² | 324,090 s² | 324,090 s² | exact |
| ρ₁ = (0.9/270)·10 | 1/30 = 0.0333… | 0.033 | exact |
| Wait, first come first served | 324,090/(270·0.6) = 2,000.5556 s | about 2,000 s | exact |
| Wait, non-preemptive priority | 324,090/(270·2·(29/30)) = 620.8621 s | 621 s | exact |
| Wait, preempt and resume | (0.9/270)·100/(2·(29/30)) = 5/29 = 0.1724 s | 0.17 s | exact |
| Plus at most one quantum | 5 s | at most 5 s | exact |
| Slowdown of long jobs | 30/29 = 1.0345 | 3% | exact |
| First-come waits at utilization 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 | 857.38 s; 2,000.56 s; 3,429.52 s | 857; 2,001; 3,430 | rel 10⁻⁶ |

**Intuition.** The wait is the remainder of the training run already on the device, and only preemption
removes it. Interactive rungs are what an agent's turn waits on, so this is the queueing decision that matters.

**Assumptions.** Poisson arrivals, with Kingman's correction in the capacity table because swarms burst;
snapshot boundaries cheap enough for a 5 s quantum; a resumed job that keeps its digests.

**Flip.** A device reserved for interactive work, or a resume that costs several seconds or changes a digest
(KILL-PREEMPT), makes running to completion the right policy.

**Lane A.** Exact rationals for the table. A seeded discrete-event simulation of each discipline (10⁶ jobs,
exponential inter-arrivals at rate 1/270, the two-point service law) gives mean short-job waits within 3% of
2,000.6 s, 620.9 s and, for preempt-resume with zero quantum, 0.172 s.

**Lane B.** The DC-09 table in `registries/cores/` regenerates these three figures from its registered inputs
(`frob cores show DC-09 --json`, digest compared); the daemon's dispatcher, driven by the same seeded arrival
trace in its simulation harness, reproduces lane A's simulated waits run for run for each discipline; a
preempted and resumed `synthetic` job has a state digest identical to the uninterrupted run; a long job's
completion time grows by the factor 30/29 within the simulation's interval.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `gb_39_reference_table`: every row as a rational.
- `gb_39_reference_simulation`: three disciplines, seeded.
- `gb_39_mechanism_core_table_digest`, `gb_39_mechanism_dispatcher_trace`, `gb_39_mechanism_resume_digest`,
  `gb_39_mechanism_long_job_slowdown`.
- `gb_39_bursty_arrivals`: with a squared coefficient of variation of inter-arrivals of 4 the simulated
  first-come wait exceeds the Poisson figure, and the capacity table's Kingman-corrected value
  (ρ/(1 − ρ))·((c_a² + c_s²)/2)·E[S] brackets it within 15%.
- `gb_39_controls`: (a) an M/M/1 formula (exponential service with the same mean) gives
  ρ·E[S]/(1 − ρ) = 441 s and must miss 2,000 s (it ignores the heavy second moment); (b) priority without
  preemption reported as preempt-resume (621 s against 0.17 s); (c) a resume that re-seeds the job's random
  stream changes its digest and is caught.

### End-to-end script and logging

NEG-02 runs `frob cores show DC-09 --json` and compares the three waits and the table digest; logs the inputs,
each discipline's wait and the fallback condition.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-703 (DC-09 worked queue: waits of about 2,000 s, 621 s and 0.2 s); KA-758 (the M/G/1 table against a
discrete-event simulation of the dispatcher: E[S] = 189 s, E[S²] = 324,090 s²; first-come waits 857, 2,001 and
3,430 s at utilizations 0.5, 0.7 and 0.8). Determinism: rational arithmetic D0; simulations seed-explicit.

## Considerations

- A single device is the worst case; the fleet scheduler across two machines changes ρ, not the argument.
- The quantum bound assumes snapshots at chunk boundaries; a job with long chunks has a longer quantum and its
  estimate says so.
