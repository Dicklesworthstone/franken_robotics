## Background

`octopod` has eight legs of three joints each. It is the largest legged archetype by contact count and by
degrees of freedom apart from the humanoid, and it exists for two reasons. First, capacity: eight feet stress
the fixed contact and row capacities of batched tiers and the referee's contact limit, and its 30 generalized
velocities fall between size buckets, so it is the natural fixture for padding and bucket arithmetic. Second,
redundancy: with eight legs the body should still stand when any one leg fails, which the static stability
analysis must confirm leg by leg. It ships in two layouts, radial (legs at 45° around a hub, group D₈) and
spider (four legs in a row on each side, group C₂). No reference robot is on file. It lands at M3.

## Scope

In: `zoo/octopod.fbot` (the radial layout is the default instance), `variants/spider.fbot`, the expectation
file and controls. Out: the template (N14b-02); the referee contact step and its limits (BD-080); the bucket
planner (BD-171); A-08 (BD-100); the capacity audit (BD-168).

## Technical approach

**Morphology.** Radial: hub; `radial(8, z, leg)`. Spider: body; `mirror(xz, four legs in a row)`. Leg = a
three-revolute module; foot end effectors with law `foot-patch`. Actuated 24; floating base 6; n_v = 30;
bodies 25; end effectors 8.

**Symmetry.** Radial: Γ_mech = D₈ (order 16), generated by the rotation by 45° and one reflection. Spider:
Γ_mech = C₂ (the sagittal mirror). Default Γ_obs equals Γ_mech in each layout.

**What preflight must report (stages 1 to 6 at defaults; no refusal).** Stage 1: 25 fills, 8 foot laws, one
leg module used eight times. Stage 2: eight equal leg subtree masses. Stage 3: 24 revolute joints, no loops,
tree mobility 30. Stage 4: `passed` with the note `not-applicable` (legged, N14b-11). Stage 5: D₈ (radial) or C₂ (spider), assertion accepted. Stage 6:
positive support margin on eight feet, and a positive margin for each of the eight single-leg failures in the
radial layout (leg locked, foot lifted; the remaining seven feet's support polygon contains the centre of
mass).

**Known answers.**

| Name | Value | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Γ_mech radial; spider | D₈ (order 16); C₂ | exact |
| Bucket of n_v = 30 in the default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} | 36 | exact |
| Padding overhead of one octopod in the 36 bucket, per power p = 1, 2, 3 | 36/30 = 1.2; 1.44; 1.728 | exact |
| Contact strata, eight feet × {open, stick, slip} under D₈ | (3⁸ + 4·3 + 2·3² + 3⁴ + 4·3⁵ + 4·3⁴)/16 = 7,968/16 = 498 | count |
| Ten leg types on eight slots under D₈ | (10⁸ + 4·10 + 2·10² + 10⁴ + 4·10⁵ + 4·10⁴)/16 = 6,278,140 | count |
| Contacts with one sample per foot | 8, inside the referee's limit of 21 (FACT: the sibling contact library sets its maximum contact count to 21) | exact |
| Contacts with three samples per foot | 24 > 21: the referee refuses with `FROB-REF-721` (referee capacity, exit 5; `observed` 24, `required` 21; BD-080's static check at compile, or its run-time check), never a truncated solve | exact (verdict) |
| One leg out, radial layout | eight cases, each with a positive static margin | exact (sign) |
| Batched capacities | the 36 bucket declares its own contact capacity C and row capacity R in the capacities table; 8 single-sample contacts need C ≥ 8 and 8 × 4 = 32 cone rows besides joint rows, and `sim.estimate` names the declared values it used | exact (table lookup) |

**Control variants.** (a) a build that drops contacts to fit the capacity (returns a solve with 21 of 24
contacts): the test asserts a refusal instead; (b) `variants/spider.fbot` asserting `D8` → FROB-REF-109;
(c) a single-leg-out case evaluated with the failed foot still counted in the support polygon: margin
overstated, caught by comparing with the seven-foot polygon.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The zoo instance (radial) and the spider variant each equal the corresponding template instance;
      identities pinned and platform-independent.
- [ ] Symmetry, preflight and known-answer rows pass in both layouts; eight of eight single-leg failures have
      a positive margin.
- [ ] One sample per foot stands on the referee's contact solve; three samples per foot is refused with
      `FROB-REF-721` (exit 5).

## Test plan

### Unit tests

- `zoo_octopod` (generated): identity, morphology, symmetry, stages, known answers, controls.
- `zoo_octopod_strata_bruteforce`: canonicalizing the 6,561 foot-state tuples under the 16 elements gives 498.
- `zoo_octopod_bucket_36` and `zoo_octopod_padding_overheads`.
- `zoo_octopod_capacity_8_fits_24_refused`.
- `zoo_octopod_one_leg_out_eight_cases`.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/zoo_goldens.sh` (NEG-31) at M3: both layouts through symmetry and stages 1 to 6; `frob phylum
plan --designs <DesignId> --worlds-per-design 1 --json` on the default instance for the bucket row; `frob sim run
<three-samples variant> --tier referee --seconds 1 --key zoo-octopod-cap-<run> --json` expecting `FROB-REF-721`,
exit 5; log lines carry the contact count, the limit and the refusal code.

### Known answers and negative controls

KA-270 (Γ_mech: D₈ radial, C₂ in a spider layout). Padding rows follow Ω_p = L_maxᵖ/E[Lᵖ] for one size.
Controls as listed. Determinism: integer counts and pinned identities; the standing row within the referee's
class.

## Considerations

- The three-samples refusal depends on an upstream limit in the sibling contact library (a sustained solve
  above 21 contacts is an upstream work item). When it lands the case becomes a pass and the expectation is
  updated by a dated amendment, not silently.
- A capacity-biased comparison is refused: an octopod against a biped under a contact cap that only the
  octopod reaches returns `capacity-biased` and no winner.
