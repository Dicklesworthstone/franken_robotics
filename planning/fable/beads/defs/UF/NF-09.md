## Background

A gear, a belt or a linkage is a transformer in the port-Hamiltonian sense: it joins two ports and conserves
power. A lossless transformer conserves power under **either sign** of its modulus, so a power balance cannot
detect a reversed gear. FrankenSim's `fs-phs` has two entry points for it, and they disagree in sign. FACT
(`frankensim/crates/fs-phs/src/interconnection.rs:91` at `483d09c682f2`, read 2026-11-21): the legacy
`transformer` carries the comment "The legacy transformer has the opposite sign to the canonical map", the
canonical map being `interconnect_mapped`. FACT (reported, not re-run): with two unit scalar stores, efforts
(2, 3) and coefficient 4, one gives rates (12, −8) and the other (−12, 8); both satisfy 2·12 + 3·(−8) = 0, and
a coefficient of −4 in one convention reproduces the other.

A convention hazard, then, not a defect: each entry point is self-consistent. The plan lists it among six
later foundation facts whose upstream work items are "added to Appendix F by description": a documented or
removed legacy transformer. It has no `FD-` number and no register priority; this bead proposes P3.

Rejected: relying on the energy check to catch a wrong direction (it cannot); reaching both entry points and
choosing per call site.

## Scope

In: the hazard report; in `fs-phs`, the legacy entry point documented at its definition and deprecated, or
removed, as FrankenSim decides; here, the witness re-run. Out: transmissions and their direction on this side
(BD-074); the port energy-balance check (BD-076); the `Component` contract (FD-FSIM-08).

## Technical approach

**Where the work is done, and under whose rules (binding).** The change is made in the `frankensim` repository
(`../frankensim` beside this one; head `483d09c682f2` and toolchain channel `nightly-2026-07-06` when this was written
on 2026-11-21, both FACT to re-read), by an agent of this program working there under that repository's own
`AGENTS.md`, gates and conventions. For FrankenSim those rules include its layer checks, its contracts, its unsafe-capsule discipline, its ban on
in-process foreign kernels in production paths and its ban on branches and worktrees (FACT:
`frankensim/AGENTS.md`, `frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`), so the work is committed on `main` in small
steps that each pass its gates. FrankenSim moves fast (FACT: 409 commits touched `crates/fs-mbd` in the thirty
days to 2026-11-21, and its checkout had 32 modified paths), so every file named in this bead is re-read at the
current head before it is edited.

1. Read that repository's `AGENTS.md` and its coordination rules first. They govern; where they differ from this
   bead, they win, and the difference is written into this bead's closing note.
2. Other agents work in the same repository. Before editing, reserve every file to be touched through Agent
   Mail and follow the repository's own coordination rules; release the reservations once the change has landed.
3. The change is additive (DESIGN): a new mode, entry point, type or feature. Existing behaviour is not changed,

4. It lands on that repository's `main` through its own tests and gates. Nothing in this repository patches,

5. The work is tracked twice: this bead, and a mirrored bead in the sibling's tracker whose identifier is stored

   `status` moves `open` → `landed` (merged on the sibling's `main`) → `adopted` (switch-over run here).

**The report.** `tests/upstream/NF-09/REPORT.md` (also the body of the mirrored bead): the evidence above; the
two-store case with both outcomes; which entry point gave which sign at the pin (`observed_at`, from the
admission probe); the request.

**The change (DESIGN), FrankenSim's choice.**

1. *Document and deprecate.* The legacy `transformer` gets a doc comment at its definition stating its sign
   convention in terms of a driving and a driven port ("a positive effort on port A produces a rate of sign s
   on port B"), the relation to `interconnect_mapped` (the same map with the modulus negated), and a
   deprecation notice pointing to the canonical entry point. Additive: no behaviour changes.
2. *Remove,* if FrankenSim's own search finds no remaining caller: then the removal follows its rules for
   removing public items, and this project, which never calls it, is unaffected.

In either form a test in `fs-phs` pins the two conventions against the two-store case, so that the relation
between them cannot drift.

**Interim posture here (the guard).** Only `interconnect_mapped` is reached, through the airlock, with the
authored sign passed explicitly. Every transformer, gear and coupling of a design joins two labelled ports
with an authored direction: positive rotation of the driving port corresponds to a stated sign of rotation at
the driven port. `actuator.check` runs a **signed motion witness** when a card is bound to a joint: a unit
positive effort on the driving port, with the driven port free and at rest, must produce a rate of the
authored sign at the driven port. A reversed witness is refused under the port-sign refusal of the design
language.

**Fixture (DESIGN).** `tests/upstream/NF-09/`, in the same form as the registered items' fixtures
(`upstream_nf_09`, `#[ignore]`d, with `expected.toml`): it checks that the legacy entry point at the pinned
commit is either absent or carries a deprecation attribute and a documented sign convention; at the pin it is
present with a comment only. Registry: this bead adds the item's row to `registries/upstream.toml` with
`bead = "NF-09"`.

**Switch-over (the compatibility train).** When the change is on the sibling's `main`: (1) move the sibling's pin
in `franken-stack.lock` to a clean commit that contains it (a pin only moves forward; a sibling's newer clean head
is never rolled back locally); (2) run `cargo xtask check-stack`, which writes a new resolution receipt
(`frob.stack-receipt/1`) under `receipts/`; (3) re-run that sibling's admission gates, the parity gates and the
gates this item names; (4) relock (`Cargo.lock` regenerated and committed, builds `--locked` on the one toolchain
T) and append the record, with the receipt hash, to `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`. Only then does the row's `status`
become `adopted` and the interim posture end. No PASS is recorded on a dirty sibling tree, and neither a pin nor
T moves while a ten-fold tournament is running. If a gate named here does not exist yet when the change lands,
the pin still moves with the gates that do exist, the row stays `landed`, and the gate's first run is made
against the new pin.

**What the switch-over re-runs (DESIGN; the plan states no rule for this item).** Row 11 of GATE-WS-09: the
signed motion witness through the airlock for both transformer entry points (after a removal, for the one that
remains, with the other recorded as removed). And GATE-SINEW-10's transmission known answers, which exercise

τ_motorᵀ·θ̇ to 10⁻¹²). Nothing on this side changes its code: the airlock already reaches only the canonical
map.

**Fallback if the change cannot land.** The guard stays; the witness is re-run by every compatibility train.

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In FrankenSim (added, in `fs-phs`):
- `two_store_case_pins_both_conventions`: unit stores, efforts (2, 3), coefficient 4; the canonical map and the legacy one give rates that are negatives of each other, one of them (12, −8); each satisfies 2·r₁ + 3·r₂ = 0 exactly.
- `negated_modulus_reproduces_the_other`: the legacy entry point with coefficient −4 equals the canonical one with +4.
- `legacy_is_deprecated_or_absent`: a compile-time check.
Here:
- `signed_motion_witness_correct_gear`: a gear authored "driven turns with the driver", ratio 4, driven port free and at rest; a unit positive effort on the driver produces a positive rate on the driven port.
- `signed_motion_witness_reversed_gear_is_refused`: the same card bound with the opposite sign; expected the port-sign refusal.
- `power_balance_cannot_see_the_sign`: both the correct and the reversed binding close the power balance to 10⁻¹² (the test that shows why the witness exists).
- `airlock_does_not_reference_legacy_transformer`: a source scan.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/upstream_nf_09.sh --phase pre|post`: both phases run `frob actuator check --json` on a design
with one correctly authored and one deliberately reversed gear and assert one pass and one refusal; phase `pre`
asserts the fixture's failure signature, phase `post` its pass and the recorded probe.

Logging per STD-E2E: the script sources the shared helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/upstream_nf_09/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`NF-09`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact
command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed). It also records the sibling's pinned commit, the stack-receipt hash, the phase, each measured
value with its unit and threshold, digests where state is compared and, on failure, the first differing value
and the artifact paths needed to reproduce. A final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and the
gates touched. No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: rates (12, −8) and (−12, 8) for efforts (2, 3) and coefficient 4; 2·12 + 3·(−8) = 0.
- Negative controls: the reversed gear must be refused by the witness; a witness that only checks power must be shown to accept it (and is therefore not the witness).
- Determinism lanes: exact small-integer arithmetic in the two-store case; identical everywhere.

## Considerations

- A reversed transmission passes every energy audit, runs, and walks backwards; the only defence is a directional check at binding time, which is why the guard does not depend on the upstream change.
- Constrained interconnections are deferred upstream, so closed-loop mechanisms do not rely on `fs-phs`'s interconnection at all; this item concerns the simple two-port map.
- No milestone waits on this bead. Its milestone label says when its switch-over gate first exists, not that the
  milestone's exit depends on it. Until the switch-over has been run, the interim posture above is the design of
  record. M1 depends only on FrankenSim, asupersync and FrankenSQLite, and no upstream work item is on its
  critical path.
- If this change never lands, the program still ships on the interim posture, at the cost stated there. A change
  that does land is adopted only through its switch-over rule: never by a local patch, a fork or a vendored copy,
  and never by rolling a sibling's newer clean head back. Neither a pin nor T moves during the ten-fold
  tournament of M7.
- The priority in the upstream register states the item's value to this project and its place in this program's
  queue. The risks carried at program level are the cost and ordering of upstream work and collisions with other
  agents in the same sibling; the mitigations are file reservation, small commits and the sibling's own gates.
