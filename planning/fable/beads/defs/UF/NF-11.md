## Background

`fs-sos` is FrankenSim's sum-of-squares crate: polynomial models, sum-of-squares programs, bounds of a
polynomial on an interval. Parts of this project's design name it as an owner worth probing before new code is
written (for mission certificates, and for a region-of-attraction routine). A bound that enters a certificate
must be a bound. Two cases were read in its source in which a returned value is not one. FACT
(`frankensim/crates/fs-sos/src/lib.rs:80–84` and `:289–308` at `483d09c682f2`, read 2026-11-21; both
mechanisms confirmed by reading; the two returned values are reported and were not re-run):

1. *Trimmed coefficients.* `Poly::new` trims trailing coefficients below 10⁻¹⁵ in magnitude. So 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·x
   (2⁻⁵⁰ ≈ 8.9·10⁻¹⁶) becomes the constant 1, and is certified as 1 on |x| ≤ 2²⁸, where the true minimum is
   1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·2²⁸ = 1 − 2⁻²².
2. *A last step rounded to nearest.* `certified_bound_on` forms its result by one subtraction rounded to
   nearest, and returns 1 for 1 − x/2 on |x| ≤ 2⁻⁵⁵, where the true minimum is 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶ (which rounds to 1 in
   double precision; a lower bound must round down).

The plan counts these among six later foundation facts (limits or defects) whose upstream work items are
"added to Appendix F by description": an authored-coefficient check and an outward-rounded result in `fs-sos`.
It has no `FD-` number and no register priority; this bead proposes P3, because no certificate of this project
depends on an `fs-sos` value.

Rejected: accepting such values with a safety margin (a margin chosen without a bound on the error is another
guess); ignoring the cases as too small to matter (a certificate is exact or it is not one).

## Scope

In: the defect report; in `fs-sos`, both mechanisms corrected or made to report their error; here, the two
probes re-run. Out: this project's exact checkers (`sos-gram`, `interval-box` and the others, BD-026, BD-132);
certificate families in FrankenSymPy (FD-FSYM-04); the admission of `fs-sos` as an airlock entry (BD-127).

## Technical approach

**Where the work is done, and under whose rules (binding).** The change is made in the `frankensim` repository
(`../frankensim` beside this one; head `483d09c682f2` and toolchain channel `nightly-2026-07-06` when this was written
on 2026-11-21, both FACT to re-read), by an agent of this program working there under that repository's own
`AGENTS.md`, gates and conventions. For FrankenSim those rules include its layer checks, its contracts, its unsafe-capsule discipline, its ban on
in-process foreign kernels in production paths and its ban on branches and worktrees (FACT:
`frankensim/AGENTS.md`, `frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`), so the work is committed on `main` in small
steps that each pass its gates. FrankenSim moves fast (FACT: 409 commits touched `crates/fs-mbd` in the thirty
days to 2026-11-21, and its checkout had 32 modified paths), so every file named in this bead is re-read at the
current head before it is edited.

1. Read that repository's `AGENTS.md` and its coordination rules first. They govern; where they differ from this
   bead, they win, and the difference is written into this bead's closing note.
2. Other agents work in the same repository. Before editing, reserve every file to be touched through Agent
   Mail and follow the repository's own coordination rules; release the reservations once the change has landed.
3. The change is additive (DESIGN): a new mode, entry point, type or feature. Existing behaviour is not changed,

4. It lands on that repository's `main` through its own tests and gates. Nothing in this repository patches,

5. The work is tracked twice: this bead, and a mirrored bead in the sibling's tracker whose identifier is stored

   `status` moves `open` → `landed` (merged on the sibling's `main`) → `adopted` (switch-over run here).

**The report.** `tests/upstream/NF-11/REPORT.md` (also the body of the mirrored bead): the evidence above with
file, lines and commit; the two cases with exact rational values; `observed_at` with what the admission probes
recorded at the pin.

**The change (DESIGN).**

1. *Authored coefficients.* `Poly::new` keeps the coefficients it is given. If trimming is wanted for
   conditioning, it is a separate, explicit operation that returns the trimmed polynomial **and** a bound on
   the change it caused on a stated domain (for a dropped term c·xᵏ on |x| ≤ X, at most |c|·Xᵏ); a certified
   bound computed from a trimmed polynomial subtracts that amount. Additive form: a non-trimming constructor
   beside the present one, the present one documented as lossy, with its loss bound available.
2. *Outward rounding.* `certified_bound_on` computes a lower bound with its final operations rounded toward
   −∞ and an upper bound with them rounded toward +∞ (directed rounding, or the next representable number in
   the safe direction after a round-to-nearest result whose error is at most half a unit in the last place),
   or returns an interval from FrankenSim's own outward-rounded interval type.
3. Tests for both, against exact rational evaluation.

**Interim posture here (the guard).** No `fs-sos` value enters a certificate. The `sos-gram` proof kind is
checked in exact rationals by this project's auditor. A bound from any floating-point routine that is used as
evidence obeys two rules: the bound is evaluated on the authored coefficients, or the error of translating
them is bounded on the actual domain; and the last operation is rounded outward. A value from `fs-sos` is a
proposal to check, never a result.

**Fixture (DESIGN).** `tests/upstream/NF-11/`, in the same form as the registered items' fixtures
(`upstream_nf_11`, `#[ignore]`d, with `expected.toml`): the two probes through the airlock, each compared with
exact rational evaluation of the authored polynomial at the interval's ends; each must return a value no
greater than the true minimum. At the pin both return 1. Registry: this bead adds the item's row to
`registries/upstream.toml` with `bead = "NF-11"`.

**Switch-over (the compatibility train).** When the change is on the sibling's `main`: (1) move the sibling's pin
in `franken-stack.lock` to a clean commit that contains it (a pin only moves forward; a sibling's newer clean head
is never rolled back locally); (2) run `cargo xtask check-stack`, which writes a new resolution receipt
(`frob.stack-receipt/1`) under `receipts/`; (3) re-run that sibling's admission gates, the parity gates and the
gates this item names; (4) relock (`Cargo.lock` regenerated and committed, builds `--locked` on the one toolchain
T) and append the record, with the receipt hash, to `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`. Only then does the row's `status`
become `adopted` and the interim posture end. No PASS is recorded on a dirty sibling tree, and neither a pin nor

the pin still moves with the gates that do exist, the row stays `landed`, and the gate's first run is made

**What the switch-over re-runs (DESIGN; the plan states no rule for this item).** Row 13 of GATE-WS-09: the two

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In FrankenSim (added, in `fs-sos`):
- `tiny_coefficient_is_kept`: p(x) = 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰·x on |x| ≤ 2²⁸; the certified lower bound is at most 1 − 2⁻²² = 0.99999976158…; the present code returns 1.
- `last_step_rounds_down`: p(x) = 1 − x/2 on |x| ≤ 2⁻⁵⁵; the certified lower bound is strictly less than 1 (the largest double below 1 is 1 − 2⁻⁵³, which is a valid lower bound of 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶); the present code returns 1.
- `trim_reports_its_loss`: trimming c·x with c = 2⁻⁵⁰ on |x| ≤ 2²⁸ reports a loss bound of 2⁻²².
- `bounds_bracket_exact_minimum`: 10⁴ random polynomials of degree at most 4 with rational coefficients on rational intervals; lower ≤ exact minimum ≤ upper, the exact minimum computed in rationals at critical points and endpoints.
Here:
- `probe_trim` and `probe_rounding`: the two probes through the airlock against exact evaluation.
- `fs_sos_value_is_a_proposal`: a mission certificate path that receives an `fs-sos` bound re-derives it with the exact checker before use, and a bundle built without that step fails `frob-verify`.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/upstream_nf_11.sh --phase pre|post`: runs the two probes, prints each returned value beside the
exact rational minimum (as numerator and denominator and as a decimal), asserts the recorded outcome for the
phase, and runs `frob prove verify --json` on a bundle whose bound was proposed by `fs-sos` and confirmed
exactly.

Logging per STD-E2E: the script sources the shared helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/upstream_nf_11/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`NF-11`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact
command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed). It also records the sibling's pinned commit, the stack-receipt hash, the phase, each measured
value with its unit and threshold, digests where state is compared and, on failure, the first differing value
and the artifact paths needed to reproduce. A final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and the
gates touched. No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: 2⁻⁵⁰·2²⁸ = 2⁻²², so the first minimum is 1 − 2⁻²²; (2⁻⁵⁵)/2 = 2⁻⁵⁶, so the second is 1 − 2⁻⁵⁶, which is not representable and lies above 1 − 2⁻⁵³.
- Negative controls: a "fix" that only lowers the trimming threshold must fail on 1 − 2⁻⁶⁰·x on |x| ≤ 2⁴⁰ (minimum 1 − 2⁻²⁰); a "fix" that subtracts a fixed epsilon such as 10⁻⁹ from every lower bound must be rejected by a tightness check on exactly representable cases (the constant polynomial 1 must return a lower bound within 2 units in the last place of 1).
- Determinism lanes: directed rounding must give the same bits on both platforms; the test runs on each.

  agents in the same sibling; the mitigations are file reservation, small commits and the sibling's own gates.
