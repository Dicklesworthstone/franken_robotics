## Background

Scenes in FrankenRobotics are built from instances: one piece of geometry placed many times, each with its own
transform, which may include scale. A ray cast against a scene is transformed into each instance's local frame,
intersected there, and the hits are compared to find the nearest. The comparison is only meaningful if every
hit parameter is in world metres.

FACT (`franken_threed/crates/f3d-math/src/ray.rs:487–490` and `vector3.rs:708–719` at `bf7abe16367e`, read
2026-11-21): `Ray::apply_matrix4` transforms its direction with `Vector3::transform_direction`, which
**normalizes the result**, as the scene-graph method it mirrors does. So after the transform the ray's
parameter is measured in the local frame's units, and under a scaled instance a hit parameter is not a world
distance. This is a convention hazard, not a defect: the function does what the library it mirrors does.

The hazard is silent and has two faces. Depths are wrong by the instance's scale; and, worse, hits from
instances with different scales are sorted in the wrong order. The plan lists this among six later foundation
facts whose upstream work items are "added to Appendix F by description": a ray transform that returns the
scale of its parameter. It has no `FD-` number and no register priority; this bead proposes P3.

Rejected: forbidding scaled instances (retrofit kits and asset reuse need them); correcting depths after
sorting (the order is already wrong).

## Scope

In: the hazard report; in `f3d-math`, a ray transform that also returns the factor by which the parameter was
rescaled; here, the two-plane fixture re-run through it. Out: the scaled-instance rules, distances and normals
in world metres on this side (the Habitat chapter's beads); ray kernels on devices (BD-187); the viewer's
picking (BD-191); the `serde_json` pin of the same sibling (FD-F3D-01).

**The arithmetic.** A world ray is p(s) = o + s·d with ‖d‖ = 1. Let A map an instance's local coordinates to
world coordinates. In local coordinates the same points are A⁻¹·o + s·A⁻¹·d. Renormalizing the direction gives
the local ray o_L + s_L·d_L with d_L = A⁻¹·d/‖A⁻¹·d‖, so s_L = s·‖A⁻¹·d‖, and the world parameter of a local
hit is

  s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖.

**The change (DESIGN).** Beside `apply_matrix4`, a method that returns the transformed ray **and** the norm of
the transformed direction before normalization (for the matrix M it is given, ‖M·d‖), with documentation of the
rule above; or a variant that does not renormalize, whose parameter is then preserved. The present method is
unchanged, and its documentation says that it changes the parameter's unit under scale. Additive.

**Interim posture here (the guard).** The adapter over `f3d-math` converts every local hit parameter by
s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖ before any comparison, sort or range gate; no local parameter leaves the adapter.

**Fixture (DESIGN).** `tests/upstream/NF-12/`, in the same form as the registered items' fixtures
(`upstream_nf_12`, `#[ignore]`d, with `expected.toml`): it asks the sibling's ray transform for the parameter

adds the item's row to `registries/upstream.toml` with `bead = "NF-12"`. The sibling is consumed as a pattern

## Test plan

### Unit tests
In franken_threed (added, in `f3d-math`):
- `uniform_scale_two`: an instance twice as large (A scales by 2); a world ray along +x; ‖A⁻¹·d‖ = 0.5; a local hit at s_L = 1.5 is at s_W = 3.
- `anisotropic_scale`: A = diag(2, 0.5, 1) and d = (0, 1, 0); ‖A⁻¹·d‖ = 2; s_W = s_L/2.
- `identity_returns_scale_one`.
- `returned_scale_equals_direction_norm`: 10³ random affine matrices and directions; the returned scale equals ‖M·d‖ to 10⁻¹².
Here:
- `two_planes_foreground_first`: an opaque foreground plane at 2 m in an instance of scale 1/2 reports a local parameter of 4; a background plane at 3 m in an instance of scale 3 reports 1. Converted: 4/2 = 2 m and 1/(1/3) = 3 m; the nearest hit is the foreground at 2 m.
- `sorting_local_parameters_is_wrong`: the mutant returns the background (local 1 < local 4) and must fail.
- `range_gate_in_world_metres`: a gate of 2.5 m keeps the foreground and discards the background; applied to local values it would do the reverse, and the mutant must fail.
- `adapter_scale_matches_sibling`: the differential test.

### End-to-end script and logging
`scripts/e2e/upstream_nf_12.sh --phase pre|post`: both phases compile the two-plane scene with
`frob scene compile --json`, cast the ray through the compiled pack and through the viewer's pick path
(`frob telemetry inspect-pixel --json` on a rendered still), and assert a hit at 2 m on the foreground; phase
`post` adds the differential table.

Logging per STD-E2E: the script sources the shared helper and writes NDJSON to
`target/e2e-logs/upstream_nf_12/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` with a human-readable transcript beside it. Every line has `ts`,
`script`, `bead` (`NF-12`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`, `metric`, `refusal`, `end`), the exact
command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's `evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism,
identities, seed). It also records the sibling's pinned commit, the stack-receipt hash, the phase, each measured
value with its unit and threshold, digests where state is compared and, on failure, the first differing value
and the artifact paths needed to reproduce. A final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed and the
gates touched. No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls
- Known answers: s_W = s_L/‖A⁻¹·d‖; the two planes at 2 m and 3 m from local parameters 4 and 1.
- Negative controls: the sort-local and gate-local mutants; a conversion that multiplies by ‖A⁻¹·d‖ instead of dividing gives 8 m and 1/3 m and must fail.
- Companion case from the same rule set (distances, not rays): a unit sphere scaled by (2, 0.5, 1): at the world point (0, 0.6, 0) the local distance is 0.20 and the clearance is 0.10 m; read as metres, the local value admits a 0.15 m probe that intersects.
- Determinism lanes: exact for the fixture's rational values; identical on both platforms.

## Considerations

- The hazard is inherited from a widely used scene-graph convention, so any renderer-derived mathematics library will have it; the guard belongs at this project's boundary whatever the sibling does.
- Depth sensors, lidar and height scans all read ray parameters; an error here is a sensing error with no physical cause, which is why the gate tests the compiled pack, the viewer and the adapter alike.
- No milestone waits on this bead. Its milestone label says when its switch-over gate first exists, not that the
  milestone's exit depends on it. Until the switch-over has been run, the interim posture above is the design of
  record. M1 depends only on FrankenSim, asupersync and FrankenSQLite, and no upstream work item is on its
  critical path.
- If this change never lands, the program still ships on the interim posture, at the cost stated there. A change
  that does land is adopted only through its switch-over rule: never by a local patch, a fork or a vendored copy,
  and never by rolling a sibling's newer clean head back. Neither a pin nor T moves during the ten-fold
  tournament of M7.
- The priority in the upstream register states the item's value to this project and its place in this program's
  queue. The risks carried at program level are the cost and ordering of upstream work and collisions with other
  agents in the same sibling; the mitigations are file reservation, small commits and the sibling's own gates.
