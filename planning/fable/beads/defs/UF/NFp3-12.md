## Background

The referee tier is FrankenSim's contact solve, consumed through the airlock crate `frob-fsim`. FrankenSim's
sustained contact solver admits at most 21 contacts in one solve, and a single robot is one island, because
every foot couples through the floating body. This project never truncates a contact set: a set above the
capacity is a refusal by name (`FROB-REF-721`, exit 5), and the only other responses are a documented patch
reduction with its qualification fixture, or a larger admitted solve. Upstream work item FD-FSIM-13 changes the
limit in FrankenSim in one of three forms: (i) the capacity as a parameter of the solve, with a stated tested
maximum; (ii) an island-wise solve; (iii) a documented patch model with a qualification fixture. The plan's
switch-over rule for that item is one sentence: "re-run the capacity fixtures of Traction".

Those fixtures are of two kinds. The ones that need only the referee (the refusal row of INT-FSIM-04 at the new
limit; a 22-contact set that now solves) are re-run by FD-FSIM-13 itself. The others are M2 capabilities of this
project, each owned by its own bead: the static capacity audit (`contact.capacity`, N18b-20), the qualified
patch reduction (N18b-21), the capacity-bias rule of GATE-TRAC-06 (N18b-22: when the arms of a comparison differ
by more than one percentage point of worlds in capacity-refused episodes, the verdict is labelled
`capacity-biased`, the envelope prints the overflow of each arm and no winner is named), the tunnelling audit
(`contact.audit`, N18b-23), their end-to-end script (N18b-24), and the referee's own coverage per robot class
(GATE-FID-03, BD-213: the share of standard-battery scenarios the referee completes, with refusals broken down
by the causes `contact-cap`, `fixed-witness-gate`, `missing-joint-class` and `kinematic-loop`; below 90% in a
reference class BET-34 is falsified for that class and the `strict` profile is the named adjudicator).

This bead is that second half. It is separate from FD-FSIM-13 for an order-of-work reason, the one that separates
NFp2-01 from FD-FSIM-02: the upstream change is priority P1 and can start as soon as the referee step exists
(M1), while these checks exist only at M2. A bead never waits on what waits on it, so the step that needs both
comes after both.

One thing changes with the capacity and must be said once: several of those M2 fixtures assert the number 21.
A humanoid's `prone` class of 40 contacts "does not fit"; a crawl on the referee "refuses with `FROB-REF-721`".
After form (i) those statements are false by design at the new pin. Rejected: leaving the old expectations and
pinning the airlock at 21 to keep them green (the item would be adopted in name only); deleting the assertions
(the refusal one above the capacity must still be shown). DESIGN: every expectation becomes the rule it always
was, "a class fits if and only if its static maximum is at most the referee capacity", with the capacity read
from the verb.

## Scope

In: the precondition check; the capacity expectations of the Traction scripts restated against the capacity
read; the capacity audit and the patch-reduction fixture at the new pin; the capacity-bias fixture and the row of
GATE-TRAC-06 it belongs to; the re-issue of the coverage table and the reading of GATE-FID-03; under form (iii),
the registered patch reduction for the upstream patch model with its bound; the move of FD-FSIM-13's row to
`adopted`; the script.
Out: the upstream change, the pin move, INT-FSIM-04 at the new limit and the 22-contact run (FD-FSIM-13); the
capacity record of the airlock and its `doctor` section (N33a-14); the verbs and rules themselves (N18b-20,
N18b-21, N18b-22, N18b-23) and their capability script (N18b-24); the coverage campaign, its table and the verb
`fidelity.coverage` (BD-213); the fixed-witness side of referee coverage and the fidelity ledger's re-baseline
(NFp3-11, for FD-FSIM-12; a change of capacity changes which scenarios the referee completes and not what it
computes on the ones it completed before, so this item has no re-baseline); the lockstep tiers' own capacities,
which nothing here changes; the decision on BET-34 (its own bead, which reads the table this bead re-issues).

## Technical approach

1. **Preconditions.** The row of FD-FSIM-13 in `registries/upstream.toml` reads `landed` with a stack receipt
   newer than the pin move; FD-FSIM-13's referee-side record is on file (INT-FSIM-04 at the new limit, the
   22-contact run committed, the form that landed, the capacity C read at the new pin and, under form (iii), the
   qualification bound). If any is missing the script stops and records nothing. If only form (ii) landed, C is
   still 21 for one island: this bead then runs steps 2 to 6 as a regression at the new pin, records that the
   single-robot limit stands, and leaves the row `landed` (FD-FSIM-13 says why).
2. **The capacity, read three ways.** `frob doctor --section referee --json` (the airlock's capacity record,
   N33a-14), `frob contact capacity <design> --json` (`referee_revision` and `referee_cap`, read by N18b-20 from
   the admission record of the pinned revision) and the capacity carried by a `FROB-REF-721` refusal must be the
   same number C, at the revision of the new pin. A disagreement stops the script: one of the three still holds
   the old constant.
3. **Expectations follow the capacity (DESIGN).** In the scripts `contact_capacity.sh` (N18b-20),
   `patch_reduction.sh` (N18b-21) and `contact_capacity_audit.sh` (N18b-24), and in the unit tests behind them,
   each assertion that names the cap of 21 is restated as the rule: with m the static maximum of a scenario class
   (the hand values stay: quadruped `stance` 16 and `prone` 24; humanoid `stance` 16 and `prone` 40; the tracked
   robot's belt class above 21), `fits` is m ≤ C, `contact.capacity` exits 0 only when every reported class fits
   and 2 otherwise, and a referee run of a scenario whose maximum is above C answers `FROB-REF-721` while one at
   or below C completes. Worked at C = 64 (illustrative; the real C is the sibling's tested maximum that the
   airlock opts in to): quadruped `prone` 24 and humanoid `prone` 40 now fit, so both `contact.capacity` calls
   exit 0 where they exited 2, and the humanoid crawl completes on the referee without a patch reduction. At
   C = 32: `prone` 24 fits, `prone` 40 does not, and the crawl still refuses with 721 and still completes under
   `--patch-reduction sole-4`. N18b-20, N18b-21 and N18b-24 state their expectations as this rule on the
   `referee_cap` they read, so this step is a check (`no_literal_cap_in_capacity_scripts`); an assertion found
   still naming the literal is restated in the same commit as the relock, under the key of its bead in the commit
   message.
4. **Capacity audit and the refusal one above C.** `scripts/e2e/contact_capacity_audit.sh` (N18b-24) is run
   whole, as a stage, at the new pin. In addition, on the tracked reference design: `frob contact capacity
   tracked-flipper --json` (per scenario class the maximum, `fits` against C, headroom, and the reductions
   offered) and, for the lockstep run of that design made by the stage's forced-overflow step, `frob contact
   audit <run> --p 1 --detector referee --key fd13-audit-<run-id> --json`: the audit's detector is the referee,
   and a window whose contact set is above the referee's capacity falls to `strict`, so the share of windows that
   fell to `strict` must not rise against the old pin (it falls only where C reaches the window's contact count;
   both shares are recorded, and the lockstep run's own overflow attributions, which belong to the lockstep
   capacity, must be unchanged). That a set one above
   C still refuses, never truncated, is asserted on the first class whose maximum exceeds C; if every class of
   every reference design fits, it is asserted on FD-FSIM-13's over-capacity test and the script says so.
5. **Capacity bias (the row of GATE-TRAC-06).** `scripts/e2e/capacity_bias.sh` (N18b-22) is run as a stage: the
   fixture `fixture:capacity-bias` (tracked against point-foot, 256 worlds per arm, a cap of 8 in its own step
   plan, seed 1337) must still return the condition `capacity-biased`, `verdict: null` and both arms' overflow
   shares, a verb asked to cite it must still answer `FROB-REF-822`, and at the default capacity the same
   comparison must still return a verdict and no label. The fixture's cap is a lockstep capacity of its own step
   plan, so a larger referee capacity must not change any of this; that is what the re-run shows. The gate's
   rows are then re-evaluated in the Gauntlet's form, `FROB_TEST_SEED=<seed> cargo test -p frob-conformance
   --test g1_known_answers -- GATE-TRAC-06`, and the receipt is recorded against the new pin by a session that
   did not implement the work, in the form BD-168 uses for the gate's M2 part (`cargo run -p frob-conformance
   --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-TRAC-06 --receipt <hash> --part M2`). The script writes no gate record.
6. **Re-issue the coverage table.** The table `referee_class_coverage` is keyed by (class, battery identity,
   stack-lock digest, transaction numerics identity), so the new pin has no row. GATE-FID-03's Gauntlet entry is
   run at the new pin, as BD-213 defines it: `FROB_TEST_SEED=17 cargo test -p frob-conformance --test g2_parity
   -- GATE-FID-03` (with `--features gauntlet-mutations` when its controls run); it submits one campaign job per
   class (`frob eval run --battery <battery> --class <class> --tier referee --adjudicate strict-fallback --record
   coverage --key <k> --json`), reads the rows back through `fidelity.coverage`, checks the partition identity
   (completed + refused by the four causes + numerical faults = scenarios) and the adjudicator rule, and writes a
   hash-named receipt. This bead then compares, per reference class, the new row with the row at the old pin:
   - the count refused with cause `contact-cap` must not rise (a rise is a finding that blocks adoption);
   - it is expected to fall for tracked and many-sample robots (HYPOTHESIS; the measurement is the test), and a
     class in which it does not fall is named with the scenarios still refused and their contact counts against C;
   - a scenario that moved from `contact-cap` to another cause (`fixed-witness-gate` most likely, once capacity
     is no longer the first refusal) is listed as moved, never counted as completed;
   - the referee's share, the adjudicator (`referee` at 0.90 or above, `batch-cpu:strict` below) and the BET-34
     status of the class are re-read from the new row, and a class whose adjudicator changed is named first.
   The old rows are not edited. The gate is recorded from the entry's receipt by a session that did not
   implement the work (`cargo run -p frob-conformance --example record_gate -- --gate GATE-FID-03 --receipt
   <hash>`). If FD-FSIM-12 was adopted in the same pin move, the campaign is run once and NFp3-11 and this bead
   read the same new rows, each for its own cause.
7. **Form (iii): the upstream patch model as a registered reduction.** If a documented patch model is what
   landed, it is entered as a row of `registries/patch_reductions.toml` (N18b-21's registry: name, group
   selector, scenario classes, the sibling's qualification fixture as its receipt and the bound FD-FSIM-13
   recorded), so that a run using it carries the `patch-reduced` condition and prints that bound, as every
   qualified reduction does; a patch applied to a foot whose remaining contacts cannot carry the tilting moment
   is still refused by the patch rule, and an unregistered name is still the `patch-reduction-unqualified`
   refusal. The disc known answer travels with it: for a uniformly loaded disc of radius a under normal force N
   with friction coefficient μ the torsional capacity is M_max = (2/3)·μ·N·a (1.6667 N·m at μ = 0.5, N = 100 N,
   a = 0.05 m).
8. **Adopt.** When steps 2 to 6 passed with C above 21 (or with a registered patch reduction under form (iii)),
   FD-FSIM-13's row in `registries/upstream.toml` becomes `adopted`, citing the receipts. Over-capacity sets
   still refuse by name above C, and the interim posture ends only in the sense that C moved.

Determinism: capacity figures and coverage rows are exact counts (class D0 for the static audit); the study of
step 5 and the audit of step 4 are repeated under new keys by N18b-24's own determinism step. Evidence of what
this bead writes: claim Fidelity for the coverage rows and Known answer for the gate row, class `Tested`,
`reality: no-claim`.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The three readings of the capacity (`doctor --section referee`, `contact.capacity`'s `referee_cap`, the 721 refusal) are one number C at the revision of the new pin.
- [ ] No script or test of N18b-20, N18b-21 or N18b-24 asserts the literal 21 as the referee capacity: each such assertion is the rule m ≤ C on the capacity read, `scripts/e2e/contact_capacity_audit.sh` passes whole at the new pin, and at C = 21 the rule gives exactly the expectations the scripts held before (`fits_rule_reproduces_cap_21`).
- [ ] For the tracked reference design, `contact.capacity` reports every class against C, and the audit's share of windows that fell to `strict` is not above its value at the old pin; both shares are recorded, and the lockstep run's own overflow attributions are unchanged.
- [ ] A scenario class whose maximum is above C still answers `FROB-REF-721` on the referee with the count, C and the upstream reason; no contact is dropped (the per-world contact list digest equals the detected set).
- [ ] The capacity-bias fixture returns `capacity-biased`, `verdict: null` and both overflow shares at the new pin, citing it answers `FROB-REF-822`, and the default-capacity comparison returns a verdict; the row of GATE-TRAC-06 is recorded against the new pin from the receipt, by a session that did not implement the work.
- [ ] A `referee_class_coverage` row exists at the new stack-lock digest for every reference class; the partition identity holds in each; the rows at the old digest are unchanged; GATE-FID-03 is recorded from the entry's receipt.
- [ ] Per class, the count refused with `contact-cap` at the new pin is not above the count at the old pin, and the comparison (old and new counts by cause, scenarios moved to another cause, share, adjudicator, BET-34 status) is in the record.
- [ ] Under form (iii): the patch model is a row of `registries/patch_reductions.toml` with its bound, and a run that uses it prints the `patch-reduced` condition and the bound.
- [ ] FD-FSIM-13's row reads `adopted` with the receipts only after steps 2 to 6 passed with C above 21 or with the registered patch reduction; otherwise it reads `landed` with the reason.
- [ ] The script refuses to start when a precondition of step 1 is missing.

## Test plan

### Unit tests

`crates/frob-conformance/tests/upstream_capacity_switchover.rs` (real registries and a real store in a temporary
directory; capacity tables and coverage rows labelled `synthetic`):
- `fits_rule_reproduces_cap_21`: with C = 21 the rule m ≤ C gives, for maxima 16, 24 and 40, fits, does not fit and does not fit, the expectations the scripts held as literals.
- `fits_rule_at_larger_caps`: C = 32 gives fits, fits, does not fit; C = 64 gives fits for all three; C = 23 gives the same answers as C = 21 (the boundary: 24 does not fit at 23 and fits at 24).
- `capacity_exit_code_follows_fits`: a capacity result with every class fitting maps to exit 0 and one with a class above C to exit 2 (the verb's rule, on stored outputs at C = 21 and C = 64).
- `three_capacity_readings_agree`: the `referee` doctor section, `referee_cap` and the capacity field of a 721 refusal built from one admission record are equal; a planted record in which the doctor rows keep 21 while the admission record says 64 is reported with both values.
- `no_literal_cap_in_capacity_scripts`: a scan of the three scripts and their test modules finds no comparison of a contact count with the literal 21.
- `contact_cap_cause_must_not_rise`: old row `{ contact-cap: 48, fixed-witness-gate: 6 }`, new row `{ 9, 11 }` over 200 scenarios with five scenarios moved: accepted, 39 fewer refusals of the cause, 5 listed as moved to `fixed-witness-gate`, 34 newly completed; new row `{ 49, 6 }`: the comparison fails naming the class and the cause.
- `partition_identity_checked`: a new row whose completed, refused and faulted counts sum to 199 of 200 is rejected before any comparison.
- `form_ii_leaves_row_landed`: with C = 21 at the new pin and form (ii) recorded, the driver runs the regression steps and does not set `adopted`.
- `patch_model_row_carries_bound` (form (iii)): the registry row for the upstream patch model loads with its receipt and bound; a run record that used it has the `patch-reduced` condition and the bound; the disc case gives M_max = 1.6667 N·m, with 1.6 N·m held and 1.7 N·m slipping.
- `precondition_guard`: a registry row reading `open`, or a missing referee-side record, stops the driver before any run.

### End-to-end script and logging

`scripts/e2e/upstream_fd_fsim_13_capacity.sh` (plain shell, sources the shared helper of STD-E2E, seed-explicit,
idempotent, non-zero on failure). Steps: `preconditions` (`cargo xtask check-stack`; `frob doctor --section
upstream --json` for the registry row; the referee-side record of FD-FSIM-13); `capacity-read` (`frob doctor
--section referee --json` and `frob contact capacity <design> --json` for the quadruped, humanoid and tracked
reference designs: one C, the per-class maxima and `fits`); `before` (`frob fidelity coverage --class <c> --json`
per class: the old rows by cause); `audit-stage` (`scripts/e2e/contact_capacity_audit.sh` as a stage, its
`assert`, `refusal` and `summary` lines read from its log: a called script that exits 0 with a failed `assert`
line fails the stage); `tracked-audit` (the two commands of step 4, the audit under its own key); `bias-stage`
(`scripts/e2e/capacity_bias.sh`, read the same way, then the Gauntlet command of step 5); `coverage` (the Gauntlet
entry of step 6, then `frob fidelity coverage --class <c> --json` per class and the comparison by cause);
`patch-model` (only under form (iii): the registry row loaded and one run with `--patch-reduction <name>` under
its own key, asserting the condition and the bound); `adopt` (the row read back as `adopted`, or `landed` with
the reason). The recorder commands of steps 5 and 6 are run afterwards by a session that did not implement the
work; the script stops at the receipts.

Log: `target/e2e-logs/upstream_fd_fsim_13_capacity/<utc-timestamp>.ndjson` and a human-readable transcript
beside it. Every line has `ts`, `script`, `bead` (`NFp3-12`), `step`, `event` (`start`, `cmd`, `assert`,
`metric`, `refusal`, `end`); `cmd` lines carry the exact command line, exit code, wall time and the envelope's
`evidence` block (claim, class, tier, determinism, identities, seed); `metric` lines carry C with its three
sources, per design and class the static maximum, `fits` and headroom in contacts, the audit's share of windows
that fell to `strict` at the old and the new pin, the overflow share of each arm of the bias fixture in
percentage points against the one-point threshold, and per class the old and new coverage counts by cause, the
scenarios moved, the share and the adjudicator, with the sibling's pinned commit and the stack receipt hash;
refusals are logged with the code, the contact count and the capacity; on failure the line carries the first
differing value (the first reading of C that disagrees, the first class whose expectation and result differ, or
the first class whose `contact-cap` count rose) and the run identity, world address and replay command needed to
reproduce. A final `summary` line gives assertions passed and failed, the gates touched (GATE-TRAC-06,
GATE-FID-03), C and the form that landed. No private-scene data is logged.

### Known answers and negative controls

- Known answers: the static maxima 16, 24 and 40 and the rule m ≤ C (16 standing contacts fit under 21, 22 do
  not); KA-420 (the capacity-bias fixture returns `capacity-biased` and no winner; citing it refuses with
  `FROB-REF-822`); the partition identity of a coverage row and the adjudicator rule at 0.90; the hand case of
  `contact_cap_cause_must_not_rise` (48 → 9 with five moved and 34 completed); M_max = (2/3)·μ·N·a = 1.6667 N·m.
- Negative controls: an adapter that keeps the first C contacts and drops the rest must be caught by the digest
  of the contact list against the detected set (the truncation the whole rule exists to forbid); a script left
  asserting the literal 21 must fail at a pin with C = 64 and be found by `no_literal_cap_in_capacity_scripts`;
  a doctor section that still prints 21 must fail `capacity-read`; a comparison whose arms differ by 2 percentage
  points of capacity-refused worlds must not return a winner; a coverage comparison that counts a scenario moved
  to `fixed-witness-gate` as completed must fail the partition identity; an adoption attempted with C = 21 and
  no registered patch reduction must leave the row `landed`.
- Determinism lanes: the static audit is exact and identical on every platform; the bias study and the audit give
  identical digests twice on one machine and across `batch-cpu` worker counts (N18b-24's own step).

## Considerations

- Capacity bias is the reason this item matters to users: a comparison between a tracked design and a point-foot
  design is honest only if both arms were simulated. A larger referee capacity removes refusals from the referee
  arm; it does not remove the rule, which still labels any comparison whose arms differ in capacity-refused
  worlds, on every tier.
- A larger dense solve costs with the cube of the row count (64 contacts are 192 rows against 63, about 28 times
  the work per factorization), so the coverage campaign at the new pin is slower per completed scenario than at
  the old one; its wall-clock percentiles are re-read from the new rows and the record says by how much.
- The expected fall of `contact-cap` is a hypothesis: scenarios that stop refusing on capacity may refuse next on
  fixed witnesses, which only FD-FSIM-12 lifts (NFp3-11). The record reports moved scenarios separately, so that
  the two items' effects on BET-34 are not confused.
- The lockstep tiers have their own capacities (C = 64 of a shape class in N18b-24's forced-overflow step), raised
  by a new step-plan identity on this side; nothing here changes them, and that step of the stage must pass
  unchanged.
- No milestone waits on this bead: until it runs, the referee at the new pin is used with the capacity it reports,
  the coverage read at the new stack prints `"not measured"` with the campaign as its next action, and FD-FSIM-13's
  row reads `landed`, which are the fail-closed states.
- Neither a pin nor the toolchain moves during the ten-fold tournament of M7, so this bead does not run then.
