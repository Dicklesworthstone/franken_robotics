## 13. Marrow: the algebraic core

### 13.1 What it owns

`frob-lie` owns the Lie-theoretic vocabulary every other crate speaks: the `LieGroup` and `LieAlgebra` traits;
the groups SO(2), SO(3), SE(2), SE(3), SE₂(3) and SE₂₊ₖ(3); product groups; joint subgroups described by a screw
basis with their Hervé class (C-01); the two retractions and their derivatives (dexp, dcay and inverses); and
frame-typed transforms, twists and wrenches (C-02). It also owns the typed adjoint, wrench push-forward and
wrench pull-back maps of §13.3, the isometry type that carries reflections with their parity, and the predicate
classes of §13.7.

`frob-algebra` owns the non-Lie algebra: finite groups as signed permutations with closure, conjugacy classes,
character tables, isotypic projectors and the Reynolds operator (C-09); the `Cone` trait with the nonnegative
orthant, second-order and positive-semidefinite cones (C-06); posets and antichains for co-design (C-12); the
pseudo-inertia cone with its affine-invariant operations (C-03); and max-plus matrices, taken from
`fs-tropical` through the airlock (C-10). To these it adds five things: the computation of a design's
mechanical symmetry group and of its observation-preserving subgroup (§13.4); real and complex irreducible
blocks as a parameterization of equivariant linear maps (§13.5); subgroup lattices, orbit counting and orbit
representatives (§13.6); the four-dimensional contact cone as one more linear image of the second-order cone,
which §18 consumes; and the mass-support predicate on the inertia cone, which §14.5 consumes.

Both crates sit in layer 1 and depend only on `frob-core` and the airlock. Neither knows what a robot is: they
receive trees, screws, matrices and channel lists, and return groups, classes, projectors, signs and refusals.

### 13.2 Decisions

- **ADR-MARROW-01: reuse `fs-ga` as the host representation.** `So3`, `Se3`, `Twist`, `Wrench` and `Mat6` are
  FrankenSim's, with deterministic elementary functions already routed through `fs_math::det` (FACT). FrankenSim
  itself has two vector vocabularies today, because `fs-mbd` defines its own `Vec3`, `UnitQuaternion` and `Pose`
  beside `fs-ga`'s (FACT). FrankenRobotics does not add a third. `frob-lie` adds traits, the groups `fs-ga`
  lacks, and frame typing.
- **ADR-MARROW-02: Cayley on the batched tiers, exponential on the referee.** Both retractions exist on every
  tier and the choice is part of `ModelId`. Reason: C-05. The Cayley map is refused when |ω|h exceeds 0.2 per
  substep (FROB-REF-212), which the step planner avoids by substepping.
- **ADR-MARROW-03: a revolute coordinate is a unit complex number.** The update is
  z₊ = z · ((1 − a²/4) + i·a)/(1 + a²/4) with a = ωh, followed by one normalization. A turn counter is updated
  by a deterministic branch rule. Limits are tested on the half-angle tangent t = s/(1 + c), which is monotone in
  the angle, plus the turn count. Reason: no trigonometric call in the hot path, and observations in (cos, sin)
  form are what policies consume anyway.
- **ADR-MARROW-04: packed single-precision layouts are generated, not typed.** There is no `Se3f32` struct.
  `frob-kir` emits structure-of-arrays layouts from the same algebraic description, so the host type and the
  kernel layout cannot disagree.
- **ADR-MARROW-05: there are two coadjoints, each with its own name, type and sign.** The group coadjoint is the
  inverse transpose of Ad and moves wrenches between frames. The infinitesimal coadjoint ad* is the transpose of
  ad and appears in the Euler–Poincaré equation. Reason: an earlier draft of the conventions described ad* with
  a phrase that is true of the generator of the group coadjoint, which differs from ad* by a sign. §13.3 states
  both in components and §13.8 tests them with fixtures that a single reversed sign cannot pass.
- **ADR-MARROW-06: two symmetry groups are computed and stored for every design in every task.** The mechanical
  group 𝔾_mech preserves the body. Its subgroup 𝔾_obs also preserves everything the closed loop can see or is
  asked to do. Only 𝔾_obs may tie policy weights, augment data, deduplicate candidates or enumerate gaits.
  Reason: a robot with a forward-facing sensor is mechanically symmetric fore and aft and is not symmetric in
  closed loop; tying weights under the larger group constrains the policy to something false (§13.4).
- **ADR-MARROW-07: equivariant linear maps are parameterized by real and complex irreducible blocks, and the
  Reynolds operator is their oracle.** The blocks give exactly the free parameters of the commutant. Reynolds
  averaging gives the same subspace and is exact; it is kept as the test, not replaced as an error. A
  representation of quaternionic type is refused (FROB-REF-214): no group that arises here has one, so meeting
  one means the group computation is wrong.
- **ADR-MARROW-08: a strict inequality that decides a discrete outcome is evaluated exactly.** Such predicates
  run either in exact arithmetic on the host or in guarded single precision with an exact integer fallback
  (§13.7). Reason: two backends that agree to the last bit but one on a limit test will still disagree about
  whether the limit is active, and after that the two worlds are different worlds (§35).
- **ADR-MARROW-09: a reflection is not a transform.** `Transform<A, B>` holds an element of SE(3). Mirror images
  are carried by a separate `Isometry` type with an explicit determinant, and the parity rule of §13.3 is applied
  by that type alone. Reason: a mirrored limb needs sign changes on axial quantities that a rigid transform never
  applies, and hiding the sign inside a "transform" is how mirrored encoders end up reading backwards.

### 13.3 Conventions fixed here: the coadjoint, the wrench maps, reflections

§11 holds the conventions table. This section states the rows Marrow implements in components, so that no
sentence about them needs interpreting. Throughout, g = (R, p) is `a_from_b`, ξ = (ω, v) is a twist, w = (τ, f)
is a wrench and μ = (π, ℓ) is a body momentum.

| Object | Definition | In components | Type or method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjoint | Ad_g maps a twist in the source frame of g to its target frame | Ad_g(ω, v) = (Rω, Rv + p × Rω) | `AdjointMap` |
| Group coadjoint (wrench push-forward) | Ad*_g := (Ad_{g⁻¹})ᵀ = (Ad_g)⁻ᵀ, so that ⟨Ad*_g w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩ | Ad*_g(τ, f) = (Rτ + p × Rf, Rf) | `WrenchPushforward`; equals `fs_ga::Se3::coadjoint` (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-ga/src/lie.rs`) |
| Wrench pull-back | (Ad_g)ᵀ, the inverse of the row above | (Ad_g)ᵀ(τ, f) = (Rᵀ(τ − p × f), Rᵀf) | `WrenchPullback` |
| Bracket | ad_ξ η = [ξ, η] | (ω × ω′, ω × v′ + v × ω′) | `LieAlgebra::bracket`; equals `fs_ga::Twist::bracket` (FACT, same file) |
| Infinitesimal coadjoint | ad*_ξ := (ad_ξ)ᵀ, the dual map: ⟨ad*_ξ μ, η⟩ = ⟨μ, ad_ξ η⟩ | ad*_ξ(π, ℓ) = (π × ω + ℓ × v, ℓ × ω) | `LieAlgebra::coad` |
| Generator of the group coadjoint | d/dt Ad*_{exp(tξ)} at t = 0 | −ad*_ξ | Derived in tests, never stored |
| Euler–Poincaré, body frame | μ̇ = ad*_ξ μ + w for a left-invariant kinetic energy | π̇ = π × ω + ℓ × v + τ, ℓ̇ = ℓ × ω + f | Used by §16 |

**The fix.** ad* is the transpose of ad. The inverse transpose belongs to the group coadjoint Ad*. The phrase
"negative transpose" is true only of the generator in the sixth row and is withdrawn as a description of ad*:
read that way, a free body would obey μ̇ = −(ad_ξ)ᵀμ, which has the wrong sign. The three wrench and twist maps
are distinct newtypes so that the compiler rejects a push-forward where a pull-back is meant. `fs-ga` offers
`Twist::ad` and `Se3::coadjoint` and no infinitesimal coadjoint (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-ga/src/lie.rs`), so
`coad` is new code in `frob-lie`, and PO-MARROW-01 is extended to cover the first four rows bit for bit.

**Why energy does not catch the error.** Take a free body with rotational inertia diag(1, 2, 3) kg·m² and
ω = (1, 1, 0) rad/s, so π = (1, 2, 0). The correct law gives π̇ = π × ω = (0, 0, −1). The reversed sign gives
(0, 0, +1). Both conserve kinetic energy, because ω · π̇ = 0 either way. Only the correct one conserves spatial
angular momentum: the body-frame rate π̇ + ω × π is (0, 0, 0) with the correct sign and (0, 0, 2) with the wrong
one. A test suite that checks energy alone passes a simulator with this defect. The fixture is hand-checkable and
is listed in §13.10.

**Reflections.** For an isometry x ↦ Px + t with P orthogonal and det P = −1:

- a twist maps by (ω, v) ↦ (det(P)·P·ω, P·v + t × det(P)·P·ω): polar vectors by P, axial vectors by det(P)·P;
- a wrench maps by (τ, f) ↦ (det(P)·P·τ + t × P·f, P·f);
- a pseudo-inertia maps by J ↦ G·J·Gᵀ with G = [[P, t], [0, 1]], which needs no parity sign because the second
  moment is a polar quantity;
- a revolute joint about axis a maps to a revolute joint about det(P)·P·a with the same coordinate. A design
  that writes the mirrored axis as P·a instead must negate the coordinate, its limits, and its encoder and gear
  signs. The `mirror` constructor always emits the first form, so a mirrored limb shares its parent's kernels.

**Small angles on the exponential lane.** The coefficient functions sin θ/θ, (1 − cos θ)/θ² and
(θ − sin θ)/θ³ are evaluated by even polynomials in θ² below a threshold and by deterministic elementary
functions above it, with the two arms joined by a select. The degree and threshold are chosen so that the
truncation error is below half a unit in the last place, and both are recorded in the kernel manifest (DESIGN:
it removes the 0/0 forms without a branch). The Cayley lane needs no such rule.

**Chart cuts.** A logarithm or a derivative of a logarithm requested within a stated margin of a rotation by π,
where the chart is cut, returns FROB-REF-213 with the offending element unless the caller supplies a second
chart. No value is returned from the wrong side of a cut.

### 13.4 The mechanical group and the observation-preserving subgroup

**What it is.** [S] for the computation; [F] for its use in equivariant networks (§23).

- 𝔾_mech is the group of C-09: automorphisms of the labeled kinematic tree that are realized by an isometry of
  the body at its reference configuration and that preserve link Regions, fills and materials (hence masses and
  pseudo-inertias), joint screw bases under the parity rule, joint limits, transmissions with their signed
  ratios, actuator cards, end-effector contact laws with their interface cards, and the topology of the power
  bus.
- 𝔾_obs is the subgroup of 𝔾_mech whose elements also preserve: the sensor suite as a set (mount, field of view,
  scan pattern, rate, clock and noise card of each sensor); every payload; the direction of gravity at the
  reference configuration; and every observation, action, reward and requirement term of the task, each of which
  must declare how it transforms. An element under which some term has no declared transformation is excluded.
- For one particular world there is a third, smaller object: the stabilizer of the context, Stab(context) ≤ 𝔾_obs,
  whose elements also fix that world's terrain, material field, boundary conditions and current contact mode.

The three have different uses and they are not interchangeable.

| Use | Group required | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Block-diagonal mass and stiffness matrices at a symmetric stance (A-12) | 𝔾_mech ∩ Stab(configuration) | Mechanics only; sensors are irrelevant |
| Weight tying in policies and critics; data augmentation | 𝔾_obs, with the task's world and command distributions invariant in law | The closed loop must be symmetric, not only the body |
| Deduplicating candidate designs before a sweep (§13.6) | 𝔾_obs of the slot arrangement | Two candidates are the same experiment only if the closed loop cannot tell them apart |
| Gait enumeration by (H, K) pairs (C-10) | 𝔾_obs | A gait is a closed-loop object |
| Exact reduction inside one world: symmetric orbit search, merging two worlds | Stab(context) | Augmentation may pair two different worlds; reduction needs the same world |
| Metamorphic test of the simulator (T-10) | 𝔾_mech with the world transformed along with the robot | The test transforms everything, so nothing is broken |

**Algorithm.** Owner: `frob-algebra::symmetry`. Determinism class D0. Verb: `analyze.symmetry` (A-03), which
prints both groups, their generators, the induced representations and the list of symmetry breakers.

1. Colour every node of the tree with an isometry-invariant key: the canonical hash of its Region, fill and
   material; the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of its pseudo-inertia (exact rationals, so no
   eigenvalues are sorted); joint class, pitch and limit widths; actuator card, absolute gear ratio and
   transmission kind; contact law and interface card; and the multiset of its children's keys.
2. Compute the canonical form of the coloured rooted tree bottom-up, which identifies isomorphic subtrees in
   O(n log n) for n bodies.
3. Enumerate candidate isometries, not candidate permutations. The abstract automorphism group of a tree can be
   large; the isometries that realize it are few. With three or more non-collinear attachment points in one
   class on the root body, an isometry is fixed, up to one reflection through their plane, by the images of
   three of them. That gives at most 2·m·(m − 1)·(m − 2) candidates for m such points, each obtained from a 3×3
   linear system solved exactly and kept only if PᵀP = I holds exactly. With fewer points (a biped's two hips, a
   differential drive's two wheels) the candidates are the signed axis permutations of the root's principal
   frame together with the generators the design's `mirror` and `radial` constructors declare. A continuous
   stabilizer (a body of revolution with a single axial limb) is reported as such, and only its
   constructor-generated finite subgroup is used.
4. Verify each candidate by descending the tree: every body, screw basis (with parity), limit interval, signed
   ratio, card and contact law must map to its image. The descent yields the body permutation σ and the sign
   vector ε on joint coordinates. The first mismatch ends the descent and is recorded, with its field path and
   its size, for the near-symmetry report of A-03.
5. Close the survivors under composition, check closure, and build the multiplication table, conjugacy classes
   and character table.
6. Induce the representations: signed permutations on joint positions, velocities and actuator channels (the
   actuator sign includes gear direction); the twist rule of §13.3 on the base; and, for each observation term,
   the representation its declared kind implies (invariant scalar, polar vector, axial vector, joint-indexed,
   foot-indexed, sensor-indexed, or a scan with a ray permutation).
7. Filter to 𝔾_obs. An element survives if it maps the sensor suite to itself with matching cards and scan
   patterns, maps every payload to a payload, fixes gravity, and has a representation on every task term. The
   first field that excludes an element is recorded as its breaker.
8. Return both groups, the representations as integer signed permutations, the breakers, and for each breaker
   the smallest typed edit that would restore the element.

**Data layout.** A group is stored as its order, its generators, one record per element (body permutation as
`u16` indices, coordinate signs as `i8`, the isometry in exact form), a multiplication table of |𝔾|² bytes, a
class map and a character table in exact field elements. A representation on a channel space is one `u16` index
and one `i8` sign per channel per element. Nothing here is floating point.

**Three routes to exactness.** A symmetry is established (i) by construction, when the limbs are instances of
one subterm under `mirror` or `radial` and the check reduces to term equality plus integer arithmetic on mount
indices modulo n; (ii) by exact field arithmetic, with entries in ℚ for mirrors and in the real subfield of the
n-th cyclotomic field for n-fold rotations, n ≤ 12; or (iii) by tolerance, for imported designs, in which case
the result is classed `Tested`, the tolerance is recorded, and the near-symmetry report says how far each failing
field is from symmetric. Routes (i) and (ii) are D0 and classed `Certified` when the certificate is emitted.

**Counterexamples that fix the rules.**

- *A forward sensor.* A quadruped built fore-aft and left-right symmetric has 𝔾_mech = C₂ × C₂ of order 4. Add
  one forward-facing lidar on the torso. The left-right mirror maps the lidar to itself; the fore-aft mirror and
  the half-turn do not. So 𝔾_obs = C₂ of order 2, and the breaker for the other two elements is the lidar mount.
  A policy tied under C₂ × C₂ would be required to walk backwards as the mirror image of walking forwards while
  seeing only forwards.
- *A fixed observation forces a fixed action.* Two identical latches, of which exactly one must be closed. At an
  observation fixed by the mirror, a deterministic equivariant controller must issue an action fixed by the
  mirror, so it commands both latches alike, and both matching commands fail. A controller that draws one latch
  at random is equivariant in law and succeeds. A failed search over tied deterministic policies therefore
  excludes only that policy class. `frob-algebra` exposes the fixed subspace of any element in any representation
  so that A-03 can list the observations at which tying forces tied actions.
- *A world that is not symmetric.* A quadruped crossing a patch that is slippery on one side only. The task's
  world distribution may still be mirror-invariant in law, which licenses augmentation, while no single world is
  mirror-invariant, which forbids reduction inside it. The policy must remain free to act asymmetrically.
- *A material edit.* Changing one foot's sole material leaves the encoder permutation intact and breaks the
  transition symmetry. Both facts are recorded: the representation still exists, the group element does not.

### 13.5 Equivariant linear maps by irreducible blocks

**What it is.** [S] for the decomposition; [F] for its payoff in learning, which is BET-07 and is tested in §23.

For real representations ρ_in on ℝ^{d_in} and ρ_out on ℝ^{d_out} of a finite group, the equivariant linear maps
are the W with ρ_out(g)·W = W·ρ_in(g) for all g. Over the reals an irreducible representation has one of three
types, read from the Frobenius–Schur indicator ι(χ) = |𝔾|⁻¹·Σ_g χ(g²) of a complex irreducible character χ:

| ι(χ) | Real type | Real irreducible built from χ | Equivariant maps between m_in and m_out copies | Free parameters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | Real | χ itself, dimension d_χ | Θ ⊗ I with Θ any real m_out × m_in matrix | m_out·m_in |
| 0 | Complex | χ and its conjugate together, dimension 2·d_χ | Blocks [[a, −b], [b, a]] ⊗ I: a complex m_out × m_in matrix | 2·m_out·m_in |
| −1 | Quaternionic | — | Refused (FROB-REF-214) | — |

So dim Hom_𝔾(V_in, V_out) = Σ_i c_i·m_i^out·m_i^in with c_i = 1 for real type and 2 for complex type. Every
irreducible representation of C₂, C₂ × C₂, the dihedral groups and S₄ is of real type. Complex type occurs for
the cyclic groups Cₙ with n ≥ 3, which are the symmetry groups of radial designs without a mirror plane: a
tri-wheel cluster with handed wheels, a hexapod whose legs are all the same chirality.

**Algorithm.** Owner: `frob-algebra::irreps`. Verb: `algebra.irreps`, which reports the decomposition, the type
of each constituent and the free-parameter count for a given pair of channel spaces.

1. From the character table, compute ι for each character and pair each complex-type character with its
   conjugate.
2. For each real irreducible, build a symmetry-adapted orthonormal basis of its isotypic component in the input
   and output spaces by applying the projector P_χ = (d_χ/|𝔾|)·Σ_g χ(g)*·ρ(g) (summed with its conjugate for
   complex type) and the transfer operators that align the copies with one another.
3. Store the two change-of-basis matrices Q_in and Q_out and the block list. A map is then
   W = Q_out·(⊕_i Θ_i)·Q_inᵀ, where Θ_i is the free block of constituent i.
4. Biases are restricted to the fixed subspace of ρ_out. Hidden layers carry copies of the regular
   representation, on which pointwise nonlinearities commute with the group (GB-02).

The decomposition costs O(d³) once per model and channel space and is cached by `ModelId`. At run time a layer is
either applied in the block basis or expanded once per optimizer step into a dense W, so inference pays nothing.

**What the blocks are and are not.** They are a parameterization. The Reynolds operator
W ↦ |𝔾|⁻¹·Σ_g ρ_out(g)·W·ρ_in(g)⁻¹ projects any matrix onto the same subspace exactly, for every type; with
signed-permutation representations it costs |𝔾|·d_out·d_in operations, which is small. The blocks earn their
place for three reasons: optimization runs on exactly the free parameters instead of projecting after every
step; the free-parameter count is reported to the agent before training; and the same bases block-diagonalize
the linearized dynamics for A-12.

**Counterexample: treating a complex-type block as real.** C₃ acts on ℝ³ by cyclic permutation of three legs.
The equivariant maps ℝ³ → ℝ³ are the circulant matrices, with three free parameters. The space splits as the
trivial representation plus one two-dimensional real irreducible of complex type, so the formula gives
1 + 2 = 3. A parameterization that gives the two-dimensional block a single scalar, as would be right for a
real-type block, yields two parameters and spans only the symmetric circulants. Those are exactly the maps
equivariant under D₃. The error silently imposes a mirror symmetry the robot does not have, and the map
x ↦ (x₂ − x₃, x₃ − x₁, x₁ − x₂), which is equivariant under C₃, becomes unrepresentable.

### 13.6 Orbits, subgroup lattices and wreath products

- **Orbit counting and representatives** [S]. Before a combinatorial sweep that assigns one of k module types to
  each of n slots, `frob-algebra::orbits` computes the cycle index of the slot action of 𝔾_obs, counts the
  orbits, and generates one canonical representative per orbit. Verb: `algebra.orbits`. Worked count (FACT,
  recomputed for this plan): ten leg types on six slots of a radially symmetric hexapod under D₆ give
  (10⁶ + 2·10 + 2·10² + 10³ + 3·10⁴ + 3·10³)/12 = 86,185 orbits out of 10⁶ assignments. If the sensors leave
  only the sagittal mirror, the count is (10⁶ + 10³)/2 = 500,500. The saving is bounded by |𝔾_obs| and is a
  factor of two for most sensed robots; it is stated before any sweep is priced.
- **Contact strata.** The same counter sizes mode enumerations: four feet, each open, sticking or slipping,
  under C₂ × C₂ give (3⁴ + 3·3²)/4 = 27 strata (FACT, recomputed for this plan).
- **Subgroup lattice and (H, K) pairs** [S]. All subgroups up to conjugacy, their normalizers, and the pairs
  K ⊴ H with H/K cyclic that C-10 needs. The groups here have at most a few dozen elements, so enumeration is by
  closure of generating sets.
- **Wreath products** [F]. When each limb has an internal symmetry group K of its own and the hub permutes the
  limbs by H, the design's group contains K ≀ H provided every limb automorphism preserves the limb's attachment
  port and the hub action preserves the decorated ports. Both conditions are checked before nested tying is
  offered. The use is narrow: steer-and-drive modules on a swerve base, symmetric grippers on a symmetric arm.

### 13.7 Exact predicates and guarded single precision

**What it is.** [S]. A predicate is the sign of an expression where the sign selects between outcomes that
differ at the boundary. `frob-lie` and `frob-kir` classify every predicate into one of three classes, and the
class is part of the kernel manifest.

| Class | Arithmetic | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| `Exact` | Integers, rationals and field elements on the host (D0) | Group closure; bracket closure and Hervé class (T-11); the sign of an LDLᵀ pivot of a pseudo-inertia given in rationals; mobility rank at rational sample points; canonical orderings and digests |
| `Guarded` | Single precision with a certified error bound, and an exact integer fallback on the same stored inputs | Joint-limit activation on t = s/(1 + c); the turn-counter branch of ADR-MARROW-03; the gap sign that activates a contact at commit; cone membership ‖γₜ‖ ≤ μ·γₙ for stick or slip; event guards of the transactional step (§16); tie-breaks in sort keys; the Cayley admission bound, tested as (ω·ω)·h² ≤ 0.04 |
| `Plain` | Single precision | A select whose two arms agree at the boundary (clamp, max, a Huber kink), where a wrong sign moves the result continuously and changes no discrete outcome |

**The guarded evaluation.** For a predicate polynomial p of low degree in single-precision inputs:

1. Evaluate p̂ in single precision, and in the same pass a bound E = c·u·S, where S is the sum of the absolute
   values of the terms, u is the unit round-off and c is a constant that `frob-kir` derives from the
   straight-line program by forward error analysis and stores in the manifest.
2. If |p̂| > E the sign of p̂ is the sign of p, and the predicate is done.
3. Otherwise evaluate p exactly on the stored inputs. Every finite single-precision number is an integer of at
   most 24 bits times a power of two, so a polynomial in such numbers is an integer computation. It is carried
   out in 32-bit limbs with widening multiplies, which exist identically on the CPU, on CUDA and on Metal, and it
   belongs to the exact-integer kernel class of §35.
4. The bit budget of step 3 is computed at derivation time from the declared ranges of the inputs. A predicate
   whose budget exceeds 256 bits is refused at build time (FROB-REF-215), which is a signal to rescale it
   (DESIGN: the cap keeps the fallback a fixed, small integer program on every backend).

The exact result is exact with respect to the stored single-precision inputs. That is what makes two backends
with bit-equal inputs take the same branch. It says nothing about how well the inputs approximate real
quantities, and under determinism class D3, where inputs differ within a tolerance, two tiers may still
legitimately take different branches near a boundary; the decision-level equivalence test of §35 counts those.

**The lint.** A select whose arms differ at the boundary and whose predicate is `Plain` is a build error in
`frob-kir`. This is the rule that keeps the class assignment from decaying.

**Counterexample (FACT, computed for this plan by emulating IEEE binary32 against exact rationals).** Take the
single-precision numbers nearest 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 as x, y, z and test x² + y² ≤ z². In single precision the two
sides are equal, so the plain test reports the point as inside the cone. For those same three stored numbers the
exact value of x² + y² − z² is +1.19 × 10⁻⁸: the point is outside. A backend that contracts the expression into a
fused multiply-add evaluates a different rounding sequence again, which is one reason contraction is off
everywhere (§35). The fixture is in the negative-control registry of §38 as a predicate that plain arithmetic
gets wrong.

### 13.8 Laws

Each law is a G0 property test over random inputs and a boundary set (identity, rotations by π, tiny angles,
large translations, sign-equivalent quaternions, rotations approaching π from both sides). Tolerances are in
units in the last place (ulp).

| Law | Statement |
|---|---|
| Group axioms | Associativity, identity, inverse for every group. |
| Retraction | τ(0) = identity; dτ(0) = identity; τ(x)·τ(−x) = identity for cay and exp. |
| Round trip | log(exp(x)) = x inside the injectivity radius; cay⁻¹(cay(x)) = x. |
| Adjoint | Ad(gh) = Ad(g)·Ad(h); Ad(exp x) = exp(ad x) to tolerance; the group coadjoint Ad* is the inverse transpose of Ad. |
| Coadjoint pair | ad*_ξ = (ad_ξ)ᵀ; d/dt Ad*_{exp(tξ)} at 0 equals −ad*_ξ; push-forward composed with pull-back is the identity. |
| Pairing | ⟨w, ξ⟩ is invariant under a change of frame: ⟨Ad*_g w, Ad_g ξ⟩ = ⟨w, ξ⟩. |
| Free body | With μ̇ = ad*_ξ μ the spatial momentum Ad*_g μ is constant. |
| Jacobians | dexp and dcay match `fs-ad` dual-number derivatives. |
| Subgroups | A classified screw basis is bracket-closed; the generated joint map composes like its subgroup. |
| Reflections | A mirror reflection applied twice is the identity on twists, wrenches and pseudo-inertias; the pairing is invariant under every isometry, proper or not; a mirrored revolute joint written with the axial image of its axis keeps its coordinate. |
| Representations | ρ(gh) = ρ(g)·ρ(h); characters are orthonormal; projectors are idempotent and sum to the identity. |
| Nested groups | 𝔾_obs is a subgroup of 𝔾_mech; every element of 𝔾_obs has a representation on every task term; removing every sensor, payload and task term gives 𝔾_obs = 𝔾_mech. |
| Blocks | The block-parameterized maps and the image of the Reynolds operator are the same subspace; its dimension is Σ c_i·m_i^out·m_i^in. |
| Orbits | The cycle-index count equals a brute-force count for every case with at most 10⁶ assignments. |
| Predicates | A `Guarded` predicate returns the sign of its exact evaluation on the stored inputs, for every input. |
| Curvature sign | With body velocities and reconstruction ξ = −𝔸(s)·ṡ, the curvature is (D𝔸)ᵢⱼ = ∂ᵢ𝔸ⱼ − ∂ⱼ𝔸ᵢ − [𝔸ᵢ, 𝔸ⱼ] and a small loop moves the body by about exp(−∬ D𝔸); the opposite bracket sign fails the holonomy fixture of A-09. |
| Cones | P(P(x)) = P(x); Moreau decomposition x = P_𝒦(x) + P_𝒦°(x) with the parts orthogonal; the four-dimensional contact cone is the image of the second-order cone under its stated linear map. |

**Mutation rule.** Each of eight planted single-sign or single-transpose mutations (reversed ad*, Ad* and its
inverse exchanged, a pull-back in place of a push-forward, a missing parity sign on a mirrored axis, linear and
angular parts exchanged in a twist, the bracket's arguments exchanged, the curvature bracket sign reversed, a
conjugate missing in the projector) must be caught by at least three independent fixtures. A convention that one
test guards is one refactor away from being wrong.

## 14. Skeleton: design language, modules, archetypes, preflight

### 14.1 What it owns

Skeleton owns what a robot is before anything moves: the design term and its two views (`frob-design`); the
reusable module format and its contracts (`frob-design`); the model compiler and its memo graph (`frob-model`);
import and export with loss reports (`frob-io`); the catalog of eighteen archetypes shipped in
`assets/archetypes/`; and the staging of the preflight that the verb `design.check` runs. The analyses the
preflight calls are Atelier's (§28, A-01 to A-16) and live in `frob-analyze`. Skeleton fixes their order, what
each stage may refuse, and the budgets they are measured against.

### 14.2 Decisions

- **ADR-SKEL-01: a design is a term, and a file is a view of the term.** The canonical form is JSON with schema
  name `frob.design/1` and extension `.fbot`. `DesignId` is the BLAKE3 hash of the canonical bytes. Reason:
  constructors, typed edits, lineage and incremental compilation all need a tree with stable subterms, and a hash
  needs one byte representation.
- **ADR-SKEL-02: two lossless views, one identity.** The same term can be read and written as an S-expression.
  The two views are in bijection on canonical forms and yield the same `DesignId`. The S-expression view is
  optional for every consumer. This closes OPEN-SKEL-1. Reason: agents edit nested terms more reliably in a
  syntax with one bracket kind and quantity atoms, while tools and wire formats want JSON. FrankenSim's `fs-ir`
  already keeps canonical S-expressions and a lossless JSON mapping in step as a tested property (FACT:
  `frankensim/crates/fs-ir/src/lib.rs`), and its reader and quantity atoms are reused through the airlock if
  they pass admission; a design is not a FrankenScript program and carries no verbs.
- **ADR-SKEL-03: no physics field has a silent default.** Fill, material, contact law, joint limits and an
  actuator's current convention must be written. Reason: each of these defaults yields a plausible wrong robot
  (§14.5 gives the mass case), and the standing rule is that a missing or misspelt parameter is a refusal.
- **ADR-SKEL-04: symmetry is computed, and a declaration is an assertion.** A design may state the groups it
  expects. The compiler computes 𝔾_mech and 𝔾_obs (§13.4) and refuses on a mismatch. The declared value never
  overrides the computed one. Reason: a declaration that is trusted is a way to tie weights under a false group.
- **ADR-SKEL-05: modules are referenced by content hash and carry contracts.** A substitution reuses exactly
  what the contract covers and invalidates the rest by rule (§14.6). Reason: reuse without a stated boundary is
  how a stale certificate survives an edit.
- **ADR-SKEL-06: shared resources are parts of the design.** The power bus and the shared thermal sink are
  written in the term, because they couple limbs that the kinematic tree separates.
- **ADR-SKEL-07: optimizers move levers only.** Continuous optimization and co-design may change a parameter
  only if the design declares it as a lever with bounds and a manifold kind. Agents may make any typed edit.
  Reason: an optimizer that can touch every number finds the numbers nobody meant to free.
- **ADR-SKEL-08: preflight is `design check`.** There is one verb, with eight stages in a fixed order, each
  able to refuse the next. Stages 1 to 6 need only the design. Stages 7 and 8 also need a task and a scene and
  run when those are supplied. Reason: check before you simulate (§28), and a second pre-simulation gate with a
  second name would be two sources of truth.
- **ADR-SKEL-09: every compile product records the fields it read.** Invalidation after an edit is computed from
  those records, not from a hand-kept table (§14.9).

### 14.3 The design term

A design is a term, not a file of numbers. Its leaves are components and its internal nodes are constructors.

- **Components.** `Body` (a Region with a fill and a material), `Joint` (a screw basis with limits), `Actuator`
  (a card reference, §15), `Transmission`, `Battery`, `Sensor` (§20), `Wheel`, `Track`, `Foot`, `Payload`. Each
  exposes typed ports (C-12): mechanical (twist and wrench at a frame), shaft (ω, τ), electrical (V, I), thermal
  (T, Q̇) and signal (a typed message with a rate). A signal may fan out to several consumers. A power port
  joins exactly one junction, and a junction balances its flows; duplicating a power port as if it were a signal
  would double the energy and is refused (FROB-REF-108).
- **Constructors.** `attach(parent_port, child)`, `serial(k, link)`, `mirror(plane, limb)`, `radial(n, axis,
  limb)`, `scale(factor, sub)`, `replace(path, component)`, `closure(port_a, port_b)` for kinematic loops, and
  `use(module, port, bindings)` for a module import (§14.6).
- **Parameters.** Every parameter has a unit, bounds, a manifold kind (ℝ, ℝ₊, S¹, S², SE(3), the inertia cone,
  or a finite set of catalog choices), and a provenance: `designed`, `datasheet`, `measured` or `assumed`. A
  parameter may also carry an uncertainty and, when it is a lever, a prior for search.

The two nested `mirror` constructors in the example of §14.4 make the symmetry C₂ × C₂ a property of the term,
not something to be rediscovered. The forward lidar in the same example reduces 𝔾_obs to C₂, and the design
says so in its `symmetry` block, which the compiler checks.

**Canonical form.** Seven rules make the bytes unique.

1. UTF-8; object keys sorted by code point; no insignificant white space; a duplicate key refuses.
2. A quantity is a string of an exact decimal and a unit token (`"0.42 m"`), or an object with that string and
   its provenance, uncertainty and source. A bare quantity string means provenance `designed`. Unit tokens and
   their parsing are `fs-qty`'s (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-qty/src/parse.rs`), or those of `frob-units` if
   INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 forces the escape hatch.
3. The canonical unit is the coherent SI unit, and the canonical decimal is the shortest that equals the exact
   rational value. A value with no terminating decimal is written as a ratio (`"1/3 m"`).
4. An angle given in degrees or turns is kept as an exact rational of a turn (`"1/8 turn"`); an angle given in
   radians keeps its decimal. Both are exact records of what the author wrote, which is what the exact symmetry
   route of §13.4 needs.
5. Integers above 2⁵³, content hashes and rationals travel as strings, so a client that parses numbers as
   binary64 cannot merge two of them.
6. Every field the compiler will read is present. Schema defaults for non-physics fields are written out with
   provenance `default`, so a later schema version cannot change the meaning of an existing design.
7. Constructor terms stay unexpanded.

The S-expression view maps objects to keyword forms, arrays to lists and quantities to atoms such as `0.42m`.
`frob design view --as sexpr` and `--as json` convert in either direction, and INV-SKEL-04 requires the round
trip to be the identity on canonical forms.

### 14.5 Mass from geometry: the fill rule

**What it is.** [S]. By default inertia is not a parameter: it is computed from a Region, a fill and a material
density (C-03), so an agent designs shapes and materials. The fill says how the Region is occupied.

| Fill | Mass and inertia | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `solid` | Density times the Region's volume moments | Wheels, feet, small brackets, counterweights |
| `{shell: t}` | Density times the moments of the Region minus those of its inward offset by t | Torsos, housings, tubes, limb segments |
| `{lattice: φ}` | A fraction φ of the solid moments | Printed infill; a first-order model that states its own isotropy assumption |
| `declared` | Given mass properties with provenance; the Region is used for collision and as the mass support | Imported robots; parts with measured mass |
| `massless` | None | Frames and markers only. A massless body that carries an actuated joint must have a child with mass, or stage 2 refuses |

**The rule.** There is no default fill (ADR-SKEL-03). A solid-filled body that alone exceeds one quarter of the
design's `mass_max` carries an advisory in the check report (DESIGN: a heuristic threshold, advisory only,
because solid is sometimes what is meant).

**Worked card (illustrative): the mass error the rule prevents.** The torso of §14.4 is a box of
0.42 × 0.20 × 0.10 m in an aluminium alloy at 2,700 kg/m³.

- Read as a solid block it weighs 0.0084 m³ × 2,700 kg/m³ = 22.7 kg.
- Read as a 2.5 mm shell it weighs 1.92 kg: the inner box of 0.415 × 0.195 × 0.095 m removes 0.007688 m³.
- The solid reading is 11.8 times too heavy for a sheet-metal torso. Through the mass spiral of GB-03, with loop
  gain 0.59, each kilogram of structure costs 2.4 kg of robot, so a 20.8 kg error in one body becomes about
  50 kg of robot that does not exist, and every actuator is then sized for it.
- Rescaling the density to hit the right mass does not repair the inertia. About the vertical axis the solid's
  radius of gyration squared is (0.42² + 0.20²)/12 = 0.0180 m², and the shell's is 0.0236 m², which is 31%
  larger, because a shell keeps its mass at the walls.

**Shell moments.** FrankenSim's material-derived inertia provides volume moments for a sphere, an ellipsoid, a
cuboid, a solid and a hollow cylinder and a tetrahedron, with a rigid transform (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-mbd-material/src/geometry.rs`). It has no hollow cuboid, capsule or mesh shell. `frob-model`
therefore computes a shell as the difference of two solids, which is exact for a box, cylinder, sphere and
capsule with a uniform wall, and uses the inward offset of the distance field for a mesh. A shell thicker than
half the Region's smallest dimension refuses as a solid written as a shell (FROB-REF-105).

**Mass support.** [S]. When mass properties are `declared`, positivity of the pseudo-inertia is necessary and
not sufficient: it admits inertias that no body inside the declared Region could have. If all mass lies in a
sphere of centre o and radius R, then ‖c − o‖² + tr(I_C)/(2m) ≤ R², where c is the centre of mass and I_C the
inertia about it, because the left side is the mass-weighted mean squared distance from o. More generally, for
a bounding ellipsoid written as {x : [x; 1]ᵀQ[x; 1] ≥ 0}, a consistent pseudo-inertia P satisfies tr(P·Q) ≥ 0.
Both are linear tests on P and are run exactly at stage 2 (FROB-REF-106). The bound applies to the declared mass
support only, never to an unrelated collision proxy. A known answer is in §13.10.

### 14.6 Reusable modules: `.fmodule`

**What it is.** [S] for the format and the port contract; [F] for reuse of certificates and analyses under
substitution. A module is a design subterm with open ports, parameters and a contract, stored as canonical JSON
with schema `frob.module/1` (and the same S-expression view), and referenced by content hash:
`"std/leg-3r-qdd.fmodule@b3:9a4e…"`. Owner: `frob-design`. Verbs: `module.list`, `module.show`, `module.check`,
`module.substitute`.

```json
{ "schema": "frob.module/1", "name": "leg-3r-qdd",
  "ports": [ {"id": "mount", "kind": "mech",    "frame": "hip_mount", "power": "into-module"},
             {"id": "bus",   "kind": "elec",    "nominal": "22.2 V",  "power": "into-module"},
             {"id": "sink",  "kind": "thermal", "power": "into-module"},
             {"id": "state", "kind": "signal",  "msg": "joint-state/3", "rate": "1000 Hz",
              "latency_max": "1 ms"},
             {"id": "sole",  "kind": "contact", "law": "foot-patch"} ],
  "params": [ {"id": "thigh", "unit": "m", "default": "0.21 m", "bounds": ["0.15 m", "0.30 m"]},
              {"id": "knee_card", "kind": "card:actuator", "default": "qdd-m"} ],
  "body": { "chain": [ "…the three links and the foot of §14.4…" ] },
  "contract": {
    "guarantees": { "mass": ["1.6 kg", "2.4 kg"], "port_inertia": "sym-psd-6",
                    "bus_demand": {"peak": "38 A", "profile": "per-task, time-resolved"},
                    "heat_to_sink": {"continuous_max": "60 W"} },
    "assumes": { "bus_voltage": ["18 V", "25.2 V"], "sink_conductance_min": "0.4 W/K" },
    "digest": "b3:…" } }
```

**Port contract.** Five kinds of port, with one sign convention: power is positive into the module.

| Kind | Effort and flow | Junction rule when two ports are joined |
|---|---|---|
| `mech` | Wrench and twist at a named frame | Twists are identified in a common frame and wrenches balance |
| `shaft` | Torque and angular velocity | Speeds are identified and torques balance |
| `elec` | Voltage and current | Voltages are identified and currents balance at the node |
| `thermal` | Temperature and heat rate | Temperatures are identified and heat rates balance |
| `signal` | A typed message with a rate and a latency bound | One producer, any number of typed consumers; carries no power |

A `contact` entry names an end effector that meets the world. It is listed because it is why a module's effect
on the robot cannot be summarized at its mount port alone. Joining ports checks kinds, frames and signs, sums
the power over the joined ports to zero, and resolves causality; an algebraic loop, a reversed sign or a copied
power port refuses (FROB-REF-108). On the referee the interconnection is FrankenSim's (FACT:
`fs_phs::interconnection::interconnect_mapped` exists at `frankensim/crates/fs-phs/src/interconnection.rs`).

**Substitution contract.** The contract holds what the rest of the robot may rely on without opening the
module (guarantees) and what the module relies on from the rest (assumptions). Its digest covers the port
signature, the parameter schema, the hash of the body term, the versions of the cards it names and its local
certificates: inertia consistency of every body over the parameter box, which is structural for geometry-derived
inertia, and passivity of every element (§15.5).

`module.substitute` replaces the module at a path by another. The substitution is *leaf-local*, and sibling
products are reused, exactly when all four of the following hold:

1. the replacement has the same port signature: kinds, frames up to the declared mount, and sign convention;
2. the replaced module is a leaf of the tree: no `closure` links it to another branch;
3. it reaches shared resources only through its declared `elec` and `thermal` ports, and its assumptions are met
   by the bus and sink as they stand;
4. the consumer of each reused product read only fields outside the replaced subterm, by the influence records
   of §14.9.

| Product | After a leaf-local substitution | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Sibling modules' expanded subtrees, frames and collision primitives | Reused | Their content hashes are unchanged |
| Sibling limbs' symbolic kinematics and closed-form inverse-kinematics kernels | Reused | They depend on the limb alone |
| Sibling limbs' local analyses: workspace, singular loci, self-collision inside the limb | Reused | Same |
| Derived kernels keyed by joint class and tree shape | Reused if the size bucket is unchanged (§17) | Kernels are keyed by shape, not by parameter values |
| Mass roll-up and composite inertia along the path to the root | Recomputed | Cheap, and always changed |
| 𝔾_mech and 𝔾_obs | Recomputed | A swapped limb can break or restore a symmetry |
| Budgets and co-design closure (A-10) | Recomputed | Whole-robot sums and a fixed point |
| Self-collision pairs between the new limb and the others | Recomputed | New geometry |
| Bus and sink analyses | Recomputed whenever the new guarantees differ from the old | Shared resources couple all limbs |
| Static stability, the baseline controller, any rollout, any verdict | Invalidated | Ground contact couples every limb through the base |
| Whole-robot certificates | Invalidated | Their statements quantify over the whole model |

A substitution that fails any of the four conditions is *global*: an edit to the bus or its battery, to the
shared sink, to a limb that takes part in a closure, or a change of port signature. A global substitution
invalidates everything downstream of `ModelId`.

**Counterexample: a contract that carries only peaks can admit and cannot exclude.** Two fingers share a bus
limited to one unit of current. Each finger's local summary says "peak demand one unit". A rule that adds peaks
concludes two units and prunes the design. But one finger draws at the first tick and the other at the second,
so the schedule is feasible. Adding peaks is a sound sufficient test and an unsound exclusion. A contract whose
`bus_demand` has no time-resolved profile is therefore usable for admission only, and the bus analysis of §15.10
is run in full before any design is excluded on electrical grounds.

**The limb port interface.** [F]. A tree limb attached at a mechanical port, with its joint torques known and
its end effectors free, acts on its parent through two quantities: its articulated inertia at the port, a
symmetric 6×6 matrix with 21 independent entries, and a bias wrench of 6 entries. The packet is 27 scalars
(108 bytes in single precision). It is computed by the backward pass of the articulated-body recursion over the
limb. Type sketch (DESIGN):

```rust
/// What a free tree limb presents to its parent at a mechanical port, at one instant.
pub struct LimbPortPacket { inertia: SymPsd6, bias: WrenchIn<PortFrame> }
```

*The semidefinite caveat.* Eliminating a joint with axis s from an articulated inertia I gives
I − I·s·(sᵀI·s)⁻¹·sᵀI, and this matrix sends s to zero. With I the identity and s the first basis vector the
result is diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1). The packet is positive semidefinite, never guaranteed definite, and the type
says so. Three consequences bind every consumer:

1. the packet cannot be inverted at the port. The parent adds its own inertia before any solve, and a chain of
   massless parents is singular, which is why stage 2 refuses it;
2. the packet does not describe a limb whose end effector is in contact. Contact rows couple every limb through
   the base, and the coupled solve of §16 is the only correct path for them;
3. the packet is a value for one configuration in one world at one instant. What a substitution reuses is the
   packet's layout and the sibling limbs' kernels, not numbers.

The interface serves the tree-operator route for mechanisms too large for a dense mass matrix (§16) and the
per-limb caches of the analyses. It is not a way to decouple contact.

### 14.8 Design patches and versioning

**What it is.** [S]. A patch is a document that applies typed edits to a named parent. Owner: `frob-design`.
Verb: `design.patch` (`design.edit` remains the single-edit form).

```json
{ "schema": "frob.design.patch/1",
  "parent": "design:b3:9f2c…",
  "idempotency_key": "agent-7/turn-41/a",
  "operations": [
    {"op": "set-param", "path": "/limbs/of/of/chain/1/body/region/capsule/1", "value": "0.24 m"},
    {"op": "set-fill",  "path": "/limbs/of/of/chain/1/body", "value": {"shell": "1.5 mm"}} ],
  "constraints": { "locked": ["power_bus", "payloads", "budgets/envelope_max"],
                   "require": ["A-11", "A-13"] } }
```

- **Result.** A new immutable design version with its `DesignId`; a structural and parameter diff; the effect on
  𝔾_mech and 𝔾_obs; and the *invalidation set*: the derived products, analyses, kernels and verdicts that no
  longer apply, each with the field that invalidated it. Nothing is modified in place.
- **Stale parent.** If the named parent is not the head the caller believes it is patching, the patch refuses
  (FROB-REF-112) and returns the current head and the diff between the two.
- **Locks and requirements.** An operation that writes a locked path refuses (FROB-REF-111). The analyses named
  in `require` run before the version is created, and a refusal from any of them refuses the patch as a whole.
- **Idempotency.** The same key with the same parent and operations returns the version already created. The
  same key with different content refuses. The binding is atomic, as §32 specifies.
- **Inertia follows geometry.** A patch never edits a derived inertia. It edits the Region, the fill or the
  material, and the inertia is recomputed. A `declared` body is the exception and is edited as data.
- **One settled pose per version.** Each design version has its own settled initial state for each contact
  surface class, computed by Kinesis (§16) and cached under its `ModelId`. A comparison (§28) refuses a pair
  whose initial states were settled for only one of the two designs: a robot that starts with its wheels inside
  the floor invalidates the pair. A buildable design that then stalls is a failed trial, not an invalid one.
- **Which verdicts survive.** A verdict names the fields its evidence read. After a patch, a verdict whose
  fields are untouched is reported as still applying, with that justification, and every other verdict is listed
  for revalidation. Rollouts are not reused across a mass or geometry edit: the share of a rollout that could be
  reused exactly was assessed as about a tenth of its compute or less (HYPOTHESIS until our own dependency
  profile is measured), so the memo graph stops at analyses, step plans, kernels and scene compiles.
- **Schema versions and migration.** A schema has a major and a minor number and a canonicalization revision. A
  minor version adds optional fields. A major version needs a migration, which reads the old object, writes a
  new one with a `migrate` lineage edge and its provenance, and leaves the old object in place so that old runs
  replay against the compiler version that produced them. `.fbot` and `.fmodule` files travel in the common
  artifact envelope of §34, which carries the schema, the canonicalization revision, the payload and parent
  digests, the generator pin, the validity region and the licence.

### 14.9 The compile pipeline and its memoization

`Design` → **elaborate** (expand constructors, import modules, resolve parameters) → **validate** (ports, units,
bounds, fills) → **five functors** (C-12): kinematic tree with loop closures; collision primitives and inertia
from Regions, fills and materials; actuator and sensor binding, including the joint rows of §15.6; budget
relations; symmetry generators → **`RobotModel`**, immutable, with a `ModelId`. The model then lowers four ways:

1. to an `fs_mbd::articulated::ArticulatedModel` for the referee tier, through the airlock;
2. to packed structure-of-arrays buffers for the batched tiers, with depth levels and a path table for sparse
   Jacobians;
3. to analysis views for Atelier (symbolic kinematics for one limb, the leg-permutation group, the budget graph);
4. to a variant record inside a size bucket, so that many different robots can share one batch (§17).

Compilation is memoized per subterm by content hash, so an edit recompiles only what it touched (INV-SKEL-02).

| Stage | Product | Memo key |
|---|---|---|
| Parse and canonicalize, from either view | Typed term | Hash of the input bytes |
| Elaborate | Expanded tree with resolved parameters | Subterm hash with the digests of the modules and cards it names |
| Validate | The same tree, or a refusal | Subterm hash |
| Kinematic functor | Frames, screws, tree, closures | Hash of the subtree's mechanical fields |
| Geometric and inertial functor | Collision primitives; volume moments by fill; pseudo-inertia | Body hash with the material card's version |
| Binding functor | Actuator and sensor channels; row descriptors | Joint hash with the card versions |
| Budget functor | Budget relations and per-subtree summaries | Subtree summary hashes |
| Symmetry functor | 𝔾_mech, 𝔾_obs, representations, breakers | Canonical tree form with the digest of sensors, payloads and task terms |
| Assemble and lower | `RobotModel`, `ModelId`, the four lowerings | `ModelId` with the compiler version |

**Influence records** (ADR-SKEL-09). Each product stores the set of source paths it read, at field granularity.
The invalidation set of an edit is the set of products whose read set meets the edit's write set. Moving a
camera changes extrinsics, coverage and 𝔾_obs; renaming a body rebuilds nothing. Two designs with different
`DesignId` can therefore share a `ModelId`, and INV-SKEL-01 still holds: the model is determined by the design,
the compiler version and the card versions.

**What the memo graph covers.** Compile products, static analyses, step plans, derived kernels and scene
compiles. It does not cover rollouts (§14.8). The ceiling of the benefit is the share of an agent's
edit-to-first-step time that is spent recompiling unchanged subterms, which is small once a rollout takes
seconds; the memo graph is kept for correctness of invalidation first and for latency second.

### 14.11 Preflight: eight stages that are `design check`

**What it is.** `frob design check <design> [--task T --scene S] [--stages a-b] [--strict-data]` runs the stages
below in order and stops at the first refusal, unless `--all` asks for every finding. Stage 1 is the compile of
§14.9. Stages 2 to 8 call Atelier's analyses and live in `frob-analyze`. Every stage returns an evidence class
and the model identity, and every refusal carries ranked repairs as typed edits, which become the envelope's
`next_actions`.

| Stage | What runs | Analyses | What it refuses | Time target, 95th percentile (TARGET) | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Stage 4 needs both tests.** Counterexample: a mecanum base assembled with all four roller axes parallel. The

the missing direction is still reached by a bracket. Curvature is the right test for a snake-like crawler and

**Stage 8: the observation alias.** [F]. Inputs: the task's admitted world family, finite or quantized as the
task declares; the sensor suite with its resolution, range, field of view, rate and noise floor, which together
define a quantized transcript; a prefix horizon; and the safe-action sets the task's requirements define. The
stage searches for worlds w₁ and w₂ in the family whose transcripts agree over the prefix and whose safe-action
sets at the decision instant are disjoint. If it finds them, no policy on this sensing interface succeeds in
both, and with the two worlds equally likely and noisy sensors no policy succeeds with probability above
(1 + TV)/2, where TV is the total-variation distance between the two transcript distributions. Two labels
keep the claim honest: `aliased-passive` when the transcripts agree for the nominal action sequence, which is
an advisory that names a probing action if one separates the worlds; and `aliased-all-probes` when they agree
for every action sequence in the task's finite probe family, which is the refusal. The repair is the cheapest
entry of the sensor catalog whose transcript differs on the pair, a higher rate, or a probe.

Known answer (hand-checkable). Two range beacons at (−1, 0) and (1, 0). A robot at (0, 1) and a robot at
(0, −1) both read √2 from each, and the local information matrix is the identity at both points, so a local
observability test passes. If the task requires moving toward the positive side, the two worlds need opposite
actions: an alias. A third beacon at (0, 2) reads 1 in one world and 3 in the other and separates them whenever
the range error is below 1.

### 14.12 Import and export (`frob-io`)

- **URDF and MJCF import**, through a small in-house XML reader (elements and attributes; no entities or DTDs),
  so no third-party parser enters the core (L-5). FrankenSim's own robot models are hand-transcribed from pinned
  URDF sources and do not parse at run time (FACT), so this reader is new work. The supported subset of each
  format is documented, and every element outside it is echoed in the loss report with its file and line.
- **Macros are expanded outside.** `frob-io` does not run xacro or any other macro processor and executes no
  script found in a model. It accepts the expanded document and records its hash and the name of the tool the
  user says produced it.
- **Every import produces a loss report**: fields dropped, defaults filled, contact parameters that have no exact
  equivalent, inertias that fail C-03 with the proposed repair, actuator limits absent and filled from a card
  marked `assumed`. Imported bodies get `fill: declared` with the source's inertial values; the mass-support
  bound is applied to them only where the source states a mass Region, and is otherwise reported as not checked.
  Mimic joints become `coupling` transmissions.
- **Export** to URDF and MJCF for use elsewhere, with the same loss report in reverse: closed loops,
  transmissions and couplings, sensor latency and clocks, contact laws, the power bus, fills (the export writes
  explicit inertials), levers, budgets, symmetry assertions and controller assumptions have no full equivalent
  and are listed. glTF export for visuals goes through FrankenSim's GLB exporter (FACT: `fs-geom` exports GLB).
- **Round trips compare meaning, not bytes**: forward kinematics, mass properties and the map of identifiers.
- **SDF** import is added through a feature matrix when a consumer needs it. **OpenUSD** import is Frontier.
  Only the core specification is settled and robotics schemas are still vendor extensions (FACT, Appendix H), so
  it waits for a real consumer.

## 15. Sinew: actuators, transmissions, power

### 15.1 What it owns, and why actuators are first-class

`frob-actuate` owns the actuator, battery and transmission cards (`.fcard`); the model ladder; the joint rows
that carry stiction and backlash into the solve; the power bus and the battery; the port-Hamiltonian form of
every element and the energy accounting at its ports; and the static functions (torque, speed and voltage
envelope, thermal steady state, bus feasibility) that A-11 and stage 6 of the preflight call. Verbs:
`actuator.list`, `actuator.show`, `actuator.check`, `actuator.envelope`, `actuator.thermal`, `actuator.bus`.

Recent sim-to-real studies agree on where the gap lives: actuator dynamics. Identifying armature, joint friction
and delay gave 46% more travel distance and 75% less rotational deviation in one 2026 study; static friction is
commonly missing from randomization; joint friction and actuator delay were called particularly critical for a
point-foot biped (FACT, Appendix H). A simulator with exact rigid-body dynamics and an ideal torque source is
precise about the wrong thing.

Electrical and thermal actuator models exist in current simulators on the CPU and on the GPU (FACT, Appendix H).
This chapter claims parity for them, not novelty. What it adds is a card with provenance on every field, a port
energy balance with a recorded residual, friction and backlash as rows of the solve, and static functions that
refuse a design before any simulation is spent on it.

### 15.2 Decisions

- **ADR-SINEW-01: the card is the unit of actuator knowledge.** It is immutable and content-addressed, every
  field carries a provenance and an uncertainty, and no actuator constant lives in code. Reason: randomization,
  strict-data refusals and calibration (§30) all need to know where a number came from.
- **ADR-SINEW-02: stiction and backlash are rows of the solve, not forces computed before it.** Reason: a
  friction force computed from the previous velocity chatters about zero and cannot hold a load at rest; a row
  with a bounded multiplier can. A dead zone computed outside the solve cannot produce the impact when the gap
  closes.
- **ADR-SINEW-03: row bounds are frozen within a solve.** The breakaway bound and its load-dependent part are
  evaluated from the state at the start of the step. Reason: a bound that falls with speed makes the friction
  potential non-convex, and the fast tiers rely on a strictly convex problem (C-06).
- **ADR-SINEW-04: winding resistance and magnet flux depend on temperature from L2 upward, and the static
  thermal check uses the temperature-dependent law at every level.** Reason: §15.7.
- **ADR-SINEW-05: three-phase machines are written in power-invariant dq coordinates, and a card must state its
  current convention.** Reason: FrankenSim's field-oriented controller uses power-invariant normalization (FACT:
  `frankensim/crates/fs-control/src/synchronous.rs`); in these coordinates the torque constant equals the
  back-EMF constant, which is a passivity check on the card; and a torque constant quoted per peak phase ampere,
  per RMS phase ampere or per q-axis ampere differs by factors of √(3/2), √2 and √3, so a missing convention is a
  silent torque error of 22% to 73%.
- **ADR-SINEW-06: a transmission loses energy in every quadrant.** Efficiency is a pair, one value for each
  direction of power flow, or a loss law. A single multiplier applied in all quadrants creates energy when the
  load drives the motor (§15.9).
- **ADR-SINEW-07: stiff electrical and thermal states take implicit rational updates on the batched tiers, and
  slow states run on their own rational clocks.** Reason: the electrical time constant L/R is below a physics
  tick, an explicit update is unstable there, and batched kernels use no transcendental built-ins (§35).
- **ADR-SINEW-08: thermal state starts from a duty history, not from ambient.** Reason: thermal time constants
  are minutes and episodes are seconds, so an episode that starts cold never meets the limit that decides the
  design (§15.7).

### 15.3 The actuator card

An `ActuatorCard` is immutable, content-addressed data: torque constant, back-EMF constant, winding resistance
and inductance, rotor inertia, gear ratio and efficiency map, Coulomb, viscous and Stribeck friction, peak and
continuous torque, speed limit, bus voltage and current limits, thermal resistances and capacitances, mass,
envelope geometry, encoder resolution, driver control rate and latency. **Every field carries a provenance**
(`datasheet` with a citation, `measured`, `generic`, `assumed`) and an uncertainty. The starter catalog contains
generic classes (small, medium and large quasi-direct-drive; hobby servo; strain-wave; hub motor) marked
`generic`. Real parts enter only with a cited datasheet (OPEN-SINEW-1).

The fields added to that list, by group:

| Electrical | `r_phase` at `t_ref`; `alpha_r`, the temperature coefficient of resistance; `l_d`, `l_q`; `flux_linkage`; `alpha_flux`, the temperature coefficient of magnet flux; `iron_loss` coefficients | Temperature-dependent copper loss (§15.7); the voltage limit and field weakening (§15.8) |

`actuator.check` runs three checks when a card is admitted and again when it is bound to a joint: the torque and

nonnegative in all four quadrants (FROB-REF-130 otherwise); and the stated peak and continuous torques are
reachable inside the card's own voltage, current and thermal limits.

### 15.4 The model ladder

Each level is a Component with shaft, electrical and thermal ports. The level is part of `ModelId`.

| Level | Contents | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Ideal torque source. Used only by mathematical tests. | [S] |
| L1 | Torque-speed envelope from the bus: i ≤ min(I_max, (V_lim − k_e·N·ω)/R); output torque η·N·k_t·i minus friction; reflected inertia N²·J_rotor added to the joint; first-order delay; position-velocity loop at the driver rate. Friction enters as the stiction row of §15.6. V_lim is the bus voltage for a DC machine and the modulation limit of §15.8 for a three-phase machine. | [S] |
| L2 | Winding current dynamics; resistance and flux as functions of winding temperature; a two-node thermal network with torque derating; the voltage limit in the dq plane with field weakening; bus voltage from the two-RC battery; state of charge. | [S] |
| L3 | The rotor as its own coordinate, with backlash rows ([S]); series elasticity as an added degree of freedom and cable and belt compliance ([F]). | [S] / [F] |
| L4 | Hydraulic actuation. | [M] |

Two options sit beside the ladder and are also part of `ModelId`: battery ageing between episodes ([F], §15.10),
and a rotor port for flight, which is a lumped element with thrust c_T·ρ·A·(Ω·r)² and a reaction torque about
its axis ([M], off the critical path, used only by the hybrid variant of §14.10).

L1 is the default for training because the literature above says it is the minimum that transfers, and because
reflected inertia also conditions the contact solve (it adds to the diagonal of A in C-06). L1 neglects the
reactive voltage drop ω_e·L_q·i_q. A card on which L_q·I_max exceeds one tenth of the flux linkage is flagged
(DESIGN: a screening threshold), and envelope claims for it require L2.

### 15.5 The port-Hamiltonian form of each element

Every element is written as ẋ = (J(x) − R(x))·∇H(x) + G(x)·u with output y = G(x)ᵀ·∇H(x), where J is
skew-symmetric and R is positive semidefinite. Then Ḣ = uᵀy − ∇Hᵀ·R·∇H: stored energy changes by the power at
the ports minus what the element dissipates, and dissipation is never negative. Elements are joined by
power-conserving junctions (§14.6), so the property survives composition. This is the form FrankenSim's
`fs-phs` implements (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-phs`), and it is what makes the checks of §15.11 statements
about structure instead of bookkeeping.

| Element | State | Stored energy H | Structure and dissipation | Ports |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Winding | Flux linkage φ = L·i (per axis in dq) | φ²/(2L) | R = R_w(T) | Electrical (v, i) |
| Electromechanical conversion | — | — | A gyrator of modulus k: τ = k·i and e = k·ω. Lossless. | Couples winding and rotor |
| Rotor | Angular momentum p = J_r·ω | p²/(2J_r) | Viscous b | Shaft |
| Gear | — | — | A transformer of modulus N, with the loss element of §15.9 in series | Two shafts |
| Coupling transmission | — | — | A transformer with a matrix modulus T: q̇ = T·θ̇ and τ_actuator = Tᵀ·τ_joint | Several shafts |
| Stiction | — | — | A monotone set-valued resistive relation between torque and speed | Shaft |
| Backlash | Relative angle δ | Zero inside the gap; ½·k_tooth·e² beyond it on compliant tiers, with e the excess of the magnitude of δ over β/2 | Tooth damping; an impact law on the referee | Two shafts |
| Series spring | Deflection | ½·k_s·δ² | Internal damping | Two shafts |
| Thermal node | Internal energy U = C·(T − T_ref) | U | Conductances to neighbours and ambient | Thermal (T, Q̇); receives every loss as heat |
| Battery | Charge q; polarization charges q₁, q₂ | ∫OCV dq + q₁²/(2C₁) + q₂²/(2C₂) | R₀, R₁, R₂ | Electrical |
| Bus | Bus capacitor charge | q_b²/(2C_b) | Harness resistance | A node: equal voltages, currents sum to zero |
| Driver | Controller states, which are signals | — | Modulation as a lossless transformer of duty; switching loss as a resistance | Two electrical, one signal |

Worked instance, the brushed or q-axis motor: x = (φ, p), H = φ²/(2L) + p²/(2J_r), ∇H = (i, ω),
J = [[0, −k], [k, 0]], R = diag(R_w, b), u = (v, −τ_load). The two rows read L·di/dt = v − R_w·i − k·ω and
J_r·dω/dt = k·i − b·ω − τ_load, and Ḣ = v·i − τ_load·ω − R_w·i² − b·ω².

With the thermal nodes included the composite is lossless up to its ports: the R terms reappear as heat flowing
into thermal storage, and the only energy that leaves is mechanical work at the shaft and heat to ambient. That
is the first law for the actuator. The second law is a per-step test: with temperatures in kelvin, the entropy
production P_loss/T_w + G·(T_w − T_amb)²/(T_w·T_amb) is never negative. Temperature, not entropy, is the stored
variable of a node.

On the referee the elements step with FrankenSim's discrete-gradient integrators, which keep the power identity
in the discrete step (FACT: stated in `fs-phs/src/circuit/battery.rs` for the battery). On the batched tiers the
arithmetic is re-derived as kernels: the current update is i₊ = (i + (h/L)·(v − k·ω))/(1 + h·R(T)/L), thermal
nodes advance on a slower rational clock and receive the heat accumulated since their last tick in a compensated
sum, and the resistance seen by the fast update is the temperature at the last thermal tick. Each of these
choices leaves a residual, which GATE-SINEW-02 bounds.

### 15.6 Stiction and backlash as rows in the solve

**What it is.** [S]. Sinew emits row descriptors from the card; Kinesis assembles them with the contact rows
(§16), and the cones are Marrow's and Traction's (§13, §18). Two row kinds are added to the joint limits that
were already rows.

**The stiction row.** For joint velocity v, step h and bound τ_b, the row's potential on the convex tiers is

  ℓ(v) = h·τ_b·ψ_ε(v),  with ψ_ε(v) = v²/(2ε) for |v| ≤ ε and |v| − ε/2 otherwise.

Its impulse −∂ℓ/∂v lies in [−h·τ_b, h·τ_b]: it balances any smaller applied impulse with the joint almost at
rest and saturates when the joint slides. The bound is

  τ_b = τ_c + (τ_s − τ_c)·σ(v⁻/v_s) + c_load·|τ_transmitted⁻|,

with Coulomb level τ_c, breakaway level τ_s, Stribeck velocity v_s, the shape σ(x) = 1/(1 + x²) or exp(−x²) as
the card says, a load coefficient per quadrant, and the superscript marking values at the start of the step
(ADR-SINEW-03). The rational shape is the default on batched tiers because it needs no exponential. Viscous
friction is not in the row; it is implicit damping on the diagonal of A. On the referee the same law is solved
set-valued, with ε = 0, by FrankenSim's implicit friction step (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-tribo/src/stribeck_friction.rs` has `solve_implicit_friction` and
`SetValuedFrictionStep`).

What the convex form costs is stated and measured: a joint holding a torque τ below its bound creeps at
ε·τ/τ_b, so over a time t it drifts by at most ε·t; and because the bound is frozen within a step, a stick-slip
oscillation can depend on h. Both appear in the fidelity ledger against the referee. The row's Hessian term is
h·τ_b/ε inside the kink and zero outside, so the objective stays a sum of positive-semidefinite terms and T-08
is unaffected.

**The backlash rows.** Backlash needs the rotor as its own coordinate (L3). With rotor angle θ, joint angle q
and ratio N, the relative angle is δ = θ/N − q and the two rows are the unilateral gaps

  β/2 − δ ≥ 0  and  β/2 + δ ≥ 0,

each with a nonnegative multiplier, exactly as a joint limit on δ. On the convex tiers they are compliant with
the card's tooth stiffness and a dissipation time; on the referee they are hard, with the card's restitution,
zero by default. Inside the gap the rotor and the link are uncoupled, which is the physics that matters: the
motor accelerates freely across the gap and arrives with a relative speed, and the encoder on the motor side no
longer reports the joint.

**Rotor elimination.** Twelve backlash joints would raise the velocity count of a quadruped from 18 to 30. They
do not raise the size of the dense factorization. Under the usual flexible-joint simplification, in which a
rotor's kinetic energy is that of its own spin, the rotor block of A is diagonal and each backlash or
motor-side stiction row couples one rotor to one joint. The rotor block is then eliminated in closed form: its
Schur complement changes only diagonal entries of the link block, at a cost linear in the number of rotors. The
simplification neglects the gyroscopic torque J_r·N·q̇·Ω of a spinning rotor carried by a link turning at Ω;
with J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², N = 9, q̇ = 20 rad/s and Ω = 5 rad/s that is 0.09 N·m (illustrative), under one percent
of the joint torques of such an actuator. It is a HYPOTHESIS that this stays negligible for the cards on file;
the test is the referee with full coupling against the reduced model, recorded in the fidelity ledger.

**Known answers** (hand-checkable; the gate is GATE-SINEW-04):

| Case | Expected |
|---|---|
| A torque ramp r·t on a joint at rest with breakaway τ_s | Motion begins at t = τ_s/r, within one step |
| A mass on a spring of stiffness k with Coulomb friction τ_c, released from rest | The amplitude falls by 4·τ_c/k per cycle, and the motion ends inside the band of half-width τ_s/k |
| A held load τ below the bound on a convex tier | Drift of at most ε·t |
| A motor reversal smaller than the gap | Zero output motion |

With N = 9, J_r = 10⁻⁴ kg·m², J₂ = 0.02 kg·m², Δω = 2 rad/s (illustrative): 0.577 rad/s and 0.0115 J |

### 15.7 Winding temperature, resistance, and thermal runaway

**What it is.** [S]. Copper resistance rises with temperature, R(T) = R₀·[1 + α·(T − T₀)], so copper loss at a
given current rises as the winding heats, which heats it further. With one thermal node of capacity C and
conductance G to ambient, at constant current I:

  C·dT/dt = I²·R₀·[1 + α·(T − T₀)] − G·(T − T_amb).

Write s = I²·R₀·α, the extra watts of loss per kelvin, and P_amb for the loss at ambient temperature.

- **If s < G** there is a stable equilibrium at T = T_amb + P_amb/(G − s), approached with time constant
  C/(G − s). Both are larger than the constant-resistance values P_amb/G and C/G by the factor 1/(1 − s/G).
- **If s ≥ G** there is no equilibrium. The temperature grows without bound at rate (s − G)/C until a limit or
  a protection acts. This is thermal runaway, and the current at which it begins is I_crit = √(G/(R₀·α)).
- **In general**, for any loss curve P(T), an operating point has a stable thermal equilibrium if and only if
  P(T) meets the removal line G·(T − T_amb) at a temperature where dP/dT < G. For a network of nodes with the
  loss in the winding node, the condition is that the conductance matrix with s subtracted from the winding's
  diagonal entry stays positive definite; for a winding and a housing in series that reads
  s < G_wh·G_ha/(G_wh + G_ha).
- **A controller that holds torque makes it worse.** Magnet flux falls with temperature,
  k(T) = k₀·[1 + α_B·(T − T₀)] with α_B negative, so holding torque needs more current as the motor heats and
  the loss becomes P(T) = I₀²·R₀·[1 + α·(T − T₀)]/[1 + α_B·(T − T₀)]², where I₀ is the current that gives the
  torque when cold. This curve is convex, so the equilibrium vanishes at a tangency that lies well below the
  estimate from its slope at T₀. The linear formula is not conservative here, and `actuator.thermal` solves the
  tangency.

**Worked card (illustrative; every number derived for this plan, none measured).** A medium quasi-direct-drive
actuator with R₀ = 0.20 Ω at T₀ = 25 °C, α = 0.00393 K⁻¹, G = 0.80 W/K, C = 120 J/K, ambient 25 °C and a winding
limit of 155 °C.

| Question | Constant-resistance model | Temperature-dependent model |
|---|---|---|
| Steady state at a constant 20 A (80 W of loss when cold) | Rise 80/0.80 = 100 K, so 125 °C: inside the limit | s = 0.314 W/K; rise 80/(0.80 − 0.314) = 164.7 K, so 190 °C: outside the limit. The loss at equilibrium is 132 W. |
| Time constant at 20 A | 150 s | 247 s |
| Time from cold to the 155 °C limit at 20 A | Never | 385 s |
| Largest continuous current for the limit | √(0.80·130/0.20) = 22.8 A | 22.8/√(1 + 0.00393·130) = 18.6 A, which is 19% lower |
| Current at which no equilibrium exists | None | I_crit = √(0.80/(0.20·0.00393)) = 31.9 A |
| At 35 A | An equilibrium 306 K above ambient; the limit is reached at 83 s | No equilibrium; growth rate 0.00136 s⁻¹; the limit is reached at 61 s |
| Holding torque with α_B = −0.0011 K⁻¹ | 22.8 A cold-equivalent | Limit reached at 15.9 A cold-equivalent, 30% lower; no equilibrium above 16.5 A, where the slope estimate √(G/(R₀·(α − 2·α_B))) says 25.5 A |

Three things follow, and each is a mechanism.

1. **The static check uses the corrected law** (ADR-SINEW-04). A-11 and stage 6 compute the equilibrium, or its
   absence, for each required operating point and for the root-mean-square current of the nominal gait, and
   refuse with FROB-REF-132. In the card above a design sized to hold 20 A passes the constant-resistance check
   and is refused by the corrected one.
2. **The continuous rating and the runaway threshold are close together under torque control.** In the card they
   are 15.9 A and 16.5 A cold-equivalent, four percent apart. A design that sits just inside its continuous
   rating has almost no thermal margin against a slightly heavier payload, and `actuator.thermal` reports the
   margin to the tangency, not only the equilibrium temperature.
3. **Episodes are too short to see any of this.** In 20 s from cold at 20 A the winding of the card rises by
   164.7·(1 − e^(−20/247)) = 12.8 K. A policy trained and evaluated on episodes that start at ambient never
   meets derating. Thermal state is therefore initialized from a declared duty history (ADR-SINEW-08): `cold`,
   `soaked(T)`, or `steady(task)`, the equilibrium for the task's mean loss from the closed form above.
   Evaluation defaults to `steady(task)`; training draws from the three.

Torque derating on the approach to a limit uses authored bands, as FrankenSim's controller does (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-control/src/thermal.rs`), and a trip latches until reset. FrankenSim's winding model
derives resistance from material and geometry and infers no temperature law (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-em/src/winding.rs`), so the temperature dependence is new in `frob-actuate` on every tier.

### 15.8 The voltage limit and field weakening

**What it is.** [S]. In power-invariant dq coordinates, with electrical speed ω_e = p·ω for p pole pairs:

  v_d = R·i_d − ω_e·L_q·i_q,   v_q = R·i_q + ω_e·(L_d·i_d + λ),   τ = p·[λ·i_q + (L_d − L_q)·i_d·i_q],

subject to v_d² + v_q² ≤ V_lim² and i_d² + i_q² ≤ I_max². For a three-phase bridge in its linear range V_lim is
the radius of the circle inscribed in the inverter's voltage hexagon, V_bus/√2 in these coordinates; for a DC
machine it is V_bus. The referee enforces the exact hexagon, as FrankenSim's controller does (FACT:
`fs-control/src/synchronous.rs`); the batched tiers use the inscribed circle, which is conservative by at most
1 − √3/2, or 13.4%, of voltage at a hexagon vertex.

Neglecting R, the voltage constraint is an ellipse in the current plane,

  (λ + L_d·i_d)² + (L_q·i_q)² ≤ (V_lim/ω_e)²,

centred at (−λ/L_d, 0) and shrinking as speed rises. The available torque at a speed is the maximum of τ over
the intersection of this ellipse with the current circle. Below base speed the intersection contains the
point (0, I_max). Above it, negative i_d moves the operating point toward the ellipse's centre: field weakening
trades torque for speed. `actuator.envelope` returns the boundary of that intersection as torque against speed,
at a stated winding temperature and bus voltage, and L2 applies it every step with the bus voltage of §15.10.

Closed forms for the non-salient lossless case (L_d = L_q = L, R = 0), which are the known answers of
GATE-SINEW-06:

| Quantity | Expression | Illustrative card: λ = 0.010 V·s, L = 100 µH, I_max = 40 A, V_bus = 48 V |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage limit | V_lim = V_bus/√2 | 33.9 V |
| Base speed, the last speed with full torque | V_lim/√(λ² + (L·I_max)²) | 3,151 rad/s electrical |
| No-load speed without field weakening | V_lim/λ | 3,394 rad/s |
| Currents on the limit above base speed | i_d = [(V_lim/ω_e)² − λ² − (L·I_max)²]/(2·λ·L), i_q = √(I_max² − i_d²) | At 1.5 times base speed: i_d = −32.2 A, i_q = 23.7 A, so 59% of peak torque |
| Top speed with field weakening | V_lim/(λ − L·I_max) if λ > L·I_max, otherwise unbounded in this model | 5,657 rad/s |

At 1.5 times base speed the card's motor delivers 59% of its peak torque with field weakening and none without
it, because that speed is above the no-load speed. A model without the ellipse gets fast swing-leg motions and
high-speed wheel bases wrong in one direction or the other, depending on which simplification it made.

### 15.9 Transmissions

- **Four-quadrant loss** [S]. With ω_motor = N·ω_joint: when the motor drives, τ_joint = η_f·N·τ_motor; when
  the load drives, τ_motor = η_b·τ_joint/N. Both lose energy. The pair is implemented as the load-dependent
  part of the stiction bound of §15.6, with coefficient (1 − η_f)/η_f on the transmitted joint torque when
  motoring and 1 − η_b when back-driven, so there is one friction mechanism and not two.
- **Counterexample: one multiplier in all quadrants creates energy.** Take η = 0.8 and N = 10, and let the load
  drive the joint with 10 N·m at 1 rad/s, which is 10 W in. The single-multiplier law τ_joint = η·N·τ_motor
  gives τ_motor = 1.25 N·m at 10 rad/s: 12.5 W arrives at the motor, and 2.5 W came from nowhere. The correct law
  gives 0.8 N·m and 8 W, with 2 W lost. The fixture refuses under FROB-REF-130.
- **Self-locking** [S]. A transmission with η_b ≤ 0 cannot be back-driven; the joint holds until the motor
  assists. For a screw with lead angle a and friction angle φ the efficiencies are η_f = tan a/tan(a + φ) and
  η_b = tan(a − φ)/tan a, and the screw locks exactly when φ ≥ a. This is the known answer for helical joints.
- **Coupling transmissions** [S]. Differential ankles, wrists and waists drive several joints from several
  motors through a constant matrix: q̇ = T·θ̇ and τ_actuator = Tᵀ·τ_joint, which conserves power. The rotor
  inertias reflect into joint space as T⁻ᵀ·diag(J_r)·T⁻¹, which is not diagonal in general. Known answer: for a
  symmetric differential with q₁ = (θ₁ + θ₂)/2 and q₂ = (θ₁ − θ₂)/2 and rotor inertias J₁ and J₂, the reflected
  inertia is [[J₁ + J₂, J₁ − J₂], [J₁ − J₂, J₁ + J₂]]: equal rotors decouple, unequal ones do not.
- **Linkage transmissions** [S]. A ratio that depends on position is a closure in the design term (§14.3), and
  its Jacobian is the transformer modulus at each configuration.
- **Belts, cables and series elasticity** [F]. An added coordinate per compliant element, with the spring row of
  §15.5. The ceiling of the benefit is fidelity on designs whose resonance lies inside the control bandwidth;
  for stiff quasi-direct drives it may be nil.

### 15.10 The power bus and the battery

**The bus.** [S]. Every member actuator's electrical port joins one node: voltages equal, currents summing to
zero, with the harness resistance in series with the battery. The bus is why limbs that the tree separates are
coupled, and why an edit to it is global (§14.6).

**The battery.** [S]. A two-RC equivalent circuit with open-circuit voltage against state of charge z:

  V = OCV(z) − R₀·I − V₁ − V₂,   dVₖ/dt = −Vₖ/(Rₖ·Cₖ) + I/Cₖ,   dz/dt = −I/Q,

with I positive on discharge, R₀ a function of cell temperature, and Q the capacity. The two pairs give the fast
and slow parts of voltage sag and recovery. FrankenSim's battery has a series resistance, one polarization
pair, a bus capacitor and a bounded state of charge that refuses on exhaustion, and states that ageing, thermal
drift and hysteresis need separate laws (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-phs/src/circuit/battery.rs`). The second
pair and the temperature dependence are therefore added here, on the referee by composing a further RC element
and on the batched tiers as kernels.

**Brownout is a feasibility question with a closed form.** Actuators under current control draw power, not
current. A bus with source voltage E = OCV − V₁ − V₂ and series resistance R can deliver power P only if
P ≤ E²/(4·R), and then V = [E + √(E² − 4·R·P)]/2. With E = 48 V and R = 0.08 Ω (illustrative) the ceiling is
7,200 W, and at 1,800 W the bus sits at 44.8 V and carries 40.2 A. A demand above the ceiling has no operating
point: the bus voltage collapses, the driver's undervoltage policy acts, and the step records a brownout event.
It never completes at the requested power. Stage 6 checks the ceiling for the peak demand of the nominal gait at
the end-of-discharge voltage and at the cold-temperature resistance, and refuses with FROB-REF-133. An exhausted
battery supplies nothing: state of charge is bounded, exhaustion is an event (FROB-REF-135 if a caller asks for
power beyond it), and no kernel clips it.

**Regeneration.** A braking actuator returns power to the bus. The driver's policy decides what happens to it:
it charges the battery up to the charge-current limit, the excess heats a shunt on the shared sink, or the
driver clips the braking torque. The three are different energy paths and different mechanical behaviours, and
each is accounted on its own ledger line (§15.11).

**Ageing.** [F]. Two slow states, capacity fade f_Q and resistance growth f_R, with Q = Q₀·(1 − f_Q) and
R₀ = R₀₀·(1 + f_R). They advance between episodes, never within a step, by a semi-empirical law in charge
throughput and calendar time whose coefficients are card fields with provenance. With no card data the states
are user-set constants. Ageing answers lifecycle questions (§31): whether the design that passes stage 6 when
new still passes at eighty percent capacity and higher resistance.

### 15.11 Energy at the ports

Because an actuator is a port component, its balance is checked every step: electrical energy in equals
mechanical work out plus copper loss i²R plus friction loss plus gear loss plus the change in stored magnetic and
thermal energy. The balance residual is recorded, and it is what lets the energy ledger of §29 report a
measured residual instead of an estimate. The claim is made only once GATE-SINEW-02 has recorded that residual.

Four rules keep the account from lying.

- **Heat that is stored is stored energy.** Copper, iron, friction and gear losses flow into thermal nodes. They
  are listed for attribution and are not added again as losses: the energy that leaves the system is mechanical
  work at the joint and heat to ambient. Counting stored heat twice produces a false defect.
- **The identity, per step and per element:** decrease of chemical energy = mechanical work out + increase of
  magnetic, rotor-kinetic, elastic, polarization and thermal stored energy + heat to ambient + residual.
- **Braking modes are separate lines:** motoring; regeneration to the battery; dissipation in a shunt; clipped
  braking. A design that depends on regeneration the bus cannot accept shows it here.
- **The first offending event is named.** When the residual of a step exceeds its tolerance, the ledger reports
  the earliest step and the element whose local balance failed, not only the episode total.

### 15.12 What agreement between tiers cannot show

Every tier reads the same card. If the card is wrong, the referee, the CPU batch tier and both GPU tiers are
wrong together, and their agreement says nothing about it. Three defences are in place and none is a proof.
Randomization draws each field from its stated uncertainty, so a verdict is a statement over the card's band. A
verdict that changes sign inside that band is labeled *card-conditional* by the sensitivity analysis (A-16) and
names the field that decides it. Under `--strict-data` a verdict resting on an `assumed` field is refused. The
only evidence about the card itself is measurement, which is the business of §30.
