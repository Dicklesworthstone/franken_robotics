## 16. Kinesis: dynamics on three tiers

Crates: `frob-dyn`. The step's kernels live in `frob-kernels`, which Spark owns (§21, §34). Kinesis calls
`frob-contact` (§18), `frob-phylum` (§17), `frob-actuate` (§15) and, through the airlock `frob-fsim`, FrankenSim's
`fs-mbd`, `fs-contact`, `fs-time`, `fs-query`, `fs-eproc`, `fs-uq` and `fs-rand`.

### 16.1 What Kinesis owns

Kinesis owns the advance of state in time and the rules that say what a computed trajectory may be cited as:

- the three tiers and their step profiles (§16.3);
- the step itself: a transaction with located events on the referee and on the strict profile (§16.5), and the
  ten-stage lockstep step on the throughput tiers (§16.6);
- the integrators (§16.7);
- the trust ladder: the fidelity ledger, the promotion rule and its tier correction (§16.8), rank agreement
  between tiers and model-form rank checks (§16.9), the audit for energy created by the simulator (§16.10), and
  the background referee with its coverage figures (§16.11).

Kinesis does not own contact geometry or contact laws (§18), the packing of different robots into one batch
(§17), device back ends (§21), clocks and packet queues (§20), or the storage of telemetry and of the energy
ledger (§29). It calls them and states what it needs from each.

Two units recur and are never mixed. A **world-step** is one physics step of one world. An **env-step** is one
control interval of one world, that is, all the world-steps between two decisions. A rate is stated in one of the
two with the decimation beside it (§36).

### 16.5 The transactional step [S]

The referee and the `strict` profile advance each world by one **transaction per accepted physics interval**
(one base interval, defined below). A transaction either commits a complete, checked interval or leaves no
trace.

**What is in the restore set.** A checkpoint taken at the start of a transaction, and restored in full on every
retry, contains: the physical state (q, v); actuator, thermal and battery states; contact memory (witness pairs,
contact frames, tangential memory, the warm-start impulses and the active set); per-contact film thickness
(§18.8); clock phases and **packet queues** (§20); controller and estimator states; the ordinals of every
sample-stamped random draw; terrain edits; the accumulators of the energy ledger; event counters; and the command
cursor. Omitting any of these makes a retried interval differ from a first attempt, which INV-KIN-02 forbids.

**The fence.** Committed state carries a monotone `commit_epoch`. Controllers, sensor samplers, telemetry and
snapshots read a named epoch and nothing else. A candidate interval is built beside the committed state and
becomes visible only when the epoch advances, in one step. On the lockstep tiers the same rule holds at the
kernel-batch boundary: a host reader sees a completed batch or the previous one.

**Time.** Time is rational (§11). The physics grid and the clock boundaries of §20 together cut time into **base
intervals** [t0, t1) of length ℓ₀ ≤ h. That partition is fixed by the schedule and never by the state. Inside a
base interval, sub-intervals are dyadic: a split may fall only at t0 + ℓ₀·k/2ᴸ for L ≤ L_max. DESIGN:
L_max = 16, which at ℓ₀ = 2 ms resolves 31 ns, so an approach at 3 m/s is located to within 0.1 µm. An event that
cannot be bracketed without exceeding L_max, or more than E_max = 64 events inside one base interval, ends the
transaction with `FROB-REF-220` instead of an approximation (the guard against accumulation points of impacts).
Both constants are OPEN-KIN-5.

```text
DESIGN  transaction(X_committed, [t0, t1))        # t1 = min(t0 + h, next clock boundary of §20)
T1  S ← checkpoint(X_committed)                   # the whole restore set above
T2  for attempt in 0 ..= N_retry:                 # N_retry = 3; attempt selects a numerical plan only
T3    X ← restore(S); work ← [(t0, t1)]
T4    while work is not empty:
        (a, b) ← work.pop_front()
T4.1    read pending actions, external forces and the noise increment for (a, b)   # refinement law below
T4.2    broad phase on swept bounds over (a, b); narrow phase at a returns, per candidate pair:
        features, normal, witnesses, material pair, signed gap g and a bound ε_g on its error   (§18.4)
T4.3    t* ← earliest time in (a, b] at which a guard may change sign:
        touchdown, lift-off, stick↔slip, joint limit, backlash closure, actuator protection, clock boundary.
        Conservative advancement on gap ÷ bounded approach speed; bisection on the dyadic grid;
        on the referee, fs-time event location and fs-contact certified CCD.
T4.4    if t* < b: (a, b) ← (a, t*); work.push_front((t*, b_old))
        if the split would pass L_max or the event count would pass E_max: refuse FROB-REF-220
T4.5    advance smooth or sustained-contact dynamics from a to b with the active set held fixed
T4.6    at b, if an event fired: evaluate every event row on the one common pre-event state; group the
        events that fall in the same finest dyadic cell; partition bodies and rows into independent islands;
        solve the coupled impulse once per island; apply reaction impulses to dynamic props; credit
        boundary work from prescribed surfaces to the energy ledger
T4.7    invalidate affected witnesses, redetect, migrate tangential memory (§18.4)
T5    checks: sub-intervals sum to (t0, t1) exactly; solver residuals; joint limits and closures inside
      tolerance; group-membership error; energy-ledger residual; path admission (no missed contact on the
      swept path, inside ε_g); loads and currents inside admitted domains; event count
T6    if every check passes: COMMIT (advance commit_epoch; publish state, impulses, sensor inputs,
      diagnostics and the attempt index together) and return
T7  after the last attempt: restore(S); mark the world Faulted with cause numerical-fault (FROB-REF-221);
    publish nothing from the failed interval
```

**Retries change numerical parameters only** (ADR-KIN-05). DESIGN, the ladder: attempt 0 is the declared plan;
attempt 1 halves the largest admitted sub-interval and forces bisection for event location; attempt 2 tightens
the solver tolerance by ten, doubles the iteration cap and clears the warm start; attempt 3 switches the island
solver (semismooth Newton to the fixed-point cone iteration of §18.5). The attempt that committed is part of the
result. The ladder is a deterministic function of state, so a replay repeats the same attempts.

**A numerical failure is not a physical failure.** A world that exhausts its retries ends with
`numerical-fault`, which the failure taxonomy (§29) keeps apart from `fall`, `slip` or `collision`. A design is
never scored as having fallen because a solver gave up, and summary statistics state how many worlds were
excluded for this cause (§35).

**Refinement law for random inputs.** A random input drawn over an interval must mean the same thing when the
interval is split. A sensor draw scheduled at a sample instant keeps its original stamp and is not affected. A
driver that acts over the interval (a Brownian disturbance force, process noise in an actuator) is defined on the
dyadic tree of the base interval: the root increment over length ℓ₀ is S = √ℓ₀·Z₀; a node with increment S over
length ℓ gives its left child S/2 + √(ℓ/4)·Z and its right child S minus that, with Z a standard normal keyed by
`(WorldAddr, base_interval, stream_id, level, index)` in the counter-based streams of §35. Each child then has
variance ℓ/2 and the two are uncorrelated, which is the law of Brownian increments. Because every node is a pure
function of its address, no noise state needs storing: a retry, a restart from a checkpoint and a migration to
another device all read the same path, and a retry can refine the path but can never redraw it.

Counterexample, hand-checkable: let the input be a fair sign, and let a step be rejected whenever the sign is
negative and then redrawn once. The accepted sign is positive with probability ½ + ½·½ = ¾. Rejection that
redraws biases the law; this fixture is in the negative-control registry.

A driver with no refinement law registered is refused under a transactional profile (`FROB-REF-223`). A
higher-order stochastic integrator would need a joint law for its iterated integrals and is not offered.

**Coupled impulses per island.** Simultaneous events are solved together, never in an imposed order, because the
order can change the outgoing velocity and the basin of a gait. Trot, bound and pronk have simultaneous
touchdowns by symmetry and are ordinary inputs, not refusals. With M the mass matrix, J the event rows and A any
independent bilateral rows that hold through the event, the contact-space inverse mass is W = J·P·Jᵀ with
P = M⁻¹ − M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹A·M⁻¹, and W = J·M⁻¹·Jᵀ when there are none. For a moving bilateral constraint
A·v⁺ = b the freely predicted velocity v_f is first corrected by M⁻¹Aᵀ(A·M⁻¹·Aᵀ)⁻¹(b − A·v_f), and the tangent
response P·Jᵀp is added to the corrected velocity.

Counterexample, hand-checkable: two 1 kg sliders joined by a rigid tie reach a wall together at 1 m/s. Stopping
both needs a wall impulse of 2 N·s. Solving the wall contact for the first slider alone gives 1 N·s; imposing the
tie afterwards leaves 1 kg·m/s shared by 2 kg, so both move into the wall at 0.5 m/s.

Redundant contacts can admit several impulse distributions with the same motion. The transaction compares and
reports observable post-event motion, total impulse and work, and never reports a pressure distribution inferred
from one resultant wrench. A configuration with no solution or several distinct motions (the Painlevé rod of
§18.16) is reported as indeterminate (`FROB-REF-224`).

**Units.** Impulses are in N·s and forces in N. A tolerance is typed by the quantity it bounds; one threshold
applied to both is a dimension error that the kernel type checker rejects (C-14).

### 16.6 The batched step [S], with per-world variants

Tiers `batch-cpu` and `gpu-*` under the `lockstep` profile execute the same ten stages in the same order. Every
stage is a named kernel or a short chain of kernels (§21), batched over worlds, with no data-dependent control
flow across worlds.

| # | Stage | Kernels | Parallel shape | Per-world data it reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kinematics: link poses and twists down the tree | `K-fk-level`, `K-twist-level` | Worlds × links at one depth | Model slot: parents, level ranges, joint placements, screw bases |
| 2 | Collision geometry: world poses of primitives and sample points | `K-geom-pose` | Worlds × primitives | Model slot: sample lists |
| 3 | Broad phase for dynamic pairs | `K-bp-sort`, `K-bp-pairs` | Per world, deterministic sort | Model slot: pair filter |
| 4 | Narrow phase: primitive pairs; samples against the scene distance field; thin primitives | `K-np-prim`, `K-np-sdf`, `K-np-thin` | Worlds × candidates | Scene slot |
| 5 | Contact compaction: count, exclusive scan, write | `K-compact` | Fixed capacity per world, overflow flagged | — |
| 6 | Dynamics terms: mass matrix, bias forces, actuators, passive forces | `K-crba-level`, `K-rnea-level`, `K-act-l1`, `K-passive` | Worlds × links | Model slot: inertias, actuator-card slots; parameter row |
| 7 | Constraint rows: contacts, limits, closures, joint stiction and backlash, cones, film friction | `K-jac-rows`, `K-joint-rows`, `K-cone-setup`, `K-film` | Worlds × rows | Interface cards; parameter row |
| 8 | Solve: diagonal scaling, then Newton on the convex objective with Cholesky and exact line search | `K-scale-diag`, `K-hess`, `K-chol`, `K-linesearch`, `K-cone-eval` | Worlds in lockstep under masks | — |
| 9 | Integrate on the group; accumulate the energy ledger | `K-cay-step`, `K-normalize`, `K-ledger-step` | Worlds × joints | — |
| 10 | Sensors, observations, rewards, resets under masks (semantics in §23) | `K-sense-*`, `K-obs`, `K-reset` | Worlds | Task slot |

Design rules for the step:

- **Fixed shapes.** Every buffer has a capacity fixed at environment creation. A world that overflows its contact
  or row capacity is marked `Degraded` with a reason; it is never undefined behaviour and never a silent
  truncation. The caller chooses refuse, reset or continue-flagged.
- **Lockstep solve.** All worlds run the same number of Newton iterations under a per-world `done` mask. This
  keeps the launch graph static (§21) and makes the iteration count part of the identity every result carries.
- **No floating-point atomics.** Compaction and reductions use count-scan-write and fixed-order trees (§35).
- **The mass matrix is small and dense per world.** For the robots in scope (18 to about 60 degrees of freedom)
  H = A + JᵀGJ is factored by a batched dense Cholesky after diagonal scaling (§18.12).
- **A tree-operator route for larger mechanisms [F].** For a contact between one link and the environment, the
  Jacobian is nonzero only on that link's ancestors, so JᵀGJ has the same branch-induced sparsity as the mass
  matrix and an LᵀL factorization ordered leaves-first has no fill. A self-contact between two limbs breaks the
  pattern and routes that world's step to the dense path under a flag. Pilot before funding: count factor flops
  on the reference robots and on the 64-DoF bucket of §17.4 with and without the tree order. Ceiling: HYPOTHESIS,
  the factor is under a tenth of our step at 18 to 35 degrees of freedom. An incumbent trace puts constraint
  assembly at 15% and the whole solve at 47% of its step without separating the factor, so the share is measured,
  not assumed: the ledger of the ancestor-sparse route (§16.23) decides whether the route applies at 18 to 35
  degrees of freedom or only to padded buckets and mechanisms above 60. ✖ KILL-TREEOP: whole-step gain below 1.15 at 64
  degrees of freedom on the M2 profile; fallback, the dense path everywhere and a cap of 64.

**Identities.** A result carries its world's own `ModelId` and the `StepPlanId` of the plan that ran it. The
`StepPlanId` is the content hash of: the shape class (the capacities below), kernel versions, contact law, step
profile, h, the number of world-steps per control interval, Newton iterations and tolerance. A physics change
changes one of the two identities; nothing else does.

**Per-world parameter variants [S].** Each world carries a `model_slot`, a `scene_slot`, a `task_slot` and an
optional parameter row, exactly as it already carries its address. Worlds whose slots point at models with the
same tree and different parameter values (link lengths, inertias, gear ratios, actuator-card parameters, foot
radii, friction) run in one batch with no masks and no padding. The parameter row carries per-world
randomization drawn from card uncertainty (§23). FACT: a leading incumbent already batches model fields per world
(MuJoCo Warp "Batched Model Fields", Appendix H), so this is parity with incumbent practice and not a
differentiator.

**Per-world model variants [F].** Worlds whose slots point at models with different trees run in one batch
inside padded capacities, the way a world already indexes one of K scene variants. The tables, the size buckets
and the conditions under which this pays are §17. Kinesis guarantees one thing to §17: INV-KIN-04, that padding
and co-tenants change no world's digest.

**Why padding can be exact.** Padded links are identity transforms with zero inertia, appended after the real ones.
Padded degrees of freedom get identity rows and a zero right-hand side, and a Cholesky factor's leading block does not
depend on later rows, so the real block of the padded factor equals the unpadded factor bit for bit (PO-KIN-02).
Reductions over a padded axis use pairwise trees aligned to powers of two with the real entries in a prefix: any
subtree of trailing −0.0 padding sums to −0.0, and x + (−0.0) = x for every x, so the tree over capacity P reduces to
the tree over the smallest power of two that holds the real entries, whatever P is (PO-KIN-03). The canonical zero is
−0.0, as §35.5 requires for sums: +0 is not neutral, because (−0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0 changes the bits of a sum that is
−0.0. Counterexample that motivates the rule, in `f32`: with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1, (a + b) + c = 1 and a + (b + c)
= 0, because the spacing near 10⁸ is 8. A reduction whose grouping depended on capacity would make a world's bits
depend on its neighbours.

**Capacities per reference workload.** DESIGN defaults, TARGETs until the M2 battery records overflow rates.
C is the active-contact capacity; R is the row capacity, four rows per contact (normal, two tangential, spin;
the spin row is masked where the effective radius is zero) plus joint rows (one limit row and one stiction row
per actuated joint), rounded up to a multiple of 8. Bytes are per world in `f32` with a dense Jacobian, from
4·(61·n_b + 17·n_v + 3·n_v² + 24·C + R·n_v + 12·R + 10·n_v), before a few kilobytes of state.

### 16.7 Integrators

- **Default, [S]:** semi-implicit Euler. Stage 8 returns v₊; stage 9 sets q₊ = q·τ(h·v₊). Joint damping and
  reflected inertia are implicit in A. Default physics step h = 2 ms with control every tenth step
  (OPEN-KIN-1).
- **Structure-preserving, [F]:** a linear-time variational integrator for articulated bodies in reduced
  coordinates on `referee` and `batch-cpu`, for long-horizon energy studies and passive-dynamics work.
- **World-steps per control interval** are fixed at admission by the clock contract of §20 (rational periods
  with phase accumulators) and enter the `StepPlanId`. No schedule is required to be an integer ratio of the
  physics tick: on the lockstep tiers a period that is not a multiple of h is served by its phase accumulator
  under the boundary ordering rule of §20, with the quantization recorded; under a transactional profile every
  clock boundary is an exact sub-interval boundary (step T4.3).

**The free-body variational step [S], the concrete content of the structure-preserving lane.** For one rigid body
with configuration g, body velocity ξ, inertia 𝕄, retraction τ = cay and discrete Lagrangian
L_d = (h/2)·ξₖᵀ𝕄ξₖ − (h/2)·(V(gₖ) + V(gₖ₊₁)), the step is a symmetric kick-drift-kick:

1. Half kick: μ⁺ = μ⁻ + (h/2)·f_pot(gₖ) + (h/2)·uₖ.
2. Solve the discrete Legendre relation for ξₖ: the momentum that the left-trivialized derivative of τ at −h·ξₖ
   (equally, the right-trivialized derivative at +h·ξₖ; the matrix of C-36) assigns to 𝕄·ξₖ equals μ⁺. Newton with the
   closed-form 6×6 Jacobian; TARGET two to three iterations to 10⁻¹³ relative.
3. Drift and transport: gₖ₊₁ = gₖ·τ(h·ξₖ), and μ⁺ is carried to the new body frame by the wrench pull-back μ° =
   (Ad_c)ᵀ·μ⁺ of §11.3 (the group coadjoint of c⁻¹, not of c), where c = τ(h·ξₖ), with the sign convention of §11 (the
   one for which μ̇ = ad*_ξ μ holds for a free body).
4. Second half kick with f_pot(gₖ₊₁) and uₖ₊₁.

What is claimed: second order in h, time-reversible to round-off, exact conservation of the discrete momentum map
in the absence of potential and input. What is not claimed: any symplectic or energy-drift property for an
articulated tree with a configuration-dependent mass matrix, where an explicit splitting is not symplectic, or
for any trajectory with dissipative contact impulses, where the property has no meaning. The articulated lane
stays [F] until GATE-KIN-01's long-horizon rows pass on a tree. Pilot before funding: the asymmetric free body
and the heavy top under this step against the Cayley semi-implicit step at h = 2 ms and 0.25 ms; falsifier, an
energy error that does not fall by a factor between 3.6 and 4.4 per halving of h. Ceiling: none on throughput;
the lane exists for trust in cost of transport over minutes of simulated time. ✖ KILL-VARIATIONAL: the implicit
solve of step 2 costs more than twice a semi-implicit step on `batch-cpu`, or the articulated form fails its
momentum row after one repair cycle; fallback, the free-body step is kept as a known-answer oracle and every
rollout uses the default.

### 16.8 The trust ladder, the fidelity ledger and the promotion rule

**The fidelity ledger** is a table, keyed by (model class, scene class, quantity, tier pair, profile pair,
contact-law pair, kernel versions), of the distribution of disagreement between a faster configuration and a
slower one on the standard battery (§38). Quantities include base displacement, yaw, stance impulse, slip
distance, peak joint torque, energy per metre, time of first failure, the count of contacts the certified
detector found and the fast tier missed, the energy-ledger residual, and **task decisions**: whether the two
configurations agree on success, on the terminal class and on the verdict of a comparison (the decision-level
equivalence of §35). A state error inside its envelope with a different decision is a disagreement.

**Promotion rule.** A design score or a policy return computed on a fast tier is labeled `provisional`. It
becomes `established` only after re-evaluation of a sample of episodes on a higher tier, with the difference
inside the promotion envelope recorded in the ledger for that quantity. The sample size is chosen by the
e-process of C-15, so clear cases promote after a handful of referee episodes. A result that fails promotion is
labeled `provisional: tier-sensitive` and reported with both numbers (NS-09).

This is FrankenSim's "speculate low, verify high" pattern and its Goodhart guard (addendum proposals 9 and D,
FACT), applied to robots.

The rule has two parts, both [S]. The first decides the label; the second produces the number that is reported.

**The label: a promote and reject pair on exceedances.** Each audited episode yields one bit: did the fast and the
higher tier disagree beyond the ledger's frozen envelope for the quantity. Two e-processes run on those bits with
rates p₀ < p₁ fixed in advance: E⁺ tests "the exceedance rate is at least p₁" and promotes when it crosses 1/α⁺; E⁻
tests "the rate is at most p₀" and labels the result `tier-sensitive` when it crosses 1/α⁻. For n audited pairs with k
exceedances, ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁). Worked values, illustrative: with p₀ = 0.05, p₁ =
0.30 and α⁺ = 0.01, a clean episode multiplies the evidence by 1.36 and an exceedance divides it by 6, so promotion
takes 16 clean pairs, or 22 with one exceedance; at α⁻ = 0.05, two exceedances in the first three pairs reject. Both
compile to an integer table (n, k) → promote, reject or continue, regenerated and compared in CI. The envelope is
hashed into the ledger before sampling, and episodes are chosen by address before outcomes exist; under those two
conditions the error rates hold at any stopping time by Ville's inequality. Independence of episodes is not needed if
the null is read conditionally: given the earlier audited pairs, each new pair exceeds with probability at least p₁
(for E⁺) or at most p₀ (for E⁻). Budgeted mode (DESIGN): 32 pairs, after which the result stays `provisional`.
Deterministic fallback when a ledger stratum is missing or the null self-test fails: a fixed 30 pairs, promoting on at
most two exceedances.

**The number: a doubly robust tier correction.** For N addresses on the fast tier, let Y⁰ᵢ be the fast outcome at
address i (for a comparison, the paired difference B − A at that address).

1. Before any higher-tier outcome exists, fix a prediction mᵢ = ĝ(Y⁰ᵢ) of the higher-tier outcome. ĝ is the
   affine map c + β·Y⁰ᵢ that the ledger holds for that quantity and stratum, and the identity (β = 1, c = 0) in a
   new stratum (§29.11); it is frozen and hashed first.
2. Fix an audit probability pᵢ ≥ p_min for each address and log it. Draw Aᵢ ~ Bernoulli(pᵢ) from the stream
   keyed by `(WorldAddr, promotion_id)`. The assignment is persisted before dispatch and survives retries.
3. Where Aᵢ = 1, run the same address on the higher tier (same scene draw, disturbances, randomization and
   policy) to get Y¹ᵢ.
4. Form Zᵢ = mᵢ + Aᵢ·(Y¹ᵢ − mᵢ)/pᵢ and report the mean of the Zᵢ with a confidence sequence valid at any
   stopping time (`fs-eproc` provides mixture confidence sequences, FACT). The weights 1/pᵢ are bounded by
   1/p_min = 20 without clipping: the floor p_min = 1/20 bounds every weight, so nothing is clipped and there is
   no clipping bias (§29.11, which owns the ledger).

Because pᵢ is set by the system and not estimated, the expectation of Zᵢ given everything earlier equals the
expected higher-tier outcome whatever ĝ is: a poor prediction widens the interval and cannot bias it
(PO-KIN-05). With mᵢ = 0 the estimate is the plain inverse-probability mean; with pᵢ = 1 it is the plain paired
mean, which is the regression test. The higher-tier episodes needed for a given interval width scale by about
(1 − ρ²) against a higher-tier-only estimate, ρ being the correlation of paired fast and higher-tier outcomes:
1.3 times fewer at ρ = 0.5, 2.0 at
0.7, 2.8 at 0.8, 5.3 at 0.9, 10.3 at 0.95. HYPOTHESIS: ρ for whole contact-rich episodes is high enough to matter.
Contact chaos decorrelates tiers, ρ is unmeasured, and it is reported separately for continuous metrics and for
binary outcomes (H-22b).
Test: ρ on 200 addresses of the standard battery for energy per metre, displacement over 2 s and success, from
paired data that GATE-FID-01 already pays for.

Variants, each labeled in the receipt: a three-level telescoping estimate E[Y₂] = E[Y₀] + E[Y₁ − Y₀] + E[Y₂ − Y₁]
over `gpu-*`, `batch-cpu` in `f64` and the referee, with samples allocated N_ℓ ∝ √(V_ℓ/C_ℓ) from pilot variances
(FACT: `fs-uq/src/mlmc.rs` and `product_mlmc_allocation.rs` implement the allocation for a per-call closure; a
batch entry point is an upstream ask); and a **segment mode** that re-simulates one-second segments from fast-tier
snapshots when whole episodes decorrelate. Segment mode estimates per-segment tier bias, which is a different
quantity from the episode outcome, and its reports say so.

Budgeted mode (DESIGN): 256 higher-tier episodes per promotion; on exhaustion the result stays `provisional`. Fallback
trigger: effective sample size below 30% of the audited count, or ρ below 0.7 for the quantity; then promotion
uses the plain paired e-process. ✖ KILL-DR records the second case as a decision (§16.17).

**Labels a result can carry.** `provisional`; `established`, with the adjudicator named (`referee` or
`batch-cpu:strict`); `provisional: tier-sensitive`; `model-conditional` (§16.9); `energy-created` (§16.10);
`capacity-biased` (§18.11). Citing any label but `established` as established is refused (`FROB-REF-820`). The
weakest label among a result's inputs is the label of the result, by the claim product of §8.

### 16.9 Rank agreement between tiers, and model-form rank checks

A fidelity envelope bounds how far a number moves between tiers. A design studio also needs to know whether the
**order** of designs moves. Both checks below write to the fidelity ledger and both have consequences.

**Rank agreement between tiers [F].** For a set of at least 40 designs in one robot class, drawn by a keyed
stream before any score exists, and one fixed controller, each design is scored on the fast tier and on the
higher tier at the same addresses. The record holds Kendall's τ, a top-weighted τ, top-k recall for k = 10 and
bootstrap intervals, per stratum of design space, because a cheap rung is biased toward designs that are easy to
control and the bias is not uniform. Consequences, TARGET thresholds on the lower interval bound:

| Top-weighted τ | Top-10 recall | What the fast tier may do in that stratum |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.7 | ≥ 0.8 | Select finalists. Every design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best fast score still goes up, q̂ being the conformal residual quantile of the tier pair. |
| 0.5 to 0.7 | any | Discard only. It may drop the clearly dominated and may not choose among the rest. |
| < 0.5 | any | Not used for ranking. Every candidate that survives static analysis is scored on the higher tier, and the campaign estimate says so before it starts. |

The same record type serves the controller rungs of the fidelity ladder (baseline controller, family policy,
fine-tuned policy, specialist), which §28 allocates; Kinesis owns the tier-pair rows. HYPOTHESIS: tier pairs
agree far better than controller rungs, since they share the controller. No published rank agreement between
rungs exists (Appendix H), which makes this measurement the first thing the ladder depends on. Pilot before
funding: the 40-design calibration set at M3 on `batch-cpu` against the referee. Ceiling: a rung that only
discards saves at most the fraction it discards. ✖ KILL-RANK: τ below 0.5 in more than half the strata for the
reference classes; fallback, tier pairs are used for promotion of individual results only, never to order a
population.

**Model-form rank checks [S].** A ranking of finalists is re-evaluated under variants that should not matter to a
physically meaningful preference. Each variant changes one thing and is a different `StepPlanId`:

| Variant family | Arms | What a flip means |
|---|---|---|
| Contact law | CL-1 against CL-2; against CL-H where admitted (§18.5) | The preference depends on compliance or on the associated-flow artifact of the convex law |
| Cone | The elliptic cone of §18.6 against a four-sided pyramid of the same μ | The preference exploits diagonal traction: a pyramid grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more than the cone |
| Stiffness | Contact stiffness ×½ and ×2, dissipation time held | The preference depends on a penalty parameter |
| Step | h and h/2, physical parameters held (ADR-KIN-05) | The preference is a discretization artifact |
| Tolerance | Solver tolerance ÷10, or twice the Newton iterations | The preference lives in solver residual |

The check runs on the top k and the runners-up of a final round (DESIGN default k = 8) and returns
`ModelRobust { variants, top_k_overlap, energy_audit }` when the top-k overlap is at least k − 1 under every
variant and the energy audit is clean, and `ModelConditional { flipped_by }` otherwise; on results the two
variants are the labels `model-robust` and `model-conditional`. Consequences of
`ModelConditional`: the ranking is reported as fidelity-dependent with both orders; the result is labeled
`model-conditional` and cannot be promoted to `established`; it cannot authorize a build packet or spend
prototype budget (§31); the cheapest experiment that would settle it is named in `next_actions`. A preference
between two designs is stated only if it holds under both qualified contact laws. TARGET cost: at most 10% of a
campaign's compute. Demotion rule: if over 20 campaigns the overlap is at least 7 of 8 in 95% of them with no
energy flag, the check becomes a periodic audit.

### 16.10 The audit for energy created by the simulator [S]

Every step closes a discrete work–energy balance: the change in mechanical energy, minus actuator work, minus
work by prescribed surfaces and external forces, plus modeled dissipation (contact damping, sliding friction,
joint damping, stiction), leaves a residual. The energy ledger (§29) already reports that residual on its own
line. The audit adds a sign and a consequence: a **positive** residual is energy the integrator or the solver
created.

- Stage 9 accumulates the residual per world in double-float (`K-ledger-step`), with the positive part kept
  separately and tagged by the contact mode in which it arose.
- Energy stored in contact compliance is stored energy, not creation: for CL-1 a penetration d against stiffness
  k holds ½·k·d² and can return at most that. The audit counts it as storage, as the ledger does heat stored in a
  modeled thermal mass.
- A result carries the flag `energy-created` under the one rule of §29.9, which owns the window and the
  denominator: created energy above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier, or above
  10⁻⁶ relative on the referee (TARGET; re-baselined once at M2 with GATE-FID-01). This section defines no other
  label or threshold. A result flagged `energy-created` cannot be `established`, and a ranking that contains one
  is `ModelConditional`.

Counterexample, hand-checkable, and the first planted exploit: a position-level penetration correction that
removes a depth d in one step gives the body a velocity d/h from nothing. With m = 1 kg, d = 1 mm and h = 2 ms
that is 0.5 m/s and 0.125 J per event. The compliant law on the same penetration at k = 10⁵ N/m stores 0.05 J
and returns less. A hopping policy that finds the first behaviour gains height from the solver. The second
planted exploit is the pyramid cone of §16.9. GATE-FID-06 requires both to be flagged and an honest policy to
pass.

### 16.11 The background referee and its coverage figures [F]

**What it is.** While a GPU run is in progress, idle CPU cores re-run a sample of its worlds on a higher tier
and compare. It is a correctness guard. It removes no training and no search, so it is not a source of speed, and
it is specified on every back end: it needs a snapshot, not shared memory.

- **Sampling.** Worlds are chosen by a hash of their address under a dedicated counter-based stream with a
  declared probability p, so estimates weight each sampled world by 1/p and no physics stream is touched.
- **Snapshots.** State is copied at the fence into a snapshot buffer: an explicit readback on CUDA; on Metal, a
  blit to a snapshot buffer read after command-buffer completion. FACT: the integration state of a 35-DoF
  humanoid is about 135 floats, so shadowing 256 worlds at 50 Hz moves about 7 MB/s; copy cost is irrelevant on
  either machine and "zero copy" is not part of the design.
- **What is compared.** Trajectories are not compared beyond a few steps, because any difference grows through
  contact. Two things are: (a) **one-step defects**, where the CPU advances one control interval from the
  snapshot and the difference in state, impulses and active set is binned by stage, which localizes a kernel or
  a law; and (b) **outcome distributions**, where whole episodes are re-run from reset at the same addresses
  with the policy weights frozen at the snapshot, and success rate, energy per metre and terminal class are
  compared as distributions through the promotion estimator of §16.8.
- **The coverage figure.** Every training and evaluation receipt carries
  `referee_coverage = { gpu_world_seconds, shadowed_world_seconds, share, one_step_defects, outcome_pairs,
  adjudicator }`. A run with no background referee prints a share of zero.

**How much it can cover.** FACT, arithmetic on the targets of §36 (Appendix H): GPU world-seconds per second are T·h,
so a humanoid batch at 1.7 M world-steps/s (the tolerance of §36.3 row 7) and h = 2 ms produces 3,400 and a quadruped
batch at 2 M produces 4,000. A referee at 0.2 to 1.0 times real time per core on 24 cores produces 4.8 to 24. The
share is therefore about 0.14 to 0.71% for the humanoid and 0.12 to 0.6% for the quadruped, and 2.4% where a device
produces only 1,000 world-seconds per second: about 0.1 to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds at that assumed referee speed.
Referee speed dominates the figure and is unmeasured: a published CPU engine runs a humanoid at about 48 times real
time per core, and a referee within a factor of ten of it would cover several percent. HYPOTHESIS: the `strict`
profile, which is our own code in `f64`, is faster per core than the referee and lifts the share accordingly; the M2
measurement decides. FACT: a published CPU engine steps a humanoid at 28,000 to 46,000 steps/s per core (Appendix H).
A `batch-cpu` tier within a factor of four of that on 24 cores would produce 0.10 to 0.16 times the humanoid GPU
tolerance of 1.7 M at a factor of four and 0.40 to 0.65 times at parity, so under the `lockstep` profile the CPU could
shadow between a tenth and two thirds of the humanoid worlds; that comparison checks kernels and arithmetic, not the
law.

**What it can detect.** A gap of five points in success rate between tiers needs about 16·p·(1 − p)/δ² episodes
per tier: 576 at p = 0.9 and 1,600 at p = 0.5, which is 11,520 to 32,000 world-seconds at 20 s per episode, or
8 minutes to 1.9 hours of background referee time at the rates above. One-step defects from a wrong kernel show
in seconds.

**The referee's own coverage.** Separately from the share of GPU work that is shadowed, the referee cannot
complete every scenario (§16.4). At M2 exit, and in every release, the ledger reports per robot class: the share
of standard-battery scenarios the referee completes, broken down by refusal cause (contact cap, fixed-witness
gate, missing joint class, kinematic loop), the share the `strict` profile completes instead, and the wall-clock
of a promotion at the 50th and 95th percentiles. HYPOTHESIS: coverage is high for point-foot quadrupeds, partial
for humanoids with sampled soles and low for tracked robots; the test is the measurement. Consequence: in a class
where the referee completes fewer than 90% of battery scenarios (TARGET; the threshold of BET-34), `established`
always names its adjudicator, and a comparison between classes with different adjudicators says so in its first
line.

Pilot before funding: on Metal, a kernel rewriting a 100 MB buffer per command buffer with a blit of 256 rows to
a snapshot buffer while 24 cores run the referee; on one RTX 4090, the same with explicit readback. Falsifier:
GPU kernel time rises by more than 5% against idle CPU, or coverage is below 0.1% of world-seconds. Ceiling: no
compute benefit by construction; the benefit is fewer false accepts. Because sampled worlds are drawn with recorded
inclusion probabilities, they also serve as the referee half of the paired tier correction (§16.8), which takes
confirmation off the wall-clock critical path without reducing its cost. ✖ KILL-SHADOW in §16.17.

### 16.12 Events, loops and limits of the claim

- **Events.** The lockstep tiers are time-stepping schemes with a velocity-scaled contact margin; they do not
  locate events. The referee and the `strict` profile do (§16.5), and the referee's audit uses event location and
  certified collision detection (`fs-time::rk45_until_event`, `fs-contact::certified_ccd`, FACT) on sampled
  windows to count contacts the lockstep tiers missed.
- **Kinematic loops.** On the lockstep tiers a closure is a compliant equality row in the convex solve, with its
  compliance stated [F]. Under a transactional profile closures are the bilateral rows A of §16.5. Exact closures
  on the referee wait for FrankenSim's constraint lane (FD-FSIM-04).
- **Derivatives.** A derivative through a step is offered only where it exists: fixed-mode derivatives on smooth
  kernels (D-8). Near a grazing contact, an ambiguous simultaneity or a changing active set the request is
  refused with the offending state, and a one-sided estimate, a generalized derivative or a smoothed surrogate
  each carry their own label. Design gradients by paired differences are §17.13.
- **No real-time claim.** Nothing here claims real-time or hardware-in-the-loop behaviour. Interactive viewing
  runs one world at presentation rate with physics unchanged.

### 16.19 Row-class solve queues

**What it is.** [F]. This subsection extends §16.6 and adds an arm to the head-to-head of §17.14. The lockstep
solve of §16.6 launches stages 7 and 8 over every world at the row capacity R for a fixed number of Newton
iterations, under masks. Padding by morphology is bounded by the buckets of §17.5. Padding by *work* is not:
inside one model the active row count changes within a stride, and worlds converge after different numbers of
iterations. A row-class solve queue regroups worlds by the work they need, so that one execution group (32 lanes
on CUDA) holds worlds with similar active row counts that are still iterating, whichever robot they belong to.
Owner: `frob-kernels` for the kernels, `frob-dyn` for the step plan. Exposure: the option `--solve-queues` of
`sim.run` and `phylum.sweep`; `phylum.plan` prints the bound below for the population in hand; the placement
receipt of §21.12 records whether queues ran.

**Mechanism (DESIGN).**

1. *Classes.* After stage 5 each world has an active contact count c_w ≤ C. Three row classes are fixed
   fractions of capacity: c_w ≤ C/4, c_w ≤ C/2 and c_w ≤ C. Class k has a queue of world indices with a fixed
   capacity Q_k, a constant of the shape class, so no buffer changes shape.
2. *Assignment.* Kernel `K-queue-assign` counts worlds per class, takes an exclusive scan and writes world
   indices in ascending world order: count, scan, write, with no floating-point atomics. A world that does not
   fit its class queue goes to the next larger class, and from the largest to the general masked lane, which
   holds every world and therefore always fits.
3. *Execution.* The kernels of stages 7 and 8 are launched once per class with that class's loop bound, and
   they read and write each world's rows in place through the index list. No state moves.
4. *Regrouping live worlds.* After every second Newton iteration (DESIGN default) the assignment is repeated on
   the `done` masks, so later iterations run over the live prefix of each queue. The number of launches stays a
   constant of the step plan; a launch over an empty prefix returns at once.
5. *Generations.* Queue capacities change only at a control-interval boundary, as a new generation of the step
   plan's execution options.

**Why no digest can change.** A world's arithmetic reads its own rows only (INV-SPARK-04). The loop bound of its
class is at least its active count, and inactive rows are identity rows that follow every active row (§35.5).
The per-world `done` test is unchanged. Reductions inside a world are anchored trees whose shape does not depend
on a bound. Queue membership, order inside a queue and co-tenants therefore enter no result: this is INV-KIN-04
applied to work classes. Until GATE-KIN-08 has passed on a backend, a run with queues carries the label
`experimental-execution` and is refused as promotion evidence (refusal: execution option not yet qualified on
this backend).

**Counterexample: classing once per episode.** A trotting quadruped with four samples per foot has 8 active
contacts in diagonal support and 16 in double support (illustrative). With C = 24 the class bounds are 6, 12 and
24, so the world crosses a class boundary twice per stride. A sort made at reset leaves it in the wrong class
for part of every stride: either the loop bound is below its active count, which drops contacts and is forbidden
by §18.11, or it sits in the largest class and nothing is saved. Assignment is therefore per step.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** Let f be the share of step time in the solve, ℓ the share of the
lane-time launched there that does arithmetic on an active row of a world still iterating, and h the added
relative overhead (assignment, indirection, extra launches). Under the serial model of §17.6,

  speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f·ℓ + h).

- FACT (Appendix H): the solve is 47% of an incumbent's humanoid step. With every idle lane removed and no
  overhead the bound is 1/(1 − 0.47) = 1.89. The mechanism cannot double the step.
- Idle lanes are the rule in a lockstep solve. If a fraction ℓ of worlds is live and liveness is independent
  across the 32 lanes of a group, the group is busy with probability 1 − (1 − ℓ)³². At ℓ = 1/4 that is
  1 − 1.0 × 10⁻⁴: every group stays busy while three quarters of its lanes wait. After regrouping, 8,192 worlds
  at ℓ = 1/4 fill 64 groups instead of 256.
- HYPOTHESIS, from the launch figures of §17.5: the solve accounts for about 30 of a step plan's 60 launches at
  5 to 10 µs each, so three classes in place of one add 60 launches, 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step. At 0.5 µs per
  world-step that is h = 0.07 to 0.15 at 8,192 worlds and 0.009 to 0.018 at 65,536. The test is the measured
  launch cost in the fingerprint (§21.16).

| Worlds | ℓ | h | Bound at f = 0.47 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,192 | 0.50 | 0.07 to 0.15 | 1.20 to 1.09 |
| 8,192 | 0.25 | 0.07 to 0.15 | 1.39 to 1.25 |
| 65,536 | 0.50 | 0.009 to 0.018 | 1.29 to 1.28 |
| 65,536 | 0.25 | 0.009 to 0.018 | 1.52 to 1.50 |

The mechanism is a modest optimization that pays at large batches and low live fractions, and it loses at 8,192
worlds if launches cost what the hypothesis says. On Metal every dispatch of a chunk is encoded serially into
one command buffer (§21.9), and the cost of an added dispatch there is unmeasured; admission is per backend.

**Relation to family lanes.** The lanes of §17.6 group worlds by tree and contact mode in order to share
constants. Queues group by amount of work across unrelated robots. Both remove masked work from the same
stages, so their gains do not multiply. The profile measures each alone and both together, and the simpler
configuration that reaches the larger measured gain is kept.

**Pilot before funding.** No new kernel. On the M2 profile of the masked path, counters record ℓ per Newton
iteration index and the active-contact histogram per reference class (`telemetry.histogram`), and the
fingerprint supplies the launch cost. Falsifier: a predicted bound below 1.15 at every batch size the campaigns

**Known answers and controls.** (1) A world's digests with queues on equal its digests on the masked path, bit
for bit. (2) The trot fixture: per-step assignment follows the 8 and 16 alternation, and the reset-time sort is
caught, by a truncation flag or by zero saving. (3) A planted kernel that sizes a reduction tree by the class
bound, in place of anchoring it, changes a digest and is caught.

### 16.20 Decoupled stepping of separated robots in one world

**What it is.** [M]. Per-world time tiles between interactions are rejected as a general mechanism (§21.20):
separate environments are already independent, and policy and sensor frames bound a tile to 5 to 20 steps. With
a fraction p of step time inside tiles of L steps and overhead o the bound is 1/(1 − p + p/L + o); ten needs
p ≥ 0.924 at L = 64 and o = 0.01, and is out of reach for any p once L ≤ 10, where the bound at p = 1 is 9.1.
One narrow case remains, for the multi-robot scenes of §25.17: several robots in one world that are far apart
for most of a shift. While a certificate shows that two robots cannot touch, exchange a message or sense each
other before the next boundary, each is stepped as its own single-robot world in its own cohort and shape
class, and the multi-robot world exists as a ledger over them. Owner: `frob-dyn` for the tile planner, with the
swept bounds of §18.4 from `frob-contact` and the clock contract of §20. Exposure: `sim.run` on a multi-robot
scene with `--decouple`; the tile ledger is a table of `telemetry.summary`.

**Mechanism (DESIGN).**

1. *Islands.* The bodies of the world are partitioned into islands: a robot with everything it carries or
   touches; each free prop on its own; the static scene in none.
2. *Tile boundary.* All islands of a world are synchronized at every tile boundary. A tile ends at the earliest
   of: (a) the next control tick of any robot in the world; (b) the next exposure or sample instant of an
   exteroceptive sensor whose range and field of view could contain another island; (c) the earliest arrival
   of a message on a declared channel between islands, which is its send instant plus the channel's minimum
   latency; (d) the earliest possible contact between two islands. Clause (a) is the fence that removed the
   general mechanism, and nothing here exceeds it.
3. *Separation bound.* For islands a and b with bounding radii r_a, r_b about reference points at distance d,
   current speeds v_a, v_b of those points, and caps ā_a, ā_b on their accelerations (from actuator cards and
   friction limits; a free prop takes g), the gap after time t is at least

     γ(t) = d − r_a − r_b − (v_a + v_b)·t − ½·(ā_a + ā_b)·t².

   A tile of length T is certified for the pair when γ(T) > 0. Clipping speeds at the cards' speed caps can
   only enlarge γ and is allowed.
4. *Decoupled stepping.* Inside a certified tile each island steps in a slot of a single-robot cohort that
   shares the world's scene slot. Its random streams keep the multi-robot world's address, with the island as
   the perturbed entity (§35.9), so a robot's draws do not depend on whether it was stepped coupled or apart.
5. *Merge.* Islands whose pair is not certified for the next tile are stepped together for that tile as one
   coupled group, under the `strict` profile with an island-wise solve, or in a multi-robot shape class where
   one is admitted. The switch happens only at a tile boundary and is recorded with the pair, the gap and the
   clause that ended certification.

**Smallest case, by hand.** Two robots are at rest with a 0.10 m gap between their bounding spheres, and each
can accelerate at 5 m/s². The gap closes when 2·(½·5·t²) = 0.10 m, at t = √0.02 s = 0.141 s. A 20 ms tile is
certified, with γ = 0.10 − 0.002 = 0.098 m. A 0.2 s tile is not: γ = 0.10 − 0.20 < 0. *Counterexample:* a bound
built from current velocities alone sees a closing speed of zero and certifies any tile length, and the robots
then collide at 0.141 s inside a tile, unseen. The acceleration term is not optional. Second case: head-on at
speed caps of 1.5 m/s each from a 3.0 m gap, the earliest contact is at 1.0 s, so fifty 20 ms tiles are
certified and the pair merges when the gap falls below 3.0 × 0.02 = 0.06 m.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** For N similar robots let φ be the share of a single-robot step spent
in the dense factor. A coupled world factored densely costs (1 − φ) + φ·N² per robot-step, in units of a
single-robot step, and the decoupled worlds cost 1 + o. At φ = 0.1, the upper end at 18 to 35 degrees of
freedom (§16.6), and o = 0.02 the ratio is 2.45 for N = 4 and 7.16 for N = 8. That is the ratio against the
weakest comparator. A comparator that factors island by island already removes the N² term, and against it the
saving is the cross-island broad phase alone. FACT (Appendix H): collision is about 4% of an incumbent's
humanoid step, so that ratio is below 1.05. The mechanism is therefore not a throughput lever. What it gives is
admission: a fleet scene runs on the single-robot shape classes, kernels and launch graphs that already exist,
each robot in its own size bucket, with no multi-robot bucket padded to N times the largest robot. Its value is
bounded by the share of a shift the robots spend certified apart.

**Pilot before funding.** Paper only. On the warehouse archetype of §19.7, with 4, 8 and 16 kinematic
route-graph actors standing in for robots, the share of robot-time certified at 20 ms tiles is computed from
traces. Falsifier: a share below 0.5 at 8 robots.

**Known answers and controls.** The two hand cases; a planted message whose latency is shorter than the tile,
which must end the tile at its arrival; a lidar that can see the other robot, which must end the tile at its
sample instant; a thrown prop, whose island must merge before impact.

### 16.21 Step adjoints through the converged contact solve

This subsection specifies the mechanism behind the M6 item "gradients through contact" and the revisit clause of
NONGOAL-15. Tier [M]. It is off the critical path and is funded only after its pilot. Owner: `frob-dyn` for the
reverse sweep, with derivative kernels produced by `derive/frob-derive` into `frob-kernels`. Verb:
`phylum.gradient --method adjoint`; the paired finite-difference method of §17.13 stays the reference.

**What it is.** On lockstep tiers stage 8 returns the velocity v⁺ that minimizes a strictly convex objective Φ(v; z),
where z collects the state at the start of the step, the action and the design parameters of the parameter row
(§16.6). At convergence the residual r(v, z) = ∇ᵥΦ is zero and the Hessian H = A + JᵀGJ has already been factored.
The implicit function theorem gives dv⁺/dz = −H⁻¹ · ∂r/∂z. In reverse mode, for the cotangent v̄ of a scalar loss
with respect to v⁺:

  solve H · λ = v̄   (two triangular solves with the factor the step already holds),   then   z̄ ← z̄ − (∂r/∂z)ᵀ · λ.

Nothing is differentiated through Newton iterations or the line search. The two solves cost about n_v² multiply-adds:
324 for the reference quadruped (n_v = 18) and 1,225 for the humanoid (n_v = 35). The products (∂r/∂z)ᵀ·λ and the
transposed Jacobians of stages 1, 6, 7 and 9 are straight-line reverse programs derived at build time from the kernel
IR that produces the forward kernels, each with an instance certificate that it equals the symbolic derivative (§12,
§21).

**The sweep.** A horizon of K control intervals with K ≤ 32 (DESIGN; §17.13 caps paired differences for the same
reason). Step buffers are stored for every world-step of the horizon, so no recomputation schedule is needed. FACT
(arithmetic on §16.6): at 27 kB per quadruped world, K = 16 and 10 world-steps per interval, the store is
160 · 27 kB = 4.32 MB per world and 8.85 GB for 2,048 worlds. `sim.estimate` prints the figure and the job is refused
when it does not fit. HYPOTHESIS: the reverse sweep costs about one forward sweep; the pilot measures it.

**Labels.** Each step's derivative carries a label of C-16. `smooth`: every contact and joint row is strictly inside
one region of its law (open, stick, slip; limit inactive or active), with normalized margin above a guard.
`fixed-mode`: the derivative is taken with active sets frozen. `one-sided`: a row sits on a boundary; both one-sided
values are returned on request. A step whose solve did not converge has no derivative, because the premise r = 0
fails, and its world is dropped from the batch gradient and counted. The label of a K-step gradient is the weakest
label on its path, reported with the number of steps under each label.

**Guards.**

- *Agreement.* In every batch, 8 random unit directions in design space are also evaluated by paired central
  differences (§17.13) on 64 seeds, and the cosine between the two sets of directional derivatives is recorded. Below
  0.65 the horizon is halved and the job repeats. Below 0.5 at K = 8 the verb returns the finite-difference result
  with the label `adjoint-rejected`.
- *Metrics.* Smooth metrics only; a binary outcome is refused as in §17.13.
- *Determinism.* Class D2 or better, since the agreement check pairs worlds (§35).
- *Manifold.* A gradient with respect to an inertia is a tangent vector of the cone in the chart of §14, and a step
  along it uses the retraction. Under a symmetry of the closed loop the gradient is averaged over the
  observation-preserving subgroup (§13), so a symmetric design stays symmetric.

**Known answers.**

- *Scalar implicit function.* Φ(v; z) = ½·m·(v − z)² + ½·g_c·v², a free term plus a contact term of curvature g_c.
  Then r = m·(v − z) + g_c·v, H = m + g_c and ∂r/∂z = −m, so v⁺ = m·z/(m + g_c) and dv⁺/dz = m/(m + g_c). With
  m = 2 and g_c = 6 the derivative is 1/4; with the contact open it is 1. At the boundary the one-sided values are 1
  and 1/4 and the label must be `one-sided`.
- *A zero gradient is not a stationary point.* A 1 kg block rests on a floor with μ = 0.5 under ideal Coulomb
  friction and is pushed with force u for one step of 0.01 s. Breakaway is at μ·m·g = 4.905 N. At u = 4 N, v⁺ = 0 and
  dv⁺/du = 0. At u = 6 N, v⁺ = 0.01 · (6 − 4.905) = 0.01095 m/s and dv⁺/du = 0.01 s/kg. For the objective "reach
  0.1 m/s" the adjoint at u = 4 N is exactly zero although raising u past 4.905 N makes progress. Under a regularized
  law whose friction ramps linearly up to a stiction speed of 1 mm/s, the stick derivative is 0.01/(1 + 49.05) ≈
  2.0 × 10⁻⁴ s/kg, 50 times smaller than in slip, and its value is set by the stiction speed, which is a numerical
  parameter (§18.5). A `fixed-mode` gradient in stick is therefore reported as uninformative, and a consumer that
  receives one takes a derivative-free step.

**Consumers.** `opt.start` with gradient steps on the design manifold, the refinement lane of §25.21.2 and the
sensitivity analysis A-16. A first-order policy learner over short horizons is not part of this item; it would be a
separate experiment under §23 with its own pilot.

**Pilot before funding.** Paper-scale and outside the workspace, on an incumbent engine that already ships
derivatives: one quadruped, 30 design parameters, a smooth tracking-and-energy objective over 16 control intervals,
256 worlds, 20 replicate batches. Arms at equal wall-clock: adjoint ascent, the stencil of §17.13, and one
derivative-free generation. Falsifier: median cosine against central differences below 0.5, or adjoint ascent needs
more than a quarter of the derivative-free arm's wall-clock to reach the same objective value. Cost: about three
engineer-days and 6 GPU-hours (TARGET).

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** One gradient costs about two sweeps against 2P + 1 = 61 for the stencil at
P = 30: at most about 30 times fewer world-steps per gradient, on smooth objectives over horizons where most steps are
`smooth`, and nothing where the objective is decided by a binary outcome or by a change of contact sequence. FACT

### 16.22 Closed-form elimination of single-loop planar closures

**Eliminated closures [F].** This extends the kinematic-loops rule of §16.12 and the linkage transmissions of §15.9.
On the lockstep tiers a closure is a compliant equality row, and exact closures on the referee wait on an upstream
constraint lane. For the loops that robots most often contain (a parallel knee, a steering linkage, a parallelogram
that keeps a foot level) neither is needed: a planar four-bar has one degree of freedom and a closed-form position
solution, so the loop can be removed from the constraint system when the model is compiled.

*Recognition.* At compile time `frob-model` marks a `closure` as eliminable when it closes exactly one independent
loop of four revolute joints with parallel axes, the mobility analysis A-01 reports one degree of freedom for the
loop, and no other closure shares a link with it. Everything else stays a row.

*The closed form.* With ground link d, input crank a at angle θ, coupler b and output link c at angle φ, both angles
measured from the ground link, the loop equation |O₂ + c·(cos φ, sin φ) − a·(cos θ, sin θ)| = b has the two solutions

    φ = atan2(k_y, k_x) ± arccos( (b² − c² − R²) / (2·c·R) ),    (k_x, k_y) = (d − a·cos θ, −a·sin θ),  R = ‖k‖.

The two signs are the two assembly branches. The branch is fixed by the design's reference pose and recorded in the
model; a compile that cannot tell the branches apart at the reference pose refuses. `frob-kir` emits kernels for
φ(θ), the transmission ratio n(θ) = dφ/dθ and n′(θ), and the same for the coupler angle. The dependent joints of the
spanning tree then follow θ: q̇_dep = n(θ)·θ̇ and q̈_dep = n(θ)·θ̈ + n′(θ)·θ̇², and the step uses the reduced
coordinates through the embedding Γ = ∂q/∂θ, with mass matrix ΓᵀMΓ and bias Γᵀ(M·Γ̇·θ̇ + h). No closure row, no
compliance and no drift remain for that loop on any tier, and the transformer modulus of §15.9 is n(θ) exactly.

*Refusal near the toggle.* Where coupler and output link line up, the argument of the arccosine reaches ±1, the
branches meet and n(θ) is unbounded. Elimination is refused when the sine of the transmission angle falls below 0.17
(about 10°, DESIGN) anywhere in the declared joint range, and the loop stays a row with that reason in the report.

*Known answers.*

- Parallelogram, a = c = 1 and b = d = 2: on the open branch φ = θ and n = 1 for every θ.
- The same linkage at θ = 90° on the crossed branch: the coupler end is at (1.2, −0.6) and φ = −143.13°. A solver
  that returns this root for a knee assembled open has folded the leg the other way.
- A crank-rocker with (a, b, c, d) = (1, 3, 2.5, 3) at θ = 90°: φ = 98.857° and n = 0.3722 on the open branch;
  φ = −135.727° and n = −0.1722 on the crossed one.
- The parallelogram at θ = 0 is a change point: all four links are collinear and elimination is refused.

*Owners and verb.* `frob-model` owns recognition and the branch record; `frob-kir` the emitted kernels; `frob-dyn` the
reduced embedding. `design.check` reports each closure as `eliminated` or `row` with the reason.

*Ceiling and pilot.* Ceiling: one constraint block fewer per such loop, and no closure error; no throughput claim is
made, and a spatial or multi-loop mechanism gains nothing. Pilot before funding: the parallel-knee archetype on
`batch-cpu` both ways, comparing knee torque and foot trajectory over a 2 s stride against the referee's bilateral
rows.

### 16.23 Ancestor-sparse assembly and the solve-stage ledger

**Ancestor-sparse assembly and the solve-stage ledger [F].** This extends the tree-operator route of §16.6. The
pilot named there counts factor operations only, and the ceiling stated there ("the factor is under a tenth of the
step at 18 to 35 degrees of freedom") is a HYPOTHESIS with no measurement behind it. On an incumbent's step trace
the two stages this route touches, constraint assembly and the solve, take 38.31 and 123.55 of 262.33 ns per
world-step, which is 61.7% of the step (FACT, Appendix H). If both vanished the step would be 2.61 times faster; a
three-fold reduction of both gives 1/(0.383 + 0.617/3) = 1.70. Those are ceilings on that incumbent's step and say
nothing yet about ours. They are large enough that the question is settled by a ledger and a pilot, not by an
assumption, and small enough that the answer cannot be a source of the ten-fold (§2.5).

*What is exploited.* For a contact or limit row r attached to one link, the Jacobian row is nonzero only on that
link's ancestor coordinates. Let n be the number of coordinates, d_k the depth of coordinate k (the number of its
ancestors, itself included) and d_r the depth of the link that row r is attached to. Three operations inside every
Newton iteration then shrink.

| Operation, per iteration | Dense count (multiply-adds) | Ancestor-sparse count |
|---|---|---|
| One row's contribution to JᵀGJ | n·(n + 1)/2 | d_r·(d_r + 1)/2 |
| Factor of H = A + JᵀGJ, ordered leaves first, no fill | (n³ − n)/6 | Σ_k d_k·(d_k − 1)/2 |
| Two triangular solves | n² | Σ_k (2·d_k − 1) |
| One row's residual and line-search evaluation | n | d_r |

*Known answers, checkable by hand.*

- Quadruped, n = 18: a six-coordinate base and four three-coordinate legs, so the depths are 1 to 6 once and 7, 8, 9
  four times. Factor: 35 + 4·(21 + 28 + 36) = 375, against (18³ − 18)/6 = 969: a ratio of 2.58. A foot row has
  d_r = 9: 45 against 171, a ratio of 3.8.
- Humanoid, n = 35: a six-coordinate base, two six-coordinate legs (depths 7 to 12), a three-coordinate waist (7 to
  9) and two seven-coordinate arms beyond the waist (10 to 16). Factor: 35 + 2·251 + 85 + 2·560 = 1,742, against
  (35³ − 35)/6 = 7,140: a ratio of 4.10. A foot row has d_r = 12: 78 against 630, a ratio of 8.1.
- A single chain (d_k = k) gives a ratio of exactly 1 in every row of the table. There is nothing to exploit, and
  the route reports that instead of running.

*The counterexample that bounds the route.* A row between two limbs (a hand on a thigh, one foot on the other) is
nonzero on both ancestor chains, and its product couples the two limbs: the leaves-first order then has fill. The
smallest case is a one-coordinate base with two one-coordinate limbs (n = 3). Rows on single limbs give H the arrow
pattern with a zero at the (limb 1, limb 2) entry; one row touching both limbs fills that entry, and a factor that
skips it is wrong. Such a world takes the dense path for that step under a flag, as §16.6 already requires, and the
share of world-steps that do so is a reported number.

*Fewer operations are not a faster step.* On a device the step is bound by memory traffic and launch structure as
much as by arithmetic (§36). The ancestor lists are data in the model table (§17.4) and are fixed per shape class,
so every lane of a bucket still runs one static launch graph; the price is gathers through an index and shorter,
irregular inner loops. Nothing here needs a compile per design. The sparsity is carried as data, and whether
unrolled per-design code adds anything to it is the separate question of §21.18.

*Pilot before funding: the ledger.* `xtask` instruments the `batch-cpu` solve and prints, per Newton iteration and
per reference robot, the operation counts of the four rows above and their measured share of the step, for the
dense path and the ancestor-sparse path on identical worlds, at contact counts drawn from the standard battery.
Two engineer-days, no device (TARGET). Falsifier: the four operations together are under one fifth of the measured
`batch-cpu` step for both reference robots. In that case the route keeps its original scope: mechanisms above 60
coordinates and padded buckets.

*Owner and verb.* `frob-dyn` owns the solve and `frob-model` the ancestor lists. `sim.run --solve-path {dense,
ancestor}` selects the path, and the `StepPlanId` includes it. The two paths order their arithmetic differently, so
they agree inside determinism class D2 and not bit for bit; a study never mixes them (§35), and a request that
would is refused with the two step-plan identities.

## 17. Phylum: families of bodies in one batch

Crate: `frob-phylum` (layer 3). Phylum calls `frob-model` for lowering (§14), `frob-algebra` for groups (§13),
`frob-dyn` and `frob-kernels` for the step (§16) and, through the airlock, FrankenSim's `fs-archive`, `fs-race`
and `fs-recompute`.

### 17.1 What Phylum owns

A design studio's workload is many different robots with few worlds each. Phylum owns what makes a population,
and not one model, the unit of work:

- **cohorts**: shape classes, model tables and slots, size buckets and the bucket planner (§17.3 to §17.5);
- **design-family lanes** that share structure and split at ambiguous guards (§17.6);
- **memory admission** for population shapes on each machine (§17.7);
- **admission of a design into a running cohort**, with edit-to-first-step latency as a target (§17.8);
- **specialization of survivors** as a gated optimization (§17.9);
- **the morphology generator**, which defines the distribution over designs (§17.10);
- **campaign quotienting**: orbit representatives, the mission-observable digest and the invalidation contract
  (§17.11);
- **quality-diversity maps per dispatch** and **paired finite-difference design gradients** (§17.12, §17.13);
- **the head-to-head experiment** that decides whether any of this stays (§17.14).

Phylum does not own the step or its kernels (§16, §21), the design term or the model compiler (§14), the
computation of symmetry groups (§13), family policies and inheritance (§23), racing, the archive and the memo
store (§28), or the scheduling policy of `frobd` (§32).

**The claim, stated at the size the evidence supports [F].** FACT (Appendix H): batching parameter values per
world is incumbent practice. Batching different trees is not available in the leading reduced-coordinate
incumbent, whose structural arrays carry no world dimension and whose documentation says heterogeneous degrees of
freedom are not available; a second incumbent requires all variants to share links, joints and names. Mixed
topologies in one batch do exist in one manipulation simulator and in two solvers of another engine, and one
incumbent solver publishes a mixed batch of three body plans at 86 k samples/s, 0.86 of the harmonic mean of its
homogeneous rates; that solver is maximal-coordinate, is documented as not deterministic, and is reported as slower
than the reduced-coordinate solvers of its framework. What is left to claim is
therefore narrow: mixed-topology populations with few worlds per design, in size buckets, in a reduced-coordinate
solver, with any one world replayable outside its cohort. The differentiators that remain are two measured
numbers, throughput and the time from an edit to the first GPU step, and one property, run-to-run replay and forks
inside the mixed batch. All three can be lost (§17.14).

### 17.4 Shape classes and model tables

A **shape class** is the set of capacities one static step plan is built for. Its hash, the `ShapeClassId`, is
part of the `StepPlanId` (§16.6). A **cohort** is a set of model slots and worlds running under one step plan.

```rust
// DESIGN: fixes intent and layout, not signatures.
pub struct ShapeClass {
    pub n_v: u16,                 // degree-of-freedom capacity: the bucket size
    pub links: u16,               // link capacity
    pub depth: u8,                // maximum tree depth
    pub samples: u16,             // collision samples tested per world
    pub contacts: u16,            // C of §16.6
    pub rows: u16,                // R of §16.6
    pub actuators: u16,
    pub sensor_layout: SensorLayoutId,
    pub single_structure: bool,   // P-A: one tree, unmasked path
}

pub struct ModelTable {           // one per model slot; read-only in a rollout; about 192 bytes per link (§21.11)
    pub model: ModelId,
    pub level_start: Vec<u16>,    // links sorted by depth; ranges per level
    pub parent: Vec<u16>,         // parent index, less than the link's own; padding links are roots of nothing
    pub placement: Vec<Se3Packed>,            // parent_from_joint
    pub screw: Vec<[f32; 18]>,    // screw basis S in R^(6x3)
    pub screw_cols: Vec<u8>,      // k = 0 (padding), 1, 2 or 3 active columns
    pub inertia: Vec<[f32; 10]>,  // zero for padding links
    pub limits: Vec<JointLimits>,
    pub actuator_slot: Vec<u16>,
    pub sample_range: Vec<(u16, u16)>,
}

pub struct WorldSlots { pub model: u32, pub scene: u32, pub task: u32, pub job: u32, pub addr: WorldAddr }
```

- **Joint classes are data.** A joint is its screw basis with a column count: k = 1 for revolute, prismatic and
  helical joints, 2 for cylindrical and universal, 3 for spherical and planar (C-01). The joint map
  τ(θ₁S₁)·τ(θ₂S₂)·τ(θ₃S₃), with masked columns as the identity, is one derived kernel with no branch on joint
  kind. This is where the Lie-typed core pays in this chapter: the joint is its screw basis.
- **Padding is exact.** Padding links are identity transforms with zero inertia, appended after the real links
  and masked out of every sum; padded degrees of freedom get identity rows. By PO-KIN-02 and PO-KIN-03 the real
  part of every result is bit-identical to the unpadded computation.
- **Runs.** Worlds of one model are contiguous in runs of 16 or 32, so one warp or SIMD group holds one model and
  the level loop does not diverge inside it. Contact counts still differ inside a run, which is why stages 5 to 8
  of §16.6 are compacted and masked per world: homogeneity by morphology is not homogeneity by work.
- **Capacities are read, not compiled.** Kernels read capacities from the step-plan header. A new bucket size
  needs new buffers and a new static launch graph, and no kernel compile.
- **Loops and rolling constraints** are rows of the coupled solve (§16.6 stage 7), not table entries.

### 17.5 Size buckets, and when bucketing recovers the padding overhead

**The bound.** Let work per world in a stage scale as Lᵖ in the size L of the robot (p = 1 for kinematics, about
2 for Jacobian and Hessian assembly, 3 for a dense factor). A cohort padded to L_max spends L_maxᵖ per world, so
its overhead against unpadded work is Ω_p = L_maxᵖ / E[Lᵖ]. With r = E[L]/L_max, the mean-to-maximum ratio,

  1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ ≤ r⁻³  for 1 ≤ p ≤ 3.

The lower bound holds because Lᵖ ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·L, so E[Lᵖ] ≤ L_maxᵖ⁻¹·E[L]; the upper bound is Jensen's
inequality, E[Lᵖ] ≥ E[L]ᵖ. The largest robot, not the mean, sets the cost.

| Population (degrees of freedom) | r | Ω₁ | Ω₂ | Ω₃ | Bounds 1/r and r⁻³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Half 18, half 35 | 0.76 | 1.32 | 1.58 | 1.76 | 1.32 and 2.30 |
| 90% at 18, 10% at 35 | 0.56 | 1.78 | 2.96 | 4.50 | 1.78 and 5.61 |
| 89% at 18, 10% at 35, 1% at 66 | 0.31 | 3.27 | 9.59 | 23.3 | 3.27 and 35.0 |

One large hybrid in a hundred designs makes a single padded batch cost three to twenty-three times the unpadded
work. A single batch padded to 64 breaks any factor-of-four budget for small robots on every super-linear stage.

**Buckets.** A bucket set with ratio q between successive sizes caps the overhead inside a bucket at q for linear
stages and q³ for cubic ones: 1.25 and 1.95 at q = 1.25, which takes eight buckets to span 12 to 66 degrees of
freedom. DESIGN: the planner chooses bucket boundaries for the population in hand by an exact dynamic program
over the sorted sizes, under a cap of eight buckets, with a registered default set {12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64} that
keeps launch graphs warm. If contact capacity, not degrees of freedom, dominates padding (rim-sampled wheels
beside point feet), buckets become two-dimensional and may fragment; the planner reports that as
`FROB-REF-741`.

**When a bucket pays for itself.** Each bucket is its own static step plan and pays its own launches. Let τ be
the unpadded cost of one world-step, c_L the launch overhead of one step plan per step, N the world count, Ω the
overhead of the coarser plan and Ω_b that of the finer one with B buckets instead of one. The finer plan wins
when

  N·τ·(Ω − Ω_b) > (B − 1)·c_L.

HYPOTHESIS for c_L: about 60 launches at 5 to 10 µs, 0.3 to 0.6 ms per step, equal to 600 to 1,200 world-steps at
2 M world-steps/s. A warm launch graph may make it nearly constant; the test is a measurement of launch overhead
per plan at M2. With those figures a further split that removes 0.05 to 0.1 of overhead needs about 10⁴ worlds in
the bucket to pay, and below about 10³ worlds per morphology two or three padded buckets are cheaper than eight.

**Worked card (padding and buckets).** Illustrative values.
- *Population.* 8,192 worlds: 90% quadrupeds at 18 degrees of freedom, 10% humanoids at 35.
- *One bucket at 35.* With stage shares of one half cubic, three tenths quadratic and one fifth linear
  (HYPOTHESIS, replaced by the M2 profile), Ω ≈ 0.5·4.50 + 0.3·2.96 + 0.2·1.78 = 3.5. At τ = 0.5 µs the step
  costs 8,192 × 0.5 µs × 3.5 + 0.45 ms = 14.8 ms.
- *Two buckets, 18 and 35.* Ω_b = 1: 4.1 ms + 2 × 0.45 ms = 5.0 ms. Bucketing wins by about 3.
- *The same mix at 512 worlds.* One bucket 1.35 ms; two buckets 1.16 ms: nearly equal. At 128 worlds the single
  padded bucket wins, 0.67 ms against 0.96 ms. The crossover is N = c_L/(τ·(Ω − Ω_b)) = 360 worlds.
- *Flip.* A launch overhead ten times smaller moves the crossover to 36 worlds and makes fine buckets nearly
  free; a tree-sparse factor that skips masked slots (§16.6) removes most of the cubic term and makes one
  bucket viable at larger N.

**What mixing can gain, stated before any speed test.** Against a comparator that runs D designs one after
another with W worlds each, the per-step ratio is D·(c_L + W·τ) / (c_L + Ω·D·W·τ). At D = 512, Ω = 1.3 and the
figures above it is about 40 at W = 16, 11 at W = 64, 3.5 at W = 256, 1.4 at W = 1,024 and 1.1 at W = 2,048.
The benefit is occupancy at few worlds per design, and it vanishes near a thousand worlds per design. That
comparator is the weakest fair one. A comparator that batches parameter variants of one tree, compiles in
parallel, or keeps one template per family (§17.14) closes most of the gap, and the gap against it is what the
experiment measures.

### 17.6 Design-family lanes and the speedup bound

A **family lane** groups worlds that share more than a shape class: the same tree, the same sensor graph and, for
a stretch of time, the same active contact mode. Inside a lane three things become shared constants instead of
per-world data: structure tables and motion subspaces; the constraint-row layout, so stages 5 to 8 need no
compaction and no masks; and the sparsity of the factor. Continuous parameters stay in per-slot columns.

A lane is valid only while every world in it has the same sign on every guard. **A lane is segmented at the first
guard whose sign is ambiguous across its members.** At the next control-interval boundary the worlds on each
side move, by index indirection, to the lane for their mode or to the general masked lane. Every lane has a fixed
capacity, so shapes never change; a lane that is full sends the overflow to the general lane. Around grazing
contacts, redundant contacts and uncertain mode sequences a world stays in the general lane.

**The bound.** If a fraction f of matched wall time is improved by a factor r and the method adds a relative
overhead h (guard tests, regrouping, extra launches), then under a serial cost model

  speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

"Matched" means the same completed work on the strongest baseline's critical path, which also caches graphs,
factors and compiled kernels. Three consequences. Improving 85% of the work without limit cannot reach ten
(the bound is 6.7). With f = 0.97, r = 40 and h = 0.02 the bound is about 13.5. And a pruning ratio is never
multiplied by a speedup that was measured on work the pruning already removed.

For the family lane itself: FACT, constraint assembly is about 15% of an incumbent's humanoid step (38 of 262 ns
per world-step in a published trace, Appendix H). HYPOTHESIS: with compaction and mask overhead in the solve, f
is 0.15 to 0.35 on our step, r is at most 4 and h is about 0.02, so the ceiling is 1.10 to 1.32. The lane is a
modest optimization. Pilot before funding: read f from the M2 profile of the mixed workload; falsifier, a
predicted bound below 1.15. ✖ KILL-FAMILYLANE: measured whole-step gain below 1.15 on the mixed workload, or
h ≥ f·(1 − 1/r); fallback, the general masked lane only, with P-A sharing of structure tables kept.

### 17.8 Admission, and edit-to-first-step latency as a target

**Edit-to-first-step latency** is the wall time from an accepted `design.edit` (a new `DesignId`) to the first
committed world-step of the new design on a GPU. It is a first-class target with its own gate, because it is one
of the two differentiators left (§17.1) and because an agent's turn is bounded by it.

| Path | Phases | TARGET, 50th / 95th / 99th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| Warm: the design fits a shape class of a running cohort | Compile with memoized subterms (§14); lower to a model table; copy tens of kilobytes into a free slot at the next reset under masks; first committed step | 0.1 s / 0.5 s / 2 s |
| Cool: a new shape class, kernels cached | The above, plus buffers and one launch-graph capture | 1 s / 5 s / 10 s |
| Cold: empty kernel cache | The above, plus kernel compilation | Reported separately; never averaged with the others |

FACT, for context (Appendix H): an incumbent's cold kernel compile has been reported at about 96 s and its cached
start at about 0.9 s; a model edit there needs a host compile, a model upload and a new graph capture; and no
incumbent publishes an edit-to-first-step latency. The comparison number therefore does not exist until it is
measured on the pinned incumbents on `trj`, which happens before this plan claims anything about it (§39).

**Slot-level dispatch [F].** One persistent environment exists per device and shape class. A slot that ends an
episode takes its next episode from any admitted job at its reset; model, scene, task and job are per-world
slots. A 96-pair comparison then occupies 192 of 8,192 slots beside other work instead of owning a launch.
Results are keyed by world address, dispatch is a pure function of queue state, and by INV-KIN-04 digests are the
same under any co-tenancy. Budgeted mode (DESIGN): at most 64 jobs per environment and 5% of slots reserved for
short jobs; no shape change enters a running environment. Fallback, triggered by any invariance failure or a 99th
percentile step time more than 10% over solo: one environment per job. The scheduling policy is §32's.

### 17.9 Specialization of survivors, a gated optimization [F]

A kernel specialized to one design (constants folded, loops unrolled, padding removed) may replace the
table-driven kernel for designs that survive several rounds of a race and will run for a long time.

**Ceiling, stated first.** FACT (Appendix H): in an incumbent's published step trace for a humanoid at 8,192
worlds, everything a design-specialized recursion could touch (kinematics, centre-of-mass terms, composite
inertia, bias forces, forward acceleration) sums to 53 of 262 ns, 20% of the step. An infinitely fast
specialized recursion gives 1.25 and one twice as fast gives 1.11; on a manipulation scene the share is 16% and
the ceiling 1.19. Generated code does not reliably beat a good generic library on CPUs either. The expected gain
is therefore 1.05 to 1.15 and the cap is near 1.2.

**Gate to fund.** Specialization is built only if our own M2 profile shows a specializable share s of at least
one third, so that 1/(1 − s) ≥ 1.5. Pilot before funding, two engineer-days: three arms on one 12-joint
quadruped (padded table-driven kernel, unpadded table-driven kernel, a fully unrolled constant-folded kernel
written by script) measured on the whole step.

**If built.** A survivor is promoted when T_remaining·(1 − 1/g) exceeds the compile cost, g being the measured
gain; compile time is capped at 60 s per design on CPU workers; the swap happens at a control-interval boundary
with a versioned state map. A specialized kernel is a new `KernelId`. It either reproduces the table-driven
digests on the pinned corpus, in which case results are interchangeable, or it is a new kernel version that owes
the fidelity battery (INV-PHYLUM-05). ✖ KILL-SPECIALIZE: measured whole-step gain below 1.2, or compile above
60 s per design; fallback, the table-driven kernel only. Derived derivative kernels for CPU solvers are a
separate matter and stay (§21, §22).

### 17.10 The morphology generator [F]

A population search and a family policy both need a stated **distribution over designs**. The generator is that
distribution, as an object with an identity.

- **What it is.** A probabilistic attribute grammar over the design term of §14: archetype, then body plan (limb
  count and arrangement through `mirror` and `radial`), then limb type (serial leg, wheel-leg, wheel, track
  unit, arm), then parameters with distributions inside their declared bounds, actuator cards drawn from the
  catalogue, and typed edits with probabilities as mutation operators. One generator per robot family: FACT, the
  evidence supports policies per morphology class, with zero-shot transfer of 70 to 85% of specialist return
  inside a class and 10 to 40% across topology-diverse designs (Appendix H).
- **Identity and addressing.** A generator is content-addressed (`GeneratorId`). Design i of seed s is a pure
  function of `(GeneratorId, s, i)` through the counter-based streams of §35, and comes with its derivation and
  its exact log-probability, so importance weights between generators are exact.
- **Validity.** Samples that fail `design check` are rejected with their refusal codes; the rejection rate per
  code is part of the generator's report. DESIGN: a generator above 50% rejection is flagged as badly
  specified, because its stated distribution is then mostly not the distribution of what runs.
- **Splits.** A `DesignSplit` separates training designs from held-out designs by seed and by structural stratum,
  and groups designs in one orbit of the observation-preserving group (§17.11) on the same side, so a mirrored
  copy of a training design cannot appear in the test set. A family-policy score is admitted only for designs
  absent from the policy's training manifest.
- **Support.** `phylum.generate --locate` returns the derivation of a given design under a generator, or reports
  that it lies outside the support. A family policy applied to a design outside the support of its training
  generator is labeled out-of-distribution and its score is not a rung of the ladder (`FROB-REF-541`).
- **Shape awareness.** The generator reports the bucket histogram and padding overhead of what it emits, and can
  be stratified by shape class so that a pretraining population does not fragment into small cohorts.
- **Cost of use.** FACT (Appendix H): pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours and at 10¹²
  steps over a thousand. The first needs an owner-approved compute envelope (§21); the second is out of scope.

Pilot before funding: sample 1,000 designs per family on the CPU and report rejection rate, bucket histogram and
padding overhead under the bucket set; falsifier, more than eight buckets needed to hold padding waste at or
below 25%. Ceiling: none on speed; the generator is what makes a family policy's claim well defined.

### 17.11 Campaign quotienting

A campaign should not simulate the same closed-loop system twice. Three mechanisms remove duplicates, each exact
and each with a stated limit. None is a source of large factors except on fixtures built to have them.

**Orbit representatives under the observation-preserving subgroup [S].** Before a combinatorial sweep of k module
types over n slots, Phylum takes the group Γ_obs of §13 (the subgroup of the mechanical symmetry group that also
preserves every sensor pose and field of view, the payload, the task's goal orientation and the terrain's
symmetry), computes its action on the slots, and enumerates one canonical representative per orbit: the
lexicographically least labeling in the orbit. The count is known in advance from the cycle index, which is the
known answer. Worked, exact: ten leg types on the six slots of a radial hexapod under the dihedral group of
order 12 give (10⁶ + 2·10 + 2·10² + 10³ + 3·10⁴ + 3·10³)/12 = 86,185 orbits, a reduction of 11.6. If sensors or
the task leave only the sagittal mirror, the count is (10⁶ + 10³)/2 = 500,500, a reduction of 2.0. The honest
range is two to twelve, and it shrinks as sensors break symmetry. A request to quotient under a group that is not
observation-preserving is refused (`FROB-REF-161`).

Members of an orbit have equal outcome distributions, not equal outcomes at equal addresses: the design g·d in
world w behaves as d does in world g⁻¹·w. Distributional results (scorecards, verdicts over a world
distribution that the group preserves) are therefore shared across an orbit. A result tied to an address is
shared only after the address is mapped through the group action, where the scene generator can do that; a
comparison paired by address against a third design is re-run.

**The mission-observable digest [F].** Two designs that lower to the same model tables, under the same task,
scenes, policy, kernels, tier and profile, are the same experiment. The digest is the hash of exactly what the
step plan and the task read: the lowered tables in canonical link order, the sensor poses and the task-visible
fields. Results are memoized on it in the memo graph of §28. Two rules make it sound and useful:

- *Structural, not proved.* "Equal digests imply identical traces" is a statement about the simulator and is not
  dischargeable in a proof kernel. It holds by construction, because the kernels read nothing but the tables, and
  it is checked by replay: sampled pairs inside a class must produce identical digests (class Tested, D2).
- *Exact lowering.* Welded sub-assemblies are rolled up in exact rational arithmetic from the design's rational
  parameters and rounded once (§14), so designs that are equal in exact arithmetic lower to equal bits.

Fixture, exact: eight identical ballast slugs in 24 collinear slots give C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements, which
enter rigid-body dynamics only through the count, Σx and Σx². There are 24,546 distinct (Σx, Σx²) pairs, so
24,546 classes, a reduction of 30.0. The negative control: two placements with equal Σx and different Σx² must
fall in different classes. The factor is a property of this fixture. Adding a placement-dependent hot spot, a
harness length or a collision shape to what the mission reads enlarges the digest and splits classes, down to
singletons.

**The invalidation contract [S].** Every memoized result records the digest of the fields it read. After an edit
a result is reused exactly when that digest is unchanged, and otherwise recomputed.

- *What survives.* Replacing a leaf module keeps its siblings' local analyses, derivations and kernels. An edit
  to a field the mission does not read (a label, a visual mesh) keeps everything.
- *What does not.* An edit that touches a shared battery bus, a shared thermal sink, a kinematic loop across
  branches or any mass or geometry of a robot in contact invalidates every episode-level result of that design,
  because the floating body couples all of it. FACT (Appendix H): exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is
  at most about a tenth of compute. The contract is hygiene and an evidence rule, not a speed lever.
- *Positive control.* A mirror swap under an element of Γ_obs must reuse every distributional result, and every
  per-address result after the address mapping of the orbit rule above.
- *Negative controls.* A shared-bus edit must invalidate. A reversed port sign must be refused. And the coupled
  bus: two fingers with identical local summaries on a bus limited to one unit of current have no feasible
  one-tick schedule and two feasible two-tick schedules, so a contract that adds peak demands prunes a feasible
  design. A local summary without time correlation is not a sound key.
- *Calibration.* DESIGN: one hit in 64, chosen by key hash, is recomputed in shadow. One mismatch stops serving
  that kind of node until it is explained. Counterexample on file: a dependency omitted from a key returned a
  stale promotion in an executed test; keys are therefore derived from declared inputs, never written by hand.

### 17.12 Quality-diversity maps per dispatch [F]

A population dispatch returns a map, not a winner. Each `phylum.sweep` updates a MAP-Elites grid (or a centroidal
tessellation) over behaviour descriptors such as mass, speed, energy per metre and footprint (`fs-archive`,
FACT), and the envelope carries the cells that changed, their elites with `provisional` scores, the count per
cell and the coverage of descriptor space. An agent's turn then yields many different good designs instead of
one optimum, which is how fewer serial rounds are bought.

- **Merging.** A cell keeps the maximum under a total order (score, then `DesignId`), so merging maps is
  commutative, associative and idempotent, and maps from two cards or two agents combine in any order.
- **Bias.** A population scored under one shared policy drifts toward designs that policy steers easily. The
  envelope therefore reports descriptor coverage beside quality, and elites stay `provisional` until the
  rank-agreement record of §16.9 or §28 permits selection in their stratum.
- **Ceiling.** FACT (Appendix H): published population co-design reports end-to-end compute gains of about three
  to four, and batched black-box search cuts rounds almost linearly in batch size up to about 128. HYPOTHESIS:
  campaigns on the benchmark of §39 need at most half the serial rounds of one-design-per-turn search. For
  parametric families this is not engine-specific; an incumbent batches the same sweep.

### 17.13 Paired finite-difference design gradients [F]

For a design with P continuous parameters θ and a smooth metric J, Phylum estimates ∂J/∂θ in one dispatch.
Worlds are laid out as a stencil: for each seed s, one world at θ and two at θ ⊕ δ·eᵢ and θ ⊖ δ·eᵢ for each
parameter, moved along the parameter's own retraction (§14) so that an inertia stays on its cone. **Every world
of a seed shares every random draw**: scene, initial state, disturbances, sensor noise and randomization come
from the same addresses, and only the design differs. The estimate is the mean over seeds of central differences
(J₊ − J₋)/(2δ) with a confidence interval. A stencil of 20 parameters and 64 seeds is 41 × 64 = 2,624 worlds.

Limits, all enforced:

- **Short horizons only.** FACT (Appendix H): in contact-rich simulation a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degree has
  been reported to produce significant divergence after 50 steps. Pairing buys variance reduction only while the
  paired worlds stay correlated. DESIGN: the default horizon cap is 50 control intervals; a longer horizon is
  admitted only where the measured variance ratio of paired to independent differences is at least 2 at that
  horizon for that class.
- **The divergence caveat.** Each pair records the first step at which the active contact sets of its two worlds
  differ. After that step the pair is two nearly independent samples and its difference is noise of variance
  about σ²/(2δ²). The estimator truncates each pair at its divergence step and labels the result
  `truncated-horizon`, or refuses if more than half the pairs diverge before a quarter of the horizon.
- **Smooth metrics only.** Energy, tracking error and margins are admitted. A binary outcome (a fall, a mode
  switch) has zero gradient almost everywhere and a finite-difference variance that grows as 1/δ; it is refused
  (`FROB-REF-540`).
- **Determinism.** Pairing requires a controlled determinism class (§35); under an uncontrolled class the verb
  refuses.
- **Bias.** Under a shared policy the gradient points toward designs that policy handles. The result is classed
  `Estimated` and a step taken along it is checked by a specialist or a fine-tuned policy at its end point.

Pilot before funding, about four GPU-hours on an existing engine or on `batch-cpu`: one quadruped, 12 design
parameters, a fixed policy; variance ratio at horizons of 25, 50, 100, 250 and 500 steps and perturbations of
1%, 3% and 10%; sign agreement over 20 replicate batches; 20 ascent steps against one generation of CMA-ES at
equal budget. Falsifier: variance ratio below 2 at every horizon of 50 or more, or sign agreement below 70%.
Ceiling: at most the ratio of evaluations, about 2P + 1 worlds per gradient against a few hundred evaluations
of a derivative-free generation, and only on the smooth, short-horizon part of the objective. ✖ KILL-FDGRAD:
the falsifier fires, or no end point is confirmed by a specialist; fallback, rank-based search (CMA-ES, racing)
with the stencil kept as a sensitivity report for A-16.

### 17.14 The head-to-head experiment

This experiment decides whether the table-driven cohort is the engine, and the plan commits to its outcome in
advance.

| Arm | What runs |
|---|---|
| A | Phylum mixed cohorts: model tables, size buckets, slot dispatch |
| B (control) | Per-family templates: at most nine padded templates, one per family of the eighteen archetypes of §14.10 that share a maximal tree (the grouping is frozen with the arm), each a fixed maximal tree with lockable joints and per-design parameter tables (level P-A only) |
| C | An incumbent's mixed-topology batch, out of process, at its pinned version |
| D | The leading incumbent run design by design with warm caches, parallel compilation and per-world parameter batching wherever the tree is shared |

- **Workload.** 512 topologies with realistic contact tails (wheels with rim samples and tracks beside point
  feet), drawn from the generators of §17.10 plus a frozen corpus of structural edits; K = 1, 8, 32 and 128
  episodes per design; arms A and B on both machines, arms C and D on `trj`.
- **Measures.** (1) Edit to first accepted result: wall time from the release of unseen structural edits to a
  completed, accepted cohort. (2) World-steps/s relative to a homogeneous batch of each bucket's largest design.
  (3) Padding overhead, bucket count and the share of the mutation corpus each arm can express. (4) Digest
  invariance (GATE-PHYLUM-02). (5) Run-to-run digests of the mixed batch for arm C under each of its solver's
  deterministic modes that accepts a heterogeneous batch; if none does, that is recorded as the finding.
- **Statistics.** A fixed-n study (§24): at least ten timed repetitions per cell after warm-up, cold runs kept
  apart, runs interleaved, a 95% lower bound on each paired ratio, a thermal soak on the Mac, and the disclosure
  block of §36 on every number.
- **Pre-declared outcomes.** If B expresses at least 95% of the mutation corpus and wins measure (1) at K ≤ 128,
  the templates become the engine and the table-driven cohort becomes an admission and preview path
  (✖ KILL-PHYLUM). If C ties or beats A on measures (1) and (2), the plan says so in its status table, withdraws
  the two performance differentiators of §17.1, and keeps the cohort verbs for what they still give: replay of
  one world outside its batch, deterministic forks and a Metal tier. If A falls below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of
  its homogeneous rates on 512 designs or needs more than eight buckets, KILL-PHYLUM fires as well.
- **Pilot before funding.** At M1 with no GPU code: padding overhead and bucket count of the design corpus (§24)
  and of 1,000 generated designs under the bucket set. Then two to three engineer-days and under five GPU-hours
  on existing engines: 64 topologies with 64 worlds each for 1,000 steps, timing the incumbent's switch overhead
  design by design, a 64-DoF maximal template with locked joints against the specialized model, and an
  incumbent's mixed batch. Falsifiers: sequential bucketed runs within a factor of two of the best mixed
  wall-clock, or the maximal template above four times the specialized model.

### 17.21 Limb-archetype kernels

**What it is.** [F]. This subsection extends §17.9 and uses the limb port interface of §14.6. Between the
table-driven kernel, which serves every design of a shape class, and a kernel specialized to one design, which
§17.9 reserves for survivors, there is a third level: a kernel specialized to a *limb archetype*. An archetype
fixes a limb's joint-class sequence and depth (DESIGN examples: a serial three-revolute leg, a four-joint
wheel-leg, a seven-revolute arm, a track flipper) and leaves every value in the model table: placements, screw
components, inertias, limits, card slots. Archetype kernels are compiled once per kernel version and never per
design, so a new design assembled from registered archetypes runs on them with no compile. Owner:
`frob-kernels` for the kernels; `frob-phylum` for decomposing a model table into a base and archetype
instances. Exposure: the option `--limb-kernels` of `phylum.sweep`; `phylum.plan` reports the archetype
coverage of a population.

**Mechanism (DESIGN).**

- *Registry.* At most 16 archetypes, drawn from the limbs of the archetypes of §14.10. A limb is an instance
  when its joint-class sequence and depth match after the canonical ordering of §14. A design with any limb
  outside the registry stays whole on the table-driven kernel.
- *Per-limb stages.* Stages 1 and 6 of §16.6 run per limb instance, with loops unrolled over the archetype's
  fixed depth and the joint-class column masks resolved at compile time. Worlds are grouped so that one
  execution group holds instances of one archetype.
- *The port is the only interface.* On the dense route a limb with k joints returns its k × k block of the mass
  matrix, the 6 × k coupling block expressed at its mechanical port, its composite inertia at the port (10
  scalars) and its bias terms, and the base kernel assembles the full matrix. On the tree-operator route it
  returns the `LimbPortPacket` of §14.6 (27 scalars).
- *Contact is untouched.* Stages 5, 7 and 8 run for the whole robot. Contact rows couple every limb through the
  base. An archetype kernel is a faster way to compute terms that the coupled solve needs, and nothing more.
- *Same bits.* The operation order inside an archetype kernel equals the table-driven kernel's order for the
  same limb, so results are bit-identical (INV-PHYLUM-05) and a design moves between the two paths without a

**Counterexample: a port packet is not the limb when its foot is down.** A point mass m on a massless leg stands

contact row the normal force is m·g and the acceleration is zero. Any path that closes the base equation from
port packets without the contact rows is wrong by exactly g here. The second control is the packet of §14.6
with value diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1): it is accepted as a packet and refused as an invertible matrix.

**Ceiling, stated first.** An archetype kernel touches the stages that per-design specialization touches, and
cannot beat it. FACT (Appendix H): those stages are 53 of 262 ns of an incumbent's humanoid step, so an
infinitely fast recursion gives 262/209 = 1.25 and one twice as fast gives 1.11. HYPOTHESIS: the gain is 1.05
to 1.15. What this level adds over §17.9 is reach: with zero compile per design it applies inside the edit loop
and to every design, where per-design kernels apply to survivors only. Two things are not claimed: a fused
kernel for all topologies at three quarters of homogeneous throughput, which rested on an occupancy miscount,

**Pilot before funding.** A fourth arm in the pilot of §17.9, at no added calendar time: the 12-joint quadruped

### 18.3 Geometry: fields, primitives, and a mesh path with enclosures

- **Everything static is a distance field.** A scene compiles to a signed-distance field (dense bricks, with a
  sparse brick tree as Frontier), an optional heightfield for open terrain, and maps of material and friction
  (§19). A distance field answers "how far, and which way" in constant time for any query point, which is what
  thousands of worlds need.
- **Everything that moves is a union of convex primitives.** Spheres, capsules, boxes, cylinders and rounded
  boxes, with convex hulls as Frontier. A robot's collision primitives are fitted from its link Regions by the
  model compiler with a recorded fitting error, or authored.

FACT: FrankenSim's narrow phase is convex-against-convex for primitives through a certified Frank–Wolfe
separation in `fs-query`, with signed-distance pairs, heightfields and mesh manifolds refused as
`MissingCapability`. The batched collision path is therefore new work in `frob-contact`, checked against
`fs-query` where both apply (ask FD-FSIM-03).

Every gap carries an error bound ε_g: the brick's interpolation bound, the primitive's fitting error and the
field's quantization. FACT (arithmetic, Appendix H): 16 bits over a ±10 m range resolve 0.3 mm, which is 30 N at
a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m; the stored range is therefore truncated near surfaces or scaled per brick (§19),
and the bound that results enters ε_g.

**The mesh path [S] on the CPU tiers, [F] on GPUs.** Triangle meshes in a bounding-volume hierarchy, built
deterministically (Morton order with total-order tie keys, fixed fan-out, stored in the scene pack by digest;
movers refit under explicit epochs). It serves two needs the fields cannot.

*Clearance certificates with one-sided enclosures.* Each body and each obstacle is bound to two sets: an **outer
enclosure** that contains it under every admitted registration error, pose uncertainty and interpolation error
over the sweep, and an **inner enclosure** that it contains. Then:

| Observation, per time slab of the sweep | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The outer enclosures are disjoint | `clear` | A certificate: the separating data per slab, checkable offline (§12) |
| The inner enclosures intersect at one time and pose in one jointly admissible world | `penetrates` | A witness: time, pose and the intersecting pair |
| Neither | `unknown`, with the slab and the error term to refine | None; the answer is not a claim |

Counterexample, hand-checkable: a nominal gap of 6 mm with 4 mm of outward error on each side leaves the outer
enclosures overlapping by 2 mm. The nominal gap is not clearance; the answer is `unknown` until an error term is
reduced. A second trap: two swept volumes can overlap in space while the bodies never touch, because they
occupy the shared region at different times; the test is per time slab, never on whole-sweep volumes. The strict
inequalities that decide `clear` are evaluated with outward rounding or in the exact-integer kernel class on
quantized coordinates (§35), never by the sign of an `f32` residual.

*Thin geometry.* A trilinear field on a 2 cm grid cannot hold a 2 mm plate: the plate vanishes or is thickened to
the grid. The scene compiler detects features thinner than two fine voxels and routes them: to **thin
primitives** where they fit (plates as boxes, cords and wires as capsule chains, rails as cylinders), evaluated
analytically by `K-np-thin` on every tier [S]; to triangle patches in the hierarchy on the CPU tiers; and to the
hierarchy on GPUs only as Frontier. A thin feature with no route is refused at scene compile (`FROB-REF-320`).

Pilot before funding the GPU hierarchy: traversal cost and shape stability on the thin-feature corpus of §19 on
`batch-cpu`. Ceiling: none on speed; it is a coverage feature. ✖ KILL-MESHGPU: traversal breaks fixed shapes or
adds more than 15% to the step; fallback, GPUs see thin geometry as analytic primitives only and meshes stay on
the CPU tiers for certificates and audits.

### 18.4 Detection, swept bounds and witnesses

- **Moving against static.** Each primitive carries sample spheres (a foot has four to eight; a wheel has rim
  samples chosen by curvature). A sample at point c with radius r is in contact when φ(c) − r is below the margin,
  with normal ∇φ(c). Field values are trilinear in `f32` with a stated interpolation error bound per brick.
- **Moving against moving.** Per world: a deterministic sort-and-sweep along a fixed axis or a uniform grid, then
  analytic pair tests. Self-collision pairs are a precomputed filter list from adjacency and nominal clearance;
  excluded pairs stay inspectable.
- **Speculative margin.** On the lockstep tiers the margin grows with approach speed, at least v·h + ε_g, so fast
  feet do not tunnel thin geometry between steps. The referee's audit (§16.12) measures what is still missed.
- **Swept bounds.** Under a transactional profile the broad phase runs on bounds swept over the sub-interval, and
  the narrow phase returns features, normal, witnesses, material pair, signed gap and ε_g (§16.5, step T4.2).

Counterexample for endpoint tests, hand-checkable: a sample sphere of radius 2 mm moves at 5 m/s with h = 2 ms,
so it travels 10 mm in a step. A 2 mm plate lies 1 mm ahead. Clearing the plate takes 1 + 4 + 2 = 7 mm, so at the
end of the step the sphere is 3 mm beyond the far face. Both endpoints are clear and the step encloses a
collision. This is the thin-barrier fixture; the mutation that must fail is the endpoint-only query.

**Witness pairs and their migration [S].** A contact carries a witness pair (one point on each body, in body
coordinates), a contact frame, and tangential memory: the stick anchor of a regularized law, accumulated
tangential displacement, and the warm-start impulse. FrankenSim's sustained step assumes the witnesses and the
frame are fixed (§16.4); a wheel rolling over a lip, a sliding sole and a rocking foot violate that. At every
redetection `frob-contact`:

1. re-localizes the witnesses by closest features, with one consistent world contact point at touching;
2. classifies the change as `same-feature`, `adjacent-feature` (face to edge to face around a lip) or
   `new-contact`;
3. keeps the tangential memory on the same feature; for an adjacent feature whose world contact point moved
   continuously (inside a tolerance δ_w), **transports** it: rotates it by the minimal rotation that takes the old
   normal to the new one and projects it onto the new tangent plane;
4. for a new contact, or when the contact point jumped, **resets** the tangential memory to zero and emits a
   `witness-reset` event.

Tangential memory is never kept silently on a changed feature (INV-TRAC-06). A law with no anchor (exact Coulomb)
transports warm starts only. The fixture is a torque-driven wheel climbing a sharp sill, flat to edge to top:
work, impulse and the feature sequence are compared under step refinement, and no couple appears that the
contact forces do not explain.

### 18.5 Contact laws

`ContactLaw` is a trait whose implementations are convex potentials ℓ_c (C-06), with one exception, CL-H, which
is a complementarity problem and says so. The law is part of the `StepPlanId` (§16.6).

| Law | Contents | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| CL-1 | Linear compliant normal with a dissipation time; regularized Coulomb friction with a stated stiction speed. | [S], default on lockstep tiers |
| CL-2 | Hunt–Crossley normal law and regularized friction in convex form. | [F] |
| CL-M | A mapping of MuJoCo's soft-contact parameters, for importing reference models. The mapping is lossy and the loss report says so. | [S], parity only |
| CL-H | Hard contact: Signorini complementarity on the normal, Coulomb friction with maximum dissipation on the cone of §18.6, non-associated. | [S] as the referee's specification; [F] as a batch law |

Contact parameters come from a card for the pair of surface materials and their condition (dry, wet, greasy,
dusty), following FrankenSim's rule that friction belongs to an interface system and not to an unordered pair of
materials (FACT: expansion charter §5.3). Stiffness, dissipation time and friction coefficients are physical
parameters; the stiction speed of a regularized law is a numerical parameter, reported, and never changed by a
step variant (ADR-KIN-05). Penetration is an error under CL-H and a state under CL-1 and CL-2; an evaluator is
told which law produced a gap.

**CL-H, the written specification of the referee.** At a contact with relative velocity u = (uₙ, u_T), where
u_T collects the two tangential components and the spin component scaled by the effective radius, impulse
λ = (λₙ, λ_T) and restitution e:

- normal: 0 ≤ λₙ ⊥ uₙ⁺ + e·uₙ⁻ ≥ 0, with the multi-contact applicability of e recorded;
- friction: ‖λ_T‖ ≤ μ·λₙ, and λ_T = −μ·λₙ·u_T/‖u_T‖ whenever u_T ≠ 0 (maximum dissipation);
- in cone form: with û = (uₙ + μ·‖u_T‖, u_T), the impulse lies in 𝒦_μ, û lies in the dual cone, and λ ⊥ û.

The term μ·‖u_T‖ is what makes the law non-associated: the conditions are not the optimality conditions of one
convex program. Dropping it gives the associated model, whose artifact is known: a sliding contact acquires a
separation velocity μ·‖u_T‖. CL-1 has that artifact in compliant form and the fidelity ledger measures it.
Solvers: a fixed-point iteration on s = μ·‖u_T‖ with a convex cone program inside each pass, which reuses stage 8
of §16.6; projected Gauss–Seidel with exact cone projection; proximal ADMM; and semismooth Newton on the referee
through FrankenSim. The residual is the natural-map residual ‖λ − Π_𝒦(λ − û)‖ in consistent units, TARGET at
most 10⁻⁶ for evaluation and 10⁻⁴ for training.

As a batch law [F], CL-H runs a fixed number of outer passes under masks, and a world over the residual bound is
`Degraded: unconverged`. Nothing guarantees convergence at a fixed count. Pilot before funding: on `batch-cpu`,
10⁴ contact states sampled from the standard battery, counting passes to a residual of 10⁻⁴; falsifier, more than
a tenth of states need over eight passes. Ceiling: negative on throughput, since each pass costs one CL-1 solve;
the benefit is the removal of creep and of the associated-flow artifact, valued by how often the model-form check
of §16.9 flips. ✖ KILL-HARDCONTACT: GATE-TRAC-09 fails after one repair cycle; fallback, per-island ADMM or
Gauss–Seidel at fixed iterations with a residual flag on `batch-cpu` only, and CL-H remains the referee's
specification and a model-form arm under `strict`.

**Response batches on a fixed active set [F].** At a converged step the factor of H is reused to solve many
right-hand sides: counterfactual impulses, adjoint columns, the stencil of §17.13 at one state. Each column
carries the bound ‖x̂ − x*‖ ≤ ‖b − H·x̂‖/λ_lo for a certified lower bound λ_lo on the smallest eigenvalue, and a
guard that sends any column crossing a stick, slip or open boundary back to the full solve. It is a diagnostic
query, not a stepping path. Ceiling: against a baseline that also factors once, none in flops; what the
mechanism adds is the per-column bound and the mode guard. Against a full re-solve per query the flop ratio is
about 14 for 64 columns at 96 rows, and only for queries of that shape. Pilot before funding: the share of
columns the guard reroutes on battery states. ✖ KILL-RESPONSE: guards reroute more than half the columns;
fallback, full solves. The anchor, the acceptance rule, the known answers and the gate (GATE-TRAC-11) are in §18.19.

Barrier contact stays FrankenSim's lane (FD-FSIM-02) and is not built here.

### 18.6 Friction, and the cone in four dimensions

Isotropic cones by default. Anisotropic elliptic cones for mecanum rollers, treads and skates. Rolling
resistance for feet and wheels is an extra row. The stiction speed of the regularized law is a reported
parameter, and creep under it is one of the quantities in the fidelity ledger. Velocity-dependent dry friction
(Stribeck) uses the lagged-coefficient mechanism of ADR-TRAC-06 and stays Frontier until it has cards with
provenance.

**Torsional friction is the fourth dimension of the contact cone [S], not a separate row.** For a contact with
effective patch radius r_eff the impulse (λₙ, λ_t1, λ_t2, λ_s) satisfies

  √(λ_t1² + λ_t2² + (λ_s / r_eff)²) ≤ μ·λₙ,

and in the anisotropic case √((λ_t1/μ₁)² + (λ_t2/μ₂)² + (λ_s/(μ_s·r_eff))²) ≤ λₙ. Both are linear images of the
second-order cone in ℝ⁴, so the `Cone` abstraction of C-06 (Jordan product, spectral decomposition, projection,
barrier) serves them unchanged and no kernel family is added. A contact with r_eff = 0 masks its spin row and is
the three-dimensional cone.

- **Where r_eff comes from.** For uniform pressure on a disk of radius a the torsional capacity is (2/3)·μ·N·a, so
  r_eff = 2a/3; for a Hertzian pressure it is 3π·a/16, about 0.59·a. The value comes from the sample geometry or
  the interface card (OPEN-TRAC-2).
- **Why one cone.** With sliding speed v and spin ω, maximum dissipation on this cone gives a tangential force of
  μ·N·v/√(v² + (r_eff·ω)²). At v = r_eff·ω it is μ·N/√2. Independent rows would grant μ·N of sliding friction and
  μ·N·r_eff of torsional friction at once, 41% more in magnitude than the surface can supply, and a foot that
  pivots while it slides would hold where it should slip.
- **What is approximated.** The cone is an ellipsoidal approximation of the true limit surface of a patch. Its
  error is measured against patch quadrature on the referee and recorded in the fidelity ledger.
- **Patches.** One four-dimensional contact can stand for a small patch. A patch that must carry a tilting moment
  keeps at least three non-collinear contacts. A pressure distribution is never inferred from one resultant
  wrench; where it matters, the patch defines its centre of pressure and its quadrature.

### 18.7 Joint-level rows: stiction, backlash and hard stops [S]

- **Hard stops** are the unilateral limit rows already in stage 7.
- **Stiction.** The friction torque of a joint lies in [−τ_s, τ_s] and opposes motion: a one-dimensional cone.
  On the lockstep tiers it is a convex potential with a stated stiction speed, the same regularization as CL-1;
  under a transactional profile it is an exact row with located stick and slip events. τ_s comes from the
  actuator card (§15) and may depend on load and temperature through the lag of ADR-TRAC-06.
- **Backlash.** With rotor angle θ, joint angle q, ratio N and gap β, the relative angle is δ = θ/N − q, the sign
  convention of §15.6, and the rows are −β/2 ≤ δ ≤ β/2: two unilateral rows on the relative angle between the rotor,
  which is its own degree of freedom at the card levels that carry rotor state (§15), and the output. Gap closure is
  an impact: an event under a transactional profile and a compliant row on the lockstep tiers. β is a per-world
  parameter and may grow with wear (§31). Requesting backlash rows for a card with no rotor state is refused
  (`FROB-REF-241`).

Counterexample for the plausible shortcut: backlash modeled as a dead zone on the torque command produces no
impact when the gap closes and no hunting in a position loop, the two effects a backlash model exists to show.
Known answers, hand-checkable: under a torque ramp τ = k·t a stuck joint breaks away at t = τ_s/k. A rotor of
inertia J = 10⁻⁴ kg·m² turning at 5 rad/s behind a ratio of 10 crosses a gap of 0.5° in β·N/ω₀ = 17.5 ms and,
closing inelastically on an output of inertia 0.05 kg·m², leaves it at J·N·ω₀/(J·N² + I_out) = 0.083 rad/s.

### 18.8 Film friction for wet and greasy floors [S]

A constant coefficient per floor texel misses one effect that decides slips: on a lubricated floor, friction
depends on how long and how hard the foot has pressed. The law below captures it at the contact, with no fluid
solver. Films that the robot spreads, slosh and coupled fluid islands are §19's Frontier and Moonshot items; they
write the film field this law reads.

- **Inputs.** From the interface card for the floor's condition: viscosity η, initial film thickness h₀ (from
  the scene's film field), combined roughness σ, the boundary-lubricated coefficient μ_b, and the dry
  coefficient μ_d that applies where there is no film.
- **State.** A film thickness h per contact, held in contact memory and in the restore set of §16.5.
- **Squeeze.** Under a normal load N on a patch of radius a the film thins by Stefan's law,
  dh/dt = −2·N·h³/(3π·η·a⁴), which integrates to 1/h² − 1/h₀² = 4·N·t/(3π·η·a⁴).
- **Coefficient.** A share ψ(h/σ) of the load rides on asperities and the rest on the film, with ψ = 1 for
  h ≤ σ, ψ = 0 for h ≥ 3σ and a monotone blend between:
  μ_eff = ψ·μ_b + (1 − ψ)·η·v·π·a²/(h·N). The second term is Couette shear, F = η·v·A/h. With h₀ = 0 the law
  returns μ_d exactly. A squeezed film recovers to μ_b, not to μ_d: a wiped wet floor is still not a dry one.
- **Regime indicator.** The Hersey number Π = η·v/(p·a) with p = N/(π·a²) is reported per contact. The card
  declares the range of Π and h/σ over which it was calibrated; outside it the result is flagged `extrapolated`,
  or refused under `--strict-data` (`FROB-REF-242`).
- **In the solve.** μ_eff is computed from committed state and held constant inside the step (ADR-TRAC-06).
- **Evidence.** The law is semi-empirical and its outputs are classed `Estimated`. FACT: `fs-tribo` has
  `resistive_film` and `stribeck_friction` modules, which the referee uses as a cross-check.

Worked values, hand-checkable from the integrated law, to 10 µm from a thick film: water (1 mPa·s) under a patch
of radius 2 cm carrying 100 N, 0.038 s; cooking oil (60 mPa·s) under the same patch, 2.3 s; water under a patch
of radius 6 cm carrying 400 N, 0.76 s. Against a stance of 0.3 s, a small foot on water reaches boundary friction
almost at once, while oil and large flat feet ride a film for the whole stance. A map of constant coefficients
cannot represent that difference.

✖ KILL-FILM: on the wet-floor battery (an oil patch and a water patch, one wheeled and one legged robot, 200
randomized episodes each) the law changes fewer than 10% of episode outcomes against a constant-coefficient
patch at μ_b, or adds more than 5% to step time. Fallback: static condition cards with one coefficient per
texel on the lockstep tiers; the film law is kept on the transactional profiles for audits.

### 18.9 Wheels, tracks and hybrids

- **Wheels.** A wheel is a body, a revolute joint and a cylinder or torus collider. Contact and friction do the
  rest, so slip, traction limits and climbing a threshold come from the physics and not from a kinematic rule.
- **Omnidirectional wheels.** Mecanum and omni wheels are an anisotropic friction ellipse aligned with the roller
  axis in the wheel frame. Individual rollers are not simulated.
- **Tyre laws [F].** Brush and Fiala models as constitutive laws from slip ratio and slip angle to contact
  force, for vehicles where carcass compliance matters. The lateral brush law has
  ‖F_y‖ = μ·N·(3ζ − 3ζ² + ζ³) for ζ = C_α·‖tan α‖/(3·μ·N) ≤ 1 and μ·N beyond, which fixes two known answers:
  the initial slope C_α and saturation at tan α = 3·μ·N/C_α.
- **Tracks [F].** A track is a set of road wheels plus a belt modeled as a contact surface with prescribed
  tangential velocity and an anisotropic friction ellipse, with distributed patches along the belt and yaw
  scrub. A full link-chain track is a Moonshot.
- **Hybrids.** A wheel-leg robot or a stair-climbing wheel cluster is a composition of the above and needs no new
  mechanism (C-12).
- **The nonholonomic model** of C-07 is not what the simulator integrates. It is the planning model, the static
  analysis model, and the known-answer oracle for rolling.

Counterexample for treating a tracked base as a rolling constraint, hand-checkable: exact lateral no-slip at
contact stations a ahead of and behind the centre requires v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, hence ω = 0. A
skid-steer or tracked base turns only by slipping, so its turning is a property of the friction law.

The oracle for rolling is the closed form Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2), sin(θ + δ/2)) with δ = ω·Δt and
sinc(0) = 1. FACT-to-recheck: a near-straight integration defect is reported in `fs-mbd::planar_drive` at the
pinned commit (heading 32 rad, wheel speeds 1 and 1 + 6·10⁻¹³ m/s, track 0.5 m, 400 steps of 1/400 s return zero
translation where about (0.8342, 0.5514) m is correct). It is an admission test at INT-FSIM-01 and an upstream
ask; `planar_drive` is not used as an oracle until it passes.

### 18.10 Terrain mechanics

| Ground | Model | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Rigid floor with a friction map (tile, concrete, wood, wet patch, grease) | CL-1 with per-texel interface cards; the film law of §18.8 where the card carries a film | [S] |
| Compliant coverings (carpet, mats) | CL-1 with covering stiffness and a rolling-resistance row | [S] |
| Soft soil under wheels and tracks | Bekker–Wong pressure-sinkage and Janosi–Hanamoto shear | [F] |
| Granular media under legs | Resistive force theory | [F] |
| Terrain that deforms and stays deformed | Rut and footprint fields | [M] |

**Soft soil.** Pressure and sinkage: p = (k_c/b + k_φ)·zⁿ for a contact of width b, so a plate under pressure p
sinks z = (p/(k_c/b + k_φ))^(1/n). Shear: τ = (c + σ·tan φ)·(1 − exp(−j/K)) for shear displacement j. For a track
of length L and ground area A under weight W at slip i, with j = i·x along the track, the thrust is
F = (A·c + W·tan φ)·(1 − (K/(i·L))·(1 − exp(−i·L/K))). Illustrative substitutions: b = 0.1 m, k_c = 1 kPa/mⁿ⁻¹,
k_φ = 1,500 kPa/mⁿ, n = 1.1 and p = 20 kPa give z = 19.6 mm; two tracks of 0.5 m by 0.1 m under 500 N with
c = 1 kPa, φ = 30° and K = 25 mm give a limit of 389 N and 293 N at 20% slip. A soil card declares the ranges of
pressure, slip and sinkage over which it holds, and a query outside them is refused (`FROB-REF-243`). The values
a gate pins are derived on file from a cited soil table read at its primary source (Appendix H); no value is
taken from a script that is not in the repository.

**Persistent deformation [M].** Ruts and footprints live in a per-world mutable buffer over shared static tiles.
A rut made in one episode must have no effect on another; reset and checkpoint fixtures detect the leak.

### 18.11 Contact capacity and the rule against capacity bias

Capacity is exposed per solver and revision: the lockstep capacities of §16.6 and the referee's cap of 21
(§16.4). It is checked twice: at model compile, from the static maximum of simultaneously active samples, and at
run time. Three responses to an overflow are allowed, and removing contacts is not one of them:

1. a documented **patch reduction** with its qualification fixture: the samples of a small patch become one
   four-dimensional contact with the patch's torsional capacity, provided the remaining contacts still carry the
   tilting moments the scenario needs;
2. a larger admitted solve (an upstream ask for the referee; a higher capacity and a new `StepPlanId` for the
   lockstep tiers);
3. a named refusal (`FROB-REF-721`, `FROB-REF-722`).

**Capacity bias.** A comparison between designs is honest only if both arms were simulated. DESIGN: when the
arms of a comparison differ by more than one percentage point of worlds in `Degraded` or capacity-refused
episodes, the verdict is labeled `capacity-biased`, the envelope prints the overflow of each arm, and the result
cannot be established (`FROB-REF-822` if cited). Fixture: a tracked design against a point-foot design under an
artificially low cap must return `capacity-biased` and no winner.

### 18.12 Precision: diagonal scaling, and why half precision is rejected

**Diagonal scaling [S].** Stage 8 solves the Newton system in scaled variables: Ĥ = S·H·S with S diagonal and each sᵢ
= 2^(−⌈eᵢ/2⌉), with eᵢ the binary exponent of Hᵢᵢ, which is the rule of §35.10 (`K-scale-diag` here and `K-scale`
there are one kernel). Reasons:

1. *Units.* Degrees of freedom mix metres and radians and their diagonal entries differ by orders of magnitude.
   One tolerance on the unscaled gradient is a dimension error. In scaled variables the gradient norm is
   dimensionless and one tolerance means the same thing for every robot.
2. *Conditioning that matters.* FACT (computed estimates on one humanoid, Appendix H): the condition number of H
   is 3 × 10⁴ to 10⁵ raw and 2 × 10³ to 9 × 10³ with unit-diagonal scaling at default compliant settings, and
   2 to 3 × 10⁶ raw and 2 to 5 × 10⁵ scaled with near-rigid contact. With single-precision round-off
   u = 6.0 × 10⁻⁸, κ·u is 0.12 to 0.18 raw and 0.01 to 0.03 scaled in the near-rigid case. The scaled figure
   governs the accuracy of the solve and the convergence of every iterative component (the passes of CL-H,
   stopping tests, line-search thresholds).
3. *Exactness.* Multiplying by powers of two changes exponents only, so the scaling introduces no rounding and costs
   no determinism class; κ(Ĥ) is within a factor of four of the unit-diagonal value, because the scaled diagonal
   entries span a ratio of up to four.

Counterexample for a plausible claim: scaling does not make the Cholesky factor more accurate. Without pivoting,
the computed factor of S·H·S is S times the computed factor of H bit for bit, absent overflow and underflow
(PO-TRAC-03), because its rounding errors are already those of the scaled problem. What scaling repairs is
everything around the factor. Admission: a card whose stiffness puts the estimated scaled κ·u above 0.05 on an
`f32` tier is refused there (`FROB-REF-244`) and runs on `batch-cpu` in `f64` (TARGET bound, OPEN-TRAC-4).

**Half precision is rejected.** The proposal was to factor H in half precision on matrix accelerators and recover
accuracy by refinement in single precision. The refuting calculation:

1. *Conditioning.* Half precision has round-off u₁₆ ≈ 4.9 × 10⁻⁴, and refinement contracts only if κ·u₁₆ < 1.
   Take a rank-one contact operator W with the proximal shift ρ = 10⁻²·tr(W)/48 for 16 contacts: its largest
   eigenvalue is tr(W), so κ = 1 + 48/0.01 = 4,801 and κ·u₁₆ = 2.34. FACT (emulated in software on three
   reference models, Appendix H): after unit-diagonal scaling, κ·u₁₆ ≥ 1 in 37 to 64% of sampled contact states,
   and a half-precision factor with single-precision refinement fails in 9 to 18% of them.
2. *Cost.* A single-precision Cholesky costs n³/3 flops and each refinement step about 3n², so k steps cost 9k/n
   times the factor they replace: 2.3 at n = 27 with seven steps, 1.5 at n = 35 with six, 3.0 at n = 18 with
   six. Refinement alone costs more than the factorization it was meant to cheapen, and the ratio falls below a
   quarter only above about 200 unknowns.
3. *Hardware.* Matrix accelerators speed large products; a per-world matrix of 18 to 64 rows is smaller than one
   panel, and Apple GPUs have none. The published gains were measured against double precision at 22,000
   unknowns, and the theory promises at most a factor of two against a single-precision baseline.

Nothing about contact solves survives. A request for half-precision solve or state storage is refused
(`FROB-REF-245`). Matrix accelerators are not adopted for policy networks of a few hundred units either (§23.8).

### 18.19 Response batches: anchor, acceptance, known answers, gate

**What it adds.** [F]. This subsection extends the paragraph "Response batches on a fixed active set" of §18.5,
which states the mechanism, its ceiling, its pilot and KILL-RESPONSE, and leaves four things unspecified: the
identity a batch is bound to, the tests a column must pass, the source of λ_lo, and a gate. Owner:
`frob-contact`. Verb: `contact.response`.

**Anchor (DESIGN).** A batch is keyed by an anchor digest over everything its factor depends on, at one
converged step of one world: the configuration, the mass matrix, the constraint Jacobian, the regularization,
the cone parameters, and the active set with a stick or slip label per row. A batch is served only against the
anchor it was factored for. Any other request refactors (refusal otherwise: response batch requested against a
stale anchor).

**Per-column acceptance.** A column b is accepted only when all four hold, each evaluated in the precision of
the step that produced the anchor:

1. the residual r = b − H·x̂ is formed explicitly, not estimated from the solve;
2. every sticking row remains strictly inside its cone, and the slack is reported;
3. no open row closes and no active normal force changes sign;
4. the bound ‖x̂ − x*‖₂ ≤ ‖r‖₂/λ_lo is attached to the column.

A column that fails test 2 or test 3 is rerouted to the full solve of §16.6 and counted.

**The lower bound λ_lo.** H = A + JᵀGJ with G positive semidefinite (§16.6), so a lower bound on the smallest
eigenvalue of A is one for H. λ_lo is a shift s for which A − s·I is shown positive definite by the
`rational-ldlt` proof kind of §12, computed once per anchor on the host; A depends on the configuration and
not on the active set, so the certificate serves every column of the batch. Where a tighter value is wanted the
same proof kind is applied to H − s·I. A batch whose λ_lo has no certificate reports its bounds as class
Estimated.

**Known answers, by hand.**

- *The bound, at equality.* H = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] has eigenvalues 1 and 3, so λ_lo = 1. For b = (3, 3) the
  solution is (1, 1). The perturbed x̂ = (1.01, 0.99) has residual (−0.01, 0.01) of norm 0.01414, and its true
  error also has norm 0.01414: the error lies along the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue. A bound formed
  with λ = 3 would claim 0.00471 and is violated; that mutation must be caught.
- *The mode guard.* A block rests on a floor with μ·N = 5 N and the anchor is in stick. A counterfactual
  tangential load of 4 N is accepted: friction answers 4 N and the block stays. A load of 6 N must be rerouted:
  on the frozen sticking set the linear response reports no motion, and the true response slides under a net
  force of 1 N.
- *The flop count.* At n = 96 unknowns and 64 columns, a full solve per column costs n³/3 + 2n² = 294,912 +
  18,432 = 313,344 flops, 20,054,016 in all. One factor and 64 solves cost 294,912 + 64 × 18,432 = 1,474,560.
  The ratio is 13.6, and it reaches 10 from 23 columns. Against a baseline that also factors once it is 1,
  which is the ceiling §18.5 already states.
