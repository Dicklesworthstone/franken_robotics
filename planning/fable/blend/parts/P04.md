## 19. Habitat: scenes, living worlds, clearance, retrofits

Owner crate: `frob-scene` (layer 2). Formats: `.fscene` (`frob.fscene/1`) and `.fclear` (`frob.fclear/1`).

Labels in this chapter: default values (spacings, capacities, tile sizes) are DESIGN choices with the reason given
where they are introduced; a worked example is a derivation from its stated premises and is not a measurement; every
dimension of a place is `assumed` (§19.8); thresholds in gate tables are TARGETs; statements about sibling code or
public sources are marked FACT with their source.

### 19.1 What it owns

Habitat owns every part of a world that is not the robot. That is: static geometry as distance fields; analytic
overlays for features smaller than a voxel; articulated fixtures, movable objects and moving surfaces; the assignment
of a contact interface and of an optical card to every surface region; optical volumes such as steam; the state of
living matter in a world (films, sloshing payloads, cords, rugs); semantic labels, spawn regions, goals and route
graphs; clearance complexes; the retrofit catalog; ingestion of real places; scene splits; and the world bank that
keeps compiled scenes resident on a device.

Habitat does not own contact laws and cones (§18), ray kernels and sensor models (§20), task and requirement
definitions (§24), mission queries (§25), adversarial search over scenes (§26) or calibration against measurement
(§30). It supplies the objects those subsystems read and the typed scene edits they apply.

| Object | Type or format | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| Scene pack | `ScenePack`, manifest `.fscene`, identity `SceneId` | Every subsystem that touches a world |
| Distance bricks, brick pool, variant directory | Device buffers described by the manifest | Traction (narrow phase), Sensorium (ray kernels), clearance |
| Analytic overlays | Typed primitive list with a spatial index | The same consumers as the bricks |
| Interface map and optical map | Region table with two independent card references per region | Traction reads the interface card; Sensorium reads the optical card |
| Fixtures, movables, moving surfaces | Articulated models and rigid bodies compiled by Skeleton (§14) | Kinesis (§16), Traction (§18) |
| Living-world state | Film patches and tiles, payload modules, cord and rug proxies | Traction (film friction), Kinesis (proxies), Sensorium (specular wet floors) |
| Clearance complex | `.fclear` | Atelier's maneuverability analysis (§28), Augur (§25), Nemesis (§26), the retrofit search |
| Retrofit catalog | `registries/retrofits.toml`, typed scene edits with prices | Nemesis, Augur, `scene.retrofit` |
| Scene split | `SceneSplit` with group identifiers | Arena (§24), Cortex (§23), the qualification lane |
| World bank | Resident set of compiled packs with an admission ledger | Spark's placement planner (§21) |

### 19.2 Decisions

- **ADR-HAB-01: static geometry is a truncated signed-distance field in bricks, sampled by manual trilinear
  interpolation written in the kernel source.** Reason: a distance field answers "how far, and which way" in constant
  time for any query point, which is what thousands of worlds need (§18), and hardware texture filtering uses
  fixed-point weights that differ between vendors and would break the determinism classes of §35.
- **ADR-HAB-02: a feature smaller than a voxel is an analytic overlay, never a voxel.** Reason: thresholds, rug edges,
  cable covers, grate slots and drain slots are between a few millimetres and a few centimetres (§19.8), a 2 cm
  lattice cannot represent them, and they are the features that decide home, kitchen and plant missions.
- **ADR-HAB-03: contact properties and optical properties are two maps, joined only by declared shared latent
  variables.** Reason: a glossy tile can produce a bad depth sample and low traction for unrelated reasons, and a
  model that ties them together fabricates a correlation.
- **ADR-HAB-04: the visual mesh is read by the viewer only.** Physics, sensors and stills read the collision field.
  The deviation between the two is measured per region and reported (§19.5). Reason: one authority for geometry inside
  the model identity, and a presentation asset that can be replaced without changing a digest.
- **ADR-HAB-05: one scene serves many worlds.** A device holds K scene variants in a shared brick pool and every world
  indexes one. One scene per world is not offered. Reason: the arithmetic of §19.15.
- **ADR-HAB-06: infeasible scenes are kept, tagged and counted.** A generator never resamples to hide a scene in which
  the nominal robot cannot reach the goal. Reason: rejection sampling changes the distribution without saying so, and
  the studio's most useful answer to some missions is "impossible, and here is the bottleneck".
- **ADR-HAB-07: living matter enters in three tiers, and only the Solid tier is on the critical path.** Reason: the
  strong form (coupled fluid, cloth and cables in training worlds) is refuted by the calculations of §19.10; a
  contact-level film law and rigid proxies capture the failures at negligible cost.
- **ADR-HAB-08: a clearance question has three answers: a feasibility witness, an impossibility cut that names the
  bottleneck, or `unresolved`.** Reason: a single traversability bit for a nominal footprint cannot be trusted for a
  rectangular or articulated body and cannot say why a body does not fit.
- **ADR-HAB-09: the building is a design variable.** Scene edits are typed and priced, and a retrofit is compared with
  a robot redesign in one budget. Reason: a failure caused by a 20 mm threshold may be cheaper to remove from the
  building than from the robot.
- **ADR-HAB-10: unobserved space is unknown, not free and not solid.** Occupancy is three-valued in every ingested
  scene, and each consumer states how it treats `unknown`. Reason: closing a scan can create a wall where a door
  exists, and leaving it open can create a route through a wall.
- **ADR-HAB-11: every dimension a generator draws carries provenance, and its provenance is `assumed` until a cited
  norm or a measurement is recorded.** Reason: realism claims need sources (OPEN-HAB-1), and validity does not.
- **ADR-HAB-12: generated scenes cross machines as seeds and identities, never as voxels.** Each machine compiles what
  it needs and checks the `SceneId`. Private real places do not cross at all unless exported with an explicit flag
  (§37). Reason: the division of labour between the machines (§21) and the privacy rule.

### 19.3 The scene pack

A `ScenePack` is immutable and content-addressed (`SceneId`). It contains:

- **A truncated signed-distance field in bricks**, quantized to 16 bits, at a fine level near surfaces and a coarse
  level everywhere, plus an optional heightfield for open terrain (§19.4 gives the layout and the reason the field is
  truncated).
- **Analytic overlays** for sub-voxel and sharp features: boxes, rounded boxes, wedges, cylinders and periodic bar
  patterns, each exact to round-off.
- **An interface map**: for each surface region, a material and a condition (dry, wet, greasy, dusty), which select
  contact cards (§18).
- **An optical map**: for each surface region, an optical card (§19.3.1), and a list of optical volumes with an
  extinction coefficient for steam and dust.
- **Shared latents**: named scalar variables per region, such as wetness, that drive the interface condition and the
  optical response through separately recorded conditionals.
- **Semantic labels**: room type, object class and a traversability class.
- **Articulated fixtures** (doors, drawers, cabinet doors, swing gates, lifts, carts) as articulated models simulated
  by the same engine as the robot, because a door is a one-joint mechanism and needs no special case. Each fixture
  carries its parameter table (§19.3.2).
- **Movable objects** (boxes, totes, chairs, pallets, toys) as rigid bodies, each with a declared behaviour.
- **Moving surfaces** (conveyors, escalators) as contact surfaces with prescribed tangential velocity, the same
  mechanism as a track (§18).
- **Living-world declarations**: film patches, sloshing payloads, cords and rugs, each at a declared tier (§19.10).
- **Spawn regions, goals, task regions and route graphs.**
- **A visual mesh in GLB for the viewer**, never read by physics or by sensors.
- **For ingested scenes**: a three-valued occupancy mask (free, occupied, unknown), per-region confidence, the
  registration residual and a scale label (§19.13).
- **An acceptance report** (§19.5) and a feasibility tag for the nominal footprint (§19.11).
- **Provenance**: a licence manifest per asset; generator name and version; the resolved value of every randomized
  quantity; the count of dimensions whose provenance is `assumed`; a group identifier used by splits (§19.14); a
  privacy mark; and the no-authorization flags of §12.

#### 19.3.1 Optical cards and optical volumes [S]

An optical card describes how a surface region responds to the active and passive optical sensors of §20. It is a
`.fcard` of kind `optical` with per-field provenance (`measured`, `datasheet`, `assumed`), in the same manner as an
interface card.

| Field | Meaning | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| `class` | `diffuse`, `glass`, `mirror`, `specular-floor`, `retroreflector`, `absorber`, `emitter` | Selects the ray rule of §20.6 |
| `r0` | Reflectance at normal incidence, used in a polynomial Fresnel approximation | Reflection weight for glass, mirrors and wet or polished floors |
| `transmittance` | Fraction passed through a thin pane | Through-glass returns |
| `albedo_ir`, `albedo_vis` | Diffuse albedo in the sensor bands | Return strength, speckle visibility |
| `roughness` | Width of the specular lobe | Whether a time-of-flight beam returns from a glossy surface off normal |
| `retro_gain` | Retroreflective gain | Saturation and bloom [F] |
| `emission` | Emitted irradiance in the sensor band (lamps, sunlit patches) | Ambient washout of projected patterns |

An **optical volume** is a box or ellipsoid with an extinction coefficient σ in m⁻¹ and a backscatter albedo. Steam
near a dishwasher, a kettle or a fryer, and dust in a warehouse, are optical volumes attached to a fixture with an on
and off schedule.

DESIGN: a region without an optical card is a compile refusal (`FROB-REF-316`), not a silent `diffuse`. A generator
that has no better information writes `diffuse` with provenance `assumed`, and the pack counts it. Reason: a default
that nobody chose is how a glass wall becomes a painted one.

The contact condition and the optical class of a region are independent fields. Where physics couples them, the
coupling is a declared latent: a region's `wetness` in [0, 1] selects the interface condition (dry or wet) through one
recorded conditional and raises `r0` and lowers `roughness` through another. Randomization draws the latent once, so a
wet floor is both slippery and glossy in the same episode, and each conditional has its own provenance.

#### 19.3.2 Fixtures and declared behaviours

| Fixture | Model | Parameters, each with provenance | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hinged door | Revolute joint | Hinge axis, leaf width and mass, closer torque law τ = τ₀ + k·θ + c·θ̇, latch state and release force, stop angle, handle geometry | Articulated |
| Sliding door, including glass | Prismatic joint | Track friction, travel, optional sensor zone that opens it | Articulated, or prescribed when automatic |
| Swing (saloon) door | Revolute joint with a two-way centring spring | Centring stiffness and damping, kick-plate height | Articulated |
| Drawer, cabinet door | Prismatic or revolute joint | Detent force, travel | Articulated |
| Swing gate, turnstile | Revolute joint with a one-way limit | Release torque | Articulated |
| Lift | Prescribed car motion with sliding doors | Door timing, sill gap, level offset | Prescribed |
| Conveyor, escalator | Moving surface | Belt speed profile, guard geometry | Prescribed |
| Cart, castor chair, wheeled tote | Free body with castor joints | Mass, castor trail, rolling and swivel friction | Free |
| People, forklifts, pets | Kinematic capsule actors on route graphs [F] | Speeds, yielding rule | Prescribed |

Every object that can move declares exactly one behaviour, and the choice is recorded in the episode manifest:

- **Static.** Part of the distance field.
- **Articulated.** Joints, limits and passive elements; pushing it transfers momentum and work.
- **Free.** A rigid body that receives two-way impulses.
- **Prescribed.** It follows a trajectory regardless of contact. It is an external energy source, and the energy
  ledger of §29 books the boundary work it does on the robot on its own line.

An object with no declared behaviour is a refusal (`FROB-REF-315`).

#### 19.3.3 What is shared and what is per world

Bricks, overlays, maps and fixture parameter tables are immutable and shared by every world that indexes the scene.
Fixture joint states, movable poses, film tiles, sloshing payload states and (where §18 admits them) rut fields are
**per-world mutable state** allocated from fixed-capacity pools and addressed by world. They are part of every
snapshot (§29).

INV-HAB-03 states the rule: a film tile wetted or a rut made in one world or one episode has no effect on any other
world or any later episode unless the task declares persistence across episodes, and then only for that world address.
The reset and checkpoint fixtures of §19.18 detect a leak.

### 19.4 The scene compiler

The compiler is a CPU job. Its stages are fixed and each writes a receipt into the acceptance report.

1. **Decode under limits.** Bytes, nesting depth of instances, polygon count, texture and material counts are bounded
   by the import profile; relative references resolve inside the pack; missing assets and unit conversions are
   recorded. A violated limit is `FROB-REF-303`.
2. **Build the metric transform graph.** Axes, handedness and units are mapped by an explicit matrix per source
   format. Negative and non-uniform scales are handled explicitly and baked before any support map or mass property is
   computed. Triangle winding is checked against the sign of the determinant of the composed node transform. The
   transform class of an import is established by §19.5.1 or the import is refused (`FROB-REF-302`).
3. **Tag.** Each part is tagged support, keep-out, movable, jointed, optical-only, transparent, deformable-by-proxy or
   unknown. A door leaf is split from its static jamb, with hinge limits, friction and swept volume.
4. **Check watertightness and orientation**, then compute signed distance with FrankenSim's distance-field machinery.
   FACT (`frankensim` at `483d09c682f2`): `fs-rep-sdf` provides `TiledSdf` (dense Morton-tiled grids with a
   constructed error enclosure and sphere-traced raycasts), `VdbGrid` (a sparse tile tree, root map to 32³ internal
   nodes to 8³ bitmasked leaves, with deterministic iteration order), `NarrowBand` and `AdaptiveSdf`; `fs-rep-mesh`
   provides generalized winding numbers (exact, and an octree approximation whose error is measured against exact), a
   BVH mesh chart with watertight ray tests and a repair pipeline with receipts; `fs-rep-frep` provides constructive
   solids as a DAG with value and gradient, a Lipschitz bound and an interval range derived from the same graph;
   `fs-topo` provides manifold and self-intersection certificates. The three representation crates depend on `fs-qty`
   only through `fs-evidence` (their manifests), so the scene compiler carries the toolchain risk tested at
   INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 and reaches them through the airlock like every other FrankenSim crate (§33). A scene that fails
   the watertight check is refused (`FROB-REF-301`), not patched. An import may request the repair pipeline
   explicitly; every closed hole is then recorded with its receipt and marked `derived: hole-fill`, and its
   neighbourhood becomes an unknown region (§19.13).
5. **Emit bricks, the directory and the pool** (below), with a recorded error bound per brick: FrankenSim's enclosure
   for the source chart, plus the trilinear interpolation error on the brick lattice, plus half a quantization step.
6. **Emit analytic overlays** for every part tagged sharp or thinner than two fine voxels (ADR-HAB-02), and the
   overlay index.
7. **Emit the region table and maps**: interface, optical, semantic, and for ingested scenes the unknown mask.
8. **Compile fixtures and movables** through the model compiler of §14, settle the movables on the referee until
   kinetic energy falls below a threshold, and store the settled poses so that a reset does not start with falling
   clutter.
9. **Check spawn poses** for interference and tip-over, compute the clearance complex for the nominal footprint and
   write the feasibility tag (§19.11).
10. **Run the acceptance fixtures** (§19.5).
11. **Freeze.** `SceneId` is the hash of the manifest, the compiler version and settings, units, tolerances, brick
    contents, overlays, maps and fixture tables. Device resources are allocated by the worker that loads the pack and
    are scoped to its manifest.

**Field layout (DESIGN).** The coarse level is a dense lattice of spacing S (default 16 cm indoors) over the whole
scene. The fine level has spacing s = S/8 (default 2 cm) and exists only in **band bricks**: 8³ fine samples for each
coarse cell that lies within the truncation distance of a surface. A per-variant directory holds one 32-bit pool slot
per coarse cell; two sentinel slots stand for "far outside" and "deep inside". Stored values are clamp(φ, −T, +T),
quantized to 16 bits, with T = 4 fine voxels (8 cm at 2 cm spacing).

The truncation corrects a defect of the earlier wording "quantized to 16 bits". Sixteen bits over a range of ±10 m
resolve 20 m / 65,536 ≈ 0.31 mm, which at a contact stiffness of 10⁵ N/m is a force step of about 30 N (DESIGN
arithmetic). Over ±8 cm the step is 0.16 m / 65,535 ≈ 2.4 µm, a ripple of at most half a step, or about 0.12 N per
contact sample at the same stiffness. The speculative contact margin of §18 is always smaller than T, so contact never
reads a clamped value. Ray kernels step by the coarse level where the fine level is absent.

**Region labels in bricks (DESIGN).** A band brick carries a palette of at most four region identifiers and a two-bit
selector per sample: 128 bytes per brick, 12.5% over the 1,024 bytes of distance samples. A brick that touches more
than four regions is recorded in the acceptance report and uses the nearest-region rule for the excess.

**Overlays (DESIGN).** The scene distance at a point is the minimum of the interpolated brick value and the exact
distance to each overlay listed for that point's index cell. The index is a lattice of 0.64 m cells with eight overlay
slots each; a cell that needs more is a compile refusal with the repair "bake at a finer level or raise the capacity"
(`FROB-REF-306`). A periodic bar pattern (a floor grate, a drain cover) is one overlay: the query point is folded
modulo the pitch and tested against one bar, which is exact and costs no more than a box. The minimum of exact
distances is exact outside the union and a lower bound on depth inside it, which is the side contact uses.

Field sampling in kernels is **manual trilinear interpolation in the kernel source**, not hardware texture filtering
(ADR-HAB-01).

### 19.5 Scene acceptance [S]

A compiled scene is not usable until its acceptance report is complete. Acceptance is a set of mechanical checks with
known outcomes; none is a matter of taste. Verb: `scene.accept`. The report is part of the pack and its hash is in the
manifest.

#### 19.5.1 Registration: four points, because three accept a reflection

An imported scene is placed by a transform whose class must be established, not assumed. Three labelled, non-collinear
landmarks and a known length fix a proper rigid registration. A reflection through the plane of the three landmarks
passes the same three checks with zero residual.

Smallest case, checkable by hand: landmarks A = (0, 0, 0), B = (1, 0, 0), C = (0, 1, 0). The map (x, y, z) ↦ (x, y,
−z) fixes all three, so a mirrored room registers perfectly. A fourth landmark D = (0, 0, 1), for example a ceiling
marker, maps to (0, 0, −1) and shows a residual of 2 m. A gravity witness does not replace the fourth point: if the
three landmarks lie in a vertical plane, the reflection through that plane preserves gravity.

Rule (DESIGN): an import whose format or pipeline admits a parity change or a general affine map requires four
non-coplanar correspondences. With four, the affine map is determined; the compiler checks that its linear part L
satisfies LᵀL = c²·I within the registration tolerance and det L > 0, and that c matches the scale witness. The
tetrahedron of the four landmarks must have a volume of at least 1% (TARGET) of the cube of their largest separation,
so that the fourth point is not nearly coplanar. A glTF camera looks along local −Z with +Y up (FACT: the Khronos glTF
2.0 specification, to be re-read before a gate cites it); the conversion to the optical frame of §20 is checked with
an off-axis point in front of the lens. Failure of any of these is `FROB-REF-302`.

#### 19.5.2 One-sided enclosures for clearance evidence

A statement that a body clears an obstacle, or that it must hit it, is made on enclosures, never on nominal surfaces.

- The **outer enclosure** of a body or an obstacle contains every point it can occupy, given the field error bound,
  the registration residual, the pose uncertainty and the swept motion.
- The **inner enclosure** is contained in the occupied set under the same uncertainties.

Disjoint outer enclosures establish clearance. Inner enclosures whose interiors intersect at the same time and pose,
in one jointly admissible world, witness penetration in that world. Anything else is `unknown` and triggers refinement
or a measurement request.

Smallest case: a nominal 6 mm gap at a doorway with 4 mm of possible outward error on each side. The outer enclosures
overlap by 2 mm, so clearance is not established; the inner enclosures are 14 mm apart, so penetration is not
witnessed. The verdict is `unknown`. Two further traps are tested: spatial sweeps of two bodies can overlap although
the bodies are never in the same place at the same time, and a universal penetration claim must cover every admitted
world, not one.

These enclosures are the cells of the clearance complex (§19.11) and the evidence behind any statement of the form
"fits" or "cannot fit".

#### 19.5.3 Collision-to-visual deviation

For each region tagged collidable, the compiler samples the visual mesh surface and evaluates the collision field
there. It records two quantities and keeps their evidence types apart: a **bounded** deviation where the sampling
density and the unit Lipschitz constant of a distance field give a bound (the largest sampled value plus the sample
spacing), and a **sampled** deviation elsewhere. A region whose deviation exceeds half a fine voxel plus the brick
bound is listed in the report and, above the pack's threshold, refused without a waiver (`FROB-REF-317`). Parts of the
visual mesh with no collision counterpart must carry the tag `optical-only`.

Stills for agents are drawn from the collision field by ray kernels (§20.9), so an agent looks at what physics has.
The viewer draws the GLB and can overlay the deviation as a heat map.

#### 19.5.4 Optical properties are separate from friction

Three checks enforce ADR-HAB-03. Changing the optical card of a region leaves every physics digest unchanged in a run
whose controller does not consume exteroception. Changing the interface card of a region leaves every ray-kernel
output unchanged. Changing a shared latent changes both in the recorded direction. The fixture is one floor tile in
four states: matte dry, glossy dry, matte wet, glossy wet.

#### 19.5.5 Infeasible cases are kept, tagged and counted

Every generated scene carries a feasibility tag for the family's nominal footprint: `witnessed`, `impossible` with the
cut attached, or `unresolved` (§19.11). A family may declare a quota of intentionally impossible scenes, tagged
`impossible: intended`; scenes that are impossible without having been intended are tagged `impossible: incidental`.
No scene is resampled because of its tag. Split manifests state the count in each class, scorecards state which
classes entered a metric, and a success rate is always reported with its numerator and denominator.

#### 19.5.6 Acceptance fixtures

| Fixture | What it checks | Passing outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Metric calibration board | A board of known dimensions placed in the scene is measured by ray kernels | Measured lengths equal the known lengths within the brick bound |
| Door-clearance gauges | For each door of clear width w and field bound b, a go gauge of width w − 2b and a no-go gauge of width w + 2b are swept through | The go gauge passes and the no-go gauge is blocked |
| Normal-direction probe | ∇φ at probes on each tagged surface | Normals point out of solids; floor normals are +z within tolerance |
| Support test | A unit block is released above each support surface on the referee | It comes to rest on the surface; a floor that exists only in the renderer fails here |
| Ray and visual registration | Identifier buffers drawn from the field and from the GLB at fixed viewpoints | Silhouette overlap at or above the pack threshold |
| Quantization ripple | A 10 kg block at rest on a brick floor with no overlay | Normal-force ripple at most 1% of weight (TARGET) |
| Tile continuity | Rays and swept wheels crossing brick and tile boundaries | No gap or step at a boundary |
| Mutable-state leak | Wet a tile in world i, reset, and read world j and the next episode of world i | No trace outside the declared persistence |

FACT: FrankenSim's `fs-scene` crate exists because robot experiments there had floors that were drawn by a renderer
and absent from physics, so that a robot could stand inside the floor; the support test is the same guard.

### 19.6 Terrain generators [S]

Deterministic functions of (family, parameters, seed) with a scalar difficulty: flat ground, slopes, stairs (rise and
run), single steps, gaps, stepping stones, rough ground (band-limited noise), rubble (random boxes), ramps and curbs.
Families tile into a curriculum grid in the manner of the legged-locomotion benchmarks.

Open terrain is a heightfield; overhangs, vertical faces and culverts are overlays or band bricks on top of it. Soil
laws under wheels, tracks and legs are Traction's (§18); Habitat supplies the material layers they read.

A **mixed route** family joins the two regimes: an exterior approach, a sill, a change of floor material and of
lighting, and a jointed door into an interior. It exists because the transition is where timing, traction and sensing
change at once, and neither a terrain family nor an interior family exercises it.

### 19.7 Interior generators: seven archetypes

Each archetype is a typed attribute grammar: Building → Floors → Rooms → Zones → Fixtures → Clutter, with dimensional
constraints (door widths, aisle widths, counter heights, stair geometry) and a seed. Geometry is emitted as
constructive solids and compiled by §19.4. Repeated modules (rack bays, shelving units, desks) are placed on the
coarse lattice where the grammar allows, so that their bricks are identical and are stored once (§19.15).

Delivery is proxy first. The **structural proxy** of each archetype is [S] and lands at M3: boxes and fixtures with
exact functional clearances, doors, ramps, thresholds, stairs, film patches, glass and mirror regions and a lighting
level. Scenes populated from asset libraries are [F] and land at M5. Within M3 the warehouse and the commercial
kitchen come first, because the M3 scenarios need them; the other five follow from the same grammar mechanism.

| Archetype | What it contains | What it tests in a robot | Failure quantity to expose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Rooms off a hallway, thresholds, rugs, stairs, furniture, cords, small floor clutter, pets and toys | Step and threshold crossing, narrow doors, clutter avoidance | Wheel slip at a rug edge, pitch excursion on a threshold, strike of an underslung load |
| Apartment | Compact plan, tight turns, a lift lobby with a sill | Turning radius, maneuvering index (C-07), docking | Clearance distribution, turning envelope, odometry drift |

### 19.10 Living worlds in three tiers

A place changes because a robot is in it: a wheel drags oil out of a patch, a carried pot sloshes, a castor pushes a
rug into a fold, a cord wraps an axle. A scene whose only state is rigid geometry and a friction map cannot produce
these failures. The strong response, coupled fluid, cloth and cable solvers in every training world, is refuted
(§19.10.1). The plan therefore admits living matter in three tiers and keeps only the first on the critical path.

| Tier | Mechanism | State it adds | Evidence class of what it outputs | First milestone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [S] | Film-friction patches: a contact-level law in which friction on a wetted region depends on dwell time, load and pad size | One thickness value per contact sample | Estimated | M3 |
| [S] | Rigid proxies: a rug as a thin free slab, a cord as a fixed ridge or a free rod, clutter and toys as rigid bodies | Ordinary rigid bodies | Tested | M3 |
| [F] | Robot-advected films: a 2.5-dimensional film field the robot spreads and carries | Sparse film tiles per world, one reservoir per contact body | Estimated | M6 |
| [F] | Payload slosh by an equivalent mechanical model | One to three sprung masses per horizontal axis | Estimated; Known answer for modal frequency | M6 |
| [F] | Articulated proxies: a cord as a chain of rigid links, a rug as hinged plates | Articulated bodies | Tested | M6 |
| [M] | Coupled islands: free-surface lattice fluid, implicit cloth, Cosserat rod, on the CPU referee for finalists only | An island beside one world | Estimated | After M6, behind a flag |

Capabilities of this kind are not new in the field. FACT (public documentation read on 2026-11-21, to be re-read from
the primary source before a gate cites it): incumbent engines ship cables, cloth and particle solvers coupled to rigid
robots, and one documents that it does not guarantee determinism for scenes with non-rigid bodies. Nothing here is
claimed as a first. The claim is narrower: the Solid and Frontier mechanisms stay inside the determinism classes of
§35 because they are rigid bodies, joints and integer-indexed fields.

#### 19.10.1 The calculations that remove the naive versions

All of the following is DESIGN arithmetic from stated assumptions; none is a measurement.

1. **Memory of a room of fluid.** A D3Q19 lattice in single precision with two population buffers needs 19 × 4 × 2 =
   152 bytes per cell. A 10 m × 10 m × 3 m room at 1 cm is 3.0 × 10⁸ cells, or 45.6 GB for one world; at 5 mm it is
   2.4 × 10⁹ cells, or 365 GB, more than the working-set limit of the large-memory machine. At 8,192 worlds the 1 cm
   case is about 374 TB. One cubic metre at 5 mm is 8 × 10⁶ cells and 1.22 GB.
2. **A spill is thinner than any affordable lattice.** A film 0.1–1 mm thick needs cells of 0.25 mm or less to be
   resolved through its depth. One square metre of floor with a 4 mm slab of such cells is 4,000 × 4,000 × 16 = 2.56 ×
   10⁸ cells, or 39 GB per square metre.
3. **Lattice Mach number at control rate.** The lattice sound speed is Δx/(√3·Δt), so a flow of speed u has Mach
   number Ma = √3·u·Δt/Δx. Stepping the fluid once per 50 Hz control tick (Δt = 20 ms) with u = 1 m/s gives Ma = 0.35
   at 10 cm cells, 3.5 at 1 cm and 6.9 at 5 mm. FACT: `fs-lbm` declares `MACH_LIMIT = 0.3` for its low-Mach regime
   (`frankensim/crates/fs-lbm/src/lib.rs`). To hold Ma at 0.1 with 5 mm cells the fluid needs Δt ≤ 0.29 ms, which is
   70 fluid steps per control tick.
4. **Relaxation time.** The lattice relaxation time is τ = 3·ν·Δt/Δx² + ½. For water (ν = 10⁻⁶ m²/s) at 10 cm cells
   and 20 ms steps, τ − ½ = 6 × 10⁻⁶; at 5 mm cells and the 0.29 ms step it is 3.5 × 10⁻⁵. A single-relaxation scheme
   is not stable that close to ½.
5. **Throughput.** If coupled matter is active in 5% of worlds and the batch must keep at least a tenth of its
   rigid-only throughput, a coupled world may cost at most c rigid worlds, with 0.95 + 0.05·c ≤ 10, so c ≤ 181. FACT:
   the sibling cloth solver reports drapes that take tens of CPU seconds for a few thousand vertices and states that
   it has no GPU path by design (`franken_textiles/README.md`); the sibling rod solver states that it is fixture-scale
   statics and that dynamics on SE(3) are successor scope (`frankensim/crates/fs-solid/src/rod.rs`); FrankenSim lists
   GPU execution as not implemented (`frankensim/README.md`). A batched rigid step costs microseconds per world. The
   bound is missed by orders of magnitude.

Conclusion: no fluid lattice, cloth solver or rod solver runs inside a training batch. What the failures need is a
friction that depends on contact history, and objects that can be pushed, lifted and wrapped. Both can be had from
mechanisms the engine already has.

#### 19.10.2 Solid: film-friction patches and rigid proxies

**The film patch.** A film patch is a region with a fluid class (density, viscosity η), an initial thickness h₀ and a
wetness latent. In the Solid tier the patch is static and inexhaustible: the robot neither removes nor carries film.

**Drainage under a contact.** Each contact sample on a patch carries one state variable, the film thickness h under
it, set to h₀ at first touch and reset when the sample leaves the patch. For a flat circular pad of radius R pressed
with normal force F on a Newtonian film, the squeeze-film relation gives dh/dt = −2·F·h³ / (3π·η·R⁴) (FACT: Stefan's
squeeze-film formula from lubrication theory, to be re-read from a primary source before a gate cites it).
Equivalently

  d(h⁻²)/dt = 4·F / (3π·η·R⁴),

so h⁻² grows linearly at a rate set by the load. The update h⁻²(t + Δt) = h⁻²(t) + 4·F·Δt/(3π·η·R⁴) is exact for a
load that is constant over the step, is unconditionally stable, and needs one division and one square root, which the
kernel dialect allows.

**Friction from the film.** With σ the combined roughness on the interface card, the film ratio is Λ = h/σ. The
coefficient offered to the cone of §18 is μ = w(Λ)·μ_b + (1 − w(Λ))·μ_v, where μ_b is the boundary coefficient of the
card for that condition, μ_v = min(μ_b, η·|v_t|·πR² / (h·F)) is the viscous shear contribution at tangential speed
v_t, and w(Λ) = clamp((3 − Λ)/2, 0, 1) weights boundary contact fully below Λ = 1 and not at all above Λ = 3. The
coefficient used in a step is computed from the state at the start of the step, so the contact solve of §18 stays
convex. Habitat owns the patch and the thickness state; Traction owns the cone row that consumes μ.

**Hand-checkable consequences.** The time for a thick film to thin to h₁ is t = 3π·η·R⁴ / (4·F·h₁²). To reach 10 µm:

| Pad and fluid (viscosities assumed) | R | F | Time to 10 µm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small foot on water, η = 1.0 mPa·s | 20 mm | 100 N | 0.038 s |
| Small foot on cooking oil, η = 60 mPa·s | 20 mm | 100 N | 2.3 s |
| Large flat foot on water | 60 mm | 400 N | 0.76 s |

Against a stance of about 0.3 s (assumed), a small foot on water reaches boundary contact within the stance, the same
foot on oil does not, and a large flat foot on water does not. Friction on a film therefore depends on dwell time,
load and pad size. A constant coefficient cannot express that, and no fluid grid is needed to express it.

**Validity.** The law assumes a smooth, rigid, flat pad. Treads and rough floors drain faster, so the drainage time is
an upper bound and the predicted slip is pessimistic for treaded soles (HYPOTHESIS; the test is a spill-pad traction
dataset imported through the Reality lane of §30, and until one exists every output of the law is `Estimated` and the
Reality cell is `no-claim`). The roughness σ is `assumed` on every card until measured (OPEN-HAB-8).

**Rigid proxies.** A rug is a thin free rigid slab with its own rug-to-floor friction and an edge step. A cord is a
fixed bevelled ridge where it is taped down and a free rigid rod where it is not. Toys, wet-floor signs, pallets and
debris are free rigid bodies. A proxy uses no mechanism that §16 to §18 lack.

A fixed step is not a sufficient proxy for a rug, and the reason can be checked by hand. To push an undriven wheel of
radius r carrying load W over an edge of height h takes a horizontal force W·tan α, with tan α = √(h·(2r − h)) / (r −
h). A castor with r = 25 mm at a 10 mm rug edge needs 4/3 of its load: 67 N for a 50 N castor. A 2 kg rug resists
sliding with about 0.5 × 19.6 N ≈ 10 N (friction assumed). The rug slides and folds long before the castor climbs it.
A fixed step would simply be climbed or would block; a free slab reproduces the push.

#### 19.10.3 Frontier: robot-advected films

**What it is.** The film thickness h(x, y, t) becomes a per-world field on a 2-dimensional lattice (cell edge Δx,
default 2 cm) stored in sparse tiles of 16 × 16 cells that are allocated from a per-device pool when first wetted.
Each contact body carries a reservoir volume V_res with a capacity. Owner: `frob-scene` for the field and its update
kernel, `frob-contact` for the friction it feeds.

**Update.** Finite volume, conservative. For each cell, h ← h − (Δt/A)·Σ_faces F_f with face flux

  F_f = [ 1_f·h_up·(v̄·n)_f − (g/(3ν))·h_f³·(∇h·n)_f ]·L_f,

where the first term is transport by the sliding contact (1_f marks faces under a contact patch, h_up is the upwind
thickness, v̄ the mean tangential velocity of the patch) and the second is gravity spreading in the lubrication limit.
Pickup moves volume from the cells under a contact into that body's reservoir; redeposition moves it back to the cells
the body later touches. Each transfer is one paired statement, so what leaves one store enters the other.

**Counterexample that seeds a negative control.** Writing the transport as 1·∇·(h·v̄), with the contact indicator
outside the divergence, creates or destroys film at the patch boundary. Three cells with h = (0, 1, 0), the indicator
on the middle cell only and v̄ = +1: the masked update removes Δt/Δx from the middle cell and adds it nowhere. The
face-flux form moves the same amount into the right-hand cell. The negative control runs the wrong form and must fail
the mass gate.

**Stability and refusal.** Gravity spreading is a nonlinear diffusion with coefficient D = g·h³/(3ν). The explicit
update is stable for Δt ≤ Δx²/(4·D), which bounds the thickness: h_max = (3ν·Δx² / (4·g·Δt))^(1/3). For water at Δx =
2 cm and Δt = 2 ms this is 2.5 mm; for a 65 mm²/s oil it is 10 mm (DESIGN arithmetic). A deeper liquid is not a film;
it is refused or sub-cycled at admission (`FROB-REF-307`).

**Cost.** A tile is 1,024 bytes of thickness. A 6 m × 6 m floor fully wet is 352 tiles, about 360 kB per world; a 1 m²
spill is about 16 kB. An exhausted pool marks the world `Degraded: film-pool`. TARGET: at most 15% added step cost
with a film active in every world.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** The mechanism adds cost and contributes nothing to
throughput. Its benefit is the number of failure classes it produces that a static patch cannot, of which the clearest
is a robot that tracks film onto a dry floor and slips on its own second pass.

**Paper-scale pilot and falsifier.** With a carried film of 20 µm, a transfer fraction of one half per wheel
revolution and a roughness of 5 µm (all assumed), the deposited film stays at or above Λ = 1 for two revolutions, 1.26
m for a 0.1 m wheel. The pilot repeats this arithmetic over the admitted ranges of the three parameters. If the
tracked low-friction length is under one wheel circumference across those ranges, advection changes no outcome and the
work package is not funded.

**Known answers.** A constant volume released on a flat floor spreads with radius proportional to t^(1/8) in the
axisymmetric case and front position proportional to t^(1/5) in the planar case (FACT: Huppert's similarity solutions
for viscous gravity currents, to be re-read before a gate cites them). Total volume, cells plus reservoirs, is
constant.

#### 19.10.4 Frontier: payload slosh

A carried liquid is modeled by the standard equivalent mechanical model: a rigid mass fixed in the container plus, for
each retained mode n, a mass m_n on a spring k_n = m_n·ω_n² and a damper, sliding horizontally at a fixed height. For
a rectangular container of length L along the motion filled to depth h, the antisymmetric modes have

  ω_n² = ((2n − 1)·π·g / L) · tanh((2n − 1)·π·h / L),  n = 1, 2, 3

(FACT: linear sloshing theory, to be re-read from a primary source before a gate cites it). Modal masses and heights
come from the same sources and are entered on a payload card with provenance; hyperbolic functions are evaluated on
the CPU when the card is admitted, never in a batched kernel.

The model is a payload module with prismatic sprung masses, which are ordinary joints and passive elements, so it runs
on every tier and inside every determinism class. Owner: `frob-scene` supplies the payload module; the model compiler
of §14 lowers it.

Known answer: for L = 0.30 m and h = 0.10 m the first mode is ω₁ = 8.96 rad/s, 1.43 Hz. That lies inside the
step-frequency band of walking robots (1–2.5 Hz, assumed), which is why a carried tray is a locomotion problem and not
only a manipulation problem.

Validity: small amplitude. When a modal displacement exceeds a fraction of L set on the card, the result is flagged
outside the validity domain; the model does not pretend to predict a wave that breaks. Benefit ceiling: none on
throughput; it exposes resonance between gait and payload. Pilot and falsifier: on paper, compare ω₁ for the hazard
list's container range with the step-frequency band; if they do not overlap for any admitted container, slosh cannot
destabilize a gait and the work package is not funded. ✖ **KILL-SLOSH.** Trigger: the frequency gate fails after one
repair cycle, or the sprung masses change no ranking of gaits or designs on the carried-container battery. Fallback:
rigid ballast, with a spill metric computed from payload acceleration.

#### 19.10.5 Frontier: articulated cord and rug proxies

**Chain cord.** A cord is 8 to 20 rigid capsule links joined by two-axis joints with bending stiffness and damping,
lying on the floor with friction. It can be dragged, hooked by a foot and wrapped round an axle, which a friction
patch and a fixed ridge cannot represent, because entanglement is a matter of topology. A cord is its own articulated
island coupled to the robot only through contact, so its degrees of freedom enter the padded capacities of §17 and not
the robot's mass matrix. Known answer: a chain 1.2 m long hung between supports 1.0 m apart sags 0.292 m (the
catenary, with parameter a solving 1.2 = 2a·sinh(0.5/a)); 20 links reproduce it within 2% (TARGET).

**Hinged-plate rug.** A rug is two to four thin rigid plates hinged along lines parallel to a free edge, with
torsional stiffness at the hinges and rug-to-floor friction. A wheel at the free edge can lift or fold it.

**Cost, pilot and kill.** TARGET: a world with one cord costs at most twice a rigid-only world of the same robot.
Benefit ceiling: the count of failure classes found that the Solid proxies miss; none on throughput. The pilot is the
proxy-adequacy experiment of §19.10.7, read in the other direction: its falsifier for this tier is that the fixed
ridge and the free slab already reproduce the chain's and the plates' failures. ✖ **KILL-PROXY.** Trigger: that
falsifier, or a cost above the target with no route to less. Fallback: Solid proxies in training batches; articulated
proxies in evaluation batches only, or not at all.

#### 19.10.6 Moonshot: coupled islands

An island is a small region of resolved matter beside one world on the CPU referee: a free-surface lattice fluid in a
box of at most about one cubic metre, an implicit cloth for one rug or curtain, or a Cosserat rod for one cable. It is
used for exactly two purposes: as an oracle against which the Solid and Frontier models are checked on finalists, and
to verify a counterexample that Nemesis (§26) attributes to living matter. It never runs in a training batch and
nothing on the critical path waits for it.

FACT: the pieces exist in the siblings as CPU code with their own evidence: `fs-lbm` has D3Q19 and D3Q27 lattices and
a mass-tracking free-surface module whose ledger is gated every step; `fs-tribo` has a quasistatic Reynolds-film
pressure solver and a regularized Stribeck friction law; `fs-solid` has geometrically exact rods for statics. `fs-lbm`
and `fs-tribo` depend on `fs-qty` and are reached only through the airlock (§33). Dynamic rods and a batched cloth are
not in the siblings and are asks upstream, never local beads.

Cost, from §19.10.1: one cubic metre at 5 mm holding Ma ≤ 0.1 at 1 m/s needs about 3,460 fluid steps of 8 × 10⁶ cells
per simulated second. That is an offline computation.

#### 19.10.7 The proxy-adequacy pilot and the kill rule

One experiment decides whether the Moonshot tier is ever funded, and it needs no new solver. On an incumbent engine
that already ships cable and cloth (pinned by the rival audit of §39, used as an instrument outside the product):
three scenes (a cord across the path, a rug with a free edge, one square metre of oil), 200 randomized episodes each,
one wheeled and one legged robot. Rerun with the proxies of this section: a 20-link chain, a hinged thin plate, a
friction patch, and the patch with the dwell-time law. The measure is the share of full-model failures that the proxy
reproduces in the same failure class within one second. Estimated cost: one engineer-week and under ten GPU-hours
(TARGET).

✖ **KILL-LIVING.** Trigger: the proxies reproduce 90% or more of the full-model failures, or an island costs more than
181 rigid worlds at 5% activity with no route to less. Fallback, named now: the Solid tier and the Frontier proxies
are the product; coupled islands are not built beyond the oracle fixtures that already exist in the siblings, and
reports say that deformable matter is represented by proxy.

✖ **KILL-FILM.** Trigger: the advected film fails its mass gate after one repair cycle, or costs more than 15% of the
step, or design rankings on the spill battery agree with those from static patches at a rank correlation of 0.95 or
above. Fallback: static film-friction patches.

### 19.11 Clearance complexes (`.fclear`) [F]

**The question.** Can this body get from here to there in this place? The earlier answer was a route graph and a
traversability check for a nominal footprint. A scalar test of that kind is wrong in both directions for a body that
is not a disc. Take a rectangular base 0.60 m wide in a corridor 0.90 m wide with a right-angle turn. Its inscribed
disc (diameter 0.60 m) says every length fits. Its circumscribed disc says that a 1.30 m long body (diagonal 1.43 m)
cannot. The truth is that the 1.30 m body passes and a 1.40 m body does not, by the closed form below.

**The object.** A clearance complex is a cubical subdivision of the planar configuration space SE(2) = (x, y, ψ) of a
footprint in one height slab of a scene, for one assignment of fixture modes. Owner: `frob-scene`. Verb: `scene.fit`.
Format: `.fclear`.

- The **footprint family** is a base shape F₀ with reference radius ρ (the largest distance from its reference point),
  inflated by a non-negative margin w: F(w) = F₀ ⊕ disc(w). A catalog of chassis widths, a payload overhang or a
  safety margin is one parameter w.
- The **clearance** c(q) of a configuration q is the distance from F₀ placed at q to the nearest obstacle in the slab,
  negative inside an obstacle. F(w) at q is collision-free exactly when c(q) > w.
- Each cell is a closed box with centre q₀, translation edge Δ and heading width δ. Because a distance function has
  Lipschitz constant one and a point at radius at most ρ moves at most ρ·|Δψ| under a rotation, every q in the cell
  satisfies |c(q) − c(q₀)| ≤ e, with

  e = (Δ/2)·√2 + ρ·(δ/2) + ε_field + u_reg,

  where ε_field is the scene's field bound and u_reg the registration uncertainty of an ingested scene. The cell
  stores the one-sided values c_lo = c(q₀) − e and c_hi = c(q₀) + e, two 16-bit numbers in millimetres. These are the
  enclosures of §19.5.2.
- The **inner complex** 𝒞_in(w) is the set of cells with c_lo > w: every configuration in them is free. The **outer
  complex** 𝒞_out(w) is the set with c_hi > w: every free configuration lies in one of them. Cells are adjacent across
  faces, with ψ periodic.

**Three answers.**

1. A face-connected path of inner cells from the start cell to a goal cell is a **feasibility witness**: the polyline
   through cell centres and shared faces stays inside boxes that are entirely free.
2. If the start and every goal cell lie in different components of the outer complex, no collision-free path exists
   for margin w: an **impossibility cut**. Face adjacency suffices for this direction because the cells are closed: a
   free configuration on a shared edge or corner makes every cell that contains it an outer cell, and those cells are
   face-connected round that edge.
3. Otherwise the answer is `unresolved`, and the cells between the two verdicts are returned for refinement.

**The bottleneck.** One sweep answers every margin. Cells are sorted by c_lo and added to a union-find structure in
decreasing order; the value at which the start and goal components merge is the largest certified margin w_in, and the
cell that merges them is the bottleneck. The same sweep on c_hi gives w_out, the margin at and above which passage is
impossible, and its bottleneck. (This is zero-dimensional persistence of the clearance filtration. FACT:
`fs-topo::cubical` implements zero-dimensional persistence for voxel fields, with chart voxelization capped at 10⁶
cells and no periodic axis, so `frob-scene` implements the sweep itself and uses `fs-topo` as a parity oracle on small
fixtures.) The bottleneck cell is mapped through the semantic labels to a named feature, so the answer reads "door D3,
certified to 31 mm of margin, impossible at 46 mm", not a cell index.

**Size and the default lattice.** A 12 m × 8 m floor at Δ = 5 cm with 32 headings is 240 × 160 × 32 = 1,228,800 cells,
4.69 MiB at four bytes per cell. With ρ = 0.40 m the error term is 35.4 mm + 39.3 mm = 74.6 mm before field and
registration error.

**Paper-scale pilot, already decisive on one point.** A body centred in a doorway has clearance equal to half the
total margin, and a witness needs c_lo > 0, so at the default lattice a doorway fit with a total margin under 2e ≈ 149
mm is `unresolved`. The hazard list puts home doors at 0.70–0.90 m and plausible bases at 0.45–0.70 m; if both are
drawn uniformly, about 22% of pairs have a margin under 149 mm, and they are the near fits, the only cases in which
the question is hard. The default lattice alone is therefore not the mechanism. **Local refinement is part of it**:
cells whose interval [c_lo, c_hi] straddles the query margin and that lie on a candidate bottleneck are subdivided in
x, y and ψ. At Δ = 12.5 mm and 128 headings e is 18.7 mm; at Δ = 5 mm and 512 headings it is 6.0 mm (DESIGN
arithmetic). Below that the floor is 2·(ε_field + u_reg): about 2 mm in a generated scene, and tens of millimetres in
a scan, where the correct output is a measurement request (§19.13).

**Envelope beyond the plane.** The slab is chosen from the body's envelope: obstacles lower than its step-over height
or higher than its top are outside the slab. Thresholds, ramps, stairs, lift sills and docking plates are
**transitions** in the route graph, each with a capability predicate (riser at most the body's step height, slope at
most its traction limit, gap at most its wheel diameter criterion). A route verdict is the conjunction of the planar
verdict in each slab and the predicates on its transitions. A door that is closed but openable is a transition whose
predicate is the body's push capability against the closer torque, not a wall.

**What the witness does and does not say.** The witness is geometric. For a base whose growth vector reaches full rank
with reversal permitted (the maneuverability analysis of §28, construction C-07), a path with positive clearance can
be followed by maneuvering inside that clearance (FACT: the standard small-time local controllability argument for
car-like and differential-drive bases, to be re-read before a gate cites it), and the witness is labeled `kinematic`;
the number of maneuvers is not bounded by the witness and its cost is C-07's estimate. For any other base the label is
`geometric-only`. A witness is never a statement about dynamics, traction or control: static rejection and static
admission are both candidates for a dynamic test, not substitutes for one.

**Unknown regions.** In an ingested scene, `unknown` space is blocked in the inner complex and free in the outer
complex. A witness never crosses unobserved space; an impossibility cut never relies on it.

**Certificates.** A witness is the list of cells on the path; a checker recomputes c(q₀) and e for each and tests
adjacency. A cut is a component labeling of the outer complex; a checker tests that every unlabeled cell has c_hi ≤ w,
that no two adjacent labeled cells carry different labels, and that start and goal labels differ. Both checks are
linear in the number of cells and need only the scene field, so they fit the certificate bundle of §12. Their class is
`Certified` relative to the compiled scene and its stated bounds. They say nothing about the real building beyond what
the scene's provenance supports.

**Closed-form known answer.** A rectangle of width b and length L can be carried round a right-angle corner between
corridors of width a exactly when L ≤ 2√2·a − 2b (for b < a). Derivation: with the outer long side touching both outer
walls at inclination θ, the inner corner clears the body when L ≤ (a·sin θ + a·cos θ − b) / (sin θ·cos θ); the
right-hand side is smallest at θ = 45°. For a = 0.90 m and b = 0.60 m the limit is 1.346 m. The complex must return a
witness for L below the limit minus its band and a cut above the limit plus its band.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** The complex saves the simulation and training that would be
spent on bodies that cannot fit, and it replaces "the policy failed" with "the door is 15 mm too narrow". The saving
is bounded by the share of candidate designs that geometry alone excludes, which is unknown and is measured on the M3
design corpus. An application built on an incumbent planning library could build the same index; the mechanism is not
claimed as unique, and no multiplier is claimed for it.

✖ **KILL-FCLEAR.** Trigger: one false witness or one false cut that survives a repair cycle, or more than 30% of
doorway queries on the archetype battery still `unresolved` after local refinement. Fallback: the route-graph
traversability check for a nominal footprint, labeled heuristic, with the feasibility tag reduced to `unresolved`
wherever the heuristic and a fine brute-force grid disagree.

### 19.12 The retrofit catalog [F]

**The idea.** Every repair a design loop normally searches lives in the robot. Some failures are caused by one feature
of the building that costs little to change: a threshold, a door closer, an exposed cord, a glass door with nothing on
it. The retrofit catalog makes the building a design variable with prices, so that "add a ramp" and "fit larger
wheels" are compared in one budget.

**The catalog.** `registries/retrofits.toml` lists typed building amendments, about forty at first (TARGET). Each
entry is a typed scene edit with its parameters, the hazards it applies to, a price with provenance, an install class
(`renter-safe`, `landlord`, `contractor`), constraints on its parameters, and a declaration of which aspects of the
scene it changes (geometry, material, optics, clocks, actors).

```toml
[[retrofit]]
id          = "threshold-ramp"
edit        = { kind = "add-overlay", shape = "wedge", params = ["rise_m", "run_m", "width_m"] }
applies_to  = ["threshold", "sliding-door-track", "dock-plate-lip"]
install     = "renter-safe"
cost        = { value = 12, currency = "USD", provenance = "assumed" }
constraints = [{ expr = "run_m >= 12 * rise_m", provenance = "assumed" }]
changes     = ["geometry"]
```

| Amendment | Scene edit | Changes | Hazard it addresses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threshold ramp | Add a wedge overlay | Geometry | Threshold, door track, dock lip |
| Door-closer adjustment | Lower the closer torque parameter | Actors | Door the robot cannot push |
| Door holder | Fix a door's mode to open | Actors, geometry | Door on the route |
| Cord raceway | Replace a cord by a low bevelled cover | Geometry | Cord across a path |
| Rug gripper | Fix a rug slab; raise rug-to-floor friction | Material, actors | Rug that slides or folds |
| Fiducial marker | Add an optical marker region | Optics | Localization alias (§20.12) |
| Glass-door decal band | Change a band of a glass region to `diffuse` | Optics | Glass not seen by optical ranging |
| Motion-activated light | Raise the lighting level in a zone | Optics | Long exposure, blur |
| Floor mat on a wet zone | Change the interface card of a region | Material | Film on tile |
| Finer drain cover | Reduce the slot pitch of a grate overlay | Geometry | Castor drop |
| Anti-slip tape on a ramp | Change the interface card of a strip | Material | Ramp traction |
| Cable bridge | Add a bevelled overlay over a tray | Geometry | Cable tray |
| Dock leveller plate | Add a hinged plate over a lip | Geometry, actors | Dock lip |
| Docking guide | Add guide geometry at a dock | Geometry | Docking tolerance |
| Bump-stop absorber | Add a compliant fixture at an impact point | Geometry, material | Impact energy at a known stop |
| Aisle marker | Add distinguishing optical regions per aisle | Optics | Identical aisles |

Prices and legality constraints are `assumed` until a source is recorded (OPEN-HAB-4). A kit that violates a
constraint or uses an install class the mission does not permit is refused (`FROB-REF-313`).

**The search.** Verb: `scene.retrofit`. Input: a failing verdict with its evidence, which is one of an impossibility
cut (§19.11), a minimal counterexample world (§26) or an alias witness (§20.12). Procedure:

1. **Localize.** Only amendments whose `applies_to` matches a feature named by the evidence, and whose patch touches
   the bottleneck region, are candidates.
2. **Enumerate kits** of at most three amendments in order of price. With forty entries the unfiltered space is 40 +
   780 + 9,880 = 10,700 kits; after localization it is typically tens.
3. **Apply and re-decide.** Each kit yields a new scene variant (which shares all untouched bricks, §19.15). The same
   procedure that produced the failing verdict is rerun on it **with the robot's policy frozen**: the policy identity
   in the evidence is unchanged, and no training step is taken.
4. **Compare with a redesign.** The cheapest design edit that flips the same verdict is requested from the design
   search (§28, §25), priced from its part cards, with its controller obtained by the route the original used. A
   redesign changes the model, so its arm is not frozen-policy and its report says so.
5. **Report.** The cheapest kit, the cheapest redesign, both prices, both verdicts on the same world addresses, and
   what remains `unresolved`. The claim made is a Design comparison (§8).

Evidence: a kit verdict from a fast tier is provisional until promoted (§16). No physical installation is checked; the
Reality cell is `no-claim`.

**Closed-form known answer: a ramp against a larger wheel.** A driven wheel of radius r that carries its own load and
meets an edge of height h quasi-statically, with no help from another axle, needs a friction coefficient of at least
tan α = √(h·(2r − h)) / (r − h) at the edge. For r = 50 mm and h = 20 mm that is 4/3, which no floor card offers: the
climb is statically impossible. A wedge with a run of twelve times its rise needs 1/12. Doubling the wheel radius to
100 mm needs 0.75. The retrofit search must return the ramp, the design search must return the wheel, and the report
must show both. The static figure is a screen and not the verdict: approach speed changes the outcome (a body arriving
at √(2·g·h) = 0.63 m/s has the energy to rise 20 mm before losses), which is why step 3 reruns the full procedure.
FACT (an executed planar sill study, listed in the source register and to be re-read before a gate cites it): of 156
cases, 62 that a sampled static test rejected succeeded dynamically.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** Bounded by the share of mission failures whose bottleneck is
a building feature that some catalog entry addresses. That share is unknown.

**Paper-scale pilot and falsifier.** Before the work package is funded: for every row of the hazard registries, record
whether a catalog entry addresses it and whether a design edit also could. If fewer than a third of hazards have an
amendment, the catalog is too thin to search and is extended or dropped.

✖ **KILL-RETROFIT.** Trigger: on the home battery, kits flip fewer than 30% of the failures attributed to a building
feature, or more than 10% of kit verdicts reverse when promoted to the referee. Fallback: the catalog remains as the
edit vocabulary that Nemesis uses to mutate scenes; the search verb is withdrawn.

### 19.13 Ingesting real places [F]

**Sources.** Two pipelines exist in the owner's projects (FACT). First, a route from walkthrough video to an
agent-built Blender twin with stable feature identifiers and a GLB export, with an accepted plan for a twin package
that links visual, geometric and traversability representations (the reconstruction skills and
`franken_surveillance_system/BLENDER_TWIN_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md`). Second, `franken_drone_geometry_reconstruction` (at
`db45703afc5e`), a reference workspace for drone video whose executable chain today runs from media custody through
clocks, calibration, scale witnesses, keyframes, correspondences and relative pose to an audited relative bundle
problem; its own status file says it does not yet optimize cameras, emit metres or publish geometry. `frob scene
import` therefore consumes the twin package first, and the drone route when that project publishes geometry
(OPEN-HAB-5). Changes to either are asks upstream.

**What import adds.** The collision compile and acceptance of §19.4 and §19.5, including four-point registration;
interface cards and optical cards, marked `assumed` unless measured; fixture articulation where a hinge axis and
limits are supplied; the unknown mask; and the scale label.

**Scale witnesses.** A scene's scale label is one of four values, ordered by authority, matching the reconstruction
project's own vocabulary (FACT: `fdgr-scale` defines `relative_only`, `estimated`, `witnessed`, `surveyed`):

| Label | Meaning | What a task may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| `relative-only` | Shape without a metric scale | Viewing only; every metric task is refused (`FROB-REF-304`) |
| `estimated` | Scale inferred from priors such as typical door heights | Exploratory runs; clearance verdicts are `unresolved` within the stated scale interval |
| `witnessed` | Scale fixed by an object of known size or a measured baseline in the capture | Metric tasks, with the witness's uncertainty in u_reg |
| `surveyed` | Scale and registration from an independent survey | Metric tasks at survey accuracy |

**Unknown regions.** Occupancy is three-valued. Each consumer states its treatment:

| Consumer | Treatment of `unknown` |
|---|---|
| Spawn and goal placement | Not allowed in or adjacent to unknown space |
| Clearance complex | Blocked for witnesses, free for cuts (§19.11) |
| Contact | Solid keep-out; a contact with an unknown surface marks the world `Degraded: contact-with-unknown` and the episode is excluded from metrics with a count (OPEN-HAB-6) |
| Ray kernels | A ray that enters unknown space returns `invalid: unknown-region` |
| Tasks | May require known traversability; a required route through unknown space is `FROB-REF-305` |

An unseen underside stays unknown. A hole closed by repair is marked and its neighbourhood stays unknown. A watertight
proxy may serve a navigation experiment while gripper contact with the same object stays unresolved; the pack records
which.

**Materials are not inferred from appearance.** An unprobed surface gets an interface card of class `assumed` with a
wide friction interval, which Nemesis searches (§26). No friction, compliance or optical value is estimated from an
image.

**Requests for a measurement.** Verb: `scene.probe-request`. When a clearance verdict is `unresolved` because of
registration or scale uncertainty, when the inner and outer answers of a mission query (§25) differ because of an
unknown region, or when a decision reverses inside the interval of an `assumed` card, the studio emits a request that
names the three-dimensional box to rescan or the surface to probe, the quantity needed and the accuracy that would
settle the decision ("the clear width of door D3 to ±5 mm"). The request is the output; nothing is assumed in its
place.

Real-place data is governed by §37: the pack is private, is stored outside the repository, and everything derived from
it inherits the mark.

**Ceiling, pilot and kill.** Ingestion has no effect on decision time; it lets a candidate be evaluated in one
particular place, which is the precondition for any Reality claim about that place (§30). Paper-scale pilot and
falsifier, before the work package is funded: take one existing twin package and check by inspection that it exports
what §19.5.1 and this section need (four non-coplanar landmarks, a scale witness, coverage or confidence per region);
if it does not, the first deliverable is an upstream ask and not an importer. ✖ **KILL-INGEST.** Trigger: GATE-HAB-03
cannot be passed with a scale label of `witnessed` or better after one repair cycle. Fallback: the place is authored
in the interior grammar from a tape-measure survey, with every surveyed dimension recorded as `measured`, and the pack
says it is a proxy.

### 19.14 Train and test hygiene

A `SceneSplit` separates training scenes from held-out scenes by seed, by parameter range and by archetype variant.
Arena checks mechanically that a reported evaluation score comes from scenes whose identifiers are absent from the
policy's training manifest (`FROB-REF-502` otherwise).

Identifiers alone do not prevent leakage, so splits are made over **groups**. A group contains a base scene and
everything derived from it: mirrored layouts, clutter variants, retrofit variants, variants that share its brick pool,
and every scan of the same building. A group never straddles a split (INV-HAB-09, `FROB-REF-311`). The smallest case
that shows why: a layout and its mirror image have different `SceneId`s and the same aisles; a policy trained on one
and scored on the other has seen the test.

Three further rules. The resolved value of every randomized quantity is stored with the episode, so a split can be
audited after the fact. The count of scenes in each feasibility class is part of every split manifest (§19.5.5). Scene
families used by the blinded qualification lane of §24 are generated from sealed seeds that discovery cannot
enumerate; Habitat exposes such a family only through a handle that returns scores, never scenes.

### 19.15 Memory arithmetic, brick pools and the world bank

**Dense fields.** DESIGN arithmetic to size the design; volumes and surface areas are assumed (a 10 m × 10 m × 3 m
home with about 1,000 m² of surface; a 60 m × 40 m × 8 m warehouse with about 35,000 m²; a six-storey building of
57,600 m³). Samples = volume / spacing³; bytes = 2 × samples.

| Scene | 4 cm dense | 2 cm dense | 2 cm band bricks with labels | 1 cm dense |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home, 300 m³ | 9 MB | 75 MB | 45–84 MB | 0.6 GB |
| Warehouse, 19,200 m³ | 0.6 GB | 4.8 GB | 1.6–3.9 GB | 38 GB |
| Building, 57,600 m³ | 1.8 GB | 14.4 GB | 2.9–7.3 GB | 115 GB |

The band occupies a fraction min(1, (A/V)·t·s) of the volume for surface area A, spacing s and a band of t = 8 to 20
voxels including brick rounding. Two corrections to earlier figures follow. A 2 cm warehouse is 4.8 GB, not the 600 MB
that belongs to 4 cm. And a narrow band does not save an order of magnitude: at 2 cm it saves a factor of one to five,
at 1 cm 1.5 to 11, and a cluttered home, whose surface-to-volume ratio is highest, gains least.

**How many scenes fit.** Assuming 12–16 GB of a 24 GB card is available for scenes beside world state, rollout storage
and the policy, and about 190 GB on the M5 Ultra (a little under 85% of its measured working-set limit of about 222
GiB), dense:

| Scene and spacing | One RTX 4090 | M5 Ultra |
|---|---|---|
| Home, 2 cm | 160–210 | about 2,500 |
| Home, 1 cm | 20–27 | about 320 |
| Warehouse, 4 cm | 20–27 | about 320 |
| Warehouse, 2 cm | 2–3 | about 40 |
| Warehouse, 1 cm | none | 4 |
| Building, 2 cm | at most 1 | 13 |
| Building, 1 cm | none | 1 |

**One scene per world is impossible.** 8,192 worlds each with its own 2 cm home is 8,192 × 75 MB = 614 GB, which is 26
times a 24 GB card and more than twice the working set of the large-memory machine. Per-world state, by contrast, is
small (about 20–53 kB for a quadruped and 56–180 kB for a humanoid, §36), so memory never limits the number of worlds;
it limits the number of distinct places. Hence ADR-HAB-05.

**Brick pools shared across variants [S].** The pool is a device buffer of band bricks addressed by slot. Bricks are
content-addressed at compile time, so identical bricks are stored once. A scene variant is a directory: one 32-bit
slot and one 16-bit coarse sample per coarse cell, 6 bytes per cell, which is 0.44 MB for the home and 28 MB for the
warehouse at the default spacings. A world holds a 16-bit variant index, which may change at reset. Three kinds of
sharing follow:

- **Movable clutter costs nothing.** Movable objects are rigid bodies, not field. Variants that differ only in where
  totes, chairs and carts stand share every brick and differ only in their movable tables.
- **Repeated modules inside a scene.** A truncated field in a brick depends only on geometry within T of that brick.
  Modules placed on the coarse lattice with identical neighbourhoods therefore produce identical bricks, and an aisle
  of identical rack bays stores one bay.
- **Variants of a base scene.** A retrofit, a moved wall or a different fixture changes only the bricks within T of
  the change.

HYPOTHESIS: the unique-brick count of a warehouse or retail scene is a small fraction of its band bricks, and a layout
variant adds a few percent. Illustration under that assumption, not a result: a warehouse base of 2.5 GB whose
variants each change 5% of bricks costs 125 MB + 28 MB per variant, so about 88 variants fit beside the base in 16 GB
where three dense copies would. Test: measured brick occupancy and deduplication ratios of the compiled archetypes
(GATE-HAB-13). Scans share least. ✖ **KILL-POOL**: if the measured deduplication on the archetype battery is below
1.5, variants fall back to dense per-variant fields behind the same directory interface, and the scene counts above
apply as written.

**The world bank.** The bank is the resident set of compiled packs on a device: pools, directories, overlays, maps and
clearance complexes, with an admission ledger. Verb: `scene.bank`.

- **Where.** The M5 Ultra, with 256 GB of unified memory, holds the bank (§21): thousands of 2 cm homes, tens of 2 cm
  warehouses, and the only copies of 1 cm warehouses and whole buildings. Each RTX 4090 holds the slice its training
  job needs.
- **Admission.** By the budget estimator, under a cap that defaults to 85% (TARGET) of the device's measured
  working-set limit; a request over the cap is refused (`FROB-REF-314`). FACT (measured on this machine, to be
  re-read): the working-set limit is about 222 GiB and the largest single buffer about 167 GiB.
- **Segments.** A pool is split into segments no larger than the largest single buffer. FACT: the Metal gateway binds
  at most 31 buffers per kernel (`ft-kernel-metal`, `MAX_ARGUMENTS`). A launch therefore binds one pool segment and
  one directory, and the placement planner of §21 groups worlds by segment.
- **Eviction.** By recompute cost and recency: a pack that takes a minute to recompile is kept longer than one that
  takes a second. FACT: an admission-and-eviction policy of this family exists in the suite
  (`frankensqlite/crates/fsqlite-pager/src/s3_fifo.rs`); whether to reuse it is decided at implementation.
- **What crosses machines.** Seeds, family parameters and `SceneId`s. Each machine compiles its packs and checks the
  identity (INV-HAB-10). Voxels never cross.
- **No zero-copy claim.** Unified memory does not make a host array and a device buffer the same object. CPU analyses
  read scene data through the same fenced snapshots as any other consumer.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** By memory, the bank holds about five times what both RTX
4090 cards hold together. Whether training on more distinct places improves held-out performance is a HYPOTHESIS.
Test: train the reference quadruped task at a fixed step budget on 16, 160 and 1,600 distinct homes and score on a
held-out group. ✖ **KILL-BANK-BREADTH**: if held-out success does not rise with breadth beyond its confidence
sequence, the bank is used for evaluation, qualification, replay, clearance and retrofit search and for large scenes,
and training keeps per-card resident sets.

## 20. Sensorium: sensors, clocks, observability

Owner crate: `frob-sense` (layer 3). Ray and sensor kernels live in the kernel library (§21) and are authored here.

Labels in this chapter: a worked example is a derivation from its stated premises and is not a measurement; default
policies and capacities are DESIGN choices; every sensor and optical parameter value is `assumed` until a card records
a datasheet or a measurement; thresholds in gate tables are TARGETs; statements about sibling code or executed
experiments are marked FACT with their source.

### 20.1 What it owns

Sensorium owns what a robot is allowed to know and when it is allowed to know it. That is: the sensor models; the
observation service that turns physical history into typed, timestamped, masked packets; the clock contract under
which sensors, links, estimators, controllers and actuators run; the queues that hold packets and commands in flight;
stills and identifier buffers for agents; the snapshot feed for the viewer; and the analyses that follow from treating
sensing as part of the design: the observability Gramian, the observation-alias witness, the observation-preserving
symmetry subgroup, extrinsic calibration, the causal-policy test and noninterference.

It does not own estimators and controllers (§22), policy training (§23), mission queries (§25), calibration of the
simulator against measurement (§30), or the finite-group machinery it calls (§13).

### 20.2 Decisions

- **ADR-SENS-01: observations come only from a deterministic observation service that is part of the model identity.**
  They never come from a renderer, a viewer, presentation timestamps or level-of-detail state. Reason: a result must
  not depend on whether someone was watching.
- **ADR-SENS-02: time is exact.** Every clock has a rational period and an integer phase accumulator. The earlier rule
  that sub-schedules are integer ratios of the physics tick is withdrawn. Reason: real rates are not integer ratios of
  one another (§20.4.6), and rounding a rate changes the task.
- **ADR-SENS-03: seven instants are distinct fields.** Sample, exposure, timestamp, arrival, decision, command and
  actuation are stored separately on every packet and command, and none is inferred from another. Reason: each pair
  that is conflated hides a class of real failure (stale data, mis-stamped data, a command applied before it could
  have been computed).
- **ADR-SENS-04: one boundary ordering rule on every tier** (§20.4.3). Reason: without it two backends can disagree
  about whether a packet that arrives exactly at a decision instant is visible.
- **ADR-SENS-05: clocks, open exposures, packet queues, command queues and noise-process states are part of every
  checkpoint.** Reason: a restore that drops a packet in flight is a different world.
- **ADR-SENS-06: sensors read the collision field, the overlays and the optical map, never the visual mesh** (with
  ADR-HAB-04).
- **ADR-SENS-07: a missing measurement is a masked value with a reason, never a number.** A numeric zero is a
  measurement only where the schema says so.
- **ADR-SENS-08: a failure with a physical cause is a function of the ray-and-scene interaction and of a latent drawn
  per surface region and episode, not an independent coin per sample.** Reason: independent coins let repetition
  average away an error that in a real sensor persists (§20.6).
- **ADR-SENS-09: truth, measurements, belief and policy observations are four namespaces with declared access.** The
  actor's input schema is frozen and is run with every privileged channel absent. Reason: the commonest silent defect
  in learned control is a leak of simulator truth.
- **ADR-SENS-10: noise streams are keyed by logical identity** (world address, sensor, sample index, component), never
  by draw order. Reason: adding a sensor must not change what the others read.
- **ADR-SENS-11: a symmetry consumer receives only the observation-preserving subgroup** (§20.13).
- **ADR-SENS-12: a camera that feeds a policy has an exposure and a rolling shutter from M2.** An instantaneous
  global-shutter camera is a labeled idealization, not the default.

### 20.3 The rule

A sensor is a map from a **history** of physical states and a calibrated stochastic process to a timestamped
observation with a frame, units, a noise specification, a validity mask and a quality flag. It is a Component: it
reads ports and fields and emits a typed signal.

Observations come from the observation service, which is part of the model identity. They never come from a renderer,
a viewer, presentation timestamps or level-of-detail state (the same rule the Wright Flyer plan sets for its modeled
pilot, FACT). The viewer follows a wall-clock presentation schedule; a sensor follows its exposure and transport
schedule on simulation time. The gym cannot implement a camera by asking the viewer for its latest frame.

### 20.4 The clock contract [S]

#### 20.4.1 Exact time

A run declares its clocks: the physics step h and, for every sensor, link, estimator, controller and actuator channel,
a period and a phase. All are rational numbers of seconds. A clock may also declare a rate error as a rational (50
parts per million is 1/20,000), which gives it the period T·(1 + ε), still rational. A drifting clock is therefore
still an exact clock.

Let Q be the least common multiple of all the denominators. Simulation time is an unsigned 64-bit count of quanta of
1/Q seconds. Each clock keeps one integer, the time of its next nominal event; at a boundary at time t, every clock
whose next event is at or before t fires and advances by its period. This is a phase accumulator: integer addition and
comparison only, so the schedule is in determinism class D0 and cannot drift. Admission refuses a lattice with Q above
2⁴⁰ (TARGET; a run of 10⁶ s then stays below 2⁶⁰ quanta) with `FROB-REF-440` and the repair "round the offending rate
to a declared rational".

#### 20.4.2 Seven instants

| # | Instant | Definition | Set by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sample** | The sensor's clock fires: the nominal trigger and the start of its exposure. | The sensor's clock |
| 2 | **Exposure** | The close of the half-open window [open, close) over which the instrument integrates. Zero length for an instantaneous sensor; one window per row for a rolling shutter. | Exposure and readout fields of the sensor card |
| 3 | **Timestamp** | The time the device writes on the packet, in its own clock with its own offset and drift, at a declared point of the exposure (start, middle or end). | The device-clock model |
| 4 | **Arrival** | The packet becomes readable by its consumer, after transport latency, jitter, loss, duplication and reordering. | The link model |
| 5 | **Decision** | An estimator or controller tick reads its inputs. | The consumer's clock |
| 6 | **Command** | The tick's output is issued, after compute latency. | The controller card |
| 7 | **Actuation** | The command takes effect at the actuator, after bus and driver latency, and holds over the following open interval. | The actuator card (§15) |

Order constraints: sample ≤ exposure ≤ arrival; a packet is used by a decision at t only if its arrival is at or
before t; decision ≤ command ≤ actuation. The timestamp is deliberately unconstrained. It is what the device believes,
and an estimator that trusts timestamps over arrival order is exposed to the errors a real one has.

Every observation exposes its **age**, decision minus sample, as an integer count of quanta. Telemetry always records
it; a policy receives it if its observation schema declares it (OPEN-SENS-3). A held value is never presented as
fresh.

#### 20.4.3 The boundary ordering rule

At a boundary at time t, in this order and on every tier:

1. Commit the physical state at t.
2. Close the exposures whose window ends at t.
3. Stamp and enqueue the samples closed in step 2, computing each arrival instant from the link model.
4. Deliver every packet whose arrival is at or before t, in the total order (arrival, sensor identifier, sequence
   number).
5. Run the estimator and controller ticks whose decision instant is t. They see exactly the packets delivered so far.
6. Latch the commands whose actuation instant is t. A latched command acts over the open interval that follows.
7. Open the exposures that start at t, then advance physics.

Consequences that the tests of §20.20 pin down:

- A zero-latency loop is legal: a sample taken at t can inform a command that acts just after t.
- A packet that arrives one quantum after t cannot influence the command decided at t.
- Integrated quantities use one half-open window convention. An impact of 0.2 N·s inside one 10 ms exposure of a force
  sensor contributes 20 N to that exposure's average and nothing to the adjacent one; an impulse exactly at t belongs
  to the window that opens at t.
- At an episode reset the clocks take a fresh origin, queues are emptied, open exposures are discarded and phases are
  redrawn from the world-address stream if the task randomizes them. The final observation of the ended episode is
  kept for the semantics of §23.

#### 20.4.4 Events inside a step

A clock event that falls strictly inside a physics step is handled by a policy declared per clock and recorded in the
model identity:

| Policy | Behaviour | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `split` | The step ends at the event instant, so the event lands on a boundary. Lateness is zero. | The referee and the strict `batch-cpu` profile, whose steps are event-located (§16) |
| `interpolate` | The sample is taken at the nominal instant from the state at the previous boundary advanced along that step's own twist by the integrator's retraction, with a declared error bound. | Any tier, per sensor |
| `hold` | The event is realized at the first boundary at or after its nominal instant. Lateness, always less than h, is recorded on the packet. | Default on throughput tiers (OPEN-SENS-1) |

Interpolation never crosses a boundary at which an impact reset a velocity. A camera that straddles an impact samples
the segmented pose history; a linear blend across the reset would fabricate motion blur and an inertial acceleration
that never occurred.

#### 20.4.5 Packets and queues, inside every checkpoint

A packet carries its sensor identifier, sequence number, the first four instants, a validity mask with reasons, and a
payload reference. Packets wait in a fixed-capacity ring per world and sensor. The capacity is computed at admission
from the cards: the longest latency plus the longest exposure, divided by the period, plus the reordering window. If
the history buffers cannot cover the declared exposure window and latency bound of a task, admission refuses
(`FROB-REF-441`); a queue never overflows silently.

The link model draws latency, loss, duplication and reordering from counter-based streams keyed by world address,
sensor, sequence number and component. A late or out-of-order packet may be folded into a buffered estimator and may
change its future outputs. Decisions already taken, and the observations they were taken on, are immutable history and
are what telemetry records.

A checkpoint (§29) contains, per world: every clock's next-event time; every open exposure with its partial integral
and, for a rolling shutter, its row position; every packet and command in flight; the states of correlated noise
processes (bias random walks); and the latent values of ADR-SENS-08. Estimator and controller histories are in the
same checkpoint under their owner (§22). Three adversarial instants are tested: just after a shutter opens, just after
a packet is dropped, and just before a controller tick.

```rust

pub enum IntraStep { Split, Interpolate { bound: Bound }, Hold }
pub struct Packet {
    sensor: SensorId, seq: u32,
    t_sample: Quanta, t_exposure: Quanta, t_stamp: DeviceTime, t_arrival: Quanta,
    lateness: Quanta, mask: MaskRef, payload: BufRef,
}

```

#### 20.4.6 A worked schedule, and the shortcut it replaces

Physics at 400 Hz and a policy at 30 Hz: h = 1/400 s, T = 1/30 s, Q = 1,200, so a step is 3 quanta and a policy period
is 40. Nominal decisions fall at 0, 40, 80 and 120 quanta. Under `hold` they are realized at boundaries 0, 14, 27 and
40 (42, 81 and 120 quanta), with lateness 0, 2, 1 and 0 quanta, that is 0, 1.67, 0.83 and 0 ms. The pattern repeats
every 0.1 s with decision intervals of 14, 13 and 13 steps, and the long-run rate is exactly 30 Hz.

The shortcut "run the policy every thirteenth physics step" runs it at 400/13 = 30.77 Hz. Over a 20 s episode that is
615 decisions instead of 600, and a reward counted per decision is overpaid by 2.6%. Under the earlier integer-ratio
rule the only alternatives were that error or a change of the physics step to suit the policy.

#### 20.4.7 Schedule races [F]

Several events can share one instant: a sample closes, a packet arrives, an estimator ticks, a watchdog fires, a
command latches. The ordering rule fixes the order between stages. Within a stage it fixes a stable logical order, and
the result must not depend on that order where the events are independent. The race check enumerates one
representative of each equivalence class of orderings (two orderings are equivalent when they differ only by swapping
independent events) and compares digests. Hand-checkable size: six events with three independent conflicting pairs
have 720 orderings in 2³ = 8 classes. FACT: `asupersync` has a deterministic laboratory runtime
(`asupersync/src/lab/runtime.rs`) in which such schedules can be driven. Owner: `frob-conformance`. Pilot and
falsifier: plant one stale read and one watchdog-against-latch race in a fixture; the check is funded beyond the
fixture only if it finds both. Ceiling: a correctness guard with no speed benefit. Kill: one planted race missed;
fallback, the fixed total order of §20.4.3 with no enumeration.

### 20.5 Proprioception [S]

- **Joint encoders** with the card's resolution and noise, quantization, index offset and wraparound; backlash as seen
  through the transmission (§15); velocity by the estimator the real driver uses, with the delay its window implies.
  First check: a prescribed rotation against a known tick count.
- **Motor current and estimated torque; winding and housing temperature; battery voltage and current**, read through a
  calibrated readout with clipping and lag. First checks: the step response of a resistor-inductor circuit and a
  thermal cool-down (§15).
- **IMU**: specific force and angular rate in the sensor frame with bias random walks, noise densities, scale and
  misalignment, saturation, sample rate and latency. For a sensor rigidly mounted at offset r from a body point O, the
  point acceleration is a_P = a_O + α × r + ω × (ω × r) and the reading is f = R_WSᵀ·(a_P − g). FACT: `fs-mbd` exposes
  `prepare_point_motion`, `point_motion` and `specific_acceleration_world` for rigid and articulated bodies; Sensorium
  uses them on the referee and derives the batched kernel from the same expression. First checks: at rest the reading
  is (0, 0, +g) with zero rate; in free fall without rotation it is zero; in free fall spinning at 5 rad/s with a 0.10
  m perpendicular offset it is 2.5 m/s² toward the axis; gravity enters exactly once for prescribed and for floating
  bases.
- **Foot contact switches** with threshold and hysteresis. **Force-torque sensors** are a port reading: the continuous
  wrench is accumulated over the exposure, each impact impulse is added once, and the sum is divided by the exposure
  duration. Wrenches are moved to the sensor frame and reference point by the coadjoint action of §11 at the time of
  application. First checks: dead weight; a known offset torque; the 0.2 N·s impulse of §20.4.3.
- **Wheel odometry**: encoder displacement, which equals travel only while the wheel rolls. First checks: a circular
  arc on a dry floor; a deliberate slip on a film patch, where the odometry error must equal the slip distance in the
  contact telemetry.
- **Tactile arrays [F]**: finite taxel footprints with normal and shear saturation. First check: the resultant load
  against the sum over taxels. No task before M6 needs them.

### 20.6 Exteroception [S]

Depth cameras, spinning and solid-state lidar patterns, height scans around the base, and semantic and instance labels
are all ray queries: sphere tracing against the scene distance field, exact tests against overlays, and analytic ray
tests against the moving primitives. Noise models cover range-dependent noise, grazing-angle dropout and range limits.
Because the ray kernels are ordinary kernels (§21), sensor outputs have the same determinism class as the physics.

A lidar beam has its own pose and its own instant inside the scan; a height scan is a lidar whose pattern is a grid
under the base. What a ray reports at its first hit depends on the optical card of the region it hits (§19.3.1):

| Optical class | Ray rule |
|---|---|
| `diffuse` | A return at the hit range, with strength from albedo, incidence and range; masked `weak` below the detection threshold |
| `absorber` | No return; masked `absorbed` |
| `glass` | Reflectance by the polynomial R(θ) = r₀ + (1 − r₀)·(1 − cos θ)⁵. A direct return only within the card's acceptance angle of normal incidence; otherwise the ray continues through the pane to whatever lies behind, or follows the mirror direction, whichever return is stronger. A sensor with several echoes reports both. |
| `mirror` | The ray follows the mirror direction for one bounce. The reported point lies on the original ray at the unfolded path length, which is the mirror image of the true surface: a phantom behind the mirror. |
| `specular-floor` | As `mirror` with weight R(θ), as `diffuse` otherwise; R rises with the wetness latent. Phantoms appear below the floor at grazing angles. |
| `retroreflector` | A return with gain; saturation and bloom are [F] |
| `emitter` | No range; raises the ambient level seen by pattern-projecting depth cameras |
| Unknown region | `invalid: unknown-region` (§19.13) |

DESIGN: one bounce at most, so the launch shape is fixed and the cost of a ray on a specular class is at most doubled.
The reflectance formula uses only multiplication, so no elementary function enters the kernel.

**Optical volumes.** A ray that crosses a volume of extinction coefficient σ over a length ℓ keeps the fraction
exp(−σ·ℓ) of its power one way and exp(−2σ·ℓ) for an active sensor, computed with the deterministic exponential of the
kernel dialect (§35). Below the detection threshold the return is masked `attenuated`. Worked case: with σ = 2 m⁻¹
(assumed) and a 5% threshold, an active sensor loses every return behind more than ln(20)/4 = 0.75 m of steam.

**Pattern-projecting depth cameras.** A pixel is valid only if the projector ray and the camera ray reach the same
diffuse point with pattern contrast above ambient. It drops out on glass (the pattern passes through), on mirrors and
polished metal (it is reflected away), on absorbers, on floors lit above the pattern's irradiance, beyond range, at
grazing incidence, and in the shadow between projector and camera. Each cause has its own mask reason.

**Why causes are not coins.** Two payload classes need different grips, and a sensor reads the class correctly three
times in four. With independent errors, a majority of three reads is right 27/32 of the time, of five 459/512, of
seven 3,807/4,096 (93%). If the error is common to all reads, as it is when the same pane of glass or the same glare
is in every frame, the answer stays right three times in four however many reads are taken. A model that draws dropout
independently per sample overstates what repetition buys. Hence ADR-SENS-08: the dropout of a ray through a given
region is determined by geometry, the optical card and one latent per region and episode, and repeated reads through
the same pane fail together.

### 20.7 Cameras with exposure and rolling shutter [S from M2]

A camera row r is exposed over [t_s + r·t_row, t_s + r·t_row + t_exp). Each row's rays are cast from the sensor pose
at that row's instant. The pose inside a step is the pose at the previous boundary advanced along that step's twist by
the integrator's own retraction (§13, §16), so it is exactly the trajectory the integrator implies, and it is
segmented at every boundary. The exposure duration comes from the card and, for cameras with automatic exposure, from
the lighting level of the zone, so a dim hallway lengthens exposure and increases skew and blur.

The cost is one retraction per row and no additional rays. TARGET: at most 5% added to the ray stage.

| Tier | What is modeled |
|---|---|
| [S], M2 | Per-row sample instants (rolling shutter); exposure represented by its midpoint; per-beam instants for lidar |
| [F] | Exposure integration with two or four sub-instants per row, with a pixel masked `mixed` when its sub-samples disagree by more than a threshold (flying pixels at depth edges) |
| [F] | Radiometric RGB: shot and read noise, glare, lens distortion; rasterized textured meshes for policies. Not needed by any task before M5 |
| [F] | Event cameras: asynchronous streams of log-brightness changes |

Hand-checkable known answers: a camera with a focal length of 600 pixels and a 16 ms readout that yaws at 2 rad/s
images a vertical edge near the optical axis with a skew of 600 × 2 × 0.016 = 19.2 pixels from the first row to the
last. A lidar spinning at 10 Hz on a robot yawing at 1 rad/s turns 0.1 rad during a scan, so a wall 5 m away is
displaced by 0.5 m across the scan seam.

For the [F] rows: benefit ceiling, none on speed; they change what a perception policy must tolerate. Pilot and
falsifier: on the analytic moving-bar fixture, compare midpoint sampling with four-sample integration; if depth error
and mask statistics differ by less than the sensor's noise over the hazard list's speed range, integration is not
built. Kill (KILL-EXPOSURE): more than 25% added ray cost; fallback, midpoint sampling.

The other Frontier sensor models named in this chapter (tactile arrays, event cameras, radiometric RGB, retroreflector
bloom, backscatter from optical volumes) are on no task's path before M5. Each is funded only when a registered task
needs it, names one analytic fixture as its pilot when it is, has no speed benefit to claim, and falls back to the
mask-only model under the same cost rule.

### 20.11 The observability Gramian [S]

**What it is.** An analysis that says which directions of state error a sensor suite cannot see over a window of
motion. For an estimator error state δx (in the invariant error coordinates of §22), a window of N steps,
state-transition matrices Φ_k from the window start, measurement Jacobians H_k and noise covariances R_k,

  W = Σ_k Φ_kᵀ·H_kᵀ·R_k⁻¹·H_k·Φ_k,  summed over k = 0, …, N − 1.

Its eigenvalues are information per unit error along its eigenvectors. Two forms are provided. The **linearized** form
uses the Jacobians of the sensor models and of the estimator's error dynamics. The **empirical** form perturbs the
initial state by ±ε along each error coordinate, replays the window as 2n worlds of one batch, and forms W from
whitened output differences; it needs no Jacobian and works on any tier. Owner: `frob-sense` for H, R and the
empirical driver; §22 supplies Φ. Verb: `sense.observability`. Atelier lists it among its analyses beside sensor
coverage (§28).

**What it reports.** The dimension of the null space against the declared gauge; the smallest eigenvalue outside the
gauge, after a declared scaling of the error coordinates; and the corresponding eigenvector in named coordinates
("translation along the corridor axis"). It is computed along a route, so the result is a profile: where on the route
the estimate degrades, and in which direction.

**Gauge.** With an IMU, encoders, contact switches and gravity, global position and yaw cannot be observed. The null
space has dimension four, and the analysis must find exactly that, analytically and by finite perturbation
(PO-SENS-09). A larger null space is a finding; a smaller one is a defect in a sensor model.

**Hand-checkable case.** A robot at (x, y) in a corridor whose walls are the lines y = ±a measures its distance a − y
to one wall with variance s². Then H = (0, −1) and, standing still for N samples, W = (N/s²)·diag(0, 1). The null
vector is (1, 0): position along the corridor is unobservable. A second beam at 45° reads (a − y)·√2 and adds nothing
along x. A beam that reaches an end wall at x = b reads b − x and makes W full rank. In an infinite corridor no beam
direction observes x, and one end wall, door frame or fiducial within range restores it.

**Its limit, which is the reason for §20.12.** The Gramian is local. Two aisles that are exact copies of each other
give a full-rank Gramian in each, yet nothing distinguishes one aisle from the other. Infinitesimal ambiguity is the
Gramian's subject; finite ambiguity is the alias witness's.

**Uses.** A warning in the design check when a route has a stretch whose smallest eigenvalue falls below a threshold;
an input to the assumption monitors of runtime shields (§22); and a score for sensor placement [F]. For the placement
use: benefit ceiling, the share of estimator failures that come from sensing geometry and not from calibration or
filter dynamics, which is unknown. Pilot and falsifier: on the M3 battery, classify estimator divergences by cause; if
under a fifth are geometric, placement by Gramian is not built. Kill: no measured drop in drift after a recommended
move; fallback, the Gramian remains a report.

### 20.12 The observation-alias impossibility witness

**The question.** Can more training on the present sensors ever satisfy this mission? Sometimes the answer is no for a
reason that has nothing to do with the body, the controller or the amount of compute: two situations that the robot
cannot tell apart require different actions.

**The object.** For a design with observation interface Y (sensors, cards, clocks, links) and a mission whose worlds
form a declared set, an alias witness is:

- two admitted worlds w₁ and w₂;
- a commitment time t* and a set of permitted probe behaviours before t*;
- evidence that the delivered observation transcripts up to t* are identical in the two worlds under every permitted
  behaviour, or have total-variation distance δ under a fixed behaviour;
- evidence that the sets of action continuations at t* that can still satisfy the mission, S₁ and S₂, are disjoint.

**The statement (PO-SENS-07).** A causal policy maps delivered observations, its past actions and its own random draws
to an action. If the transcripts are identical, it takes the same action in both worlds at t*, and that action lies
outside S₁ or outside S₂. No causal policy satisfies the mission in both worlds, and private randomization does not
help. In the stochastic case with equal prior weight on the two worlds and a fixed experiment with observation laws P
and Q, the probability of choosing correctly is at most (1 + TV(P, Q))/2, which bounds mission success.

**Hand-checkable example: a mission that is unsolvable for want of a sensor.** A robot stands at a T-junction. A
charging plate lies at the end of the left branch in world L and at the end of the right branch in world R; each
branch is 10 m long. The two branches are mirror images. The robot's only exteroceptive sensor is a planar lidar; the
plate lies below the scan plane and is detected only by contact or within 0.5 m. The battery allows 12 m of travel.

- Each world alone is solvable: turn the correct way and drive 10 m.
- In both worlds the lidar returns the same scan at the junction and along either branch until 0.5 m from its end,
  whatever the robot does. The transcripts are identical up to the turn.
- S_L is "turn left" and S_R is "turn right". A wrong turn is discovered after 9.5 m and costs 9.5 + 9.5 + 10 = 29 m
  in all, against a budget of 12 m.

No policy succeeds in both worlds, and with equal priors none succeeds more than half the time. Training cannot change
that. Four changes can, and they are of four different kinds: a camera that sees a marker on the plate from the
junction (a sensor); a fiducial at the junction (a retrofit, §19.12); a battery good for 29 m (a body); or a label
given to the robot before it starts (information by commitment). The studio's answer is the witness and these
alternatives with their prices.

**The same structure with noisy sensing.** Two payload classes need disjoint final grip forces. A probe reads the
class correctly with probability 3/4. By exhaustive enumeration: with no read, success is 1/2; with three independent
reads 27/32; with five 459/512; with seven 3,807/4,096, which first exceeds 90%. If a common fault affects every read,
success stays at 3/4 after any number. And a deadline changes the answer: a rule that stops when one class leads by
two reads is right 90% of the time with 3.2 reads on average, but it is still undecided after six reads with
probability 27/512, and forcing a guess then yields 225/256, below 90%. Every sensing policy is therefore compared
under the mission's deadline, with delay priced.

**Where witnesses come from.**

| Source | Method | Tier | Class of the result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structural | A hidden parameter has no information path to any delivered packet before t*: no ray can intersect geometry that depends on it, and no contact with it can occur before a computed earliest time. Uses the dependency analysis behind noninterference (§20.17). | [S] | `Certified` relative to the model, for every policy |
| Symmetry and repetition | Candidate world pairs from the scene's own symmetries and repeated modules (a mirrored layout, a translated aisle) under which the sensor suite is invariant and the goal is not. | [S] to propose; checked as below | As checked |
| Enumerated | On a finite abstraction (a lattice of actions and readings in exact arithmetic), exhaust every causal policy tree. | [S] for small models | `Certified` for the abstraction |
| Searched | Nemesis (§26) searches world pairs for minimal transcript distance subject to disjoint success sets. | [F] | `Tested`, and labeled experiment-specific unless one of the rows above also applies |

**Checking.** A claimed witness is checked independently of how it was found: both worlds are replayed on the referee
under each permitted behaviour, the bytes of every delivered packet up to t* are compared, and the disjointness of the
success sets is established by the method the witness names. A witness that covers only a declared set of probe
behaviours says so; it is not presented as a bound over all policies. Owner: `frob-sense`. Verb: `sense.alias`, which
returns a witness or "none found", never "none exists" unless a structural or enumerated argument supports it.

**Three engineering answers.** A perceptual ambiguity can be resolved by extracting the information (a better sensor,
a faster or earlier packet), by moving to acquire it (a permitted probe, a different mount), or by changing the
mechanics so that the information is unnecessary (a passive compliance whose response suits both cases). Mission
queries (§25) price all three, together with retrofits, against one another. The timing variant, in which the right
sensor exists but its packet arrives too late, is the docking fixture of §20.16.

**Effect on other verbs.** A training or optimization run requested on a mission with a standing witness for its
interface is refused (`FROB-REF-445`) with the witness attached, unless the caller overrides for diagnosis.

**Ceiling of the benefit, stated before any speed test.** The witness saves the training compute that would have been
spent on missions that are unsolvable under their interface. The share of such missions is unknown; it is measured on
the M3 mission corpus before the search lane is funded. Paper-scale pilot and falsifier: a suite of 64 layouts in
which the aliased ones are planted by construction; the lane must find every planted alias and report none on the
rest.

✖ **KILL-ALIAS.** Trigger: one false witness, that is, a policy exhibited that satisfies the mission in both worlds of
an accepted witness. Fallback: the searched lane is withdrawn; structural and enumerated witnesses, and pairs declared
by a mission author, remain.

### 20.13 The observation-preserving symmetry subgroup [S]

The symmetry group 𝔾 of a design (C-09) is computed from masses, inertias, joints and actuator cards. It ignores what
the robot sees and what it is asked to do. A quadruped built symmetric fore-aft and left-right has 𝔾 = C₂ × C₂, of
order four. Mount a depth camera facing forward on its sagittal plane and the fore-aft reflection is no longer a
symmetry of the closed loop: an obstacle ahead is seen, an obstacle behind is not. A policy constrained to be
equivariant under all of 𝔾 would be required to respond to the unseen obstacle as the mirror image of its response to
the seen one.

**Definition.** 𝔾_obs is the set of g in 𝔾 that also preserve:

- every sensor: g maps each mount frame to the mount frame of a sensor with an identical card, and maps its pattern
  (beam set, pixel grid) to itself **including its timing**;
- every clock, link and latency of the interface;
- the payload's inertia and any declared asymmetry of the load;
- the task: its goal set, requirement predicates and reward terms (declared by Arena, §24);
- the law of the scenes: the scene family is invariant under the corresponding reflection or rotation of the world.

The elements that preserve a structure form a subgroup (PO-SENS-05), so 𝔾_obs ≤ 𝔾. It is stored with the tuple
(design, sensor profile, task, scene family). Owner: `frob-sense` for the predicates, `frob-algebra` for the group
operations. Verb: `sense.symmetry`.

**Two cases worth checking by hand.**

- The quadruped above: the left-right reflection maps the camera to itself and each image row to itself, so it
  survives if rows are read out top to bottom. The fore-aft reflection and the half-turn do not. 𝔾_obs has order two,
  and data augmentation doubles the data; it does not quadruple it.
- A spinning lidar on the same plane of symmetry: a reflection reverses the order in which beams fire. With per-beam
  instants (§20.6) the mirrored robot would need a lidar that spins the other way, so the reflection is broken at
  first order in the product of body rate and scan time. It is restored only if the scan is treated as instantaneous,
  and the report says which was assumed.

An IMU mounted off the plane of symmetry breaks the reflection of raw observations too. The symmetry analysis (§28)
reports it as a near-symmetry with its repair: move the IMU by the stated offset, or declare the lever-arm correction
part of the observation map.

**Rule (INV-SENS-08).** Equivariant weight sharing, augmentation, deduplication of closed-loop results and quotients
of gait orbits use 𝔾_obs. A request that names an element outside it is refused (`FROB-REF-443`). Open-loop mechanics
(mass-matrix structure, static analyses) may still use all of 𝔾.

**Test.** For each generator of 𝔾_obs: run a world and its image under g, with noise stream identifiers permuted by g,
and require the observation streams to correspond exactly up to the signed permutation. For an element of 𝔾 outside
𝔾_obs the same test must fail; that is the negative control. Equivariant policies built on 𝔾_obs are Frontier and
belong to §23.

### 20.14 Calibration as a least-squares pose graph [S]

**What it is.** Extrinsic calibration of a sensor rig is weighted least squares on a graph, and is named as such.
Nodes are sensor frames and the base. The unknowns are corrections δ_v in 𝔰𝔢(3) to each sensor's mount transform, with
the base anchored at zero to remove the six-dimensional gauge. Edges are pairwise relative-pose measurements (camera
to IMU, lidar to odometry, hand to eye), each with a residual r_e in 𝔰𝔢(3) and a 6 × 6 weight W_e, the inverse of its
covariance. Linearized, the stacked residual is r = B·δ, where B is the block incidence matrix of the graph with
adjoint blocks on the tail side. The Gauss–Newton step solves the normal equations

  (Bᵀ·W·B)·δ = Bᵀ·W·r,

and the update is applied on the group by the retraction of §13. Owner: `frob-sense`. Verb: `sense.calibrate`.
Consumers: the simulated robot's own self-check after an impact; fault injection ("extrinsic bump"); and Ground (§30),
which runs the same solver on logs from a real rig.

**The consistency residual.** The part of r that no assignment of extrinsics can explain is r_⊥ = (I −
B·(Bᵀ·W·B)⁻¹·Bᵀ·W)·r. The projector is orthogonal in the metric W, not in the Euclidean metric; rotations in radians
and translations in metres have different scales, and the Euclidean projector gives a different and wrong
decomposition whenever W is not a multiple of the identity (the negative control). Two different faults look
different:

- A **bumped sensor** is a change of extrinsics. The solve absorbs it: δ̂ at that node moves by the bump and r_⊥ stays
  at noise level. It is detected as a change of δ̂ beyond its covariance.
- A **faulty edge** (one bad pairwise estimate, a time offset on one pair) is inconsistent round every cycle through
  it: r_⊥ is not small.

**What the graph must be for this to work.** On a tree, B is square after anchoring and r_⊥ is identically zero: there
is no redundancy, a bump and a bad edge are indistinguishable, and a request for fault localization is refused
(`FROB-REF-446`). Detecting a bad edge needs that edge to lie on a cycle. Identifying which edge is bad needs more,
and the smallest cases show it. Scalar offsets, unit weights, one edge biased by β:

- **Three sensors, three pairwise edges (one cycle).** The least-squares residual is β/3 on every edge. The loop is
  known to be inconsistent; the bad edge is not identified.
- **Four sensors, six pairwise edges.** The residual is β/2 on the bad edge, β/4 on the four edges that share a node
  with it and zero on the opposite edge. The bad edge has the unique largest residual, by a factor of two.

Known answer for the bump: on a four-sensor rig with all six pairwise measurements, a planted lidar bump of 2° and 15
mm is recovered within 0.1° and 1 mm (TARGET), attributed to the lidar node, with r_⊥ at noise level.

### 20.15 The causal-policy test [S]

A policy is causal if its command at a decision instant depends only on the observations delivered to it by then, its
past actions and its own random draws. The simulator can violate this on the policy's behalf in many small ways, and
the test catches them without reading the policy's code.

**Procedure.** Run a world and a twin that differs only in something the policy cannot yet know: terrain beyond every
sensor's reach or after the decision time; the friction of a surface not yet touched; a measurement whose packet is
delayed past the decision; a randomization parameter of a later phase. Record, at every decision instant, a digest of
the bytes delivered so far and a digest of the command. Wherever the delivered-observation digests of the two runs
agree, the command digests must agree. Verb: `sense.causal-test`. Owner: `frob-sense`, with fixtures in
`frob-conformance`. It runs on every policy before its first evaluation is recorded, and on the baseline controllers
of §22.

**Planted leaks that must each fail** (the negative controls): a privileged state channel wired into the actor's
input; a reset mask that lets history cross an episode boundary; a sensor read at its current value instead of from
its delivered packet; a curriculum parameter passed as an observation; a noise stream keyed so that a hidden parameter
shifts the draws of a visible sensor.

An asymmetric critic may read privileged state during training under a schema marked training-only. The actor's
inference schema is frozen, and the test runs with the privileged channels physically absent, not zeroed.

### 20.16 The docking fixture: a known answer with a closed-form boundary

One small model exercises the clock contract, strict comparison, the alias witness and exact arithmetic together.
Position is in metres and time in seconds.

**Model.** Three ticks of one second. x₀ = v₀ = 0. During tick k the acceleration is g·u_k, with a hidden gain g ∈ {1,
2} fixed for the episode and a command |u_k| ≤ U, for U in [½, 1]. The mission is (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0) exactly. The only
informative packet is a reading of v₁ with additive error in [−ε, +ε].

**If the packet arrives before the second command.** Write p = u₀. The terminal conditions force the second command to
be u₁ = 1/g − 2p and the third to be u₂ = p − 1/g (from v₃ = g·(p + u₁ + u₂) and x₃ = g·(2p + u₁)). The command limits
in both worlds leave 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + ½)/2. The reading lies in [p − ε, p + ε] if g = 1 and in [2p − ε, 2p + ε] if g =
2; the two required values of u₁ differ by ½, so the intervals must be disjoint, which is ε < p/2. Choosing the
largest admissible p gives the exact boundary

  **ε < (U + ½)/4**, equality excluded.

With U = ¾ and p = ¼ the commands are (¼, ½, −¾) for g = 1 and (¼, 0, −¼) for g = 2, both reaching (1, 0), and the
tolerable error is below 1/8. With p = 5/8 it is below 5/16: the first command is a probe, and a bolder probe buys a
sensor two and a half times worse. At ε = 5/16 exactly, both worlds can report 15/16 while requiring u₁ = −¼ and u₁ =
−¾: the strict predicate fails at equality.

**If the packet arrives after the third command is committed.** A causal controller issues one command vector in both
worlds. The final position of a common vector is g·(2p + u₁), which cannot equal 1 for both gains. Every case is
excluded. This is an alias witness (§20.12) whose cause is timing, not the absence of a sensor.

**Grid known answer.** On U = ½ + i/32 for i = 0…16 and ε = j/64 for j = 0…32, the boundary admits a cell exactly when
2j < 32 + i: 344 of 561 cells are admitted, 217 excluded, and nine lie exactly on the boundary (the even i). All 561
cells are excluded when the packet is late.

**A hypothesis set widened by a hair.** Admit a third gain, 33/32. At U = ¾ and ε = 1/64 the reading (65/64)·p is
possible for g = 1 and for g = 33/32 under every first command, so both worlds receive the same remaining commands;
their final positions are c and 33c/32, and the smallest achievable worst error is 1/65 m, at c = 64/65. Every cell
admitted under the two-gain mission is wrong for the three-gain mission. The rule this fixture enforces everywhere: a
verdict is bound to the declared support of the hidden parameters, the delivery deadline, the error units and the
target tolerance, and a change to any of them invalidates it.

**Arithmetic.** The fixture is evaluated in exact integer arithmetic (class D0) and, as a control, in single
precision. FACT (an executed boundary-screen experiment on this machine's Metal device and on both RTX 4090 cards,
listed in the source register and to be re-read before a gate cites it): on a dense grid of 65,536 cases, unguarded
single precision gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions; a guard band with exact fallback gave no final
errors; an exact integer kernel gave none and produced identical bytes on every device. The fixture is therefore also
the first test of the exact-integer kernel class and of guarded single precision (§35).

Owner: `frob-sense` with `frob-conformance`. Nothing about a real docking device follows from it.

### 20.17 Noninterference [S]

Five properties, each tested by changing one thing and comparing digests.

| Property | Change | Must be unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation | Turn the viewer, stills or the telemetry level on or off | Every physics digest and every delivered observation |
| Truth | Change simulator truth behind a sensor that is disconnected, masked or not yet delivered | The actor's inputs and commands (§20.15) |
| Sensor set | Add, remove or reorder sensors that no controller consumes | The outputs of every other sensor, and physics |
| Batch | Run the same world address alone, in a batch of 8,192, and beside worlds with different sensor suites | That world's delivered observations (with the batch-independence test of §35) |
| Optics against contact | Change an optical card, or an interface card | Physics digests, or ray outputs, respectively (§19.5.4) |

The sensor-set property is what ADR-SENS-10 buys. A generator that hands out draws in call order fails it the first
time a sensor is added.
