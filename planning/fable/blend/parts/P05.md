## 21. Spark: GPU backends and the kernel toolchain

### 21.1 What Spark owns

Spark owns everything between a derived kernel and a device result: the kernel library and its manifests, the
kernel intermediate representation and its emitters, the two device backends, the device arena and its leases, the
placement planner, the binary kernel cache, the hardware fingerprint, the CPU-versus-device check with its
divergence ledger, and the device side of the division of labour between the two machines. It does not own the
physics of a stage (§16, §18), the meaning of a family of bodies (§17), the determinism classes (§35) or the
performance targets (§36). It implements them and reports against them.

| Item | Where |
|---|---|
| Crates | `frob-kir` (layer 1), `frob-kernels` (layer 3), annexes `derive/frob-derive`, `spark/frob-spark-cuda`, `spark/frob-spark-metal` |
| Verbs | `spark.devices`, `spark.fingerprint`, `spark.probe`, `spark.check`, `spark.precompile`, `spark.place`, `spark.cache`; `fleet.status`, `fleet.plan` |
| Records | Kernel manifest `frob.kernel/1`, kernel IR `frob.kir/1`, step plan `frob.stepplan/1`, fingerprint `frob.fingerprint/1`, placement receipt `frob.placement/1` |
| Admission | INT-CUDA-01 (`cudarc`) and INT-FT-01 (`ft-kernel-metal`), both at M0 (§33) |

### 21.2 Position relative to suite doctrine

FACT: FrankenSim's accelerator doctrine states "FrankenSim does not ship an accelerator backend", requires a
"Permanent CPU reference", and defines twelve evidence fields (AE-01 to AE-12) for any device run: device
identity, driver and runtime, compiler and backend, kernel source hash, kernel and workload identity, CPU axes,
reduction and determinism policy, CPU-versus-device comparison, the retained CPU reference, cancellation outcome,
phase attribution, and a go or no-go decision (`frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`). In-process foreign
kernels are forbidden in FrankenSim's production paths. The suite's working GPU code lives outside FrankenSim's
workspace in what its manifests call the engine-kit pattern, and in FrankenTorch's `ft-kernel-metal`, which
FrankenTorch calls its sanctioned unsafe boundary (FACT: `frankentorch/crates/ft-kernel-metal/src/lib.rs`).

FACT: the same doctrine names a falsifier. A device pilot is refused when the top three kernels jointly account
for less than 50% of end-to-end wall time, or when no candidate suitable after transfer and synchronization costs
holds at least 15% of it. Spark applies both thresholds to its own optimization funding (§36.8).

**ADR-SPARK-01.** FrankenRobotics has an explicit owner mandate for CUDA and low-level Metal. It meets the
mandate without weakening the core:

- The core workspace is `unsafe_code = "deny"` with Franken-only runtime dependencies and never depends on a GPU
  crate.
- GPU backends live in **annex workspaces** under `spark/`, each with its own lockfile and a registry of unsafe
  capsules with a `SAFETY.md` each.
- Binaries that use a GPU are built from an `apps/` workspace that links the core and an annex.
- Every device run emits a receipt with FrankenSim's twelve evidence fields, unchanged. FrankenRobotics adds
  fields beside them (placement per stage, fingerprint digest, determinism class, capacities); it never renames
  or drops one of the twelve.
- The CPU reference is permanent (§16).

Owner ratification is OPEN-SPARK-1; the fail-closed default is that GPU results are labeled exploratory until
ratified.

**What is claimed.** Compiling one kernel source for several backends is prior art (Warp for CPU and CUDA;
Genesis's compiler for CUDA, Metal and others; FACT, Appendix H) and is not claimed as new. The claim Spark may
make, once GATE-SPARK-02 has passed, is narrower: kernel arithmetic is derived once and emitted for a CPU
reference, CUDA and Metal, and the measured cross-backend disagreement is stored per kernel.

### 21.5 The kernel library

A kernel is a manifest, its arithmetic, and its sources. The manifest holds: `KernelId` (content hash), its
**class** (§21.8), inputs and outputs with shapes and physical dimensions, the determinism class it claims, the
identities it declares (the certificates of §12 attach here), whether a derivative program exists (D-8), and the
symbol of its CPU reference. It also holds a **stage signature** (DESIGN), because a device kernel is only as safe
as what the host believes about its buffers:

- the read footprint and the write footprint, per buffer, as index ranges in terms of capacities;
- aliasing rules (which arguments may be the same slab; none by default);
- layout: element type, extents, stride unit (bytes or elements) and stride signedness, logical origin and
  allocation extent;
- the ownership epoch the kernel reads and the epoch it writes (§21.10);
- resource bounds taken from the IR: live values, stack bytes, loop bounds, buffer argument count;
- the numerical contract: precision profile, error bound if one is certified, neutral elements used for padding.

FACT to recheck at INT-FNP-01 and INT-FT-01: the pinned FrankenNumPy uses signed byte strides and the pinned
FrankenTorch unsigned element strides. The smallest case that breaks a naive hand-off: a 2 × 2 contiguous `f32`
view has strides (8, 4) under the first convention and (2, 1) under the second. Copying (8, 4) verbatim as
element strides onto a 16-element buffer reads element 8 + 4 = 12 at index [1, 1], where element 3 belongs, and
both reads are in bounds, so nothing faults. A view crossing that boundary therefore carries its stride unit and
signedness; a view that the receiver cannot represent is materialized and the copy charged to the receipt, or
refused under a zero-copy contract (`FROB-REF-617`). This is an admission test and an upstream ask (Appendix F).

Families at M2: kinematics (`K-fk-*`, `K-twist-*`), dynamics (`K-crba-*`, `K-rnea-*`), actuators (`K-act-*`,
including the within-world reduction for a shared bus current limit, `K-act-bus`), collision (`K-geom-*`,
`K-bp-*`, `K-np-*`, `K-compact`), constraints and solve (`K-jac-*`, `K-cone-*`, `K-hess`, `K-scale`, `K-chol`,
`K-linesearch`, `K-refine`), integration (`K-cay-step`, `K-normalize`, `K-anchor`), sensing (`K-ray-*`,
`K-sense-*`, with exposure-time and rolling-shutter bins), learning (`K-gemm`, `K-act-fn`, `K-adam`), utility
(`K-scan`, `K-sort`, `K-seg-reduce`, `K-reduce`, `K-variant-gather`, `K-commit`, `K-snapshot`), and the exact
family (`K-x-pred`, `K-x-digest`, `K-x-hist`, `K-x-philox`, `K-g-pred`, `K-canary`). One stable integer-key
radix sort (`K-sort`) and one count, exclusive-scan, write compaction (`K-compact`) serve every stage; no stage
carries its own sort.

### 21.6 Two layers of source

**Skeletons are written by hand, twice.** The control structure of a kernel (loops, gathers, scans, sorts,
barriers inside a thread group) is written once in Rust for `batch-cpu` and once in the **shared dialect**: the
common subset of CUDA C++ and Metal Shading Language, made portable by a per-backend prelude of macros for
address-space qualifiers, kernel signatures and thread-group barriers. This is the pattern already working in
`frankensim/apps/instrument-lab/render-gpu-piano` (one `kernel.h`, a prelude in `cuda.rs`, a prelude in
`metal.rs`, and a CPU emulation that is the oracle; FACT). Dialect rules:

1. No backend intrinsic outside the prelude.
2. No floating-point atomics. Integer atomics are allowed only for operations whose result does not depend on
   arrival order: maximum, minimum, bitwise or, and wrapping addition.
3. No built-in transcendental functions and no reciprocal-square-root built-in. Elementary functions are `det_*`
   functions written in the dialect itself, ported from `fs-math`'s deterministic set.
4. Floating-point contraction off; no fast-math relaxations; no explicit fused multiply-add.
5. Explicit loop bounds that are literals or capacity constants of the shape class, unsigned 32-bit indices, no
   recursion, no pointer arithmetic beyond buffer plus index.
6. No grid-wide barrier. A stage that needs every world to finish before the next begins is two launches.
7. Integer arithmetic in exact kernels is unsigned and 32 bits wide; shift counts are masked. Signed overflow
   is undefined in both source languages and is therefore unreachable by construction. Sixty-four-bit integers
   and atomics are used only where the fingerprint records them for every device family in use. A count that can
   pass 2³² follows the two-limb rule of §21.26.
8. A mask is a select, never an arithmetic blend: m·a + (1 − m)·b turns an infinity in the unselected arm
   into a NaN.
9. Padding trails the active lanes and holds the neutral element of the operation that will consume it (§35.5).

FACT: two habits of the suite's piano renderer are deliberately not inherited. It compiles its CUDA source with
multiply-add fusion on and builds its double-float product on `fmaf` and `fma` (`cuda.rs`, `metal.rs`), and it
steps a whole stretch in one persistent launch whose thread groups meet at an atomic grid barrier, which on Metal
"very rarely" leaves one group unscheduled, "about once per few hundred launches", so the host reruns the stretch
(`metal.rs`). Rules 4 and 6 remove both.

**Arithmetic is derived, once.** The mathematical body of a kernel (a Cayley step, an adjoint transport, a
cone projection, a Jacobian row) is produced at build time by `derive/frob-derive` using FrankenSymPy, lowered to
a program in `frob-kir`, and emitted as Rust and as dialect text, together with its derivative programs and its
certificates (C-14). Generated source is checked in with its digest; CI regenerates and compares; generated code
is never edited by hand. Derivation is per kernel body, never per whole robot, which keeps expressions small: a
published attempt to expand a humanoid mass matrix symbolically ran for over an hour and crashed (FACT, Appendix
H). If FrankenSymPy hangs on a derivation it runs in an isolated worker with a time and memory cap.

|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-KIR | Trigger: at M2 the derivation toolchain cannot express or emit more than 20% of the kernel arithmetic, or emitted kernels miss BET-01's speed bound after one optimization pass. Fallback: arithmetic is hand-written in Rust and the dialect and bound by differential tests; certificates fall back per KILL-LEMMA. The skeleton pattern is unaffected. |

### 21.7 The kernel IR

`frob-kir` is typed static single assignment with bounded loops. It merges two requirements: every value carries
a physical dimension, and every program carries the resource facts a backend needs before it compiles anything.

```text
DESIGN: frob.kir/1, intent not syntax.

types      f32 | f64 (CPU targets only) | f32dd (double-float pair) | u32 | bool
           vec3<T> | mat3<T> | se3<T> | sym6<T> | array<T, N>          N is a literal
           q16_16 | q32_32                                              reserved: parsed, never lowered to a GPU
dimension  every floating value has an exponent vector over the fs-qty base dimensions; sums must be homogeneous
statements single assignment
           for i in 0..N { ... }                                       N a literal or a shape-class capacity
           select(pred, a, b)                                          branchless
operators  add sub mul div sqrt neg abs min max clip compare           min and max are defined on NaN and on ±0
           and or xor shl shr addw mulw mulhi                           u32, wrapping, shift counts masked
           load(slot, lane, offset) store(slot, lane, offset, v)       offset a literal
excluded   sin cos exp log pow rsqrt fma, any approximate mode
metadata   content hash (BLAKE3); math_mode in {Ieee754Safe, ExactInteger, FixedPoint (reserved)};
           max_regs; max_stack_bytes; operation counts by type; loop bounds; buffer argument count;
           class claimed; CPU reference symbol; derivative program; proof_digest (optional)
```

- **Dimension typing** is T-09: a program type-checks only if every sum is homogeneous, decided exactly.
- **Bounded loops** replace strict straight-line form where a body repeats over links at one depth, rows or
  lanes. A loop bound is a literal or a capacity, so every program still has a finite, known operation count and
  a derivative program by the same transformation.
- **Elementary functions** are `det_*` subprograms written in the IR from +, −, ×, ÷, √ and selects, so they
  inline as ordinary arithmetic and inherit the class of those operators. The design needs few of them: a
  revolute coordinate is a unit complex number, and group updates are Cayley maps (§11, §13).
- **Rewrites allowed** are those that are exact in IEEE arithmetic: bounded common-subexpression elimination,
  dead-code removal, commutation of + and ×, x·1, x + (−0), double negation, select simplification, and folding
  of exact constant operations. Reassociation and distribution are not rewrites; a Horner form is chosen at
  derivation time, before the program is fixed. Scheduling by live range to cap register pressure reorders
  independent operations only. Extraction over an e-graph, if the M2 profile ever asks for it, is restricted to
  the same exact rules under a node budget.
- **Resource metadata** is a proof obligation, not a comment (PO-SPARK-03): the emitter refuses a program whose
  bounds exceed a backend limit, for example the 31 buffer arguments of a Metal compute function (§21.9).
- **Fixed-point types** `q16_16` and `q32_32` are reserved words with defined wrapping and saturation semantics.
  No kernel in the library uses them through M5. They exist so that a later embedded target [M] (§31) lowers the
  same programs without a second IR; the exported artifact would carry the no-authorization flags of §12.

### 21.8 Kernel classes: floating, exact-integer and guarded

| Class | Arithmetic | Used for | Determinism |
|---|---|---|---|
| `float` | `f32` (and `f32dd` accumulators) under `Ieee754Safe` | The physics step, sensing, learning | D2 by design; D1 a HYPOTHESIS (§21.15) |
| `exact` | Unsigned 32-bit integers under `ExactInteger` | Strict predicates, digests, counts and scans, sort keys, histogram bins, random streams, phase accumulators | D0 on every backend |
| `guarded` | An `f32` residual with a certified error bound, plus an `exact` fallback | Strict predicates whose inputs live on the device as floats and whose cases are mostly far from the boundary | D0 in its decisions |
| `stat` | Any of the above, across worlds | Statistics rings and histograms | Never feeds state (INV-SPARK-04) |

**Why a class and not a habit.** The smallest case that breaks a bare comparison: is 16,777,216 less than
16,777,217? In integers, yes. In `f32` the second number is not representable and rounds to the first, so the
strict comparison is false. A requirement verdict, a termination test or a region boundary that is decided this
way is wrong exactly where it matters.

FACT (a recorded pilot, Appendix K; reproduced at GATE-SPARK-08 before any gate cites it): a one-inequality
boundary classifier with inputs on a grid of 2⁻²⁸ ran as a real Metal command buffer on this M5 Ultra and as a
CUDA kernel on each RTX 4090 of `trj`, with readback and independent integer checking. On a 65,536-row grid dense
near the boundary, the bare `f32` sign of the residual gave 452 false inclusions and 23,438 false exclusions,
identically on every device. An exact unsigned-integer kernel returned every label correctly on all three GPUs
with identical bytes. A guarded `f32` kernel that deferred to exact arithmetic whenever the absolute residual was
at most 2⁻²¹ (the conversion and addition sequence bounds the error by 5·2⁻²⁵) decided none of the dense grid on
the device and 58,254 of 65,536 rows of a more widely spaced grid, with no error after fallback. The same pilot
is a negative placement result: end to end, the host integer loop was faster than any device (about 34 µs against
about 290 µs on Metal, of which under 13 µs was device time, and over 1.2 ms on one RTX 4090), and two-card
sharding was slower than one card.

DESIGN rules that follow:

1. **Representation before placement.** First choose a representation that preserves the strict inequality, then
   place the kernel where its data and its consumer already live.
2. **A guard is proved, not tuned** (PO-SPARK-02). `frob-derive` bounds the rounding error ε of the residual from
   its actual operation sequence. If |r̂| > ε the sign of r̂ is the sign of the exact residual and the kernel
   decides; otherwise it emits the case to the exact kernel. The fallback fraction is recorded in the receipt.
3. **A small host-resident job stays on the host.** The planner (§21.12) moves a predicate to a device only when
   its inputs are already resident there; otherwise dispatch overhead dominates.
4. **Digests are integer mixes.** `K-x-digest` folds the raw bit patterns of the canonical state slabs of one
   world into four `u32` words in slab order. A run digest is the wrapping sum of per-world digests keyed by
   address, which is independent of slot order. The device digest is a tripwire classed `Tested`; identities in
   the record (`EpisodeId`) are BLAKE3 hashes computed by the host over committed snapshots (§29).
5. **Random streams are exact.** Philox is integer arithmetic, so a draw is D0 everywhere; the conversion
   u = (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴ to a uniform `f32` in [0, 1) is exact (§35.9).

### 21.9 Backends

`KernelBackend` is a core trait in `frob-kernels`: create buffers, compile a library, build a step plan, launch
it, read back, and return a receipt.

- **CPU.** `batch-cpu` is itself a backend whose kernels are Rust symbols, so one harness drives every tier. The
  strict `batch-cpu` profile of §16 (transactional, event-located) is a different step plan on the same backend.
- **CUDA (`spark/frob-spark-cuda`).** `cudarc` pinned to an exact version with the driver, NVRTC and dynamic
  loading features, as in FrankenSim's `render-gpu` (FACT: `cudarc = "=0.19.9"` with features `driver`, `nvrtc`,
  `dynamic-loading`). The driver and NVRTC load at run time, so the build needs no CUDA toolkit. Dialect source
  compiles per device architecture to a cached binary; `frob spark precompile sm_89` fills the cache on a host
  without a GPU (the same recipe as `render-gpu --precompile`, FACT). Compile flags disable multiply-add fusion.
  Each device has one context and one host thread. With two RTX 4090s, worlds are sharded across devices and only
  small statistics cross through the host: the cards have no NVLink (FACT: the driver on `trj` reports a PCI host
  bridge between two cards of 24,564 MiB each) and peer-to-peer transfer is unverified (OPEN-SPARK-6).
- **Metal (`spark/frob-spark-metal`).** FrankenTorch's `ft_kernel_metal::compute::Gateway`, described below.
- **A third backend through `wgpu` [F]** is possible for other hardware because the dialect is small, but it is
  not planned and is not admitted as a dependency.

**CUDA mechanisms (DESIGN).**

- *Launch graphs.* The step plan of a bucket is captured once as a launch graph spanning one control interval.
  Published measurements of another simulator show throughput falling to roughly a quarter to a third when the
  graph is re-captured every step (FACT, Appendix H), so nothing in a rollout may change a shape. Replaying a
  warm graph has near-constant host cost, independent of its node count (HYPOTHESIS; measured by the
  fingerprint, §21.16).
- *A control block.* Everything that varies between replays of the same graph (step counter for random streams,
  epoch, cancel word, decimation phase) lives in a small device buffer that the first kernel of the graph
  advances. The graph's arguments never change, so it is never re-captured.
- *Stream-ordered pools.* The arena is one allocation made at environment creation. Transient device memory
  (snapshot staging, readback) comes from a stream-ordered pool, so the allocations of a cancelled stream are
  released in stream order and cannot leak. If the pinned `cudarc` lacks a binding for graph capture or for the
  asynchronous pool calls, the capsule declares the missing driver symbols itself under its `SAFETY.md`; if that
  is refused, transient buffers are preallocated in the arena and the hot path is unchanged (OPEN-SPARK-4).
- *No synchronization inside a control interval.* The host synchronizes once per chunk (§21.11).

**The Metal gateway as it exists.** FACT, read from `frankentorch/crates/ft-kernel-metal` version 0.1.3
(`src/compute.rs`, `src/lib.rs`): `Gateway::open` returns the process-wide device and command queue;
`library` compiles Metal Shading Language under `MathMode::Safe`, which disables fast math (`library_with`
selects otherwise); `Library::pipeline` builds a compute pipeline and exposes
`max_threads_per_threadgroup` and `thread_execution_width`; buffers are `StorageModeShared` allocations made by
`buffer_f32`, `buffer_u32` and `buffer_zeroed`, read by `read_f32`, `read_u32` and `read_u8` and rewritten in
place by `write_f32` and `write_u32`; `Gateway::batch` encodes several kernels in order into one serial compute
encoder of one command buffer, checks read and write declarations for aliases, borrows every output exclusively
until `submit` returns, and returns a `Receipt` with the kernel count and the device's execution time. The
gateway also reports `max_buffer_length`, `recommended_working_set_bytes`, `allocated_bytes` and
`max_threadgroup_memory`. State is resident: a buffer persists across command buffers.

| Limit today (FACT) | Consequence for Spark (DESIGN) | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Completion is synchronous: `dispatch` and `submit` block until the command buffer finishes | One `Batch` per chunk of control intervals; one blocked host thread per device; host work overlaps device work only on other threads | FD-FT-01 |
| At most 31 buffer arguments per kernel (`MAX_ARGUMENTS`) | Arrays are packed into a few slabs; kernels index through an offset table in a parameter buffer, as the piano renderer's `P[P_*]` slots do | FD-FT-01 |
| Buffers bind at offset zero | Slab-relative indexing inside kernels; no sub-buffer views | FD-FT-01 |
| Shared storage only; no private storage, blit or heap | Readback is a host copy after completion | none needed |
| Host data is copied in and out; typed helpers for `f32`, `u32` and bytes out | Scene fields and tables upload once; 16-bit fields are packed into `u32` words; rings are read by copy | FD-FT-01 |
| Compilation from source only; no function constants; no binary archive | Shape-class constants are `#define` lines prepended to the dialect text; a warm start recompiles from cached source | FD-FT-01 |
| One device and queue per process; the queue holds 64 command buffers | One Metal worker per process; no uncommitted command buffer is ever held | none needed |
| The wait cannot be interrupted | A watchdog marks the device stalled and the supervisor restarts the worker | FD-FT-01 |

Until the asks in Appendix F land, Spark works inside these limits. It does not patch the gateway and does not
schedule a change to it.

**Metal readback rules (DESIGN).**

- **R1.** The host reads a device slab only after the command buffer that last wrote it has completed and
  returned its receipt. Under the gateway today that is a compile-time fact, since a batch holds each output by
  exclusive borrow until `submit` returns (FACT: compile-fail tests in `compute.rs`).
- **R2.** A terminal state other than `Completed` invalidates every slab the batch could write. This is not
  hypothetical: "when another process hangs the GPU, macOS restarts it and aborts every command buffer in
  flight" (FACT: `lib.rs`). The unit of commit is therefore the chunk, which is one command buffer. A chunk
  reads its checkpointable state from the current one of two committed slabs and ends with `K-commit`, which
  writes the new state to the other. Only a `Completed` receipt makes the other slab current. On failure the
  current slab was never written, so the arena restores from it and resubmits, at most four attempts with
  growing waits, then refuses with `FROB-REF-607`. Every retry is counted in the receipt. A replay from the
  committed slab is bit-identical because no kernel has data-dependent scheduling.
- **R3.** All storage is shared today, so no blit and no managed-resource synchronization exist to be forgotten.
  When private storage arrives through FD-FT-01, results are blitted to a shared snapshot slab inside the same
  command buffer and read under R1; managed storage is not used.
- **R4.** A run is labeled native on a device only if its receipt carries a completed command buffer with start
  and end times from that device (FACT: `Receipt.device_seconds` spans Metal's `GPUStartTime` to `GPUEndTime`).
- **R5.** Timers cover completion, never enqueue alone.
- **R6.** The device's thread execution width is read from the pipeline, never assumed, and thread-group sizes
  are multiples of it.

### 21.10 The device arena, leases and the binary kernel cache

**Arena (DESIGN).** Every per-world quantity lives in preallocated structure-of-arrays slabs aligned to 256
bytes: state (configuration, velocity, anchors, warm starts, actuator and battery state), kinematic and dynamic
cache, rows, contacts, variant and scene tables, rollout storage, policy weights and optimizer state, the
statistics ring, the two committed slabs, the snapshot slab and the control block. Slabs are packed into few
device buffers so that every kernel stays under the argument limit, and no slab exceeds the device's largest
buffer (§36.10). After environment creation there is no device allocation (INV-SPARK-03).

**Leases.** A slab is touched by the host only under an epoch-fenced lease:

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
enum Access { Device, HostRead, HostWrite }
struct SlabLease { slab: SlabId, epoch: u64, access: Access }   // valid only while `epoch` is the committed epoch
```

A host-read lease exists only on the current committed slab, the snapshot slab and the previous slot of the
double-buffered ring, so diagnostics read epoch e − 1 while the device writes epoch e. A host-write lease
(resets by the host, admission of a model variant into a free slot) exists only between chunks. On Metal the
lease is the borrow the gateway already enforces; on CUDA it is a runtime epoch check around stream-ordered
copies. A violation is `FROB-REF-614`. `spark.place` prints the residency map: where each slab lives, its size,
its last committed epoch and who may read it.

**Binary kernel cache.** FACT: the piano renderer caches CUBINs under a key built from Rust's default hasher
over source and options plus the architecture name, written aside and renamed (`cuda.rs`); a compile takes
about 5 s on an idle host and can take 15 minutes on a heavily loaded one (`render-gpu/README.md`). DESIGN:

- The key is BLAKE3 over the expanded dialect text, compile options, prelude version, compiler identity, device
  architecture and driver version. The default hasher is not stable across toolchains and is not used.
- The value is a CUBIN on CUDA. On Metal it is the expanded source and its digest, because the gateway compiles
  from source only; a precompiled archive is part of ask FD-FT-01.
- A hit is checked against its digest before use; a corrupt entry is a miss. An offline host that cannot recompile
  refuses with `FROB-REF-612`.
- The directory is content-addressed and may be copied between hosts. Its size is capped (4 GiB by default,
  DESIGN); eviction prefers entries that are cheap to recompile.
- A change of compiler or driver changes the key, so the old binary is never loaded, and it also marks the
  CPU-versus-device check stale (§21.13).

### 21.11 Execution model: shape classes, variants, buckets and the step plan

Environment creation allocates every buffer, compiles or loads every kernel, and builds a static **step plan**:
an ordered list of launches with fixed grid sizes. A rollout is policy forward, then the step plan repeated for
the control decimation, then observation, all on the device. The host receives a small statistics ring and
snapshots on request.

**Shape class and bucket (DESIGN).**

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures. This is the device-side view of §17.4's `ShapeClass`: the `_cap` fields are its
// capacities, the fields after `sensor_layout` are device-side keys, and `WorldRow` is its `WorldSlots` with
// bucket-local indices. Appendix I holds the one definition.
struct ShapeClass {                 // content-hashed; one kernel set and one launch graph per class per device
    nv_cap: u16,                    // degrees of freedom: one of 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, 64 by default (§17.5)
    link_cap: u16, depth_cap: u8,
    contact_cap: u16, row_cap: u16, sample_cap: u16, actuator_cap: u16,
    sensor_layout: LayoutId, policy_layout: LayoutId,
    contact_law: LawId, precision: PrecisionProfile, iterations: u8,
}
struct Bucket { class: ShapeClass, device: DeviceId, model_variants: u16, scene_variants: u16, worlds: u32 }
struct WorldRow { model_variant: u16, scene_variant: u16, task: u16, job: u16, addr: [u32; 4] }   // 8 + 16 bytes
```

A bucket is one shape class on one device: one arena, one compiled kernel set, one step plan, one launch graph.
A world is a row of indices into the bucket's tables. Three kinds of variation ride on that row:

1. **Parameter variants [S].** Worlds of one topology carry different masses, lengths, gains, card parameters and
   contact parameters. This is parity with MuJoCo Warp, which already batches model fields per world (FACT,
   Appendix H), and is stated here as a requirement: no kernel compile and no graph capture per design.
2. **Topology variants in one bucket [F].** A variant table holds, per link, the parent index, depth, joint
   class as a column mask over a 6 × 3 screw basis (so one-, two- and three-axis joints are data), placement,
   ten-number inertia, limits and an actuator-card slot, plus the start of each depth level. Padding links are
   identity transforms with zero inertia at trailing indices. Arithmetic: about 192 bytes per link, so a 64-link
   variant is about 12 kB and 256 variants about 3 MB (DESIGN estimate). Admitting a new variant is a host write
   of those rows into a free slot between chunks.
3. **Design-family buckets [F].** A family (§17) is packed so that constants shared by its members are stored
   once and referenced by index: tables of unchanged rigid sub-assemblies, actuator cards, collision geometry.
   Continuous parameters occupy columns. Sharing is by content hash of design constants only. State-dependent
   work is never shared between designs on the throughput tiers: exact sharing would require identical inputs
   and identical rounding, and reuse of a nearby trajectory would need its own error certificate.

**Layout rules.** Worlds of one variant are contiguous and allocated in multiples of the device's execution
width (32 on both device families today; FACT for CUDA warps, and the gateway's own note for Apple silicon), so
that lanes of one wave share control flow. Padding obeys §35.5, which makes a world's result independent of the
capacity of the bucket it ran in. Masks apply to every kernel, to rewards and to gradient accumulation: a padded
contact that stays active would change the physics.

**How many buckets.** The cost of padding a batch to its largest member, when kernels do full work on padding,
is Ω_p = L_max^p / E[L^p] for work that grows as the p-th power of size L, and 1/r ≤ Ω_p ≤ r⁻ᵖ where r is the
mean size over the maximum. Worked values: degrees of freedom 18 and 35 mixed evenly give 1.3, 1.6 and 1.8 for
p = 1, 2, 3; mixed 90 to 10 they give 1.8, 3.0 and 4.5. With the default capacities the worst member of a bucket
sits just above the previous capacity (25 in the 36 bucket), for Ω₁ ≤ 1.44 and Ω₃ ≤ 3.0. Masked lanes that exit
early pay less than this bound: Newton's Kamino solver runs a mixed batch of three body plans at 0.86 of the
harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates (FACT, Appendix H). An extra bucket costs one more step plan per step. If
a step plan costs L seconds of launch overhead and a world-step costs t, splitting N worlds off pays when
N·(1 − 1/Ω)·t > L. HYPOTHESIS: with about 60 launches of 5 to 10 µs each and t = 0.5 µs, the break-even at
Ω = 1.3 is 2,600 to 5,200 worlds; the launch cost is measured by the fingerprint and the bucket set is frozen at
M2 (OPEN-SPARK-3). The bound on what family batching can deliver end to end is in §36.5.

|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-BUCKET | Trigger: on the reference family corpus more than eight buckets are needed to hold Ω₃ ≤ 2, or the mixed batch of GATE-SPARK-10 runs below 0.5 of the harmonic mean of its homogeneous rates after one optimization pass. Fallback: at most nine padded per-family templates, each a homogeneous environment, scheduled in turn; parameter variants are unaffected. **Persistent environments and slot dispatch [F].** The unit of work is a world, not a job. One environment per
device and shape class stays resident in the daemon. A slot whose episode ends pulls its next episode from any
admitted job at the masked reset, by writing a new `WorldRow`. Results are keyed by world address, never by slot
or arrival, and dispatch is a pure function of queue state, so a job's digests are the same alone and in any
co-tenancy (§35.5). No shape change enters a running environment (`FROB-REF-611`). Why it matters: a 96-pair
comparison fills 192 of 8,192 slots, 2.3% of a batch; without sharing, the rest idles. Ceiling, stated before
any test: the gain is at most the reciprocal of the mean fill fraction of per-job batches, and is nothing for
jobs that already fill a batch. Defaults (DESIGN): at most 64 jobs per environment and 5% of slots reserved for
short jobs. Pilot before the work package is funded: a queue trace replayed on `batch-cpu` with and without
sharing. The dispatch policy belongs to the daemon (§32).

**Chunks, cancellation and faults.**

- **Chunks.** The host synchronizes once per chunk of control intervals. The chunk length is the shorter of one
  rollout segment and the number of intervals that fit the cancellation latency target (250 ms at the 99th
  percentile, TARGET).
- **Cancellation** is at the kernel-batch boundary: request, drain, finalize, with an executor-minted report
  (AE-10). A dropped handle is not a cancellation receipt.
- **Faults.** Device loss, out-of-memory, compile failure and a per-world non-finite sentinel are typed refusals
  (`FROB-REF-6xx`, §21.21). A world that produces a non-finite value is frozen and marked `Faulted` for teleport.
  A world that overflows a capacity is marked `Degraded` with the reason, and the caller's policy chooses
  refuse, reset or continue-flagged.
- **Stalls.** The barrier that stalls the suite's piano renderer is excluded by dialect rule 6. What remains is
  a command buffer that fails (rule R2) or never returns; for the latter a watchdog on a second thread marks the
  device `Stalled` (`FROB-REF-615`) when a chunk exceeds the upper end of its predicted time by a set factor,
  and the supervisor restarts the device worker from the last committed epoch. `ft-kernel-metal` has a
  fault-injection seam for the first case (FACT: `inject_command_buffer_faults`, present in builds with debug
  assertions).

### 21.12 The placement planner and its receipts

`spark.place` turns a request into a plan before anything runs. For each stage of the batched step (§16), for
the policy, the learner, the statistics and the snapshot path, it chooses a backend and writes down why.

```text
DESIGN: frob.placement/1 (one row per stage)
  stage, kernel ids, requested backend, actual backend, fallback reason, fallback count,
  transfer bytes host→device and device→host, synchronization count, resident duration,
  device seconds, command buffers or graph replays, retries
header: request mode (strict | prefer | mixed-allowed), fingerprint digest, shape class, bucket, capacities,
        determinism class attained, check currency per kernel
```

Rules (DESIGN):

1. **Strict means strict.** Under `strict:gpu-cuda` or `strict:gpu-metal`, a stage with no kernel on that
   backend, a stale check under `--require-current-check`, or a missing arithmetic class is an error
   (`FROB-REF-604`, `-605`, `-616`) raised by the planner. No world is stepped and nothing is scored.
2. **Mixed placement is opt-in.** Only `mixed-allowed` lets a stage fall back to `batch-cpu`, and the receipt
   then shows actual differing from requested, the reason, and the bytes and synchronizations it cost. `prefer`
   never mixes: if the named backend cannot run every stage, the whole plan moves to one backend that can, and
   the plan says so before the run starts.
3. **The denominator is whole.** Transfers, synchronizations, compilation and retries are in the receipt and in
   every throughput figure derived from it (§36.8).
4. **A cost model decides small jobs.** A job goes to a device only if its predicted device time including
   dispatch, transfer and synchronization is below its host time. The crossover sizes are calibrated per device
   family from the fingerprint. The pilot of §21.8 is the cautionary case: under 13 µs of device time inside
   about 290 µs end to end.
5. **Each device closes its own loop.** A hybrid plan may claim acceleration only for stages that ran on the
   device and returned a device-timed receipt (rule R4).

### 21.13 The CPU-versus-device check

`frob spark check` runs each kernel, and the full step, on `batch-cpu` in `f32` and on the device from identical
inputs, compares them under the kernel's determinism class, and writes the result to the **divergence ledger**.
A device result is not citable until its kernels have a current check on that device family, driver and compiler
(AE-08). The check runs again whenever any of those change, which the fingerprint digest detects.

Additions (DESIGN):

- The ledger stores, per kernel and per pair of placements, the distribution of disagreement in units in the
  last place as an integer histogram (§35.7), the worst case with its input, and the class attained. This is the
  stored disagreement record of §21.2.
- For a class D3 pair the check also runs the decision-level equivalence test of §35.4 on the full step.
- The check varies what must not matter: batch size, slot, bucket capacity, co-tenant variants and shard count
  (§35.5).
- A first divergence is located by bisection over the digest chain and recorded as step, kernel and quantity.
- Exact and guarded kernels are compared for byte equality on every backend, never under a tolerance.

### 21.14 Precision

Single precision on devices: Metal has no double type, and an RTX 4090 runs double precision at about 1/64 of its
single-precision rate (FACT: `render-gpu/README.md`). Four measures keep single precision adequate (BET-08):
anchored positions (§35.8), group-preserving updates with per-step normalization (C-05), fixed-order pairwise or
compensated sums, and a contact solve carried out in power-of-two-scaled variables with a reported conditioning
figure (§35.10).

Accumulators that need more (time offsets, odometry, the energy ledger) use a double-float pair type in the
dialect, as `render-gpu-piano` does (FACT). Unlike that renderer, the product does not use a fused multiply-add
(ADR-SPARK-10): it uses the split product, with the splitter 4097 = 2¹² + 1 for `f32`, at 17 operations instead
of 3. Two start-up canaries (`K-canary`) prove on each device that the compiler kept the transformations:

- Sum: the error-free sum of 1 and 2⁻³⁰ must return the pair (1, 2⁻³⁰). A compiler that simplifies the error
  term algebraically returns (1, 0).
- Product: the error-free product of 1 + 2⁻¹² with itself must return (1 + 2⁻¹¹, 2⁻²⁴), since the exact product
  1 + 2⁻¹¹ + 2⁻²⁴ is a tie that rounds to even.

A failed canary refuses the device for double-float kernels (`FROB-REF-609`).

Half precision is not used anywhere in the step; the calculation that rules it out is in §21.20.

### 21.15 Determinism on devices

Run-to-run determinism per device family is a design requirement, achieved by the dialect rules, sort-based
compaction and fixed-order reductions. Newton 1.8 and Isaac Lab 3.0 on PhysX document opt-in bitwise run-to-run
determinism for the same batch on the same GPU (FACT, Appendix H), so that property is parity. Two stronger
properties are documented in no incumbent read, and each is claimed here only after its gate:

- **A world's trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in** (§35.5, GATE-SPARK-15). Newton's own solver
  tests hold a world equal to its isolated run only to 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ (FACT, Appendix H).
- **Bit equality across CPU, CUDA and Metal for floating kernels.** This is a HYPOTHESIS. IEEE 754 requires
  correct rounding of +, −, ×, ÷ and √, CUDA documents compliance, but Apple confirmed in September 2026 that
  Metal offers no control over rounding mode or subnormal handling and the language specification permits
  flushing subnormals (FACT, Appendix H). GATE-SPARK-01 measures it instead of assuming it.

Exact and guarded kernels do not share that uncertainty: their decisions are integers and are compared for byte
equality on every backend (GATE-SPARK-08).

| | |
|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-D1 | Trigger: GATE-SPARK-01 or GATE-SPARK-02 shows that a device family cannot hold bit equality with `batch-cpu` for the floating kernels of the step. Fallback: that family is class D2 for itself and class D3 against others, with the decision-level test of §35.4; a teleported world is labeled a tolerance replay; a comparison whose arms ran on different families is unpaired (§35.3). The wording of the replay claim in §6 and §29 changes with it. |

### 21.16 The hardware fingerprint

Every figure in this chapter that depends on a machine is measured once per machine state and bound to a digest.
`spark.fingerprint` writes the record; `spark.devices` lists devices with their current digests.

```text
DESIGN: frob.fingerprint/1
  machine   host, operating-system build, CPU model, performance and efficiency core counts, memory
  device[]  family (cuda | metal), name, stable identifier, architecture, driver, runtime, compiler

```

FACT, as starting values the fingerprint must reproduce: this M5 Ultra has 36 CPU cores, an 80-core GPU and
256 GB of unified memory; its Metal device reports a working-set limit of about 222.7 GiB and a largest single
buffer of about 167.0 GiB; `trj` has two RTX 4090 of 24 GB with no link between them. 64-bit atomics are reported

disclosure block of §36.7. A receipt without a fingerprint digest, or with one that no longer matches the
machine, is refused as evidence (`FROB-REF-606`).

### 21.17 Division of labour between the machines, and the fleet scheduler

**Policy [S].** The two machines are not a fast GPU and a slow GPU. They have different jobs.

| Work | This M5 Ultra | `trj` |
|---|---|---|
| World bank: many scene variants resident; 2 cm warehouses and buildings; 1 cm interiors (§36.10) | Yes | Only the variants of the running job |
| Training specialists, family policies and long runs | Small and medium runs on `gpu-metal`; development | The training tier, one shard per card |
| Evaluation on large scenes; camera-heavy worlds (§36.12) | Yes | Low resolution only |
| Referee, `batch-cpu` shadowing, replay and teleport targets | Yes, 36 cores | Its CPU is often heavily loaded (FACT) |
| Derivation, certificates, proof and symbolic work | Yes | No |
| Viewer, stills, the dashboard | Yes | No |
| Large-memory jobs of any kind | Yes | No |

**What crosses.** Seeds and scene recipes (bytes to kilobytes), design and module differences (kilobytes),
model-variant tables (kilobytes to megabytes), policy weights (megabytes), receipts, digests and telemetry
summaries. **What never crosses:** compiled scene fields, world state, rollout buffers. A scene is regenerated
on the receiving machine from its seed with the pinned generator, and must arrive at the same `SceneId`
(GATE-HAB-02 already requires equality across Linux and macOS). A world that failed on `trj` is replayed on this
Mac from its address, its design, its policy weights and its scene recipe; nothing else is needed, and the
replay is exact or a tolerance replay according to the class attained (§35.3). TARGET: at most 1 GB crosses per
campaign-hour, metered per transfer class; an attempt to ship a field or a state slab is `FROB-REF-618`.
A comparison whose arms ran on different device families is unpaired unless class D1 holds (§35.3).

**Fleet scheduler [F], outline.** Owner: the daemon (§32); Spark supplies the resource model.

- A job carries a resource vector (device family or any, memory, shape class, determinism class required,
  envelope class of §36.14), a role tag, and the cost interval of `sim.estimate`.
- One queue per device. `fleet.plan` assigns by role affinity first, then shortest estimated work first with
  aging so that long jobs cannot starve. There is no adaptive controller beyond that.
- Job specifications and results are content-addressed records; an idempotency key makes a re-sent job a no-op.
- If a machine drops out, its jobs re-queue on the other only where role and class allow; otherwise they wait.
- Balance uses measured queue length and estimate error, nothing more elaborate for two nodes.

Ceiling, stated before any test. Adding this Mac's GPU to training adds at most its share of the pair of cards:
0.125 at BET-09's threshold of 25% of one RTX 4090, 0.30 at the stretch of 60%. So the gain of a fleet cannot
come from training throughput. It can come only from moving non-training work off the training cards, and
removing a fraction g of the work on the critical machine speeds a campaign by at most 1/(1 − g): a factor of
1.5 needs g ≥ 1/3. The pilot is therefore a phase ledger of one campaign on `trj` alone. If non-training work is
under a third of its wall-clock, the scheduler is not funded and the policy table above is applied by hand.

### 21.18 Specialization for survivors only; the fused step kernel

**Specialized kernels [F].** A specialized kernel is the generic kernel with one design's constants folded in
and its loops unrolled. Ceiling, stated first. FACT (MuJoCo Warp's own event trace of a humanoid at 8,192
worlds, Appendix H): of 262 ns per world-step, the stages a design-specialized recursion could touch (kinematics,
centre-of-mass terms, composite inertia, bias forces, forward acceleration) sum to 53 ns, 20% of the step; the
solve is 124 ns and constraint construction 38 ns. An infinitely fast recursion would give 262/209 = 1.25; a
recursion twice as fast gives 1.11; a manipulation scene gives a share of 16% and a ceiling of 1.19. Generated
code also does not reliably beat a good generic library (FACT, Appendix H).

DESIGN:

- Specialization is never the default and never part of the edit loop.
- It is built only for the survivors of a tournament that go on to finalist training, and only if our own M2
  profile shows a specializable share of at least one third of the step, which is the share at which the ceiling
  reaches 1.5.
- A specialized kernel must be bit-identical to its generic kernel: unrolling keeps the operation order and only
  exact constant operations are folded (§21.7). `spark check` enforces it; a mismatch is `FROB-REF-613`.
- Survivor cohorts move to specialized kernels only at an acknowledged chunk boundary, with a versioned state
  map, and the specialized kernel is another cache entry.
- Pilot before the work package is funded: three arms on one 12-joint quadruped at 8,192 worlds, measuring
  whole-step time and compile time: (a) the padded generic kernel, (b) the unpadded data-driven kernel, (c) a
  fully unrolled, constant-folded kernel written by script.

| | |
|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-SPECIALIZE | Trigger: in the pilot (c) over (b) is below 1.2 on the whole step, or (c) takes more than 60 s to compile; or the M2 profile shows a specializable share under one third. Fallback: data-driven kernels only. Derivation stays for correctness, certificates and the derivative kernels of CPU solvers, where rigid-body derivatives dominate. |

**Fused per-world step kernel [M].** One persistent kernel per world group would keep a world's working set in
thread-group memory for a whole control interval and touch device memory only at its ends, in the manner of the
piano renderer's persistent launch (FACT). The lever is memory traffic (§36.11). Ceiling, stated first: the
renderer itself refuses a block that needs more than 48 KiB of shared memory (FACT: `cuda.rs`), and one
quadruped world's working set is about 35 kB (§36.10), so a block holds one world, and occupancy is expected to
fall to a few percent of the device's lanes (HYPOTHESIS). The benefit is therefore unproven in sign. It is behind
a flag, piloted on CUDA first (on Metal the thread-group memory limit is read from the device), and off every
critical path.

|---|---|
| ✖ KILL-MEGA | Pilot: within one week, fuse kinematics, dynamics terms and one Newton iteration for the reference quadruped and compare with the staged kernels at 8,192 worlds, digests unchanged. Trigger: paired lower bound below 2.0 on the whole step. Fallback: the staged step plan, which is the design of record. |

### 21.20 Rejected mechanisms, with the calculation that refutes each

| Idea | Refuting calculation | Used instead |
|---|---|---|
| Factor the contact Hessian in half precision on tensor cores and refine in single precision | Half precision has unit round-off 2⁻¹¹ ≈ 4.9·10⁻⁴; refinement needs κ·u < 1. On contact states of three robots, after unit-diagonal scaling, κ·u ≥ 1 in 37 to 64% of states and refinement fails outright in 9 to 18% (FACT, recorded measurement, Appendix H). Each refinement step costs about 3n² operations against n³/3 for the factorization it replaces, a ratio of 9k/n for k steps: 2.3 at n = 27 and k = 7, 1.5 at n = 35 and k = 6. The published four- to five-fold gain is against double precision at n = 22,000; Apple GPUs have no such units | Single precision in scaled variables (§35.10). The factorization is 1,944 operations at n = 18 and 14,292 at n = 35, under a tenth of the step |

The whole copy is small: 8,192 worlds at 30 kB are 0.25 GB, about half a millisecond of read and write at an RTX 4090's memory bandwidth (§36.11), an eighth of one batch step at 2.0 M world-steps/s | Fork by indexed copy of the chosen rows into free slots (`K-snapshot`); warm template environments stay resident (§27, §29) |

### 21.26 Counts beyond 2³² without 64-bit atomics

**What it is.** [S] One design for device-side counting that is correct on every backend, given that exact kernels
are 32 bits wide (§21.6, rule 7) and that 64-bit atomics cannot be assumed.

**Facts.** FACT to recheck (a reported compile probe on this M5 Ultra; re-run by `spark.fingerprint`): Metal Shading
Language 3.2 and 4.0 offer no 64-bit integer atomic add and no 64-bit compare-and-swap. By rule 7 a kernel may use
64-bit atomics only if every device family in use records them, so no kernel of a mixed run uses them. A 32-bit
count wraps at 2³² = 4,294,967,296 increments. A run at 4 M world-steps/s reaches that in 18 minutes if one
histogram bin receives every sample.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN:
- *Bins.* `K-x-hist` increments `u32` bins with an integer atomic add, which is order-independent (§35.7).
- *Drain.* At each chunk commit the host reads the bins of the committed slab and adds them into `u64` totals in the
  ledger. The first kernel of the next chunk zeroes the device bins. Totals never live on the device.
- *A no-wrap bound, checked at plan time.* The step plan records, per histogram, the largest number of increments
  one bin can receive in a chunk: worlds × control intervals per chunk × samples per interval. The plan is admitted
  only if that product is at most 2³² − 1. Otherwise the chunk is shortened, or the plan is refused (refusal: a
  device count could wrap within a chunk).
- *Sums wider than 32 bits*, such as integer sums of quantized values, are never accumulated by atomics. Each world
  writes its own `u32` partial sum, and `K-seg-reduce` adds two-limb values in its fixed tree:
  (lo, hi) + (lo′, hi′) = (lo + lo′ mod 2³², hi + hi′ + carry), with the carry computed by an unsigned comparison.
- *Digests* are wrapping sums by definition (§21.8) and need neither.

Owner: `frob-kernels` (`K-x-hist`, the two-limb reduce); `frob-chronicle` (drain and totals); the Spark annexes.

**Known answers.** Two-limb add: (0xFFFFFFFF, 0) + (1, 0) = (0, 1). Boundary of the bound: 65,536 worlds × 4,096
intervals × 16 samples is exactly 2³² and is refused; one sample fewer per chunk is admitted. Planted overflow: a
mutant plan that lets one bin receive 2³² + 5 increments in a chunk reads 5 on the device.

## 35. Numerics and determinism

This chapter fixes what a number in FrankenRobotics is allowed to claim: which results are exact, which are
bitwise repeatable and where, which agree only inside a tolerance, and which claim nothing. Every other chapter
that compares, replays, pairs or cites a number does so under a class defined here. The classes are tested by the
Gauntlet's G5 tier (§38) and by the gates of §35.15. Everything in this chapter is [S], with two exceptions:
class D1 on devices is a HYPOTHESIS under KILL-D1, and the asynchronous variant behind the `fast-training`
profile is [F] under KILL-SHARD.

| ADR | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-NUM-01 | Every verb declares the minimum determinism class of its inputs, and refuses below it | A statistic that pairs, replays or attributes is only as good as the replay under it (§35.3) |
| ADR-NUM-02 | Class D2 includes independence from batch size, slot, co-tenants, bucket capacity and shard count | Incumbents document replay of a batch, not of a world taken out of its batch; the studio forks and teleports single worlds |
| ADR-NUM-03 | Under class D3, agreement of task decisions is tested beside agreement of states | A state tolerance neither implies nor is implied by agreement on a verdict (§35.4) |
| ADR-NUM-04 | The contact solve runs in power-of-two-scaled variables and reports a conditioning figure | Tolerances and regularization must mean the same for every row and every robot in a batch (§35.10) |
| ADR-NUM-05 | A nondeterministic training profile exists only as labeled class D4, relaxing the learner and shard order and never the physics | The asynchronous two-device variant must not pass as deterministic (§35.2) |
| ADR-NUM-06 | Anchored offsets are centred on their cell | A shift by the cell side is then always exact (§35.8) |

### 35.1 Determinism classes

Every result records its class. A class is claimed by a contract and tested by the Gauntlet's G5 tier.

| Class | Guarantee | Where it applies | Tested by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D0 Exact** | Integer, rational or symbolic results | Static analyses over ℚ, group computations, certificates, world addressing; exact-integer kernels and the decisions of guarded kernels on every backend (§21.8); integer and uniform random draws (a normal variate built from `det_*` floating-point functions is D2 per backend and D1 across backends only where GATE-SPARK-01 passes); rational clocks and phase accumulators (§20) | GATE-SPARK-08 |
| **D1 Bitwise across backends** | The same bits on `batch-cpu` (`f32`), CUDA and Metal | Algebraic kernels, if GATE-SPARK-01 passes for the device family. A HYPOTHESIS until then (KILL-D1) | GATE-SPARK-01, -02 |
| **D2 Bitwise per backend** | The same bits run to run and across worker counts on one device family, driver and compiler; and across batch size, slot, co-tenant worlds, bucket capacity and shard count (§35.5) | The design requirement for every kernel | GATE-SPARK-15 |

The referee tier in `f64` aims at FrankenSim's cross-ISA class on CPUs by using its deterministic elementary
functions; FrankenSim's `fs-mbd` currently calls platform `sqrt`, `sin` and `cos` and makes no cross-ISA claim
(FACT), so that is ask FD-FSIM-07.

Two rules govern the classes. **The weakest input wins** (INV-NUM-06): the class of a result is the weakest class
among the kernels, placements, profile and inputs that produced it. **A class is never raised by relabeling**,
only by running again under a stricter profile.

What is parity and what is not. Newton 1.8 and Isaac Lab 3.0 document opt-in bitwise run-to-run determinism for
the same batch on the same GPU, and Newton states that CPU and GPU results usually differ (FACT, Appendix H).
Class D2 as the same batch repeated is therefore parity. The two properties this plan adds, each claimed only
after its gate, are the independence of a world from its batch (D2 as defined above) and bit equality between a
device and the CPU reference (D1). Under class D3 a replay on another backend is a tolerance replay, which an
incumbent can also layer.

### 35.2 Profiles

A profile is a named set of pins. A run declares one; a result carries it.

| Profile | Class | What is pinned | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reference-replay` | D2 on `batch-cpu` and on the referee; D1 against a device where attained | Solver, arithmetic, toolchain, order and addresses. Declared state and logical events are byte-identical on repeat and across checkpoint and restart | The record; teleport targets; attribution; the numeric side of certificates |
| `device-replay` | D2 on one device family | Stable reductions, sorted contacts, select-only masks, the fingerprint. Identical on repeat and under any permutation or regrouping of worlds | Training, evaluation and paired comparison on that family |
| `cross-backend` | D1 if attained, otherwise D3 with the decision-level test | Physically scaled state error, impulse and work residuals, event times and task decisions; the first divergence is kept | Promotion; teleport, labeled exact or tolerance; results that cross machines |
| `fast-training` | D4, labeled | Only the knobs listed below, all recorded | Producing weights, and nothing else |

**Scope of `fast-training` (DESIGN).** The physics step stays class D2 in every profile. The profile relaxes
only: the order in which shards deliver collected segments and gradient sums (asynchronous collection, with a
declared bound on version lag); the learner's reduction order and matrix-product implementation (`K-gemm` may
use a device matrix path); and minibatch composition by arrival. Its only output is a set of weights tagged
`trained_under: D4`. Three rules bind it:

1. No number observed during such a run (a learning curve, a training return) is evidence (`FROB-REF-861`).
2. Every claim about the resulting policy comes from re-evaluation under `device-replay` or stricter, which is
   deterministic given the weights.
3. The profile is off by default and is enabled per job, never per installation.

Ceiling, stated before any test: inference and learning together are about 13% of training wall-clock on one
incumbent stack (§36.4), so relaxing them gains at most 1.15 on one device. The profile's real use is the
asynchronous two-device variant of §36.6.

**Why pairing and attribution need replay.** Seeding a generator fixes the draws, not the order of a reduction.
A difference of one unit in the last place grows like e^(λt) in contact-rich dynamics and reaches order one
after about ln(2²⁴)/λ ≈ 16.6/λ seconds, for a largest Lyapunov exponent λ that is measured per task. The variance
of a paired difference scales with 1 − ρ: if uncontrolled arithmetic lowers the correlation between arms from
0.95 to 0.50, a comparison needs (1 − 0.50)/(1 − 0.95) = 10 times the episodes (illustrative values). A paired
test under class D3 remains valid as statistics, which is why it is allowed and labeled. Under class D4 nothing
about an arm can be replayed, so its verdict could not be reproduced, promoted or attributed; that is the reason
for the refusal, and the refusal is by code, not by convention.

### 35.4 Decision-level equivalence under class D3

A state tolerance and a decision are different things. The smallest case: a success predicate x ≥ 1 m, one
placement ending at 1.0000001 m and the other at 0.9999999 m. The state error is 2·10⁻⁷ m, inside any envelope;
the verdicts differ. Conversely two placements may end a metre apart and agree that the robot fell.

DESIGN. For a pair of placements claimed at class D3, on a frozen ensemble of world addresses with frozen
policies, `spark check` and `eval.promote` compute per world:

1. state error in physically scaled units at checkpoints, until the first divergence beyond the envelope;
2. impulse and work residuals;
3. event times (first contact, termination);
4. **task decisions**: termination cause, each success predicate, each requirement verdict, the winner of each
   design comparison in the ensemble, and the pass or fail of each gate metric;
5. the margin of each decision, that is, the distance of its deciding quantity from its boundary.

The pair is decision-equivalent when all of the following hold (thresholds are TARGETs, OPEN-NUM-2):

- the one-sided 95% upper bound (exact binomial) on the rate of discordant decisions is at most 1%. With 4,096
  worlds that admits roughly thirty discordant worlds; with zero discordant worlds the bound is about 3/n, so
  at least 300 worlds are needed to show 1% at all;
- discordances are not one-sided: the bound on the net rate (one placement passing where the other fails, minus
  the reverse) is at most 0.5%;
- continuous outcomes (return, energy per metre, time to failure) are equivalent by two one-sided tests inside
  the margins of the fidelity ledger;
- the ranking of the designs in the ensemble agrees at the top, by the rank-agreement measure the plan uses
  between evaluation rungs (§28).

Discordant worlds are listed with their margins. A decision whose margin is smaller than the divergence envelope
is reported as `boundary-sensitive` on both placements, whichever way it fell. The first divergence of each
discordant world (step, kernel, quantity) goes to the divergence ledger.

### 35.5 A world does not depend on the batch it ran in

**Statement (INV-NUM-03).** Given a policy (weights and normalizers), a world's trajectory is a function of its
address, design, scene and task alone. It is the same bits when the world runs alone, in any batch, at any slot,
beside any co-tenants, in any bucket whose capacities it fits, and on any number of shards of one device family.

FACT: Newton, which documents bitwise replay of a batch, holds a world equal to its isolated run only to 10⁻⁵
to 10⁻⁴ in its own tests (Appendix H). The property is therefore worth having only if it is exact.

Design conditions, each a rule elsewhere in this plan:

1. No kernel reads or writes another world's rows (INV-SPARK-04).
2. A per-world reduction is a pairwise tree anchored at index 0 over power-of-two blocks, so which two active
   elements are added first does not depend on capacity.
3. **Padding neutrality (INV-NUM-02).** Padding trails the active lanes and holds the neutral element of its
   consumer: −0.0 for sums, 1.0 for products, +∞ and −∞ for minimum and maximum, and identity rows with zero
   right-hand side in the factorization, which follow every active row and so never enter an active entry.
4. A mask is a select.
5. The number of solver iterations launched is a constant of the shape class; a world whose `done` mask is set
   is copied forward, not recomputed.
6. Randomness, resets and task assignment are functions of the world address (§35.9).
7. Sort keys are total orders within a world.
8. Statistics across worlds never feed state. Observation normalizers are frozen within a collection segment and
   are constants of the policy in evaluation.

Two counterexamples show that the rules are not pedantry. **Padding with +0.0 is not neutral:** the sum of the
single active element −0.0 is −0.0 alone and +0.0 when one +0.0 lane is appended, because (−0.0) + (+0.0) = +0.0;
the values compare equal, the bits differ, and a digest over bits changes with capacity. **A batch normalizer is
a neighbour:** two worlds observing 1 and 3 and normalized by their batch mean see −1 and +1; the first world
alone sees 0, takes a different action and follows a different trajectory.

The test is GATE-SPARK-15. Slot dispatch (§21.11), teleport, forks and the memo graph all rest on it.

### 35.6 The bans

In batched kernels, on every backend and in every profile except where §35.2 says otherwise:

- no fast-math relaxation of any kind;
- no fused multiply-add, contracted or explicit;
- no built-in transcendental function and no reciprocal-square-root built-in;
- no floating-point atomic;
- no strict predicate in bare floating point.

A lint enforces the list in dialect sources and in the Rust of `frob-kernels` (`mul_add`, `sin`, `cos`, `exp`,
`ln`, `powf` and their kin are rejected; `sqrt` is correctly rounded and allowed), with `FROB-REF-251` at build
time. The IEEE probe records how each device family rounds an explicit fused operation, as data for
OPEN-NUM-1; the ban does not wait on it.

### 35.8 Anchored positions

Single precision resolves about 7.6 µm at 100 m from the origin and worse beyond, which is too coarse for contact
(GB-04). A world position is therefore an integer cell index plus an `f32` offset from the centre of a cell of
side 8 m. The offset lies in [−4, 4), where resolution is at worst 2⁻²² m, about 0.24 µm. Scene fields are
addressed cell-relative. Quantities that are differences (gaps, link-relative poses, velocities) never touch
absolute position.

**Re-anchoring is exact (PO-NUM-01).** When the magnitude of an offset reaches 4, `K-anchor` subtracts or adds
8.0 and moves the integer index by one. For 4 ≤ |x| ≤ 16 the shifted value is exact by Sterbenz's lemma (the
difference of two floats within a factor of two of each other is representable). Offsets are checked every step
and a body cannot cross 12 m in one step, so the precondition holds; a step that would break it refuses
(`FROB-REF-249`). The obligation is discharged exhaustively: the two binades and two signs hold 2²⁵ values.

**Why the offset is centred.** With offsets kept in [0, 8), a body that drifts to −2⁻²⁵ m must add 8. The
number 8 − 2⁻²⁵ is not an `f32` (the spacing just below 8 is 2⁻²¹), so the sum rounds to 8.0: the position has
moved and has left the cell's range. The centred convention never adds the cell side to a small number.

Cell indices are signed 32-bit integers per axis and a scene declares its bounds. The difference of two anchored
positions is the float difference of the offsets plus 8 times the integer difference of the cells, and is formed
only between neighbours. GATE-SPARK-05 runs the precision battery at distances up to 1 km from the origin.

### 35.9 Randomness by address

Counter-based streams keyed by logical identity: `(WorldAddr, step, stream_id)`, built on FrankenSim's Philox
streams (FACT: `fs-rand`). No device-side generator state exists. Common random numbers for a comparison are
therefore free: two designs given the same addresses see the same world.

Additions (DESIGN):

- **Draws are exact.** Philox is 32-bit integer arithmetic, so a draw is class D0 on every backend. A uniform
  `f32` in [0, 1) is (x ≫ 8)·2⁻²⁴, which is exact (PO-NUM-03). Normal variates follow `fs-rand`'s construction,
  ported to the dialect under the `det_*` rule.
- **A draw depends on nothing else.** Not on slot, bucket, variant index, shard, device or batch size.
- **Streams are registered.** `stream_id` names a purpose (disturbance, each sensor's noise, reset pose, each
  randomized parameter, action sampling, minibatch permutation) in a registry. Adding a stream changes no draw
  of any other stream.
- **Streams are keyed by the physical entity they perturb**, wherever that entity exists independently of the
  design: a gust by time, floor friction by tile, a sensor's noise by the hash of its port name. Pairing by seed
  is otherwise not pairing by physical field. The smallest case: a sequential stream r₀, r₁, r₂. Design A draws
  a push (r₀) and a friction (r₁). Design B has one more leg and draws a per-leg value first, so its push is r₁
  and its friction r₂. The seeds are equal and the two designs were pushed differently.
- **Proposal draws carry exact weights.** Because every draw is addressed, a disturbance drawn from a tilted
  proposal has an importance weight that is computed, not estimated (§26).

### 35.10 Diagonal scaling for single-precision contact solves

The convex solve factors H = A + JᵀGJ per world in single precision (§16). Two things matter: the condition
number that governs the accuracy of that solve, and the meaning of the solver's tolerances.

FACT (textbook results; Appendix H). Cholesky factorization without pivoting is invariant under two-sided
scaling by powers of two: absent overflow and underflow, the computed factor of D·H·D is exactly D times the
computed factor of H, since every intermediate is scaled by an exact power of two. The attainable accuracy of
the direct solve is therefore governed by the condition number of the unit-diagonal-scaled matrix whether or not
the scaling is applied. Scaling to unit diagonal is within a factor n of the best diagonal scaling of a symmetric
positive definite matrix.

FACT (two recorded computations on reference robots with simplified contact rows; Appendix H). For a 29-joint
humanoid over 201 poses, κ₂(H) is 3·10⁴ to 1·10⁵ raw and 2·10³ to 9·10³ scaled at default contact impedance,
and 2·10⁶ to 3·10⁶ raw and 2·10⁵ to 5·10⁵ scaled near-rigid. Over sampled contact states of three robots the
scaled medians are 1.2·10³ to 2.7·10³. With unit round-off 6.0·10⁻⁸, κ·u in scaled variables is 1·10⁻⁴ to
5·10⁻⁴ at default impedance and 0.012 to 0.03 near-rigid.

So the plausible shortcut "scale the matrix to make the solve accurate" is wrong: scaling does not change one
bit of a Cholesky solve. What it changes is everything around the factorization. DESIGN: the solve stage runs in
scaled variables.

1. **Exact scaling.** `K-scale` sets D_ii = 2^(−⌈e_i/2⌉), where e_i is the binary exponent of H_ii read from its
   bit pattern. The scaled diagonal lies in [½, 2). No logarithm is evaluated and no rounding is introduced.
2. **Tolerances in scaled variables.** The gradient-norm and decrement tests, the sufficient-decrease test of the
   line search, the pivot breakdown test and the regularization are all stated on the scaled system. One
   tolerance then has one meaning across rows whose raw diagonals span four orders of magnitude, and across the
   different robots of a mixed batch.
3. **A conditioning figure in the certificate.** The step reports κ̂ = (max L̂_ii / min L̂_ii)², read off the
   factor at no cost, and κ̂·u. It is a lower bound on κ₂ of the scaled matrix and is named a lower bound. The
   referee sampler computes κ₂ in `f64` for sampled worlds, and the ledger holds both.
4. **Precision profiles.** `default` uses no refinement. `stiff`, for near-rigid contact and the optional
   hard-contact law, adds one step of iterative refinement whose residual is accumulated in double-float
   (`K-refine`). One step multiplies the relative error by about κ·u, taking an error of order 0.03 to order
   10⁻³. The number of refinement steps is a constant of the shape class, so the launch graph stays static.
5. **A limit.** A world whose κ̂·u exceeds 0.1 is marked `Degraded: ill-conditioned`; under the `refuse` policy
   the run refuses (`FROB-REF-248`).

The smallest case for item 2: H = diag(1, 10⁻⁸) and a residual (0, 10⁻⁷). Its norm passes an absolute tolerance
of 10⁻⁶ while the error in the second unknown is 10⁻⁷/10⁻⁸ = 10. In scaled variables, with D = diag(1, 10⁴), the
residual is (0, 10⁻³) and the test fails, as it should.

### 35.12 A number never claims more than its certificate

Every numerical result records what bounds it: solver gradient norm and iteration count, maximum constraint and
closure violation, group-membership error, the energy-ledger residual, the number of worlds marked `Degraded`
or `Faulted`. A summary statistic over worlds states how many worlds it excludes and why.

Added to the record of every step and every result (DESIGN):

- the conditioning lower bound κ̂ and κ̂·u, the precision profile and the refinement steps taken;
- the fallback fraction of each guarded predicate;
- capacity headroom: the largest active contact and row counts against their capacities;
- the determinism class, the profile, the placement receipt and the fingerprint digest;
- for a decision, its margin (§35.4).

And three rules of wording, enforced by schema. A lower bound is called a lower bound and an estimate an
estimate. A quantile read from a histogram is an interval. A device digest is a tripwire classed `Tested`; it is
never called an identity, and no result is called verified.

## 36. The performance program

### 36.1 Position

Engine throughput is a parity requirement. It is not the source of the ten-fold (§2, §39), and §36.4 shows by
arithmetic why it cannot be. The performance program therefore has three jobs: keep the engine inside a stated
tolerance of the strongest incumbent measured on the same hardware; measure in a way that cannot flatter us; and
supply the phase ledger from which the ten-fold test is computed. The measurement rules are [S]. The numbers are
TARGETs. Two-device strong scaling beyond the incumbent's own is [F] under KILL-SHARD.

| ADR | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-PERF-01 | Engine targets are ratios to incumbents measured on our own hardware; published figures give the planning values | Published incumbent figures exist for the reference tasks; an absolute target without a comparator hides a gap |
| ADR-PERF-02 | Two units, world-steps and env-steps, each written with its decimation | An env-step count means nothing without the number of physics steps inside it (§36.2) |
| ADR-PERF-03 | Every target is a tolerance or an ambition and says which | A reader must know which numbers the plan depends on |
| ADR-PERF-04 | A ratio is admitted on a paired lower bound, never on a point estimate | §36.6 |
| ADR-PERF-05 | Ceiling first: no speed experiment without its computed ceiling | §36.5 |

### 36.2 Units

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **World-step** | One physics step of length h of one world: stages 1 to 9 of the batched step (§16). Stage 10 runs on control ticks |
| **Env-step** | One control tick of one world: d world-steps, then observation, reward and reset logic. A row says whether policy inference and learning are included |
| **Decimation d** | Control period divided by h. An integer for every reference workload. Where a task's rational clocks (§20) make it non-integer, the mean number of world-steps per control tick is reported as a rational |
| **World-second** | One second of simulated time of one world: 1/h world-steps |
| **Device-second** | One second of one device's wall-clock occupancy, including compilation, admission, transfer and synchronization |
| **Rate** | Completed work over wall-clock from submission to completion. Steps of worlds marked `Degraded` or `Faulted` are counted separately and are not in the numerator |

Conversion: for one run, world-steps/s = d × env-steps/s. Ordering (INV-PERF-01): in env-steps/s, training is at
most the full environment step, which is at most physics only. The smallest contradiction: 400 k env-steps/s of
training at d = 10 is 4.0 M world-steps/s inside training, and cannot stand beside a physics-only figure of 2.0 M
world-steps/s on the same card. The harness checks the ordering on every report (GATE-SPARK-19).

FACT (Appendix H): incumbents do not share a decimation. One publishes its quadruped task at a control period of
20 ms over a physics step of 4 ms (d = 5); another reports 1.20 M env-steps/s of physics as 4.8 M physics steps
per second (d = 4). The reference workloads here use h = 2 ms and d = 10. Parity is therefore measured twice: at
**matched settings** (the incumbent run at our h, d and iteration count where its solver allows) and at **native
settings** (each stack at its own recommended step for the task), the second in env-steps/s and in time to a
return threshold.

### 36.3 Targets

All figures are TARGETs. None is a measurement. A **tolerance** is a parity band: the plan's argument holds
anywhere inside it, and leaving it has a named consequence (§36.16). An **ambition** is a better number that we
intend to reach and on which nothing else in the plan depends. Where an incumbent can be run on our hardware,
the tolerance is a ratio to that measurement (GATE-SPARK-21) and the absolute figure beside it is the planning
value implied by the published number. Incumbent references are FACTs from Appendix H, each to be re-read from
its primary source before a gate cites it.

The tolerance ratio for physics rows is 0.67, a step at most 1.5 times slower than the incumbent's. Reason: the
environment step is between 0.29 and 0.87 of training wall-clock on incumbent stacks (§36.4), so such a step
lengthens training by a factor between 1.15 and 1.44, which the plan's other levers must absorb and which the
ten-fold test charges in full. Beyond that the engine is a liability (KILL-PARITY).

### 36.4 Amdahl limits

Each statement below is arithmetic on a FACT from Appendix H.

1. **An infinitely fast environment step.** On Isaac Lab with one RTX 4090 the environment step is 87% of
   training wall-clock for a humanoid rough-terrain task (94 k, 88 k and 82 k env-steps/s nested), so removing
   it entirely speeds training by at most 1/(1 − 0.87) ≈ 7.7; for a dexterous hand 6.7; for a cart-pole 1.9.
2. **An infinitely fast physics kernel.** On mjlab with MuJoCo Warp physics is about 29% of the environment
   step (347 k against 1.20 M env-steps/s), so a faster physics kernel alone gains at most 1.4.
3. **Inference and learning.** On Isaac Lab they are about 13% of training wall-clock, so removing both
   gains at most 1.15.
4. **The design cycle.** A cycle of 120 s with no training step, against an incumbent quadruped cycle of 360 to
   420 s (the 300 s of item 6 plus an agent turn of 60 to 120 s), is 3.0 to 3.5. Against a 30-minute humanoid run
   it is about 15. Both hold only if a baseline controller can stand in for a trained policy, which is untested
   (HYPOTHESIS; §22, §39).
5. **Compute per decision.** With an incumbent cost of about 410 device-seconds per trained candidate, a
   screening cost c_s per design, a fraction f of designs that go on to training and a training cost c_t of our
   own, the ratio is 410/(c_s + f·c_t). Ten-fold needs c_s + f·c_t ≤ 41 device-seconds. At c_t = 410: f ≤ 7.6%
   if c_s = 10, f ≤ 0.7% if c_s = 38, and it is impossible if c_s ≥ 41.
6. **Agent latency.** If an agent takes 60 to 120 s per turn and the incumbent trains for about 300 s per turn
   (a round planning value just under the published 325 s on two RTX 4090), deciding one design per turn gains at
   most (300 + L)/L, between 3.5 and 6, with zero compute on our side.
7. **Deleting training.** For the composite quadruped iteration of about 5.9 minutes (354 s; §2.2), with
   compilation inside the published training time, deleting training and compilation altogether gives 6.6.

Conclusion. On quadruped briefs no single phase reaches ten-fold: not the step, not the cycle, not compute per
decision unless few designs are trained and screening is cheap. The factors the plan bets on beyond these
(designs decided per agent turn, fewer turns to a qualified design, a cheaper training rung through family
policies and inheritance) are defined and tested in §2, §23 and §39. This chapter's duty to them is parity and
honest measurement.

### 36.5 The speedup bound for family batches, and the ceiling-first rule

**The bound.** If a fraction f of the strong baseline's matched wall time is improved by a factor r, and the
method adds overhead h as a fraction of that baseline time, then

    speedup ≤ 1 / (1 − f + f/r + h).

Worked values: infinite acceleration of 85% of the time gives at most 6.7; f = 0.97, r = 40 and h = 0.02 give
about 13.5; with h = 0.02 no r reaches ten-fold unless f ≥ 0.92.

**Applied to a family batch.** A generation of 256 designs at 16 worlds and 10 s each is 20.5 M world-steps,
about 10 s of simulation at 2.0 M world-steps/s. Run design by design, it also pays 256 switches (compile or
upload, graph capture). What a mixed batch removes is the switching; what it adds is padding, here taken as 30%
of the simulation time. If a switch costs 1 s, f = 256/266 = 0.96 and the bound is 266/13 ≈ 20. If a switch
costs 0.1 s, f = 0.72 and the bound is 35.6/13 ≈ 2.7. The gain of family batching therefore depends almost
entirely on the incumbent's cost of switching designs, which is unpublished (HYPOTHESIS for both values) and is
measured at GATE-SPARK-21 before §17's experiment is funded.

**Three rules for using the bound (DESIGN).**

1. f, r and h are measured on the strong baseline's critical path: a comparator that also caches graphs,
   factors and compiled kernels.
2. A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a device speedup that was measured on work already removed. The
   smallest case: 100 candidates of 10 s each; pruning removes 90; the ten survivors are the hard ones and
   accelerate by 2, not by the 5 measured on the full set. The result is 1,000/50 = 20, not 10 × 5 = 50.
3. Queue time for proofs and transfers counts toward decision time.

**Ceiling first.** Before any optimization or speed experiment is funded, its ceiling is computed from the
current profile with this bound, and an experiment whose ceiling is below its goal is refused (`FROB-REF-866`).
FACT, Appendix K: a specialized solver missed its speed gate at 1.345 times warm and 1.088 times cold, and a later
measurement left a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch (parsing, assembly, checking, serialization
and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost would have reached about 1.46 (842.8 ÷ 575.7). The
ceiling was computable before the experiment ran.

### 36.6 Admission by paired lower bound; weak and strong scaling

**The rule (DESIGN).** A performance ratio (ours against an incumbent, after against before, two devices against
one) is admitted only as the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound of a paired ratio:

- at least ten pairs; the two arms of a pair run back to back in randomized order on the same host state, on the
  same workload and addresses;
- with ℓᵢ = ln(aᵢ/bᵢ), mean ℓ̄ and standard deviation s, the bound is exp(ℓ̄ − t·s/√n), t being the 95th
  percentile of Student's distribution with n − 1 degrees of freedom (1.833 for n = 10);
- the incumbent arm is its strongest warm or cached configuration, frozen after tuning on development fixtures
  only (§39);
- compilation, admission, synchronization, transfer, fallback, refusal and verification are all in the
  denominator;
- on this Mac a 20-minute sustained load precedes measurement; on `trj` the host load is recorded.

A gate threshold applies to the bound, never to the mean. A ratio stated without one is refused
(`FROB-REF-863`).

**Optimizations.** An optimization lands only with its ceiling computed beforehand, with identical digests
(classes D1 and D2) or an unchanged fidelity battery, and with a paired lower bound of at least 1.03 on the row
it claims to improve.

**Two devices.** Scaling from one RTX 4090 to two is measured both ways, because they answer different
questions.

- *Weak scaling* holds the load per device constant (row 6). Shards are independent, so the tolerance is 1.8.
- *Strong scaling* holds the job constant: the same training to the same threshold at equal task quality (row
  5). FACT: MuJoCo Playground's own two-card run gains 1.26. Fixed costs explain it: about 45 s of its 410 s is
  compilation, the learner runs on one stream, and sample efficiency falls as the batch grows.
- Each shard must pass the memory estimator on its own, and peer access, bandwidth and free memory are read from
  the fingerprint at admission.

### 36.7 The disclosure block

Any Performance claim carries all of: hardware and driver; backend, compiler and kernel versions; world count;
time step, substeps and control decimation; solver iterations and tolerance; contact capacity and mean active
contacts per world; sensors enabled; whether the policy and learning are included; scene; determinism class;
warm-up and duration; whether launch graphs were captured; median and 99th-percentile step time; peak memory. A
public dispute over one simulator's headline number, later re-measured two orders of magnitude lower under
corrected settings (FACT, Appendix H), is the reason this block is mandatory.

Added fields (DESIGN):

- the unit of every rate and the decimation beside it;
- row capacity, and the counts of worlds marked `Degraded` and `Faulted`;
- the profile and precision profile; the fingerprint digest; the placement receipt with requested and actual
  backend per stage, fallback count, transfer bytes and synchronization count;
- cold or warm, with compile and admission times and the cache state;
- for a mixed batch: buckets, variants per bucket and the padding fraction;
- thermal state and soak duration; host load;
- for a ratio: the number of pairs, the lower bound, and the comparator's revision and configuration;
- rows that did not fit in memory, kept in the table and marked;
- energy where the platform exposes credible data, and a named gap where it does not, never a zero.

A figure without the complete block is refused as a Performance claim (`FROB-REF-862`).

### 36.8 Method

1. **Profile before optimizing.** Kernel microbenchmarks precede physics tuning. The first profile at M2 decides
   where effort goes.
2. **Account for the whole workflow.** Transfers, synchronization, compilation, resets and telemetry stay in the
   denominator (the rule in FrankenSim's accelerator doctrine, FACT).
3. **One lever at a time, with a behaviour proof.** An optimization lands only with identical digests (classes D1
   and D2) or an unchanged fidelity battery.
4. **Paired, interleaved runs** on a quiet host, with a thermal soak on the Mac. `trj` is often heavily loaded
   (FACT), so its measurements record the load.
5. **Regressions are test failures** against a tolerance band, on the smoke battery.
6. **Ceiling first** (§36.5), and the doctrine's own screen: effort goes to a kernel only if the top three
   kernels hold at least 50% of end-to-end wall time and the chosen one at least 15% (FACT: thresholds of
   `frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`).
7. **Measure the incumbent first** (GATE-SPARK-21, M0): on `trj` and on this Mac, by phase, starting with what
   needs no agent: cold start, a parameter edit and a topology edit to first step, physics and full step at
   matched and native settings, training to threshold on one and two cards. The harness is an out-of-process
   tool (§38); the comparator revisions are frozen by §39.
8. **Calibrate across sizes.** Every row is measured at 1, 8, 64 and 256 worlds and upward to capacity, giving a
   throughput-against-worlds curve. Rows that do not fit in memory stay in the table, marked.
9. **Timers cover completion**, never enqueue alone (INV-PERF-02). A run reports its latency distribution,
   env-steps/s, world-seconds per wall-second, completed episodes per second, valid steps, peak memory, and
   energy where available.
10. **Every estimate is an interval.** `sim.estimate` returns a calibrated interval for time and an exact figure
    for memory, never a point for time; its calibration is per fingerprint and is redone when the digest changes.

### 36.9 Where time goes

FACT (MuJoCo Warp's event trace of a humanoid at 8,192 worlds, Appendix H): of 262 ns per world-step, the solve
is 47%, constraint construction 15%, kinematics and smooth dynamics 20%, collision 4%, integration 4%. A
manipulation scene at 513 ns shifts to 44% solve and 32% collision. On a full environment step of another stack
physics is under a third (§36.4). HYPOTHESIS for our step, to be replaced by the M2 profile: the constraint solve
40 to 60%, collision 15 to 25% once worlds sample a scene distance field, dynamics terms 10 to 20%.

**Referee coverage.** A device produces T·h world-seconds per second; a referee on c cores at a real-time
factor ρ per core covers c·ρ of them. For H at the tolerance of row 7, T·h = 1.7 M × 2 ms = 3,400; 24 cores at
0.2 to 1.0 cover 4.8 to 24, that is 0.14 to 0.7%. For Q at row 1's floor it is 0.12 to 0.6%, and at the
ambitions they fall to between a half and two thirds of those. So the referee is a sampler by necessity: about 0.1
to 2.4% of GPU world-seconds across device rates from 1,000 world-seconds per second upward, at a referee speed of
0.2 to 1.0 times real time per core on 24 cores (HYPOTHESIS; measured at M2), and row 17 asks only that its share
be reported. HYPOTHESIS: the
`batch-cpu` tier, not the referee, is the tier that can shadow a tenth or more of humanoid worlds, since row
13's tolerance of 275 k world-steps/s is 550 world-seconds per second, 16% of 3,400.

### 36.11 The bandwidth-bound observation

Observation (a derived estimate; a HYPOTHESIS until GATE-SPARK-22 measures bytes touched per world-step): at
these state sizes an RTX 4090's step is bound by memory bandwidth, not by arithmetic.

- FACT (Appendix H): an RTX 4090 has about 1,008 GB/s of memory bandwidth and 72 MB of L2 cache; this M5 Ultra
  has 1.2 TB/s, shared with its CPU.
- A batch outgrows the L2 cache above about 2,100 worlds of Q or 750 of H, so at the batch sizes of §36.3 every
  kernel streams from device memory.
- If a world-step touches B bytes, the ceiling is η·(bandwidth)/B with an efficiency η of 0.5 to 0.8
  (assumption). With an assumed traffic of 100 to 500 kB for Q and 0.2 to 1.5 MB for H, the ceilings are 1 to
  8 M and 0.3 to 4 M world-steps/s. Arithmetic is not the bound: 1 to 3·10⁵ operations per step of Q at 5 to
  20% of the card's peak rate would allow 14 to 165 M.
- The estimate is contradicted in its middle: its central humanoid ceiling, 0.7 to 1.1 M, is below the 2.55 M
  that MuJoCo Warp measures on the same class of card. Either a step touches fewer bytes than assumed or η is higher.
  That is why the quantity is measured and not assumed.

Consequences (DESIGN):

1. **Bytes touched per world-step is a first-class figure**, measured per launch and reported in the disclosure
   block. The ambition of row 1, 4.0 M world-steps/s, allows at most 126 to 202 kB per world-step of Q.
2. **The dense Jacobian is the first suspect.** At 11.5 kB, three passes in each of four Newton iterations are
   138 kB, the whole budget. Profile-gated remedies, each with a behaviour proof: a fused assemble, factor and
   solve chain; storing a contact row only over the columns it touches (the base and one leg); a line search
   that does not re-read the Jacobian. They are tested at sizes 18, 30 and 60.
3. **No order of magnitude is available per step.** MuJoCo Warp already measures 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s
   on this class of card.
4. **On Metal the bound is elsewhere.** BET-09's threshold, a quarter of 2.0 M world-steps/s of Q at an assumed
   240 kB per step, needs 120 GB/s, a tenth of the chip's bandwidth. Command-buffer overhead and arithmetic rate
   are what the fingerprint must measure.

### 36.12 Camera memory arithmetic

A 640 × 480 frame with 4 bytes of colour and 4 bytes of depth per pixel is 2,457,600 bytes. Two such cameras on
256 worlds are 1.17 GiB per frame set; on 8,192 worlds, 37.5 GiB, more than a card holds. With 16 GB usable a
card holds at most about 3,200 two-camera worlds before any physics state. A 64 × 64 depth image in `f32` is
16,384 bytes: two per world on 8,192 worlds are 0.27 GB. Exposure-time and rolling-shutter bins multiply the
ray work, not the stored frame.

Rules (DESIGN): frame buffers are slabs of the arena like any other and enter `sim.estimate`; a camera's
resolution and cadence are part of the task identity, because changing the cadence changes the control problem,
so neither is a performance knob; camera-heavy evaluation belongs on the Mac (§21.17).

### 36.13 The distributed PPO normalization rule

For synchronous training on two shards (DESIGN):

1. Behaviour weights and normalizers are frozen during collection.
2. Each shard reduces its loss-gradient **sums** and its count of valid samples. The host adds the sums in
   device order and divides by the **global** valid count.
3. An empty shard contributes zero and still participates. If the global count is zero the update is skipped.
4. Minibatch order is fixed by address-keyed streams whenever equivalence between shard counts is being tested.
5. Weights are published only after a complete update. An asynchronous variant needs a declared bound on
   version lag and runs only under the `fast-training` profile (§35.2).

Known answer: one sample with gradient 1 and three with gradient 0 give 0.25, on one shard and on two, however
the four are split. The shortcut that fails: averaging the shard means. Split as {1} and {0, 0, 0} it gives
(1 + 0)/2 = 0.5; with an empty shard it divides by zero or halves the answer.
