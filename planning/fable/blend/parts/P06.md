## 22. Reflex: estimation, control, gaits, skills, shields

### 22.1 What it owns

Reflex owns every closed loop that needs no learning, and the safety layers that wrap any loop, learned or not:
the state estimator, the baseline controller synthesized from a model, the sampling-based predictive controller,
the gait analysis, skill artifacts, the energy-budget safety tier and the runtime shields.

| Crate | Layer | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| `frob-control` | 4 | The invariant filter; controller synthesis by morphology class; control programs over the shared cone solver; the residual wrapper and the ladder; the sampling controller; skill artifacts and their witnesses; backup controllers |
| `frob-gait` | 4 | Periodic orbits of a closed loop, Floquet stability, continuation in design parameters, design gradients (analysis A-04 made quantitative) |
| `frob-shield` | 5 | The energy budget and its monitor; barrier filters with their optimality check; assumption monitors; margin calibration |

DESIGN: the traits `Controller`, `ActionFilter` and `StateSource` are declared beside the step plan in `frob-dyn`
(layer 3). `frob-gait`, `frob-learn` and `frob-shield` accept any implementation of them, so no crate in layer 4
needs an edge to another crate in layer 4 (rule L-1). The enumeration of gait patterns is finite group theory and
lives in `frob-algebra`; `frob-control` reads phase tables from there, and `frob-gait` owns the orbits.

**Why it exists.** Training a policy for every candidate design costs minutes to about an hour per candidate. A
published quadruped joystick policy trains in about 410 s on one RTX 4090, humanoid policies train in under 30
minutes on two, and published agent-driven design loops spend about an hour of reinforcement learning per round
(FACT, Appendix H). Reflex synthesizes an estimator and a baseline controller from the model in seconds, so a new
design can be simulated at once (GOAL-13). The baseline is for feedback, not for peak performance.

**What that is worth, and what is untested.** If a baseline controller can stand in for training, the design
cycle of §28 is about 3.0 to 3.5 times shorter than an incumbent quadruped cycle and about 15 times shorter than
a humanoid one (FACT: arithmetic on the published figures, Appendix H). Whether it can stand in is a HYPOTHESIS.
GATE-REFLEX-02 asks only that designs traverse five metres; it does not ask whether the baseline ranks designs
as trained policies do. GATE-REFLEX-04 asks that, per morphology class, with consequences fixed in advance.

**What Reflex does not claim.** No controller here is called deployable. A controller that works in the model is
a `Tested` object in the model. Reality is a separate claim (§8, §30).

### 22.3 The estimator [S]

A contact-aided invariant extended Kalman filter on SE₂₊ₖ(3) with IMU biases: inputs are the IMU, the encoders
and the contact switches; outputs are base pose and velocity with covariance (C-13). Wheeled bases use wheel
odometry with the IMU on the same filter structure. Every evaluation can run with `--state estimated` or
`--state privileged`, and the difference is reported, because a controller that only works on privileged state
is not a result.

DESIGN additions, each a correctness condition for the sentence above:

- **Declared error convention.** The filter manifest states whether its error is left- or right-invariant and
  carries the covariance transport between the two, Σ_right = Ad_X̂·Σ_left·Ad_X̂ᵀ to first order. Biases, delays
  and uncertain contact add terms that are not group-affine, so log-linearity is evaluated, not assumed: the
  filter reports the measured departure from the linear error equation on the standard battery.
- **Gauge freedom.** Under proprioception and gravity, absolute position and yaw are unobservable: a subspace of
  dimension four. The filter's linearization must have exactly that null space at every estimate (PO-REFLEX-01),
  checked analytically and by finite perturbation of the true initial state along those directions.
- **Contact handling.** A contact point is added to the state at touchdown and marginalized at liftoff. A contact
  whose slip estimate exceeds its threshold is down-weighted, never trusted and never silently dropped.
- **Namespaces.** Truth, measurements, belief and policy observations are separate namespaces with declared
  access (§20). An out-of-sequence measurement may update the filter's future outputs; decisions already taken
  and the observation actually delivered at each decision instant are preserved in the record.
- **Scoring rule.** Estimator error is scored after one translation and one yaw alignment per trajectory, and
  never after a fitted scale. Counterexample: an estimate of 2 m for a true motion of 1 m has zero residual after
  a fitted factor of ½. A scale fit is refused (FROB-REF-464).
- **A comparator.** A conventional quaternion extended Kalman filter runs on the same packets. Normalized
  innovations, covariance coverage and aligned drift are reported for both. A filter that reports small
  uncertainty while drifting sets the flag `overconfident`, which is a failure distinct from drift.

Verb: `control.estimate` replays a run's packets through the filter and its comparator. Gate: GATE-REFLEX-03.

### 22.4 Baseline controller synthesis by morphology class

`frob control synth <design>` detects the morphology class from the model and assembles a controller. The last
two columns are the ladder of §22.7: what to try next for the class, and the evidence that must exist first.

| Class | Baseline controller | Tier | Next control experiment | Evidence required first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legged | A gait from the atlas (C-10) chosen by leg count and target Froude number; footstep placement by a capture-point heuristic; stance forces by a single-step force-distribution program over friction cones; swing by Cartesian PD through the leg IK kernel; joint PD at the driver | [S] | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus a learned residual for trot | Foot slip, friction margin, recovery after a payload shift |
| Legged, predictive | Centroidal single-rigid-body model-predictive control over the cones of C-06 | [F] | Contact-wrench feasibility with whole-body tracking | Momentum balance, step recovery, actuator margins |
| Multi-legged (six or more) | A phase-oscillator network on the atlas's phase lags; a support-aware wave or tripod schedule; the same stance program | [S] | A symmetry-reduced gait family, and adaptation with one leg disabled | Static support margin at rest; fall and energy rates under disturbance |
| Biped balance | LQR on the linearization about a stand, then the legged controller. Standing balance and a supported weight shift come before walking. | [F] | Centroidal planning with foot-wrench feasibility | Momentum balance, foot-wrench feasibility, step recovery |
| Wheeled | Wheel speeds from the commanded body twist through the kinematic connection (C-07), traction-limited acceleration, path tracking through flat outputs; wheel loops saturate at the card's current limit | [S] | Slip-aware odometry and receding-horizon docking | Circle tracking in the ideal model, then torque-limited acceleration and braking |
| Wheeled, steered | A curvature command converted to steering angles and paired wheel rates | [S] | Low-speed nonholonomic planning with steering lag | A turn-radius sweep including reverse and saturation |
| Omnidirectional | Body twist to wheel speeds through the connection, after the growth-vector rank check of A-07 | [S] | Traction-aware allocation under roller slip | Lateral tracking; a planted roller misalignment found as a rank drop |
| Tracked | Skid-steer with a slip estimate | [S] | Torque allocation that minimizes scrub energy and heading loss | Pivot work on two surfaces against an independent model |
| Hybrid | Roll where the route graph allows, step where it does not; an explicit drive, stand and step mode graph with readiness guards | [F] | Transitions selected by predicted energy, slip and clearance | Deployment under load; impulse and completion time across a threshold |
| Arm on a base | Operational-space control with the base as a floating frame | [F] | Payload-dependent whole-body planning and impedance | Grasp retention, base tip margin, task-space error on a moving target |

Gains come from the model: joint-space natural frequencies from the linearization and the reflected inertia, not
from a table.

DESIGN rules that hold for every row:

- **Errors live on the group.** Pose error is e = log(T_d⁻¹·T), and the desired twist is expressed in the same
  tangent frame as the measured twist. Near a rotation error of π the controller re-charts by a declared rule; a
  derivative requested at the cut receives a typed refusal, never a silent sign flip.
- **The whole-body program.** Unknowns are joint accelerations, contact wrenches and joint torques. Constraints
  are the equations of motion, the four-dimensional contact cone of §18 (normal, two tangential, spin), the
  actuator box read from the card's torque-speed envelope at the current speed, and joint limits. The objective
  is a prioritized list of task residuals with slack.
- **Infeasibility is an answer.** An infeasible program returns its active inequalities, its residuals, a dual
  certificate (§22.5) and the prescribed fallback, which is the last feasible command or the backup controller of
  §22.12. The task records every fallback (INV-REFLEX-01).
- **Actuator bounds are not negotiable.** The driver model of §15 enforces the envelope in the plant. A run that
  succeeded only while the controller requested more than the continuous envelope for longer than the card's
  duty rating is marked `actuator-infeasible` and counts as a failure of the engineering constraint.
- **Schedules are proposals.** A relaxed or smoothed contact schedule is re-checked under the committed contact
  law before it is used.
- **Identity.** A `ControllerId` is the content hash of the class, the derived gains, the programs and their
  iteration counts, the planning configuration, the compute-latency model (the delay from decision to command,
  in the clock contract of §20) and the symmetry group actually used (INV-REFLEX-02).

### 22.5 One cone solver [S]

The force-distribution and predictive programs are small dense conic programs (tens to a few hundred variables).
They are solved by the symmetric-cone solver that `frob-algebra` provides to the contact solve, compiled as
kernels so the baseline controller runs batched on any tier.

DESIGN. The solver runs a fixed number of iterations in lockstep across worlds and ends in one of three states
per world, which the caller must inspect:

| State | Meaning | What is returned |
|---|---|---|
| `optimal` | Primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity and stationarity all within tolerance | The solution and the four residuals |
| `infeasible` | A dual certificate was found | A vector y with Aᵀy in the dual cone K* and bᵀy < 0 |
| `unconverged` | Neither of the above within the iteration budget | The last iterate, marked unusable; the caller takes its fallback |

For a program "find x in K with A·x = b", a vector y with Aᵀy ∈ K* and bᵀy < 0 proves that no solution exists,
because 0 ≤ ⟨Aᵀy, x⟩ = yᵀb < 0 would follow. The nonnegative orthant and the second-order cone are self-dual, and
the four-dimensional contact cone is a second-order cone after scaling the tangential and spin components by
the friction coefficient and the effective patch radius. The certificate is rounded to rationals and re-checked
exactly (PO-REFLEX-03), which is what makes an infeasibility report class `Certified` for the stated program.

Smallest case, by hand. A body of weight 50 N must be pushed along level ground with a horizontal force of 30 N
through one contact with μ = 0.5. Unknown x = (fₙ, fₜ) with |fₜ| ≤ μ·fₙ, and the task fixes x = (50, 30). Take
y = (0.5, −1). Then y lies in the dual cone {(a, c): a ≥ μ·|c|}, and yᵀb = 25 − 30 = −5 < 0. The certificate
reads as physics: every admissible contact force has 0.5·fₙ − fₜ ≥ 0, and the task needs −5 N.

### 22.6 Reporting: three outcomes, never a bare failure [S]

`control synth` returns one of three outcomes, never a bare failure: `not-synthesizable` with the reason (for
example no gait in the atlas satisfies static stability with these leg counts), `synthesized-failed` with the
failure taxonomy of §29, or `ok`. A design that fails under its baseline is not thereby a bad design, and the
report says that.

DESIGN additions:

- `not-synthesizable` carries a witness whenever the reason is a program: the dual certificate of §22.5 for a
  force or torque demand that the body cannot meet, with the binding actuator or contact named.
- `synthesized-failed` carries the fallback log, the first layer that failed, and the next rung of the ladder
  with the evidence that rung requires.
- `ok` carries the saturation report, the fraction of ticks spent in each layer, and the standing of the result:
  a baseline score is `screening` until GATE-REFLEX-04 has passed for the design's class and task family.

### 22.7 An inspectable baseline plus a learned residual [F]

**What it is.** The command is u = limit(u_base(x̂, t) + Δ(o)), where u_base is the synthesized controller, Δ is
a small learned network trained by Cortex (§23), and `limit` is the actuator limiter. The residual has bounded
authority: |Δⱼ| ≤ ρⱼ, a declared fraction of each channel's range (DESIGN default ρ = 0.3, decided by
OPEN-REFLEX-3). Its output layer starts at zero, so training starts from the baseline's behaviour.

**What it must satisfy.**

- With Δ ≡ 0 the wrapped controller reproduces the baseline's digests bit for bit in class D2 (PO-REFLEX-02).
- Each tick records its layer: `baseline`, `baseline+residual`, `shield-filtered`, `fallback:<reason>` or
  `backup`. A residual channel pinned at its bound for more than a declared fraction of ticks sets the flag
  `residual-saturated`: the baseline is then not the controller, and the result says so.
- Two comparisons are kept apart when bodies are compared (§28). A shared controller isolates the change in the
  body. Controllers retuned with equal resources compare the best systems. A report states which one it is.

**The ladder.** Each rung is tried only when the rung below has failed for a recorded reason that is not a
design refusal, and each rung names the evidence it needs.

| Rung | Controller | Cost per design (TARGET) | Escalate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Synthesized baseline | Under 5 s of CPU | `synthesized-failed` with a control cause in the taxonomy |
| R1 | Baseline with a handful of gains tuned by derivative-free search | Under 2 min on a fast tier | Tracking or recovery limits remain after tuning |
| R2 | Baseline plus bounded residual | At most 20% of a specialist's training steps | `residual-saturated`, or the class has no baseline |
| R3 | Whole-body inverse dynamics plus residual | As R2 | The task needs contact sequences the baseline cannot schedule |
| R4 | Specialist policy trained end to end, or a family policy fine-tuned (§23.11) | One training run | Finalists only (§28) |

Verb: `control.ladder` returns the rung a design is on, the reason, and the next experiment with its required
evidence. Gate: GATE-REFLEX-05. Kill gate: KILL-RESIDUAL.

### 22.8 The sampling-based predictive controller [F]

**What it is.** Controller kind `mpc-sample`: at each control tick it samples perturbed command sequences, rolls
each out on the design's own compiled model inside the batch, and applies the first command of a weighted
average. It needs no training and no gait. It is prior art on incumbent simulators, including real-time control
of a real quadruped from about 30 samples on a CPU (FACT, Appendix H), so it is no differentiator. It is here
because two roles need it.

**Algorithm (DESIGN).**

1. The decision variable is a set of spline knots of joint targets over a horizon T_h, passed through the
   actuator model of §15. Defaults: T_h between 0.4 and 0.9 s, control at 50 Hz.
2. For each of N_it annealing iterations, draw N_s perturbations of the knots from counter-based streams keyed
   by (world address, tick, iteration, sample), so a planner run replays exactly.
3. `K-fork` copies the world's full state, warm starts and queues included, into N_s planning worlds.
4. Roll each planning world over the horizon on a planning configuration: a coarser step, fewer Newton
   iterations, fewer contact samples. The configuration is part of the `ControllerId`.
5. `K-cost` scores each rollout with morphology-independent terms: body-twist tracking, attitude error on the
   group, a centre-of-mass height band, electrical power, friction utilization above 0.8, and limit barriers.
6. `K-weight` forms softmax weights, updates the knots, shrinks the noise, and the loop repeats.

The three kernels are requirements on the kernel library (§16, §21). Planning worlds are forks: the digest of
the controlled world must not depend on how many forks ran beside it (the batch-independence test of §35).

**Cost arithmetic.** Let h_p be the planning step and H = T_h / h_p. One tick costs N_s·H·N_it model steps.

| Configuration (FACT, Appendix H) | N_s | H | N_it | Steps per tick | Steps per 20 s episode at 50 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Published quadruped trot, 20 ms planning step | 2,048 | 16 | 2 | 65,536 | 6.6 × 10⁷ |
| Published humanoid jog, 20 ms planning step | 2,048 | 25 | 4 | 204,800 | 2.0 × 10⁸ |
| Lowest published sample count, 0.9 s horizon, 2 ms step | 30 | 450 | 3 | 40,500 | 4.1 × 10⁷ |

DESIGN arithmetic on those FACTs:

- The quadruped configuration runs in real time on one RTX 4090 in its publication, so an episode of 20 s costs
  about 0.33 GPU-minutes with one design occupying the whole card. The humanoid configuration at the same step
  rate costs about 1 GPU-minute. At a 4 ms planning step the figures are about 1.7 and 5.2 GPU-minutes.
- A policy rollout costs 500 world-steps per simulated second at a 2 ms step. The sampling controller costs
  N_s·N_it·(T_h/h_p)·50 planning steps for the same second: about 4 × 10³ to 2 × 10⁴ times as many steps for
  the rows above, and about 2 × 10⁵ if the 2,048-sample humanoid row plans at the rollout's own 2 ms step. One
  device at the 2 M world-steps/s target (§36) drives about one world in real time on the cheapest row.
- Training once costs under 10 GPU-minutes for the quadruped and under 60 for the humanoid, after which an
  episode costs about 0.01 GPU-seconds in a batch. The break-even is therefore about 30 episodes per quadruped
  design and about 60 per humanoid design, and 6 and 12 at a 4 ms planning step.
- Acceptance needs many more episodes than that. Bounding a failure rate at 1% with zero failures at 95%
  confidence takes ⌈ln 0.05 / ln 0.99⌉ = 299 episodes: about 99 and 299 GPU-minutes under sampling control,
  against about 10 and 60 to train once. As an evaluator it is about ten and five times more expensive.
- As a screen it is cheap: one episode costs 30 to 60 times less than training, three episodes 10 to 20 times.

**Rules that follow.**

- **A screening rung of at most three episodes per design by default.** A paired screening comparison may use up
  to the break-even count of its class (30 for the quadruped setting and 60 for the humanoid at a 20 ms planning
  step; 6 and 12 at 4 ms), and stays labeled `screening: sampling-control`.
- **Never a stand-in evaluator.** Episodes whose controller kind is `mpc-sample` are refused as the scoring
  source of acceptance, promotion or qualification (FROB-REF-817, INV-REFLEX-03), unless the task declares the
  sampling controller as the delivered controller and the episodes meet the four conditions of the
  delivered-controller lane (§22.22).
- **Plant equals model.** The planner holds the model it is controlling. Its success says what the body can do
  under a clairvoyant model, not what a deployable policy will do. The result carries the flag
  `model-privileged` unless the planning model's parameters were drawn independently of the plant's
  randomization and the planner ran on estimated state.
- **Declared latency.** The planner's compute time is a modeled delay of one control period unless a measured
  figure for a named computer is supplied. A zero-delay planner is labeled as such.

**The two roles.**

- *Out-of-distribution fallback.* When the baseline is `not-synthesizable` or `synthesized-failed`, and the
  family policy of §23.11 reports the design out of distribution, the sampling controller gives a first
  behaviour to look at, with telemetry, in one to three episodes.
- *Demonstration source.* Its rollouts are demonstrations for distillation (§23.10). The arithmetic limits this
  too: at the cheapest row and the throughput target, 4,000 simulated seconds of demonstrations cost about 67
  GPU-minutes, which is more than one quadruped training run. It pays when pooled: 200 designs at 20 s each for
  a family prior, against 200 specialists at about 7 minutes each (about 23 GPU-hours), or for a class that has
  a morphology-independent cost and no shaped reward yet.

Verb: `control.mpc` (run, with `--episodes` capped at three unless `--demonstrations` names a distillation job,
`--compare` names a paired screening comparison, or `--freeze` writes a planner record).
Gate: GATE-REFLEX-06. Kill gate: KILL-MPC.

### 22.9 Gaits as orbits (`frob-gait`) [F]

Analysis A-04 listed the gaits a body can have. `frob-gait` computes whether a listed gait exists as a periodic
orbit of the closed loop, whether it is stable, how it moves when the design changes, and what a design change
does to a metric measured on it.

**A screening signal, with its published cost.** FACT (Appendix H): published full-order orbit solves take about
4 to 260 s of CPU at 20 to 68 states with initial guesses supplied, which is about 1% to 63% of a 410 s incumbent
quadruped training run. Continuation is not guaranteed to arrive, because turning points and bifurcations stop
it. An orbit is not an accepted controller: published hardware gaits needed added heuristics to stabilize.
Every result of this section is therefore labeled `screening: gait-orbit`. Floquet multipliers describe the
chosen feedback law on the chosen tier, and local stability only. A locally stable orbit on flat ground supplies
no estimate of recovery on a staircase.

**22.9.1 The map.** The state is x = (q, v, a, c): configuration, velocity, actuator and thermal states, and
controller state (the gait clock, and the filter state when the loop runs on estimated state). One control
period on a batched tier is a map F_θ: x ↦ x⁺, with θ the design parameters. The baseline's gait clock forces
the loop periodically, so a stride of N control periods is the stroboscopic map P_θ = F_θ^N, and the stride
period is a rational number in the clock contract of §20.

On flat ground under a body-frame velocity command the closed loop is equivariant under horizontal translation,
and under yaw when heading is not tracked. Those coordinates are removed and listed in the result; x̄ is the
state without them. A gait is a fixed point P̄_θ(x̄*) = x̄*, which is a relative periodic orbit of the full state
whose drift is the stride displacement.

**22.9.2 Shooting with symmetry, stability without it.** For a gait with spatio-temporal group H and spatial
group K, H/K is cyclic of some order m with generator σ (C-10), and the orbit satisfies the twisted condition
F̄^(N/m)(x̄*) = ρ(σ)·x̄*. Shooting solves r(x̄) = ρ(σ)⁻¹·F̄^(N/m)(x̄) − x̄ = 0 for x̄ in Fix(K) by Newton with a line
search, seeded by the atlas's phase lags and a settled stance. The problem is m times shorter and of lower
dimension than the full stride.

Rule: stability is computed over the full reduced state, never over Fix(K) alone. The monodromy matrix is
M = D P̄_θ(x̄*) = (ρ(σ)⁻¹·D F̄^(N/m)(x̄*))^m with the Jacobian taken in every direction (PO-GAIT-01). M is
block-diagonalized by the isotypic projectors of C-09, so each multiplier is attributed to a symmetry type, and
a multiplier outside the unit circle in a non-trivial block is a symmetry-breaking instability named by its
block. The spectrum goes to FrankenSim's Floquet analysis through the airlock, which takes a supplied monodromy
and also reports non-normal transient growth (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-spectral/src/floquet.rs`,
`analyze_monodromy`).

Counterexample, the smallest. Take ẋ = −x and ẏ = +y with the symmetry y ↦ −y. On Fix(K) = {y = 0} every orbit
decays with multiplier e^(−T). The full-space multiplier in the antisymmetric block is e^(+T) > 1. A restricted
computation reports a stable gait for a robot that falls over sideways.

**22.9.3 Derivatives, by tier.**

- *Compliant tiers.* F is a composition of kernels, each piecewise smooth. The Jacobian is built column by
  column, and each column is a tangent world rolled for one stride beside the nominal one: n tangent worlds for
  a reduced state of dimension n (DESIGN estimate: n is 40 to about 130 for the robots in scope). The first
  implementation uses central differences in `f64` on `batch-cpu` at two step sizes with a Richardson check.
  The second uses the derivative programs of C-14 and is differentially tested against the first.
- *Referee.* On the event-located flow, a perturbation is carried through each transverse event by the saltation
  matrix Ξ = D R + (f⁺ − D R·f⁻)·D g / (D g·f⁻), for a guard g(x) = 0, a reset R and the vector fields f⁻ and f⁺
  before and after. Each event records its chart, its guard margin, its identity and the denominator D g·f⁻.
- *What exists.* FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-orbit`, `fs-time/src/hybrid_sensitivity.rs`, `fs-mbd/src`):
  FrankenSim has shooting, pseudo-arclength continuation and monodromy, with a shooting module that disclaims
  hybrid saltations, and a separate saltation correction. It has no hybrid multiple-shooting solver on
  articulated models and no derivatives of the articulated dynamics with respect to state or design. The
  compliant-tier route is therefore ours and comes first; the referee route waits on upstream asks (Appendix F).
- *Labels.* Every derivative carries one label of the set of D-15 and C-16 (§9, §10); this section defines none of
  its own. Its four cases map onto that set: a derivative with no event in the stride is `smooth`; an estimate on
  one side of a guard is `one-sided`; a set-valued result, which carries several selection Jacobians, is
  `generalized`; and the derivative of a declared smoothed surrogate is `smoothed`. Compliant-tier Jacobians with
  an unchanged active set are `discrete-step`, event-located ones on the referee are `saltation`, and central
  differences are `finite-difference`. Near grazing, where |D g·f⁻| falls below its threshold, an ordinary
  derivative is `refused` (FROB-REF-431) and the offending state, the perturbation directions and the one-sided
  estimates are returned.

**22.9.4 Simultaneous impacts are handled.** Trot, bound and pronk put two or four feet down at one instant by
symmetry.

- On the compliant tiers nothing is special. Contact force is a continuous function of state, the stride map is
  continuous and piecewise smooth, and M is computed as above. One extra check runs: the touchdown times of each
  simultaneous group are split by ±δ and the one-sided Jacobians are compared. If they differ beyond tolerance
  the result carries both as selection Jacobians and the label `generalized`.
- On the referee an exactly simultaneous group is one coupled impact solve per island (§16). Nearby trajectories
  reach the same feet in some order, so the stride map has one smooth piece per ordering. The analysis computes
  the outcome and the saltation product for every ordering of a group of up to four feet (24 orderings) and for
  the coupled solve, and reports the order sensitivity: the largest difference between outgoing velocities in
  the mass norm.
- The verdict is `stable` only if all selections agree within tolerance, or if one quadratic norm contracts
  under every selection (a common Lyapunov matrix from a small semidefinite program in the shared solver).
  Otherwise it is `order-sensitive`, reported with the range of spectral radii and a finite-perturbation
  recovery test over sampled disturbances, class `Tested`.

Fixture, by hand. A rigid bar of mass m and central radius of gyration k falls flat at speed v onto two
supports at distance a either side of its centre, with plastic impacts. Let β = a²/k². An upward impulse p at
one support changes that support's velocity by p·(1 + β)/m and the other's by p·(1 − β)/m.

- Coupled solve: impulses m·v/2 at each support stop the bar. It ends at rest.
- One support first, by an arbitrarily small time: the first impulse is m·v/(1 + β) and leaves the other end
  approaching at 2β·v/(1 + β). The second impulse stops that end and changes the first end's velocity by
  2β·(1 − β)·v/(1 + β)². For β < 1 this is positive: the first end lifts off and the bar is left rocking.
- A uniform bar of half-length l on supports at ±l/3 has β = 1/3: the first end leaves at v/4 and 1/16 of the
  kinetic energy survives, against none in the coupled solve. For β ≥ 1 the first contact stays closed, both
  impulses are positive, and every ordering gives rest.

The switch is the sign of the off-diagonal contact-space term J₁·M⁻¹·J₂ᵀ = (1 − β)/m. Outcomes are continuous in
the ordering when it is zero or negative and jump when it is positive, which agrees with the published
kinetic-angle condition for plastic impacts (FACT, Appendix H). The analysis reports that term for every
simultaneous group. On a compliant tier the same bar moves from rest to rocking over a time offset of the order
of the contact time constant: a steep, finite slope, which the split check finds.

**22.9.5 Continuation in design parameters.** Pseudo-arclength continuation of r(x̄, θ) = 0 follows a path in θ:
one parameter, or the line between two designs joined by a typed edit. A child design warm-starts from its
parent's orbit. What happens on the path is a typed outcome, not an error:

| Outcome | Detection | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `arrived` | Residual below tolerance at the target | The orbit and its spectrum at the target design |
| `fold` | A multiplier of M at +1 in the trivial block, so I − M is singular and the branch turns back | The parameter value of the fold; no orbit of this type beyond it on this branch |
| `period-doubling` | A multiplier at −1 | The doubled orbit is offered as a new seed |
| `torus` | A complex pair crossing the unit circle | Reported; quasi-periodic motion is not followed |
| `symmetry-breaking` | A multiplier at +1 in a non-trivial block | The broken generator; the lower-symmetry branch is offered as a seed |
| `mode-change` | The orbit's contact sequence changed | The old label no longer applies; a new shooting problem is posed |
| `diverged`, `budget` | Newton failure after step halving; the budget ended | Nothing partial is published as an orbit (§35) |

**22.9.6 Design gradients.** At a fixed point with I − M nonsingular, d x̄*/dθ = (I − M)⁻¹·∂P̄/∂θ. For a metric
J(x̄*, θ) accumulated over the stride (cost of transport, peak torque margin, friction utilization),
dJ/dθ = ∂J/∂θ + λᵀ·∂P̄/∂θ with (I − M)ᵀ·λ = ∇J. This is the implicit-function theorem at a fixed point. It costs
one more tangent world per design parameter and one small solve (PO-GAIT-02). It is refused at a fold
(FROB-REF-431). The gradient of a multiplier is taken by differences along the continuation path. Where a
gradient is refused, the caller is routed to derivative-free search in §28.

**22.9.7 What a result contains.** A `GaitOrbit` record holds the `ControllerId`, the tier, (H, K, σ), the
stride length N, x̄*, the residual, the removed coordinates, the multipliers with their block labels, transient
growth, order sensitivity, the derivative labels, the metrics, and the standing `screening` (INV-GAIT-01).

Verbs: `gait.atlas` (enumeration and phase lags; also analysis A-04), `gait.orbit`, `gait.continue`,
`gait.gradient`. Gates: GATE-GAIT-01 to -03. Kill gate: KILL-GAIT.

### 22.10 Skill artifacts (`.fskill`)

**What it is.** A skill is a behaviour stated at the task boundary, independent of the body that performs it.
Format FMT-FSKILL, extension `.fskill`, schema `frob.skill/1` (DESIGN sketch; values illustrative):

```json
{
  "schema": "frob.skill/1",
  "name": "push-cart-level",
  "task_frame": "cart_handle",
  "interface": { "command": "wrench", "observables": ["handle_pose", "handle_twist"], "clock_hz": "50/1" },
  "phases": [
    { "id": "approach", "exit": "contact(handle)", "reference": "twist-profile#a1" },
    { "id": "push", "exit": "distance_to_goal < 0.15 m", "reference": "wrench-profile#p1" }
  ],
  "assumptions": { "mu_min": 0.4, "payload_kg": [0, 40], "slope_max": 0.05 },
  "secondary_costs": ["electrical_power", "winding_temperature"],
  "exact_layer": { "map": "constrained-mass-solve", "allocation": "cone-program", "witness": "dual" },
  "residual": null,
  "evidence": { "class": "Tested", "authorization": "the no-authorization flags of §12" }
}
```

**Layer 1, exact [S].** For a morphology and a contact mode m, the equations of motion
M·v̇ + h = Sᵀ·u + J_cᵀ·f_c with the mode's contact constraints give, after a constrained mass solve that
eliminates v̇ and f_c, an affine map from actuator effort to the task-space quantity:
w = w₀(q, v) + B_m(q)·u. The allocation program minimizes ‖B_m·u − (w* − w₀)‖² plus the secondary costs, subject
to the actuator boxes, the contact cones (with μ_min for forces the robot commands) and unilateral support
limits. It is a cone program in the shared solver and it returns one of two things:

- a feasible-allocation witness (u, f_c), which replays; or
- before any training, an infeasibility witness: a direction c in task space, the support value
  max cᵀ(w₀ + B_m·u) over admissible u, which is below cᵀw*, the gap, and the binding actuator or contact
  (FROB-REF-448). This is the dual certificate of §22.5, checked exactly.

Smallest case, by hand. A planar two-link arm has link lengths 0.4 m and 0.3 m and joint angles 0 and π/2. Its
tool point is (0.4, 0.3) m and its Jacobian is J = [[−0.3, −0.3], [0.4, 0.0]] m/rad, so a tool force F maps to
joint torques τ = JᵀF = (−0.3·F_x + 0.4·F_y, −0.3·F_x). A tool force of (10, 0) N needs torques (−3, −3) N·m.
If each actuator is limited to 2.5 N·m, the second row alone gives |F_x| ≤ 2.5/0.3 = 25/3 N. The witness is the
direction c = (1, 0), the support value 25/3 N, the requirement 10 N and the binding actuator, joint 2. Taking
the lower rows of a space-frame screw Jacobian in place of the point Jacobian fails this fixture.

**Limits written into every artifact.**

- *Instantaneous.* A feasible witness at each sampled state of a reference is necessary and not sufficient. The
  map cannot show multi-step dynamic feasibility such as capture-point recovery or a momentum-assisted jump.
- *Scope.* Task-space skills of wheeled, wheel-legged and manipulating robots, and the stance phases of legged
  ones. Legged locomotion needs a phase sequence to transfer, which §22.9 and §23.12 address.
- *Floating base.* A fixed-base pullback τ = JᵀF is wrong for a floating-base robot. The map must come from the
  contact-consistent constrained solve.
- *Refinement.* A transported skill refines its specification only if, for every admitted target state, every
  abstract command and every target disturbance, each target successor is related to an allowed abstract
  successor, with the command chosen from information available before the disturbance. Counterexample: a
  target whose successors are {stable, fall} cannot refine an abstract transition whose only successor is
  stable by selecting its stable branch (FROB-REF-444).
- *On failure* the report returns the contact mode, the requested wrench, the support function of the attainable
  set in the failing direction, and a counterexample that re-runs.

**Layer 2, learned residual transport [M].** A frozen, morphology-conditioned residual acts in task space above
the allocation program. Its inputs are made dimensionless by the body's mass m₀, a length ℓ₀ and gravity g, with
momentum scale m₀·√(g·ℓ₀) and angular-momentum scale m₀·ℓ₀·√(g·ℓ₀). Dimensionless inputs give dynamic similarity
only when every dimensionless group matches; published similarity transfer shows no loss on dynamically similar
contexts for a pendulum and a planar runner only (FACT, Appendix H). The experiment has three treatments at
equal compute and equal source data: training from scratch on the target, a morphology-conditioned policy with
full transfer learning, and interface-based transfer with limited adaptation. Both denominators are reported:
the incremental cost per target and the amortized cost with pretraining. It is off the critical path; its pilot,
ceiling and kill gate are in §22.17.

Verbs: `skill.compile` (a `.fskill` and a design give the per-mode maps and the program), `skill.check` (a
witness of either kind over a set of states), `skill.transport`, `skill.show`. Gates: GATE-REFLEX-08, -09.
`skill.transport` moves a skill artifact to another body with the learned residual of layer 2 and is [M].
`mission.transport` (§25.3) is a different verb: it moves a certified claim through an interface map with a bounded
error and is [F]. Neither stands in for the other.

### 22.11 The energy-budget safety tier (`.fenergy`) [F]

**The idea.** Certifying a contact sequence is hard. Bounding the energy that can reach a boundary is not.
Passive contact, with any friction coefficient μ ≥ 0, any restitution between 0 and 1 and any sequence of
impacts, can dissipate or redistribute mechanical energy and cannot create it. So if everything that could
deliver energy to a boundary is bounded, and the absorber at that boundary can take that much, the stop holds
for every contact sequence. No contact model appears in the argument.

**The budget.** For a declared boundary (a stop, a bumper, a door or drawer at the end of its travel, a lift
platform, a compliant cover that may touch a person) and a time t:

    E_deliv(t) = T_ub(t) + E_drive(t) + E_ext + E_stored(t) + E_reserve  ≤  W_abs

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| T_ub | Upper bound on the kinetic energy of every body on the load path, ½·v̂ᵀ·M̄·v̂, with measured speeds inflated by their sensing error and masses at their caps. It includes reflected rotor inertia. | Model, encoder cards |
| E_drive | ∫ max(P_elec, 0) dτ over the cutoff delay Δt_cut, bounded by P_max·Δt_cut: what the drives can still inject before the cutoff acts. Δt_cut is detection plus transport plus driver plus brake delay. | Actuator cards; clock contract of §20 |
| E_ext | Upper bound on work done by sources that are not cut: gravity over the stroke, a conveyor, another machine | Scene and declaration |
| E_stored | Elastic and other stored energy that can be released onto the boundary: series springs, preloads | Model |
| E_reserve | Numerical and sensing reserve, at least the energy ledger's residual bound over the window | Ledger of §29 |
| W_abs | Lower envelope of the work the absorber takes before its limit (bottoming, or a stated force limit) | Interface card with provenance |

**The monitor.** One accumulator and one comparison per control tick: kernel `K-energy-monitor`, in the
exact-integer kernel class of §35 with declared unit scales, so the admission predicate is a strict inequality
evaluated exactly on every backend. It outputs the margin W_abs − E_deliv. At zero margin it cuts drive power
and hands over to the backup controller of §22.12. The longest admissible cutoff delay is a closed form:
Δt_cut,max = (W_abs − T_ub − E_ext − E_stored − E_reserve) / P_max.

**Premises, each checked or declared.**

1. *Sources are complete.* Every port of the design's port graph (C-12) that can inject power into the load
   path is in the budget with a bound, or is cut by the monitor. The check is mechanical: `shield.budget` walks
   the port graph and refuses a budget with an unlisted source (FROB-REF-451).
2. *Caps are card values.* Mass caps and speed sensing errors come from cards with provenance.
3. *The cutoff acts within Δt_cut.* If the packet-delay distribution has a tail beyond Δt_cut, the claim becomes
   a probability and the artifact says so.
4. *The absorber is on every forward path to the boundary.* This is a geometric premise, taken from a declared
   guide or from the clearance analysis of §19. Without it the claim covers only contacts made through the
   absorber.

**What the claim is.** "For every contact sequence and friction coefficient, the energy delivered to the
declared boundary during a stop does not exceed the absorber's stated minimum capacity, in the model, under
premises 1 to 4." The inequality is an exact-integer predicate that the offline auditor recomputes
(PO-SHIELD-01). Its class follows the claim product of §8: `Certified` for the arithmetic and no better than the
weakest input, so an absorber envelope marked `assumed` yields an `Estimated` claim. Reality is `no-claim` until
bench data for the absorber and the drive are imported through Ground (§30). One absorber measurement and one
drive measurement would then serve every later policy for that hardware, since no term depends on the policy.

**What it does not support.**

- Peak force, pressure or injury thresholds, except at the absorber and only if W_abs was defined up to a force
  limit.
- Quasi-static clamping after the stop. A drive that keeps pushing poses a force question; the budget answers an
  energy question.
- Contacts that bypass the absorber, and sources that are not in the port graph: a second robot, a falling
  object, a person pushing.
- Regulatory conformity, or permission to operate near people. The artifact carries the no-authorization flags
  of §12. FACT (Appendix H): a published technical specification for collaborative robots states transient
  contact limits through transferred energy. The budget is a quantity of that kind; it is not an assessment
  against any such document.
- Anything about the simulator's contact law. The simulator is used the other way round, as a negative control
  that must never show an exceedance for any μ, any restitution or any contact law.

**Worked card (illustrative values; the arithmetic is the content).** A cart of 8 kg effective mass approaches
a stop at 0.4 m/s: T_ub = ½·8·0.4² = 0.64 J. The drive can inject 20 W during a 25 ms cutoff delay: 0.50 J. The
floor slopes by at most 8% over a 40 mm absorber stroke: 8·9.81·0.040·0.08 = 0.25 J. A series spring holds
0.10 J. The total is 1.49 J against an absorber rated at 50 N over 40 mm, W_abs = 2.00 J. The longest admissible
cutoff delay is (2.00 − 0.99)/20 = 50.5 ms, or 45.5 ms with a further 0.10 J of reserve.

Counterexample to the obvious shortcut. Budgeting the payload's kinetic energy alone omits the rotors. A rotor
of inertia 5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m² behind a 50:1 reduction, driving a wheel of radius 0.05 m, has an effective mass of
50²·(5 × 10⁻⁵)/0.05² = 50 kg, more than six times the 8 kg cart. `shield.budget` computes T_ub from the full mass
matrix, in which reflected inertia already appears (§16).

Format FMT-FENERGY, extension `.fenergy`, schema `frob.energy/1` (DESIGN sketch; quantities are decimal strings
so that no binary rounding enters the predicate):

```json
{
  "schema": "frob.energy/1",
  "boundary": "dock_stop_A",
  "load_path": ["chassis", "payload", "drive_left", "drive_right"],
  "unit_scale": { "energy": "1e-6 J", "time": "1e-6 s" },
  "terms": {
    "kinetic_ub": { "mass_cap_kg": "8", "speed_error_mps": "0.01", "includes_reflected_inertia": true },
    "drive": { "p_max_w": "20", "cutoff_delay_s": "0.025" },
    "external": [{ "source": "gravity", "bound_j": "0.25", "basis": "slope <= 0.08 over 0.040 m" }],
    "stored": [{ "source": "series_spring", "bound_j": "0.10" }],

```

admissible cutoff delay from 50.5 ms to 45.5 ms.

Verbs: `shield.budget` (build and admit a budget for a design and a boundary), `shield.check` (replay a run
against a budget). Gate: GATE-SHIELD-01. Kill gate: KILL-ENERGY.

### 22.12 Runtime shields (`frob-shield`) [F]

**What a shield is.** An `ActionFilter`: a stage between the policy or the baseline and the actuator limiter,
which replaces the proposed command u_π by the nearest command that keeps a set of barrier rows satisfied. It is
a runtime filter. Its results are class `Tested`, typed as a claim product, and nothing converts them into a
proof (INV-SHIELD-02).

**22.12.1 The filter.** Minimize ‖u − u_π‖² in a metric R, subject to A(x̂)·u ≥ b(x̂) + δ and lo ≤ u ≤ hi. Each
row is the condition ḣᵢ + α(hᵢ) ≥ 0 for a barrier function hᵢ ≥ 0 on a reduced-order model (22.12.4), written
linearly in u. The margin δ has three declared parts: an inter-sample term L·Δt, where L bounds the time
derivative of the row's left side over a control period; an estimation-error term; and the measured
template-to-full term of 22.12.4. Two rules of formulation:

- *Quantifier order.* There exists one command, chosen from delivered information, that satisfies the rows for
  every admitted disturbance and estimation error. The reverse order lets the command depend on the
  disturbance, which no controller can do.
- *Split cones.* Contact forces the robot commands are limited to the inner cone K(μ_min). Forces the
  environment may apply range over the outer cone K(μ_max). Taking the infimum over the inner cone for forces
  the environment chooses is optimistic.

**22.12.2 The projection.**

- *One row, diagonal metric: exact.* u(λ) = clip(u_π + λ·R⁻¹·a, lo, hi), and aᵀu(λ) is nondecreasing and
  piecewise linear in λ with at most 2n breakpoints. Sort them and take the smallest λ ≥ 0 with
  aᵀu(λ) ≥ b + δ (PO-SHIELD-02). If the maximum of aᵀu over the box is below b + δ, the row cannot be met within
  the actuators and the backup runs.
- *Counterexample to the closed form that ignores the box.* R = I, u_π = (1, 0), box [0, 1]², row
  u₁ + u₂ ≥ 1.5. The unboxed projection is (1.25, 0.25), which leaves the box. Clipping it gives (1, 0.25),
  which violates the row. The breakpoint solution is λ = 0.5 and u = (1, 0.5).
- *Two to four rows.* A fixed number of iterations of the shared solver, then a mandatory optimality check:
  primal feasibility of rows and box, nonnegative multipliers, complementarity and stationarity, each within a
  stated tolerance. If any part fails, or the rows conflict, the step invokes the backup controller atomically.
  A partially converged iterate never reaches a driver: only a `Checked<Command>` can be passed on
  (PO-SHIELD-03, INV-SHIELD-01).
- *More than four coupled rows* are refused when the shield is fitted (FROB-REF-454).

**22.12.3 Backup controllers and monitors.** Backups live in `frob-control`, each with a declared domain.

| Class | Backup | Property |
|---|---|---|
| Wheeled, tracked | Damping injection on the drive outputs, then brakes where the card has them | With collocated outputs y and K_d ⪰ 0, the command u = −K_d·y gives dH/dt ≤ −yᵀ·K_d·y ≤ 0 in the port model of C-12: stored energy never increases (PO-SHIELD-04). Holding on a slope needs the brake's holding torque, and the domain says so. |
| Arm | Posture hold by energy shaping, with damping | Passive with respect to the shaped energy, for a fixed base |
| Legged | Widen the stance, lower the body, freeze | `Tested` only; no passivity claim once contacts switch |

Monitors watch the assumptions: a friction estimate outside [μ_min, μ_max], innovation spikes in the estimator,
unmodeled impact impulses, tracking error above the calibrated margin, actuator saturation. An alarm also
triggers the backup. The violation bound per episode is

    P(violation) ≤ E[N_breach]·δ₁ + E[N_alarm]·δ₂ + δ₃

with δ₁ = P(monitor silent | breach), δ₂ = P(violation | alarm) and δ₃ = P(violation with no breach and no
alarm). Each is estimated with an interval on independent hidden-state labels from the simulator, never from
the monitor's own output. Counterexample to self-labeling: a monitor that is silent on every breach it cannot
see reports δ₁ = 0 from its own log. The bound is not claimed under a shift of the world distribution, because a
shift changes δ₁ itself; shifted worlds are reported as a separate row.

**22.12.4 Reduced-order barrier filters with a measured margin.** Barriers are written on a template of 2 to 12
states: a single integrator or a unicycle for a base (keep-out zones, speed limits, door-pinch zones), a linear
inverted pendulum or a capture point for a legged body. A tracking controller realizes the template's filtered
command on the full robot. The template's guarantee reaches the robot only through the tracking error ε between
commanded and realized template state, and ε depends on inputs, disturbances and the full state, so it cannot
be computed in advance (FACT, Appendix H). It is therefore measured, and labeled as measured:

- *Calibration.* n episodes on a fast tier across the calibrated family. The score of an episode is its largest
  tracking error.
- *Margin.* ε̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)·(1 − α)⌉-th smallest score. For an episode exchangeable with the calibration set,
  the error is at most ε̂ with probability at least 1 − α. The barriers are tightened by ε̂.
- *Scope.* The margin is keyed by design class, controller or policy identity, task family and tier. A new
  policy, or a design outside the class, has no margin, and the shield refuses to run on a borrowed one
  (FROB-REF-457).
- *Drift.* A running test on the conformal p-values raises an alarm when exchangeability fails. The regime ends
  and calibration restarts inside the new one.
- *Tier.* A sample of calibration episodes is replayed on the referee, and the tier difference from the ledger
  of §16 is added to ε̂.

FACT (Appendix H): reduced-order barrier filters run on real legged and aerial hardware under a tracking
assumption, and their published hardware results say constraints are "rarely violated". They are strongest for
wheeled and tracked bases and for slow legged motion. No full-order certificate is attempted here:
sum-of-squares methods reach about five to ten states; a diagonally dominant relaxation reached 30 states only
for balancing with pinned feet and no input limits; and FrankenSim's `certify_roa` uses a fixed quadratic
Lyapunov function with a largest example of two states (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-sos/src/roa.rs`).

**22.12.5 Reachable-set tables for small subsystems [M].** For a subsystem of at most four reduced states with
a bounded sensing error E (a wheel at a threshold, a tray about to tip, braking across two floor materials), a
table over at most 8 modes × 16,384 cells is built by backward viability pruning. A cell K_m keeps a common safe
action only if R_mn((K_m ⊕ E) ∩ G_mn, D) ⊆ K_n ⊖ E for every guard G_mn that may be enabled, where R_mn is the
reset, D the disturbance set, and inter-sample sweep, latency and chatter are accounted for. At run time the
whole belief set is looked up; outside the table the answer is `uncovered` and the backup domain applies. The
reduction of a robot to four states is an assumption that the monitors of 22.12.3 watch. It is not proved. The
table is a payload of the shield record, with no file format of its own. It is off the critical path.

**22.12.6 Shields during training.** The filter is a stage of the environment. The learner's sampled action is
the command before the shield, its likelihood is the likelihood of that command (§23.6), the applied command is
logged beside it, and each intervention is an event that a reward term may price. A shield that intervenes on
more than a declared fraction of ticks sets the flag `shield-dominated`.

**A rule for every adaptive element here** (the margin calibrator, the drift alarm, the alarm thresholds): it
ships with a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback, or it does not
ship.

Verbs: `shield.fit` (rows, margins, backup and monitors for a design, a controller and a task family),
`shield.check`, `shield.report`. TARGETs: at most 5% added step cost; at least 85% of unshielded return kept.
Gates: GATE-SHIELD-02 to -05. Kill gates: KILL-SHIELD, KILL-TUBE.

### 22.13 Symmetric controllers under stabilizer intersection [S]

**Two groups.** Γ_mech is the group of C-09: automorphisms of the design that preserve link lengths,
pseudo-inertias, joint screws with their reflection parity, actuators and gear ratios. Γ_obs ≤ Γ_mech is the
subgroup that also preserves every sensor pose and field of view, the payload, the task's goal, and the
symmetry of terrain and guards. Both are stored on each robot-plus-task pair. A quadruped built symmetric
fore-aft and left-right has Γ_mech = C₂ × C₂; with a front-only lidar its closed loop has Γ_obs = C₂.

**The group a calculation may use** is the intersection of Γ_obs with the stabilizers of what that calculation
holds fixed, computed when it is used:

    Γ_use = Γ_obs ∩ Stab(gravity in the body frame) ∩ Stab(boundary conditions)
                  ∩ Stab(current contact mode) ∩ Stab(commanded twist)

On a side slope the left-right mirror leaves Stab(gravity) and the controller's left and right gains untie. With
an off-centre payload the mirror is not in Γ_obs at all. A reduction requested under a larger group is refused
(FROB-REF-461, INV-REFLEX-04), and the full model always stays executable.

**Rules.**

- *Parity.* Under an orthogonal matrix P a polar vector maps by P and an axial vector by det(P)·P. A mirrored
  leg may need a sign change at its encoder. Reflections are not elements of SE(3); they act on the design term,
  not through a frame transform.
- *Exact reduction against distribution symmetry.* Tying gains or block-diagonalizing a linearization inside one
  world needs that world and its data to be invariant. A symmetry of the distribution of worlds pairs two
  different worlds: it supports augmentation and paired tests (§23.13) and no reduction inside either world.
- *Linear blocks only.* A linearized operator that commutes with the action preserves the isotypic blocks. An
  equivariant nonlinear map need not decouple into independent blocks.
- *Equivariance in law.* Fixture: two identical latches, exactly one must close, and the observation is fixed
  by the mirror that exchanges them. A deterministic equivariant controller must issue matching commands, and
  both matching commands fail. A controller that draws one of the two successful choices uniformly is
  equivariant in law. A tied deterministic controller that fails here has excluded only its own class. The
  controller's manifest states whether pathwise or in-law equivariance is required and who shares the draw.
- *Asymmetric worlds need asymmetric actions.* A quadruped crossing a patch that is slippery on one side must
  be free to act asymmetrically. Terrain is part of the observation, so an equivariant controller is conditioned
  on the transformed terrain; it is not constrained to mirror-symmetric commands.
- *Symmetry at an event.* A symmetry saves work across an impact only if it commutes with the event. For a
  group element g and a transverse event e with saltation Ξ_e the identity is
  D g(x⁺)·Ξ_e(x⁻) = Ξ_(g·e)(g·x⁻)·D g(x⁻). Test: reflect a four-foot landing and exchange the left and right
  sole laws; a one-sided perturbation transformed before or after the impact must predict the same change. The
  identity transports a local derivative to the corresponding event. It does not license block reduction of a
  trajectory that the symmetry does not fix.
- *A change of group at a mode switch.* Γ_use is recomputed when the contact mode changes, so gains tied in one
  mode untie in the next. Nothing here requires the command to be continuous across that change (DESIGN). An exact
  reduction is the full-model calculation carried out under a group that leaves it invariant, so in each mode the
  reduced controller issues the commands the full model issues, and whatever jump the command makes at the switch
  is the jump of the mode-scheduled law itself, the same with the reduction on or off. The check is that
  comparison on both sides of the switch, within the determinism class; a disagreement means a wrong stabilizer
  and is refused (FROB-REF-461). A law that needs a continuous command across modes states that as its own
  requirement; symmetry neither supplies it nor removes it.

Verb: `control.symmetry` returns Γ_mech, Γ_obs, the stabilizers in force and Γ_use, with the generators each
stabilizer removed. Gate: GATE-REFLEX-07.

### 22.20 A lifted linear planning model for one soft-soil regime

**What it is.** [M]. A lifted linear predictor is a finite matrix acting on a dictionary of functions of the
state. As a global model for control through contact it is not built. A contact-rich hybrid flow has no
finite-dimensional invariant subspace of such functions, and size alone settles it: a quadratic dictionary on
60 states has 1 + 60 + 1,830 = 1,891 functions, so one dense map has 1,891² ≈ 3.58 × 10⁶ coefficients before
any mode jump is represented. One bounded experiment is kept, off the critical path. It covers a single regime
with no contact-mode change, a tracked or wheeled base driving on soft soil (§18.10), where the dynamics are
smooth and a rollout of the compiled model is comparatively expensive. There a lifted model is tried as the
*planning model* of the sampling controller of §22.8 and judged at equal tracking accuracy. Owner:
`frob-control`; bands come through the airlock from `fs-surrogate`. Verbs: `control.lift` (fit, calibrate,
report) and `control.mpc --planner lifted:<id>`.

FACT: `fs-surrogate` provides exact DMD, EDMD over a polynomial dictionary with ridge regularization, and
split-conformal forecast bands per horizon with an optional simultaneous adjustment; it states that the band is
its only accuracy statement and holds only inside the calibration distribution
(`frankensim/crates/fs-surrogate/src/koopman.rs`). Its EDMD is autonomous and has no input term. The
input-affine fit below is therefore ours, and a request for it goes upstream (Appendix F).

**Model (DESIGN).** The reduced state x has 7 entries: forward speed, yaw rate, the slip of each track, the
sinkage of each track, and pitch. The inputs are u = (u_L, u_R), the two track torque commands. The dictionary
ψ(x) holds every monomial of total degree at most 2: C(9, 2) = 36 functions. The predictor is bilinear in the
lifted state and the input,

  z⁺ = A·z + u_L·B_L·z + u_R·B_R·z,  z = ψ(x),

fitted by ridge least squares on triples (x_k, u_k, x_{k+1}) from `batch-cpu` rollouts in `f64`. The state is
read back from the linear monomials and lifted again at every step. The three 36 × 36 maps hold 3,888
coefficients, and one planning step is three matrix–vector products, 3,888 multiply–adds.

**Admission.** The model carries its regime as data: the soil card with its declared ranges of pressure, slip
and sinkage (§18.10), a box of speed and yaw rate, a slope range, and the condition that every track sample
stays in contact. A simultaneous 95% forecast band over the planning horizon is calibrated on trajectories held
out from the fit. A query outside the regime, or a planner tick whose measured state leaves the box, is refused
and that tick is served by `mpc-sample` on the compiled model (refusal: lifted planner outside its calibrated
regime). Results carry `screening: sampling-control` and `surrogate`, are class Estimated, and inherit every
rule of §22.8, including that the controller never stands in for a trained policy as the evaluator of record.

**Known answer and counterexample.**

- *Linear recovery.* Data from x⁺ = 0.9·x with the dictionary {1, x} must return the map diag(1, 0.9) and the
  eigenvalue 0.9 to 10⁻⁹.
- *A mode switch defeats a polynomial lift even when the fit is exact on its data.* Take the reset map
  x⁺ = |x| and the dictionary {1, x, x²}. On the samples x ∈ {−1, 0, 1} the targets are {1, 0, 1}, which x²
  reproduces exactly, so the fitted predictor is x⁺ = x² with zero training residual. At x = 0.5 it predicts
  0.25 where the truth is 0.5. A zero residual on the fitting set is no evidence. Only the held-out band is,
  and only inside one mode.

**Ceiling, stated first.** The lifted model can remove at most the planner's rollouts, N_s·H·N_it model steps
per tick: 4 × 10⁴ to 2 × 10⁵ in the configurations of §22.8, against one plant step. Inside the controller the
ceiling is that ratio. On a design cycle it is the share of wall time that the screening rung of §22.8 spends
on soft-soil tasks in the phase ledger of §39, which is unmeasured and expected to be small. Nothing in M0–M7
depends on this item.

**Pilot before funding.** On `batch-cpu` only: fit and calibrate on the tracked skid-steer of §16.6 over one
soil card, and report band width against horizon. Falsifier: the simultaneous band at a 0.9 s horizon is wider
than the task's tracking tolerance (DESIGN default: 5% of commanded speed and 0.05 m of path offset). Equal
accuracy is then unreachable and the experiment stops.

### 22.21 The pre-slip aliasing fixture and the sampled-data backup domain

This subsection extends §22.12.3 and §22.16. It defines the control named in BET-25, states when a monitored
assumption may count toward the bound of §22.12.3, and gives a two-mode sampled-data fixture whose backup domain is
checked by hand. Tier [S]: a known answer with negative controls. Owner: `frob-shield`, with the fixture registered in
`frob-conformance`. Verbs: `shield.fit` (the rule and its refusal) and `shield.check` (replay of the fixture).

**The fixture.** A cart on a level floor approaches a keep-out line at v₀ = 2.0 m/s with its wheels rolling and no
drag. A brake command asks for a deceleration of 5.0 m/s²; the floor delivers min(5.0, μ·g). There are two worlds:
A with μ = 0.8 (μ·g = 7.848 m/s², so the wheels hold and the cart decelerates at 5.0) and B with μ = 0.3
(μ·g = 2.943 m/s², so the wheels slide). The modes are `stick` and `slip`. The control period is Δt = 0.02 s. The
sensors are wheel speed, body acceleration and range s to the line.

| Quantity | World A | World B |
|---|---|---|
| Stopping distance v₀²/(2·decel) | 4/10 = 0.400 m | 4/5.886 = 0.6796 m |
| Observations before the first braking tick | Identical in both worlds, sample for sample | |

**Why the histories are identical.** Before braking the traction demand is zero, so no contact law reads μ, and the
delivered transcripts of the two worlds are equal. A monitor is a function of the delivered transcript. It therefore
returns the same output in both worlds up to the first braking tick, whatever its design. The fixture asserts
transcript equality as a precondition and is void on any tier where it fails.

**Arm 1: a nominal controller with a slip monitor.** The controller assumes μ ≥ 0.5 and brakes when s ≤ 0.45 m (the
0.40 m of world A plus 0.05 m). A monitor compares wheel speed with body speed and raises an alarm on slip; the
backup is full braking. In world A the cart stops 0.05 m short. In world B the alarm is raised at the first braking
tick and the backup is already running: the cart crosses the line at √(4 − 2·2.943·0.45) = 1.162 m/s and stops
0.6796 − 0.45 = 0.2296 m beyond it. For this stratum P(violation | alarm) = 1, and the bound of §22.12.3 is at least
E[N_alarm], which says nothing. The alarm is correct and too late.

**The rule this fixture enforces.** For an assumption with a monitor and a backup, let t_d be the first tick at which
the delivered transcript of a violating world differs from that of every admitted non-violating world, and let ℓ be
the monitor's latency in ticks. The pair is *timely* if the state at t_d + ℓ lies in the backup's domain for every
violating world of the belief set. An assumption whose monitor is not timely may not be listed as monitored: its
whole range goes inside the quantifier of §22.12.1, so the rows hold for every value in the belief set. `shield.fit`
refuses a shield that lists such an assumption as monitored, and returns the first distinguishing tick, the state
there and the two changes that would admit it. Here they are: brake by s ≤ 0.7196 m, or limit the approach speed to
1.57 m/s, the largest v with v²/5.886 + 0.02·v ≤ 0.45.

**Arm 2: the shield with the belief set inside the quantifier.** Belief set μ ∈ [0.3, 0.8], so μ_min·g = 2.943 m/s².
The backup is full braking, with domain

  D = {(s, v) : s ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g) + v·Δt}.

*Invariance, by hand.* If (s, v) ∈ D at a tick, coasting for one period leaves s − v·Δt ≥ v²/(2·μ_min·g). The filter
permits coasting only inside D and otherwise brakes. Braking therefore begins at a tick whose range is at least
v²/(2·μ_min·g), and the deceleration from then on is at least μ_min·g in either mode (5.0 in `stick`, μ·g ≥ μ_min·g
in `slip`), so the cart stops at or before the line for every μ in the belief set. At v₀ = 2.0 m/s the threshold is
0.6796 + 0.04 = 0.7196 m. The shield has no violation in either world. Its cost is visible: in world A the cart stops
between 0.28 m and 0.32 m short of the line, depending on where the sampling instant falls, where 0.05 m would have
sufficed.

**Negative control: the continuous-time barrier in a sampled loop.** Drop the term v·Δt and test
h = s − v²/(2·μ_min·g) ≥ 0 at each tick. At a tick with s = 0.70 m, h = 0.0204 and the filter permits coasting. At the
next tick s = 0.66 m, braking begins, and in world B the cart stops 0.6796 − 0.66 = 0.0196 m beyond the line. The
overshoot of this mutant is at most v·Δt = 0.04 m. This is the inter-sample term L·Δt of §22.12.1 at its smallest
size.

**What the fixture does not show.** It has one degree of freedom and an exact stopping law. It says nothing about the
tracking margin of §22.12.4 or about legged backups, whose domains are `Tested` only.

**Gate.** GATE-SHIELD-06 (Known answer, M4). On the referee and on the `strict` profile, over 1,000 paired episodes
with v₀ drawn from [1, 3] m/s and friction pairs from [0.3, 0.8]: the two transcripts are bit-equal before the first
braking tick in every pair. Arm 1 violates in the low-friction world in every episode whose stopping distance exceeds
its trigger range, with the overshoot within 1 mm of the closed form. Arm 2 has no violation in 10⁴ episodes. The
mutant without the inter-sample term produces at least one violation, none larger than v₀·Δt. `shield.fit` refuses
Arm 1's monitored-assumption listing and prints both admitting changes.

### 22.22 The sampling controller as the delivered controller

**The delivered-controller lane [F].** This amends the rules of §22.8 and ADR-REFLEX-03. The rule "never the
evaluator of record" guards one substitution: scoring a design under a sampling controller and reporting the score
as if a trained policy had earned it. It does not cover the case where the sampling controller is itself what the
mission delivers. An accepted answer is a design, a controller and an evidence package (§2.1). When the controller
in that package is of kind `mpc-sample`, episodes run under it are the only evidence about it, and a refusal keyed
on the controller kind would leave the package with no evidence at all.

DESIGN: a task or mission may declare `controller.delivered = mpc-sample`. The declaration freezes a planner
record, whose digest is part of the `ControllerId`.

```
planner_record  (frob.control.planner/1)                                      DESIGN, fixes intent
  kind            mpc-sample
  budget          samples N_s · horizon steps H · iterations N_it      model steps per tick = N_s·H·N_it
  planning_step   h_p · control rate
  cost_terms      content hash of the class-default or task-declared cost
  planning_model  ModelId rolled out by the planner · source of its parameters (nominal | estimated | plant)
  state_source    estimated (the filter of §22.3) | privileged
  compute_target  named computer · measured planning time per tick: median, 99th percentile · deadline
```

Episodes under a frozen planner record are admitted as the scoring source of acceptance, promotion and
qualification **for that record only**, when four conditions hold. Otherwise the refusal of §22.8 stands.

1. *Not model-privileged.* The planning model's parameters do not come from the plant's randomization draw, and
   `state_source = estimated`. An episode flagged `model-privileged` is refused as acceptance evidence.
2. *A plant the planner does not own.* The plant runs on the tier the battery names and is promoted to the referee
   by the rule of §16.8, as for any controller. The planner keeps its own model and its own planning step.
3. *Measured latency.* `compute_target` carries a measurement. The planner's output is delayed by the measured
   99th-percentile planning time, rounded up to whole control periods, and a tick that misses its deadline holds
   the previous plan. A record with a modeled or zero delay is refused for acceptance.
4. *The same bar.* The battery, the requirement verdicts and the failure bound are those of any other controller,
   and the cost is charged to the phase ledger of §39 in real GPU-seconds and CPU-seconds.

The verdict names what was accepted: `accepted with delivered controller mpc-sample@<record digest>`. It says
nothing about a trained policy for the same design, and a later change of controller voids it under §28.13.

*Why condition 1 is not a formality.* A cart at 2 m/s must stop before a wall on a floor whose friction is drawn
from {0.2, 0.8}; stopping distance is v²/(2·μ·g). A planner that reads the plant's draw brakes at 1.019 m on the
slippery floor and 0.255 m on the grippy one and never fails. The same planner holding the nominal μ = 0.5 begins
braking 0.408 m from the wall and overruns by 0.612 m (1.0194 − 0.4077) on every slippery world, half of the draws,
unless its estimator detects the floor in time. The privileged run reports zero failures in 299 episodes for a
controller that fails every second episode.

*Break-even, stated before any test.* FACT arithmetic on the figures of §22.8. A 1% failure bound at 95% confidence
needs 299 failure-free episodes; training once costs about 600 GPU-seconds for the quadruped and 3,600 for the
humanoid. The lane is cheaper than training only below 600/299 = 2.0 GPU-seconds per 20 s episode for a quadruped
and 3,600/299 = 12.0 for a humanoid. The published settings cost about 20 and 62 GPU-seconds per episode: 10 and 5
times over. At the throughput targets of §36 (2 M and 0.5 M world-steps/s) the break-even budgets are 4.0 × 10⁶ and
6.0 × 10⁶ model steps per episode, which at 50 Hz is 4,000 and 6,000 model steps per tick. One setting inside the
quadruped budget is N_s = 64, H = 16 at a 20 ms planning step and N_it = 3 (3,072 steps per tick), against 65,536
for the published quadruped setting. HYPOTHESIS: a planner that small completes the reference tasks for some
classes. FACT (Appendix H): controllers with about 30 samples run a real quadruped from a CPU; the published
successes use hand-built swing-foot or stepping references; success in one study fell from 10 of 10 to 6 of 10 and
0 of 10 when the spline family changed; nothing was found for tracked robots or for a varied design corpus.
Ceiling: no speed benefit at any published setting. The benefit is a capability: a design whose deliverable
controller is a planner can be accepted at all, with its price on the ledger.

*Owner, verbs and refusal.* `frob-control` owns the planner record and the latency model; `frob-arena` admits the
episodes. `control.mpc --freeze` writes the record and `eval.qualify` consumes it. An acceptance request whose
scoring episodes are `mpc-sample` without a frozen, non-privileged, latency-measured planner record is refused,
under the existing refusal of §22.8, with the missing condition named.

### 23.1 What it owns

Cortex owns the vectorized environment and its semantics, the learning algorithms and their correctness rules,
the networks, policy checkpoints with their manifests, distillation, family policies, inheritance along design
edits, and the detectors that keep a learned result from resting on a simulator artifact (GOAL-14, GOAL-16).

|---|---|---|
| `frob-learn` | 4 | `VecEnv`, tensor views, rollout storage, PPO and the other algorithms, networks, on-device learning kernels' host side, the FrankenTorch bridge, checkpoints, distillation, family policies, inheritance, exploitation detectors |

Task terms are written in `.ftask` and compiled by `frob-spec` (§24) into a `TaskProgram` of kernel stages,

**What learning is for here.** Training is the dominant cost of published design loops (FACT, Appendix H). The
plan's lever is to train fewer designs: screen with Reflex, reuse with family policies and inheritance, and spend
specialist training on finalists. Engine throughput is a parity requirement: an infinitely fast environment step
speeds training by at most about 7.7 times on one incumbent stack and about 1.4 times on another (FACT,
Appendix H).

### 23.3 The environment API and its semantics [S]

`VecEnv` exposes `reset(mask)`, `step(actions)`, `snapshot(worlds)` and `restore(snapshot)` over device-resident
buffers. Host code receives views; nothing is copied unless asked. `snapshot` and `restore` are what make replay
and teleport possible (§29). The semantics below replace the common convention of masked automatic reset with
post-reset observations, which cannot support a correct bootstrap.

```rust
/// DESIGN sketch. The vectorized environment. Buffers stay on the device. Explicit reset is the default.
pub trait VecEnv {
    fn spec(&self) -> &TaskProgram;
    fn reset(&mut self, mask: &WorldMask, seeds: &SeedPlan, cx: &Cx<'_>) -> Result<ResetView<'_>, Refusal>;
    fn step(&mut self, actions: ActionView<'_>, cx: &Cx<'_>) -> Result<StepView<'_>, Refusal>;
    fn snapshot(&self, worlds: &[WorldId]) -> Result<Snapshot, Refusal>;
    fn restore(&mut self, snapshot: &Snapshot) -> Result<(), Refusal>;
}

pub struct StepView<'a> {
    pub obs: TensorView<'a>,             // after the transition; for an ended world, its final observation
    pub reward: TensorView<'a>,          // integrated over the control interval
    pub dt_sim: TensorView<'a>,          // simulated duration of the interval, per world
    pub terminated: MaskView<'a>,        // success, failure, or an intrinsic mission deadline
    pub truncated: MaskView<'a>,         // an external collection limit on a continuing task
    pub invalid: MaskView<'a>,           // numerical failure: a study error, not a transition
    pub needs_reset: MaskView<'a>,       // terminated | truncated | invalid
    pub applied_action: TensorView<'a>,  // after the limiter and any shield
    pub final_obs: Option<TensorView<'a>>, // masked-autoreset mode only; `obs` then holds post-reset values
    pub info: InfoView<'a>,              // sample ages, validity masks, control layer, shield events
}
```

Rules, each with a fixture in GATE-CORTEX-06:

- **E-1 Explicit reset.** A world with `needs_reset` set stays ended until the caller resets it. Stepping it is
  refused (FROB-REF-521).
- **E-2 The final observation is preserved.** In explicit mode the `obs` of an ended world is its final
  observation. In the labeled mode `autoreset: masked` the world is reset inside the step, `final_obs` carries
  the final observation and `obs` the post-reset one. The mode is in the run manifest and in every result
  (INV-CORTEX-01).
- **E-3 Termination and truncation are different facts.** Termination is success, failure or an intrinsic
  mission deadline. Truncation is an external collection limit on a task that would have continued. If both are
  set, termination controls the bootstrap.
- **E-4 Invalid physics is a study error.** A world that is `Faulted`, or `Degraded` beyond the task's policy
  (§16), sets `invalid`. It has weight zero in policy updates and is kept, with its trace, in benchmark and
  failure counts. A fall is a valid transition.
- **E-5 Actions are checked before state changes.** A non-finite action is refused before any state is mutated
  (FROB-REF-522). For bounded actions the out-of-range rule is declared, and proposed and applied values are
  both logged.
- **E-6 Spaces are typed.** Each channel has a shape, a dtype, a channel id, a unit, a frame, a representation,
  a validity mask and a sample age from the clock contract of §20. Normalization is versioned and invertible,
  and clipping is disclosed.
- **E-7 Identity and order.** Worlds have logical ids and per-world seeds. A batch split across two devices
  returns in the caller's order. Recurrent hidden state is reset only for worlds that ended, and it is part of
  every checkpoint and snapshot.
- **E-8 Access classes.** Every observation channel is `actor`, `critic-only` or `evaluation-only`. The actor
  schema is frozen and is run with the other channels absent; a read outside it is refused (FROB-REF-524).
- **E-9 Causality.** Histories with identical delivered observations, past actions and policy random draws up to
  a decision instant yield the same command. The test perturbs future terrain, hidden friction and measurements
  not yet delivered, one at a time.

**Bootstrap targets.** For a transition with reward rₜ, next observation o′ₜ (the final observation if the world
ended) and value function V:

    δₜ = rₜ + γₜ·(1 − terminatedₜ)·V(o′ₜ) − V(oₜ)
    Aₜ = δₜ + γₜ·λₜ·(1 − needs_resetₜ)·Aₜ₊₁

The value bootstrap is cut by termination only. The advantage trace is cut at every reset. Known answer: a true
8 s deadline with terminal reward 0 gives a bootstrap target of 0; an external cut at the same instant on a
continuing task with V = 10 and γ = 0.99 gives 9.9. A single `done` flag cannot produce both. A fully observed
finite-horizon task gives the actor the time remaining, or declares the clock hidden.

**Physical discounting.** γ(Δt) = exp(−β·Δt) with β in 1/s declared by the task, and trace decay
λ(Δt) = exp(−κ·Δt). The control interval may then differ between designs, tasks or rungs without changing what is
optimized. With β = 0.5025 /s, γ is 0.99 at 20 ms and 0.99499 at 10 ms. A constant reward rate r is then worth
r·Δt/(1 − e^(−β·Δt)): 2.000·r at 20 ms and 1.995·r at 10 ms, both near r/β = 1.990·r. With one per-step γ = 0.99
at both rates the same behaviour is worth 2.0·r and 1.0·r.

**Time-aware rewards.** A running term is a rate, integrated over simulated time across the physics substeps of
the interval. An event term is counted once per event identity. A terminal term is counted once. Counterexample:
a penalty of 1 per control step over a 10 s episode totals 500 at 50 Hz and 1,000 at 100 Hz for the same motion.
Evaluation metrics stay in physical units (§24), so a change of reward weights does not change what success
means.

**Two units of throughput.** An environment step is one control interval; a world-step is one physics step. At
ten physics steps per interval, 400 k environment steps/s is 4.0 M world-steps/s. Every figure in this chapter
and in §36 names its unit.

### 23.4 Device-resident tensor views [S]

A `TensorView` is a typed, strided view into a device buffer that rollout and learning kernels share. It carries
the device, the allocation id, the dtype, the extents, a logical origin and signed strides in elements, an
ownership epoch and the intended access. Rollout storage (observations, sampled and applied actions,
log-probabilities, values, rewards, flags, final observations) stays on the device for the whole collection.
Observations never leave it during a rollout.

- **One stride convention, converted at one boundary.** FACT (Appendix H; re-run at admission, §33): the pinned
  FrankenNumPy uses signed byte strides and the pinned FrankenTorch uses unsigned element strides. A contiguous
  2 × 2 `f32` view has strides (8, 4) in the first and (2, 1) in the second. Copying the numbers verbatim into a
  view on a 16-element buffer reads element 12 at index [1, 1] where element 3 belongs, and both reads are in
  bounds. A view with byte origin 4 and strides (8, −4) indexes elements (1, 0, 3, 2) and has no unsigned form.
- **Unrepresentable views.** A view the receiver cannot represent is materialized, with the copy charged to the
  receipt, or it is refused under a zero-copy contract (FROB-REF-547).
- **Leases.** A host read of a device buffer is valid only after the command buffer of its epoch has completed.
  A view carries its epoch, and a read of a stale epoch is refused. Diagnostics read double-buffered ring slots,
  so they never wait on the step.
- **Masks apply everywhere.** Padding worlds and padded degrees of freedom (§17) are masked in kernels, rewards
  and gradient accumulation. A padded element never contributes to a mean (INV-CORTEX-07).

### 23.5 Declarative task specifications with requirement terms [S]

A task is data with a `TaskId`: observation terms (each with unit, noise, delay, and the representation under
which it transforms, C-09), action terms (joint position targets through the driver loop by default; torque or
velocity as alternatives), reward terms with units and explicit normalization, termination conditions,
randomization and curriculum. Declaring the representation of each term is what lets the framework build
equivariant networks, generate mirrored data, and test declared invariances mechanically. The format is `.ftask`
(§24). What Cortex requires of each kind of term (DESIGN):

| Term | Required beyond the above | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | Access class; rate as a rational period; sample age exposed | E-6, E-8 |
| Action | Bounds; the registered action law; the entry in the mapping table of §23.7 | P-3 |
| Reward | Kind: `rate` (per second), `event` or `terminal` | Time-aware integration |
| Termination, truncation | Which conditions belong to the mission and which are collection limits | E-3 |
| Requirement | A predicate ψ(x) ≥ 0 with a unit and a scale s; a temporal operator over a bounded window | Below |
| Randomization | The card field each range comes from | §23.15 |
| Curriculum | A schedule over training distributions only | Evaluation episodes are fixed and never chosen by a curriculum |
| Reset | A settled-pose cache of precomputed quasi-static poses (TARGET: 64 per design, penetration at most 0.5 mm) | No spawn impulse in the first transitions |

**Requirement terms.** A requirement is the acceptance metric of a mission (§24) and may also shape training.

1. *Unit-normalized margins.* Each predicate declares a scale, and its robustness is ρ = ψ/s, a pure number,
   before any minimum or maximum combines predicates. Counterexample: the minimum of a clearance of 0.05 m and a
   thermal margin of 12 °C is 0.05 in metres and 12 if the clearance is written as 50 mm. With scales of 0.10 m
   and 10 °C the margins are 0.5 and 1.2, and the clearance binds in every unit system.
2. *Past-time online, future-time offline.* "Historically", "once" and "since" over a bounded past window
   compile to streaming kernels with a monotonic deque, and may be used as a reward or a monitor. "Always",
   "eventually" and "until" over a future window give a verdict only on a completed trace. A future-time term
   offered as an online reward is refused (FROB-REF-554), never truncated: a 600 s window at 50 Hz is 30,000
   samples.
3. *Requirements do not replace rewards.* Bounded-time requirements are the acceptance metric and a
   falsification score; training keeps its reward terms (FACT, Appendix H). A policy is accepted on the
   requirement verdict, whatever its return.

### 23.6 Algorithms and the PPO rules

- PPO with generalized advantage estimation, value normalization and an adaptive step size [S].
- SAC, and teacher-student distillation from privileged to proprioceptive observations [F].
- Behaviour cloning from the baseline controller as a warm start [F]: Reflex supplies demonstrations for free.
- TD3 and evolution strategies [F]; the latter use FrankenSim's derivative-free optimizers through the airlock
  for small policies and for the gains of rung R1 (§22.7).

Verbs: `train.start` (algorithm, network, devices, tying and profile are arguments), `train.status`,
`train.stop`, `train.export`. The environment has no verb of its own; `train.*`, `eval.*` and `sim.run` reach it.

**The PPO rules.** Each is a rule with a test. Together they are the admission battery of GATE-CORTEX-07 for any
PPO implementation.

| Rule | Statement | Test |
|---|---|---|
| P-1 Two masks | The value bootstrap is cut by termination only; the advantage trace is cut at every reset (§23.3). | The 0 and 9.9 fixture; both flags set gives 0 |
| P-2 Frozen behaviour | Policy weights and the observation normalizer do not change during collection. | An update attempted mid-collection is refused; two collections from one snapshot are identical |
| P-3 Same random variable | The likelihood ratio is computed for the random variable that was sampled. Either the sampled latent command is stored with its density and a deterministic limiter follows (`latent-then-limit`), or the policy is defined on the applied clipped action and has point masses at the bounds (`clipped-with-endpoint-mass`). | Unit-variance Gaussians clipped at +1 with means 0 and 1: the endpoint ratio is 0.5/0.158655 = 3.15149; ordinary densities at +1 give e^0.5 = 1.64872. Each law returns its own number; a mixture is refused (FROB-REF-527) |
| P-4 Clipped surrogate, both signs | With L = min(ρ·A, clip(ρ, 1 − ε, 1 + ε)·A), the unclipped branch is active for A ≥ 0 when ρ ≤ 1 + ε, and for A < 0 when ρ ≥ 1 − ε. | Finite differences of the loss at ρ = 0.5, 1 and 2 with ε = 0.2, for both signs of A and at both clip boundaries |
| P-5 Critic gradient from the current output | The value loss differentiates the current forward value, not the value stored at collection. | Critic output 2 with stored value and return 0: the gradient of c_V·(V − R)² into the value head is 4·c_V, not 0 |
| P-6 Episode boundaries in sequences | A sequence model evaluates each transition under its own episode history. Packed episodes do not see across a reset, and a minibatch that starts mid-episode carries the right position and prior history. | Two independently reset one-step episodes packed together: a frozen policy's ratio is 1 on both |
| P-7 Physical discount | γ and λ come from β, κ and the interval's simulated duration. | The 2.000 and 1.995 fixture across control rates |
| P-8 Time in the input | A finite-horizon task gives the actor the time remaining or declares the clock hidden. | With the clock, the value at the deadline equals the terminal reward; without it the result is labeled |
| P-9 Valid samples only | Invalid worlds and padded elements have weight zero in every loss and statistic. | A planted `Faulted` world changes no gradient and appears in the failure count |
| P-10 Global, versioned, invertible normalization | Advantage and value normalization use statistics over all valid samples of the update (§23.9); the normalizer is stored with the checkpoint. | The normalizer round-trips; the shard fixtures of §23.9 |
| P-11 Entropy follows the action law | Entropy and multi-dimensional saturation use the registered law of P-3. | The entropy of the clipped law at a bound matches a quadrature reference and differs from the Gaussian's |

FACT (read at FrankenSim commit `483d09c682f2`, `crates/fs-g1-train/src/ppo.rs`; the numbers come from
executable fixtures and are re-run at admission, §33). The suite's only PPO violates P-4, P-5 and P-6:

1. For a negative advantage its branch test is reversed: it follows the ratio below 0.8 and clips it above 1.2.
   At ratios 0.5, 1 and 2, finite differences of the fixture loss in log σ give 0, −0.5 and −1; the code gives
   −0.25, −0.5 and 0.
2. Its value gradient uses the value stored at collection. For a critic output of 2 with stored value and
   return 0, the gradient into the shared critic bias is 0 where the squared-error objective gives 2.
3. It evaluates a concatenated trajectory as one sequence. Two independently reset one-step episodes give the
   frozen policy a second likelihood ratio of 1.582459, where replaying each episode gives 1.

It also drives its advantage recursion with a single `done` flag, which P-1 forbids, and its action sampler has
correct marginals and a wrong joint law, which rule P-12 forbids (§23.25). These are admission tests and upstream
asks (§33, Appendix F), and the reason for ADR-CORTEX-03.

gradient clipping, a passing end-to-end MLP-with-Adam training test, safetensors and a limited ONNX export; it
has no PPO and no `vmap`. FACT about the suite's only PPO: `frankensim/crates/fs-g1-train` hand-rolls PPO with
manual backpropagation and depends only on `libm`; its exported checkpoint has an all-zero policy head, and its
bead says it "proves model/training/export plumbing, not learned humanoid locomotion".

DESIGN:

- **On device**, the policy forward pass, the backward pass and the optimizer are kernels in the shared dialect
  (matrix products, activations, Adam). Observations never leave the device during a rollout, which is the main
  source of GPU-simulator throughput.
- **FrankenTorch has three jobs**: the CPU trainer for small runs and tests; the gradient oracle against which the
  on-device backward kernels are differentially tested; and policy interchange through safetensors.
- **An upstream ask** (FD-FT-02) requests real device execution in FrankenTorch. If it lands, the on-device
  trainer switches to it under the rule in Appendix F.
- **The lesson from `fs-g1-train` is a gate rule**: a training gate tests learned behaviour against a negative
  control, never plumbing (GATE-CORTEX-01).
- **Which machine.** The two RTX 4090 train. The M5 Ultra holds the world bank, large scenes, replay, the viewer
  and large-memory evaluation (§21). Only seeds, weights and receipts cross between them. A policy trained on
  CUDA is evaluated on Metal inside a declared determinism class, and the report names the class (NS-10).
- **Precision.** Networks run in single precision. Half-precision and tensor-core paths are rejected for contact
  (§21) and are not adopted for policies of a few hundred units either: the arithmetic is too small to repay
  the risk.
- **Profiles.** Training may run on a labeled D4 profile, with nondeterministic reductions in the learner only
  and never in physics. A D4 result is re-evaluated on a D2 or D3 profile before it is cited; citing it earlier
  is refused (FROB-REF-867).
- **The learner is a parity item too.** FACT (Appendix H): on one incumbent stack the learner is about 13% of
  training wall-clock. Its share on ours is recorded in the phase ledger of §39.

### 23.9 Multi-device PPO: the normalization rule [S]

Each device is one shard that holds complete worlds, replicated immutable packs and frozen behaviour weights.

1. Each shard returns sums, not means: the sum of per-sample loss gradients and its count of valid samples.
2. Sums are reduced across shards in a fixed order and divided by the global valid count. An empty shard
   contributes zero and still takes part. If the global count is zero the update is skipped.
3. Advantage and observation statistics are merged from per-shard triples (n, mean μ, sum of squared deviations
   S) by the exact pairwise formula: n = n_a + n_b, d = μ_b − μ_a, μ = μ_a + d·n_b/n,
   S = S_a + S_b + d²·n_a·n_b/n. No shard normalizes with its own statistics.
4. Weights are published only after a complete update. Minibatch order is frozen for equivalence checks. An
   asynchronous variant needs a stated bound on version lag and is [F].

Known answers. One sample with gradient 1 and three with gradient 0 give 0.25 on one device and on two shards of
sizes 1 and 3; averaging the shard means gives 0.5. Shards {0, 0} and {4} have global mean 4/3 and variance
32/9; each shard's own variance is 0.

Determinism: with a fixed shard layout and reduction order the update is class D2 per backend. Across layouts it is
D3, with the envelope recorded (PO-CORTEX-03). Scaling TARGET: at least 1.25 times (tolerance; the ambition is 1.6,
§36.3 row 5) from one RTX 4090 to two at constant global workload and equal task quality, measured also at constant
load per device. Below that, further sharding is not funded, and the second card runs independent seeds or a second
design.

### 23.10 Distillation [F]

One mechanism, four teachers. The student is trained by supervised learning on states the student itself
visits, relabeled by the teacher (dataset aggregation).

| Teacher | What it gives | Cost and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| The baseline controller (§22.4) | A warm start for any class that has a baseline | Seconds. The student inherits the baseline's limits. |
| The sampling controller (§22.8) | Demonstrations for a class that has a cost and no shaped reward; pooled seeds for a family prior | About 67 GPU-minutes per 4,000 simulated seconds at the cheapest configuration and the throughput target. The teacher is `model-privileged`. |
| Specialists | A family policy distilled from many specialists | The specialists must exist; it pays when they were trained anyway for finalists |
| A privileged teacher | A proprioceptive student from a teacher that reads `critic-only` channels | The student's schema is run with those channels absent |

Rules: the student is evaluated on its own rollouts with its own observation schema, and teacher states are never
used at evaluation. The distillation gap is reported as the student's share of the teacher's return, with the
requirement verdicts of both. A student trained on shuffled teacher labels must fail the threshold. A distilled
policy's manifest lists its teachers, and it takes the weakest standing among them.

Verb: `train.distill`. Gate: GATE-CORTEX-14. Kill gate: KILL-DISTILL.

### 23.11 Family policies per morphology class [F]

**What it is.** One set of weights per morphology class (quadruped-like legged, multi-legged, biped and
humanoid, wheeled, tracked, wheel-leg hybrid), conditioned on the design. It is not one universal policy.

**Architecture (DESIGN).** Tokens come from the typed design term, one per joint and one per contact site.

- Static features: the screw basis; the joint placement as log(base_from_joint); scaled subtree inertia; an
  actuator-card summary; tree depth and parent index; the symmetry-orbit label.
- Dynamic features: the unit-complex joint angle; velocity; the last action; the contact flag.
- A shared per-token encoder, a plain transformer over the tokens with a tree-distance bias, masked pooling and
  a per-token decoder, with 1 to 5 million parameters. Padding tokens are masked (§17).
- Canonical inputs. Frames are heading-normalized, and the design term is in the canonical form of §14, so two
  descriptions of one robot give one input. Training also augments over equivalent descriptions (axis
  conventions, joint order). FACT (Appendix H): a published multi-embodiment policy kept 3.6% of its return on
  equivalently re-described robots, and 80.6% when trained across conventions.

**Pretraining distribution.** Per class, designs are drawn from the grammar of §14: the class templates;
parameter perturbations of them (limb lengths and masses within ±20 to ±30%, actuator cards within one family,
payloads within the task range); and single-limb edits as a separate, labeled stratum. Three held-out sets are
fixed before training: parametric designs inside the box, single-limb edits, and out-of-class designs that the
policy must decline. Membership is decided by a stated rule on the parameter box and the topology signature.
Outside it the policy is refused (FROB-REF-534) and the fallback of §22.8 applies.

**The compute envelope question.** FACT (Appendix H): results at 16 to 100 training embodiments cost about 36 to
60 GPU-hours; the results that reach 85 to 97% of specialists used far more, about 19,200 GPU-hours in one case.
Pretraining at 10⁹ to 10¹⁰ steps costs about 1 to 30 GPU-hours, and at 10¹² steps over a thousand. The machine's
standing job limit is one device for one hour (OPEN-SPARK-2). Pretraining therefore does not start without an
owner-approved envelope (OPEN-CORTEX-1). The fail-closed default is no family policy: the baseline screens, and
finalists get specialists with inheritance.

**The numbers the evidence supports** (FACT, Appendix H; all are simulation returns):

| Setting | Return with no adaptation (zero-shot), as a share of a specialist's | Fine-tuning cost |
|---|---|---|
| Within one class, in a parametric neighbourhood of the trained templates | 70 to 85% | About 2 to 25% of a from-scratch run stopped at matched return (1 to 10% against the fixed budget one source used; 20% in a second) |
| Across topology-diverse designs at affordable pretraining | 10 to 40% | A gain of 2 to 3 times at best, and none on flat terrain |
| Outside the training distribution | Fails outright | Not applicable |

Every figure in the first row is for flat or mildly varied ground with fixed actuation. No source measures a class
prior on stairs, slopes or wet floors, with payload or with actuator-card changes; the held-out evaluation of
GATE-CORTEX-11 therefore runs on the task family's interior terrain set.

**When it pays (DESIGN arithmetic on those FACTs).** With pretraining cost P, specialist cost s per design and
fine-tuning fraction f, the family policy pays after N > P/(s·(1 − f)) designs. At P = 60 GPU-hours and f = 0.1 that
is about 590 quadruped designs (s = 410 s) and about 70 to 130 humanoid designs (s = 0.5 to 1 GPU-hour). The ceiling
on the training phase is 1/f, between 4 and 50 times for f between 0.25 and 0.02, and on the whole cycle it is
whatever share training has in the phase ledger of §39.

**The rule for using it as an evaluation rung.** A family policy's score, with no adaptation or after a brief
fine-tune, may rank designs only after its rank agreement with specialists has been measured for that class and
task family (INV-CORTEX-06). This is the decisive early experiment of the program:

- A calibration set of 40 designs in one class. Each is scored three ways: by the family policy with no
  adaptation on the fast tier; after a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps; and by a specialist on the referee.
- Reported: Kendall τ, top-10 recall and bootstrap intervals, and the per-episode correlation across tiers on
  256 paired episodes per design.
- Consequences, fixed in advance. A lower 95% bound on τ of at least 0.7 near the top of the ranking, together with
  the winner check of §23.28, lets the rung select finalists. Between 0.5 and 0.7 it discards only, and at least the
  top quarter goes up to the fine-tune rung. Below 0.5 it is not a rung (FROB-REF-537). Allocation between rungs is
  the ladder rule of §28.
- What is known. FACT (Appendix H): no published rank agreement between a shared policy and specialists was
  found. The nearest figure is a Spearman correlation of 0.40 overall, and 0.50, 0.60 and 0.83 on three robots,
  between a critic and rollouts of the same shared policy. Weight sharing in architecture search gave Kendall τ
  of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three benchmarks.
- Bias, not only noise. FACT (Appendix H): shared controllers pull a search toward designs that are easy to
  steer, and early evaluation favours fast learners. Rank agreement is therefore reported per region of design
  space, and the audit lane of §28 samples what the rung discarded and plants a known winner.

Verbs: `train.family` (pretrain, after the envelope check), `policy.apply` (a design, with or without
fine-tuning; refused out of distribution). Gates: GATE-CORTEX-11, -12. Kill gate: KILL-FAMILY.

### 23.12 Inheritance along design edits [F]

**What it is.** A child design's policy starts from its parent's, through the typed edit that joins them (§14).

| Edit | Effect on the policy | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Parameter edits: set a parameter, regear, reshape, swap an actuator within a family | Token features change for a family policy; a specialist's channels are unchanged | The default once its gate has passed for the edit class. FACT (Appendix H): about 2 to 25% of an early-stopped from-scratch run for transfer from a prior to a similar body; about 50% in the one parent-to-child study; no figure for a small typed edit |
| Single-limb topology edits: add or remove a limb, mirror a pair, swap a wheel for a foot | Tokens are added or dropped; new specialist channels start at zero; the edit's declared correspondence of frames, actuator roles, sensors, contact roles and phase clocks maps the rest; unmatched recurrent state is reset | An experiment behind GATE-CORTEX-13. FACT (Appendix H): 33 to 100% of from-scratch steps, and no gain on flat terrain |

Rules:

- *Transport by the mapping table.* Twist-like quantities are carried by the adjoint and wrench-like ones by its
  dual. Limbs related by an element of Γ_obs reuse blocks with the sign and frame changes of §22.13. A mapping
  that cannot be built is a refusal, not a guess (FROB-REF-531).
- *Disclosure and a matched arm.* A candidate that inherits weights names its donor in its manifest. A
  comparison of an inherited child with a fresh one is run both ways of §22.7: with a shared controller, and
  with equal-resource retuning.
- *Scheduled resets against loss of plasticity.* FACT (Appendix H): PPO degrades under continual learning, on a
  changing task and, after long training, on a stationary one. A lineage therefore resets when its audit twin falls
  more than 5% below the fresh control, and in any case at depth k (DESIGN default k = 5, OPEN-CORTEX-3), when the
  child is trained from the class prior, or from scratch where there is none. One child in m (default m = 8) is also
  trained fresh as an audit, and its inherited twin must be within 5% of its return.
- *Inheritance biases the search.* FACT (Appendix H): an inherited controller favours bodies near the parent,
  and morphological diversity declines faster under inheritance. A diversity-seeking search (§28) therefore
  counts inherited and fresh evaluations separately and does not compare them on one scale.

Verb: `train.inherit` (child, donor, edit path), which refuses without a mapping table and reports the steps to
the parent's requirement verdict. Gate: GATE-CORTEX-13. Kill gate: KILL-INHERIT.

### 23.13 Equivariance, only where the closed loop has it

**Which group.** Weights are tied only under Γ_obs of §22.13: the subgroup that preserves sensors, payload, task
and terrain symmetry as well as the mechanism. A quadruped with a front-only lidar is tied under C₂, not under
its mechanical C₂ × C₂. Tying under Γ_mech constrains the policy to something false.

**Two mechanisms, two claims.**

- *Exact reduction [F].* An equivariant network whose layers commute with the representations that the task
  terms declare. Hidden layers carry copies of the regular representation, so pointwise nonlinearities commute
  with the group. Linear layers are parameterized by isotypic blocks: scalars for real type, and 2 × 2 blocks
  [[a, −b], [b, a]] for complex type, which the cyclic groups Cₙ with n ≥ 3 have and radial robots without a
  mirror need. Reynolds averaging, W ↦ |Γ|⁻¹·Σ ρ_out(g)·W·ρ_in(g)⁻¹, gives the same space exactly and serves as
  the test oracle; it is only more expensive. Quaternionic-type blocks are not built, because no point group or
  leg-permutation group in scope has them. Wreath products, for limbs that are themselves symmetric, sit behind
  the same gate.
- *Augmentation [S].* Transformed copies of each transition, with a symmetry loss. It needs only a symmetry of
  the distribution of worlds, not invariance of each world.

**In law, not pathwise.** A stochastic policy is equivariant when π(ρ_U(g)·u | ρ_O(g)·o) = π(u | o). At an
observation fixed by g, a deterministic equivariant policy must emit an action fixed by g, which the two-latch
fixture of §22.13 shows can be wrong. The policy class therefore keeps its randomness at fixed points, and the
failure of a tied deterministic policy excludes only that class.

**The experiment for BET-07.** Three arms on the same seeds: an equivariant policy conditioned on the
transformed terrain; an ordinary policy with augmentation; an unaugmented baseline. The arms are ranked
separately on sample efficiency, on compute and on performance outside the training distribution. A one-sided
slippery patch is in the battery, so a policy that cannot act asymmetrically is caught. FACT (Appendix H):
augmentation made a baseline transformer as data-efficient as an equivariant model in one study; in another,
hard constraints were more vulnerable to distribution shift and gains were a few per cent. The expected benefit
is modest, which is why KILL-EQUIV exists.

### 23.14 A geometric-algebra tree policy [M]

**What it is.** Link tokens that carry multivector features (motors from `fs-ga`), layers equivariant to rigid
motions and to relabelling, and message passing along the kinematic tree.

**What the evidence supports.** FACT (Appendix H): invariance to heading and to description conventions is
necessary, and canonical inputs with augmentation recover most of it. No published geometric-algebra policy for
multi-embodiment locomotion was found. Such layers ran 1.4 to 7.5 times slower per epoch than plain ones; a pure
geometric-algebra network failed as a control network through slow convergence; and in one study removing
morphological message passing did no harm. Gains near two times are published only for supervised dynamics
prediction and for imitation.

**The experiment.** The same embodiment set and the same wall-clock budget (not equal parameters), three seeds
per arm. Arm A, the control, is the plain morphology transformer of §23.11 with heading normalization and
convention augmentation. Arm B is A with a geometric-algebra encoder and decoder only. Arm C is full
geometric-algebra tree message passing. Measured: time to a return threshold, return with no adaptation on
held-out designs, and retention under re-description. The ceiling is 0 to 1.3 times on an amortized pretraining
run in reinforcement learning, so it is not a source of the result the ten-fold test measures. It is off the
critical path. Gate: GATE-CORTEX-17. Kill gate: KILL-GATREE.

### 23.15 Randomization from cards [S]

Every randomized parameter traces to an uncertainty on a card (actuator, interface, material, sensor). There are
no free-floating ranges. Objectives may be the mean or a tail measure (conditional value at risk) over the
randomization.

DESIGN additions:

- *Resolved values are stored.* Each episode's drawn values are in its record, so a failure can be replayed and
  attributed (§27).
- *Physical and numerical parameters are separate.* A smaller time step must not silently stiffen a fitted foot
  spring. Randomization touches physical parameters only; numerical ones are varied by the detectors of §23.16.
- *Adaptive randomization [F]* may reweight inside a card's support. A range beyond the card is labeled
  `beyond-card`, and its results cannot be cited for the carded hardware.
- *Held out.* Delay, gains, sensor corruption and layout are randomized through their own cards and the scene
  splits of §19. Complete buildings and material conditions are held out.

### 23.16 Detecting exploitation (GOAL-16), and rank robustness of finalists [S]

Before a policy result is reported it is re-evaluated under perturbations that should not matter to a
physically meaningful behaviour. A collapse outside the envelope sets a flag on the result.

| Perturbation | Flag |
|---|---|
| Half the time step | `dt-sensitive` |
| The next tier up | `tier-sensitive` |
| Friction and stiffness moved within their card uncertainty | `parameter-sensitive` |
| The alternative contact law, including the hard-contact batch law of §18 where it is built | `law-sensitive` |
| Solver tolerance divided by 10, and Newton iterations doubled | `tolerance-sensitive` |
| Contact stiffness halved and doubled | `stiffness-sensitive` |
| Controller rate changed; rotor inertia restored to its card value where a model omitted it | `rate-sensitive` |
| The mirrored policy on a symmetric robot, which must score the same within noise | `mirror-asymmetric` |
| The causal test of E-9 | `leak-sensitive` |
| A per-step work-energy audit from the ledger of §29: ΔE_mech − W_act ≤ ε | `energy-created` |

The first four rows and the mirror test speak for themselves. The others have these reasons. FACT
(Appendix H): in an executed planar study, success fractions of 0.093 and 0.780 became 1.000 and 1.000 with a
1 kHz loop or with added rotor inertia, so controller rate and rotor inertia are pinned before any benchmark.
The energy audit catches penetration recovery that pumps energy into a hopping body. The planted exploit for the
law check is a friction cone linearized as a box, which grants √2·μ along its diagonals, 41% more traction.

**Rank robustness of finalists [S].** A flag on one policy is not enough when designs are compared; what matters
is whether the ranking survives. In each final round the top k designs and the runners-up are re-evaluated under
the model-form variants above (contact law, cone, stiffness, step, tolerance), with the energy audit. The result
is typed:

- `model-robust { variants, top_k_overlap, energy_audit }` when the top-k set, and its order within noise,
  survive every variant; or
- `model-conditional` when a variant flips the ranking. A model-conditional result cannot be promoted to
  `established` (§16) and cannot spend build or prototype budget in §31; an attempt is refused (FROB-REF-830).

TARGET cost: 5 to 10% of a campaign. Demotion rule: if over 20 campaigns the top-8 overlap is at least 7 of 8 in
95% of them with no energy flag, the check becomes a periodic audit.

Verbs: `eval.detect` (the detectors, on one result), `fidelity.modelform` (rank robustness of a finalist set; defined
in §16.9). Gates: GATE-CORTEX-04, -15.

### 23.17 Evaluation protocol [S]

Held-out scenes (§19); at least five seeds; interquartile mean with a stratified bootstrap interval; pairwise
comparisons through the e-process of C-15.

DESIGN additions:

- *One study type per study.* A comparison is declared as an anytime-valid e-process study, or as a fixed-n
  exact study with its reachable verdicts stated. The two are never mixed in one study (§24).
- *Pairing needs determinism.* Paired statistics on common random numbers are refused under an uncontrolled
  determinism class (§35).
- *Matched compute.* Results report time to a target success rate and performance at matched compute. More
  rollouts with an easier sensor model do not establish a win.
- *Estimated state.* Every reported return has its `--state estimated` twin, and the gap is part of the result.
- *Fixed evaluation episodes.* No curriculum, adaptive randomization or search chooses evaluation episodes.
- *Holdout retirement.* A reversal on a sealed holdout is reported against the frozen system. If it then informs
  training or selection, that holdout is retired and a new one is drawn.
- *Acceptance is elsewhere.* A return is not an acceptance. Acceptance is the requirement verdict in the blinded
  qualification lane of §24, on worlds that the discovery side never read.
- *Method gates* use ten paired seeds and a 95% paired lower bound, with competing arms and a potency control.

### 23.18 The Gymnasium-compatible adapter [F]

**What it is.** `bindings/frob-gymnasium`, an optional annex outside the core workspace. It presents the
five-result step contract (`obs, reward, terminated, truncated, info`) and a vector interface to Python learning
stacks. Its purpose is that rival stacks can drive this environment in the ten-fold test (§39), so that
comparison does not depend on our learner. The exclusion of Python from the core stands: the adapter contains no
physics, the native worker is the stepping authority, and the core never links an interpreter (rule L-5).

- *Autoreset is declared.* The adapter states which of the host library's vector autoreset conventions it
  presents, maps it onto `explicit` or `autoreset: masked`, and always supplies the final observation in `info`.
- *Spaces* carry units, bounds, channel ids and sample ages as metadata. Normalization is the versioned one of
  the checkpoint.
- *Transport.* Tensors cross as device-resident views where the host stack shares the device and the stride
  conversion of §23.4 applies. Otherwise one copy per step is made and charged to the receipt (OPEN-CORTEX-4).
- *Parity.* An episode driven through the adapter and the same episode driven natively have equal digests in
  class D2.
- *Provenance.* Results obtained through the adapter record the external learner's identity and versions as
  untrusted provenance. They are admissible to the rival arm of the ten-fold test and not to our method gates.

Gate: GATE-CORTEX-16. Kill gate: KILL-GYMADAPT.

### 23.25 The joint law of sampled actions

**What it is.** [S] A rule for the action sampler, added to the PPO battery of §23.6 as P-12: the joint law of the
sampled action equals the law that the likelihood computation assumes, across the channels of one decision, across
consecutive decisions of one world, and across neighbouring worlds. Marginal checks do not establish it.

**Why it is a rule.** FACT (`frankensim/crates/fs-g1-train/src/ppo.rs:172–189` at `483d09c682f2`; to be re-run at
admission, §33.11): `gaussian_action` draws each channel from `gaussian_sample`, a Box–Muller cosine over a counter
advanced by the constant 0x9E3779B97F4A7C15, with the phase uniform taken from the top 53 bits of the counter times
0x94D049BB133111EB. The phase is therefore a linear function of the counter modulo 1, and consecutive draws differ in
phase by the fixed fraction 0.167245 of a turn, an angle of 0.334490·π. For a uniform angle θ, cos θ and cos(θ + φ)
have the same sign with probability 1 − φ/π, so two adjacent channels agree in sign with probability 0.665510 where
independence gives 0.5. Reported counts in 100,000 two-channel calls per seed are 66,552, 66,552 and 66,551, while
each channel alone is positive half the time. The trainer scores these samples with a product of marginal densities,
so its likelihood ratio is computed for a random variable that was not the one sampled, which is the failure rule
P-3 exists to prevent.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN:
- Every noise draw is a Philox output keyed by `(WorldAddr, decision index, stream id, channel)` (§35.9). A
  Box–Muller pair consumes two uniforms from distinct counters, and its second output is used or discarded by a
  declared rule. No draw is a function of a neighbouring draw's counter.
- The sampler has a manifest: the declared joint law (a product Gaussian, or a full covariance when the policy has
  one), the transform and bounds of P-3, and the stream layout.
- `frob-conformance` qualifies a sampler against its manifest with four statistics over N decisions: the
  sign-agreement frequency of every pair of channels; the same for one channel at consecutive decisions; the same
  for one channel in adjacent worlds; and the empirical covariance of the standardized noise. Under a product law
  every agreement frequency has mean ½ and standard deviation ½/√N.
- `train.start` refuses a sampler that has no qualification receipt for the pinned kernel (refusal: action sampler
  not qualified against its likelihood).

Owner: `frob-learn`; kernels in `frob-kernels`; fixtures in `frob-conformance`.

**Known answer and negative control.** The recurrence above, re-implemented as a planted sampler, must be rejected:
its adjacent-channel agreement is 0.6655, more than 100 standard deviations from ½ at N = 10⁵. The qualified sampler
must show every agreement inside ½ ± 0.005 at N = 10⁵ (TARGET: 3.2 standard deviations; the number of pairs tested
is recorded, so the family-wise rate is stated).

### 23.26 The actor's information contract covers every field it can read

**What it is.** [S] Rule E-10, extending E-8 and E-9 of §23.3. The actor's information contract lists every value an
online actor can read at a decision instant, with its delivery instant under the clock contract of §20: observation
channels, earlier rewards, termination and truncation flags, action masks, and each field of `info`. Outputs of the
trainer and of the scorer are outside the actor's view unless the mission declares them delivered.

**Why.** A reward is a function of state, and delivered to the actor it is a sensor. Smallest case, on the docking
model of §20.16 (three one-second ticks, hidden gain g ∈ {1, 2}, acceleration g·u): with first command u₀ = ¼ the
position after one tick is x₁ = g/8, so a shaped reward −|1 − x₁| is −7/8 under g = 1 and −3/4 under g = 2. When the
velocity packet arrives late, no causal policy docks in both worlds (§25.15). If the wrapper hands that reward to
the actor before the second command, the two worlds are distinguished and the commands (¼, ½, −¾) and (¼, 0, −¼)
dock both. An exclusion computed without reward visibility in its contract is then false for the environment that
was run.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN:
- `ActorView` is a typed projection of `StepView` that contains only fields of access class `actor`. `reward`,
  `terminated`, `truncated`, masks and every `info` field carry an access class and a delivery instant. The default
  class of `reward` and of every `info` field is `trainer-only`. A recurrent policy that takes the previous reward
  as an input requires the task to declare reward as an `actor` channel.
- The digest of the contract (channels, classes, instants) is part of the `TaskId`, of every memo key and of every
  certificate's assumption set (C-18), so a cached answer is not reused under a different contract.
- `sense.causal-test` (§20.15) gains four planted leaks that must each fail: the previous reward wired into the
  actor; an `info` field that carries a hidden parameter; an action mask computed from hidden state; a termination
  flag delivered before its instant.
- The Gymnasium-compatible adapter (§23.18) returns rewards to the learner, as that interface requires, and records
  whether the driven policy consumed them. A run whose policy read an undeclared field is labeled and cannot be
  cited for a mission answer (refusal: actor read a field outside its information contract).

Owner: `frob-learn` (types), `frob-sense` (test), `frob-spec` (declaration).

**Known answer.** The late-packet docking exclusion, run through `VecEnv` and through the adapter. With the reward
hidden the verdict is excluded. With the reward declared and delivered before the second command the verdict
changes to feasible, with the witness above. Both runs are required: a harness that reports the same verdict in
both is defective.

### 23.27 A threshold crossing is not attainment

**What it is.** [S] A rule for every figure of the form "time to a target", which includes the training-time parity
rows of §36.3 and GATE-CORTEX-05. A recorded score crossing is a descriptive event. Attainment is a claim about the
policy's population performance, and it needs error control over every evaluation look that could have produced the
crossing.

**Counterexample.** A policy with true success rate 0.89 is evaluated against a target of 0.90 with 30 episodes per
look. A look with 30 successes of 30 has an exact one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.05^(1/30) = 0.90497, above the
target. One look does this with probability 0.89³⁰ = 0.0303. Across 50 independent looks at the unchanged policy the
probability of at least one such crossing is 1 − (1 − 0.0303)⁵⁰ = 0.78548. A rule that stops the clock at the first
crossing reports attainment, about four times in five, for a policy that never attains.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN:
- Evaluation during training runs on a fixed development stream, disjoint from training worlds, at a cadence
  registered before the run. The run record holds every look, not only the best.
- `train.status` reports two fields. `crossing` is the first look whose point score met the target, and is
  descriptive. `attained` is the first look at which a procedure valid for the registered cadence rejects "at or
  below target". The default procedure allocates error across looks as αₖ = α/[k·(k + 1)], the series of §24.13,
  and applies an exact one-sided binomial bound at level αₖ for success targets, or a confidence sequence on bounded
  returns (C-15) for return targets. With all-success looks against a 0.90 target at α = 0.05, look k needs at
  least ln(1/αₖ)/ln(1/0.9) episodes: 36 at k = 1, 46 at k = 2, 74 at k = 10, 103 at k = 50.
- Alternatively the checkpoint chosen on the development stream is evaluated once on a final holdout. That
  qualifies the chosen checkpoint and leaves its first attainment time unknown; the reported time is then an upper
  bound and is labeled as one.
- Non-attainment within the training budget is right-censored at the budget and summarized by a restricted mean.
- In a parity comparison both arms are scored by the same rule on the same stream sizes. An incumbent's published
  time to threshold is context; the figure re-measured under this rule is the comparator.

`eval.preanalyze` prints, for a registered cadence, episode count and target, the probability that an unchanged
policy at stated true rates produces a crossing. Owner: `frob-learn`, with the study machinery of `frob-arena`.

In 2,000 simulated trainings of an unchanged policy at true success 0.89, with 50 looks of 30 episodes, the first-crossing rule reports attainment in 78.5% ± 3 points and the spending rule in at most 5% (exact one-sided test at the 1% level); the four episode counts above are reproduced exactly. ### 23.28 The winner check for a selecting rung

**A selecting rung must pass a winner check [S].** This extends the consequences fixed in §23.11 and the
rank-agreement row of §28.10. A lower bound of 0.7 on the top-band Kendall τ admits a rung to selection. But τ
counts inverted pairs and weighs every pair equally; it does not say that the best design survives a cut.

*Counterexample.* Forty designs. The cheap rung reproduces the true order exactly, except that the true best is
ranked third. Among the true top ten, 2 of 45 pairs are inverted, so τ = (43 − 2)/45 = 41/45 ≈ 0.911, far above
0.7. A cut that keeps the top 5%, two designs, discards the winner.

*Rule.* For a rung admitted to selection with m finalists wanted, the number of designs advanced is the largest of
three numbers: m; the survivor count of the conformal margin rule (§28.9, layer 2); and the **containment rank**
k_c(m), the smallest k such that, in the calibration experiment and in every audit-lane draw to date for the class
and task family, the higher rung's top m all lie inside the lower rung's top k. The ledger row of §28.10 gains two
fields: `containment_rank(m)`, and the **regret** of a cut, which is the higher-rung score of the best design the
cut discards minus that of the best design it keeps. Positive regret means the winner was lost. A selection whose
retention is below k_c(m) is refused, with k_c(m) as the repair. Where a class has no calibration, the rung
discards by the fixed table and does not select.

*Owner and verbs.* `frob-opt`. `eval.rank-agreement` reports τ, top-k recall, the containment rank and the regret
of the requested cut; `opt.cascade` reads k_c(m) when it sizes a rung transition.

### 23.29 Short-horizon gradient training with design parameters

**Short-horizon gradient training with design parameters [M].** This is the learning arm that would consume the
step adjoints of §16; the gradient mechanism, its step labels and its kill gate are specified there. It is off the
critical path and is not funded before the step-adjoint gate has passed.

*What it is.* Policy parameters θ and a continuous design vector φ (up to about 20 entries of the parameter row of
§16.6) are updated together from analytic gradients of a short rollout. For a window of K control steps, K ∈ {16,
32}, the loss is the negated sum of rewards in the window plus a learned value V_ψ at its end. Its gradient in θ
and φ is one reverse sweep through the window: policy network, step adjoints, reward terms. The critic V_ψ is
fitted to temporal-difference targets as in on-policy actor-critic training. Three guards make it usable where
contact changes.

1. *A cosine monitor.* On a fixed share of windows (DESIGN default 1 in 16) the analytic design gradient is
   compared with the paired central difference of §17.13 on the same world addresses. The running median cosine is
   logged. Below 0.5 the window halves; at K = 8 and still below 0.5 the design update switches to the paired
   stencil and the run is labelled `gradient: stencil`.
2. *Step labels.* A window that contains a step the adjoint labels anything other than `smooth` contributes to the
   policy update and not to the design update, because its design gradient is one-sided or fixed-mode.
3. *Design updates are proposals.* φ moves inside the family's box. Every design the run reports is a typed edit
   sequence, recompiled and scored by the ordinary ladder (§28.9). No verdict cites a gradient.

Training with analytic gradients is a labelled D4 training profile (§35); every score that enters the ladder is an
evaluation on a D2 profile.

*Why the window is short.* At the closed-loop exponent quoted in §27.9, 0.16 per control step, a perturbation
grows by e^(0.16·K): 13 at K = 16, 167 at K = 32 and 7.8 × 10⁸ at K = 128. A gradient through 128 steps is
dominated by its variance, and its mean tells the optimizer nothing. The exponent is a published figure for other
systems; the monitor measures ours.

*Rival check.* FACT (Appendix H): several incumbents differentiate their step, and short-horizon actor-critic
training on a differentiable simulator is published. This is not a differentiator, and a composed incumbent can
host it. It is here because joint refinement of a selected design and its controller is the one stage where
population methods are known to be slow.

*Ceiling, before any speed test.* The finalist refinement stage only. Its share of the phase ledger (§39) bounds
the end-to-end gain, and nothing here helps screening.

*Pilot before funding.* One quadruped on rough ground and stairs with 20 design parameters, three seeds. Arms at
equal GPU-hours: this method; PPO with a derivative-free outer loop on φ; PPO with the paired stencil. Falsifier:
the method does not reach the matched, referee-promoted return in at most 0.35 of the derivative-free arm's
GPU-hours, or the median cosine is below 0.5 on more than 40% of monitored windows.

*Owner and verb.* `frob-learn`; `train.start --algo short-horizon-ac --design-params <list>`.
