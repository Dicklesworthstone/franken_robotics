## 25. Augur: mission queries and inverse design

Crates: `frob-mission` (queries, semantics, the search over families), `frob-synth` (constructive lanes that
propose designs) and `frob-cert` (the family-certificate payload of the `.frobcert` bundle and its checking
rules). Formats: `.fmission` and the family section of `.frobcert`.

Labelling convention for this chapter. Numbers inside a fixture are exact consequences of the fixture's stated
model; they are DESIGN, because the fixture is a specification of a known answer, and each is hand-checkable from
the equations printed beside it. Numbers that report what a plan-time pilot observed are FACT and cite a path
inside the planning reproduction bundle (Appendix H; §25.22). Thresholds in gates are TARGET. Figures from the
literature are FACT with their source in Appendix H, each to be re-read from the primary source before a gate
cites it. No number in this chapter is a measurement of FrankenRobotics, because no FrankenRobotics code exists.

### 25.1 What Augur owns and what it is for

Every other subsystem answers a question about one design: does it close, does it stand, how does it score on
this task, is it better than that one. Augur answers a question about a set of designs. The unit of work is a
**mission query**: the caller supplies an admissible design grammar with part and fabrication tolerances, a
family of worlds, an observation interface, a policy class, a horizon, success and hard-loss predicates, and the
quantifiers that relate them. The answer is a pair of design sets, an inner set I and an outer set O, with

  I ⊆ F ⊆ O,

where F is the set of designs that satisfy the mission (§25.4.1). Every design in I carries a checked reason why
it works. Every design outside O carries a checked reason why it cannot work. The designs in O ∖ I are returned as
unresolved cells, each with the competing explanations that keep it open and the cheapest next test that could
close it. Witnesses (a controller, a force allocation, a counterexample world) travel with the answer in a
portable bundle that a separate program checks on a machine with no GPU and no access to the proposing agent.

The contrast with scoring one design is the reason the subsystem exists.

| | Scoring one design | A mission query |
|---|---|---|
| Input | One design, one task, one policy | A family of designs, a family of worlds, a class of policies, explicit quantifiers |
| Output | A number with an uncertainty | Two sets, I and O, with witnesses and unresolved cells |
| A negative result | "This design scored 0.44 with this policy" | "No design in this cell works under any admitted policy", or "unknown, and here is why" |
| What training failure means | The design looks bad | Nothing about the design: a failed search does not discharge an existential quantifier (§25.4.2) |
| What an edit costs | Re-run | Re-check only the obligations the edit touches (§25.8) |
| Who can audit it | Whoever can re-run the simulator | Anyone with `frob-verify` and the bundle (§25.5) |

A worked illustration of why the quantifiers are part of the question. Ask for the least expensive modular carrier
that moves a fragile box from a rack, across a wet strip, onto a counter. Four choices change the answer: whether
the controller sees a surface label; whether its policy may use earlier slip; whether the payload can be weighed
before the journey; and whether a different policy may be trained for each body. Augur can also return the
smallest alteration of the body, the sensor suite or the world that makes the mission attainable. A cheaper ramp
strip, a different axle ratio and a better sensor then become competing, testable engineering decisions with
separate procurement and operating costs.

```
 mission, world uncertainty, observations ──┐
                                            ├─► causal design game ─┬─► exact or conservative family bounds ──┐
 parts, site edits, fabrication limits ─────┘                       └─► trajectories on batch-cpu, CUDA, Metal │
                                                                          └─► controller witness or            │
                                                                              counterexample ─────────────────┤
                                                                                                              ▼
      next probe, measurement or edit ◄── unresolved cells with competing explanations ◄── independent checker
                 │                                                                              │
                 └────────────► back into the game                         admitted cells (I), excluded cells (D ∖ O)
```

The gym, the batched tiers and the referee are execution engines for experiments that a query chooses. They are
not the product of this chapter.

### 25.2 Decisions

- **ADR-AUGUR-01: the answer to a design question is a pair of sets with proofs, never a score.** Reason: a
  score cannot distinguish "this design fails" from "this search failed", and it cannot be reused after an edit
  without re-running. Sets with per-cell proofs can.
- **ADR-AUGUR-02: the quantifier prefix is written by the caller and is part of the mission identity.** There is
  no default prefix. Reason: the same plant gives opposite answers under "one command per known build" and "one
  command for the whole family" (§25.4.2), and evidence for the easier order looks identical in a table of
  results.
- **ADR-AUGUR-03: conventional formulations first.** A query is lowered to the most ordinary sound formulation
  available: a lifted robust linear program, a scenario-tree program, a quantifier-free interval test, an
  enumeration. A specialized family solver or kernel is written only after repeated changed queries show, with
  measured timings, that the ordinary route is the bottleneck. Reason: in every plan-time pilot the conventional
  formulation tied the specialized one (§25.22).
- **ADR-AUGUR-04: propose anywhere, check on the CPU in exact or outward-rounded arithmetic.** A proposer may use
  single precision on CUDA or Metal, a floating-point solver, a learned heuristic or an agent's guess. Nothing a
  proposer says is believed. A cell enters I, or leaves O, only when the checker accepts a certificate for it.
- **ADR-AUGUR-05: the checker shares no code with the proposer.** The checking rules in `frob-cert` depend on
  `std` only and carry their own rational and interval arithmetic. Reason: a Monte Carlo run and a solver that
  share one mistranslated model agree on the wrong answer; independence of the checker is the only defence the
  format can offer.
- **ADR-AUGUR-06: region evidence is per constraint and low-dimensional.** Interval and Taylor-model certificates
  are issued for one constraint on a sub-box of about six parameters. Joint paving of a design box of ten to
  twenty dimensions is not attempted (§25.6). Reason: bisection arithmetic and the published record.
- **ADR-AUGUR-07: unknown is a verdict.** A cell is inner, excluded or unresolved. Solver failure, timeout, an
  enclosure that straddles a threshold and a mode sequence that cannot be enclosed all yield unresolved. Nothing
  converts unresolved into either of the other two.
- **ADR-AUGUR-08: answers are anytime.** Each decided cell is individually certified, so the answer at any
  moment is a valid answer to "what is known under this budget". More work can only move cells out of the
  unresolved set (INV-AUGUR-04).
- **ADR-AUGUR-09: a rival's answer goes through the same checker.** `frob-verify` accepts a bundle from any
  producer. If a short robust program over a conventional solver produces the same checked scope at similar
  turnaround, the studio ships that program behind the same verbs and says so (§25.27, KILL-AUGUR-TIE).
- **ADR-AUGUR-10: the weakest input sets the class.** An answer's class is the claim product (§8) of its cells'
  classes. A cell proved by a certificate is `Certified`; a cell supported by replayed trajectories is `Tested`;
  an empirical coverage statement is `Estimated`. No operation in `frob-mission` raises a class.
- **ADR-AUGUR-11: `.fmission` is canonical JSON with exact numbers as strings.** `MissionId` is the BLAKE3 hash
  of the canonical bytes. Rationals are reduced integer pairs "p/q" with a positive denominator; integers above
  2⁵³ never travel as JSON numbers (§32).
- **ADR-AUGUR-12: hidden as-built parameters live in the world, not in the design index.** A policy may be
  indexed by the known nominal build. It may not be indexed by a tolerance the controller cannot observe
  (§25.4.2).

### 25.3 The user contract

#### 25.3.1 Verbs

The `mission` cluster has six verbs. `experiment.suggest` is a seventh verb in its own cluster because it also
serves comparison and calibration (§28, §30). All seven are generated from the verb registry (§32), accept
`--estimate`, take a budget, and return the standard envelope. Long queries return a job handle after a bounded
synchronous wait.

| Verb | Kind | Mathematical output | What a design agent does with it | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mission.synthesize` | run | Inner and outer feasible sets over named parameters and topologies; witness controllers; unresolved cells; cost bounds when an objective is given | Chooses from a whole family of wheels, legs, transmissions and sensors under constraints | [F]; [S] on linear static and finite-game fragments |
| `mission.refute` | run | A scoped impossibility bound, or a witness that defeats one proposed design and controller; the binding constraint and the smallest admitted change that relaxes it | Stops an unproductive policy search; asks for the smallest physical change that would help | [F]; [S] on the same fragments |
| `mission.distinguish` | run | The smallest admissible intervention or instrument whose readings separate alternatives that imply different mission decisions; otherwise two witness mechanisms with opposing margins | Decides whether to collect a measurement, move a mount or fund more simulation | [F] |
| `mission.separate` | run | An executable, physically admissible pair of worlds in which a design ordering or a diagnosis changes, minimized, with the edit that removes the effect | Learns which terrain or interior feature exposes a failure and ships it as a regression (search machinery in §26) | [F] |
| `mission.transport` | run | A map between task interfaces and a local policy with a bounded error, or a counterexample naming the contact mode where authority is lost | Reuses a carry or step skill on another morphology and retrains only the named modes | [M], with an [F] static core |
| `mission.revalidate` | run | Surviving certificates, invalidated regions, required reruns and their cost, after an edit to the mission, the design family, a card or the world | Changes a motor or a floor tolerance without redoing the qualified part of a study | [F]; the dependency-keyed part is [S] |
| `experiment.suggest` | read | Ranked information-gathering proposals with testable predictions: a measurement, a probe, a simulation at a higher tier, a cell to split | Spends the next unit of budget where it can change a decision | [F] |

A point query is `mission.synthesize` with every design parameter bound; it returns one cell. An existence query
asks only whether I is nonempty; it returns `true` with a witness, `false` with an exclusion of the whole domain,
or `unknown` with the reason. The body is a free output parameter of a set comprehension, and binding it is a
special case, not a different interface.

`mission.synthesize` is also the one-verb route from a brief to a set of survivors: it carves the family, runs
the cohorts it needs, races them and returns cells with verdicts. That it saves serial agent turns is a
HYPOTHESIS; the test is whether agents given this verb reach accepted answers in fewer turns than agents driving
the lower verbs by hand (measured inside GATE-AUGUR-07). If they do not, it remains a convenience.

CLI forms follow the registry: `frob mission synthesize carrier.fmission --json`,
`frob mission refute carrier.fmission --design d:41aa… --policy p:07c2…`,
`frob mission revalidate a:93f0… --after-edit e:5d1c…`, `frob experiment suggest a:93f0… --budget gpu-s=600`.

#### 25.3.2 The `.fmission` schema (`FMT-FMISSION`, `frob.mission/1`)

| Field | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `design_domain` | A design family from §14: template or grammar, admitted topologies, free and bound parameters with unit, chart and box, part and fabrication tolerances, and the admitted interface-preserving isomorphisms | Free parameters name their chart (for example `inverse-length`), because convexity and monotonicity are properties of a chart |
| `world` | A nonempty declared family: geometry, physics, hidden parameters, initial-state uncertainty. `kind` is `bounded-set` (worst case) or `population` (a named law) | Fixed before any candidate is tested. Empty or inconsistent families are refused |
| `world.persistence` | For each hidden parameter: `fixed-per-execution` or `resampled` at a named instant | No default (§25.4.2) |
| `observation` | The channel Y: fields, noise bounds, and timing in the instants of §20 (sample, exposure, timestamp, arrival, decision, command, actuation) | A field with no arrival instant is refused |
| `policy_class` | `causal` on the history of Y; what indexes the policy (`nominal-design`, `cell`, `family`); actuation authority; randomization; decision makers | `clairvoyant` is accepted only as a labelled relaxation that can exclude, never include |
| `horizon` | `static`, a finite time, a tick count, or a declared terminal mode set | Unbounded horizons need a declared completion |
| `predicates` | `accept`, the Boolean acceptance predicate A; `hard_loss`; optional `margins`. Atoms carry their comparator (`<`, `≤`, `=`) and units, and may reference requirement terms of a `.ftask` (§24) | Shaping rewards are not predicates |
| `quantifiers` | The ordered prefix of binders: `exists policy` with its scope, `forall world`, `forall execution`, or `prob world ≥ p` with population and confidence | Part of `MissionId` |
| `objective` | Optional: a declared cost over the design domain, and the claim wanted (`feasible-candidate`, `minimum-with-matching-lower-bound`, `pareto`) | Scalarizing several objectives is the caller's decision and is recorded |
| `evidence` | Profile: `exact` (rational certificates), `enclosed` (outward-rounded intervals), `empirical` (sample coverage under a named population); accepted classes | An answer never mixes profiles inside one cell |
| `resolution` | The largest unresolved set the caller will accept, in a stated measure, and any mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligations | A vacuous enclosure cannot satisfy a mandatory obligation |
| `response` | Which parts of the answer to return | — |
| `budget` | Wall seconds, CPU core-seconds, GPU seconds, referee seconds, checker seconds, `sync_wait_ms` | §25.3.4 |

Every cell names its topology, coordinate chart, units, bounds, whether each end is closed, the assumptions it
depends on, and whether the whole cell shares one policy or has a constructively selectable policy per known
design. An empty `inner` means no certified witness so far. An empty outer set (the whole domain excluded) means

its probability mass is reported only under a weighting of topology classes and continuous parameters that the
caller supplied.

#### 25.3.4 Budgets, estimates and stops

- `--estimate` returns the cost of admission, lowering, proposing and checking separately, with intervals, and
  runs nothing. Checker time is a budget line of its own because it is part of decision time (§25.12).
- A query that would exceed a session budget is refused with the estimate attached.
- **A budget stop returns the certified cells.** DESIGN: because each decided cell is certified on its own, a
  query that stops on budget returns exit 0 when the unresolved set satisfies `resolution`, and exit 2 with the
  flag `resolution-unmet` when it does not. Exit 4 is returned only when the budget ended before admission and
  the first checked cell, in which case nothing is published. This is the standing rule "a budget stop publishes
  nothing partial as a result" applied to a result whose parts are separately complete; the reconciliation is
  recorded as OPEN-AUGUR-7 for the owner of §32.
- Long queries are jobs. Progress events report decided measure, unresolved measure, certificates checked, and
  the current next test. Cancellation drains: proposals in flight are discarded, checked cells are kept.
- A mission answer has an address `a:…` and is immutable. A later query with more budget produces a new answer
  that refines the old one.

#### 25.3.5 Chance constraints

A probabilistic prefix names three things: the population the probability is over, the event whose probability
is bounded, and the confidence level of the evidence. A worst-case bound over an interval of a material
parameter and a probability over manufactured units are different statements and use different binders.

A mission of several stages either preserves the joint law across stages or allocates failure budgets that add
up to the mission allowance. The smallest case that shows why: among 20 equally likely worlds, two stages each
pass in 19. If their failed worlds differ, 18 of 20 missions pass. If they coincide, 19 of 20 pass. Independent
stages with those marginal rates give 0.95² = 0.9025. If each stage instead has conditional success of at least
0.95 at every history that reaches it, the product is a lower bound. Marginal stage certificates alone do not
identify mission reliability, so Augur reports joint episode outcomes when it has them and refuses to multiply
marginals without a stated dependence assumption (`FROB-REF-846`).

Empirical cells go through the study types of §24: a fixed-n exact study with its reachability pre-analysis, or
an anytime-valid study, never a mixture. Confirmation of a mission answer on sealed worlds uses the blinded
qualification lane.

#### 25.3.6 Minimum, repair and gap claims

- A claimed minimum is returned as an incumbent cost together with a checked matching lower bound. Without the
  lower bound the claim is `feasible-candidate` and says so.
- Strict feasible sets can have an unattained infimum. In the docking family of §25.15, at read error ε = 5/16,
  repair by raising the actuator bound requires U > 3/4; on a continuous catalog there is no smallest increase.
  Augur returns the open threshold, any feasible priced candidate and its gap to the infimum. A discrete catalog
  may attain a smallest repair, and then the answer says `attained: true`.
- `mission.refute` reports the binding constraint from the dual of the excluding relaxation, and the smallest
  admitted change that relaxes it, as a typed edit to the design, the sensor suite, the world or the mission.
- Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations (§25.8). A feasible witness can survive an edit that
  destroys the optimality of the design it belongs to.

Refusals are listed in §25.24.

### 25.4 Semantics

A mission answer is only as good as the definition it answers. This section fixes the definition so that an
optimizer, a learned policy, a cache or a GPU kernel cannot change the question while answering it.

#### 25.4.1 Objects and the feasible set

- **D**, the design domain, is the disjoint union of the parameter spaces of the typed assembly graphs that the
  query admits, quotiented only by admitted, interface-preserving isomorphisms (§14). Two designs that differ by
  a relabelling the mission cannot observe are one design; nothing else is identified.
- **W**, the world family, is the nonempty declared set of world conditions: geometry, physics, hidden
  parameters and initial-state uncertainty. Its scope is fixed before candidates are tested. A body that cannot
  satisfy an admitted clearance or load case carries that failure; the case is not removed from W to make room.
- **Π_Y** is the set of causal policies on the history of the available channel Y: at each decision instant the
  command is a function of the observations that have arrived by that instant under the clock contract of §20.
- **T(d, π, w)** is the set of maximal admitted executions of design d under policy π in world w. It is a set
  because contact can be set-valued and because unobserved disturbances remain free.
- **A(τ)** is the mission's Boolean acceptance predicate on an execution τ, including temporal requirements,
  hard limits and terminal outcomes.

The robust design family is

  F = { d ∈ D : ∃ π ∈ Π_Y  ∀ w ∈ W,  T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅  ∧  ∀ τ ∈ T(d, π, w), A(τ) }.

The clause T(d, π, w) ≠ ∅ matters: a design whose dynamics are undefined in some admitted world does not satisfy
the universal quantifier vacuously.

#### 25.4.2 Quantifier order

The prefix ∃π ∀w ∀τ says: one policy, chosen knowing the nominal design and nothing about the hidden world, must
succeed in every world on every admitted execution. Three weaker statements are easy to prove by accident, and
each is refused as evidence for F.

| Weaker statement | How it arises | Why it is not F |
|---|---|---|
| ∀w ∃π (clairvoyant) | A controller is tuned or trained after the hidden world is known; a per-world optimum is computed | The controller uses information it will not have |
| ∃π for each as-built unit | One controller per realized tolerance | Two indistinguishable builds that need different commands cannot each get their own |
| ∃τ instead of ∀τ | A favourable contact branch is selected in simulation | In set-valued contact, a stick or slip fork with any failing admissible branch defeats a universal certificate |

**The standing regression.** A one-axis pusher moves a package by y = max(0, g·u − d), with build gain g, command
u and hidden gap d. On g ∈ [1/2, 2], u ∈ [0, 2], d ∈ [0, 1/4], with required y ∈ [3/4, 5/4]: for every known
gain the command u = 1/g works for every gap (g·u = 1, so y ∈ [3/4, 1]); no single command works for the whole
gain family, because g·u ∈ [1, 5/4] for both g = 1/2 and g = 2 would need u ≥ 2 and u ≤ 5/8. "A command per
known build" returns the whole interval. "One command for the family" returns the empty set. The two queries
differ by one binder, and this pair is a permanent regression test of the public interface (§25.26).

Rules that follow from the order.

- **Index a design-dependent policy by the known nominal build.** Hidden as-built tolerances stay in W until an
  admitted calibration observation reveals them. Assigning one controller to each realized tolerance would
  certify a choice that is not available. Each cell records whether it shares one policy or has a constructively
  selectable policy per known design.
- **A rollout refutes a particular controller, not a design.** Excluding a design under ∃π requires an argument
  that covers every admitted controller. A finite experiment that fails to train a controller does not discharge
  the quantifier, and Augur never converts a failed training run into an exclusion.
- **A refuter must match the claim it refutes.** For the statement "every controller has some failing
  disturbance", one successful trajectory against one disturbance is not a counterexample; a counterexample is
  one body and one causal controller that meet the mission for every admitted disturbance.
- **Hidden parameters are persistent unless declared otherwise.** Take a prescribed out-and-back motion of one
  second each way with a positive gain that is hidden and fixed for the execution, g ∈ {1, 2}. The net
  displacement is g − g = 0 in both worlds. If the gain is resampled per move, the reachable net displacements
  are {−1, 0, 1}. Treating a fixed parameter as a per-step disturbance enlarges the world family: it is
  conservative for inclusion and unsound for exclusion (it would exclude a return-to-start mission that the
  persistent model satisfies). Treating a resampled disturbance as fixed is the opposite error. So
  `world.persistence` has no default.
- **Private randomization does not rescue a guaranteed mission.** Where the prefix is ∀w, a randomized policy
  must succeed with probability one in every world. Randomization changes the answer only under a `prob` binder.
- **Each actuator has a named decision maker** with its own history of received packets, observable actions and
  local measurements at its commitment time. A centralized controller is admitted only when the information
  actually reaches it, and its command actually arrives, by the same deadline (§25.17).
- **The checker rejects evidence for the easier order.** A certificate records the prefix it proves. A bundle
  whose proofs are valid for a different prefix than the query's is rejected even when every hash is consistent
  (`FROB-REF-843`).

#### 25.4.3 Progress, Zeno behaviour and unknown

Each execution must progress to the horizon or to a declared terminal outcome. The rules:

1. **Deadlock, undefined dynamics and Zeno behaviour without an admitted completion cannot earn success.** An
   execution that stops before the horizon, or accumulates infinitely many events in finite time, does not
   satisfy A.
2. **A model-level failure of progress is a failing execution.** When deadlock or a Zeno accumulation is a
   property of the declared hybrid model, shown by an exact or enclosed argument, it blocks inclusion, and when
   it is shown for every admitted policy it supports exclusion.
3. **An admitted completion restores evaluation.** If the mission model declares what follows an accumulation
   point (a bouncing contact that comes to rest and enters a declared resting mode), A is evaluated on the
   completed execution.
4. **A numerical failure is unknown.** Non-convergence, step-size collapse, a retry budget exhausted in the
   transactional step, a NaN, or an integrator that cannot locate an event leave the verdict unresolved, unless
   independent evidence establishes physical failure or impossibility under the mission model. A solver error is
   never an exclusion.
5. **Comparators are evaluated as declared.** Strict, non-strict and equality atoms are distinct. In the docking
   family at U = 3/4 and ε = 5/16 the separation between the two reading sets is exactly zero and the strict
   predicate fails. Conversely on 0 < w ≤ 1 every realization has w > 0 although the infimum is 0. A uniform
   positive lower bound is sufficient for strict feasibility and can itself be requested as a robustness margin,
   but that stronger requirement is stated in `predicates.margins`, not assumed.
6. **Scalarization is the caller's decision.** Several objectives are not combined into one number by Augur.

#### 25.4.5 Four proof rules

These are the only ways a cell changes status. Each is implementable and checkable independently, and each has
a deliberately invalid fixture that the checker must reject.

**Rule 1: necessary-condition exclusion.** Relax the controller and the dynamics: allow any contact force inside
an outer wrench cone, any torque under an upper envelope, any observation the controller might wish for.
Infeasibility of the relaxation eliminates every realization it covers. The certificate records the set
inclusions that make it a relaxation (the true force set is inside the outer cone; the true torque set is under
the envelope) and the dual separating vector. Two limits:

- Infeasible sample points do not eliminate their enclosing cell. Exclusion of a cell needs a certificate that
  is valid over the cell: a parametric dual vector with a sign proof, or a monotonicity argument.
- The relaxation must enclose every admissible state and energy source. A quasi-static work cut for a passive
  sill crossing of an 8 kg body over a 12 mm rise requires 8 × 9.81 × 0.012 = 0.94176 J against a 0.90 J budget
  and excludes the crossing; 0.05 J of approach kinetic energy, absent from the budget, removes the exclusion.
  Static rejection is a candidate for a dynamic test until the model it uses contains the dynamics' resources.

**Rule 2: constructive inclusion.** Supply one causal controller, an admissible contact schedule, event guards,
and a sound reachable tube or an exact witness for the whole cell. Check actuator bounds, material domains and
the mission predicate over the enclosure. At an uncertain mode boundary, branch conservatively or return
unresolved. The referee is used to hunt for counterexamples, which can only remove a cell from I. Scope today:
exact witnesses exist for static, quasi-static and finite-game cells; sound reachable tubes for contact-rich
full-order cells do not exist in the suite, and such cells stay unresolved with `Tested` trajectories attached
(OPEN-AUGUR-5).

**Rule 3: monotone inheritance or symmetry.** A verdict moves across a cell only under the conditions for that
verdict. For exclusion, transport the relaxation and the violating witness. For inclusion, require a checked
realization or refinement that preserves admissible disturbances, causal information, realizable policies,
mission outputs and all admitted branches (§25.4.6). Matching outer wrench boxes cannot transport success across
an unobserved joint stop. For symmetry, the group element must fix the mission, map W onto itself and map Π_Y
onto itself; restricting a policy class to equivariant deterministic policies is a change of Π_Y. With two
symmetric actuators where success needs exactly one of them to act, the winning actions (0, 1) and (1, 0) are
exchanged by the symmetry and the only invariant deterministic actions, (0, 0) and (1, 1), both fail.

**Rule 4: exact partitions and composition.** The union of proved inner cells is inner. To remove a cell E from
the outer set, prove E ∩ F = ∅; disjointness of E from the known inner cells leaves E open. A mission contract
composes with a subtask only when the first stage's guaranteed end set lies within the second's admissible entry
set, with matching clocks, uncertainty dependencies and state memory. Two individually successful videos, one
per stage, prove no such inclusion.

#### 25.4.6 Monotonicity and its test

Monotonicity is the cheapest source of family verdicts and the easiest to misuse. Write d ≼ d′ for an order on a
chart (a larger rating, a longer stroke, a wider stance in a stated coordinate).

- **Exclusion transports downward** from d′ to d ≼ d′ when the relaxed feasible set at d is contained in the
  relaxed feasible set at d′. The obligation is a set inclusion between relaxations.
- **Inclusion transports upward** from d to d′ ≽ d when (i) every disturbance admitted at d′ is admitted at d,
  (ii) the witness controller is realizable at d′ and receives the same or a finer observation history, (iii)
  the mission outputs at d′ satisfy A whenever those at d do, and (iv) every admitted branch at d′ is simulated
  by an admitted branch at d. The obligation is an alternating refinement, not a set inclusion.

The first does not give the second. Enlarging a convex set of allowable contact forces admits more loads in a
static model, so widening a physical sole helps the static inclusion; the same wider sole may collide during
swing, which is a new branch at d′ that no branch at d simulates. A design that passes the static test and is
"monotonically better" is still outside I until condition (iv) is checked.

**The test** (`monotone-transport`, §25.5.2) has three parts, all run by the checker:

1. *Structural sign.* Every dependence of the lowered constraint expressions on the transported parameter has a
   proved sign over the whole cell: the outward-rounded enclosure of the partial derivative excludes zero, or
   the parameter enters only as the bound of a set that provably grows. A sign that holds at sample points is
   not a proof.
2. *Side conditions present.* The four inclusion conditions, or the one exclusion condition, each appear as a
   sub-certificate. A transport with a missing side condition is rejected (`FROB-REF-852`), not weakened.
3. *Scope.* The transport names the model stratum it holds in (fixed contact mode sequence, fixed topology,
   fixed observation timing). Crossing a stratum boundary ends the transport.

A falsification harness accompanies the proof: for each declared monotone direction, random ordered pairs in the
cell are evaluated at the highest available tier, and a single reversal withdraws every verdict transported
along that direction and files the pair as a counterexample (GATE-AUGUR-08).

#### 25.4.8 Arithmetic, and where proof assistants are used

- Deterministic set arithmetic uses interval and affine bounds with outward rounding, and checked linear-program
  duals where they apply.
- A certificate may be proposed in low precision on CUDA or Metal, then rationally reconstructed or enclosed,
  then checked on the CPU. For a linear program the proposer's floating-point solution names an active set; the
  basis system is solved exactly over ℚ; the checker tests exact primal feasibility, and for an optimum, exact
  dual feasibility. If reconstruction fails the cell is unresolved.
- Condition numbers, residual bounds, domain versions and the strictness of each inequality are checker inputs,
  not proposer annotations.
- On polynomial subdomains a sum-of-squares bound with a checked residual and a finite domain may be tried [F].
- Strict predicates evaluated in bulk on a device use the exact-integer kernel class or guarded single precision
  with an exact fallback (§35). Unguarded single-precision comparison is not admitted for a verdict: on a
  65,536-row boundary screen it produced 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions (FACT, §25.22).
- Proof assistants are used on reusable proof rules and on translations (§12). A theorem prover is not
  dispatched for each wheel and terrain pair.

#### 25.5.4 What `frob-verify` checks, and what it does not

It checks:

- bundle integrity and schema; that every hash it can recompute matches;
- that each proof's kind and version are in the closed registry;
- that the prefix, horizon, policy class and cell each proof establishes are the query's;
- that every side condition a rule requires is present;
- every witness, by exact rational or outward-rounded recomputation from the primitive equations in the bundle,
  using its own big-integer, rational and interval arithmetic with declared size limits (a certificate beyond
  the limits is refused, not approximated);
- partition coverage and the open or closed status of every boundary;
- that the answer's claim class equals the weakest class among its cells;
- that `authorization: none` is present.

It does not check, and a bundle must not be read as establishing:

- that the primitive equations describe the physical robot, floor or sensor. That is the Fidelity and Reality
  claims of §8, and no certificate in this chapter makes them;
- that the lowering from the design and mission to the primitive equations is correct. This is mitigated, not
  solved: the equations are in the bundle for review, each lowering has an invalid fixture, and supported
  templates are lowered twice by independent code (PO-AUGUR-01);
- any trajectory. A `trace-witness` is accepted as well-formed; replaying it is a separate act by the referee
  (§29), and the cell stays `Tested`;
- that the unresolved set is as small as it could be, or that the search was competent;
- cost data, catalog prices, or the weighting behind a reported probability mass;
- any statistical statement beyond recomputing a bound from counts.

#### 25.5.5 One false certificate

A certificate is false when `frob-verify` accepted it and a concrete execution, allocation or world inside its
stated scope contradicts it. One false certificate has four consequences, none discretionary:

1. The proof kind and checker version involved are disabled in the registry until the cause is found.
2. Every stored bundle that used them is marked withdrawn; dependent answers are re-derived or downgraded to
   `Tested`.
3. In the ten-fold test the arm that submitted it is disqualified from the scoped claim (§39).
4. The counterexample becomes a permanent fixture in the checker's mutation corpus.

A truthful unresolved cell is never a false certificate. It counts against coverage where a definitive answer
was required, and nowhere else.

### 25.6 Region evidence within its real scope [F]

**What is not attempted.** Paving a joint design box of ten to twenty dimensions with certified feasible and
infeasible boxes. The arithmetic: 10⁴ boxes are 13 bisections; in 20 dimensions that is less than one cut per
axis, and uniform boxes have side 0.64 of the domain (0.41 in 10 dimensions), so a constraint surface that
depends on most parameters crosses nearly every box. The published record agrees (FACT, Appendix H): interval
design of parallel robots is described by its own practitioners as exponential branch and bound, suited to small
and medium problems, with a worked example of five design parameters; a three-dimensional dextrous-workspace
paving took about five hours and left an error bound of up to a third of the certified volume; Taylor models
delay the growth with dimension very little when dependent intervals lack cancellation structure.

**What is built.** Certificates for one constraint over a sub-box of the parameters that constraint depends on,
with at most about six parameters, combined by products, intersections and monotonicity.

- **The constraint-to-parameter graph.** Each lowered constraint g_k lists the parameters S_k it reads. Legged
  static analyses are sparse by construction: joint torque, reach and clearance of one leg depend on roughly
  three to six parameters of that leg (HYPOTHESIS; the measured sparsity for the plan's robot classes is
  OPEN-AUGUR-3, and closure constraints of linkage legs raise the coupled dimension).
- **Per-constraint pavings.** For each g_k, an inner set I_k and an outer set O_k in the coordinates S_k, built
  by bisection with interval, affine or Taylor-model enclosures and forward–backward contractors. Each box
  carries an `interval-box` or `taylor-box` certificate.
- **Combination.** A design is in the region answer's inner set when it lies in every cylinder over I_k, and
  outside the outer set when it lies outside some cylinder over O_k. The joint set is represented implicitly, by
  k membership tests; it is never enumerated. Its volume, when wanted, is an `Estimated` quantity from sampling.
- **Monotone bounds first.** A mass budget or a rating bound that is monotone in one parameter removes its half
  space with one `monotone-transport` certificate and no bisection.
- **Dual witnesses for static conditions.** Where a condition is a linear or second-order-cone feasibility
  problem (static equilibrium with ratings, friction cones, torque envelopes), the exclusion is an `lp-farkas` or
  `conic-farkas` certificate. A parametric dual with a sign proof covers a whole cell at once, as in §25.5.3,
  which is why linear static families do not need paving at all.
- **Pointwise checks remain.** An agent needs a check at the points it proposes. Region certificates are a
  source of evidence quality and of tolerance robustness (a box around a design is a statement about every
  as-built unit inside it). They are not presented as a speed lever, and deciding volume is not the same as
  saving simulations.

**Scope of a region certificate.** It covers the static rigid model named in the bundle. It says nothing about
dynamic performance, run-up, landing or recovery.

**Killed forms, not built.** Sampling only inside a certified inner ellipsoid: it cuts off the feasibility
boundary, where optima sit, and misses disconnected islands. A logarithmic change of variables to linearize
posynomial constraints for an integer-linear decision procedure: the logarithm of a posynomial is a convex
log-sum-exp, neither linear nor polynomial. Cylindrical algebraic decomposition for reachable sets: doubly
exponential beyond four or five variables.

**Pilot before funding.** A 12-parameter quadruped box (three link lengths, two hip offsets, three masses, gear
ratio, motor mass, body length and width) with static joint torque in three stance postures, reach to a foothold
set, and self-clearance. Pave with 10⁴ boxes in four arms: plain intervals, affine forms, contractors, and
per-constraint decomposition. Falsifier: the best arm leaves more than 90% undecided, or more than 95% of the
decided volume falls to a single monotone bound. Cost: about two engineer-days and CPU minutes (TARGET).
Ceiling of benefit, stated before any speed test: none is claimed; pointwise static checks with an existing
library give the same pruning for proposed designs.

### 25.7 Searching a family: quotients, cuts and cell refinement [F]

**The loop.** `frob-mission` keeps a worklist of cells. For each cell it tries, in order of cost: a monotone or
symmetry transport from a decided neighbour; a static exclusion; an exact or finite-game inclusion; a region
certificate; a simulation campaign that can refute an inclusion or support a `Tested` verdict; a split. A cell
that resists everything stays unresolved with its explanations. The order in which cells are attempted is a
scheduling heuristic and may use `fs-voi` opportunity loss; scheduling never controls validity, because every
decided cell has its own certificate.

**Discrete families: typed holes and checked cuts.** A topology family is a bounded design term with typed holes
(§14): a mount hole is a frame, a legal joint subgroup and a port signature; a contact attachment is a
conservative wrench envelope; battery, thermal, payload and geometric requirements are typed quantities with
tolerances. The search fills holes best-first and prunes a partial term only with a bound that is checked over
every legal completion of that node: an optimistic upper capability bound against a requirement, or a
conservative lower resource bound against a budget. Dominance pruning between partial terms requires a recorded
monotonicity argument. A minimized counterexample yields a cut only when it provably generalizes over the
excluded family. Each cut is a certificate; **a prune is wrong if any discarded design is independently
feasible under the same mission, even when the eventual winner survives.**

- *Pilot before funding.* Exhaust a frozen catalog of 1,024 leaves on the CPU, with and without cuts. Falsifier:
  one false cut, or less than 70% of evaluation cost saved (TARGET).
- *Ceiling.* The saving is bounded by the fraction of the catalog that static bounds can decide; it is measured
  on the frozen catalog before any claim.

**The mission-observable quotient.** Two designs need separate evaluation only if the mission can tell them
apart. For a mission M, `frob-mission` compiles a projection π_M from designs to exactly the invariants that
enter M's lowered equations: total pseudo-inertia, collision hulls, joint and actuator parameters, port
coordinates, sensor visibility, bus and thermal parameters, cost terms. Designs share a class only when their
projections are equal over all active observables; adding a placement-dependent observable (a hotspot, a harness
length) enlarges the projection and splits classes. Results are memoized on the class (§28).

- *Known answer.* Eight equal ballast slugs in 24 collinear slots: C(24, 8) = 735,471 placements enter rigid-body
  dynamics only through (N, Σx, Σx²), giving 24,546 classes. Slots {0, 3} and {1, 2} of a two-slug variant have
  equal Σx = 3 and different Σx² (9 and 5) and must not be merged.
- *Status of the rule.* "Equal projections imply identical modelled traces" is a statement about the simulator.
  It is a structural rule with a bit-identical replay test on sampled class members, classed `Tested`; it is
  not a proof-kernel obligation.
- *Honest scope.* The size of the quotient is a property of the fixture. Independent material or compliance
  changes can make every class a singleton. No ratio is claimed.

**Starter designs across families.** When the design domain spans several archetype families (wheeled, tracked,
legged, hybrid), `mission.synthesize` first sizes one starter design per family by the static analyses and the
co-design closure (§25.21.1) and ranks the families before any simulation. This is orchestration, not a lever of
its own. Falsifier: on 20 missions the eventual winner lies outside the top two ranked families more than 15% of
the time (TARGET), in which case the ranking is dropped and every family is searched. Interpolating between
topologies through continuous coupling weights is not built: the limit of a vanishing limb is ill-conditioned.

**Inconsistent missions.** Admission tests the predicates against the world family with every design constraint
relaxed. If they conflict, the query is refused with a minimal conflicting subset, found by deletion filtering
over the linear and interval relaxation, with a Farkas certificate where the conflict is linear
(`FROB-REF-842`). This is [S] for linear conflicts and [F] beyond.

### 25.8 Revalidation after an edit [F]

`mission.revalidate` answers: after this edit, which of my verdicts are still proved, which are withdrawn, what
must be rerun and what will it cost. The edit may be to the mission, the design family, an actuator or material
card, a sensor, a clock, or the world.

**A hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck.** Each reusable object records the
implication it proved. A room segment may guarantee a lower wheel-traction margin under a stated surface and
normal-load interval; a changed motor alone can invalidate that normal-load interval through acceleration. Reuse
requires checking the newly implied assumptions against the saved ones.

**Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations.** A cost-9 cart may be the certified minimum while a
cost-5 relay is excluded by a doorway. Widen only the relay's doorway. The cart and its complete successful
trace remain valid, and the optimum moves to 5. Before returning a minimum or a complete Pareto set after an
edit, Augur revalidates the competing exclusions, the global cost bound, catalog membership and prices. It
preserves the cart's witness and withdraws the stale minimum, including when an amendment adds a new admissible
catalog part.

**The assumption lattice, with polarity.** Assumption sets are ordered by inclusion of admitted worlds and
commands. Each transport of a verdict across an edit is marked with its direction:

| Edit | Inclusion (a universal guarantee with the same witness) | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow the disturbance set | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Enlarge the disturbance set | Must be rechecked | Preserved when its witness lies in the old set and the policy class and information contract are unchanged |
| Relax an actuator limit | Preserved if the old command is still realizable and the plant and observations still realize the old dynamics | Must be rechecked |
| Relax a requirement | Preserved | Must be rechecked |
| Improve a sensor, or deliver a packet earlier | Preserved if every new observation history is one the old policy was proved on, or the old observation can be reconstructed from the new one | Can be destroyed: an observation-based impossibility may no longer hold |
| Delay a packet across a decision instant | Must be rechecked | Must be rechecked |
| Simultaneous edits to two fields | Joint check; per-field monotonicity rarely proves an interaction | Joint check |

**Mechanism.** Every certificate lists its dependency support: the physical parameters, metric intervals,
stages, scene cells, clocks, cards and model versions it actually used, including implicit ones (a common noise
model, the gravity frame, a calibrated renderer, a torque derating, a preprocessing graph). An edit disjoint
from a certificate's support preserves it. An edit inside the support triggers either the precise obligation
whose slack the changed interval must still fit, or a rerun. Each cheap revalidation carries an independently
checkable mask over evidence obligations. For a missed dependency, the withdrawal names the two worlds or the
new mode that invalidates it. The dependency-keyed part of this is the memo graph of §28; `frob-mission` adds
the polarity table and the optimality obligations.

Two structural rules come from other chapters and are applied here unchanged. From the module contracts of §14:
swapping a leaf module preserves its siblings' local certificates, while an edit that touches a shared battery
bus, a shared thermal sink, a cross-branch kinematic loop or base-coupled contact invalidates global ones; the
control pair is a mirror swap, which must reuse, against a shared-bus edit, which must invalidate. From the fork
rules of §29: a simulation prefix is reused after an intervention only when the intervention is classified as
leaving that prefix unchanged.

**What the plan-time pilot showed.** On a four-state camera-delivery fixture (two scenes that commit at ticks 2
and 4; a packet delivered at tick 1, then renamed, then at tick 2, then at tick 3), a semantic receipt and a
conventional dependency graph given the same eligibility guard evaluated 2, 0, 0 and 1 scene pairs and
recomputed the verdict 1, 0, 0 and 1 times, and both matched fresh replay; the variant that omitted delivery
time inherited a stale promotion (FACT, §25.22). So a correctly keyed graph ties, and there is no reuse
advantage to claim on that fixture. The opportunity that remains is obtaining a physical non-dependence or
sensitivity certificate cheaply enough to matter.

**Pilot before funding, with a hard stop.** Freeze a campaign of 30 amendments before either system sees it:
visual-only, camera-timing, sole geometry, floor law, torque, shared-noise, load-authority and facility edits;
edits that matter to telemetry and not to a decision; and an apparently cosmetic change that advances a packet by
one control tick. A separate engineer implements the incremental incumbent (demand-driven recomputation with
solver sensitivity information). Every decision is replayed independently. Record every incorrectly inherited
success and every unnecessary invalidation; count unsafe reuse as a false accept even if a later change happens
to restore feasibility. Falsifier: any incorrectly inherited verdict. Continue only in cells where the two
systems are equally correct and measured critical-path costs support the registered target. Ceiling: bounded by
the dirty fraction of the campaign; a dependency analysis at plan time puts exact reuse of rollouts after a mass
or geometry edit at no more than about 10% of compute (HYPOTHESIS until measured on our own memo graph), so the
benefit lies in analyses and certificates, not in trajectories.

### 25.9 Design–world duality and minimal counterexamples [F]

Redesigning the body and designing the world are two sides of one decision problem. Given feasible rivals d_A and
d_B, `mission.separate` searches a physically admitted world family for a world where the predicted preference
changes by a practically important margin. The smallest such change is often more informative than the largest
simulated reward: for a threshold, one changed edge radius; for a warehouse, a short compliant mat under load;
for a kitchen, a joint condition of layout, optics and friction near a turn.

- **Worlds are built from typed procedural primitives and real captures** with scale and uncertainty (§19).
- **Smallness is defined in authored units**: lexicographic edit count first, then normalized physical distance.
- **Priors are distinct from admissibility.** A rare admissible case can be a useful counterexample while
  carrying negligible operational probability; both facts are reported, separately.
- **Monitors, not rewards.** Temporal goals compile into monitors on signed clearance, work, support, sensor age
  and completion time (§24). Hard limits are frozen separately from shaping rewards.
- **The loop.** Propose a design; search for violating environments; reproduce the violation on the referee;
  minimize the violating scene; update the admissible region. Two experiments are carried through every
  iteration: the failing world, and the counterfactual edit that removes the effect.
- **A shrinking trajectory that changes the failure mechanism gets a new event classification.** Minimization
  preserves the first cause (§29) or it starts a new counterexample.
- **A scene rewrite declares the causal path it changes**: geometry, material, optics, clocks or actors. Moving a
  light in a commercial kitchen can alter the depth-dropout distribution without altering friction; wetness may
  affect both. An intervention that does not know which path it changes is not admitted.
- **One route proves nothing universal.** A route through one environment cannot establish a universal design
  verdict; it is a `trace-witness`.
- **Fair baselines.** Scenario-based baselines receive the same world generator and the same monitors.

The search machinery (rare-event exploration, bounded-weight estimation, delta-debugging minimization, the
`.fmcw` format, and the solvability evidence that keeps a counterexample world admissible) is Nemesis, §26.
Augur contributes the question (a pair of designs and a decision, not one design and a score), the admissibility
contract, and the dual query: the smallest priced edit to the world that restores the mission, drawn from the
retrofit catalog of §19 and entered into the same budget as edits to the robot (§25.17).

Pilot and falsifier: the first hybrid-contact slice of §25.23 (row 3). Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-SEPARATE.

### 25.10 When a better body cannot rescue a blind policy [F]

A design agent should be able to ask whether spending more compute on training with the present sensor suite can
ever satisfy the mission.

**The observation-indistinguishability witness** (`alias-pair`). Construct two physically admissible latent
states that require disjoint successful actions. Fix the permissible probe actions, the time available, the
observation histories and the policy's information. For equal prior classes and a fixed observation experiment
with distributions P and Q, the best probability of distinguishing the states is (1 + TV(P, Q))/2, where TV is
the total-variation distance; this caps action success when success requires distinguishing them. If active
probing is allowed, the bound is taken over all permitted probe strategies, or it is labelled as applying to the
specified experiment only. A claimed universal observability bound fails if a permitted policy can perform a
safe probe the model left out.

**The exact fixture.** Two sealed payload classes require disjoint final forces, [2, 4] N and [7, 9] N. A binary
probe reports the correct class with probability 3/4.

| Sensing policy | Success per class | Meets a 90% target |
|---|---|---|
| Blind | 1/2 | No |
| One read | 3/4 | No |
| Three independent reads, majority | 27/32 | No |
| Five independent reads | 459/512 | No |
| Seven independent reads | 3807/4096 | Yes |
| Nine reads sharing one common fault bit | 3/4 | No |
| One read of a distinct 95% modality | 19/20 | Yes |
| Stop at a vote gap of two, unbounded horizon | 9/10, with 16/5 expected reads | Yes, only without a deadline |
| The same rule, forced to guess after six reads | 225/256 | No |

Exact Bayes classification on the full observation sequence attains the same numbers. Three lessons are part of
the specification. First, error dependence, not per-read accuracy, sets the value of redundancy: the common-mode
row is the assumption that reverses the sensor-budget decision. Second, "seven" is a fixed-horizon result and is
not a lower bound on expected cost across adaptive policies: the sequential rule reaches 90% in 3.2 expected
reads. Third, the chance of still waiting after six reads is 27/512, and forcing a fair guess then gives 225/256,
below 90%; so delay is priced and the mission deadline is enforced for every sensing policy that is compared.

**Three engineering answers to one perceptual ambiguity.** The first robot task conceals one of two container
classes behind identical exteriors and admits three design edits: a load-sensitive wrist (extract the
information), a different camera placement (move to acquire it), or a compliant passive gripper whose success
sets overlap (change the mechanics so the information is unnecessary). All three are compared against a
calibrated forcemeter and an optimal finite-belief controller, with common-mode faults and occluded placements
held out.

**A body can remove the need for a sensor.** The two force bands are disjoint for a controller that commands
terminal force. Consider a different, explicitly modelled plant: a displacement-driven jaw with a series spring
of stiffness K closes by x mm on one of two linearly elastic objects, both in contact at x = 0, with stiffnesses
k₁ = 1 and k₂ = 4 N/mm. At quasi-static equilibrium F_j = x / (1/K + 1/k_j). Put c = 1/K in mm/N. With at most
6 mm of stroke, a single command works on both hidden classes exactly when

  max{ 2(c + 1), 7(c + 1/4) }  ≤  x  ≤  min{ 4(c + 1), 9(c + 1/4), 6 }.

In (c, x) coordinates this is an exact polyhedral family, and it is an inner cell with a shared policy.

- At K = 4 N/mm, any x ∈ [7/2, 9/2] mm works; x = 4 mm gives 3.2 N and 8 N.
- With stroke capped at 6 mm the stiffness must be at least 28/17 N/mm. At that boundary x = 6 mm, the stiffer
  object receives 7 N and the softer 56/15 N. The binding condition is the stroke available for the 7 N
  requirement.
- Relaxing the stroke reveals the force-compatibility bound K ≥ 4/3 N/mm.

This change of body alters the map from command to force. It leaves the information bound for a force-commanded
plant intact. A conventional impedance engineer given the same equations designs the same jaw; the value claimed
is that the three answers are returned by one query in one comparable form. The falsifiers are physical: break
the assumed correlation between required grip and object stiffness by allowing the same stiffness in both hidden
classes, and the passive jaw supplies the same force to both and cannot meet disjoint bands; misaligned initial
contact, hysteresis, settling time and impact overshoot are further tests. The agent chooses between a better
sensor, a passive constitutive law and a low-cost probe by their robustness over independently measured
variations. This is a co-design question with a clear experimental answer even if simulation speed is unchanged.

Pilot: run (§25.22, the sensor-alias pilot). Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-ALIAS.

### 25.11 Transfer of control through a physical interface [M]

A shared neural network can save training when robots differ. The stronger goal is to tell an agent which part
of a closed-loop skill can be transferred, and why. `mission.transport` works at the task boundary.

**The object.** A skill (§22, `.fskill`) is represented at its task interface by an observable state, an action
or wrench interface, reachable predecessor and terminal sets, and clock and environment assumptions. A source
robot A and a new embodiment B may realize the same interface through different internal contact modes.

**The relation.** Seek a relation R(x_A, x_B) and a causal refinement map from abstract commands to target
efforts such that:

1. every admitted initial target state is related to an abstract state compatible with its observable history;
2. for each permitted abstract command, the implementable interface selects one target command using only
   information available before disturbances act;
3. for every admitted target disturbance and every resulting target successor, there is an allowed abstract
   successor related to it, respecting any shared disturbance law;
4. observations, task outputs, timing and target progress are preserved.

This is an alternating simulation: the controller chooses, then the environment chooses. **A target with
successors {stable, fall} cannot refine an abstract stable-only transition by selecting just its stable
branch.** Approximate relations carry error and timing budgets small enough to preserve the mission's acceptance
predicates.

**The [F] static core.** For a carried tray, match tray pose, velocity, allowable contact wrench and
acceleration limits. A quadruped and a wheeled carrier have fundamentally different support transitions, so only
the tray stabilization objective and its feedback state are ported, and only when the target's reachable wrench
set at the tray interface covers the requested one. That coverage test is a polytope or cone containment and is
certified with `lp-primal` and `lp-farkas`. Residual motions are resolved by whole-body inverse dynamics (§22).

**The counterexample output.** At a ramp crest the target may lose lateral authority. The verb returns that
contact mode, the requested wrench, the attainable set and a rerunnable counterexample. This localizes the
required retraining or body edit to one mode.

**The experiment that decides funding.** Three treatments at equal compute and equal source data: training from
scratch; an ordinary cross-morphology conditioned policy with full transfer learning (§23); and interface-based
transfer with limited adaptation. Record new trials to target, irrecoverable failures, task success, contact
impulse and energy. Freeze the target morphology and terrain before tuning. A successful transfer of a skill
segment does not certify the remainder of the mission.

- *Pilot before funding.* The tray interface between one wheeled carrier and one quadruped on the CPU referee,
  with the ramp-crest loss of authority planted. Falsifier: the interface arm needs as many new target trials as
  the conditioned-policy arm, or it reports coverage where the referee shows a fall.
- *Ceiling.* The published record for policy inheritance across single-limb topology edits is 33–100% of
  from-scratch steps (FACT, Appendix H: transfer results), so the saving on a changed-support transfer may be
  nil; the claim is localization of what must be retrained.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-TRANSPORT.

### 25.12 Physics across design families on CUDA and Metal [F]

A family query wants many related designs in one batch, not only many copies of one body. The batching mechanism
is Phylum (§17): per-world model variants inside padded capacities and size buckets. This section states what
Augur requires of it and what Augur may conclude from it.

**Packing.** A family is packed by joint topology, shared rigid subtrees, sensor graph, active contact mode and
layout. Continuous parameters live in columns; motion subspaces are shared constants where the family is
homogeneous.

**Segmenting.** A diverging batch is segmented at the first guard whose sign is ambiguous. Exact
common-subexpression sharing across designs is allowed only if all inputs and rounding rules agree. Reusing a
nearby trajectory needs its own error certificate:

- in smooth phases, a bound on the defect from a local tangent metric, a residual enclosure and the horizon;
- at a contact guard, a transversality margin, event-order separation, a bounded reset map and a bound on the
  induced change in event time;
- around grazing, redundant contacts or uncertain mode sequences, a fork to separate referee solves.

A small distance in design space alone gives no right to reuse a contact history. After any merge, public
timestamps and sensor histories are checked for each logical world.

**Placement.** Candidate screening and residual evaluation run in bulk. The two RTX 4090 take independent design
and scene shards; the Metal worker takes the same portable cell description under its precision profile. Each
native device completes the closed loop on its own before a hybrid profile may claim acceleration. Certificates,
sensor tensors and physics states are batched by residency. The checker queue runs on the CPU; its capacity and
transfer time count toward decision time. A fair comparator also caches graphs, factors and compiled kernels.

**The scaling limit.** If a fraction f of matched wall time is improvable by a factor r and the new method adds
relative overhead h, the speedup is at most

  1 / (1 − f + f/r + h)

under that serial cost model. Infinite acceleration of 85% of a workflow gives at most 6.7. With f = 0.97,
r = 40 and h = 0.02 the ceiling is about 13.5 (illustrative values, not measurements). The three terms are
measured on the strong baseline's critical path before any family kernel is written. Two rules follow. Cheap GPU
kernels on already-pruned cells are unlikely to decide anything. A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a GPU
speedup that was measured on work the pruning already removed. For overlapped queues the serial model does not
apply, and the actual critical path and the throughput of accepted outcomes are measured instead.

**What the native pilot showed** (FACT, §25.22). Two 65,536-row docking-boundary screens ran on the M5 Ultra and
on both RTX 4090. Guarded single precision gave identical decision bytes on each card, on a two-shard placement
and on Metal (18 of 18 digest comparisons) with no final error, at the price of an exact host fallback on every
row of the near-boundary grid and on 7,282 rows of the separated grid. An exact integer kernel, on Metal and on
one RTX 4090, classified every row on the device with no fallback. The host integer path was still the fastest
at that size: 23–35 µs on the Mac's CPU against 209–309 µs through Metal, of which 7–13 µs was GPU-active; and
0.42–0.78 ms on the Linux host's CPU against 0.95–1.66 ms on one card and 2.56–4.39 ms on two shards. Dispatch
and transfer dominate a small host-resident job. This says nothing about GPU-resident simulation. It does fix
two rules of this chapter: strict predicates on a device use the exact-integer class or a guard band with exact
fallback, and a family screen is placed where its data lives.

Kill rule carried into §39: time a real amended-mission workflow before writing a specialized CUDA or Metal
family solver; if the cost that cannot be removed already exceeds one tenth of the strongest baseline's time, a
ten-fold improvement requires changing another stage or changing the claim.

### 25.13 The continuous morphology family with an exact answer [S]

This fixture is specified completely so that it can serve as a known answer for `mission.synthesize`,
`mission.refute`, the certificate format and the checker.

**The mission.** A robot carries a 400 N vertical load on four point feet at (±a, ±b). Its centre of mass can
move through a rectangle with half-dimensions 150 mm (along x) and 75 mm (along y). Each foot can deliver any
independently commanded normal reaction from 0 to 160 N. The pose is fixed, the contacts are coplanar, and all
other wrench components are zero. The allocator observes the load and its position before it allocates. Given
half-length a = 1/3 m, how narrow can the robot be and still balance the entire continuous payload envelope?

**The answer.** Half-width b = 3/14 m, a foot-centre spacing of 3/7 m.

**Derivation.** Order the feet [++, +−, −+, −−] and write u = 1/a, v = 1/b. The payload envelope is the convex
hull of the four mission vertices (W, P_x, P_y) = (400, ±60, ±30), because 400 N × 0.15 m = 60 N·m and
400 N × 0.075 m = 30 N·m. Put r = (W + u·P_x)/2, the total force on the two +x feet, and s = (W + v·P_y)/2, the
total on the two +y feet. With t = f₊₊ the equilibrium solutions are the one-parameter family

  f = ( t,  r − t,  s − t,  W − r − s + t ),

and the vertex is feasible exactly when

  max{ 0, r − c₊₋, s − c₋₊, r + s − W }  ≤  min{ c₊₊, r, s, c₋₋ + r + s − W }.

At the vertex (400, 60, 30) with u = 3 and equal ratings of 160 N: r = 290, s = 200 + 15v, and the binding pair
is t ≥ r + s − W = 90 + 15v (from f₋₋ ≥ 0) against t ≤ 160 (the rating of the ++ foot). So the ++ foot must
carry at least 90 + 15/b N, and the vertex is feasible exactly when v ≤ 14/3, that is b ≥ 3/14 m. Reflections
cover the other three vertices, and for a fixed design the set of balanced wrenches is convex, so the vertices
cover the rectangle.

| Vertex P (N·m) | Forces [++, +−, −+, −−] at b = 3/14 m (N) |
|---|---|
| (60, 30) | (160, 130, 110, 0) |
| (60, −30) | (130, 160, 0, 110) |
| (−60, 30) | (110, 0, 160, 130) |
| (−60, −30) | (0, 110, 130, 160) |

- *Inner cell.* v ∈ [2, 14/3], with the affine witness (160, 130, 40 + 15v, 70 − 15v) at the vertex (60, 30)
  and its reflections; each component is affine in v, so checking the two ends of the cell checks the cell.
- *Excluded cell.* v ∈ (14/3, 6], by the dual y = (0, 1/2, 1/2) of §25.5.3. At b = 1/5 m the ++ foot would need
  165 N from a 160 N rating.
- *Boundary.* The boundary design has zero reserve on two constraints. A designer who wants continuous four-foot
  contact imposes a positive lower force and re-solves; an unreported force floor is a rejected certificate.

**Generalization.** Take a full-rank planar template with points qᵢ, actual feet pᵢ = A·qᵢ + d, total vertical
load W and spatial first moment P. Set B = A⁻¹ and e = −B·d. Then Σ fᵢ = W and Σ qᵢ·fᵢ = B·P + e·W. With
0 ≤ fᵢ ≤ cᵢ these are linear constraints in the inverse geometry (B, e), the ratings c and the vertex forces,
**when the mission vertices (W, P) are fixed independently of the design**, or when each transformed row has
separately been shown affine in the chosen variables. Two equivalent formulations:

- *Lifted.* One force vector per mission vertex, shared (B, e, c); a robust linear program. For the fixture: 22
  variables, 12 equalities, 16 inequalities.
- *Projected.* Write the wrench zonotope Σ [0, cᵢ]·gᵢ with generators gᵢ = (1, qᵢ). Every facet of a
  full-dimensional three-dimensional zonotope is parallel to two generators, so for each pair with a nonzero
  cross product n = g_j × g_k and each mission vertex w emit the pair of support inequalities
  Σᵢ min(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ) ≤ n·w ≤ Σᵢ max(0, cᵢ·n·gᵢ). At most 2·V·C(m, 2) rows suffice for m contacts and V load
  vertices. For the fixture: 6 variables and 32 distinct inequalities.

Convexity in inverse coordinates requires a suitable domain, such as positive bounded inverse lengths. General
lateral friction, contact switches and coupled torque limits need different owners (§18, §22). The method builds
on established static-equilibrium projection and actuation-aware feasible regions (FACT, Appendix H: Bretl and
Lall; Orsolino et al.).

**Where convexity fails.** A body mass that changes with the design breaks it even with a fixed centre of mass:
the midpoint counterexample of §25.4.7. Mass–geometry dependence is kept when CAD edits and payloads are lowered,
and a convex hull through two witnesses is not a certificate.

**Where authority fails.** At b = 1/4 m the ++ force may be anything in [150, 160] N; the midpoint allocation
(155, 135, 105, 5) N balances the vertex (60, 30). A passive suspension of four equal springs on rigid planar
ground adds the compatibility equation f₊₊ + f₋₋ = f₊₋ + f₋₊ and produces (175, 115, 85, 25) N, 15 N over the
rating, with every foot still loaded. The typed certificate therefore states whether the controller can realize
the assumed load sharing and which load observations arrive in time; the same body is inner under
`independent-normal-force` and excluded under `passive-equal-spring`.

**Validation grid.** u, v ∈ {2, 3, 4} per metre and four independent ratings in {0, 80, 160, 240} N give 2,304
bodies; the four mission vertices and their centroid give 11,520 body–load states. The exact answer: 2,254
feasible states, 114 bodies that cover the whole mission, and 1,596 bodies that a support-polygon-plus-total-
capacity screen accepts and the exact model rejects (first example: u = v = 2, ratings (80, 80, 80, 160) N,
vertex (60, 30), where t would need to be at least 180 N and at most 80 N).

**What the pilot showed** (FACT, §25.22). Two exact formulations agreed on 11,520 of 11,520 states; the checker
reconstructed all 2,254 feasible allocations; a conventional solver agreed on 102 of 102 spot checks; the lifted
robust program and the projected formulation each reached the optimum in one solve. The specialized formulation
therefore has no speed advantage, and ADR-AUGUR-03 follows: the lifted program is the first implementation, and
a specialized kernel is earned through repeated changed queries with real timings.

### 25.14 Probe and instrument design [F]

Two materials may produce the same entire force history at one pad radius and prefer opposite grippers at the
mission dwell. Fitting a more expressive curve at that radius cannot select the hardware. The question is not
"what are this object's physics" but "what is the cheapest realizable experiment that separates the design
decisions, or which two indistinguishable worlds require different decisions".

**The experiment grammar.** The studio has a grammar of experiments beside its grammar of bodies. An experiment
may change fixture compliance, pad radius, sensor position, command waveform, exposure time or permitted preload.
It is compiled through the same power, frame, observation and contact interfaces as a production body (§14, §18,
§20), so a probe is a small robot with a task. Each proposal returns three things: an executable simulated
experiment; a bill of fixture parts; and a measurement contract (what is read, when, with what error bound, and
which reading implies which decision) that a separate engineer could execute. Theory and precedent exist for
choosing tests among hypotheses that require different decisions, and for learning probing actions in simulation
(FACT, Appendix H: decision-region determination; active exploration for system identification). The bet here is
narrower: synthesize an instrument, check the decision it resolves, and reuse its calibrated result for a whole
family.

**The algorithm of `mission.distinguish`.**

1. Keep the set of physical mechanisms compatible with the archived observations and their uncertainty.
2. Partition the mechanisms by the design decision each implies.
3. If every mechanism implies the same decision, stop: identification is not needed.
4. Otherwise choose the least costly realizable intervention whose possible readings, over the admitted nuisance
   intervals and measurement error, form disjoint closed sets for every pair of mechanisms with opposite
   decisions.
5. Where observation sets still overlap, return two witness mechanisms, their opposing design margins, and a way
   to carry the uncertainty into the mission.
6. Always admit testing the target directly, at mission conditions, as a candidate.
7. If the observations fit no admitted mechanism, report a discrepancy; do not pick the nearest.

**`experiment.suggest`** is the scheduler's public face over the same machinery. Its inputs are competing
designs or unresolved cells, their uncertainty, the remaining budget and the allowed parameter changes. It
returns ranked information-gathering proposals (a probe, a measurement on the real part, a rerun at a higher
tier, a cell to split), each with a testable prediction per mechanism and its cost. Ranking uses decision
relevance, with `fs-voi` where its finite Gaussian menu fits (FACT: that is its present limit); a proposal on a
design that cannot change the decision is worth nothing. The ranking orders work; it never licenses a verdict.

**The fixture.** A gripper holds its closure displacement. A compact pad A has contact radius a₀ and initial
normal load 120 N. A broad pad B has radius 2a₀ and initial load 100 N. The Coulomb coefficient is 0.5 and the
supported tangential load is evaluated at the handover, T = 1 s. The normalized load relaxes as
R(r, t) = q + (1 − q)·exp(−t / (τ·f(r))) with radius ratio r = a/a₀, q ∈ [0.18, 0.22], τ ∈ [0.9, 1.1] s. Four
synthetic laws differ only in f:

| Law | f(r) | Meaning | Preferred pad at T |
|---|---|---|---|
| V | 1 | Intrinsic relaxation | Compact: 29.66 N against 24.72 N |
| P | r² | Relaxation time grows with the square of contact size | Broad: 41.15 N against 29.66 N |
| C | min(r, 1)² | Capped drainage length | Compact, as V for r ≥ 1 |
| D (withheld) | For r ≥ 1 the exponent is −[k·min(s, 1/4) + (1 + (1 − k)/3)·max(s − 1/4, 0)] with s = t/τ and k = 1/r²; for r < 1, as P | Follows P until s = 1/4, then reaches the V value at s = 1 | Compact |

The values are at q = 0.20, τ = 1 s. At r = 1 all four histories coincide for every q, τ and t, so a recording
at the compact radius, at any frame rate, cannot choose the pad. Contact size is a physically motivated
distinguishing variable (FACT, Appendix H: contact-size dependence in gel indentation); the laws themselves are
artificial and the loads are stipulated.

Probe candidates are r ∈ {1/4, 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 3} and t ∈ {1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2} s, with normalized read
error bounded by ±0.02 and cost 0.25 s of setup plus the exposure t. Each law is scored on 225 deterministic
cases (25 pairs of q and τ, three probe errors, three errors on the archived reading).

| Method | V | P | C | D | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archived trace alone | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | refuses 225 | 0 |
| Probe generated for {V, P}: r = 1/4 at 62.5 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 225 wrong | 0.3125 s |
| Probe generated for {V, P, C}: r = 2 at 125 ms | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Programmed expert, same laws, grid and intervals | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 wrong | 0.375 s |
| Direct: measure B at mission dwell, with the archived A reading | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 225 correct | 1.25 s |

For the three-law probe the readings lie in [0.8737, 0.9362] under V and C and in [0.9520, 0.9982] under P; the
nearest ends are 0.0158 apart after error and nuisance are included, so the separation is real inside the
admitted class.

**The lesson, which is normative.** A probe that is perfect in class can reverse on held-out cases: the
three-law probe was right in 675 of 675 admitted cases and wrong in 225 of 225 cases of the withheld law, whose
early trace is identical to P's. An in-family residual check cannot expose that alias, because the reading falls
inside the predicted interval. Therefore:

- a probe verdict is a **conditional** witness, and its condition (the admitted mechanism set) is in the
  certificate and in the answer's assumptions;
- a stopping rule derived from a probe is not used on an uncharacterized material;
- the direct measurement at mission conditions is always in the candidate set and is the stronger comparator;
  an early reading taken during the same stroke may be continued to the mission dwell, which preserves the audit
  trail and forfeits the exposure saving;
- generating a probe for a mechanism class that omits a member (the two-law probe on law C) is the same failure,
  one level down.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** With setup h, common validation cost v and engineering cost e per
decision, the ratio of direct to probe cost is (h + 1 + v + e_direct)/(h + 1/8 + v + e_probe). With equal
engineering it is at most 8 at zero overhead and 3.33 at the stipulated h = 0.25 s. Against the programmed
expert, who chooses the same schedule from the same intervals, it is 1. No ten-fold effect is supported.

**The family consequence.** Which combinations of pads can sustain a 75 N pull? If the contacts are independently
loaded and total pull is additive, a sum of per-pad lower bounds admits an assembly and a sum of upper bounds
excludes it. With compact and broad modules of 100 g and 120 g, at centred nominal readings (q = 0.20, τ = 1 s,
zero realized read error) the minimum certified mass is three compact pads (300 g, lower bound 85.37 N; the best
lighter assembly has upper bound 61.72 N) under V, and two broad pads (240 g, lower bound 80.30 N; the best
lighter assembly has upper bound 73.01 N) under P. Three ways this certificate fails, each a fixture:

- *Additivity.* A common pressure regulator or coupled deformation invalidates it. The first two-pad scene tries
  to break the assumption.
- *Wrench balance.* Two point pads at transverse positions ±s, no admissible contact couple, and a 75 N pull at
  offset e carry 37.5·(1 ± e/s) N. At e/s = 0.15 one pad needs 43.125 N, above the nominal broad P-law upper
  bound of about 42.152 N, although the two lower bounds sum to about 80.304 N. Centred and eccentric pulls are
  separate fixtures.
- *Nuisance.* An admitted low-force V observation (q = 0.18, τ = 0.9 s, compact read error −0.02) drops the
  three-compact lower bound to 73.79 N, below 75 N. The minimum-mass certificates hold at the centred nominal
  readings only, and say so.

Pilot: run (§25.22). Next experiment before funding the grammar: hide multi-pad spacing, actuator coupling, a
finite layer and friction variability in an independently implemented contact model; give both arms unseen
layouts, the same probe grammar and direct tests at mission dwell. Falsifier: a single false assertion of a
lower bound under cross-pad coupling, or generic interval propagation plus direct measurement matching the
authoring and validation cost. Kill gate: KILL-AUGUR-PROBE.

### 25.15 Buying information with early control inputs [S] as a fixture, [F] as a method

A first control input can be a probe: it changes what later observations reveal. Whether that helps depends on
when the observation arrives. The docking family is the first mission slice (M1, CPU only) because it is exact,
dynamic, causal, and small enough to check by hand.

**Model.** Measure position in metres and time in seconds. A robot docks in three one-second ticks from
x₀ = v₀ = 0, with acceleration g·u_k held constant over tick k, hidden fixed gain g ∈ {1, 2}, commands bounded by
abs(u_k) ≤ U, and target (x₃, v₃) = (1, 0). Exact zero-order-hold integration gives

  v₃ = g·(u₀ + u₁ + u₂),   x₃ = g·(5u₀/2 + 3u₁/2 + u₂/2).

The only informative packet is the first velocity reading v₁ = g·u₀. It arrives either before the second command
is decided, or after the third command is committed.

**On-time packet: a witness.** Choose u₀ = 1/4. The packet identifies g = 4·v₁. Then u₁ = 1/g − 1/2 and
u₂ = −u₀ − u₁. The sequences are (1/4, 1/2, −3/4) for g = 1 and (1/4, 0, −1/4) for g = 2, both within U = 3/4,
and both reach (1, 0) exactly.

**Late packet: an exclusion.** A causal controller must issue the same command vector in both worlds. The
terminal position for a common vector scales with g, so it cannot equal 1 for both gains. Private randomization
cannot give guaranteed success in both either. The same hardware and the same two plants admit opposite answers
to the robust mission solely because of the delivery time. This is an `alias-pair` certificate whose scope is
"all admitted policies".

**The exact sensor–actuator trade-off.** Let U ∈ [1/2, 1]. Give the early packet additive error in the closed
interval [−ε, +ε], with no later informative packet. Write p = u₀. The terminal equations force u₁ = 1/g − 2p
and u₂ = p − 1/g. Intersecting the actuator bounds for both worlds gives 1 − U ≤ p ≤ (U + 1/2)/2. At a reading
possible in both worlds the required second commands differ by 1/2, so the two reading sets, centred at p and
2p, must be disjoint: p > 2ε. Maximizing the separation gives the exact family

  ε < (U + 1/2)/4,   with equality excluded.

For U = 3/4 the probe p = 1/4 tolerates ε below 1/8; the probe p = 5/8 tolerates ε below 5/16, a tolerable
sensor error 2.5 times larger on the same actuator. At equality both worlds can report 15/16 while requiring
second commands −1/4 and −3/4. Physics, sensor and controller appear in one hardware design inequality, and
`mission.refute` at ε = 5/16 reports the binding constraint and the open repair threshold U > 3/4 (§25.3.6).

**The grid that checks the implementation.** 17 actuator bounds at spacing 1/32 and 33 error levels at spacing
1/64 give 561 cells: 344 admissible, 217 excluded, nine of them exact boundary cases. All 561 late-packet cells
are excluded.

**Hidden manufacturing tolerance reverses the verdict.** Keep the packet, the deadline and the exact target, and
admit one more gain, 33/32. At U = 3/4 and ε = 1/64 the reading (65/64)·p is possible for both g = 1 and
g = 33/32 for every first command, so both worlds receive the same remaining commands. Their final positions are
c and 33c/32, and the smallest achievable worst absolute error is 1/65 m (at c = 64/65), attainable with zero
final velocity. More generally, with bounded probes and ε > 0, a nondegenerate interval of hidden positive gains
contains an indistinguishable pair that requires different second commands, so an exact docking guarantee
fails; a finite position tolerance requires solving the amended game. The rule: **gain support, delivery
deadline, error units and target tolerance are bound to a classifier's eligibility.** A screen compiled for
{1, 2} is not reused on {1, 33/32, 2}; under that amendment every one of its positive labels is wrong.

**What the pilot showed** (FACT, §25.22). The two command sequences were checked in rational arithmetic. An
independently formulated scenario-tree comparator, built from the raw state transition, used two linear-program
extrema for the first command and one common-command feasibility program per actuator bound; those 51 solves
matched 561 of 561 cells, and three further solves checked the fixed-probe control. On the amended three-gain
mission all 34,590 nominally feasible labels on each 65,536-row native grid reverse. An ordinary observation-tree
robust planner given the same model, actions and latencies gets the same answer; there is no claim of a new
algorithm, and no product or device timing follows from a scalar model.

**The method beyond the fixture [F].** For a mission with a movable velocity sensor, a soft docking pad, or real
contact, Augur seeks the error and delay threshold at which one causal policy still works, records whether the
threshold is attained, and independently searches for an observation history consistent with two worlds that
defeats its last common action. Sensor processing, latency and actuator authority are charged to one budget. The
lowering is a finite observation game when the hidden set and the packet schedule are finite, and a `Tested`
campaign otherwise. Pilot before funding the general method: recover this family through the public verbs,
including the equality counterexample and the withdrawal under the three-gain amendment; the conventional
scenario-tree program is the baseline and may be the shipped implementation.

### 25.16 Proving that a replacement improves a decision when neither robot has a guarantee [F]

An engineer asks: can replacing this sole cause a formerly successful delivery to fail? The two success sets are
expensive to compute and may be mostly unknown. Ask directly for the relation.

**Definition.** For two fixed causal controllers, a regression is a shared world input in which A succeeds and B
fails. Build the product of the two systems driven by the same exogenous scene, payload and clock law, and ask
whether the terminal pair (A succeeds, B fails) = (1, 0) is reachable.

- Unreachability of (1, 0) over an enclosure of every admitted joint execution, with progress and nonempty
  executions, establishes dominance on the cell (`product-unreachable`).
- A concrete admissible paired trace that reaches (1, 0) is a counterexample (`trace-witness`).
- An outer enclosure that intersects (1, 0) triggers a witness search or an unresolved verdict.

**Joint coverage is checked explicitly.** The diagonal pairs (0, 0) and (1, 1) project onto both full marginal
outcome sets and hide (1, 0) when the two internal branch choices can vary independently. Only the declared
shared inputs and disturbances are coupled; every other admissible branch combination is retained for a
worst-case assertion. A learned controller retuned for each body needs its own information and budget contract
and is a different query.

**The hand-checkable example.** Let one measured surface parameter z range over [0, 1]. A completes when
z ≥ 3/5 and B when z ≥ 2/5. Each has both successful and failing worlds; neither has a guarantee. A separate
interval for each binary output allows all four pairs. The coupled constraint eliminates (1, 0): the replacement
loses no previous success and adds the interval [2/5, 3/5). Under a uniform law the gain is 1/5; without a law
the interval of newly covered surfaces is still an engineering answer. A relative zero-loss assertion does not
establish that either robot meets the absolute mission, so the verb emits the per-design absolute hard-limit
verdicts beside the paired verdict, with the required telemetry and energy accounting, even when the relation
was proved cheaply.

**Shared inputs are compiled at physical coordinates.** Two tyres following different paths over the same floor
need not receive equal friction samples at corresponding ticks. The product uses one space-indexed material
field and each robot's actual contact point. Shared latent lighting produces different sensor residuals once
the cameras diverge. This is where the product differs from pairing episodes by world address (§28): pairing by
address shares seeds, and a seed indexed by tick is not a field indexed by position. The checker confirms that
each projection of the product solves its original plant and that the common variables express the requested
physical comparison. The initial domain is split where a mode transition could break relational invariance, and
a numerical branch that cannot be enclosed goes to per-case replay.

**Challenger.** A conventional hybrid reachability tool on exactly the same coupled product with common inputs
and the same terminal monitor (FACT, Appendix H: Flow*). The proposed advantage is selective closure of decision
cells, reuse across amendments, and a robot-specific lowering that preserves spatially coupled disturbances.
Every assumption translation, uncertainty split and checker call is charged to both arms.

- *Pilot before funding.* One contact transition (a sole replacement on a single step edge) with a shared
  space-indexed friction field: close parameter boxes where every compatible branch pair has identical joint
  labels, split or replay the rest, and feed the labels to the frozen paired rule of §24. Falsifier: one
  mislabelled draw against per-case referee replay, or the conventional tool closing the same cells at similar
  cost.
- *Ceiling.* Bounded by the fraction of paired draws whose joint label can be closed without replay, measured on
  the pilot before any claim.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-DOMINANCE.

The stronger combined test, scheduled after both pilots: replace a sole, perturb the clock, and ask which
previous A/B conclusions can still be proved without rebuilding the contact and observation history (§25.8).

### 25.17 Designing the robot, the facility and the handoff together [M]

**The flagship query** is a brownfield logistics commission. An engineer provides a partial 3D survey of a
stockroom, a retail aisle and a commercial kitchen; a catalog of body modules; permissible room changes; pack
temperatures; and delivery windows. A shared doorway and an unloading counter are the bottlenecks. The query
asks for a Pareto set over body cost, facility-change cost, energy per delivered item, worst admitted delivery
time, and route availability after one module or charger drops out. The answer can be one modified carrier, two
simpler robots with a handoff, a small threshold retrofit, or a finding that every allowed alternative leaves a
mission unmet. A dry warehouse loading bay, a furnished apartment and sloping outdoor service terrain are
transfer tests with new geometry, measurements and holdouts.

1. **Compile a surveyed site into possible worlds.** Scans and CAD are registered to a gravity frame (§19).
   Clearance, door swing, movable racks, wheel–floor friction, lighting, clock uncertainty and material
   provenance are carried separately. An unseen gap beneath a shelf has possible geometry, not assumed geometry.
   A route depends on the swept body volume and turning radius including the payload. Sensor views follow the
   same reconstructed geometry and their own latency. The site model attaches actionable residuals: the one
   doorway width, or wet-surface measurement, on which two solutions disagree.
2. **Compose mission stages across robots.** Each approach, latch, carry, handoff and recharge exports entry and
   exit sets, occupancy intervals, available observations, resource budgets and load-wrench limits. Stages
   compose only if every promised exit lies in the next admitted entry under a compatible clock and the shared
   payload state (rule 4). Two robots cannot each spend the same reserve charge, use the same doorway during
   incompatible intervals, or both count the payload support. The timed discrete schedule is searched as a
   master problem; continuous subproblems on the referee and the batched tiers generate feasible witnesses and
   sound infeasibility cuts; a timed-out motion search leaves its candidate open. Each actuator has a named
   decision maker with its own history at commitment time.
3. **Give the optimizer a priced world-edit grammar.** A removable ramp, a sensor fiducial, a change of docking
   pose, a wheel or sole swap, a repositioned handoff surface, where the brief permits them, each with
   installation time, access constraints, floor availability, calibration, part tolerances and future
   maintenance (the retrofit catalog of §19). **Editing the floor and editing the robot enter the same budget.**
   The comparator receives the same actions and costs and conventional mixed-integer scheduling, motion-planning
   and robust-optimization tools. The answer is a lower bound plus feasible alternatives, the remaining
   optimality gap, and the intervention that removes it.
4. **Model operation over time.** Tyre and track wear, changing traction, motor thermal reserve, battery sag,
   cleaning cycles and load uncertainty are coupled only through versioned owner models (§15, §31). Shared
   disturbances vary across vehicles. One cold episode does not validate a shift: replay a hot restart and the
   loss of a charger at a handoff. Family claims stay conditional on calibrated duty-cycle ranges and on the
   specified recovery controller.
5. **Earn difficult matter one decision at a time.** A sloshing tote changes braking and handoff forces, a
   hanging cable changes allowable turns, a compliant carton changes allowable grip. Start with rigid ballast, a
   damped pendulum and a single compliant contact as independent reference cases (the rigid proxies of §19).
   Promote fluid, rod or textile coupling only where a held-out design ranking actually changes and comparison
   with measured or independently resolved motion improves.
6. **Return counterfactuals an engineer can act on.** Under each change budget: the cheapest repair when
   attained, or an infimum bound and the gap of an admitted candidate; the minimal obstruction for each excluded
   alternative; the smallest site survey that could settle a tie; and a dispatchable simulated acceptance
   mission for each survivor. After a door is narrowed or a gripper supplier changes the material,
   `mission.revalidate` reports which prior measurements, certificates and trained skills survive by their
   actual physical dependencies.

**The information fixture.** In a handoff, A sees the payload bit and B must choose its matching latch before
observing A. A pooled-history planner succeeds on both bits. Every deterministic local choice for B fails on
one. Delivering the bit to B by its commitment restores success. The certificate records channel capacity and
worst admitted delivery time, and the counterexample is checked with on-time and with late delivery.

**The acceptance fixture** begins with two carriers, one counter, one door, two material states and a single
handoff. The discrete schedules are enumerated, and the comparator uses the same strongest continuous
subsolvers. A complete reference verdict requires a feasible witness or a checked exclusion for every relevant
continuous subproblem, with optimality bounds for any minimum; unresolved cells are reported when the continuous
subsolver times out. Four failures are hidden: a protruding payload corner, a common loss of traction, a skewed
handoff clock, and a supply change. The studio must withdraw the affected family claims and export a minimized
failing scenario for each.

**Stop rule.** Expansion to a multi-room shift is funded only after the fixture discriminates at least two
feasible engineering strategies and recovers each seeded failure. If the fixture fails, the work reduces to the
failed subtask, and until that subtask is revalidated its uncertainty propagates through the handoff contract.

- *Ceiling.* Unknown, and stated as such: this is the largest scope in the plan and no pilot beyond the handoff
  bit and the shared-bus schedule has run. Nothing in M0–M7 depends on it.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-FACILITY.

### 25.18 How much a factory must tell a shared controller [F] on supplied sets, [M] from contact

A product engineer can pay to measure, store and transmit a calibration label for each manufactured variant. The
query asks for the minimum number of distinct labels that lets one controller serve all admitted variants and
payloads by a common commitment deadline. The information passed from manufacturing becomes a design variable
beside the mechanism and the sensor. A label that arrives after a decision is committed has zero value for it.

**The encoder premise comes first.** When the factory can identify the variant by the labelling deadline, the
colouring below computes what the controller needs. When the factory measures an unknown build, the query
requires an encoder from available station records to tags, and a decoder from the received tag and the field
observation to commands. Suppose builds A and B need opposite commands, and their possible station records are
{left, shared} and {shared, right}. Both can yield `shared`, so every encoder emits the same tag in that case,
and no number of tags guarantees success on both if the field history also agrees. An independently established
build identity, or another separating factory measurement, restores the premise; its cost, uncertainty and
delivery time are priced.

**The colouring.** For a fixed observation-identical prefix let K_s be the set of commands that work for every
admitted payload and tolerance of variant s. A set of variants can share a label exactly when their command sets
have a common point. Build a hypergraph on variants with an edge for each inclusion-minimal collection whose
intersection is empty. The least number of labels is the least number of colours with no monochromatic edge. A
colouring with one feasible command per colour certifies an upper bound; a checked impossibility of colouring
with one fewer proves minimality. For finitely many convex command sets in dimension m, Helly's theorem bounds a
minimal obstruction by m + 1 sets. Hidden contact modes can make the sets nonconvex and remove that shortcut. If
one variant has an empty command set on its own, no label makes the fixed-prefix mission feasible.

**One label map across histories.** The factory commits to one label map before the later history is known. For
finitely many exogenous public histories, collect the minimal conflicts at each history among the variants that
admit it, colour their union once, and return an action for every reachable (label, history) pair. With variants
A, B, C and three public readings that require opposite command bits for A and B at the first, B and C at the
second, C and A at the third, two labels suffice at each reading separately and a fixed map needs three. Both
counts are tested. For action-dependent histories the query is a causal policy tree with a common factory map
and shared-prefix decisions.

**Known answers.**

- *Pairwise compatibility is not enough.* Commands (x, y) ∈ [0, 1]². Three variants require x ≥ 2/3, y ≥ 2/3
  and x + y ≤ 1. Every pair intersects; the triple does not. Two labels suffice: (2/3, 2/3) for the first two
  and (0, 0) for the third. One label is impossible, since x + y ≥ 4/3 conflicts with x + y ≤ 1.
- *Deadlines change feasibility.* A one-axis placement with error e = u − bᵢ − q − δ, where the fixture offset
  bᵢ ∈ {−1/4, 0, 1/4} mm identifies the variant, the manufacturing offset q ∈ [−1/32, 1/32] mm is hidden, the
  payload term δ ∈ [−w, w] mm is hidden, and success is abs(e) ≤ 3/8 mm. The largest tolerable payload half-width
  w is 3/32, 7/32 and 11/32 mm with one, two and three labels delivered before commitment, and 3/32 mm for any
  number of labels delivered late or for one common action.

**What the pilots showed** (FACT, §25.22). An independent SMT encoding returned unsatisfiable for one shared
label and satisfiable for two, with its own two-label witness. On the deadline fixture, belief-window
intersection and symbolic elimination agreed on all five cases. So ordinary elimination already answers the
query on supplied sets, and no advantage over an SMT or mixed-integer baseline is claimed.

**The open problem is extraction.** The expensive step is obtaining K_s from a physical gripper across friction,
pose, deadline and compliant contact. For a real commissioning question only inner and outer command sets may be
known: a constructive common action needs inner sets, an impossibility needs inconsistent outer sets. The verb
returns a lower and an upper label count when they differ, and names the one measurement that would narrow the
gap. The first purchase decision is whether deriving command sets from a qualified gripper is cheaper than
testing or labelling every part directly.

- *Pilot before funding the [M] part.* One manufacturing fixture: derive inner and outer command sets for three
  gripper variants from the referee, compare the label interval with direct per-part testing at equal cost. The
  SMT or mixed-integer baseline receives the same command envelopes, their full derivation costs, and equal
  access to existing labels. Falsifier: derivation costs more than direct labelling, or an inner set contains a
  command the referee shows failing.
- *Ceiling.* The label count saved, times the per-label cost, minus derivation cost; unknown until the pilot.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-LABELS.

### 25.19 Using geometric phase to invent locomotion [F] on kinematic fixtures, [M] as an atlas

A wheeled snake or an admitted wheel–leg mechanism can move through cycles of internal shape even though its
shape ends where it started. The forward oracle exists already: the holonomy construction of §10 (C-08) and the
connection-curvature analysis A-09 (§28) rank morphologies by displacement per shape cycle. Augur turns the
oracle into an inverse query with a body edit in it: **find the least costly periodic shape change that gives a
specified displacement, has zero net yaw and fits a doorway; and if none exists, name the hardware change that
enables that direction of motion.**

**Setting.** Represent the pose by g ∈ SE(2) and the shape by r. On a qualified no-slip, fixed-rank,
quasi-static stratum, derive or learn a local connection g⁻¹·ġ = −A(r)·ṙ from the rolling constraints and the
force model. A change of coordinates changes the representation of A; reconstructing the same physical gait in a
different chart or body frame must predict the same physical motion after transforming back, and that is a test
(FACT, Appendix H: Hatton and Choset).

**Procedure.** Start with a mechanism with two shape variables and one contractible loop. Integrate the ordered
body twists on the group to obtain the holonomy. Use the curvature of the connection to propose small loops that
generate useful translation; retain group ordering for large loops. A wheel or foot arrangement that changes the
constraint distribution is a body edit in the same search, so the agent can ask which hardware change enables a
direction of motion at a given posture, and then ask for a finite example with actual control, thermal and
clearance limits.

**Limits that are part of the specification.**

- Contact-mode transitions require hybrid reset maps and fresh constraint-rank checks. A loop in shape space
  that crosses slip or lift-off cannot borrow its old connection.
- A four-wheel fixed-axis skid-steer turns by scrubbing. With fore and aft axles at ±a, exact lateral no-slip
  requires both v_y + a·ω = 0 and v_y − a·ω = 0, which forces ω = 0 for a > 0. Its yaw needs a separately
  qualified slip model, and the no-slip connection must refuse it.
- A Lie-bracket accessibility direction is infinitesimal and depends on the drift and the admissible inputs.
  With ẋ = u ∈ [0, 1] the rank is one and negative x is still unreachable from zero. A finite mission needs the
  integrated witness, not the rank.

**Known answer.** A two-wheel differential drive with a free caster, wheel radius ρ and half-track w, under ideal
rolling. Its shape variables are the two wheel angles. The square loop (left wheel forward by δ, right wheel
forward by δ, left wheel back by δ, right wheel back by δ) produces zero net yaw, zero net advance and a sideways
displacement of exactly 4w·(1 − cos θ) with θ = ρ·δ/(2w), which is ρ²·δ²/(2w) to leading order: the Lie bracket
of the two wheel fields. The reversed loop produces the opposite displacement.

**Reference cases.** A fixed steering configuration (no holonomy); a loop and its reverse; a joint-axis
reflection; a lateral-slip counterexample that must withdraw a gait. The comparison arm is direct collocation
and trajectory optimization over the same loops, in cycles per metre at the same actuator costs. Scores: the
computed group displacement, the ordered-cycle reversal residual, operating-region coverage, energy, and time to
recover a new gait after changing wheel spacing.

- *Pilot before funding.* The differential-drive loop and a three-link kinematic crawler on the CPU referee.
  Falsifier: the reversal residual or the chart-change residual exceeds tolerance, or trajectory optimization
  finds every proposed gait at lower total cost.
- *Funding rule for the atlas [M].* A shape-space atlas is funded only after an independent full referee rollout
  admits at least one newly proposed gait, and a changed-contact test withdraws a formerly valid one.
- *Ceiling.* Exact only for principally kinematic systems; for dynamic walking it is a diagnostic. Published
  full-order orbit solves take about 4–260 s of CPU at 20–68 states (FACT, Appendix H: orbit-library solve
  times), so a gait proposal is a screening signal.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-PHASE.

### 25.20 Computing with symmetry at the instant of contact [F] as a test, [M] as an amortization

A symmetry of the body (§10, C-09) saves work only while it commutes with the events of the hybrid dynamics. The
compiler checks that claim at an individual event. For a group element h, pre- and post-event states x⁻ and x⁺,
and a transversal event e, let S_e map perturbations across the event, including the change in its time (the
saltation matrix of the hybrid constructions in §10). A consistent relabelling of states, contacts and
parameters must give

  D h(x⁺) · S_e(x⁻)  =  S_{h·e}(h·x⁻) · D h(x⁻).

This is a commuting-diagram test with a physical observable.

**The test.** Reflect a four-foot landing and exchange the left and right sole laws. In the symmetric fixture,
transforming a one-sided perturbation before the impact or after it must predict the same change in tray roll
and ankle load. Repeat with an asymmetric payload, unequal packet latency, a grazing toe, and two simultaneous
contacts; each of these must break the identity or route to the rules below.

**Rules.**

- A small or uncertain guard-crossing speed makes a single-event saltation matrix unreliable; derivatives near
  grazing are labelled unsupported.
- A new event order makes the correspondence itself ambiguous. Preserve both continuations, split the symmetry
  class, or run full referee trajectories.
- A passing check transports a local derivative to a corresponding event, and no more. Reducing one nominal
  trajectory into symmetry blocks requires a symmetry that fixes its nominal state, history, parameters and
  event, or a separately checked intertwining relation between the admitted subspaces at that trajectory's
  endpoints.
- A sliding impact and its mirror can obey the identity while a normal-speed perturbation changes tangential
  speed at either impact. That cross term stays in the full derivative. Mirrored sliding events and an invariant
  sticking event are exercised separately.
- Finite performance claims still need finite changes and the complete operating envelope.
- A proof about fixed-shape gain changes cannot transport a derivative through the replacement of a wheel or a
  limb.
- Simultaneous touchdowns that are simultaneous by symmetry (trot, bound, pronk) are admitted, not refused; they
  are the case the identity is for.

**Use.** A change of symmetry may itself locate the physical decision boundary an engineer needs to inspect, so
a failed identity is reported as a finding with its event, not as an error.

- *Pilot before funding.* The identity in CPU reference coordinates with relocated one-sided finite differences
  on the mirrored four-foot landing. Falsifier: the identity holds on the asymmetric-payload mutation, or fails
  on the symmetric fixture beyond the finite-difference error.
- *The [M] question.* Whether checking the symmetry mask and event map once per family reduces the time to
  decide a design amendment on either GPU. The challenger gets an event-aware differentiable simulator, graph
  automorphisms, finite-difference fallback and the same sealed costs. Where mode bifurcations dominate, the
  event telemetry is kept and the assumption of amortized tangent work is retired.
- *Ceiling.* At most the group order on the tangent work of symmetric events, and tangent work is a fraction of
  decision time that is measured first.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-AUGUR-SYMEVENT.

### 25.21 Constructive lanes (`frob-synth`)

The lanes above decide. These propose. A design that comes out of `frob-synth` is a candidate with a label; it
enters I only through §25.4.5. Each lane is exposed as a strategy of `mission.synthesize`
(`--strategy codesign|refine|linkage|continuation`) and reports its candidates into the same answer.

Two small exact constructions ride on the same machinery and need no lane of their own.

- *Mass placement inside a chassis volume* [F]. Given a target pseudo-inertia and the CAD support of a body,
  find nonnegative masses on a finite basis of admissible sites that realize it; the placement is an `lp-primal`
  witness and infeasibility an `lp-farkas` exclusion. Known answer: mass 1 kg in a ball of radius 1/10 m with
  principal moments 1/125 kg·m² each passes positivity, the triangle inequalities and the per-axis bound
  I ≤ m·r², yet needs a mean squared radius of 3/250 m² where only 1/100 m² is available (a thin shell reaches
  1/150 kg·m²), so no placement exists.
- *Single-actuator-loss robustness* [F]. For each catalogued actuator removal, a rank screen on the remaining
  wrench map, then a bounded feasible allocation as a linear program. It answers the static half of "route
  availability after one module drops out" (§25.17); dynamic recovery needs more than rank and stays `Tested`.

#### 25.21.1 Monotone co-design [S] for catalog closure, [F] for Pareto antichains

**What it is.** Components are feasibility relations between a poset of delivered functionality and a poset of
required resources (§10, C-12; posets and antichains live in `frob-algebra`). Carrying a given payload for a
given shift requires motor torque, electrical supply and heat rejection; adding battery capacity adds mass that
flows back into the torque requirement. In the monotone admissible fragment this feedback is a least fixed point
over resource antichains, found by Kleene iteration x₀ = ⊥, x_{k+1} = f(x_k). The lane returns the antichain of
minimal resources (least mass, least power) that deliver the functionality, or a proof that the iteration
leaves the admitted resource bound, which is an exclusion of the catalog under the stated relations.

**Stated conditions.** The order on each poset; monotonicity and Scott continuity of each component map; the
bounds; the convergence criterion (a fixed antichain, or exit from the bound). On finite catalog chains
termination is guaranteed.

**Scope.** Actuator, gearbox, battery, compute and material selection (FACT, Appendix H: Censi; Zardini et al.
for component selection). Gait performance is not monotone in link lengths, so locomotion enters only through a
surrogate that comes from simulation, and a result that rests on such a surrogate is `Estimated`.

**Do not force the plant into a monotone budget model.** If a changed gearing trades a larger static moment for
a blocked swing, or introduces a contact mode, the possibilities are kept in separate cells or the original
mechanics are dispatched. Shared resources are joint and time-dependent: the two-finger bus of §25.4.7 has zero
feasible one-tick schedules and two feasible two-tick schedules with identical local motor limits, and it is
the negative control for any contract compiler. The research question is how much of a physically measured robot
and facility can share such contracts across edits, counting the cost to derive and independently validate them.

- *Known answers.* The mass-spiral closed form of worked card GB-03; the two-finger bus.
- *Pilot before funding the [F] part.* A 20-variant sweep of wheel radius, mass and gearbox against a
  dependency graph given the same checked envelopes and warm solves, tracing the whole feedback path from
  electrical limits to drive torque, normal load, traction and controller-visible state. Falsifier: any pruned
  design is independently feasible under the same mission; or the lane mis-ranks more than 20% of the top
  designs against the referee.
- *Ceiling.* Bounded by the share of the design decision that is component sizing; a conventional monotone
  co-design solver is the comparator and may be the implementation.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-SYNTH-CODESIGN.

#### 25.21.2 Trajectory optimization with design variables [F]

**What it is.** Local refinement of one design for one motion: a direct transcription whose decision variables
are states, controls and contact forces at knot points and a vector θ of continuous design parameters (link
lengths, attachment points, trunk shape, motor mass, gear ratio). Constraints are the dynamics defects, friction
cones, actuator envelopes from the cards (§15), joint limits and the mission's hard limits. The contact
schedule, the topology and the task are fixed in advance. Gradients with respect to θ come from the derived
kernels (§21). The solver is sequential quadratic programming or an augmented Lagrangian (FACT: `fs-ascent` has
both, with certificates of KKT residuals; `fs-adjoint` has an implicit-function-theorem module). No collocation,
multiple-shooting or differential-dynamic-programming trajectory optimizer was found in a search of `fs-opt`,
`fs-ascent`, `fs-control` and `fs-adjoint` (FACT-to-recheck at the pinned commit), so the transcription is new
work here, on the CPU.

**Its published scale** (FACT, Appendix H; to be re-read). On a 12-degree-of-freedom quadruped with a design
vector of 4 to 17 parameters, the worst observed solve was 252 s against 600 s for a covariance-matrix evolution
strategy on 8 threads, and cost improvements held in simulation within 10% of those found in optimization
(Dinev et al.). A two-joint monoped took about 9 hours for 10³ trajectory problems at 33.72 s per solver call
(Fadini et al.). The surviving claim is therefore: about 12 degrees of freedom, up to 17 continuous parameters,
a fixed contact schedule, 4 to 10 minutes on 8 CPU threads.

**What it is not.** It is not a design search, a controller, or evidence. The optimized plan assumes rigid
contact and ideal actuators unless the cards say otherwise. No source read reports the fraction of optimized
designs that pass an independent closed-loop evaluation; that fraction is the deciding number and is
unmeasured. A refined design therefore leaves the lane labelled `candidate`, with its nominal trajectory and
its sensitivity to each parameter, and goes to the fidelity ladder of §28.

- *Pilot before funding.* With an existing external optimizer, outside the product, generate 20 candidate
  designs for three missions (trot at 1 m/s, a 0.3 m step, a 5 kg payload) on a 12-degree-of-freedom quadruped,
  each within 10 minutes on 8 threads; evaluate each on the referee route of §24 against the nominal design on
  held-out terrain. Falsifier: more than 10 of 20 fail or do not beat nominal, or solves exceed the budget.
  Cost: about three engineer-days and 10 GPU-hours (TARGET).
- *Ceiling.* It refines; it does not decide more designs per turn. An existing optimizer over an existing
  dynamics library does this today, so the lane is parity, not an advantage.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-SYNTH-TRAJOPT.

#### 25.21.3 Bounded exact linkage synthesis [F]

**What it is.** Constructive design of parallel legs, ankles, steering linkages and small closed chains from
precision poses, in exact arithmetic, inside the real limits of the symbolic sibling. FACT
(`frankensympy/crates/fsym-solvers/src/real_rur.rs`, `fsym-polys/src/groebner.rs`): the real solver is capped at
8 variables, 64 equations, 4,096 terms, total degree 256, 4,096-bit coefficients, 2 × 10⁸ work units and
512 MiB, for zero-dimensional ideals only; Gröbner bases are capped at 1,024 basis polynomials and 10⁶ pending
pairs; there is no homotopy solver. Unbounded whole-robot Gröbner synthesis is therefore not built.

**Three steps.**

1. *Three-pose planar dyads.* Given three positions P₁, P₂, P₃ of a moving pivot in rational coordinates,
   eliminate the crank radius from ‖Pᵢ − O‖² = ‖P₁ − O‖²: the fixed pivot O solves the rational 2×2 system
   2·(Pᵢ − P₁)·O = ‖Pᵢ‖² − ‖P₁‖², i = 2, 3. Degenerate cases split by determinant rank. With a lug-pair catalog
   and pivot keep-in regions this enumerates dyads exactly.
2. *Finite higher-order templates.* Five-pose Burmester problems and planar four- and five-bar templates are
   posed as loop-closure polynomials in half-angle charts, with a Rabinowitsch saturation s·δ(x) − 1 = 0 to
   exclude zero-length and collinear roots. Only zero-dimensional systems inside the caps are submitted; a
   system outside them is refused (`FROB-REF-857`), not truncated.
3. *Circuit, branch and clearance certificate.* Real roots are isolated by interval contraction. One assembly
   circuit must traverse all target poses in order, with the reciprocal of the loop-closure Jacobian determinant
   bounded along the way, no self-collision, and the required reach. A tolerance box around the dimensions
   carries the certificate to as-built units.

The algebra is textbook; the certificate is the value. Step 3 exists because **a root of the synthesis equations
is not a mechanism.** Take ground pivots O₂ = (0, 0) and O₄ = (4, 0), crank O₂A of length 1, coupler AB of
length 4 and rocker O₄B of length 3: a crank-rocker. The distance from A to O₄ ranges over [3, 5] and never
reaches 1 or 7, so A, B and O₄ are never collinear and the sign of (B − A) × (O₄ − A) is constant on each
assembly circuit. At crank angle 0 the closure equations give B = (11/3, ±√80/3); at crank angle π they give
B = (11/5, ±12/5). A design whose precision poses are (crank 0, B above) and (crank π, B below) satisfies every
synthesis equation and cannot be driven from one pose to the other without disassembly. The circuit certificate
is the constant nonzero sign, proved over the crank interval by enclosure.

**Output.** A linkage as a `.fmodule` (§14) with its port contract, plus certificates of kinds `interval-box`
and `partition` for the circuit, branch, clearance and tolerance claims.

- *Known answers.* P₁ = (0, 0), P₂ = (2, 0), P₃ = (0, 2) give O = (1, 1) and radius² = 2; collinear positions
  give determinant zero and a refusal; five finitely separated poses give at most four real Burmester pairs
  (FACT, classical; Appendix H); the crank-rocker circuit defect above.
- *Pilot before funding.* 240 template instances (three-pose dyads with a lug catalog and keep-in regions),
  comparing the lane with multi-start numerical optimization of the same dimensions under the same circuit and
  clearance checks. Falsifier: a certified linkage binds or changes circuit on the referee; or more than a
  quarter of the template instances fall outside the caps.
- *Ceiling.* Three-position synthesis has closed-form circumcentres, so no speed claim is made against a
  competent baseline; the claim is the certificate.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-SYNTH-LINKAGE.

#### 25.21.4 Numerical continuation [M], on the CPU

**What it is.** For mechanism families whose synthesis or kinematics systems exceed the exact caps (four- and
six-bar path synthesis, Stewart–Gough forward kinematics, 6R inverse kinematics, parallel ankles), solve one
generic complex instance of the family once, then treat each new specification as a parameter homotopy
H(z, t) = F(z; (1 − t)·p₀ + t·p₁) and track only the solution paths.

**Decisions inside the lane.**

- *CPU, double precision.* Path tracking wants `f64`; Metal has no double precision; the GPU is not its home.
  An adaptive predictor–corrector with endgames, and recovery of ill-conditioned paths in extended precision.
- *Start systems by monodromy with a trace test*, not by a polyhedral start system, whose one-time cost for
  large families is unpriced. The generic solve is a ledgered, cached artifact with its own budget.
- *Certification outside the proof kernel.* Each claimed root gets a Smale α-test,
  α(f, z) < (13 − 3√17)/4 ≈ 0.157671, evaluated in exact rational or interval arithmetic by the checker:
  existence of a unique root nearby, distinctness by disjoint balls, reality by conjugate-ball disjointness. An
  α-test proves a nearby nonsingular root and nothing about roots that were not found.
- *Labels.* `Complete` only when the certified count equals an independent generic count from a trace test, with
  no lost paths and no positive-dimensional components. Otherwise `Partial(certified_n, lost_n)`. A `Partial`
  result can support inclusion of the designs it found and can never support an exclusion.

**Scale of the problem** (FACT, Appendix H; to be re-read). The nine-point four-bar path-synthesis problem has
1,442 cognate triples, 4,326 solutions, against 286,720 paths for the best multihomogeneous homotopy; a six-bar
case in the literature needed a solution set of about 1.5 million; Stewart–Gough forward kinematics has 40
solutions and 6R inverse kinematics 16.

- *Pilot before funding.* Stewart–Gough forward kinematics on the CPU: recover 40 certified roots for ten
  generic parameter sets by parameter homotopy from one monodromy solve. Falsifier: any lost path that the trace
  test does not flag, or a `Complete` label on a run that missed a root.
- *Ceiling.* It answers "all isolated solutions" for one family at a time; nothing on the critical path needs
  it, and random mutation plus the exact lane covers the plan's reference robots.
- *Kill gate.* KILL-SYNTH-CONT.

### 25.23 Delivery order and stop decisions

The aim is to find the first class of amended engineering decisions where the whole workflow can be an order of
magnitude faster, starting where correctness is independently decidable and extending to high-dimensional
contact, vision and learning only after a cost has been located that survives strong baselines.

| Order | Milestone | Working slice | Evidence that buys the next investment | Decision if the test fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | M1 | A fresh agent is given the docking mission. Through the CLI or MCP it changes the allowed motor authority, the read error and the delivery tick, and asks for the feasible sensor–actuator region | `frob-verify` reproduces the boundary ε < (U + 1/2)/4, finds the exact shared reading at equality, and accepts an ordinary scenario-tree program as a competing answer. The agent explains which hardware restriction to relax | Repair the mapping from sensors and actuators to the game before adding physics. Ship the reference algorithm if it gives the better answer per engineering hour |
| 2 | M1–M3 | Amend the stance, material and load-sharing assumptions of §25.13–§25.14 through one schema | One edit preserves a valid witness; a second forces withdrawal; a third changes the required instrument. All three match independent full recomputation | If uncertainty in the physical contract dominates, spend the next increment on measurement, or settle for a narrower envelope |
| 3 | M3 | Seek a decision in hybrid contact that the cheap model gets wrong, starting from a dry and split-grip stance, one periodic gait and a rear-wheel pull-over | A paired world or an edited body reverses a design decision for a reproducible physical reason and breaks an overbroad proof. A refined controller or body recovers under the named conditions | If scene reduction changes the mechanism, or collocation wins, keep the counterexample and advance the conventional control path |
| 4 | M4 | Time source-to-verdict amendments on heterogeneous bodies with sensing, controller updates, contact and independent checking | The matched rival spends enough time on repeatable work for ten-fold to remain possible. A breakdown by stage locates the saving. Both native backends agree on accepted outcomes for the tested profile | Stop specialized GPU work in cells where transfers, authoring, training, checker work or baseline reuse rule out the target |
| 5 | M8 | Return an engineer's Pareto set for the two-carrier brownfield handoff, then commission the multi-room shift | A modified site, a modified body and a relay policy are compared under one mission. Withheld evidence overturns or supports a named prediction. Speed and physical claims are reported separately | Reduce to the failed subtask; until it is revalidated its uncertainty propagates through the handoff contract |

**The first slice an implementer can finish.** Two gains, two packet timings, one actuator bound, one
observation-noise family. Translate the query to a conventional scenario-tree linear program; export the exact
success region and the equality counterexample; check both outside the process; mutate the packet timestamp and
rerun. The compulsory interfaces are a causal history, a feasible controller, two indistinguishable worlds, an
exact physical parameter chart, and an independent checker. The verb returns what additional measurement or
actuator authority would restore feasibility. This ships as a CPU worked example before the first complex GPU
kernel exists, and it is the M1 exit demonstration for this chapter.

**The second slice, which could reveal a ten-fold opportunity.** Change one measured surface law in a warehouse
with multiple routes, body variants and sensor mounts. Compile the exact dependency support of each accepted
result (§25.8). Prove that an edit disjoint from a result's support preserves it, or recheck the precise
obligation where the changed interval still lies within slack. A conventional dependency graph, an incremental
optimizer and a hot-started simulator are eligible comparators, and their fastest correct implementation sets
the bar.

**A release cell names its capability and its limit**, for example "exact static family with independent active
vertical reactions", "time-bounded sensor game certificate", or "empirical mobile-manipulator ranking in the
sealed kitchen setup". No cell is described more broadly than the fixture that qualified it.
