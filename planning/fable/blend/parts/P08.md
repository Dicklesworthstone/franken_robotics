## 24. Arena: tasks, requirements, fixtures, metrics, qualification

Crates `frob-spec` and `frob-arena`, both in layer 4.

### 24.1 What Arena owns

Arena defines what a robot is asked to do and how an answer is scored. It owns the task families, the task file
`.ftask`, the requirement language and its monitors, the mission-level verdict, the fixtures whose answers are known
before any run, the metrics and scorecards, the batteries and the design corpus, the two study types, and the blinded
qualification lane. `frob-spec` holds the language: parser, unit and clock checker, normalizer, monitor compiler and
the verdict type. `frob-arena` holds the registry of tasks and fixtures, the metric definitions, scorecards, study
plans and the qualification service.

Arena does not own the machinery it scores with. The boundaries are fixed here so that no other chapter has to guess.

A note on numbers. A value in this chapter that follows from a stated formula is a derived value: it is a known answer
of §24.16 and is recomputed by its test. Every other number carries its label where it appears.

| Concern | Owner | What Arena contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Observation, action and reward terms; reset and bootstrap semantics | Cortex (§23) | The task file carries the terms. Arena never reads a reward to decide a verdict |
| Mission queries with quantifiers over designs, policies and worlds | Augur (§25) | The acceptance predicate of a mission query is a requirement of this chapter |
| Search for worlds that break a design; rare-failure bounds | Nemesis (§26) | The falsification score is the requirement margin; infeasible worlds are counted here |
| Attribution of a failure | Autopsy (§27) | The outcome that an intervention flips is a verdict of this chapter |
| Paired comparison, racing, the ladder of rungs, promotion between tiers | Atelier (§28), Kinesis (§16) | The study types and the confirmation lane wrap those mechanisms |
| Telemetry channels, snapshots, the failure taxonomy | Chronicle (§29) | Requirements read channels by name, kind and clock |
| Scene splits and held-out scene families | Habitat (§19) | Arena enforces the split at scoring time (GATE-ARENA-02) |

### 24.2 Decisions

- **ADR-ARENA-01. Requirements decide acceptance; reward terms train.** [S] A mission is accepted or rejected by a
  requirement that returns a verdict and a margin. Reward terms remain the training signal. Reason: as an evaluation
  metric a requirement is close to definitional, a pass or a fail with a distance to the boundary. As the only
  training signal the margin is the weak baseline in the field's own results. FACT (Appendix H, temporal-logic
  reinforcement learning): decomposing a formula into timed subgoals and invariant constraints raised task success by
  an average of 31.6% over the best baseline, in a field where earlier methods used margins as sparse terminal
  rewards; and margin-only optimizers reached between 6.67% and 29.9% success in a study of robustness metrics for
  robot tasks.
- **ADR-ARENA-02. Bounded time only.** [S] Every temporal operator carries a finite window. Reason: a monitor needs
  fixed memory, a verdict needs a finite horizon, and formulas with unbounded time admit no learning guarantee except
  where they are decidable within a finite horizon (FACT, Appendix H).
- **ADR-ARENA-03. Every atom is normalized by a declared scale.** [S] An atom states an inequality and the physical
  scale that makes its margin dimensionless. Reason: the minimum of a clearance in metres and a thermal margin in
  kelvins has no meaning, and the composition rules of the language are minima and maxima.
- **ADR-ARENA-04. Past time online, future time offline.** [S] Operators that look backwards are computed inside the
  step and are available as signals. Operators that look forwards are evaluated on a completed trace and are the
  verdict. Reason: an online value of a future-time operator is a silently truncated window.
- **ADR-ARENA-05. The declared comparator decides at the boundary, in exact arithmetic.** [S] The sign of a
  floating-point margin does not decide a case that sits on the boundary. Reason: a strict and a non-strict inequality
  differ exactly there, and single precision misclassifies near-boundary cases in bulk (§24.5).
- **ADR-ARENA-06. Clocks are explicit and rational.** [S] Every signal and every requirement names its clock. Periods
  are rational numbers of seconds with a phase. Reason: sensor, policy and physics rates are not integer multiples of
  each other in real robots, and a requirement evaluated on an unstated clock is a different requirement on every
  backend.
- **ADR-ARENA-07. A study is anytime-valid or fixed-n, never both.** [S] Reason: each kind of inference is valid only
  under its own stopping rule (§24.12).
- **ADR-ARENA-08. Discovery and confirmation are separate lanes with separate data rights.** [S] Reason: an optimizing
  agent that may resubmit to the same held-out worlds will eventually pass by chance (§24.13).
- **ADR-ARENA-09. An infeasible scenario is kept and counted.** [S] Reason: a failure is a failed cell, never an
  excluded cell (§8), and a suite that sheds its impossible cases reports a success rate over a population nobody
  declared.
- **ADR-ARENA-10. A fixture is complete before its first run.** [S] Every mass, constant, seed and rule of a fixture
  is recorded and hashed before either design runs, with a derivation on file for each analytic answer. Reason: a
  fixture whose constants can move after the outcome is seen is a tuning harness.

### 24.4 The task file `.ftask` [S]

Format FMT-FTASK, schema `frob.task/1`. DESIGN: the canonical form is JSON, and its BLAKE3 hash is the `TaskId`; an
S-expression view is lossless and optional, as for designs (§14). A task is data. It has nine sections.

| Section | Contents | Semantics defined in |
|---|---|---|
| `interface` | Required ports; observation and action layouts with units, frames and rates | §23 |
| `clocks` | Named clocks, each a rational period in seconds and a phase | §24.5, §20 |
| `terms` | Observation, action, reward and termination terms, each with unit, noise, delay and the representation under which it transforms | §23 |
| `signals` | The telemetry channels that requirements read: name, unit, channel kind, clock | §29 |
| `requirements` | Atoms with scales, online signals, the verdict formula, labelled examples | §24.5 |
| `randomization` | Draws, each traced to an uncertainty on a card and bound to a named random stream | §15, §23 |
| `reset` | The settled-pose cache specification | §24.7 |
| `world` | Scene family or scene set, with its split | §19 |
| `study` | Default study type, effect margin and budget | §24.12 |

The requirement section in the S-expression view has this grammar. The JSON form is the same tree.

```
Requirements ::= "(requirements" Atom+ Online* Verdict Example* ")"
Atom      ::= "(atom" Ident Cmp (":scale" Qty | ":exact") ")"
Cmp       ::= "(" (">" | ">=" | "<" | "<=" | "=") Expr Expr ")"
Expr      ::= Signal | Qty | "(" ("+" | "-" | "*" | "min" | "max" | "abs") Expr+ ")" | "(delay" Qty Signal ")"
Past      ::= Ident | "(and" Past+ ")" | "(or" Past+ ")" | "(not" Past ")"
            | "(H" Win Past ")" | "(O" Win Past ")" | "(S" Win Past Past ")"
Future    ::= Past | "(and" Future+ ")" | "(or" Future+ ")" | "(not" Future ")"
            | "(G" Win Future ")" | "(F" Win Future ")" | "(U" Win Future Future ")"
Win       ::= "(" Qty Qty (":round" ("inward" | "outward"))? ")"
Online    ::= "(online" Ident Past ")"
Verdict   ::= "(verdict" Ident Future ")"
Example   ::= "(example" TraceRef ("satisfied" | "violated") ")"
```

`H`, `O` and `S` are the past-time operators historically, once and since. `G`, `F` and `U` are the future-time
operators always, eventually and until. A window is a closed interval of simulated time.

### 24.5 The requirement language [S]

**Signals and atoms.** A signal is a telemetry channel with a unit, a channel kind and a clock (§29). An atom is a
comparison of two expressions of the same dimension, together with a scale s of that dimension. The unit checker
rejects an expression that adds or compares different dimensions and an atom that has no scale. An atom over an
integer-valued signal, such as a count of chassis strikes, is declared `:exact` and has margin +1 or −1.

**Margins.** Every formula has a Boolean value and a margin ρ, a dimensionless number whose sign agrees with the
Boolean value away from the boundary. Time below is the tick index of the requirement's clock.

| Formula at tick t | Margin ρ |
|---|---|
| Atom with expression e ≥ 0 or e > 0 and scale s | e(t) / s |
| ¬φ | −ρ(φ, t) |
| φ ∧ ψ ; φ ∨ ψ | min ; max of the two margins |
| G_[a,b] φ ; F_[a,b] φ | min ; max of ρ(φ, t′) over t′ in [t + a, t + b] |
| φ U_[a,b] ψ | max over t′ in [t + a, t + b] of min( ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) over t″ in [t, t′) ) |
| H_[a,b] φ ; O_[a,b] φ | min ; max of ρ(φ, t′) over t′ in [t − b, t − a] |
| φ S_[a,b] ψ | max over t′ in [t − b, t − a] of min( ρ(ψ, t′), min of ρ(φ, t″) over t″ in (t′, t] ) |

Two properties are tested as laws (PO-ARENA-04): ρ > 0 implies the formula holds, and ρ < 0 implies it fails. At ρ = 0
the margin decides nothing.

Counterexample for composition without scales. A clearance margin of 0.02 m and a thermal margin of 3 K compose to a
raw minimum of 0.02, so clearance binds. Express the clearance in millimetres and the raw minimum is 3, so the thermal
margin binds. With scales of 0.10 m and 10 K the margins are 0.2 and 0.3 in either unit system, and clearance binds in
both. The binding atom of a requirement is what an agent acts on, so it must not depend on the unit system (the
metamorphic relation on unit scaling, §38).

**Clocks.** DESIGN: a clock is a rational period p/q seconds and a phase, advanced by an exact accumulator (§20). A
task names its clocks, for example physics at 1/500 s, an inertial sensor at 1/500 s, a policy at 1/50 s, a lidar at
1/10 s and a camera at 1/30 s; the camera is not an integer multiple of the physics period and needs no special case.
Each requirement names the clock it is evaluated on. Three rules remove implicit resampling.

1. A signal on a faster clock enters a requirement through a declared reduction over each requirement tick: `last`,
   `min` or `max`. Channels of kind interval extremum, such as the minimum clearance swept during a step, are reduced
   by the matching extremum. A missing declaration is refused (FROB-REF-525).
2. A signal on a slower clock is held, and its sample age is itself a signal that a requirement may bound. A
   requirement about what the robot knew reads delivered packets with their timestamps from Sensorium; a requirement
   about what happened reads simulation state at committed physics ticks. The two are different signals with different
   names.
3. A window [a, b] denotes the ticks j with a ≤ j·T ≤ b, compared in exact rational arithmetic. A bound that is not a
   multiple of the period T is refused unless the window declares outward or inward rounding (FROB-REF-538). Ticks
   that coincide with another clock's boundary follow the ordering rule of §20.

A verdict on a sampled trace says nothing about the time between samples. A requirement on a state-kind channel is
therefore labelled `sampled` in its verdict, and one on an interval-extremum channel is labelled `interval`.

**Online evaluation.** [S] Past-time formulas compile into kernels that run in the last stage of the batched step
(§16), beside observations and rewards. The kernel for a sliding minimum or maximum over a window of W ticks uses the
block decomposition that keeps a prefix and a suffix extremum per block of W samples: fixed memory of 2·W values per
operator node and world, at most three comparisons per sample, and no data-dependent control flow, which the lockstep
rule of §16 requires. The referee uses the monotonic-deque formulation of the same function. DESIGN: minimum, maximum
and negation are exact in floating point, so both formulations return identical bits for identical atom values on
every backend, after negative zero is canonicalized and a NaN marks the world `Faulted`. The capacity of an online
window is 4,096 requirement ticks (TARGET); a longer window is offline only (FROB-REF-528). An online signal has three
uses: an input to early termination, a score for splitting in §26, and an optional added reward term (§24.6).

**Offline evaluation.** [S] The verdict formula is evaluated on the completed trace by a two-pass evaluator on the
CPU. The reach of a formula, the sum of its nested window ends, is computed statically; a task whose episode horizon
is shorter than the reach of its verdict is refused at admission (FROB-REF-526). A window that the recorded trace does
not cover, because the episode was truncated or samples are missing, yields `undetermined` with the reason, never a
value from the part that exists (INV-ARENA-02). A verdict whose truth is already fixed by a prefix, as when an
always-clause is violated or an until-clause is met, is reported as decided at the tick that fixed it. That is how an
environment ends an episode on success or on a hard violation without truncating a window.

**The boundary.** [S] DESIGN: an atom is decided on the device only when its normalized margin lies outside a guard
band of 2⁻²¹ (TARGET) around zero. A world with a verdict atom inside the band is flagged `boundary` and that atom is
re-evaluated on the CPU with its declared comparator, in exact rational or integer arithmetic where the atom's inputs
belong to the exact-integer kernel class of §35 and in double precision otherwise. FACT (planning archive,
boundary-classifier runs on this Mac's Metal GPU and on both RTX 4090 cards; Appendix H): on a dense 65,536-case
boundary screen, unguarded single-precision strict comparison gave 452 false admissions and 23,438 false exclusions;
with a 2⁻²¹ guard and an exact fallback there were no final errors; an exact 32-bit integer kernel classified all
65,536 cases correctly with identical bytes on every device. The exact scalar docking mission of §25 supplies the
smallest case in which the comparator matters: at command limit U = 3/4 and read error ε = 5/16 the separation between
the two worlds' readings is exactly zero. The strict requirement correctly fails. A non-strict comparator would admit
a mission that cannot be completed, because both worlds can then report 15/16 while needing different next commands.

**The verdict.** The mission-level verdict is an object with schema `frob.verdict/1`, stored with the episode in
`.frun` and summarized in the ledger. It is the unit that scorecards, studies and searches count.

```json
{ "schema": "frob.verdict/1", "task": "t:cart-sill-dock@6be1…", "requirement": "docked",
  "world": {"run_seed": 17, "world": 41, "episode": 0}, "model": "m:9f2c…", "controller": "c:5e02…",
  "verdict": "violated", "margin": -0.31, "binding_atom": "cleared", "binding_time_s": "8",
  "decided_at_s": "8", "reason": "stuck", "coverage": {"kind": "interval", "missing_samples": 0},
  "boundary": {"flagged": false, "guard": "2^-21"}, "atoms_min": {"cleared": -0.31, "upright": 0.84},
  "evidence": {"class": "Tested", "tier": "batch-cpu", "determinism": "D2", "standing": "provisional"},
  "authorization": {"execution_authorized": false, "hardware_safety_signoff": false,
                       "sim_to_real_certified": false},
  "trace_digest": "b3:77a0…" }
```

A verdict takes one of four values: `satisfied`, `violated`, `undetermined` and `invalid`. `invalid` records a
numerical fault; it is a study error, excluded from policy updates and retained in every benchmark and failure count.
The verdict is a pure function of the trace and the `TaskId` (INV-ARENA-01).

**Stages.** A mission with several stages is one requirement over one continuous episode, with the stages chained by
until-clauses. Concatenating separately recorded stage episodes proves nothing about the mission, because the end
state of one need not lie in the entry set of the next. Stage pass rates are reported jointly. Counterexample: among
20 equally likely worlds two stages each pass in 19. If the failing worlds differ, 18 of 20 missions pass; if they
coincide, 19 of 20; the product 0.95² = 0.9025 is neither.

**Checking a requirement someone wrote.** [S] FACT (Appendix H): a 2023 language model translated natural-language
statements into temporal-logic formulas with 77.7% accuracy over 300 samples. A requirement is therefore admitted only
with labelled example traces: at least two satisfying and two violating, with one near the boundary of each comparator
(DESIGN). `task.check` runs the monitors on the examples and refuses a requirement whose labels disagree
(FROB-REF-539). It also runs a mutation check: reversing a comparator or deleting an atom must change the label of at
least one example. An atom that no example can distinguish is reported as vacuous, and a vacuous hard-limit atom is a
refusal.

### 24.6 Requirements and rewards

| Use | What is read | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptance of an episode | The offline verdict and its margin | Arena |
| Falsification score | The minimum margin of the verdict over an episode, minimized over worlds | Nemesis (§26) |
| Splitting score for rare failures | An online past-time margin | Nemesis (§26) |
| Outcome flipped by an intervention | The verdict | Autopsy (§27) |
| Early termination | The decided-early flag of the verdict; hard-limit atoms | Cortex (§23) |
| Training signal | Reward terms, integrated over simulated time | Cortex (§23) |
| Optional added reward term | An online past-time margin, or timed subgoals and invariants derived from the verdict formula | Cortex (§23), [F] |
| Runtime monitors | Hard-limit atoms shared with the shields | Reflex (§22) |

Hard limits are frozen separately from shaping rewards. A curriculum may change training distributions and reward
weights; it may not change an atom, a scale or a window, because that changes the `TaskId`. The added reward term is
off by default. It is a Frontier option with a pilot and a kill gate (§24.17). The ceiling of its benefit is the gap
in task success between a shaped reward and the same reward plus the term; the nearest published figure is the 31.6%
of ADR-ARENA-01, measured on other tasks against weaker baselines, and no effect on decision time is claimed.

### 24.7 The settled-pose reset cache [S]

An episode that starts with a foot 2 mm inside the floor begins with an impulse that no robot would feel, and a paired
comparison in which one design starts settled and the other does not compares spawn artifacts. DESIGN: episodes start
from precomputed quasi-static poses.

- **Contents.** For each (`ModelId`, spawn specification, contact law, kernel versions) the cache holds K = 64 settled
  states (TARGET): configuration, contact warm starts, actuator states at rest and estimator initial state. The key is
  a content hash, so the cache is a node of the memo graph of §28 and a design edit that changes mass, geometry or
  contact surfaces invalidates it.
- **Settling.** States are settled in double precision on `batch-cpu` until base and joint speeds stay below 10⁻³ in
  SI units for 0.2 s with maximum penetration at most 0.5 mm (TARGET). The first contact impulse after loading a
  cached state on any tier must lie within 5% of the static support force (TARGET).
- **Selection.** The pose index is drawn from a counter-based stream keyed by the world address, so two designs paired
  on one address start from the same spawn specification, each in its own settled state. The initial equilibrium is
  recomputed for each design; a state is never transported from one design to another.
- **Failure.** A world that does not meet the settle criterion on its tier is marked `Degraded: unsettled` and
  counted. A task whose cache is missing for the requested model is refused (FROB-REF-529).

### 24.8 Metrics and scorecards [S]

Every metric has a unit and a definition on file. The core set:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Tracking error | Root-mean-square error between commanded and achieved planar velocity and yaw rate |
| Success, fall and collision rates | Per the task's verdict and termination rules, with the failure taxonomy of §29 |
| Requirement margin | The margin of the verdict formula for the episode, with the binding atom and its time |
| Time to verdict | Simulated time at which the verdict was decided. Non-attainment is right-censored at the horizon and summaries use the restricted mean |
| Cost of transport | Energy divided by (mass × g × distance), reported twice: mechanical work at the joints, and electrical energy from the battery |
| Energy per completed task | Reported twice: mean energy conditional on completion, and total energy of all attempts divided by the number of completions, which is undefined at zero completions |
| Froude number | v² / (g × leg length), so gaits are compared at matched dynamic similarity |
| Actuator utilization | Fractions of time at the torque, speed and thermal limits, per joint |
| Friction utilization | The distribution of ‖γₜ‖ / (μ·γₙ) over stance |
| Slip distance | Tangential travel of a contact while in stance, computed as the time integral of the normal-load-weighted mean tangential speed over the contact quadrature, zero below a declared normal-force floor |
| Clearance | Minimum distance between non-contact links and the scene |
| Tail robustness | The 10% conditional value at risk of the task score over the randomization |
| Maneuver cost | Path length and time for the tight-space tasks, against A-07's prediction |
| Fallback count | Controller fallbacks taken, by kind; every fallback is recorded (§22) |

The two energy figures can order designs oppositely. Design A with one 10 J success and one 20 J failure, against
design B with two 12 J successes, gives 10 J against 12 J conditional on completion and 30 J against 12 J per
completion. Both are published. The slip metric must be invariant to refinement of the quadrature: duplicating a
quadrature point and halving the two weights leaves it unchanged (a known answer of §24.16). A quantile is reported
only with the sample count that could have observed it: seeing one value above the population's 99th percentile with
95% probability takes 299 independent episodes, and five episodes leave a 95.1% chance that even the largest lies
below it.

A **scorecard** is a table of metrics by scene class with intervals, normalized against the reference robot of the
same class. No single scalar is reported unless the task defines one. DESIGN: every scenario of a battery lands in
exactly one outcome column (INV-ARENA-03).

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `satisfied`, `violated` | Verdicts, the second broken down by failure class |
| `undetermined` | The trace did not cover a window; the reason is listed |
| `invalid` | Numerical fault. Counted as a study error and as a failure of the tier, never dropped |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition shows that this body cannot satisfy the requirement in this world (§26.5); the witness is attached |
| `infeasible-for-class` | The same for every body of the declared class; evidence that the scenario should leave the suite by a recorded decision, not by attrition |
| `refused` | The run was not admitted; the refusal code is listed |

Success rates are printed with two denominators, all scenarios and scenarios not shown infeasible, side by side.
Removing a scenario from a battery is a recorded event with a reason and changes the battery's identity.

### 24.9 Batteries and the design corpus

- `smoke`: minutes; every template and reference robot on a handful of scenarios. Runs on every change.
- `fixtures`: the analytic layers of the fixtures of §24.10 and §24.11 and the monitor known answers. Runs on every
  change that touches contact, actuators, clocks or `frob-spec`.
- `standard`: the fidelity battery of §38, about 200 scenarios per robot class.
- `full`: every task family across every archetype with the full randomization.
- `hard`: the hard-world set of §26 for the robot class. It is discovery data and is never a confirmation sample.
- `covering`: a scene set chosen by the coverage selection of §26.11, with one slot in five drawn at random from
  held-out scenes.
- **The design corpus** for BET-05 and BET-06: about 200 designs made of templates, parameter perturbations of them,
  and designs with planted defects of known kind (an undersized knee actuator, an inertia that breaks static
  stability, a wheelbase too long for an aisle). Labels are fixed before any analysis is tuned. The corpus also holds
  the planted winners used to audit screening (§26.10) and the planted single causes used to test attribution
  (§27.16).

### 24.10 Fixture 1: a loaded cart crosses a sill and docks [S]

**Question.** For a fixed motor and a current-limited cart, does a 75 mm front wheel improve loaded delivery across a
12 mm door sill relative to a 60 mm wheel? The larger wheel has a better lever at the lip and a smaller tread force
for the same torque; the fixture exists to find which effect dominates once load distribution, motor lag, impact and
momentum are included. Everything below is DESIGN: a synthetic fixture with explicit values, registered as
`fixture:cart-sill-dock`, so that an implementer can build it and a result can falsify it. Measured profiles replace
synthetic ones only under a new fixture identity.

**Bodies.** Chassis frame: x forward, y left, z up, origin midway between the axles on the axle line.

| Part | Value |
|---|---|
| Axles and wheels | Axles at x = ±0.16 m (wheelbase L = 0.32 m); wheel centres at y = ±0.12 m; front-wheel drive, rear wheels passive |
| Chassis | Uniform box 0.40 × 0.20 × 0.08 m, 5.0 kg, centred 50 mm above the axle line, so it overhangs each axle by 40 mm |
| Payload | Uniform box 0.18 × 0.14 × 0.08 m, 2.2 kg, bolted to the chassis top face with its bottom flush; centred at x = δ, where δ is the fore-aft offset of the condition |
| Wheels | Uniform cylinders 30 mm wide. Rear pair: radius 60 mm, 0.2 kg each, in both designs |
| Design A | Front pair: radius 60 mm, 0.2 kg each, spin inertia 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m². Total mass 8.000 kg |
| Design B | Front pair: radius 75 mm at the same density and width, so 0.3125 kg each (0.1125 kg more) and spin inertia 8.7890625 × 10⁻⁴ kg·m², a ratio of (75/60)⁴ = 625/256. Total mass 8.225 kg. The front axle rides 15 mm higher, pitching the chassis up by asin(15/320) = 2.69° |
| Rotor | Additional reflected rotor inertia per driven wheel: zero in the primary fixture |

| Initial state | Zero current; winding and pack held at 20 °C |
| Shaft loss | Viscous damping 0.002 N·m·s/rad on each of the four wheel shafts |
| Outer loop, 100 Hz | Wheel-rate target 0.30 m/s divided by the loaded radius: 5.0 rad/s for A, 4.0 rad/s for B. Current command 2 A·s/rad times the rate error, clipped to ±6 A, held for 10 ms |

At +6 A, if that voltage would raise the current, v = R·i + k_e·ω holds the limit when available, and symmetrically at −6 A. A limit crossing is located inside the step. If no admissible voltage keeps the winding inside its rating, a physical limit violation is recorded |
| Power ledger | Net DC power is Σ v_j·i_j over the two drives; bus current is that sum divided by 24 V; positive and negative parts are integrated separately |

| Contact | Coulomb friction μ, one value for all wheel–floor and wheel–sill contacts of a condition, 0.8 nominal; inelastic impact; g = 9.81 m/s² |
| Start | Stationary, centred and parallel to the lane, front axle at x = −0.50 m, settled under gravity from the reset cache of §24.7, separately for each design |
| Clocks | Physics 1/2000 s; outer loop and requirement clock 1/100 s; atoms are interval extrema over the 20 physics ticks of each requirement tick |
| Docked | The minimum world x of the whole loaded collision envelope exceeds 0.10 m, and roll and pitch stay within 5°, continuously for 0.5 s, with the dwell complete within 8 s of the start. The dwell restarts whenever a condition lapses |
| Hard limits | Roll or pitch beyond 35°; any chassis contact with the sill; winding current beyond the rating by more than 0.01 A. Each is absorbing |
| Stall | The command is saturated and the cart advances less than 2 mm over 0.5 s. A stall names the failure reason; the verdict still comes from the requirement at the horizon |
| Horizon | 8 simulated seconds. Timeout, rollover, strike and stall each carry a reason |

The requirement, in the S-expression view:

```lisp
(requirements
  (atom cleared    (>  (- env_min_x 0.10 m) 0 m)                    :scale 0.10 m)
  (atom upright    (>= (- 5 deg tilt) 0 deg)                        :scale 5 deg)
  (atom tilt-ok    (>= (- 35 deg tilt) 0 deg)                       :scale 35 deg)
  (atom current-ok (>= (- 6.01 A i_max) 0 A)                        :scale 6 A)
  (atom no-strike  (=  strikes 0)                                   :exact)
  (atom saturated  (=  drive_saturated 1)                           :exact)
  (atom advancing  (>= (- x_front (delay 0.5 s x_front)) 0.002 m)   :scale 0.002 m)
  (online dwell    (H (0 s 0.5 s) (and cleared upright)))
  (online stalled  (and (H (0 s 0.5 s) saturated) (not advancing)))
  (verdict docked  (U (0 s 8 s) (and tilt-ok current-ok no-strike) dwell)))
```

`env_min_x` is the rearmost point of the loaded collision envelope, `tilt` the larger of |roll| and |pitch|, `i_max`
the larger winding current magnitude, each an interval extremum over a requirement tick.

The until-form says that the hard limits hold at every tick before the dwell completes, and that it completes by 8 s.
Written this way the dwell cannot latch: `H` over 0.5 s is false the tick any condition lapses. FACT (planning
archive, sill pilot audit; Appendix H): the hand-written scorer of an earlier planar pilot of this fixture had a
latched dwell timer and no chassis-strike guard. Both defects are unrepresentable here.

**Energy.** Net bus energy is integrated from the start to adjudication for every trial and reported with duration,
gross draw and returned energy. The two per-completion figures of §24.8 are both published. Energy is descriptive in
version 1 of the fixture and takes no part in the rule.

**The rule.** The study is fixed-n (§24.12). Let n be the number of complete pairs, b the pairs in which only B docks
and a the pairs in which only A docks. The effect estimate is (b − a)/n. Form two-sided 97.5% exact Clopper–Pearson
intervals [L_b, U_b] and [L_a, U_a] for the two discordance probabilities; the interval [L_b − U_a, U_b − L_a] then
covers their difference with probability at least 95%.

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| Prefer B | L_b − U_a > +0.05, and B violates no hard limit in a condition where A does not |
| B inferior | U_b − L_a < −0.05 |
| Practical tie | The interval lies inside [−0.05, +0.05] |
| Inconclusive | Otherwise |

Both-succeed and neither-succeed counts, invalid pairs and the worst-case range for missing pairs are reported, with unresolved counts given per arm and per action regime, because a policy can enter states where the solver fails preferentially: a candidate with 2 successes in 2 valid trials and 98 unresolved has a complete-case rate of 100% and a success rate anywhere in [2%, 100%], against a baseline at 60 of 100, and its unresolved worlds are re-run on the referee before a ranking that depends on them is promoted. A
preference needs complete pairs. If an apparent improvement reverses under a halved step, a refined contact feature or
the alternative contact law, the result is reported as fidelity-dependent and both runs are kept.

**The pre-analysis.** The rule is enumerated before any pair runs. Every count pair (a, b) with a + b ≤ n is
classified; the table is regenerated by `eval.preanalyze` and compared in CI.

| n | Count pairs | Prefer B | B inferior | Practical tie | Inconclusive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 2,145 | 521 | 521 | 0 | 1,103 |
| 86 | 3,828 | 1,031 | 1,031 | 1 | 1,765 |
| 128 | 8,385 | 2,517 | 2,517 | 4 | 3,347 |
| 256 | 33,153 | 11,384 | 11,384 | 39 | 10,346 |

Three consequences follow. First, no outcome at n = 64 can establish a practical tie: perfect concordance gives
[−0.0662, +0.0662]. A tie first becomes reachable at n = 86 and only for (a, b) = (0, 0); with 5% discordance each way
the chance of that outcome is 0.9⁸⁶ = 0.0116%. Second, with no A-only pair a preference for B needs at least 15 B-only
pairs of 64, and 17, 19, 21 and 23 with one to four A-only pairs. Third, the batch has little power for a modest
effect. Each alternative below is a pair of probabilities, of a B-only pair and of an A-only pair:

| n | P(prefer B) at discordance (0.17, 0) | at (0.25, 0.02) | at (0.40, 0.02) | P(tie) at (0.02, 0.02) | P(tie) at (0, 0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 0.117 | 0.406 | 0.962 | 0 | 0 |
| 128 | 0.775 | 0.906 | above 0.999 | 0.072 | 1 |

The pair (0.17, 0) is the share of the friction range in which only the 75 mm wheel meets the front lift-off threshold
of the analytic layer; it is a reference effect size, not a prediction. The 64 conditions are therefore a development
batch: they can prefer a design or call it inferior, they cannot declare a tie, and they are not used to tune anything
that the holdout then judges. The holdout is sized from this table for the claim that is wanted, runs through
`eval.qualify` on sealed conditions (§24.13), and uses the level of its request. A claim of equivalence may instead
register the standalone test that bounds the difference by the total discordance probability: zero discordant pairs in
64 give an exact one-sided 95% bound of 1 − 0.05^(1/64) = 0.0457, inside the five-point band. The inference is
registered before the holdout is opened, and the development batch keeps its original analysis.

**Mechanism controls.** Two registered follow-ups separate the mechanisms. One sets B's gain to 3.125 A·s/rad to match
the force slopes, leaving the caps at 40 N and 32 N. The other is a 2 × 2 of rotor inertia (zero or 0.0002 kg·m²) and
control cadence (100 Hz or 1 kHz). FACT (planning archive; Appendix H): in an exploratory planar sweep the two
designs' success fractions were 0.093 and 0.780 at 100 Hz with zero rotor inertia and 1.000 and 1.000 at 1 kHz or with
added inertia. A comparison that leaves rotor inertia and controller rate unpinned is a comparison of loop stability.

### 24.11 Fixture 2: a quadruped on a split-grip landing [S]

**Question.** Does a wider sole help a quadruped arrive on a landing whose surface has a low-grip strip under one
side? On a uniform Coulomb surface a wider foot has no more translational traction at the same normal load; the
proposed advantage is a change in the share of the sole that rests on the high-grip side of a material boundary. The
fixture, registered as `fixture:quad-split-grip`, is built so that the limiting model predicts a reversal, which a
count of contact area cannot produce.

| Item | Value (DESIGN) |
|---|---|
| Body | The reference 12-actuator quadruped template of §14 at its pinned `DesignId`, with identical actuator limits, equal limb masses and a symmetric nominal stance |
| Controller | One crawl controller from Reflex (§22), frozen before either sole is tested |
| Sole A, sole B | Rectangular plates 80 × 40 mm and 80 × 70 mm, the 80 mm side along the direction of travel, on all four feet; the same sole law, sole height and ankle origin; mass and spatial inertia matched by a declared insert. Mounting and leg interference are checked for each, and each design is settled independently |
| Course | A ramp 1 m long rising at 6°, then a level landing 1.2 m long |
| Surfaces | Friction 0.8 everywhere except a longitudinal strip of friction 0.25 on the landing, 0.20 m wide, whose inner boundary runs through the centres of the left soles in the nominal straight gait and whose outer edge lies outside both footprints throughout |
| Command | 0.25 m/s along the ramp from rest; stand once all feet have cleared the crest by 0.40 m |
| Pass | Standing reached within 12 s and held for 1 s; no body strike; roll and pitch below 30°; less than 50 mm of slip per foot over its loaded stance, by the slip metric of §24.8 with a 1 N floor |
| Cases | All-dry, split-grip, and split-grip reflected to the right side |
| Development set | Nominal strip position and offsets of ±5 mm, centred payload |
| Holdout | Ramp at 8°; strip offsets ±12 mm; initial yaw ±2°; lateral payload shift ±20 mm; the full factorial of eight cells with its eight reflected counterparts, and the four dry cells |
| Contact laws | Distributed rigid Coulomb contact as the baseline; then a finer distributed compliant sole with a declared pressure and shear law, with the step halved and the footprint quadrature refined until the classification stops changing |

**The limiting model.** For uniform normal pressure, the share of a sole of width w that rests on the high-grip side
when its centre is displaced by z into that side is f = clip(½ + z/w, 0, 1). Its longitudinal friction capacity per
unit normal load is c(z, w) = 0.8·f + 0.25·(1 − f) = 0.525 + 0.55·clip(z/w, −½, ½), and at the slip limit the plate
carries a yaw moment of 0.275·N·(w/4 − z²/w) about its centre for |z| ≤ w/2.

| Offset z | Capacity, 40 mm sole | Capacity, 70 mm sole | Yaw moment per newton, 40 mm ; 70 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.525 | 0.525 | 2.75 mm ; 4.81 mm |
| +12 mm | 0.690 | 0.619 | 1.76 mm ; 4.25 mm |
| −12 mm | 0.360 | 0.431 | 1.76 mm ; 4.25 mm |

The narrow sole has more capacity when the foot lands toward the high-grip side and less when it lands toward the
strip. The two held-out offsets therefore carry opposite predictions, and the wide sole always carries the larger yaw
moment. Area alone cannot predict traction when pressure redistributes; the actual load split and moment are measured
under each contact law.

**Executable known answers.** A plate-drag rig realizes the limiting model: one sole plate on a compliant layer under
a centred normal load, guided against yaw, pulled along the boundary until it slips. The pull force at slip must equal
c(z, w)·N and the guide moment the value above. The slip metric is checked for invariance under duplication of a
quadrature point. The reflected cases are a metamorphic relation: the mirrored robot on the mirrored course returns
the mirrored result, inside its determinism class.

**Decision rule.** Results are counts on a finite suite. Reflected pairs are related and are reported as pairs. A sole
is preferred only if it completes more held-out cases under both contact laws with no new hard-limit failure. A
reversal between the laws is reported as a fidelity-dependent ranking, which is a finding about the tiers and not
about the soles. Distance, work, worst slip, peak torque and falls are published for every design and condition. A
comparison with a learned residual on top of the frozen controller is a later study with equal training resources.

### 24.12 Two study types [S]

A study is a registered plan to answer one comparative or absolute question. It declares its type when it is created
and keeps it (INV-ARENA-05).

| | Anytime-valid study | Fixed-n exact study |
|---|---|---|
| Inference | A betting e-process or a confidence sequence (C-15), valid at every stopping time | An exact interval or test at a sample size fixed in advance |
| Stopping | When the evidence crosses its threshold or the budget ends; unfinished episodes are cancelled | At n complete units, never earlier |
| Before the first episode | Null hypothesis, effect margin, bound on outcomes, betting rule, level, budget | n, the rule, the level, and a pre-analysis: which verdicts are reachable at that n, and the power at declared alternatives |
| Reports | The e-value, the confidence sequence, pairs used, the stopping reason | Counts, the interval, the verdict, and the pre-analysis hash |
| Interim information | Any; validity does not depend on it | Progress only. Outcomes may be looked at, and the verdict is computed once, at n |
| Typical use | Screening, racing, promotion between tiers, bisection | Registered A/B questions, equivalence claims, qualification of a finalist |

The types are never mixed inside one study. A fixed-n interval computed at the moment an anytime-valid study stops has
no coverage guarantee, and a fixed-n study that stops when its running p-value first looks good loses its level.
Counterexample for the second: an exact one-sided sign test at nominal level 5% on pairs that favour either design
with probability ½, looked at after every pair. After 8 looks the chance of a false rejection is 13/256 = 5.08%; after
16 it is 8.76%, after 32, 12.9%, and after 64, 17.4%. A request for either mixture is refused (FROB-REF-552).

A fixed-n study without a pre-analysis is not admitted, and a study that names a verdict its rule cannot reach at the
declared n and level is refused (FROB-REF-532). Fixture 1 shows why: at 64 pairs its rule cannot return one of its
four verdicts.

Four rules hold for both types. Units are paired by world address, and when several episodes share a cluster such as
one building, the cluster is the unit of inference. Paired statistics are refused under an uncontrolled determinism
class (§35). An N-way race spends α/(N − 1) per comparison, so that a null race keeps its best candidate with
probability at least 1 − α. And a comparison of designs declares its controller arm: a shared controller isolates the
change of body, controllers retuned with equal resources compare the best systems, and a candidate that inherits
weights declares its warm start and receives a matched transfer baseline.

### 24.13 The blinded qualification lane [S]

An e-process makes one comparison valid under optional stopping. It does nothing about an agent that submits fifty
candidates to the same held-out scenes until one passes. With independent attempts at level 5% each, fourteen null
submissions give a 51% chance of at least one false promotion, and five hundred give certainty for practical purposes.
The qualification lane closes that hole. Its verb is `eval.qualify`.

**Lanes.** The discovery lane is everything an agent may read while it designs: training scenes, the development
splits, batteries, the hard-world set, its own runs and telemetry. The confirmation lane is a service inside `frobd`
that owns a sealed family of worlds and runs registered requests against it. A **campaign** is a set of confirmation
requests that share a claim family, meaning a task, a baseline class and a held-out world family, and a total error
budget α. All agents working on the claim family share the campaign and its index sequence. Opening a campaign needs
the capability `qual:admin`; an agent cannot reset its index by opening another.

**The protocol for request i of a campaign.**

1. **Commit.** The request names candidate, baseline, task, claim, study type and rule, tier plan, number of clusters
   and budget. It is hashed into the ledger with an idempotency key before any confirmation world exists
   (INV-QUAL-04). For a fixed-n study the pre-analysis is computed at this request's level and attached.
2. **Spend.** The request receives the level α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)] and the ledger records it. The index advances for
   every committed request, including ones that end inconclusive or are refused after commitment (INV-QUAL-03).
3. **Draw.** The service derives fresh world clusters for this request from a sealed key, the campaign identifier, the
   index i and a cluster counter. A cluster is the unit that shares a generator draw, such as one building with its
   materials. No cluster serves two requests and none has appeared in any discovery dataset (INV-QUAL-02).
4. **Run.** Candidate and baseline run paired on the clusters at the tier plan, with promotion to a higher tier by the
   rule of §16. The study runs at level α_i with clusters as units.
5. **Answer.** The agent receives a receipt and nothing else: the decision, the index, the level spent, the number of
   units used and a hash.
6. **Close.** When the campaign closes, the key is revealed, the clusters and their episodes become readable, and the
   family is retired into discovery. It never serves as a confirmation family again.

```json
{ "schema": "frob.qualify.request/1", "campaign": "q:threshold-delivery/3", "index": 7,
  "alpha_total": "0.05", "alpha_i": "0.05/56",
  "candidate": {"design": "d:7c03…", "controller": "c:91ab…"},
  "baseline": {"design": "d:41aa…", "controller": "c:91ab…"},
  "task": "t:cart-sill-dock@6be1…", "claim": "candidate docks more often than baseline by more than 0.05",
  "study": {"type": "fixed-n", "n": 256, "rule": "paired-clopper-pearson/1", "preanalysis": "b3:5d10…"},
  "tier_plan": {"run": "batch-cpu", "promote_to": "referee"}, "clusters": 256, "budget": {"cpu_core_s": 90000},
  "commit": {"ledger_seq": "18812", "hash": "b3:e41f…", "idempotency_key": "k:2c9a…"} }
```

```json

  "alpha_i": "0.05/56", "alpha_spent": "0.05*7/8", "decision": "not-promoted", "units_used": 256,
  "sealed_until_close": ["world addresses", "per-unit outcomes", "telemetry", "stills"],
  "receipt_hash": "b3:90c2…", "authorization": {"execution_authorized": false,
  "hardware_safety_signoff": false, "sim_to_real_certified": false} }
```

**Why the error rate holds.** Because 1/[i·(i + 1)] = 1/i − 1/(i + 1), the levels sum to α·m/(m + 1) after m requests
and to α in the limit (PO-ARENA-02). Request i tests a candidate that the agent chose with everything it had seen
before, on clusters that are independent of everything it had seen before, so conditional on the past the chance of a
false promotion at request i is at most α_i. A union bound over requests gives a campaign-wide chance of any false
promotion of at most α, for any number of retries and any adaptive strategy. The argument needs three things and the
lane enforces exactly those: the request is fixed before the draw, the clusters are fresh, and the level is spent
whether or not the agent likes the answer.

**What blinding adds.** Freshness alone controls the error rate. Blinding protects the claim's population. If an agent
could read confirmation worlds, they would inform its next candidate, the held-out family would stop being held out,
and a promotion would describe worlds the design was fitted to. So discovery cannot read confirmation worlds
(INV-QUAL-01): no verb returns a sealed address, outcome, trace or still to a session without `qual:admin` before the
campaign closes, and an attempt is refused and logged (FROB-REF-840). If a reversal on a sealed family is ever used to
inform training or selection, that family is retired at once.

**What a retry costs.** The sample size of a test grows with ln(1/α_i). Relative to a single test at α = 0.05, request
1 needs about 1.23 times the units, request 10 about 2.57 times, request 100 about 4.08 times and request 500 about
5.15 times. A fixed-n rule changes with the level too: for the rule of fixture 1 a practical tie first becomes
reachable at n = 99 for request 1, n = 178 for request 10 and n = 328 for request 500, and at 64 pairs request 1 has
478 preferring outcomes of 2,145 where the development level had 521. Retrying is allowed and is never free.

**Standing.** A result is `qualified` only with a receipt whose decision is `promoted`. Reports cite the campaign, the
index and the level. Citing a discovery-lane comparison as a qualification is a protocol refusal (FROB-REF-845). The
receipts, the ledger of levels and, after close, the cluster derivation are part of the `.frobcert` bundle and are
replayed by `frob-verify` (§12).

### 24.19 Traction capacity is a slice of the wrench set at the commanded wrench

**What it is.** [S] A metric, and two analytic rows for Fixture 2 (§24.11), used in every comparison of soles, tyres
and pads. The scalar friction capacity Σ μᵢ·Nᵢ is the largest longitudinal force with the yaw moment left free. A
robot commands a wrench. The capacity that ranks two designs is the largest force along the commanded direction
among contact forces that also deliver the commanded moments.

**Counterexample.** A two-point sole carries 50 N on each point, at lateral offsets ±20 mm, one point on μ = 0.25
and the other on μ = 0.8. The largest forward force is 12.5 + 40 = 52.5 N, and it comes with a yaw moment of
(40 − 12.5) N × 0.020 m = 0.55 N·m. If the command is a pure forward force, the two longitudinal forces must be
equal, and the largest is 2 × 12.5 = 25 N. A sole on uniform μ = 0.4 supplies 40 N at zero yaw. By scalar capacity
the split sole wins, 52.5 N against 40 N. At the commanded wrench it loses, 25 N against 40 N.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN:
- `traction_at_wrench(direction, moment command)` is a cone program over the contact wrench set of C-06: maximize
  the force along the direction, subject to the per-point friction cones, the measured normal loads and the moment
  equalities. It is the program of §22.5 with another objective, so it needs no new solver. Owner: `frob-control`
  for the program, `frob-arena` for the metric, `frob-analyze` for the static margin of A-08.
- Scorecards (§24.8) report the scalar capacity under the name `capacity, moment free`, and the slice under the name
  of the commanded wrench. A comparison of contact geometries cites the slice.
- Fixture 2 gains two rows. The plate-drag rig runs once guided against yaw, as now, and once free to yaw, which
  enforces zero moment physically. For the uniform-pressure strip model with the boundary through the centre and
  longitudinal traction only, the zero-yaw capacity per unit normal load is (μ_L + √(μ_L·μ_H))/2, which is 0.3486
  for μ_L = 0.25 and μ_H = 0.8, against 0.525 with the moment free, and it does not depend on the sole width. The
  closed form is a lower bound when lateral traction may also carry moment; the cone program gives the exact value.

**Known answers.** Two-point rig: 52.5 N and 0.55 N·m guided; 25 N free; 40 N both ways on uniform μ = 0.4. Strip
model at zero offset: 0.525·N guided for both widths; at least 0.3486·N free for both widths.

### 24.20 Auditing a task before any design runs on it: foil bodies and intent invariants

**Task audit [F]; intent invariants [S].** The detectors of §23.16 ask whether a good score is an artifact of the
solver. A behaviour that is physically real and unintended passes every one of them: sliding on the belly where
walking was meant, tumbling down a flight to reach the goal region, riding a wall, satisfying the predicate only at
the instant it is checked. Faster search finds the cheapest such loophole sooner, and one loophole invalidates every
evaluation of the campaign that ran under it. `task.audit` attacks the task itself before any candidate is scored.

*Foil bodies.* A library `assets/foils/` of bodies that are deliberately over-capable or degenerate, written in the
design language and marked `foil: true`. Each runs in the task's own scene and is scored by the task's own
requirement and cost. A foil is never a candidate: offering one to `opt.*`, `eval.compare` or `eval.qualify` is
refused.

| Foil | What it is | What it probes |
|---|---|---|
| Free centroidal body | One rigid body with the mass and inertia of the design class, applying bounded wrenches at the contact sites the task designates, inside friction cones; no limbs, no actuator limits | Whether the predicate can be met by forces alone, without the intended mechanism |
| Relaxed family member | The current design with card limits multiplied by 3 (DESIGN) and collision enabled on every link | Behaviours the real design is only barely kept from |
| Degenerate shapes | A sled, a ball, a tumbling box and a single-leg hopper of the class's mass | Sliding, rolling, tumbling and bouncing routes to a satisfied verdict |

*Search.* The sampling controller of §22.8 and rank-based search over open-loop parameters, filling a
quality-diversity archive (§28.8) over behaviour descriptors: the fraction of time each site class touches the
ground, the range of base attitude ‖log R‖, the airborne fraction, and the sliding fraction (tangential speed at
stance contacts above a card threshold). The objective is many distinct high-scoring behaviours, not one optimum.

*Intent invariants.* Past-time predicates in the requirement language of §24.5 over channels Chronicle already
records. A task family declares which it adopts.

| Invariant | Signal |
|---|---|
| Contact only at designated sites (feet, wheels, tracks) | Contact provenance per site |
| Attitude excursion inside a bound | ‖log R‖ of the base |
| Airborne and sliding fractions inside their bounds | Contact state; tangential speed at stance contacts |
| No net positive work by a contact | The `contact-active` monitor of §29.9 |
| The body stays inside the route corridor | The scene's route graph (§19) |
| Dwell: success is held for a declared time, and is not first reached in the last 5% of the horizon (DESIGN) | The verdict's own online signal |

*Findings and their resolution.* A finding is (foil, behaviour cell, score, violated invariant, replay address,
proposed clause), the clause being a typed edit of the `requirements` section. The author accepts the clause, which
yields a new `TaskId` because a task is content-addressed, and the foil's trace is added as `(example … violated)`,
so the amended requirement is tested against the loophole from then on. Or the author marks the behaviour intended,
and the trace is added as `(example … satisfied)`. A high-scoring degenerate behaviour that violates no adopted
invariant is reported `unclassified`. Campaign results cite the `TaskId` they ran under. `eval.qualify` on a task
with an unresolved finding, or with no audit record for its `TaskId`, is refused. The audit runs on discovery worlds
only and reads no confirmation world.

*In-campaign check [S].* The winning episodes of the top-k designs are evaluated against the adopted invariants. A
design whose wins violate one is flagged `wins-outside-intent` and cannot be promoted until the author resolves it.

*Hand case.* Goal region x ∈ [4.9, 5.1] m and requirement `(F (0 s 10 s) in_goal)`. A sled foil moving at a constant
1 m/s is inside the region for t ∈ [4.9, 5.1] s and never stops: the verdict is satisfied with a dwell of 0.2 s. The
proposed clause is `(F (0 s 10 s) (H (0 s 2 s) in_goal))`. The sled's longest dwell is 0.2 s < 2 s, so the amended
requirement is violated by the foil; a body at rest at x = 5 m from t = 6 s satisfies it from t = 8 s.

*Owners and verb.* `frob-arena` owns the audit, the foil library and the invariants; `frob-control` and `frob-opt`
supply the search; findings are stored by `frob-chronicle` keyed by `TaskId`. Verb: `task.audit` (run; budgeted; a
long call returns a job handle).

*Ceiling, pilot and gate.* Ceiling: the compute of campaigns that a loophole would have invalidated. How often
authored tasks contain one is unmeasured. FACT (§2.2, source register): in one published agent design loop 15.8% of
trained candidates were degenerate after a full training run. HYPOTHESIS: at least one accepted finding in a fifth of
authored tasks; test: the count over the task families of §24.3 at M3. Pilot before funding: `task.audit` on ten
authored tasks with a budget of 5% of a reference campaign each. If no finding is accepted by an author, only the
in-campaign check is built.

### 24.21 The item bank: importance sampling of battery items for close comparisons

**Item bank [F].** The cost of a population decision scales with designs × battery items × replicates. Racing and the
ladder work on the first factor and the tiers on the cost of an episode. Nothing so far models the items, although
most items say nothing about two close designs: scenes both pass and scenes both fail. The item bank keeps a
persistent model of items and uses it only to choose which items a comparison runs. The estimand never changes.

*Item, law and bank.* An item is (scene instance, task, disturbance law, family of initial conditions), identified by
content hash. A battery version fixes a law p(i) over its items; p is the estimand's weight and is never edited by the
bank. The bank stores, per morphology class and battery version, a response model fitted by regularized maximum
likelihood on every (design, item, outcome) row of discovery data in the archive, in canonical row order:

    success:      P(Y = 1 | d, i) = σ(a_iᵀ·θ_d − b_i),   θ_d ∈ ℝᵏ, k ≤ 5 (DESIGN)
    continuous:   Y | d, i  ~  N(μ_i + a_iᵀ·θ_d,  s_i²)

The fit has a version, and the version is part of the identity of every comparison that used it.

*Proposal and estimator.* For a contrast of designs A and B with paired differences D_i = Y_B,i − Y_A,i,

    q*(i) ∝ p(i)·√(E_model[D_i²]),      q(i) = (1 − ε)·q*(i) + ε·p(i),      ε = 0.1 (DESIGN),
    Ĉ = (1/n)·Σ_j w(i_j)·D_{i_j},        i_j drawn from q by an addressed stream,   w(i) = p(i)/q(i) ≤ 1/ε.

E_q[w·D] = Σ_i q(i)·(p(i)/q(i))·E[D_i] = Σ_i p(i)·E[D_i]: the estimate is unbiased for the contrast under the frozen
law whether or not the response model is right. The model buys efficiency and nothing else. Items that do not
discriminate are down-weighted in q and never removed from p.

*Arithmetic, exact.* Let D be ±1 on a fraction f of items and 0 elsewhere, with mean μ. Uniform sampling has variance
f − μ². Under q* the weight is f on the discriminating items and the variance is f² − μ². At f = 0.1 and μ = 0.05
these are 0.0975 and 0.0075, a ratio of 13. With the defensive mixture at ε = 0.1 the weight on a discriminating item
is 1/(0.9/f + 0.1) = 0.1099 and the variance is 0.1 × 0.1099 − 0.0025 = 0.0085, a ratio of 11.5.

*Counterexample: a wrong model costs up to 1/ε.* Let the model place q* on a set of items disjoint from the items
that discriminate. Each discriminating item then has q = ε·p and weight 10, the second moment is f × 10 = 1.0, and
the variance is 0.9975 against 0.0975 under uniform sampling: 10.2 times worse, with the estimate still unbiased. The
mixture bounds the damage at 1/ε. It does not remove it, and the gate therefore measures realized interval width
and not the model's own forecast.

*Rules.*

- **Anytime studies on discovery worlds only.** The weighted increment w·D lies in a span 1/ε times the span declared
  for D, so a range-driven sequence would lose by (1/ε)² in sample size. Item-bank sampling is admitted only with the
  variance-adaptive betting sequence, and the span it declares is the widened one.
- **Refused for `fixed-n` studies and inside the blinded qualification lane.** The exact rule of §28.6 counts
  discordant pairs under the frozen law, and confirmation draws from p on fresh sealed clusters. The fit never reads
  a confirmation world.
- **Not composed with the tier-correction estimator.** Promotion pairs are drawn from p; multiplying the two weights
  would allow 10 × 20 = 200.
- **A rare-failure bound refuses item-bank episodes**, as it refuses any episode not drawn nominally (§26).
- **Replicate noise is untouched.** Variance between seeds inside an item is not reduced by choosing items.

*What an agent reads.* Beside the verdict: the episodes used and the uniform-equivalent count, and the separating
items ranked by discrimination ‖a_i‖ with their difficulty b_i, which says in which capability the two designs
differ.

*Owners and verbs.* `frob-arena` owns the battery law and the bank; `frob-opt` owns the proposal and the estimator;
rows come from `frob-chronicle`. `eval.compare --sampling item-bank` runs a comparison; `eval.items` (read) returns
the bank rows for a battery and class with their sample counts and fit version.

*Ceiling and expectation.* The idealized ratio is 1/f when a fraction f of items discriminates. With model error, the
mixture and replicate noise the expectation is 4 to 8 (HYPOTHESIS; test: the replay study below). The saving
multiplies with the tier correction of §29.11, which lowers the cost of an episode instead of their number.

### 24.22 Exposure domains and the coupling test

**Exposure domains [S]; chosen couplings [F].** This extends the `randomization` section of the task file (§24.4),
pairing rule 2 of §28.6 and the keyed streams of §35.9. Those rules say that a draw is keyed by the physical entity
it perturbs. What is still unstated is which kind of entity each draw belongs to, what happens to a disturbance that
a mission event triggers, and how anyone checks that a coupling left each arm's own distribution alone. Every draw
in `randomization` now declares one of four exposure domains, and the task compiler in `frob-arena` lowers the
section to a versioned exposure table that is part of the task's identity.

| Domain | Key, beside `WorldAddr` and `stream_id` | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `field` | An anchored coordinate cell or a fixture identifier | Sampled once per world at stable coordinates; a robot reads it where it is | Friction of a floor tile; the mass of a cart in the scene |
| `process` | A node of the dyadic tree over physical time (§16.5) | An exogenous process in physical time; a change of step or control rate reads the same path | A gust; a push scheduled at 3 s |
| `event` | A mission role and an occurrence index | Exists only where the task defines the exposure by a declared mission event; its physical time may differ between arms by definition | A push when the base first crosses the docking gate |
| `private` | The hash of a port name | Noise with no counterpart in the other arm | Noise of a sensor that only one design carries |

Roles are the typed ports and task regions of §14 and §19, so they survive edits. An `event` draw is legal only when
the exposure is defined by a mission event in the task; keying by the order of contacts, footfalls or draws remains
refused (§28.6).

*The coupling test.* `task.check --coupling` runs four checks on a task and a pair of controllers or designs. A
coupling that fails one is refused.

1. *Marginal invariance.* For each arm, outcomes under the coupled protocol and under independent sampling agree in
   distribution within a tolerance fixed in advance. A coupling may change the joint law of the two arms and nothing
   about either arm alone.
2. *Insertion invariance.* Adding unused draws, a sensor or a limb changes no exposure of another domain entry (the
   sensor-set property of §20.17, applied to exposures).
3. *Rate invariance.* Halving the physics step changes no `field` value, and a `process` increment over a coarse
   interval equals the sum of its fine increments.
4. *Retiming.* Under a deliberately retimed controller, a `field` stays at its coordinates, a `process` push occurs at
   the same physical time, and an `event` push occurs at each arm's own crossing.

*Counterexamples, hand-checkable.* A push keyed by control tick 1,500 occurs at 3.0 s with a 2 ms step and at 1.5 s
with a 1 ms step. A push keyed by "the tenth footfall" occurs at 5.0 s under a gait period of 0.5 s and at 4.0 s
under 0.4 s: a disturbance meant to be timed now follows gait phase, and the two arms were pushed in different
states. The first keying fails check 3 and the second fails check 4.

*Choosing among legal couplings [F].* Where more than one legal keying exists (friction by tile or by fixture), the
choice is made on pilot addresses by the measured variance of the contrast, the table version is then locked, and
confirmation addresses are drawn only afterwards. Both marginals and the contrast variance are recorded. For equal
marginal variances σ², Var(D) = 2σ²·(1 − ρ): moving the paired correlation from 0.50 to 0.95 lowers the variance of
a contrast ten times at a fixed count. That is an illustration of what is at stake, not a prediction.

*Owners and verb.* `frob-arena` owns the compiler and the test; `frob-scene` caches `field` samples per world; the
stream registry of §35.9 holds the domains. Verb: `task.check --coupling`. A paired study on a task whose exposure
table has not passed the test is refused.

### 24.23 The idle arm of the task audit

**What it is.** [S] One more arm for `task.audit` (§24.20). The foil bodies probe ways of satisfying a task without
the intended mechanism. The idle arm probes the cheapest of them: doing nothing. A requirement or a reward built from
safety atoms alone is satisfied in full by a robot that never leaves its start. Owner: `frob-arena`. Verb:
`task.audit`.

**Hand case.** A delivery task uses the past-time requirement `(H (0 s 2 s) (and clear-margin thermal-ok spill-ok))`
as its online reward over 60 s. A parked robot keeps all three margins positive for the whole episode and collects the
full reward. A robot that carries the tray through the pass and loses part of its spill margin for one second collects
less. Search prefers the parked robot.

**Mechanism.** DESIGN.

- The audit runs the candidate design, and each foil, under three idle laws: zero command with the brakes as carded; a
  hold-posture command from the baseline controller of §22.4; and the backup controller of §22.12.3.
- A task is flagged `rewards-idleness` when an idle run's requirement verdict is `satisfied`, or when its return is at
  least a declared fraction of the best audited return (DESIGN default 0.5).
- The finding carries a proposed clause, like every finding of §24.20: a progress atom with its scale (for the hand
  case, approach speed above 0.15 m/s on a scale of 0.50 m/s, which gives the parked robot a margin of −0.3), or a

## 26. Nemesis: adversarial worlds and rare failures

Crate `frob-nemesis`, layer 5.

### 26.1 What Nemesis owns

Randomization from card uncertainty reports the tails it happens to sample. It never looks for a failure and never
reduces one to its cause. Nemesis does both. It searches the space of scenes, disturbances and parameters for worlds
in which a candidate violates its requirement, keeps only worlds the candidate's own body could have solved, checks
that a failure is not an artifact of the simulator, shrinks it to the smallest world that still fails, and stores that
world as a regression. It also answers the other question about failures, how rare they are, and keeps the search for
failures apart from the bound on their rate.

A value in this chapter that follows from a stated formula is a derived value and a known answer of §26.14; every
other number carries its label.

Nemesis owns the legality set, the adversarial search, the solvability oracle, the artifact guard, shrinking and the
format `.fmcw`, the hard-world set, rare-failure estimation, the audit of screening, and the selection of covering
scene sets. It consumes requirement margins from Arena (§24), scene grammars and typed scene edits from Habitat (§19),
snapshots and forks from Chronicle and Autopsy (§29, §27), promotion between tiers from Kinesis (§16), and through the
airlock FrankenSim's `fs-uq`, `fs-eproc`, `fs-dfo` and `fs-archive`.

| Mechanism | Tier | Reason for the tier |
|---|---|---|
| Legality set, solvability bookkeeping, artifact guard, hard-world set | [S] | Bookkeeping over mechanisms that exist elsewhere in the plan |
| Adversarial search by margin | [F] | Established in falsification practice; its gain over randomization on robots in interiors is unmeasured |
| Minimal counterexample worlds | [F] | Delta debugging is established for programs; its yield on physical worlds is unmeasured |
| Certification by crude sampling; bounded-weight importance sampling | [S] | Exact binomial bounds and bounded likelihood ratios |
| Splitting and cross-entropy tilting as exploration | [F] | Published gains are real and uneven (§26.9) |
| Screening audit; coverage selection | [S] | Stratified sampling; a greedy algorithm with a classical guarantee |

### 26.2 Decisions

- **ADR-NEMESIS-01. Search inside a legality set, and keep priors apart from admissibility.** An unconstrained
  adversary wins by building worlds no robot is meant to face. Legality is a set; the nominal randomization is a
  distribution on part of it. A rare but admissible world can be a useful counterexample with negligible operational
  probability, so the search does not discard it and the report states both facts.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-02. Only solvable worlds count.** A world is a counterexample to a design only with a witness that the
  candidate's own body, actuators and mission constraints could have satisfied the requirement in it. This is what
  separates a useful trap from an impossible one.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-03. A failure counts after promotion.** A failure found on a fast tier is replayed one tier up and
  under the numerical variants of §26.6 before it enters any count. FACT (Appendix H): in one study 62.5% of unsafe
  simulated test cases led to unsafe behaviour on a test track; simulated failures do not all transfer, and the first
  transfer to check is between our own tiers.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-04. Smallest, not worst.** The smallest change that flips a verdict is more informative than the world
  with the lowest score. Every failure is shrunk before it is reported.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-05. Exploration and certification never share data.** Search-selected, tilted, forked and
  battery-selected episodes find failures. Only nominal draws, or draws with recorded bounded weights, enter a bound.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-06. Everything Nemesis finds is discovery data.** Hard worlds and counterexamples train and test
  designs; they are never a confirmation sample of §24.13.
- **ADR-NEMESIS-07. Screens are audited, not trusted.** Whatever a cheap stage discards is sampled, and a known winner
  is planted.

### 26.3 The search space and the legality set [S]

A world is a base scene, an edit list, a disturbance schedule and a parameter vector. The base scene is a seeded
derivation in a scene grammar, so it has a `SceneId`. An edit is one atomic, typed change in authored units. Every
edit declares which causal paths it changes.

| Edit class | Examples | Declares that it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Geometry | A sill height, an edge radius, a door width, the place of an obstacle, a stair riser | Collision geometry and clearance |
| Material | Friction of a floor patch, a compliant mat, the presence of a film | Contact; optics as well if the material is wet or glossy |
| Optics | The position of a light, a glass panel, a reflectance | Sensing only |
| Fixture | A door-closer torque, a conveyor speed, a cart left in an aisle | Dynamics of fixtures |
| Clock and link | A sensor latency, a dropout burst, packet jitter | Timing only |
| Disturbance | A push of stated impulse at a stated time and direction, a payload shift | Exogenous forces |
| Parameter | A value inside a card's uncertainty: actuator gain, friction coefficient, mass, sensor bias | Model parameters |

Moving a light can change the depth-dropout distribution without changing friction, and wetness may change both. An
edit that cannot say which path it changes is not admitted, because Autopsy (§27) will later treat the edit as an
intervention on one mechanism.

**The legality profile.** DESIGN: a legality profile, schema `frob.legality/1`, lists a bound for every editable
quantity with a provenance class: `cited` with its source, `card` when it is the uncertainty of a card, or `assumed`.
Dimensional norms for buildings come from cited sources (OPEN-HAB-1 in §19); until then the bounds are `assumed` and
every counterexample that touches one says so. Legality is checked on the final world, not only on each edit
(INV-NEMESIS-02): two individually legal obstacles can narrow a passage below its minimum width.

**Admissibility and prior.** Each world carries its legality verdict with the bounds it touches, and its density under
the task's nominal randomization where one is defined. The first decides whether the world may be used; the second
says how often it would arise and decides nothing.

**Small.** The size of a world relative to its base is lexicographic: the number of atomic edits, then the normalized
physical distance, each edit's magnitude divided by the width of its legal range.

### 26.4 The falsification score and the search [F]

**Score.** A binary failure has zero gradient almost everywhere. The search therefore minimizes the requirement margin
of §24.5, the dimensionless distance of the episode from violating its verdict formula. A world whose margin is
negative, after the boundary check of §24.5, is a candidate failure. Minimizing a robustness margin is established
falsification practice with a standing tool competition (FACT from a search summary, Appendix H, to be confirmed at
the source). The same search serves the design-pair question of §25: for rivals A and B, find a legal world in which
the predicted preference reverses by a practically important margin.

**Methods.** Every campaign runs the first row at equal budget as its baseline arm.

| Method | Space | Foundation | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card-uniform randomization | All | The task's randomization | Baseline arm and fallback |
| Cross-entropy search | Continuous parameters, as normal germs | The proposal fit of `fs-uq` (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance/proposal.rs`, a weighted mean update at unit covariance) | Tilts the sampler toward low margin, truncated at legality bounds |
| Derivative-free descent | Continuous parameters | `fs-dfo` | Local descent on the margin under common random numbers |
| Quality-diversity archive | Mixed | `fs-archive` | Keeps failures binned by failure class and binding atom, so one mode does not absorb the budget |
| Grammar mutation | Discrete edits | Habitat's grammar | Structural changes, guided by which requirement atoms an edit has moved before |
| Splitting on forks | Per-step disturbances | Snapshots and child addresses | Failures driven by noise that arrives after a fork (§26.9) |

A scenario-language sampler given the same generator and the same monitors is an admissible second baseline arm. When
the question is which of two designs is better, the order in which candidate worlds are evaluated may follow a
value-of-information ranking (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-voi` provides a ranking-flip probability); the ranking
orders work and never decides a verdict. Every evaluated world is recorded with its address and with the law that
proposed it, so a later importance-weighted estimate remains possible. Search runs on a fast tier. Nothing it finds is
a counterexample until the oracle and the guard have passed it.

**Ceiling, stated before any speed test.** HYPOTHESIS: for failures of nominal probability near 3 × 10⁻⁵, the search
reaches a first failure with at least 3 times fewer simulated steps than card-uniform randomization (GATE-NEMESIS-02).
A planning recomputation that charges the oracle and the shrinking puts the ratio near 6 (Appendix C), and published
measurements of adaptive stress testing range from no gain over naive sampling on a policy-in-the-loop task to 2 to 20
times in a driving study (FACT, Appendix H). The ceiling of this lever on the cost of one found failure is therefore
under ten, and its effect on the time to a design decision is bounded by the share of that time spent finding
failures, which the phase ledger of §39 measures.

### 26.5 The solvability oracle [S]

A failing world is classified before it counts.

| Class | Witness | Counts as |
|---|---|---|
| `solvable-causal` | A controller that uses only the candidate's observation interface satisfies the requirement in this world, within every actuator and mission limit: the baseline controller at other gains, another member of the policy family, or a sampling controller on the candidate's sensors (§22) | A counterexample to the candidate's controller or policy |
| `plant-feasible` | A trajectory found with privileged state satisfies the requirement within the body's limits | A counterexample, tagged plant-feasible only: the body can do it, and whether the sensors allow it is a separate question for the observation analyses of §20 and §25 |
| `not-excluded` | Necessary conditions pass (a route exists in the clearance complex of §19, wrench and torque envelopes suffice, the energy budget closes) and no trajectory was found | Unresolved. Reported, not counted either way |
| `infeasible-for-candidate` | A checked necessary condition fails, with its dual vector or its bottleneck named | Not a counterexample. Counted in Arena's infeasible column (§24.8) and offered to the retrofit query of §19 |

Three rules. A witness is itself a simulated run, so it passes the artifact guard: a witness that succeeds through
solver-created energy is not a witness. An infeasibility verdict needs a checked necessary condition; a failed search
for a witness proves nothing and yields `not-excluded`. And the oracle runs for the candidate's body, not for a
generic point robot: connectivity of free space at a clearance radius certifies occupancy only, not steering,
traction, reach or perception.

### 26.6 The artifact guard [S]

Before a failure or a witness counts, it is replayed three ways (INV-NEMESIS-05).

1. **One tier up.** From the fast tier to `batch-cpu` in double precision, and for anything that will enter the
   hard-world set, on the referee. The decision-level equivalence test of §35 decides whether the higher tier
   reproduces the failure: the same verdict, the same failure class and the same binding atom, with the failure time
   inside the recorded envelope.
2. **Numerical variants.** Half the step; twice the solver iterations; the alternative contact law; a solver tolerance
   ten times tighter. These are the perturbations that should not matter to a physical failure (§23).
3. **Energy audit.** The energy ledger of §29 is read for the run. A failure or a witness whose integrator-and-solver
   residual exceeds its envelope is flagged.

A failure that vanishes under any of these is labelled `artifact-candidate` with the variant that removed it. It is
excluded from the hard-world set and from counterexample counts, and it is written to the fidelity ledger of §16 as a
tier-sensitive case, because a failure of the simulator is also a finding. Two planted artifacts seed the negative
controls: a friction cone linearized as a box grants √2 times the friction coefficient along its diagonals, and
penetration recovery can pump energy into a hopping body.

### 26.7 Shrinking to a minimal counterexample world [F]

**Input.** A failing world w = base ⊕ {e₁ … e_n} whose base passes. If the candidate fails in the base world there is
nothing to shrink and the base is the counterexample.

**Test relation.** A subset of the edits *fails* when the world built from it is legal, replays the candidate
deterministically, violates the same requirement atom with the same failure class, and does so on at least 48 of 64
paired seeds (TARGET). A subset that fails by a different mechanism is not a pass and not the same failure: it is
recorded as a separate finding with its own classification, and the shrink continues on the original mechanism.

**Algorithm.** Delta debugging. Partition the edits into k parts, starting at k = 2. If some part fails, recurse on
it. Otherwise if some complement fails, recurse on it with k − 1 parts. Otherwise double k, and stop when k exceeds
the number of edits. The result is 1-minimal: removing any single edit makes the failure disappear. Then each
remaining continuous edit is moved toward its nominal value by bisection until the failure is lost, and the last
failing value is kept.

**What minimal means.** 1-minimal under removal of atomic edits, and nothing stronger. Counterexample: let the world
fail when edits a and b are both present or when c, d and e are all present. With the edits listed as (a, c, d, e, b),
delta debugging tests the halves {a, c} and {d, e, b}, which pass, then the singletons and {e, b}, which pass, then
finds that the complement {c, d, e, b} fails, and ends at {c, d, e}: three edits, 1-minimal, while the two-edit
counterexample {a, b} exists. DESIGN: when the original list has at most 12 edits, every subset of one or two edits is
also tested, 78 tests at most, and the smaller counterexample replaces the result. A world that passes this check is
labelled minimum among pairs; no world is called the closest failure, because failure sets are not monotone.

**Size.** TARGET: at most three atomic edits. A shrunk world with more is kept and labelled `non-minimal(k)`.

**The file.** Format FMT-FMCW, extension `.fmcw`, schema `frob.mcw/1`.

```json
{ "schema": "frob.mcw/1", "id": "w:3fa9…", "task": "t:kitchen-tray@88c1…", "requirement": "delivered",

  "edits": [
```

The file carries what replays it (INV-NEMESIS-04): identities, edits, addresses and state digests. A file that does
not replay with identical digests on its originating backend is invalid. It also carries its passing neighbours, the
worlds with one edit removed. By 1-minimality they exist, and each is an interventional contrast in the sense of §27:
the failing world and its neighbours are the two experiments that are carried through the loop together, the failure
and the edit that removes it. A minimal world is also the input to the retrofit question of §19, whether a cheap
amendment to the place fixes the failure with the policy frozen.

**The loop.** Propose a design; search for violating worlds; classify; guard; shrink; update the hard-world set and
the region of worlds the design is known to handle; edit the design or the place; repeat.

### 26.8 The hard-world set [S]

For each task family and robot class, Nemesis keeps a versioned set of minimal counterexample worlds, schema
`frob.hardset/1`, as a list of `.fmcw` identities with the failure mode each exposes. `nemesis.hardset run` evaluates
a design against the set of its class and returns a scorecard with the columns of §24.8. A world enters the set only
as `solvable-causal` or `plant-feasible` and only after promotion on the referee. A world leaves the set by a recorded
decision. Three uses are refused: as a confirmation sample (FROB-REF-567), as nominal draws for a bound
(FROB-REF-560), and as the only content of a battery, since a battery made of past failures overfits them (§26.11).

### 26.9 Rare failures: exploration and certification

Two questions are routinely confused. *Where does this design fail?* is a search, and any method that finds failures
faster is welcome. *Is its failure probability below p⋆?* is a claim, and only estimators with a valid finite-sample
bound may make it. The certification lane issues a statistical bound; its proof class is `Tested` at confidence 1 − δ,
never `Certified`, which §12 reserves for certificates a checker accepted.

| Estimator | Returns | Valid bound at a fixed budget | Lane | Foundation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crude nominal sampling, zero-failure rule | p ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n) after n episodes without a failure | Yes, exact | Certification | Ours; integer counts and outward rounding |
| Crude nominal sampling, sequential | A confidence sequence for p that ends certified, refuted or exhausted | Yes, at any stopping time | Certification | `fs-eproc` (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-eproc/src/bernoulli.rs`, a Beta(½, ½) mixture with a cap of 10⁶ samples) |
| Bounded-weight importance sampling | An estimate of p, a two-sided confidence sequence, and a lower bound for refutation | Yes, for an explicit Gaussian law and a proposal fixed before the run | Certification, for refutation and for estimates | `fs-uq` (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_importance.rs`) |
| Cross-entropy tilting | A proposal for a later stage; failures | No | Exploration | `fs-uq` proposal fit |
| Splitting on forks | Failures; a rate that is unbiased in theory | No | Exploration | Ours, on Chronicle's forks |
| Extreme-value extrapolation | Not built | Checking a fitted tail at 10⁻⁶ takes the same nominal episodes that certify directly | — | — |

**Crude sampling [S].** The cost of a claim is fixed by arithmetic. With δ = 0.05:

| Target p⋆ | Nominal episodes with no failure | Time at an illustrative 400 episodes per second |
|---|---|---|
| 10⁻⁴ | 29,956 | 75 s |
| 10⁻⁵ | 299,572 | 12.5 min |
| 10⁻⁶ | 2,995,731 | 2.1 h |

Throughput is §36's to establish; the middle column does not move. Inside the qualification lane the level is that of
the request, so the tenth request needs 76,959 clean episodes for 10⁻⁴ instead of 29,956 (§24.13). Proving absence has
a fixed price that no sampler discounts without assuming where the failures live.

**Bounded-weight importance sampling [S].** The proposal is a defensive mixture q = ε·p + (1 − ε)·p_shifted, so the
likelihood ratio w = p/q is at most 1/ε, and ε·w·1[failure] is an observation in [0, 1] whose mean is ε·p. A
confidence sequence on that mean, divided by ε, bounds p at every stopping time. What `fs-uq` provides (FACT, same
file): exactly this construction for parameters that are independent or jointly Gaussian in normal germs, a proposal
chosen before the run or fitted on a separate completed pilot whose data are never reused, checkpoints, and a cap of
10⁶ evaluations. What we add (DESIGN): declared monotone transforms from normal germs to non-Gaussian card
distributions on our side of the airlock; the same mixture for discrete scene edits, where the weight bound holds for
the same reason; and a variance-adaptive betting sequence on the scaled observation, because the range-based sequence
that `fs-uq` feeds is, in its own words, much wider than the descriptive error for rare events. The scope is narrow
and is stated in every result. With weights bounded by 1/ε, the upper bound after n runs without a failure cannot fall
below about ln(1/δ)/(ε·n), so importance sampling is never the cheaper way to certify absence. It earns its place in
two cases: refuting a target, by a lower confidence bound that exceeds p⋆, and tightening an estimate once failures
have been observed.

**Exploration [F].** Cross-entropy tilting moves static parameters toward low margin; it needs whole episodes and
reuses no snapshot. Splitting restores a snapshot of a world that has reached a distress level and continues it many
times under fresh per-step disturbances. DESIGN: a child world has the address of its parent extended by a fork path,
its per-step disturbance streams after the fork tick are re-keyed by the child address (the replicate mode of §27.3),
and its prefix is shared bit for bit. The distress score is an online past-time margin (§24.5) with fixed levels
chosen before the run. A fork replays its parent identically unless noise enters after the fork, so splitting applies
only to failures driven by per-step disturbances; a request to split on static parameters alone is refused
(FROB-REF-569).

TARGET, in its surviving form: with an informative score, exploration reaches failures of nominal probability 10⁻⁴
to 10⁻⁵ with 10 to 100 times fewer episodes than crude sampling. FACT (Appendix H): the measured gains in the sources
read are 2 to 20 times in one driving study and about 5 times in mean-square error on one policy-in-the-loop task.
Splitting needs per-step disturbances; tilting and the defensive mixture also act on static scene parameters. The
evidence cuts both ways (FACT, Appendix H). The splitting estimator is unbiased whatever the score and the number of
replicas. In one published study adaptive splitting with temporal-logic scores estimated 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ for a true
4.8 × 10⁻⁵, with a spread five times the mean, and its authors note that neither splitting nor sampling has a
guarantee on estimation error at a fixed budget; in the same study a cross-entropy sampler returned 7.6 × 10⁻²² for a
true 2.0 × 10⁻³. On a policy-in-the-loop task at p near 2.5 × 10⁻⁵, plain splitting had a relative mean-square error
of 1.02 against 1.09 for naive sampling. That is why exploration issues failures and rates labelled `Estimated`, and
never a bound.

**The catalogue of modes.** Every failure that passes the guard is filed by failure class and binding atom. A
Good–Turing monitor estimates the probability mass of modes not yet seen as the number of modes seen exactly once
divided by the number of failures seen. It is the stopping signal for exploration.

**The decision model.** States: the finalist's failure probability is below the target p⋆ or not. Actions: explore,
draw nominal episodes, stop and certify, stop and refute, stop exhausted. Loss: a false certification costs far more
than a false refutation, which costs more than wasted episodes. The policy is a fixed table, not an adaptive
controller.

```text
Decision:          Is the failure probability of finalist F on task T, at tier X, below p⋆?
Policy:            1. Explore to 20% of the budget, or until the unseen-mode mass is at most 0.05.
                   2. If a bounded-weight or nominal lower bound exceeds p⋆: stop, refuted.
                   3. Spend the rest on nominal episodes under the sequential binomial bound.
                   4. End certified (upper bound below p⋆), refuted, or exhausted with the bound reached.
Applies to:        Finalists only. A screening rung never buys a rare-failure bound.
Obligation:        The bound holds at confidence 1 - delta for independent nominal episodes with a failure
                   predicate frozen before sampling, scoped to its tier and its randomization.
Primary risk:      Exploration data leaking into the bound. Countermeasure: episodes carry their proposal
                   law; the bound refuses any episode not drawn nominally (INV-NEMESIS-03).
Failure modes:     An uninformative distress score; forks that carry no fresh randomness.
Fallback:          Crude nominal sampling alone.
Fallback trigger:  Under 1% of forks reach the next level; a failed null self-test of the bound.
Budgeted mode:     Exploration at most 20% of the budget; then a refusal that carries the bound reached.
Baseline:          Crude nominal sampling with the zero-failure rule at the same budget.
Rollback:          A configuration flag to the baseline; bounds already issued stay valid.
Verification:      Coverage of the bound on synthetic studies with known p; planted two-mode failure found;
                   a tilted episode offered to the bound is refused. Shadow first, then default.
```

Worked numbers, illustrative. Splitting to 10⁻⁶ with six levels of conditional probability 0.1 and 10⁴ continuations
per level uses 6 × 10⁴ fragments, about fifty times fewer than the 3 × 10⁶ nominal episodes of the table, and under
independence its coefficient of variation is at least √(6 × 0.9 / (0.1 × 10⁴)) = 7%; correlation between continuations
of one parent makes it larger. If throughput is ten times lower than illustrated, the 10⁻⁶ claim costs 21 hours:
certify 10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.

**Scope of every bound.** The probability is under the task's nominal randomization, on the stated tier, for the
frozen requirement. It is a statement about the model at that tier. Under the claims of §8 it is never Reality, and a
bound obtained on a fast tier is provisional until promoted.

### 26.10 Auditing what screening discarded [S]

The cascade of §28 spends cheap stages to discard designs before expensive ones. A comparison among survivors says
nothing about what was discarded: an e-value between the eighth and ninth finalists is silent about the design a
screen threw away at the first rung. Nemesis audits every screening stage in two ways. This section owns the
audit: its verb is `nemesis.audit`, its gate is GATE-NEMESIS-07, and the cascade applies it stage by stage without
restating it (§28.12).

- **Sampling discarded strata.** Discards are stratified by reason for discard, design family and score band. Before
  any score exists, a keyed random draw assigns a share of each stratum to the audit lane, 10% of the stage's spend
  (TARGET). Audited designs run at the next stage at a fixed sample size as if they had survived, and are never
  cancelled by a race. The result is a false-discard rate per stratum with an exact binomial bound: with no top-k
  design among m audited discards the rate is at most 1 − δ^(1/m), which is 9.5% at m = 30 and 5.0% at m = 59 for
  δ = 0.05. The approximation ln(1/δ)/m (10.0% at m = 30) is not used.
- **Planting a winner.** A design whose rank at the final stage is known from an earlier full evaluation, and which
  the screen represents poorly, such as a gait outside the atlas or a body far from the policy family's training set,
  enters the population under a blinded identity. It must survive every screen.

A missed planted winner, or an audited miss rate above the declared tolerance, labels the campaign's results
`screen-biased` and bypasses the stage for the affected strata until it is re-qualified. The audit lane is also the
only calibration source for the rank-agreement measurement between rungs (§28): it supplies designs that were not
selected by the scores being calibrated.

### 26.11 Covering scene sets by submodular selection [S]

A battery has a budget, and the scenes worth running are those that expose failure modes other scenes miss. DESIGN:

- **Incidence.** A matrix of scenes against failure modes, built from the catalogue of §26.9 over a reference set of
  designs. A scene exposes a mode when at least one reference design fails in that mode in that scene after the guard.
  Each mode has a declared weight: frequency × severity × number of designs affected.
- **Objective.** f(S) is the total weight of modes exposed by at least one scene of S. It is monotone and submodular:
  a scene is worth only what earlier scenes missed.
- **Algorithm.** Lazy greedy selection: keep scenes in a priority queue by their last computed marginal gain, and
  recompute only the top, since gains never increase.
- **Certificate.** For a selection S of k scenes, the best selection of k satisfies OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the sum of the k
  largest remaining marginal gains (PO-NEMESIS-03). Greedy also guarantees f(S) ≥ (1 − 1/e)·OPT_k at fixed
  cardinality. With unequal scene costs the selection ranks by gain per second and the guarantee weakens to the
  constant of the cost-benefit variant; the certificate still applies and is usually far tighter than either constant.
- **Held-out slots.** One slot in five is filled at random from held-out scenes, so the battery cannot consist only of
  yesterday's failures.

Worked instance, illustrative: 40 modes of total weight 100. Greedy gains of 31, 22, 14, 9, 6 and 4 cover 86. The six
largest remaining gains sum to 12, so OPT₆ ≤ 98 and the selection is within 88% of the best six-scene battery, against
a worst-case guarantee of 63%.

```text
Obligation:        Weighted coverage is monotone submodular; the certificate above is checked on every
                   selection and stored with the battery.

```

## 27. Autopsy: counterfactual debugging

Crate `frob-autopsy`, layer 5.

### 27.1 What Autopsy owns

`frob explain` assembles what happened: the failure class, the energy ledger, saturation, slip, and the earliest
anomaly before the terminal event, which it labels `Estimated` because ordering anomalies in time is a heuristic.
Autopsy turns that heuristic into a measurement. It restores the world to a moment before the failure, changes one
thing, runs forward, and records whether the failure still happens. A proposed cause is tested by intervention; it is
never inferred from the order in which monitors or solver stages reported.

Autopsy owns the semantics of forks (their classification and the earliest tick at which a given change may be
applied), the intervention vocabulary `do(factor := value)`, placebo forks, interventional contrasts with their flip
rates, coalition experiments and budgeted Shapley summaries, minimal repair sets, paired re-runs from the episode
start for design parameters, the reading of first divergences on forks (§27.10; the algorithm and its verb belong to
§29.8), and the transcript of a debugging session. It consumes snapshots, ring buffers, replay and the fork mechanism
from Chronicle (§29), verdicts from Arena (§24), the typed world edits of §26.3 and
the typed design edits of §14, the shrinker of §26.7, and the tiers of §16.

A value in this chapter that follows from a stated formula is a derived value and a known answer of §27.16; every
other number carries its label.

Everything in this chapter is [S] except one claim. Forks, classification, contrasts with a stability report,
coalition tables and the divergence algorithm are established technique applied carefully. The claim that an agent
repairs designs in fewer turns with these tools than with logs is a HYPOTHESIS with its own gate (GATE-AUTOPSY-08).

Counterfactual replay from restorable simulator state is established practice, and incumbent simulators expose
restorable state and opt-in run-to-run determinism on one GPU (FACT, Appendix H). Nothing here is claimed as new. What
the engine must supply for it to work is listed as needs: an exact null replay, replay of one world outside the batch
it ran in, forks that mix interventions on different model variants in one batch, and a double-precision tier that
runs the same model.

### 27.2 Decisions

- **ADR-AUTOPSY-01. A cause is what an intervention removes, net of a placebo.** A factor is named only when removing
  it prevents the failure more often than an irrelevant perturbation does.
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-02. Classify a fork before restoring state.** Whether a change may be applied at a mid-episode state
  is decided from the model difference it makes, before any state is touched.
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-03. Design-parameter interventions re-run from the episode start.** History-dependent state would
  otherwise answer a different question (§27.8).
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-04. Attribution reports a flip rate at round-off scale; double precision is the default for one
  failure.** Closed-loop legged control amplifies round-off enough to flip marginal outcomes in single precision over
  long windows (§27.9). Single precision on the originating tier is admitted by a measured rule (the single-precision
  lane, §27.19).
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-05. Contrasts first; Shapley values are a summary on a budget.** The table of what each intervention
  and each pair did is the result. A Shapley summary is computed only when that table does not already answer the
  question.
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-06. Diagnostic interventions are not repairs.** Switching a torque limit off locates a cause; it is
  not an edit anyone can build. The two kinds are typed apart.
- **ADR-AUTOPSY-07. The cause is a cause in the model.** Every attribution is scoped to the model, the tier and the
  declared factor list.

### 27.3 Forks from snapshots [S]

A fork is a parent episode, a fork tick and a change set. DESIGN:

- **State.** A fork starts from a complete checkpoint in the sense of §29: physical state; contact feature memory and
  impulses; clock phases and packet queues; controller, filter and policy hidden states; actuator electrical and
  thermal states; terrain edits; the command cursor. Random streams are counter-based and keyed by address, so they
  have no state to save.
- **Where forks can start.** At any stored snapshot; at any tick held in the per-world ring buffer; and at any tick of
  any replayable episode, by replaying the world from its address up to that tick. The ring buffer is an optimization,
  not a requirement. At a per-world state of tens of kilobytes (§36), twenty snapshots covering two seconds for each
  of 8,192 worlds would take several gigabytes, so full-state rings are kept for a reservoir of worlds and every other
  failing world is reconstructed by replay, at the cost of one episode prefix on the CPU.
- **Identity.** A child world has the address of its parent extended by a fork path. It shares its prefix with the
  parent by reference. A fork never mutates its parent (INV-AUTOPSY-04).
- **Randomness.** By default a child reads the same streams as its parent, so the intervention is the only difference.
  A replicate changes one declared thing more: a state perturbation of stated size, or the key of one named stream.
- **Batching.** One failing world is forked K times into a batch of K children. This relies on the rule that a world's
  trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in (§35). Children that carry different model variants use the mixed
  batches of §17.
- **Horizon.** A fork starts at a lead Δ before the failure, from the ladder 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 s, and by default runs
  to one second past the parent's failure time (TARGET), or to the episode horizon when asked.

### 27.4 Classifying a fork [S]

The service classifies every requested change before restoring anything, and returns the class, the earliest valid
fork tick, and the fields of the checkpoint that forced a later or earlier start. The five classes below are the
intervention semantics, and this section owns them. The fork mechanism of §29.8 (snapshots, restore, the shared
prefix, the verb `run.fork`) implements exactly these five and adds none.

| Class | What changes | Where it may start | State handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuation | Nothing (the null fork), or a placebo | Any checkpoint whose physical, clock, sensor and policy contracts match the run | Restored bit for bit |
| Exogenous intervention | A future disturbance, a setpoint, a scene property the robot has not yet touched | The fork tick | Restored; nothing regenerated |
| Contact-affecting intervention | Friction or compliance of a surface in contact at the fork tick; the contact law | The fork tick | Positions and velocities restored; contact memory discarded and the first solve run cold with a raised iteration cap; the fork is labelled |
| Information intervention | Sensor noise or latency; an estimator replaced by privileged state | The fork tick | Packet queues and filter state re-initialized as the intervention states; labelled diagnostic where no hardware could realize it |
| Structural or design-parameter intervention | Mass, inertia, geometry, a gear ratio, an actuator card, a sensor mount, controller gains or memory, policy weights | The authored initial condition only | The edited design is settled from its own reset cache (§24.7); paired exogenous draws are reused; contact memory, proprioception, sensor histories and policy actions are regenerated |

A continuation whose contracts do not match is refused (FROB-REF-580). A structural change requested at a mid-episode
tick is refused with the earliest valid tick, which is zero, and the list of fields that forced the restart
(FROB-REF-581). Negative control: grafting the contact warm start of the 60 mm wheel of §24.10 onto the 75 mm design
at the lip must return a structured refusal (FROB-REF-582), not a run.

### 27.5 Interventions and the placebo [S]

An intervention is written `do(factor := value)`. A factor is a path into the world or the model whose mechanism is
declared: an edit class of §26.3 for worlds, a typed edit of §14 for designs, or one of a short list of diagnostic
factors such as `torque_limit := off`, `estimator := privileged` and `latency := 0`. Each intervention changes one
declared mechanism, and the model difference it makes is computed and stored with the fork (INV-AUTOPSY-02). A change
that would alter two mechanisms, such as a wet floor that changes both friction and depth returns, is split into two
interventions or refused (FROB-REF-586).

Three forks give every intervention its meaning.

- **The null fork** applies the empty intervention. It must reproduce the parent bit for bit on the originating
  profile (INV-AUTOPSY-01). If it does not, the record is unsound and nothing is attributed (FROB-REF-583).
- **A placebo fork** applies a change that no physical cause could depend on: one state coordinate perturbed by a
  relative 10⁻¹². The **placebo flip rate** ρ₀ is the fraction of placebo forks whose verdict differs from the
  parent's. It measures how much of an apparent rescue is mere sensitivity.
- **Replicates** of an intervention apply the same change with independent perturbations of the same size. A second
  series at a relative 10⁻⁷, the scale of single-precision round-off, is run for every intervention that changes the
  outcome.

### 27.6 Interventional contrasts and the flip rate [S]

For a failing world and an intervention c with R replicates, Autopsy reports three numbers.

| Quantity | Definition |
|---|---|
| Rescue rate | The fraction of replicates of do(c) in which the requirement is not violated |
| Contrast | The rescue rate minus the placebo flip rate ρ₀ |
| Flip rate under perturbation | The fraction of replicates whose verdict differs from the unperturbed fork of the same intervention, at 10⁻¹² and at 10⁻⁷ |

Over a set of failing worlds the rescue rate estimates the probability of necessity, P(no failure under do(¬c) |
failure). A factor is named only when its contrast is tested above zero against the placebo forks, by an exact
one-sided test at the fixed replicate count or by an e-process when forks are bought one at a time, with the level
divided by the number of factors (INV-AUTOPSY-05).

| Label | Condition (TARGET thresholds) |
|---|---|
| `but-for` | One factor with contrast at least 0.5, tested above the placebo, and a flip rate of at most 0.1 at 10⁻¹² |
| `contributing` | Tested above the placebo with a smaller contrast |
| `joint` | No single factor rescues and a coalition does |
| `alternative` | Several single factors each rescue |
| `precedes-snapshot` | No intervention at the fork tick rescues, and the same change applied from the episode start does |
| `precision-sensitive` | The failure is not reproduced in double precision and is not admitted to the single-precision lane (§27.9, §27.19) |
| `chaotic: unattributable` | ρ₀ above 0.2. No factor is named |

Illustrative numbers: of 200 failing worlds re-run from their forks, `do(torque_limit := off)` prevents the failure in
164, `do(friction := nominal)` in 38, and a placebo in 12. Net of the placebo the contrasts are 0.76 and 0.13. For a
marginal failure, or for causes that interact, the report names a set of factors and not a top factor.

### 27.7 Coalitions and budgeted Shapley summaries [S]

Causes interact, so single interventions are not enough. For a factor list N of size m, the value of a coalition S ⊆ N
is v(S), the rescue rate when all of S are applied together.

**Budget.** DESIGN: at most 12 factors, all singles and all pairs. That is 78 coalitions; with 8 replicates each, 64
placebo forks and the null fork it comes to 689 forks, and the 10⁻⁷ series adds 8 forks for each coalition that
rescues. With forks of two or three seconds this is a small batch on any tier. For each pair the interaction v({i, j})
− v({i}) − v({j}) + v(∅) is reported.

**Shapley summary.** The Shapley value of factor i is φᵢ = Σ over S ⊆ N∖{i} of |S|!·(m − |S| − 1)!/m! · [v(S ∪ {i}) −
v(S)]. It is computed exactly for m ≤ 5, which needs 32 coalitions, and by sampled permutations with an interval
otherwise. It is computed only when no single factor and no pair changes the outcome. Exact values over ten factors
would take 2¹⁰ = 1,024 coalitions and leave no budget for a single replicate, so the stability the method depends on
could not be measured.

**What the summary cannot say.** Two hand-checkable cases are in the counterexample catalogue (Appendix N).

- Two factors that rescue only together (v of the pair is 1, of each single 0) and two factors that each rescue alone
  (v is 1 for either single and for the pair) both have Shapley values (½, ½). The repair sets differ: both factors in
  the first case, either one in the second. The coalition table distinguishes them and the summary does not.
- With v(∅) = 0, v({1}) = 1, v({2}) = 0 and v({1, 2}) = 0 the values are (½, −½). They sum to v(N) − v(∅) = 0 as they
  must, a single-factor repair exists, and factor 2 has a negative value because applying it cancels the rescue. A
  Shapley value can be negative and never establishes that a factor is necessary.

FrankenSim's Shapley estimator is variance-based and says of itself that its effects "are not causal effects" (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_shapley.rs`). It is not used here; the interventional estimator is a few hundred
lines of our own.

**Minimal repair sets.** The shrinker of §26.7 runs over the intervention set with the test "the failure no longer
occurs", and returns repair sets that are 1-minimal. A rescue at a fork is not a minimal repair in any stronger sense,
and it is not evidence that an edited design passes the task; that takes §27.8 and then a study.

### 27.8 Design parameters re-run from the episode start [S]

An agent wants to know whether a different gear ratio would have prevented a thermal trip. Applying the new ratio one
second before the trip answers a different question: what happens to a motor that was heated by the old ratio's
currents and is then geared differently. Winding temperature, battery state of charge, wear, estimator bias, a
policy's hidden state and the contact memory all carry the history of the old design, and none has a canonical image
under the edit.

Counterexample, hand-checkable. One thermal node with time constant 60 s, ambient 25 °C and a trip at 115 °C. At a
gear ratio of 12 the steady-state rise for the task's torque is 192 K, so the motor trips at 60·ln(192/102) = 37.95 s
of a 45 s task. Heating scales with the inverse square of the ratio, so at 16 the rise is 108 K.

| Experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| Ratio 16 from the episode start | Peak temperature 25 + 108·(1 − e^(−0.75)) = 82.0 °C at 45 s. The task passes with 33 K in hand |
| Ratio 16 applied at 36.95 s, one second before the trip | The winding is already at 113.3 °C and still heading for 133 °C. It trips at 42.4 s. The fork says the edit does not prevent the failure |

The fork is not wrong about the experiment it ran. It ran the wrong experiment. DESIGN: an intervention classified as
structural or design-parameter is never applied to a mid-episode state (INV-AUTOPSY-03). `autopsy.rerun` does the
following instead.

1. Apply the typed edit, which gives a new `DesignId` and `ModelId` and a reset cache of its own.
2. Re-run the failing worlds from the start on the same world addresses, so scene, disturbances and noise are paired,
   while events that depend on the robot's actions keep their own logically keyed streams.
3. Re-run a sample of worlds that passed, because a repair can break what worked.
4. Report the paired outcomes, labelled `Estimated`, and hand the pair of designs to `eval.compare` for a verdict
   under §24.12.

The controller must be able to act on the edited body without retraining: the baseline controller re-synthesized from
the edited model (§22), a family policy (§23) or a sampling controller. A policy trained for the original body alone
is refused for design factors (FROB-REF-587), since its failure on the new body would be attributed to the edit. A
forced fork of a design parameter remains available in a diagnostic mode, labelled as such, because it is occasionally
the question someone means to ask.

### 27.9 Precision for attribution [S]

FACT (Appendix H): learned continuous-control policies in closed loop have measured maximal Lyapunov exponents of 0.03
to 0.16 per control step on walker and cheetah tasks, and a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degrees in a torso angle produces
significant divergence after 50 steps. The uncontrolled systems are not chaotic; the sensitivity comes from the policy
with contact. At 0.16 per step over 80 steps, which is 2 s at that benchmark's 25 ms control period, a perturbation
grows by about 3.6 × 10⁵. Single-precision round-off of 6 × 10⁻⁸ then reaches about 2% of the state scale, enough to
flip a marginal failure; double-precision round-off of 10⁻¹⁶ stays near 4 × 10⁻¹¹.

DESIGN: by default attribution runs on `batch-cpu` in double precision, or on the referee. For a failure recorded on
a single-precision tier the procedure is:

1. Replay the null fork on the originating backend and require identical digests.
2. Replay the same checkpoint in double precision on `batch-cpu` and apply the decision-level equivalence test of §35:
   the same verdict, failure class and binding atom, with the failure time inside the recorded envelope.
3. If the two agree, attribute in double precision. If they do not, the failure goes to the single-precision lane
   (§27.19) with the numerical-variant interventions first; only if that lane does not admit it is the failure
   labeled `precision-sensitive`, attribution refused (FROB-REF-584) and the world passed to the artifact guard of
   §26.6.

Every attribution reports its flip rates at 10⁻¹² and at 10⁻⁷, and the growth of the separation between the placebo
forks and the parent over the fork horizon, so a reader sees how far the result is from the regime where nothing can
be attributed.

### 27.10 The first-divergence algorithm [S]

Comparing two runs, a fork with its parent, one tier with another, or one backend with another, starts with the first
place they differ. Chronicle's teleport does this for exact classes by stepping digests. The general algorithm covers
tolerance classes and avoids one trap. §29.8 owns it and `telemetry.divergence` runs it; it is restated here because
every attribution reads its output.

```text
first_divergence(A, B, mode):
  align the committed ticks and events of A and B by stable identifiers; a variable with no counterpart
    is listed as unpaired and is never matched by position
  if mode is exact:
      c_t = H(c_{t-1}, d_t) is the chained digest of the per-tick state digests d_t, stored at checkpoints
      bisect over checkpoints for the least k with c_k(A) ≠ c_k(B)
      replay both runs from checkpoint k − 1 and compare d_t tick by tick: the first unequal tick is t1
  if mode is tolerance, with a scale per class of variable:
      for each block of ticks, in chronological order:
          [lo, hi] = bounds on the block maximum of the scaled error
          hi ≤ 1 : the block agrees; go on
          lo > 1 : a mismatch lies inside; descend into the block
          otherwise : unresolved; refine this block before looking at any later block
      where samples are missing, report the earliest possible interval and the coverage gap
  at t1, re-run the tick with per-stage digests or residuals: the first stage over tolerance is s1
  report t1, s1, the variables that differ under their physical names and units, and the first tick at
    which a requirement atom or a task decision differs
```

**The trap.** Bisection is valid only on a predicate that is monotone and decided. "Some tick up to t differs" is
monotone. "The states differ at tick t" is not, because an error can vanish and return. Counterexample: two traces of
eight ticks that differ at ticks 1 and 5 and agree elsewhere. A midpoint search on the pointwise predicate probes tick
3 or 4, sees agreement, moves right and reports tick 5. The chained digest reports tick 1. The same trap applies to
`design bisect` over a lineage (§28), where the predicate must be "the outcome has changed by this edit" and each step
must be decided by a study.

**Rules for reading the result.** The first recorded discrepancy is labelled with its temporal resolution. The order
in which solver stages report residuals inside a tick is not evidence of a physical cause. For a fork against its
parent the first divergent tick is the intervention tick or later, and the useful output is the order in which classes
of variable leave tolerance and the first decision-level divergence. When topology differs between the runs, only
shared interfaces and task observables are compared: a leg that exists in one run has no counterpart in the other.

### 27.11 The decision model for attribution

States: the failure has a single removable cause, a joint cause, or none that can be attributed. Actions: buy a fork,
name a factor or a set, or decline. Loss: naming a wrong cause sends an agent to edit the wrong thing, which costs a
design iteration and its compute; declining costs one fallback to the heuristic ordering.

```text
Decision:          Which repair edit should the agent try first for this failure?
Artifacts:         Typed interventions; a fork ledger; the placebo flip rate; a rescue rate per factor and
                   per pair; minimal repair sets; one label from §27.6.
Obligations:       The empty intervention replays the failure bit for bit, so the starting point is exact.
                   Each intervention changes one declared mechanism (model difference on file).
                   A cause is named only when its contrast beats the placebo under a test at a stated level.
Failure modes:     Chaos makes every nudge look like a cause. The cause precedes the snapshot.
                   The failure does not survive double precision.
Fallback:          The first-anomaly ordering of §29, labelled Estimated.
Fallback trigger:  Null-replay mismatch; placebo flip rate above 0.2; failed equivalence in double precision.
Budgeted mode:     12 factors, singles and pairs, 8 replicates, 64 placebo forks: 689 forks. A Shapley
                   summary only if no single and no pair changes the outcome.
Baseline:          The heuristic ordering; and one-at-a-time contrasts without coalitions, to measure
                   whether ten forks answer as well as seven hundred.
Rollback:          A configuration flag to the heuristic; recorded forks remain valid experiments.
Verification:      Planted single and joint causes; a planted chaotic failure; the thermal counterexample.
```

### 27.13 What Autopsy does not claim

- **Not a cause in the world.** The result is a cause in the model at a tier. FACT (Appendix H): causal models of
  robot failures learned from simulation reached a sim-to-real accuracy of 70% and 72% in one study, with variables
  chosen by the experimenter.
- **Not beyond the factor list.** A failure whose cause is not among the declared factors yields no named factor. The
  planted no-cause control tests this.
- **Not a minimal repair.** Repair sets are 1-minimal under the tested interventions.
- **Not necessity from a Shapley value.** §27.7.
- **Not a passing design.** A rescue at a fork, or a re-run on the failing worlds, is not a verdict on the task. A
  study is.
- **Not an answer in chaotic regimes.** Above the placebo threshold nothing is attributed.
- **Not a proof.** A contrast is a replayable experiment and has class `Tested` on its tier; an attribution drawn from
  contrasts has class `Estimated`.
- **Not a differentiator of the engine.** An incumbent with restorable state and a thin layer can host the same method
  (§39 includes that arm).

### 27.19 Attribution in single precision on the originating tier

**The single-precision lane [F].** This amends ADR-AUTOPSY-04 and §27.9. Double precision on the CPU remains the
default for one failure, where it costs little. It is not a requirement of the evidence. What the evidence
supports is a flip rate measured under perturbations at or above round-off scale (§27.5), and that measurement can
be made on the tier where the failure happened.

*Why it is admissible.* FACT arithmetic on the exponent quoted in §27.9, 0.16 per control step: the growth factor
e^(0.16·n) is 24.5 at n = 20, 602 at n = 40 and 3.6 × 10⁵ at n = 80. Single-precision round-off of 6 × 10⁻⁸
therefore reaches 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ of state scale after 20 steps, 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ after 40 and 2% after 80; at that
benchmark's 25 ms period these are fork windows of 0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s. A verdict that moves under a relative
change of 10⁻⁵ in state also moves under sensor noise and friction scatter two orders larger. It is marginal in
any arithmetic, and the right output is the label `marginal`, not more digits. HYPOTHESIS: the exponent of our
closed loops is of the same order. It was measured on walker and cheetah benchmarks under learned policies, and
the gate below measures ours.

*Procedure* for a failure recorded on a single-precision tier.

1. The null fork on the originating profile must give identical digests (unchanged, INV-AUTOPSY-01).
2. R₀ placebo forks at a relative 10⁻⁷ on the originating tier over the fork window W (DESIGN default R₀ = 32).
   Record the placebo flip rate ρ₀ and the measured growth G(W) of the separation between placebo forks and parent.
3. *Admission.* ρ₀ ≤ 0.1 and G(W)·u ≤ 10⁻⁴ of state scale, with u = 6 × 10⁻⁸. Attribution then runs on the
   originating tier in single precision with at least 16 replicates per intervention at 10⁻⁷, contrasts tested
   against the placebo as in §27.6, and the record carries `tier: <backend>, precision: f32`.
4. *Not admitted.* Shorten W to the longest window that passes, within the bound the first-divergence algorithm of
   §27.10 gives on how late a fork may start, or escalate to double precision as in §27.9.
5. *A failure that double precision does not reproduce* is no longer refused outright. It is attributed in this
   lane with the numerical-variant interventions first: half step, doubled solver iterations, the alternative
   contact law, the stricter step profile. A rescue by one of them gives the label `tier-specific: <variant>` and
   passes the world to the artifact guard of §26.6. Only when the lane is also not admitted is the failure
   labelled `precision-sensitive` and attribution refused.

*A cross-check that can fail.* One attribution in k (DESIGN default k = 8), and every attribution that enters a
report, is repeated in double precision on `batch-cpu`; the top factor, its label and the sign of each contrast
are compared, and the agreement rate is a ledger figure.

*Known answers.* (i) The linear map x ← 1.1735·x has exponent ln 1.1735 = 0.16 per step: after 40 steps a
perturbation has grown by 602, and the measured G must match within 1%. (ii) Negative control: a pendulum released
5 × 10⁻⁸ rad from upright with no control, with the verdict "falls to the positive side". A placebo of 10⁻⁷ rad
flips the side whenever its sign is negative, so ρ₀ is about one half; the lane must not admit the case, and the
label is `marginal`. At a placebo of 10⁻¹² the side is stable, which is why the 10⁻¹² series alone would have
hidden the problem.

*Cost and ceiling.* One failure with ten factors and 16 replicates is 160 forks of 2 s: 320 world-seconds,
seconds on either tier, and the lane saves nothing. A cluster of 200 failures with pair interactions (55
interventions × 32 replicates × 2 s) is 7.0 × 10⁵ world-seconds: about 3 minutes on a device producing 4,000
world-seconds per second, against 12 minutes to 2 hours on `batch-cpu` at 1,000 to 100. Ceiling: the wall time of
bulk attribution, by the ratio of device to CPU world-seconds; and attribution of tier-specific failures, which
the double-precision rule could not attribute at all.

*Owner and verb.* `frob-autopsy`. `autopsy.attribute --lane {f64, tier-f32, auto}`; `auto` applies the admission
rule and records which lane ran and why.
