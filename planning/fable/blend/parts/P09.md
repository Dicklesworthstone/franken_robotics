## 28. Atelier: the studio loop, analyses, comparison, lineage, cascade, decision cores

### 28.1 What Atelier owns

Atelier is the design studio: the loop in which a design is checked, given a controller, simulated, explained, edited
and compared, and every rule that decides how evaluation effort is spent inside that loop. It is two crates.
`frob-analyze` holds the analyses A-01 to A-26, each of which returns a typed finding with ranked repairs. `frob-opt`
holds comparison, lineage queries, optimization, the evaluation ladder and its allocation rules, the co-design
cascade, revalidation, experiment suggestion, registered predictions, reports, and the compiler for the decision-core
tables of §28.19.

### 28.2 Decisions

- **ADR-ATEL-01: the order of the loop is a rule.** Check before you simulate, simulate before you train, train before
  you claim. Reason: each stage is cheaper than the next by roughly an order of magnitude (HYPOTHESIS; settled by the
  phase ledger of §39) and each can refuse the next.
- **ADR-ATEL-02: two study types, never mixed inside one study.** A comparison is declared either as an anytime-valid
  e-process study or as a fixed-n exact study with a pre-analysis of which verdicts are reachable at that n. Reason:
  time-uniform inference and a sealed fixed-n rule compute different decisions; data collected under one is not
  re-read under the other.
- **ADR-ATEL-03: successive halving is the fixed baseline and the fallback of the ladder.** Every adaptive allocation
  rule ships in shadow behind it and reverts to it on a named trigger. Reason: an adaptive rule can fail in a way that
  costs validity or the best design; the fixed table can only cost compute. One asymmetry governs every rule in this
  chapter: reporting an artifact-dependent result as established, or eliminating the best design, costs far more than
  a wasted higher-rung episode.
- **ADR-ATEL-04: a cheap rung discards; it does not select.** A rung may choose finalists only in the strata where the
  rank-agreement ledger (§28.10) has measured that it can. Reason: no published measurement of rank agreement between
  a shared policy and trained specialists exists, and the nearest neighbours are weak (§28.10).
- **ADR-ATEL-05: a hash identifies; only an implication licenses skipping a recheck.** A content hash says that an
  input or a result is the same object. It says nothing about whether a verdict survives a different input. Reuse of a
  verdict needs a dependency argument or an implication argument, recorded and independently checkable (§28.13).
- **ADR-ATEL-06: decision rules are compiled to tables.** Every adaptive decision in the studio is compiled ahead of
  time into an integer guard table, an automaton or a monitor with a version, stored under `registries/cores/`,
  regenerated in continuous integration and diffed. The runtime evaluates tables. No decision reads the wall clock,
  device load or arrival order. Reason: replay, audit, and a rollback that is a configuration flag.
- **ADR-ATEL-07: parametric models rank experiments; distribution-free tests issue verdicts.** The
  value-of-information model of DC-05 never issues a verdict, so a wrong model costs efficiency and never validity.
- **ADR-ATEL-08: agents are the discrete optimizer.** Agents choose topology, leg count and actuator families. The
  studio supplies fast continuous optimization, population evaluation and honest comparison underneath.
- **ADR-ATEL-09: the controller gate.** No adaptive controller is enabled by default without a replay scenario,
  evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback. Adoption is always shadow (report both rules,
  act on the old), then canary, then default; the incumbent in each shadow run is the plan's own reference path.
- **ADR-ATEL-10: two planes.** Kernels, step plans and physics never depend on the dispatcher, the memo policy or any
  estimator. The decision plane reads results and writes requests; it cannot change what a world computes.
- **ADR-ATEL-11: decision cores are named DC-01 to DC-11.** Reason: the letter D already names determinism classes (D0
  to D4) and doctrine rules; a second meaning would make "D2" ambiguous in exactly the sentences where both appear.

### 28.3 The loop

```
 design ──► check ──► synth controller ──► simulate ──► explain ──► edit ──► compare ──┐
   ▲        (static,   (seconds)           (batch or     (telemetry,  (typed)  (anytime-  │
   │         seconds)                       GPU)          energy,              valid or   │
   │                                                      forks)               fixed-n)   │
   └── revalidate ◄── lineage, archive, ladder, cascade, optimize, train ◄────────────────┘
```

The order is a rule, not a suggestion: check before you simulate, simulate before you train, train before you claim.
Each stage is cheaper than the next by roughly an order of magnitude, and each can refuse the next. Two things are
added to the loop of the first studio milestone. An edit does not only produce a new design; it produces an
invalidation set, the verdicts and derived assets that the edit puts in question, and `result.revalidate` says which
survive (§28.13). And a population of designs does not walk the loop one at a time; it climbs the ladder of §28.9
together, in mixed batches (§17), with the cascade of §28.11 deciding who climbs.

The loop is measured, not assumed. Every verb records its phase (authoring, check, synthesis, simulation, training,
promotion, review) and its cost in the phase ledger that the ten-fold test reads (§39). A phase that accounts for less
than nine tenths of elapsed time cannot deliver a ten-fold improvement alone even if its cost falls to zero, so the
ledger is read before any speed claim is made about a stage.

Every analysis returns its result with an evidence class, the model identity, and, when it finds a problem, ranked
repairs as typed edits. Four rules are added.

1. **A bound says which side it is on.** A feasibility result is typed `exact`, `inner` (everything reported feasible
   is feasible) or `outer` (everything reported infeasible is infeasible), and it names the authority model it
   assumed. DESIGN, because the same body gives different answers under different load-sharing models: under
   independent foot forces a stance needs 155 N at the worst foot, and under equal passive springs the forces are
   (175, 115, 85, 25) N against a 160 N cap (FACT, executed stance enumeration; source register).
2. **An exclusion encloses every state and every energy source.** A static or energy argument may exclude a design
   only for a model whose states and energy sources it encloses. A-20 therefore always includes approach kinetic
   energy.
3. **An analysis is a memo node.** Its key is the model identity restricted to the fields it reads, its own version
   and its parameters; its determinism class is D0 for the exact analyses. An edit that does not touch those fields
   recomputes nothing (§28.14).
4. **A screening analysis is labelled as one.** A-04 orbit results, A-17 Gramian conditioning and A-26 regions are
   screening signals. They may discard under the rules of §28.9 and may not establish a comparison.

**Frontier analyses and their pilots.** A-04, A-17's alias witness, A-18, A-24's witness and A-25 are piloted and
gated in the chapters that own their mathematics (§22, §20, §19, §22, §30); A-15 in §31; A-09 remains among the first

robot; its whole value is fewer wrong gradient steps and fewer misread rankings. Pilot: on recorded runs of the
reference quadruped, test whether a region's validity label predicts the sign agreement between a smoothed gradient

nowhere" at a 95% lower bound; fallback: A-26 is a descriptive report with no validity label.

### 28.5 Explain

After a simulation, `frob explain <run>` assembles: the failure taxonomy with first-cause ordering (§29.10); the
energy ledger (§29.9); the saturation report by joint; friction utilization and slip; the gap between estimated and
privileged state; and a ranked list of typed edits predicted to help, computed from A-16. The list is the envelope's
`next_actions`.

Four additions make the explanation harder to misread.

- **Description and cause are different outputs.** The first-cause ordering is a description: it orders anomalies by
  time and is labelled `Estimated`. A causal statement ("torque saturation caused the fall") comes only from a
  controlled intervention, which is the fork machinery of §27 under decision core DC-06. `explain` offers that as a
  `next_action`; it does not imply it.
- **Explaining a comparison.** `frob explain compare <comparison>` clusters the paired-episode differences by contact
  signature and scene region and names the two or three regimes in which the candidate gained or regressed, within a
  token budget. A paired difference is attributed to a regime only if both arms' traces for that world address are
  present; the address-keyed sample of §29.6 guarantees that the same worlds were traced for both arms.
- **Gait by symmetry mode.** Joint trajectories are projected onto the isotypic components of the
  observation-preserving subgroup (A-03) and the energy per component is reported. One line tells the agent which gait
  emerged and how far a payload or a sensor broke the symmetry. It is a readability aid and makes no claim about
  performance.
- **The first physical consequence of an edit.** When a run follows an edit, `explain` leads with the change the agent
  made, the first event at which the two lineages' traces part (the first-divergence algorithm of §29.8), and the
  evidence for the comparison.

### 28.6 Comparing designs honestly (C-15)

`frob eval compare A B --task T` pairs episodes by world address, so both designs meet the same scenes, the same
disturbances and the same randomization draws. A comparison declares its study type before it draws an episode.

**Study type `anytime`.** A betting e-process runs per metric. The comparison stops when a threshold is crossed or the
budget ends, cancels the unfinished episodes, and returns a verdict with the e-value, the confidence sequence for the
effect, the number of pairs used, and the tier. Episodes are consumed in address order, never in completion order, so
preemption and reordering by the daemon leave the e-value unchanged. A metric enters an e-process only with a bound on
its paired difference that was fixed before the first observation (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-eproc` requires an
explicit `LossSpan`, which "must not be estimated from the race data"); the span comes from the task definition (§24),
and a metric without one is refused for this study type.

**Study type `fixed-n`.** The rule is frozen before sampling: n pairs, the counts b (candidate succeeds, baseline
fails) and a (the reverse), the effect (b − a)/n, and exact Clopper–Pearson intervals on the two discordant
probabilities at 97.5% two-sided each, with opposite endpoints subtracted, which gives at least 95% simultaneous
coverage. The candidate is promoted if the lower endpoint exceeds the margin and hard feasibility holds; the verdict
is a practical tie if the interval lies inside ±margin, inferior if the upper endpoint is below −margin, and otherwise
inconclusive. Two rules attach.

- *Reachability pre-analysis.* `eval.compare --plan` enumerates every outcome the frozen rule can produce at the
  declared n and margin and reports which verdicts are reachable. A study whose declared verdict set contains an
  unreachable verdict is refused unless the request acknowledges it. Known answer: at n = 64 with a five-point margin
  there are 2,145 count pairs, of which 521 promote, 521 are inferior, 1,103 are inconclusive and none is a practical
  tie, because perfect concordance gives [−0.0662, +0.0662]; promotion with a = 0 needs b ≥ 15; a tie first becomes
  reachable at n = 86, and only for (0, 0) among 3,828 pairs (FACT, executed enumeration; the endpoint
  1 − 0.0125^(1/64) = 0.0662 and the counts C(66, 2) and C(88, 2) are reproduced by hand).
- *Exact curtailment.* A fixed-n study may stop early only when every admissible completion of the unresolved labels
  yields the same verdict. No e-process may stop it.

**Pairing rules.** These are requirements on every paired study; violating one is a refusal, not a warning.

1. Pair by (world, replicate). A counter that depends on training progress is not part of an address. Counterexample:
   seeds keyed by (world, epoch) on a 64-wide batch pair only morphologies whose indices agree modulo 64.
2. Exogenous fields are indexed by physical coordinate, not by event order. Two robots on different paths must read
   one space-indexed material field at their own contact points; two tires on different paths need not receive equal
   friction samples at corresponding ticks, and a comparison that pairs "the k-th friction draw" has paired nothing.
3. Each design is settled separately. The settled equilibrium is recomputed per design; a design that starts in
   penetration makes the pair `unscored`; a buildable design that stalls is a failed trial, not a missing one.
4. Pairing imposes no minimum number of episodes per design.
5. The accounting identity holds: a_only + b_only + both + neither + unscored = planned, each an integer, and the
   verdict states its denominator. An agent cannot optimize failures out of the denominator.
6. Paired statistics are refused under an uncontrolled determinism class (§35). Both arms run at D2 or better on one
   backend, or at D3 with decision-level equivalence recorded for the task. A D4 run never enters a comparison.

**Several metrics.** The average of the per-metric e-values is an e-value under arbitrary dependence and tests the
global null that the candidate is no better on any listed metric (FACT: `fs-eproc::combine_average`; the product is
valid only for independent comparisons, `combine_product`). A claim about one named metric uses that metric's own
e-process, with the e-value form of the Benjamini–Hochberg rule across metrics when several are claimed (FACT:
`fs-eproc::e_benjamini_hochberg`, false discovery rate at most α under arbitrary dependence).

**Several designs.** A race over N candidates uses the mixture construction of `fs-race::race_field`: rounds are the
only clock, every survivor consumes one observation per round in index order, a candidate's elimination evidence is
the average of the pairwise e-processes against all original opponents, and an elimination needs that mixture to reach
at least 1/α, so the true best is eliminated with probability at most α (FACT:
`frankensim/crates/fs-race/CONTRACT.md`). An implementation that eliminates on any single pairwise crossing runs each
comparison at α/(N − 1) instead.

**What a comparison returns.** The verdict (`settled`, `tied`, `inconclusive`, `incomparable` or `infeasible`, all
legitimate dispositions); the statistic of its study type; the pairs used with the accounting identity; the tier; the
first divergent event between the arms when one exists; each design's absolute hard-limit verdict beside the paired
verdict, because a relative result does not establish that either design meets the mission; and a rank-robustness
field (`model-robust`, `model-conditional` or `unchecked`, from A-19). A verdict from a fast tier is `provisional`
until promoted (the trust ladder of §16, decision core DC-01); a promoted verdict also carries the estimate at referee
fidelity from the tier-correction ledger (§29.11).

**What a comparison does not control.** One comparison is valid under optional stopping. It does nothing about an
agent that submits fifty candidates to the same held-out worlds until one passes. That is the job of the blinded
qualification lane of §24, with its fresh sealed world clusters and its error budget α_i = α / [i·(i+1)] for the i-th
submission. A design comparison that is to be reported as a claim (§8) goes through that lane; `eval.compare` on
discovery worlds is a development result.

**A relation, not two scores [F].** "Can this replacement make a formerly successful delivery fail?" is a question
about the pair, and the statistical comparison answers it only in distribution. The exact form, reachability of the
outcome pair (success, failure) in the product of the two closed loops driven by the same exogenous inputs, is a
mission query (§25). Atelier's part is the pairing discipline above, without which neither form means anything.

### 28.7 Lineage: diff, blame, bisect, merge

Design versions form a content-addressed graph whose edges are typed edits. `design diff` lowers the difference of two
terms to a list of edits. `design blame` names the edit that introduced a parameter value. `design bisect --predicate
"<task> succeeds"` walks a lineage path by binary search, deciding each step with the anytime-valid comparison, and
returns the edit that changed the outcome (NS-06).

- **An edit returns its invalidation set.** `design.edit` takes the expected parent and an idempotency key, refuses a
  stale parent, and returns the new immutable version, the structural and parameter diff, the symmetry effect, and the
  set of derived assets and stored verdicts whose dependency support the edit touches (§28.13). The agent sees what
  its change put in question before it spends anything.
- **Bisect is honest about non-monotone lineages.** Binary search is correct only for a predicate that is monotone
  along the path. Counterexample: outcomes along six versions are pass, fail, pass, pass, pass, fail. The midpoint
  passes, the search goes right and reports the last edit, while the first edit also changed the outcome and the
  second changed it back. `design bisect` therefore returns a pair of adjacent versions with different decided
  outcomes, which is always a true statement, labelled `a-change`. It labels the result `first-change` only after the
  prefix has been scanned (every earlier version decided) or when the caller declares the predicate monotone, which is
  recorded as an assumption on the result.
- **Bisect controls its own error.** Each probe is an e-process at α / ⌈log₂ L⌉ for a path of L edits, so the whole
  search errs with probability at most α by the union bound. An inconclusive probe ends the search with the bracket
  reached and the evidence so far.
- **Merge is order-independent.** Lineages and archives from several agents merge as grow-only sets of versions and
  per-cell maxima under a total order (fitness, then `DesignId`). The merge is commutative, associative and
  idempotent, so the result does not depend on who synchronized first. FACT: `fs-archive::MapElites::add` replaces an
  elite only on strict improvement, so equal fitness keeps the first arrival; the total order removes that dependence
  on arrival.
- **Migration keeps old replay possible.** A schema migration reads the old object and writes a new one with migration
  provenance. The old version stays addressable and replayable with the worker version that produced it.

### 28.8 Optimization

Agents are the discrete optimizer: they choose topology, leg count and actuator families. The studio supplies fast
continuous optimization and honest evaluation underneath.

- **Continuous parameters.** CMA-ES with restarts, Bayesian optimization with trust regions and multiple fidelities,
  and Riemannian quasi-Newton for static objectives on manifold-valued parameters, all from FrankenSim (FACT: `fs-dfo`
  with restart schedules, `fs-bo` with `turbo` and `mf` modules, `fs-ascent::riemann`), reached through the airlock.
- **Racing.** Candidates in a generation are compared by e-processes and the dominated ones are cancelled
  mid-evaluation, so compute follows the survivors. The benefit depends on separation: the sibling's own battery
  asserts a saving of at least 2 on a separated field and below 1.5 on an inseparable one, with 11.7 and 1.03 measured
  on its fixtures (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-race/tests/battery.rs`, `CONTRACT.md`). The plan therefore expects two
  to five times fewer evaluations when candidates are separated and nothing otherwise (HYPOTHESIS; settled per
  campaign by the saving the race ledger records).
- **Quality-diversity.** A MAP-Elites archive (`fs-archive`, FACT) over behaviour descriptors such as mass, speed,
  energy per metre and footprint. `frob opt archive show` gives an agent a map of different good designs instead of
  one optimum. Mutations are typed edits, including adding and removing a limb, and one generation is one mixed batch
  (§17) under the class's family policy (§23).
- **The fidelity ladder.** Static analyses, then a shared family policy on a fast tier, then a fine-tuned policy, then
  a specialist, then promotion on the referee, then a rare-failure estimate for finalists. Each rung certifies or
  escalates. §28.9 specifies it.
- **Co-design with control.** The inner loop is a controller that needs no per-design training: the synthesized
  baseline controller, which costs seconds, or the family policy of the design's morphology class. Policy fine-tuning
  is spent only on survivors and specialist training only on finalists.
- **A family per dispatch [F].** A neighbourhood of designs (a sweep, a finite-difference stencil with common random
  numbers, an evolutionary generation) is evaluated in one mixed batch, which cuts sequential agent rounds. A
  20-parameter central stencil with 64 addresses per design is 41 × 64 = 2,624 worlds and fits one device. The ceiling
  is stated before any test: published population co-design reports compute gains of about three to four times, not
  ten (FACT, source register), and parameter sweeps inside one topology are something incumbents batch today. Paired
  finite-difference design gradients are used only over short horizons and smooth metrics, because paired worlds
  decouple under contact (a perturbation of 5 × 10⁻⁴ degrees is reported to diverge after 50 steps; FACT, source
  register). Pilot and falsifier: on one quadruped with 12 design parameters, measure the variance ratio of paired
  against independent central differences at horizons of 25 to 500 steps and the sign agreement over 20 replicate
  batches. Kill gate KILL-FDGRAD: a variance ratio below 2 at 250 steps, sign agreement below 70%, or no
  specialist-checked gain over a same-budget CMA-ES generation; fallback: derivative-free search only.
- **One verb for the whole campaign.** `frob opt cascade --spec <mission or task> --budget …` runs the cascade of
  §28.11 from a specification to a ranked, typed result set, streaming progress with denominators. It is orchestration
  over the verbs of this chapter and is judged as such: if agents with the one-verb form do no better than agents
  driving the verbs by hand in the A/B of GATE-ATEL-03, it stays a convenience and is not counted as a lever.

### 28.9 The ladder of evaluation rungs

A design is never scored by the most expensive method first. Six rungs, each about an order of magnitude dearer than
the one below, each allowed to decide only what its evidence supports.

| Rung | What scores the design | Cost per design | What it may decide |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 Static | `design.check` and the bound analyses | Seconds of CPU; under 2 s for `check` (TARGET) | Refuse a design; exclude with a typed bound. It never ranks survivors |
| R1 Shared policy | The family policy of the design's morphology class, zero-shot, on a fast tier (§23); the synthesized baseline controller where the class has no family policy (§22) | Tens of GPU-seconds (HYPOTHESIS; the phase ledger records it) | Discard, under §28.10. Select only in strata where rank agreement has been measured |
| R2 Fine-tuned | The family policy fine-tuned to the design at a declared fraction of specialist steps. Each fraction is its own rung for calibration | Minutes | Discard, and select among survivors |
| R3 Specialist | A policy trained or inherited for this design alone | One training run. The leading incumbent trains a quadruped velocity policy in about 410 s on one RTX 4090 and humanoids in under 30 minutes (FACT, source register) | Rank finalists, as `provisional` |
| R4 Referee | Paired re-evaluation on the referee at the same world addresses (DC-01), with the tier-correction estimate (§29.11) | Referee episodes: 16 / 64 / 256 at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile per established verdict (TARGET, unmeasured) | Establish, or label `tier-sensitive` |
| R5 Rare failure | A failure-rate bound for finalists (DC-04, §26) | n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉ nominal episodes for a bound p* at confidence 1 − δ | Certify, refute or exhaust the bound, scoped to its tier |

A sampling-based controller that needs no training is admitted as an R1 scorer and as an out-of-distribution fallback.
It is never the evaluator of record: at roughly 0.3 to 1 GPU-minute per quadruped episode and 1 to 5 per humanoid
episode, a few hundred episodes cost more than training once (FACT, source register). A policy may be trained under
the labelled D4 training profile of §35; every score that enters the ladder is an evaluation on a D2 profile.

**What the evidence says about the middle rungs** (FACT, source register; every figure is from published work on other
engines and is re-read before a gate cites it). Within one morphology class a family policy reaches 70 to 85% of
specialist return zero-shot, and fine-tuning needs 1 to 20% of from-scratch steps. Across topology-diverse designs at
affordable pretraining the zero-shot figure is 10 to 40% and the fine-tuning gain two to three times. After a
parameter edit an inherited policy needs 1 to 20% of from-scratch steps; after a single-limb topology edit, 33 to
100%. Cheap rungs are biased as well as noisy: early evaluation underestimates slow learners, and a population scored
under one shared controller drifts toward designs that controller steers easily.

**Layer 1 of the allocation rule: the fixed table [S].** Successive halving with η = 3 inside each stratum: at each
rung transition the top ⌈n/3⌉ designs by the lower rung's score advance, on a schedule written to the ledger before
the campaign starts (FACT: `fs-race::successive_halving` returns a `BracketLedger`; its kills are rank-based and carry
no e-process guarantee). The table is the baseline every adaptive rule is measured against, on the same population and
the same addresses, in GPU-seconds to the same accepted decision and in audited top-k recall. It is also the fallback
of every trigger in this chapter.

**Layer 2: conformal survivor sets with an audit lane [F].** This layer owns the population stage. For each rung pair
and stratum:

1. *The audit lane.* A tenth of the rung's spend (DESIGN default) goes to designs drawn by a keyed random stream
   before any score exists. Each is evaluated at the higher rung at a fixed n and is never cancelled by an e-process.
   The audit lane is the only source of calibration. Calibrating on the designs that advanced is biased toward
   agreement and is forbidden.
2. *Calibration.* With n audit designs, the residuals are rᵢ = |y_hi,i − ĝ(y_lo,i)|, where ĝ is a monotone map fitted
   on a disjoint part of the audit set. The margin q̂ is the ⌈(n + 1)(1 − α)⌉-th smallest residual. At α = 0.05 this
   needs n ≥ 19; with fewer the stratum falls back to layer 1.
3. *The survivor set.* Every design whose lower-rung score is within 2·q̂ of the k-th best lower-rung score advances,
   where k is the number of finalists wanted. A rung cannot separate designs that are closer than twice its own error,
   so everything inside that margin goes up.
4. *The trust check.* If at most 3k designs survive, the rung separates the top band and is trusted there. If more
   survive, the request buys more lower-rung episodes, or skips the rung for the top band, or, when the budget forbids
   both, truncates to 3k by lower-rung score and labels the campaign `budget-truncated`.
5. *The alarm.* A test martingale on the audit designs' conformal p-values watches exchangeability. At a value of 20,
   which is 1/α at α = 0.05, the stratum's calibration is void, the stratum reverts to layer 1, and calibration
   restarts inside the new regime.

Obligations: coverage of at least 1 − α for designs exchangeable with the audit set, on average over calibration draws
(with n = 39 the coverage averages 0.95 and falls below 0.885 in one calibration draw in twenty; arithmetic from the
Beta law of split-conformal coverage); two designs separated by more than the margin at the lower rung are misordered
at the higher rung with probability at most 2α; top-k recall is measured by the audit lane and never claimed from the
theory.

**Layer 3: value of information at the finalist stage [F].** When the survivors of the last population rung number at
most 64 (DESIGN, the budgeted mode of DC-05), allocation passes, one way, to decision core DC-05: a table of
experiments (more fast-tier pairs, referee pairs, a fine-tune, a bench measurement), each with a cost and a
closed-form expected value of sample information, bought in order of value per cost until none clears the budget
price. The handover is one-way: no design receives requests from both layers. Layer 3 orders work; every verdict still
comes from DC-01 and the comparison rules of §28.6.

**The numbers the evidence supports.**

| Quantity | Figure | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Saving from anytime-valid stopping and elimination | Two to five times when candidates are separated; about none when they are not | FACT for the sibling fixtures (`fs-race` battery: at least 2 asserted, 11.7 measured, on a separated field; 1.03 on an inseparable one); HYPOTHESIS for robot design populations |
| Information-gain selection against a capable agent | Unsupported; plausibly below 1.5 times. A language-model tuner beat random search more often than Gaussian-process optimization at 10 and at 30 evaluations, and model-based search lost to a genetic algorithm on a robot co-design benchmark | FACT, source register. Layer 3 is therefore a stop rule and a budget allocator |
| Referee cost saved by pairing with the fast tier | Variance scales by 1 − ρ²: 1.96 times at ρ = 0.7, 5.3 at 0.9, 10 at 0.95. Published cross-fidelity correlation collapses at large dynamics gaps | Arithmetic; ρ unmeasured. Expect about two times until ρ above 0.9 is measured |
| Rank agreement of R1 with specialists on the referee | Unmeasured anywhere. Nearest neighbours: rank correlation 0.40 overall | FACT, source register; §28.10 |
| Share of a population that must leave R1 for R2 | About the top quarter, not the top twentieth | HYPOTHESIS inferred from the neighbours; settled by GATE-ATEL-07 |
| Compute gain of published shared-policy population methods | About three to four times | FACT, source register |

**The ceiling, stated before any speed test.** The ladder cannot cost less than one R1 score for every design, the
audit lane, and R3 to R5 for the finalists. A worked case, illustrative and not a measurement: 81 designs with rungs
of 10 s, 3 min and 30 min cost 40.5 h if every design is trained; halving at η = 3 costs 13.5 min + 81 min + 270 min ≈
6.1 h; with q̂ = 0.04 on a unit-scaled score, a third-best lower-rung score of 0.74 puts the cut at 0.66, six designs
survive, and the ladder costs about 2.6 h with the audit lane. With q̂ = 0.10 about twenty designs survive, more than
3k = 9, and layer 2 gives back most of its advantage. The size of q̂ is exactly what the rank-agreement ledger
measures, which is why that experiment comes first.

**Pilot and falsifier for layers 2 and 3.** Log once, on an incumbent engine or on `batch-cpu`: 64 designs × 3 rungs ×
32 addresses (about one GPU-day, TARGET). Replay four choosers over the log with no further simulation: an agent
reading the log so far, the knowledge-gradient chooser, e-race elimination, and uniform allocation. Metric:
evaluations until a true top-3 design is identified at 95% confidence, over 100 replays. KILL-LADDER fires if layer 2
does not reach halving's audited top-k recall at fewer GPU-seconds; KILL-VOI fires if the knowledge-gradient chooser
is less than 1.5 times better than the agent. Fallbacks: layer 1 alone; value of information as a stop rule only.

### 28.10 The rank-agreement ledger

The ladder is only as good as the agreement between adjacent rungs, and that agreement has not been published for the
case that matters. The ledger measures it and gives the measurement consequences.

```
rank_agreement row
  key        morphology class · task family · rung pair (lo, hi) · stratum (block of archive cells)
             policy and kernel versions · effort profile · battery version
  measures   n_designs
             Kendall τ on the top band, with a bootstrap interval    top band = top m of either rung,
             top-k recall of lo against hi                           m = max(10, 3k)
             Spearman ρ over the stratum
             mean signed residual per region (the bias of the cheap rung)
             conformal margin q̂ at α · exchangeability martingale value
             per-episode cross-tier correlation ρ_ep on paired addresses (for R3 → R4)
  provenance audit-lane draws · calibration experiment · claim_id · recording session
```

Rows come from two sources: the audit lane of every campaign, continuously, and one calibration experiment per
morphology class. That experiment is the decisive early experiment of the program. Forty designs in one class are each
scored by the shared policy on the fast tier, by a fine-tune at 5% of specialist steps, and by a specialist promoted
on the referee; it reports Kendall τ on the top band, top-10 recall and bootstrap intervals, and ρ_ep on 256 paired
episodes per design. It costs about 45 GPU-hours, or about 5 if the specialists of the family-policy experiment of §23
are reused (TARGET).

What published neighbours report (FACT, source register): the value predictions of a shared policy correlate with
rollouts of the same policy at a rank correlation of 0.40 overall and 0.50, 0.60 and 0.83 on three robots, over 17
designs, which is an easier comparison than a shared policy against specialists; in architecture search with shared
weights, sparse Kendall τ of 0.236, 0.740 and 0.159 on three benchmarks; a universal controller reaches up to 80% of
individually trained controllers; and in the 17-design study the top design was the same under both scores. Agreement
of 0.7 or better near the top is therefore not supported, top-1 agreement at modest correlation is plausible, and the
default is that R1 discards.

Each rung pair in each stratum is in one of three modes. The thresholds 0.5 and 0.7 are the kill thresholds of the
calibration experiment; 0.2 is a DESIGN default that the experiment resets (OPEN-ATEL-2). All conditions are on the
lower bootstrap bound.

| Mode | Condition | What the lower rung may do |
|---|---|---|
| `selective` | τ on the top band ≥ 0.5; calibration valid (n ≥ 19, martingale below 20); audited miss rate at most α | The conformal survivor rule of §28.9 |
| `discard-only` | 0.2 ≤ τ < 0.5; or the stratum is unmeasured; or a fallback trigger of DC-02 or DC-03 fired | The fixed table of layer 1: the top third advances, so the top quarter always does. Where a margin exists, a design within 2·q̂ of the k-th best is never eliminated. The audit lane keeps running |
| `uninformative` | τ < 0.2, or the planted winner was lost in this stratum | Nothing. The rung is skipped in this stratum, its budget moves up one rung, and designs it eliminated earlier in the campaign are re-opened through the audit lane |

An unmeasured stratum is `discard-only`; the default fails closed, and the default is the fixed table. The
synthesized baseline controller is a scorer like any other: when it serves as R1 its rank agreement with trained
policies is measured in the same ledger and it has no exemption. Low agreement has four further consequences.

- **The report says so.** Every campaign report prints the mode of every rung and stratum. Finalists chosen under any
  mode other than `selective` are described as "k survivors", never as "the top k".
- **Bias is handled by region.** A region where the cheap rung systematically under-scores (bodies that are hard to
  steer with a shared controller) gets its own margin by per-bucket calibration; a bucket below the calibration floor
  refuses instead of extrapolating (FACT: `fs-eproc::hardening::MondrianConformal` implements this refusal).
- **The tier correction switches off.** Where ρ_ep is below 0.7 on at least 200 paired addresses, the estimator of
  §29.11 is disabled for that stratum and promotion uses referee episodes alone.
- **The program is told.** If GATE-ATEL-07 fails for every morphology class tested, KILL-RANK fires: R1 is
  `discard-only` everywhere, the plan's benefit claim for the ladder shrinks to stopping and elimination, and §39
  records which factor of the ten-fold thesis was lost.

### 28.11 The six-stage co-design cascade

The cascade is the ladder applied to a population, with a control built into every stage. `opt.cascade` runs it; each
stage is also callable alone.

| Stage | Content | Rungs | Output | Control built into the stage | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Deduplicate and check | One canonical representative per orbit under the observation-preserving subgroup; the mission-observable digest; `design.check` with the bound analyses | R0 | Representatives; typed exclusions | Two designs that must merge and two that must split | §17, `frob-analyze` |
| 2 Reuse and synthesize | Memo hits for analyses, controllers, step plans and episodes; module contracts reused where the edit is local; bounded exact linkage synthesis when the brief asks for a mechanism | R0 | Reuse receipts; candidate set | A mirror swap must reuse; a shared-bus edit must invalidate | `frob-opt`, §14, §25 |
| 3 Screen | Gait-orbit screening (A-04), family policy zero-shot, task-wrench attainability (A-24) | R1 | Survivor set with the mode of each stratum | The blinded stratified audit of discards and the planted winner (§28.12) | `frob-opt`, §22, §23 |
| 4 Race | A mixed-morphology race on paired addresses; fine-tuning for survivors, specialists for finalists | R1 to R3 | Finalists, `provisional` | A null race keeps the best with probability at least 1 − α | `frob-opt`, §17 |
| 5 Rank robustness | A-19: the top-k and the runners-up re-evaluated under model-form variants, with the created-energy audit | R3, R4 | Each finalist typed `model-robust` or `model-conditional` | Planted cone and energy-pumping exploits must be caught | `frob-analyze`, §16, §18 |
| 6 Qualify | Sealed confirmation on fresh world clusters with pre-committed requests; promotion on the referee; the rare-failure bound | R4, R5 | A qualified design comparison with receipts | False-promotion rate at most α under adaptive retries on no-signal worlds | §24, §26 |

**Stage 1 is cheap and its factor is modest.** Ten leg types on six slots with dihedral symmetry give (10⁶ + 2·10 +
2·10² + 10³ + 3·10⁴ + 3·10³)/12 = 86,185 representatives, an 11.6-fold reduction; with only a sagittal mirror left,
because a sensor broke the rest, (10⁶ + 10³)/2 = 500,500, a 2.0-fold reduction (exact counts). The stage is worth
having and is not a large factor once sensors break the symmetry.

**Stage 5 variants.** (a) The alternative contact law, where the hard-contact batch law is available (§18); (b) the
circular against the pyramidal friction cone; (c) contact stiffness at one half and at twice its value; (d) half the
step; (e) one tenth of the solver tolerance. The stage costs at most a tenth of campaign compute (TARGET). A finalist
is `model-robust {variants, top-k overlap, energy audit}` when the top-k set and the order of the top two survive
every variant and no finalist's advantage coincides with created energy or cone excess (§29.9). Otherwise it is
`model-conditional`, the report shows both orders as a fidelity-dependent ranking, and two things are blocked: the
result cannot be reported as a design comparison without its condition, and it cannot spend prototype budget, so a
build-packet request (§31) is refused. A design is preferred only if it wins under both qualified contact laws.

**Every stage may end the campaign honestly.** "Every admitted alternative fails", "tied within the margin" and
"inconclusive at this budget" are results. A stage that cannot run its control (no planted winner available, no second
contact law admitted) reports the control as `not-run`, and the result carries that.

### 28.12 Screening audits

A screen can throw away the winner, and nothing downstream will notice: an e-value between the eighth and ninth
survivors says nothing about a design discarded at the first rung. Three audits make the loss observable.

1. **The stratified audit of discards.** The audit lane of §28.9, stratified by stage, reason for discard, morphology
   class and archive cell, at rates fixed in the campaign manifest. It is blinded: an audit design and a survivor are
   the same job type with the same budget, and the evaluating job cannot tell which it is running. Results feed the
   rank-agreement ledger. With no true top-k design among m audited discards, the rate of discarded winners is at most
   ln(1/δ)/m at confidence 1 − δ.
2. **The planted winner.** Before the campaign a design known from an earlier specialist-and-referee result to belong
   in the top k is inserted in a form the screen represents poorly: an unusual gait, or a morphology class with few
   examples in the family policy's training set. Its identity is sealed in the campaign manifest hash and revealed at
   close. It must reach the finalists. If it does not, the finalists are labelled `screen-unsound`, the offending rung
   becomes `uninformative` in that stratum and the campaign re-opens.
3. **Prune soundness.** An exclusion made by an analysis or a contract is wrong if any discarded design is
   independently feasible under the same mission, even when the eventual winner survives. Pruned designs are sampled
   and run at R1 or above. One feasible pruned design suspends that pruning rule for the campaign and files the case
   as a negative-control seed. The shared-bus case of §28.4 is the standing example.

### 28.13 Revalidation after edits (`result.revalidate`)

An edit is an event that puts evidence in question. The studio does not ask "is the cache still warm"; it asks which
verdicts survive, which are revoked, and what the cheapest set of reruns is. `frob result revalidate <verdict or
claim> --after-edit <edit>` answers with the surviving obligations and the licence for each, the invalidated regions,
and the required reruns with their cost. The mission cluster's revalidation verb (§25) is this engine applied to a
mission answer.

Every stored verdict has this shape (`frob.verdict/1`):

```
verdict record
  claim        claim_id · claim type (§8) · statement · quantifier prefix
  kind         inclusion (a witness exists) | exclusion (no witness can exist) | comparison | optimum | estimate
  assumptions  disturbance set · world set · hypothesis support · actuator limits · requirement ·
               policy class · information contract (channels and their instants) · catalog and prices ·
               model identity · tier · kernel versions · effort profile
  obligations  feasibility obligations   (the witness is valid)
               optimality obligations    (every cheaper rival is excluded; the cost bound; catalog membership)
               each with: evidence_id · dependency support (the exact fields it read) · checker
  provenance   evidence closure · decision-policy id · trace ids (§29.13)
```

**Four grades of reuse.**

| Grade | Licence to skip the recheck | What is recorded | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Identity | The obligation's full key is unchanged | The memo hit (§28.14) | [S] |
| 2 Dependency | Every changed field lies outside the obligation's recorded dependency support | A mask over the obligations, checkable against the supports without rerunning anything | [S] |
| 3 Implication | A changed field lies inside the support, and a polarity rule of the assumption lattice preserves the obligation | The rule's name, the old and new assumption sets, the order relation between them and its check | [F] |
| 4 None | — | The rerun and its cost estimate | — |

**The assumption lattice with polarity.** Each component of the assumption set carries a partial order: inclusion for
sets, pointwise order for limits, refinement for information. The table gives, for each direction of change, whether
an inclusion result and an exclusion result survive.

| Component | Change | Inclusion (a witness exists) | Exclusion (no witness can exist) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disturbance or world set | Narrowed | Preserved, with the same witness | Recheck |
| | Enlarged | Recheck | Preserved, if the policy class and the information contract are unchanged |
| Hypothesis support (admitted hidden parameters) | Enlarged | Recheck | Preserved |
| Actuator limits | Relaxed | Preserved, if the old command stays realizable and the plant still realizes the old dynamics | Recheck |
| | Tightened | Recheck | Preserved |
| Requirement | Relaxed | Preserved | Recheck |
| | Tightened | Recheck | Preserved |
| Policy class | Enlarged | Preserved | Recheck |
| | Shrunk | Preserved only if the witness policy is still in the class | Preserved |
| Information contract | Refined: more, earlier or better observations, the old ones still recoverable | Preserved | Recheck |
| | Coarsened or delayed | Recheck | Preserved |
| Catalog and prices | Part added or price lowered | Preserved | Recheck. This is where an optimum is lost: every cheaper rival must be excluded again |
| | Part removed | Preserved unless the witness uses the part | Preserved; an optimum that used the part is void |
| Free space of the scene | Enlarged | Preserved, if the witness trace does not touch the changed region | Recheck every exclusion that used the removed obstacle |

Five rules govern the table.

1. **A rule applies only to a pure change in its direction.** A larger motor relaxes a torque limit and also changes
   mass and rotor inertia. It is not a relaxation; the model diff sends it to grade 4. A room segment may guarantee a
   wheel-traction margin under a normal-load interval, and a changed motor can leave that interval through
   acceleration.
2. **Feasibility and optimality are separate obligations.** A cost-9 cart may be the certified minimum while a cost-5
   relay is excluded by a doorway. Widen the doorway: the cart and its trace remain valid, so its feasibility
   obligations survive; the obligation "every cheaper rival is excluded" read the doorway and is revoked. The verdict
   splits into `feasible: preserved` and `optimal: revoked, recheck`. A cheaper, newly feasible rival always revokes
   an incumbent's optimality claim, including when an amendment adds a catalog part.
3. **Joint edits need a joint check.** Polarity rules compose only when the edited components have disjoint dependency
   supports for the obligation. A simultaneous edit to observation timing and to the floor law is checked jointly.
4. **Improving something can destroy a result.** A better sensor preserves witnesses and can void an impossibility
   that rested on two worlds being indistinguishable.
5. **Unsafe reuse is a false accept**, even if a later change restores feasibility. One false reused verdict disables
   the rule that licensed it until it is repaired, and a checker rejects an unknown rule name or a changed quantifier
   prefix even when every hash is consistent.

**Counterexample: widening by a hair.** In the docking fixture of §25 the gain is 1 or 2 and one velocity reading
decides the action. Add the gain 33/32 to the hypothesis support: it is indistinguishable from gain 1 at an error
radius of 1/64, and every one of the 34,590 positive decisions on each test grid is wrong for the amended mission
(FACT, executed; source register). "Hypothesis support enlarged: recheck inclusions" is not a formality.

**The negative result, kept on purpose.** Fixture: two scenes whose decisions commit at ticks 2 and 4; a camera packet
arrives at tick 1. Four states follow: the initial one; a display label is renamed; delivery is delayed to tick 2;
delivery is delayed to tick 3. A semantic receipt and a conventional dependency graph given the same eligibility
condition both evaluated 2, 0, 0, 1 scene pairs and recomputed the verdict 1, 0, 0, 1 times, and every verdict matched
a fresh replay. An implementation whose key omitted delivery time kept a stale promotion at the fourth state (FACT,
executed; source register). Three consequences are binding. On small cases a correctly keyed dependency graph ties a
semantic receipt, so grade 3 has no demonstrated advantage. What decides safety is the completeness of the key:
delivery time is a dependency of a decision. Grade 2 is therefore the default and is Solid; grade 3 is Frontier,
behind a flag, and its open value is whether a useful non-dependence certificate can be obtained cheaply for a rich
plant.

**The ceiling, stated before any speed test.** Rollouts and training are at least nine tenths of per-design compute
and depend on every mass and geometry parameter, so exact reuse after a dynamics-affecting edit is at most about a
tenth of compute (FACT for the phase shares of published loops, source register; HYPOTHESIS for this system until the
phase ledger exists). Reuse is total for model analyses after a controller-only, reward-only or scene-only edit.
Revalidation is an evidence rule and a hygiene measure. It is not a source of the ten-fold.

**Pilot with a hard stop for grade 3.** A campaign of 30 amendments is frozen before any implementation sees it:
visual-only edits, camera timing, sole geometry, floor law, torque, shared noise, load authority, facility edits, and
one apparently cosmetic change that advances a packet by one control tick. A separate session implements the
incremental dependency graph. Fresh solving, grades 1 and 2, and grade 3 are run from the same cache state and dirty
fraction; every decision is replayed; every incorrectly inherited success and every unnecessary invalidation is
recorded. KILL-REUSE fires on any unsafe reuse, or if grade 3 saves less than a fifth of the recheck cost that grade 2
leaves (TARGET). Fallback: grades 1 and 2 only.

### 28.14 The memo graph

Memoization does not stop at the compiled model. One content-addressed graph covers everything downstream of it. The
`Memo` trait is declared in `frob-core`, wraps each verb's executor in `frob-verbs`, and stores in `frob-chronicle`.

| Node kind | Key | A hit is served within | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subterm compile | Subterm content hash, compiler version, card versions | D0 | The incremental-compilation invariant of §14 |
| Analysis | The model identity restricted to the fields the analysis reads; analysis version; parameters | D0 | An edit outside those fields recomputes nothing |
| Controller synthesis | Model identity, task interface, synthesis version | D0 or same-backend D2 | |
| Step plan | Shape class: topology bucket, capacities, contact law, sensor layout | — | A parameter edit recompiles nothing; parameters are per-world data (§17, §21) |
| Kernel binary | Kernel source hash, compiler, driver, device family | — | The binary cache of §21 |
| Scene compile | Scene identity, compiler version | D0 | §19 |
| Episode | The full identity: model, scene, task, controller or policy, world address, tier, kernel versions, effort profile, device family | Same-backend D2; D1 where the device family holds it | Never across a D3 boundary |
| Comparison, summary | The identities of its inputs and the rule version | Derived | Recomputed from memoized episodes; it reaches the identical e-value |

**Store policy.**

- *Keys are derived, not written.* A key is generated from the declared inputs of the node kind. Each kind has a
  property test that perturbs every declared input and requires the key to change, and a live-dependency test that
  perturbs every field the computation reads and requires the key to change or the output to stay the same. A missing
  input in a key is the failure this policy exists to prevent (§28.13, the stale promotion).
- *Hits are bit-identical or not served.* Results are placed by index and random streams are keyed by address, so a
  hit equals a recomputation bit for bit inside D0 and same-backend D2. Tolerance-level reuse (FACT:
  `fs-recompute::Store::can_skip` serves a node cached under a looser requirement if it achieved enough) is allowed
  only for analyses that record an achieved error, never for episodes.
- *Shadow recomputation.* One hit in 64 (DESIGN), chosen by key hash, is recomputed and compared. One digest mismatch
  stops serving that node kind and files a determinism violation (the trip-wire pattern of `fs-recompute`, FACT:
  `StoreError::DeterminismViolation`).
- *Loss model.* States: valid hit, stale hit, miss. Actions: serve, recompute, shadow. A stale result served costs 100
  units against the GPU-seconds of a needless recomputation (DESIGN ratio, recorded in the memo policy's contract).
- *Budget.* 64 GB by default (DESIGN). On exhaustion the store evicts and never blocks. Eviction is the S3-FIFO policy
  FrankenSQLite's pager uses (FACT: `frankensqlite/crates/fsqlite-pager/src/s3_fifo.rs`), with admission weighted by
  recompute cost because misses are not equally expensive. Nodes in the evidence closure of a live claim are pinned
  (§29.16).
- *Rollout and rollback.* Shadow, then D0 analyses, then D2 episodes. `--no-memo` disables serving; the store can be
  deleted at any time without losing a result that a claim cites, because cited evidence is pinned in Chronicle, not
  in the cache.
- *Baseline.* Full recomputation per verb with compile-only memoization.

Where the reuse is, and is not: the comparison that follows a 2,048-world run of the previous version on the same
scene reuses that version's episodes at the comparison's addresses; a controller-only edit reuses every model
analysis; a mass or geometry edit reuses no rollout. Targets, unmeasured: `design check` after a one-parameter edit in
0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 s at the 50th / 95th / 99th percentile (TARGET).

FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-recompute` is a content-addressed Merkle graph with a skip oracle, pins that are never
evicted and an independent checker. It is consumed through the airlock if admission passes (§33); otherwise the same
node record is implemented in `frob-chronicle`.

### 28.15 Campaign-level deduplication

Two designs that the mission cannot tell apart are evaluated once. The mechanism belongs to Phylum (§17): one
canonical representative per orbit under the observation-preserving subgroup, and a mission-observable digest, which
is the compiled model restricted to the quantities that enter the mission's equations (total inertia, collision hull,
joint and actuator parameters, port coordinates, sensor visibility, thermal and electrical bus, task objective), with
results memoized on it. Atelier's part is three rules.

- The digest is a memo key like any other and obeys the store policy of §28.14: derived from declared inputs,
  shadow-recomputed, stopped on one mismatch.
- "Equal digests imply identical trajectories" is a structural rule with a bit-identical replay test. It is a
  statement about the simulator and is not offered as a proof obligation to any proof checker.
- The factor is reported, not promised. A fixture built to have a large factor (ballast slugs that enter the dynamics
  only through count and first and second moments reduce 735,471 placements to 24,546 classes) shows the mechanism is
  exact; it says nothing about the factor on a real brief. Placements with equal first moment and different second
  moment must split, and adding a placement-dependent quantity to the mission enlarges the digest and splits classes.

### 28.16 Experiment suggestion

`frob experiment suggest <designs or answer> --budget …` returns a ranked list of information-gathering proposals,
each with a testable prediction: more pairs at a rung, a referee batch, a fine-tune, a probe on a bench, a measurement
of a card field, a cell of a mission answer to split. It is advisory. It never decides validity (ADR-ATEL-07).

- **Ranking by decision value.** Each proposal is an action with a cost and a variance component it reduces
  (numerical, statistical or model). Its value is the reduction in expected opportunity loss of the current choice per
  unit cost, and the list ends with STOP when nothing clears the price (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-voi` provides
  `action_value`, `recommend` with a stop threshold, `expected_opportunity_loss` and a knowledge-gradient routine over
  a finite Gaussian menu; its own comparison test is a three-design fixture, so no saving is measured).
- **Partition by decision, not by parameter.** For a measurement, the compatible physical mechanisms are partitioned
  by the design decision they imply; the cheapest realizable intervention whose possible readings separate
  incompatible decisions is proposed; where observation sets still overlap, two witness mechanisms with opposing
  design margins are returned; when all mechanisms imply the same choice, identification is skipped. A-25 checks that
  the proposed excitation can identify what it claims to.
- **Measuring the target directly is always on the list.** Counterexample: a short probe synthesized for three
  admitted material laws chose the right gripper pad in 675 of 675 in-class cases; a fourth, delayed law followed the
  same early trace and reversed all 225 held-out decisions; measuring the candidate pad at mission dwell was right in
  900 of 900 (FACT, executed; source register). An in-class-perfect probe cannot expose structural error, so every
  suggestion states its model class and lists the direct measurement as a comparator with its cost.
- **Every suggestion registers its prediction.** The predicted outcome interval of the chosen experiment is registered
  (§28.17) before it runs. The calibration of those intervals is the fallback trigger of DC-05.

### 28.17 Registered predictions

Before a run, an agent may register a prediction: `frob predict register --metric cot-elec --interval 0.42 0.50
--confidence 0.8 --for <planned-run>`. It is hashed and frozen before the run starts and scored afterwards with a
proper scoring rule. `frob predict report` shows each agent's calibration. The mechanism costs almost nothing and
answers a question nothing else does: whether an agent understands the robot it is designing or is searching blindly
(adapted from FrankenMining's forecast ledger, FACT).

The studio registers its own predictions under the same rule: the intervals of `sim.estimate`, the outcome intervals
of `experiment.suggest`, and the saving a race or a ladder expects. A refit after scoring opens a new prediction
version; the old one stays scored. Calibration is put to work in two places. An agent whose 80% intervals cover far
from 80% of the time is told so in `next_actions`, with the suggestion to buy evidence before the next edit. And the
studio's own miscalibration is a fallback trigger (DC-05: coverage of 80% intervals outside 65 to 92% over 30
experiments).

### 28.18 Reports

`frob report design <id>` writes an evidence package: the design and its lineage, every analysis, scorecards,
comparisons with their verdicts, certificates, device receipts and replay addresses, as a directory with an HTML index
(FrankenSim's evidence-package pattern, FACT: `fs-package`, with a standalone checker in `fs-checker`).

A report is generated from the artifact graph of §29.13 and prints nothing it cannot resolve. Each statement carries
its claim type, its `claim_id`, the boundary of its claim product (the weakest input, §8), the mode of every ladder
rung that selected it, its rank-robustness type, its qualification receipt if it has one, and the machine-readable
no-authorization flags of §12. `frob report campaign <id>` adds the phase ledger, the screening audit, the
planted-winner outcome and the list of fallbacks that fired. Reports are deterministic: the same graph produces the
same bytes. A losing experiment is reported with the same completeness as a winning one, so that it can be reproduced
as readily.

### 28.19 Decision cores

Eleven decisions recur across the studio, and each one moves compute or changes what is reported as established. Each
is given one mathematical family, compiled ahead of time into a small runtime artifact, with a loss model, proof
obligations, a deterministic fallback and a budgeted mode. Nothing in this section is measured: failure signatures are
predicted, substituted numbers are illustrative unless marked TARGET, and every core ships in shadow behind its
baseline and is re-examined against the first profile at M2.

#### 28.19.1 The core contract

A core is a record `registries/cores/<id>.toml`, validated by the existing contract check (§34), with these fields:
the decision, its owner crate and its timescale; the family and its compiled artifacts (`frob.core-table/1`, with a
version and a content hash); the loss model as declared states, actions and cost constants; the proof obligations by
identifier; the deterministic fallback and its triggers; the budgeted mode; the adoption status (`shadow`, `canary`,
`default`); a replay scenario, a calibration report and a demonstration manifest (commands that run in under 60 s,
with checksums, and the `claim_id`s they support). Every decision a core makes is written as an evidence record: the
identities it read, the table version, the ledger frontier, the action taken, and, while in shadow, the action the
baseline took. Each record carries the core's decision-policy identifier and version (§29.13), so any verdict can be
traced to the exact rule that produced it.

Three conventions hold for every core below. The parameters of fallbacks, triggers and budgets (30 pairs, a quantum of
5 s, a 3% gain and the like) are DESIGN defaults recorded in the core's contract; changing one is a new version of the
decision policy. For a Frontier core the fallback is also its kill fallback: if the core's calibration fails
GATE-ATEL-13 after one repair cycle, the core stays on its fallback, and the registry says so. And a core's pilot is
its shadow run: it acts on nothing until the comparison of its actions with the baseline's has been read.

The eight families sit in three planes of at most three: evidence (sequential tests, conformal prediction and risk
control, value of information), failure (rare-event simulation, counterfactual forks, submodular coverage) and
platform (queueing, certified rewrites). Successive halving is a fixed table, not a family.

#### 28.19.2 The eleven cores

**DC-01 · Promote, reject or keep sampling.**

- *Artifacts.* One bit per paired episode: did the fast tier and the referee disagree at this address beyond the
  fidelity ledger's frozen envelope for the quantity? Two likelihood-ratio e-processes on the exceedance rate: E⁺
  tests the null "rate ≥ p₁" and promotes on crossing; E⁻ tests the null "rate ≤ p₀" and labels the result
  `tier-sensitive` on crossing. Both compile to one integer guard table (n, k) → promote, reject, continue, per ledger
  stratum, with the envelope hash and the address schedule.
- *Loss model.* States: faithful, sensitive. Actions: promote, reject, buy a pair at cost c. Thresholds α⁺ =
  c/(π·C_fp) and α⁻ = c/((1 − π)·C_fn), with π the ledger's share of sensitive results and C_fp, C_fn declared in the
  contract (OPEN-ATEL-1).
- *Obligations.* Wrong promotion at most α⁺ and wrong rejection at most α⁻ under optional stopping, by Ville's
  inequality, provided episodes are chosen by address before outcomes exist and the envelope is frozen first.
  Independence between episodes is not needed: under any conditional exceedance probability p ≥ p₁ each factor of E⁺
  has expectation (1 − p)(1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁) + p·p₀/p₁ ≤ 1.
- *Fallback and trigger.* Fixed 30 pairs, promote on at most 2 exceedances (at p₁ = 0.30 this promotes wrongly with
  probability 0.0021 and at p₀ = 0.05 it promotes with probability 0.81; binomial arithmetic). Triggers: a missing
  ledger stratum; an identity mismatch; a failed null self-test of the table.
- *Budgeted mode.* 32 pairs per result; on exhaustion the result stays `provisional`.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* ln E⁺ = (n − k)·ln((1 − p₀)/(1 − p₁)) + k·ln(p₀/p₁). With p₀ = 0.05, p₁ = 0.30 and
  α⁺ = 2/(0.1 × 2,000) = 0.01, promotion takes 16 clean pairs, or 22 with one exceedance; at α⁻ = 0.05 two exceedances
  within the first three pairs reject. A clean referee episode multiplies the evidence by 1.36 and an exceedance
  divides it by 6. If C_fp is ten times smaller, or p₁ = 0.5, eight pairs suffice.
- *Primary risk.* An envelope tuned after outcomes. Countermeasure: the envelope is hashed into the ledger before
  sampling. Negative control: the planted tier-sensitive policy of the fidelity gate (§16) must be caught.

**DC-02 · Which designs advance.**

- *Artifacts.* The bracket schedule of the fixed table; per rung pair and stratum the survivor rule of §28.9 with its
  margin; the one-way handover marker to DC-05; the label `budget-truncated`.
- *Loss model.* Eliminating a true top-k design, against the rung cost of promoting a poor one. The first is far
  dearer (ADR-ATEL-03).
- *Obligations.* Those of §28.9; top-k recall is measured by the audit lane; no design is served by two layers.
- *Fallback and trigger.* Halving at η = 3. Triggers: under 19 calibration points; martingale at or above 20; audited
  miss rate above α; survivors above 3k for two generations; a change of identity or scope.
- *Budgeted mode.* Audit lane at a tenth of rung spend; survivors capped at 3k, then truncation by lower-rung score
  with the label.

**DC-03 · The survivor margin and the mode of a rung.**

- *Artifacts.* The table of margins q̂ by (rung pair, stratum, α); the keyed list of audit draws; the exchangeability
  martingale; the mode automaton of §28.10 with hysteresis: a mode changes only at a generation boundary, and a
  stratum needs two consecutive generations of valid calibration to become `selective`.
- *Loss model.* As DC-02, plus the cost of the audit lane itself.
- *Obligations.* Coverage at least 1 − α for designs exchangeable with the audit set; exchangeability of the audit
  draws by construction, because they are drawn by key before scores exist; a pair separated by the margin is
  misordered with probability at most 2α; the automaton never changes inside a generation.
- *Fallback and trigger.* `discard-only` with the fixed table. Triggers as DC-02.
- *Budgeted mode.* The audit lane's tenth of rung spend. When that buys fewer than 19 audit designs in a stratum,
  strata are merged upward to the morphology class before the stratum falls back.
- *Failure modes.* Calibration from survivors only; drift of the optimizer toward a region the audit set does not
  cover; starved strata. Each is detected by the martingale or by the calibration floor.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* n = 39, α = 0.05: q̂ is the 38th smallest residual, say 0.04; the third-best
  lower-rung score is 0.74; the cut is 0.66.

**DC-04 · Certify, refute or stop on a rare failure.**

- *Artifacts.* The certification table n = ⌈ln(1/δ)/p*⌉; a sequential binomial bound that ends certified, refuted or
  exhausted (FACT: `fs-eproc::bernoulli` provides a likelihood-mixture confidence sequence for a Bernoulli rate); a
  splitting schedule on a distress score, with fixed levels and child world addresses for forks; a failure-mode
  catalogue with a Good–Turing monitor of unseen mass (modes seen once divided by failures seen); a truncated-weight
  lower bound for refutation.
- *Loss model and split of budget.* False certification far outweighs false refutation. Explore first, to a fifth of
  the budget or until unseen mass is at most 0.05; refute if the lower bound exceeds p*; spend the rest on nominal
  sampling, for finalists only.
- *Obligations.* The bound holds at confidence 1 − δ for independent nominal episodes with a frozen failure predicate,
  and is scoped to its tier. Splitting supplies discovery and `Estimated` rates only. Failures are replayed one tier
  up. A bound refuses episodes selected by a battery or by a search.
- *Fallback and trigger.* Crude Monte Carlo alone. Triggers: under 1% of forks reach the next level (the score is
  uninformative); forks without fresh randomness.
- *Budgeted mode.* Exploration at most a fifth; on exhaustion a refusal that carries the bound reached.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* At δ = 0.05, bounds of 10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁶ need 3 × 10⁴, 3 × 10⁵ and 3 × 10⁶ nominal
  episodes: 75 s, 12.5 min and 2.1 h at 400 episodes per second. Splitting to 10⁻⁶ with six levels of probability 0.1
  and 10⁴ trajectories per level uses 6 × 10⁴ fragments at a coefficient of variation near 7% (cv² ≈ L(1 − p₀)/(p₀N)).
  Forks find failures much sooner than waiting; published adaptive stress testing reports 10 to 100 times fewer
  episodes at rates of 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁵ for failures driven by per-step disturbances with a good score (FACT, source
  register). Proving absence has a fixed price that no sampler discounts without assuming where failures live. If
  throughput is a tenth of that figure, 10⁻⁶ costs 21 h: certify 10⁻⁵ and report 10⁻⁶ as `Estimated`.
- *Sibling facts.* `fs-uq` has Gaussian importance sampling and multilevel Monte Carlo; no splitting code exists in
  the suite (FACT, to be re-read).

**DC-05 · Which experiment next, or stop.**

- *Artifacts.* An experiment table (simulation at a rung, a bench measurement, a real batch) with cost in GPU-second
  equivalents at a declared exchange rate, a closed-form expected value of sample information and their ratio; a
  Gaussian posterior on the finalists' score differences with sampling, tier-bias and reality-gap variance components
  read from the ledgers; the stop rule: no ratio clears the budget price.
- *Loss model.* Opportunity loss of the accepted design plus experiment cost.
- *Obligations.* The value is zero when no outcome can change the choice. Its intervals are scored against registered
  predictions. It never issues a verdict.
- *Fallback and trigger.* The fixed order: check, simulate, train, referee. Trigger: 80% intervals cover outside 65 to
  92% over 30 experiments.
- *Budgeted mode.* 64 candidates, closed form only.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃ and s̃² the variance the experiment
  removes from Δ ~ N(m, s²). With m = 0.03 and s² = 0.03² (sampling) + 0.04² (tier bias), 200 more fast-tier pairs are
  worth 0.0013 for 1 GPU-second and 8 referee pairs are worth 0.0055 for 27; the fast tier wins sixfold per unit cost.
  A second fast batch is worth 10⁻⁵, so the referee goes next: cheap episodes are bought until only tier bias is left,
  which only the referee can shrink. At m ≥ 0.15 no experiment can change the choice: stop and decide. Assumed:
  per-pair noise 0.3 on the fast tier and 0.1 on the referee; 30 core-seconds per GPU-second.

**DC-06 · Which cause and which repair rank first.**

- *Artifacts.* Typed interventions (`do(torque limit off)`, `do(friction := nominal)`, `do(estimator := privileged)`),
  each one declared mechanism wide; a fork ledger; a placebo flip rate ρ₀ from an intervention that should not matter;
  a probability of necessity per cause; minimal repair sets by delta debugging; the labels `but-for`, `joint` and
  `chaotic: unattributable`. The output is which repair edit ranks first.
- *Loss model.* A wrong first repair costs an agent turn and its compute; naming a cause in a chaotic regime sends the
  agent after noise, which costs more than saying nothing.
- *Obligations.* The empty intervention replays the failure bit for bit, so the starting point is exact. Each
  intervention changes one declared mechanism, checked by model diff. A cause is named only when its flip rate beats
  ρ₀ under a DC-01-style test. Design-parameter interventions re-run from the episode start, because winding
  temperature and the policy's own history differ; environment and controller-state interventions may fork from a
  snapshot under the classification of §29.8. Attribution arithmetic runs in double precision.
- *Fallback and trigger.* First-anomaly ordering, labelled `Estimated`. Triggers: a replay mismatch; ρ₀ above 0.2.
- *Budgeted mode.* 12 interventions, singles and pairs, about 700 forks. A coalition-value summary is computed only
  when no single or pair flips the outcome, and is labelled a description: such values can be negative and cannot
  establish necessity.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* Of 200 failing worlds re-run from snapshots, removing the torque limit prevents 164,
  nominal friction prevents 38 and a placebo nudge prevents 12. Net of placebo: 0.76 and 0.13. If the placebo rate is
  near a cause's rate, nothing is attributed; if only the pair prevents the failure, the cause is joint.

**DC-07 · Whether a result may carry a reality band.**

- *Artifacts.* A one-sided band, real ≥ sim − q̂, per scope key (robot, firmware, floor, policy); a test martingale on
  the band's conformal p-values; the table of scopes.
- *Loss model.* Citing a simulated result as predictive outside its calibrated family, against withholding a usable
  prediction inside it.
- *Obligations.* Coverage at least 1 − α within one calibrated robot and task family, varying by about six points at
  20 calibration episodes (FACT, source register). Nothing for designs that were never built. Conditions of real
  episodes come from a pre-registered draw; DC-05 chooses only how many. The alarm ends the regime and calibration
  restarts inside the new one.
- *Fallback and trigger.* `reality: no-claim`. Triggers: under 19 calibration points; martingale at or above 20; any
  change of scope key.
- *Budgeted mode.* The number of real episodes is what DC-05 buys at the declared exchange rate. Below the calibration
  floor no band is issued; there is no smaller band for a smaller budget.

**DC-08 · Which solver effort profile a run uses.**

- *Artifacts.* Declared effort profiles (iteration counts, tolerances, substeps), each its own model identity; a guard
  table that holds, per stratum, the cheapest profile whose risk bound (n·R̂ + 1)/(n + 1) is at most τ on the fidelity
  battery; a rule that re-runs unconverged worlds one profile up, flagged. If the chosen profile misses the throughput
  budget, the run shrinks its world count or is refused; the profile does not move.
- *Loss model.* An under-converged result that passes as evidence, against wasted iterations.
- *Obligations.* A monotone loss, enforced by taking the running maximum over higher profiles; expected loss at most τ
  for worlds exchangeable with the battery; the profile never reads the wall clock and never changes inside a run; the
  table changes only at a ledger refresh.
- *Fallback and trigger.* The default profile of §16. Triggers: fewer than 19 battery points in the stratum; a change
  of identity.
- *Budgeted mode.* At most one step up per world per run.

**DC-09 · Which job runs when.**

- *Artifacts.* Size classes from the system's own `--estimate` (interactive: at most 30 s); preempt-resume at snapshot
  boundaries with a quantum of at most 5 s; deficit round-robin per agent, weighted by budget; an admission guard; a
  capacity table of wait by class against utilization; a monitor of estimate calibration, with a conformal interval on
  step time per device family.
- *Loss model.* Agent idle time on interactive rungs, against preemption overhead and unfairness.
- *Obligations.* Utilization below 0.8 per device; a resumed job keeps its digests; an ageing bound prevents
  starvation. Arrivals are assumed near-Poisson; swarms burst, so the capacity table carries Kingman's correction for
  arrival variability.
- *Fallback and trigger.* Round-robin per agent, run to completion. Triggers: a fifth of jobs exceed twice their
  estimate; a resume digest differs.
- *Budgeted mode.* One preemption per batch job per 30 s; batch share at least 30%.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* One device at utilization 0.7 with 90% of jobs taking 10 s and 10% taking 30 min: a
  10 s job waits about 2,000 s first-come-first-served (W = λ·E[S²]/(2(1 − ρ))), 621 s with non-preemptive priority,
  and 0.2 s plus one quantum with preempt-resume; long jobs slow by 3%. The wait is the remainder of the training run
  already on the device, and only preemption removes it. If a device is reserved for interactive work, or a resume
  costs several seconds, running to completion is right.
- *Scope.* The core decides when a job's episodes run and never which episodes exist or in what order a study consumes
  them.

**DC-10 · Which scenes form a battery.**

- *Artifacts.* A scene × failure-mode incidence matrix weighted by frequency, severity and designs affected; a
  lazy-greedy trace of marginal gains; a certificate OPT_k ≤ f(S) + the k largest remaining gains; one slot in five
  reserved for random held-out scenes.
- *Loss model.* Uncovered failure-mode weight, against battery runtime.
- *Obligations.* Weighted coverage is monotone and submodular, so greedy selection reaches (1 − 1/e)·OPT at a fixed
  number of scenes; with unequal scene costs the cost-benefit variant is used and its weaker ratio is stated; the
  matrix records the identities it was built under.
- *Fallback and trigger.* The standard battery of §38. Trigger: a change of identity since the matrix was built.
- *Budgeted mode.* Any greedy prefix is a valid battery.
- *Failure mode.* A battery that overfits past failures; the held-out slots and the rule that DC-04 refuses
  battery-selected episodes contain it.
- *Worked example, illustrative.* 40 modes of total weight 100; greedy gains of 31, 22, 14, 9, 6 and 4 cover 86; the
  six largest remaining gains sum to 12, so OPT₆ ≤ 98 and the battery is within 88% of the best six-scene battery,
  against a worst case of 63%.

**DC-11 · Whether a kernel rewrite is accepted.**

- *Artifacts.* A closed rule table. Class E is bit-exact under round-to-nearest with contraction off: commutation,
  common-subexpression elimination, dead-code elimination, scaling by a power of two away from overflow and underflow,
  and scheduling. Class R is equal over the reals only: reassociation, distribution, fused multiply-add. A certificate
  is (source hash, target hash, rule trace), replayed by an independent checker.
- *Loss model.* A silent bit change invalidates replay and both ledgers; a skipped rewrite costs a few per cent of
  speed.
- *Obligations.* Each class-E rule is shown exact once on its domain, and digests match on a pinned corpus on every
  backend. A class-R rewrite needs a polynomial-identity certificate (C-14), an error bound, the fidelity battery and
  a new kernel version; a new version is a new identity and resets evidence.
- *Fallback and trigger.* The unrewritten derived kernel. Triggers: a digest mismatch; a whole-step gain under 3%.
- *Budgeted mode.* 60 s of search per kernel; one class-R candidate per release.
- *Worked example.* In `f32` with a = 10⁸, b = −10⁸, c = 1: (a + b) + c = 1 and a + (b + c) = 0, because the spacing
  of `f32` near 10⁸ is 8. Sharing a subexpression used three times saves two evaluations and changes no bit.
  Regrouping makes a different kernel, which must be named as one.
- *Ceiling.* The Lie-algebraic recursions are about 16 to 20% of an incumbent's step, so specializing them is worth
  about 1.2 times at most (FACT, source register). Class R and any wider search are worth their certificates only if
  our own M2 profile shows more than about a fifth of step time in derived arithmetic.

#### 28.19.3 Composition: timescales and interference

Timescales, slowest first: DC-11 at build; DC-10 per battery revision; DC-08 at ledger refresh; DC-07 per real batch;
DC-03 per generation; DC-02 per rung transition; DC-05 per experiment; DC-04 per finalist; DC-06 per failure cluster;
DC-01 per episode batch; DC-09 per quantum. Each loop treats every slower one as a constant, and no loop writes a
quantity that a faster loop is reading inside its own period. DC-04 → DC-06 → DC-10 is a one-way pipeline: failures
found feed attribution, attributed modes feed the incidence matrix, and nothing flows back inside a campaign.

Where two controllers touch the same quantity, one rule assigns it and one test shows the rule holds.

| Controllers and the shared quantity | Rule | Interference test |
|---|---|---|
| DC-02 and DC-03 survivors, DC-05: what runs next | Conformal survivors own the population stage, value of information owns the finalists; the handover is one-way | In a replayed campaign no design receives requests from both |
| DC-01 stopping, DC-03 calibration: higher-rung episodes | Audit designs run at fixed n and are never cancelled | No audit episode was stopped by an e-process |
| DC-01, DC-02, DC-03, DC-05 and DC-09: GPU-seconds, completion order | DC-09 decides when, never what; e-values consume episodes in address order | Random preemption and reordering leave digests and verdicts identical |
| DC-08 profile and DC-09: throughput | DC-08 never reads the wall clock and never changes inside a run | Under a saturated queue the profile and the digests are unchanged |
| DC-08, DC-11 and the ledgers of DC-01 to DC-03: model identity | An identity change resets evidence and calibration; a class-E rewrite changes no identity | Pooling across two model identities is refused; 1,000 worlds keep their digests after a class-E rewrite |
| DC-04 certification and DC-10: which scenes are sampled | Bounds use nominal draws only | A bound refuses battery-selected episodes |
| DC-07 band and DC-05: real episodes | Conditions come from a pre-registered draw; DC-05 chooses only how many | Assigned conditions equal the draw |
| DC-01 inside an N-way race: error rate | The mixture construction of §28.6, or α/(N − 1) per comparison | A null race of 81 keeps the best with probability at least 1 − α |
| The memo policy and every core: outcomes | A hit is bit-identical to a recomputation, so no core can tell | A partly memoized comparison reaches the identical e-value |

Two tensions are reconciled by construction. Conformal exchangeability against drift: the alarm ends the regime and
calibration restarts inside the new one. A parametric model (DC-05) beside distribution-free tests (DC-01 to DC-03):
the parametric model never issues a verdict.

### 29.2 Decisions

- **ADR-CHRON-01: metadata in a ledger on FrankenSQLite, bulk data in content-addressed column files.** Reason: the
  ledger needs transactions and queries; traces need sequential writes and deduplication. This is the pattern of
  FrankenSim's `fs-ledger` (FACT).
- **ADR-CHRON-02: the logical chunk partition is fixed per run and independent of device sharding.** Reason: after a
  retry splits a shard, two different range keys can cover the same transition. With a fixed partition, identical work
  always has an identical key, so a duplicate is detectable and a conflict is provable.
- **ADR-CHRON-03: evidence aggregates are integers.** On the evidence path an aggregate is a count, an integer sum, a
  minimum, a maximum, a bitwise or, or a histogram of integer counts. Quantiles come from histograms. Order-dependent
  sketches are not used. Reason: integer addition, minimum, maximum and or are commutative and associative, so the
  result has the same bits under any scheduling, shard split or merge order. The same rule gives the one positive
  licence for device-side atomics: integer maximum, minimum, or and add only.
- **ADR-CHRON-04: worlds are chosen for tracing by address, never by arrival.** Reason: a sample that depends on which
  slot finished first is not replayable and is not the same for two arms of a comparison.
- **ADR-CHRON-05: digests are integer mixes computed in-kernel; BLAKE3 is used on the host.** Reason: integer
  arithmetic is exact on every backend.
- **ADR-CHRON-06: a fork is classified before any state is restored.** Reason: restoring first and discovering later
  that the change invalidated the restored state produces a trajectory of no design.
- **ADR-CHRON-07: no claim without a trace.** Every claim that crosses the agent boundary has a `claim_id` that
  resolves to evidence, to the version of the rule that decided it and to the executions that produced it. A claim
  that does not resolve is not printed.
- **ADR-CHRON-08: the numerical residual is its own line, and created energy is audited.** The integrator's and the
  solver's energy error is never folded into a physical term, and energy that appears from nowhere is a finding about
  the simulator, reported against the result that benefited.
- **ADR-CHRON-09: retention never removes evidence silently.** Deletion leaves a tombstone; a metric whose samples are
  gone is refused, not interpolated; chunks in the evidence closure of a live claim are pinned.

### 29.3 What is stored, and where

Run and episode metadata, events, summaries, receipts, verdicts and predictions live in a ledger on FrankenSQLite (the
pattern of FrankenSim's `fs-ledger`, FACT). Bulk data lives in content-addressed column files. Everything carries the
identities of §11.

| Ledger table | One row is | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `run`, `job` | A run or job: manifest hash, identities, state, budgets, terminal receipt with counts | §32 |
| `episode` | (run, world, episode): outcome, T0 metrics, digests, failure class | Chronicle |
| `chunk`, `frontier` | A published chunk; the contiguous committed frontier (§29.4) | Chronicle |
| `event`, `snapshot` | A T1 event; a T3 snapshot with its content hash | Chronicle |
| `fidelity`, `rank_agreement`, `tier_correction` | A stratum of the three ledgers of disagreement | §16, §28.10, §29.11 |
| `verdict`, `prediction`, `core_decision` | A verdict record (`frob.verdict/1`); a registered prediction; one decision of a decision core | §28 |
| `memo_node` | A node of the memo graph with its key, class and cost | §28.14 |
| `claim`, `evidence`, `edge` | The artifact graph (§29.13) | Chronicle |
| `alpha_spend` | A pre-committed confirmation request and its error budget | §24 |
| `pin`, `tombstone` | A retention pin with its reason; a record of something deleted | Chronicle |

**The `.frun` format** (`FMT-FRUN`, schema `frob.run/1`). `frob run export <run>` writes a run as a manifest in
canonical JSON and a set of column files. The manifest holds the run identity; the model, scene, task and policy
identities; kernel versions, tier, determinism class and effort profile; the chunk length; channel schemas; the chunk
index with content hashes and chain digests; the frontier; every gap with its reason; and the no-authorization flags
of §12. A column file has a header (schema identifier, channels with kind, unit, frame and quantization, tick range)
and fixed-width little-endian columns, quantized integers or `f32`. A `.frun` is readable with no device and no
daemon, and `frob-verify` checks its hashes and chains with the standard library alone. Integers that can exceed 2⁵³
(ticks, sequence numbers, digests) are strings in every JSON view.

### 29.4 The chunk protocol

A world's record is published in fixed logical chunks. Chunk i of episode e of world w on stream s covers control
ticks [i·L, (i + 1)·L), cut short only by the episode's terminal tick; L is fixed for the run at admission
(OPEN-CHRON-2). The key does not mention a device, a shard, a batch slot or a retry.

```rust
// DESIGN: intent, not signatures.
struct ChunkKey { run: RunId, world: u32, episode: u32, stream: StreamId, index: u32 }

struct ChunkRecord {
    key: ChunkKey,
    content: Blake3,        // hash of the column file bytes
    prev: Blake3,           // chain digest of chunk index − 1; zero for index 0
    chain: Blake3,          // H(prev ‖ content ‖ key)
    state_digest: u128,     // in-kernel digest of the world's state at the chunk's last tick
    lease_gen: u64,         // fencing token of the writer
    commit_seq: u64,        // assigned inside the publishing transaction; a string on the wire
    gaps: Vec<Gap>,         // dropped intervals, each with a reason
}
```

**Publishing is one transaction** in the ledger, and it does five things or nothing.

1. *Lease.* The writer's `lease_gen` equals the run's current lease generation. Any reassignment of worlds to workers,
   by retry, shard split or device loss, increments the generation, so every older writer is fenced out; a healthy
   writer renews at its next chunk boundary. A late lease is refused and nothing is written.
2. *Alignment.* The chunk covers exactly its partition extent, or ends at the episode's terminal tick. A range that is
   not a partition extent is refused, so partially overlapping ranges cannot be committed.
3. *Predecessor.* `index` is the frontier of (run, world, episode, stream) plus one, and `prev` equals the chain
   digest at the frontier.
4. *Duplicate or conflict.* If the key is already committed with the same content hash, the original receipt is
   returned and no row is written. If it is committed with different bytes, the run is marked `determinism-violated`,
   serving from the memo graph stops for the node kinds involved, and the publish is refused (FROB-REF-878).
5. *Commit.* The row is inserted, the frontier advances, and `commit_seq` is the run's counter plus one.

The receipt is (key, chain, commit_seq). Three properties follow. The frontier is contiguous: for every (world,
episode, stream) the committed chunks are exactly indices 0 to the frontier, with no hole. A reset that reuses tick
zero cannot collide, because the next episode has a different key. And a crash after commit but before the receipt
reaches the writer is harmless: the retry finds the key committed with the same hash and gets the original receipt.

**Pagination is pinned to the committed frontier.** A query fixes, on its first page, the run, the value of
`commit_seq` at that moment, its filters and a total order (tick, world, episode, stream, sample identifier, so that
samples with equal timestamps are ordered). Rows committed later are invisible to that query. The cursor is the pin
plus the last order key. Appends between pages can therefore neither skip nor repeat a row. A summary statistic names
the frontier it was computed at, and a verdict cites the frontier it read.

**Stopping.** Cancellation takes effect at safe boundaries: the worker drains, discards unpublished state, and
acknowledges with the last committed tick per world, the outstanding device work and the observed cancellation
latency. A budget stop publishes nothing partial as a result; the chunks already committed remain as telemetry, and
the terminal receipt says `budget-stop`. Every terminal receipt counts completed, failed, refused and unstarted
trials, and separates three things that must never be merged: a numerical refusal, a budget stop and a physical
failure.

### 29.5 Telemetry levels, streams and channel kinds

| Level | Contents | Default scope |
|---|---|---|
| T0 | Per-episode metrics, the energy ledger totals, integer histograms and per-chunk minimum-maximum envelopes of declared channels (§29.7), and state digests at intervals | Every world, always |
| T1 | Events: contacts made and broken, limit and saturation episodes, faults, refusals | Every world |
| T2 | Traces at control rate: joint states, torques, contact wrenches, estimator state | An address-keyed sample of worlds (§29.6), and every failing world |
| T3 | Full state snapshots at intervals, sufficient for exact restart | Sampled and failing worlds |

A per-world ring buffer keeps the last seconds at T2 and T3 on the device and is flushed when a world fails, so
failures are always recorded in full without paying to record everything. The ring is also frozen around a mode change
or a requested tick.

Telemetry is organized in nine streams. A chunk belongs to one stream.

| Stream | Retained fields | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Kinematics | Base and joint state as group elements and velocities | Replay, viewer, metrics |
| Contact | Feature pair, normal, gap, impulse, patch, stick or slip, solver residual of the owning island | Slip and traction diagnosis; the audit of §29.9 |
| Energetics | Every term of the energy ledger, per port | The energy ledger |
| Control | Policy or controller identity, the provenance of each input sample, requested and applied action, saturation, fallback taken | Exploit detection; action-likelihood accounting (§23) |
| Sensing | Capture and delivery instants, validity mask, bias state, calibration identity | A-17, A-21, estimator diagnosis |
| Solver | Mode, iterations, residuals, step reductions, precision, contact capacity used, effort profile | DC-08; `Degraded` and `Faulted` counts |
| Runtime | Placement per stage (requested and actual), resident bytes, transfers, queue delay, shape bucket | The performance program (§36); DC-09 |
| Task | Goal events, termination against truncation, hard violations, reward and requirement components | Scorecards (§24) |
| Provenance | Identities, versions, lease generation, frontier | The artifact graph |

Every channel declares its kind, unit, frame, sampling, quantization and permitted consumers. The kind fixes how the
channel may be downsampled; a downsampling that ignores it is a defect that changes physics on the page.

| Kind | Example | Correct downsampling | Wrong, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| State | Joint angle, base pose | A sample, or a minimum-maximum envelope; orientation by a group-aware mean or a sample | A componentwise mean of quaternions leaves the group |
| Interval mean | Mean power over a tick | Duration-weighted mean | A sample drops energy |
| Impulse | Contact impulse | Sum over the window | A mean or a sample changes momentum transfer |
| Event | Contact made, limit hit | All events, or a count | Decimation deletes the event that explains the failure |
| Cumulative counter | Energy drawn, distance | Last value | A sum counts everything twice |

At a jump (an impact, a reset map, a mode switch) the state before and the state after are both recorded. Telemetry
that was dropped is marked with its interval and its reason, and a metric whose samples are missing is refused
(FROB-REF-888), not estimated from its neighbours. Channels are labelled as visible to the acting policy or as
privileged; the isolation itself is enforced in §20 and §23, and Chronicle's label is what an audit reads.

### 29.6 Choosing which worlds to trace: a reservoir by address

T2 and T3 cannot be kept for every world. The sample is chosen by a hash of the world's address:

```
h(w) = Philox( key = run_seed,  counter = (world, episode),  stream_id = "telemetry-sample" )   as u64
fixed rate p     trace w  iff  h(w) < ⌊p · 2⁶⁴⌋
fixed size k     trace the k worlds with the smallest h(w)
```

The stream identifier is its own, so the choice is independent of every random draw the physics makes. The fixed-size
form is a reservoir sample without the usual dependence on arrival order: the k smallest hashes of a set are a uniform
sample of size k without replacement, and the k smallest of a union are the k smallest of the per-shard k smallest, so
shards merge exactly. Four properties follow from the sample being a pure function of addresses.

- Replay needs no record of the choice; it recomputes it.
- Two designs compared on the same addresses are traced in the same worlds, so every traced pair is a pair.
- Co-tenancy, slot assignment, preemption and shard count cannot change it.
- Inclusion probabilities are known, so population estimates from traced worlds are weighted: 1/p for the fixed rate,
  N/k for the fixed size with N worlds admitted. Failing worlds are always traced; they form a stratum of weight one,
  and an estimate over all worlds is the stratified sum of the two.

### 29.7 Deterministic integer histograms

Distributional summaries (the 95th percentile of knee torque, the distribution of friction utilization) are histograms
of integer counts over integer bins.

- **Quantization.** A channel declares a quantum u. A value x becomes q = round-half-even(x/u), saturated to the
  channel's integer range, with one overflow bin on each side. The result depends only on the bits of x.
- **Bins.** For magnitude |q| below 2ˢ, one bin per integer, exact. Above, the octave e = ⌊log₂|q|⌋ is split into 2ˢ
  equal sub-buckets, index (|q| >> (e − s)) & (2ˢ − 1). With m magnitude bits there are (m − s + 1)·2ˢ bins per sign,
  and every bin's width is at most 2⁻ˢ of its lower edge. Defaults (DESIGN): run-level histograms m = 24, s = 5, 640
  bins per sign, relative resolution 3.1%; per-world T0 histograms m = 16, s = 3, 112 bins per sign with 16-bit
  saturating counts, about 450 bytes per channel per world.
- **Arithmetic.** Count-leading-zeros, shifts, masks and integer add: exact on every backend. Merging two histograms
  is integer addition of counts. A run-level histogram may be accumulated on the device with integer atomic add
  (ADR-CHRON-03).
- **Queries.** `frob telemetry histogram <run> --channel <name> --quantile 0.95` returns the bounds of the bin that
  contains the quantile, as an interval, with the count. It never returns a point it did not measure.

Order-dependent quantile sketches (the compactor and digest families) are excluded from the evidence path: their
output depends on insertion and merge order, so the same worlds replayed under a different shard split give different
bits. Counterexample on the smallest scale: a compactor of capacity two that keeps the lower of each sorted pair
summarizes the stream 1, 2, 3 as {1 with weight 2, 3} and the stream 3, 2, 1 as {2 with weight 2, 1}, so the medians
are 1 and 2; integer bin counts are 1, 1, 1 in both orders.

### 29.8 Replay, teleport and forks (GOAL-10)

A world is determined by its address, its model, scene, task and policy identities, and its kernel versions. `frob sim
replay <episode>` reproduces it. Adding `--tier batch-cpu` **teleports** it: the run restarts from the nearest
snapshot on the CPU, compares digests step by step with the recorded ones, and at the first difference re-runs that
step with per-stage digests to name the stage and kernel where the tiers part (the per-subsystem digest technique of
the Wright Flyer plan, FACT). Digests are a 128-bit integer mix computed in-kernel, since integer arithmetic is exact
on every backend; BLAKE3 is used on the host for snapshots.

A world's trajectory must not depend on the batch it ran in. Replay of one world alone, in a batch of different size,
and among different co-tenants must give the recorded digests on the same backend (GATE-CHRON-01).

**What a snapshot contains.** Physical state as group elements, velocities and anchored cell indices; contact feature
memory, impulses and every solver warm start; the phase accumulators of every clock and every packet queue with its
packets (§20); controller, estimator and filter state, including a policy's recurrent state; actuator thermal and
electrical state; the step counter that indexes the counter-based random streams (there is no generator state to
save); scene state the robot can change (movable objects, and film or wear fields where those features are on);
learning state when the run is a training run; the command cursor; and the provenance identities. Full snapshots are
relocatable; sparse differences are stored between them. A snapshot is bound to its numerical profile: restoring it
under another profile is a comparison replay, not a continuation.

**Fork classification.** `frob run fork <episode> --at <tick> --change <set>` classifies the request before it
restores anything, and returns the earliest valid fork tick with the fields that forced it.

| Class | Example | Condition | Restart point | What is reused |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continuation | Replay; teleport; the empty intervention | The physical, clock, sensor and policy contracts all match the recorded run | The nearest snapshot | Everything. On the same backend it replays bit for bit |
| Intervention | A changed setpoint; a declared disturbance; a controller mode; privileged state substituted into the estimator; a change to a part of the scene the robot has not yet touched or sensed | The change takes effect at a future tick, and no state held in the snapshot depends on a changed field | The latest snapshot before the first tick at which recorded state depends on a changed field | The prefix up to that snapshot; exogenous draws by address |
| Structural substitution | A larger wheel; a gear ratio; a mass; a sensor mount; a contact law; global friction | A model field changes, or recorded state at tick zero already depends on the changed field | The authored initial condition, settled again for the new design | Exogenous draws only, by address and by physical coordinate. Contact memory, proprioception, camera histories and policy actions are regenerated |

Negative control: grafting the contact warm start of a 60 mm wheel onto a 75 mm design at a threshold lip must return
a structured refusal (FROB-REF-882) naming the contact memory as the field that forces a restart. A prefix is reused
only when it is identical by construction, never because it is "close".

**The first-divergence algorithm.** `frob telemetry divergence <a> <b>` answers "where do these two computations
part", for a replay against its record, two tiers, or two designs of one lineage.

1. *Align.* Committed ticks and events of both runs; canonical variables by stable identifier (link, joint, contact
   feature pair, channel).
2. *Exact classes.* Compare the chain of state digests. Because each chain value covers the whole prefix, "the
   prefixes agree up to chunk i" is monotone in i and binary search over chunks is valid. Inside the first unequal
   chunk the per-step digests are compared in order, and the first unequal step is re-run with per-stage digests.
3. *Tolerance class.* For an interval of ticks, the stored envelopes give an upper bound on the largest scaled
   difference and the stored samples give a lower bound. An upper bound at or below tolerance certifies agreement on
   the interval; a lower bound above tolerance proves a mismatch inside it; anything else is unresolved and is refined
   chronologically, earliest sub-interval first.
4. *Never bisect on an undecided or pointwise predicate.* Counterexample: two traces differ at ticks 1 and 5 and agree
   at ticks 2 to 4. A midpoint search on "tick k differs" probes tick 3, sees agreement, goes right and reports tick
   5.
5. *Missing samples.* The answer is the earliest possible interval and a coverage gap, not a tick.
6. *Stage.* Within the first supported interval the algorithm refines to the first stage over tolerance.
7. *Changed topology.* Two designs with different bodies are compared on shared interfaces and task observables only.
   A new leg has no counterpart.
8. *Decisions.* Beside the state divergence, the first tick at which a task decision differs (a contact-mode sequence,
   a termination, a success) is reported, which is what the decision-level equivalence test of §35 reads.

The report is a statement about where two computations part. It is not a statement about cause, and the order in which
solver stages report residuals is not evidence of a first physical cause.

### 29.9 The energy ledger (GOAL-11) and the audit for created energy

For each world and episode:

```
battery energy out = Σ actuators (copper loss + friction loss + gear loss + Δ stored) + mechanical work at joints
mechanical work at joints = Δ kinetic + Δ potential + contact dissipation (normal damping + sliding friction)
                            + joint damping + work on fixtures and payloads + numerical residual
```

Every term is a port quantity (C-12), accumulated in double-float on the device. The numerical residual is reported as
its own line, "integrator and solver", and is never folded into a physical term. Breakdowns are available by joint, by
limb, and by stance and swing. Cost of transport decomposes along the same lines, so an agent can see that, say, 38%
of the energy went to copper loss in the knees during stance.

Three refinements. On the electrical side, bus power Σ vⱼ·iⱼ is integrated with its positive and negative parts
separately, so regeneration is visible and is never netted against draw without saying so. Energy per task is reported
two ways: the mean over completed episodes, and the total over all attempts divided by completions. A design that
succeeds once for 10 J and fails once for 20 J reports 10 J and 30 J; a rival with two 12 J successes reports 12 J and
12 J; the first figure alone would rank them wrongly. And every energy figure names the residual it was computed with.

**The audit for energy the simulator created.** A fast contact model can hand a policy energy that no actuator
supplied. The ledger makes this measurable, and three monitors watch for it in every world.

| Monitor | Quantity | Flag | Threshold (TARGET) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residual sign | r = Δ kinetic + Δ potential − (actuator work + external work − all dissipation), per step; created energy is Σ max(0, r) over a window | `energy-created` | Created energy above 1% of the absolute work exchanged in any 0.5 s window on a fast tier; above 10⁻⁶ relative on the referee |
| Contact passivity | The net work a contact does on the bodies over one contact episode, from touch to release | `contact-active` | Net positive work above the tier's residual bound: the contact returned more than it absorbed |
| Cone excess | Friction utilization ‖f_t‖ / (μ·f_n) per contact | `cone-excess` | Utilization above 1.05 for more than 1% of stance time; a pyramidal cone admits up to √2 along its diagonal |

A flagged episode keeps its result and gains the flag. A design or policy whose advantage in a comparison coincides
with flagged episodes is typed `model-conditional` by A-19 (§28.11), and the exploitation detectors of §23 read the
same flags. Known fixtures: a body bouncing on a stiff penalty contact at a step near the stability limit gains height
and must raise `energy-created` and `contact-active`; the same drop under the convex law at the default step must
raise neither; a block pushed along the diagonal of a pyramidal cone must raise `cone-excess`.

### 29.10 Failure taxonomy

Mechanical classes: `fall`, `stuck`, `collision`, `self-collision`, `slip`, `torque-saturation`, `speed-saturation`,
`thermal-limit`, `limit-impact`, `timeout`, `numerical-fault`. Classes for the world and for perception: `film-slip`
(traction lost on a contaminated surface), `thermal-runaway`, `brownout`, `perception-dropout` (a sensor blinded by
glass, steam or glare), `localization-alias`, `pinch`, `snag`, `slosh-tip`, `gait-instability`, `observation-alias`,
`timing-race`, `model-drift`. A class of the second group is reported only when the feature that can produce it is on
in the scene or the model.

Every terminal outcome also has a kind: `physical` (the simulated robot failed), `numerical` (the solver or integrator
refused or faulted), `budget` (a limit stopped the run) or `protocol` (the request was invalid). A numerical outcome
leaves the verdict unknown; it is not counted as a physical failure and it is not dropped from the denominator.

Monitors timestamp their first anomaly; the earliest anomaly before the terminal event is reported as the first cause,
with stills at −0.5 s, 0 s and +0.2 s. The ordering is a heuristic and is labeled `Estimated`. It is a description.
Attribution by intervention is §27.

### 29.11 The tier-correction ledger

Promotion (§16, DC-01) says whether a fast-tier result is faithful. It does not say what the number would have been on
the referee. The tier-correction ledger supplies that number from the pairs promotion already pays for, with an
estimator that stays unbiased when the fast tier is wrong.

**What a row holds.** Key: model class, scene class, task, quantity, tier pair, kernel versions, effort profile,
battery version. Measures: the number of paired addresses; mean and variance on each tier; mean and variance of the
difference; the per-episode correlation ρ_ep; the regression coefficient β; the audit probabilities used; the
effective sample size; the share of the stratum's scenarios the referee can complete; the estimator version and the
frontier.

**The estimator [S].** N addresses run on the fast tier. Each gets a prediction mᵢ of its referee outcome, fixed
before any referee outcome of the round exists (by default mᵢ = c + β·Y_fast,i with c and β from earlier rows of the
ledger; β = 1 and c = 0 in a new stratum), an audit probability pᵢ ≥ 1/20 (DESIGN floor), and an audit bit Aᵢ drawn
from the address-keyed stream "referee-audit". Referee episodes run only where Aᵢ = 1. `eval.promote` returns θ̂ with
its interval beside the label that DC-01 issues.

```
Zᵢ = mᵢ + Aᵢ · (Y_ref,i − mᵢ) / pᵢ            θ̂ = (1/N) · Σᵢ Zᵢ
```

- *Unbiased under a wrong fast tier.* E[Aᵢ/pᵢ] = 1 given everything fixed before the draw, so E[Zᵢ] = E[Y_ref,i]. A
  bad prediction inflates the variance and cannot move the mean. The estimator is of the doubly robust kind (right if
  either the audit probabilities or the outcome model is right), and here the audit probabilities are exact by
  construction, because the draw is an addressed stream.
- *Two exact limits.* With every pᵢ = 1 it is the plain referee mean, exactly. With no audited address it is refused.
- *Bounded weights without clipping.* The floor on pᵢ bounds every weight by 20, so nothing is clipped and no clipping
  bias exists. Likelihood-ratio weights for tilted failure proposals are a different object and belong to §26, where
  clipped mass is reported.
- *Variance.* About (1/N)·[Var(Y_ref) + E((1 − p)/p·(Y_ref − m)²)]. For a constant audit fraction n/N, N much larger
  than n and the best linear prediction, this is (1 − ρ_ep²)·Var(Y_ref)/n: the saving in referee episodes is 1/(1 −
  ρ_ep²).
- *Sequential use.* Zᵢ is bounded once the metric has a span, so a confidence sequence on θ̂ lets a promotion stop
  early against a declared indifference margin. For a design comparison the same construction is applied to the paired
  contrast, and both referee arms run only on audited addresses.

**Limits and fallbacks.** Budget: 256 referee episodes per promotion (DESIGN), after which the result stays
`provisional`. The estimator is switched off for a stratum, and promotion falls back to referee episodes alone, when
the effective sample size (Σ wᵢ)²/Σ wᵢ² falls under 30% of the audited count, or when ρ_ep is under 0.7 on at least
200 paired addresses (DESIGN triggers; 0.7 is the kill threshold of the rank-agreement experiment). The same audited
pairs feed DC-01's exceedance bits and this estimator, and the audit draw is frozen within a round, so the two cannot
steer each other. The loss model in the contract: wrong establishment 100, a referee episode 1, a wrong refusal 10
(DESIGN constants). Calibration: interval coverage is checked against full-referee values on the standard battery
(§38).

**The ceiling.** The saving is 1.96 at ρ_ep = 0.7, 5.3 at 0.9, 10 at 0.95 and 25 at 0.98 (arithmetic). Paired worlds
decouple under contact, and published cross-fidelity correlation becomes neutral at large dynamics gaps (FACT, source
register), so the plan expects about two until a higher ρ_ep is measured. The estimator is adopted because it is
nearly free, not because it is a large factor.

**The referee's reach.** A promotion needs referee episodes, and the referee does not reach every scenario:
FrankenSim's articulated contact step admits at most 21 contacts and its event solve has no persistent time stepper
(FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-mbd/src/articulated_contacts/step.rs`, `fs-contact/src/sustained/solve.rs` `MAX_CONTACTS
= 21`; re-read at the pinned commit). The ledger therefore records, per stratum, the share of battery scenarios the
referee completes and the wall-clock of a promotion, and both figures are reported at the M2 exit. A promotion in a
stratum the referee cannot complete returns `provisional: referee-unavailable`.

**A background sampler [F].** Idle CPU cores re-run address-sampled fast-tier worlds on the referee from their
snapshots and write rows into the fidelity and tier-correction ledgers. At the planned throughputs it covers on the
order of 0.1 to 2% of GPU world-seconds (arithmetic from the referee's speed; source register), and it reports that
coverage figure. It is a correctness guard and a free source of pilot variances. It is not a speed lever, and its
mechanism belongs to §16.

**Segments are a different quantity [F].** Restarting one-second segments on the referee from fast-tier snapshots
shows where in an episode the tiers part. It estimates per-segment fidelity, not the episode-level outcome, and is
never used for promotion. Falsifier: if segment disagreement does not predict episode-level exceedance better than
chance on the standard battery, the mode is removed; the ceiling of its benefit is diagnostic time, not referee cost.

### 29.12 Summaries an agent can afford

`frob telemetry summary --budget 800` returns a hierarchical summary sized to a token budget, with handles for
drill-down. Numbers carry units and evidence classes. A compact encoding is available (the suite's MCP tools already
use TOON, FACT). Raw traces are never the default answer.

Every summary states its denominator and the frontier it was computed at. Counts sum to what was planned: completed,
failed, refused, unstarted, and for a paired study the five cells of the accounting identity of §28.6. Progress is
reported with its denominator ("1,312 of 2,048 episodes committed"), never as a bare percentage. Each number that
supports a claim carries the `claim_id`, so a drill-down handle and an evidence trace are the same thing.

### 29.13 The artifact graph: every claim traces to its evidence

Four kinds of identifier tie a statement to what supports it.

```
ClaimId            BLAKE3( claim type ‖ subject identities ‖ statement ‖ scope )
EvidenceId         BLAKE3( kind ‖ payload hash )
                   kinds: episode set · chunk range · snapshot · certificate · receipt · ledger row · analysis result
DecisionPolicyId   ( dotted rule name, version )        e.g. ("atelier.dc01.promote", 3)
TraceId            128-bit, time-ordered identifier of the execution that produced an evidence item
```

Rules. An envelope that carries a claim carries its `claim_id`. `frob evidence trace <claim_id>` returns the closure:
the evidence items, the rule versions that decided, the executions, and the claims it depends on. A claim whose
closure contains a dangling or tombstoned node prints `evidence: unavailable` and cannot be cited (FROB-REF-869). Each
gate row in `registries/gates.toml` lists the `claim_id`s that its recorded PASS rests on, and each demonstration
manifest lists the claims it supports with commands that run in under 60 s. Trace identifiers and wall-clock times
identify executions; they never enter a content hash, so two executions of the same work produce the same evidence
identifiers and different traces.

### 29.15 Simulation testing of the daemon and the ledger

The job state machine, the idempotency binding, the chunk publisher, pagination, the lease manager, the memo store and
the dispatcher are concurrent code whose failures are rare interleavings. They are tested by deterministic simulation,
not by waiting.

- **Harness.** asupersync's lab runtime supplies virtual time, seeded scheduling, fault injection, lineage-driven
  fault selection, recorded replay and minimization of failing schedules (FACT: `asupersync/src/lab/` contains
  `chaos.rs`, `injection.rs`, `ldfi.rs`, `replay.rs`, `replay_minimization.rs`, `schedule_minimizer.rs`, `dual_run.rs`
  and `explorer.rs`). The device is replaced by a fake whose episode outcomes are a pure function of the world
  address, so a whole campaign simulates in milliseconds.
- **Fault menu.** A crash at every await point of the publish and submit paths; a torn ledger write; duplicate and
  reordered delivery; delay; lease expiry and takeover by a second writer; device loss; a full disk; a clock jump;
  cancellation at every boundary; a client that disconnects with a job running and reconnects by submission key.
- **Lineage-driven selection.** From the lineage of a successful run, the faults that could have broken its outcome
  are tried first, before random ones.
- **Oracles.** The invariants INV-CHRON-01 to -04 and -12, INV-ATEL-02 and -06, and the one-job-one-charge rule of
  §32.
- **Minimization.** A failing run is reduced to a seed, a fault list and a schedule of minimal length, stored as a
  regression that replays in under a second.
- **Keeping the fake honest.** Dual-run scenarios execute the same small job against the fake device and against
  `batch-cpu` and require the same ledger state, so the fake cannot drift into testing a protocol the real workers do
  not follow.

Targets: every await point on the publish and submit paths is hit by a crash fault at least once, and 10⁵ seeded
schedules run per release (TARGET). This extends the chaos tier of §38, which injects cancellation at kernel-batch
boundaries; it does not replace it.

### 29.16 Retention

| Class | Contents | Default policy |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence-pinned | Chunks, snapshots, ledger rows, certificates and receipts in the closure of a live claim; qualification receipts; registered predictions | Kept until the claim is withdrawn; then tombstoned |
| Failure captures | T2 and T3 rings of failing worlds | Kept while the run is the head of a lineage or is cited by an open comparison, bisect or autopsy; then 30 days (OPEN-CHRON-4) |
| Sampled traces | T2 and T3 of sampled worlds that did not fail | Evicted under the store budget, oldest unpinned first |
| T0 and T1 | Metrics, histograms, digests, events | Kept for the life of the run record |
| Memo artifacts | Cached results (§28.14) | 64 GB by default; evict, never block |

`frob store status` reports what each class holds and what is pinned and why; `frob store collect --dry-run` lists
what a collection would remove. Pins are taken when a verdict is issued: the verdict's evidence closure is pinned in
the same transaction that writes the verdict, so the interval that explains a decision cannot be collected while the
decision stands. Collection marks from pins, open jobs, lineage heads and registered predictions, sweeps the rest by
class, and writes a tombstone for everything it removes: content hash, class, time, reason. A later citation of a
collected object fails with that reason. Telemetry derived from a private scene inherits the scene's privacy class
(§37) through retention and export.
