## 30. Ground: calibration against reality

Crate `frob-ground` (layer 5). Cluster `ground`. The admission lane and the `reality:` report line are [S].
Identification, card updates, bands, drift detection and as-built ingestion are [F]. Nothing in this chapter is
on the critical path of a milestone before M5, and no gate in it needs hardware.

### 30.1 What Ground owns

- **The Reality claim of §8**: its statuses, its scope key, the line every report prints, and the refusals that
  stop a simulated number from being read as a measured one.
- **Admission of real data**: logs from a robot or a bench rig, single bench measurements, and scans of built
  parts, each with its licence, provenance, measurement uncertainty and privacy mark.
- **A sim-to-real procedure in ten steps** (§30.6): clock fitting, identifiability analysis and excitation
  design, identification in a fixed order, Bayesian card updates, frozen predictions, sealed trials, holdout
  retirement, conformal bands per calibrated cell, drift monitoring, and the feedback of discrepancy into later
  studies.
- **The replication kit** (§30.18) and the **synthetic rehearsal** on which every gate of this chapter is
  decided (§30.21).

Ground does not own hardware. It reads files and writes files (INV-GROUND-07). It has no driver, no bus, no
real-time loop and no hardware-in-the-loop mode, and the non-goal on deployment stacks in §6 stands. Scans of
real places are Habitat's lane (§19). Ground handles robots, parts and rigs.

### 30.2 The Reality claim, and why it is mostly `no-claim`

Reality is the one claim the first release mostly cannot make. The lane exists so that the first admitted dataset
has somewhere to land: a dataset of real-robot measurements enters with its license, its provenance and its
measurement uncertainty, and a comparison is recorded as a Reality result with its scope. Until then every report
prints `reality: no-claim`. FrankenTextiles recorded the same limit about its own domain: "More numerical
permutations cannot supply those observations" (FACT).

Four reasons make `no-claim` the normal state. They are stated here so that no later chapter argues around them.

1. **No data exists.** Nothing in this plan has been compared with a physical robot (FACT, Appendix H). A
   measurement that has not been taken cannot be replaced by a larger batch.
2. **Agreement between tiers cannot reveal a wrong card.** Every tier consumes the same actuator card (§15), so
   Fidelity and Parity results say nothing about whether the card describes a real motor. Through M4 the
   catalogue holds generic classes only (OPEN-SINEW-1). A report that supports a benchmark claim therefore
   prints the share of card fields whose provenance is `generic` or `assumed` (DESIGN: it is the one number that
   tells a reader how much of the model was guessed).
3. **A Reality result has a scope and no meaning outside it.** The scope is a *cell* (§30.5): which physical
   units, which controller and firmware, which surface, which task family, inside which envelope of conditions.
4. **A design that was never built has no reality band.** Band coverage is a statement about draws exchangeable
   with the calibration draws (§30.12). An unbuilt design is not such a draw, and the distance between it and
   the calibrated ones cannot be measured.

| Status | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `no-claim` | Nothing measured supports the number. | The default. A legitimate terminal state, not a failure. |
| `synthetic` | The comparison ran against data generated by a simulator standing in for reality. | Never promoted (INV-GROUND-06). |
| `published-anchor` | A simulated quantity was compared with a figure published for the same robot. | A Parity result (§8), never Reality: published test conditions are loose. Scheduled by the Gauntlet (§38). |
| `candidate(cell)` | A posterior was fitted in this cell and has not been scored on sealed data. | Changes no randomization width (INV-GROUND-05). |
| `calibrated(cell, n, level)` | A frozen prediction was scored on sealed data and a band is issued. | Valid only inside the cell. |
| `retired(cell, reason)` | A drift monitor fired, the scope changed, or the calibration expired. | Reverts to `no-claim` with the reason. |

Every report carries one line of the form
`reality: <status> [cell=<id> n=<n> level=<1−α> band=<q̂ with unit> dataset=<hash prefix>]`.

### 30.5 Data admitted, and the cell

A real dataset is a `.frun` bundle whose manifest declares `origin = "real"`. No new file extension is
introduced; the manifest schema is `frob.ground.dataset/1`.

```toml
# frob.ground.dataset/1 (DESIGN)
[dataset]
id       = "blake3:…"            # over the canonical manifest and every chunk hash
origin   = "real"                 # "real" | "synthetic"; a synthetic set can never be relabelled
kind     = "robot-log"            # "robot-log" | "bench" | "scan"
license  = "CC-BY-4.0"            # or "private"; private data never enters a kit (§30.18)
acquired = { by = "operator-id", on = "2027-03-14", rights = "owner" }
[rig]
units       = ["unit-07"]         # serial-level identity; one unit is one cluster (§30.12)
controller  = "blake3:…"          # controller identity; firmware as reported by the device
firmware    = "2.4.1"
surface     = "sealed-concrete/dry"
envelope    = { temperature_C = [18, 24], payload_kg = [0, 2], speed_m_s = [0, 1.2] }
[[instrument]]
name = "encoder-knee-L"; quantity = "angle"; unit = "rad"; sigma = 1.5e-4; bias_bound = 3.0e-4
instant = "sample"; clock = "mcu"; calibration = "cal-2027-02"
[[clock]]
name = "mcu"; nominal_hz = 1000; marks = ["pulse-0", "pulse-1"]   # raw source timestamps are retained
[split]
by          = "day"               # "hardware" | "condition" | "day"; never a random split of rows
calibration = ["2027-03-14"]; validation = ["2027-03-15"]; holdout = ["2027-03-21"]
sealed      = ["validation", "holdout"]                           # readable only through ground.score
attempts    = "all"               # every attempted trial is a row, including aborted ones
```

Admission rules (DESIGN, each with its reason):

- **Licence, provenance, uncertainty and rights are required fields.** A dataset without them is refused
  (FROB-REF-873), because a Reality result must be able to name what it rests on.
- **Splits follow hardware, condition or day.** Rows inside one episode are dependent, so a random split of rows
  leaks the validation set into the fit.
- **The full denominator is recorded.** A log that contains only the trials that completed calibrates a band on
  survivors. `attempts = "all"` is checked against the operator's trial counter.
- **Sealed chunks are hash-committed at admission** and are read only by `ground.score`, once, with a ledger
  event (INV-GROUND-03).
- **Synthetic stays synthetic.** Nominal parts and simulated logs carry `origin = "synthetic"` for life.
- **Privacy is inherited.** A dataset recorded in a private place carries the private mark of §37.

The cell is the scope key of every Reality result:

```
CellKey = ( design: DesignId, units: set of unit ids, controller: ControllerId, firmware: text,
            surface: class, task_family: TaskFamilyId, envelope: box over the recorded conditions )
```

A change to any component is a scope change (§30.13). Two cells are never pooled (FROB-REF-814).

### 30.7 Clock fitting [F]

Each source clock is mapped to the reference clock by t_ref = a + (1 + s)·t_src, with offset a and skew s. There
are two unknowns, so **one shared pulse fixes only one combination of them**. At a skew of 100 ppm, two clocks
that coincide at the start differ by 0.8 ms after 8 s (FACT, arithmetic), which is most of a tick at 1 kHz and
enough to move a packet across a decision instant (§20).

The fit is weighted least squares over m ≥ 2 independent marks that bracket the trial. For two marks at
t₁ < t₂ with independent timing error σ_m each (PO-GROUND-02):

- skew uncertainty σ_s = √2·σ_m/(t₂ − t₁);
- uncertainty of an aligned instant σ_t(t)² = σ_m²·[(t₂ − t)² + (t − t₁)²]/(t₂ − t₁)², which is at most σ_m²
  inside the bracket and grows quadratically outside it.

With σ_m = 50 µs and marks 10 s apart, σ_s is 7.1 ppm (FACT, arithmetic). If skew can change during the trial,
the fit is piecewise affine over more marks and reports its largest residual; a trial with fewer marks than the
model needs is refused (FROB-REF-876).

Rules:

1. **Scored instants lie inside the bracket**, or the score is refused.
2. **The clock is never fitted to the event being scored.** Counterexample: the prediction says the wheel
   crosses the sill at 4.212 s and the log shows 4.180 s. Choosing the offset that aligns the two reports a
   crossing-time error of zero and an offset of 32 ms. The whole error has been moved into the clock.
3. **Cross-correlating command and response is not a clock fit.** It estimates offset plus actuator delay: a
   delay of 8 ms with an offset of −8 ms produces the same logs as no delay and no offset. Without a shared
   hardware mark the identified quantity is reported as "delay plus offset" and the card's delay is not updated.
4. **Raw timestamps are kept.** Aligned times are derived data carrying the fit's uncertainty. A test of
   whether a packet arrived before a decision uses the interval and answers `undecided` when it straddles.
5. **Each instrument names which of the seven instants of §20 its timestamp denotes.** A timestamp with no
   declared instant is refused.

### 30.8 Identifiability and excitation design [F]

**Analysis.** For a planned experiment with predicted measurements y(θ), Ground forms the Fisher information
F = Jᵀ·R⁻¹·J, where J = ∂y/∂θ is computed on the referee in double precision at the prior mean and at eight
prior draws (TARGET), and R is the instrument covariance including shared calibration error. Parameters are
scaled by their prior standard deviations, so F is dimensionless. Four checks follow.

| Check | Rule (thresholds are TARGETs) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Rank | A singular direction of the scaled J with σ_min/σ_max below 10⁻⁶ | Structurally unidentified in this experiment |
| Precision | The Cramér–Rao bound √((F⁻¹)_ii) is at most half the prior standard deviation | Parameter i is identified by the experiment |
| Aliasing | A pair with correlation of magnitude at least 0.95 in F⁻¹ | Reported as an aliased combination, with the combination that is identified |
| Profile | A profile likelihood on the synthetic twin of the experiment, for finalists | A flat profile overrides a good local bound |

Some aliases are structural and known in advance. They are planted in GATE-GROUND-03 and seed the
negative-control registry.

| # | Aliased quantities | Smallest case | What separates them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Armature N²·J_r and link inertia about the joint axis | A joint moving alone: only I_link + N²·J_r appears | A payload of known inertia, or a second joint moving |
| 2 | Coulomb friction and a constant bias torque | Motion in one direction: τ_c·sgn(q̇) + τ_b is one number | Reversals |
| 3 | Viscous friction and back-EMF damping | Voltage drive: b + k_t·k_e/R is one number | A current measurement, or current-mode drive |
| 4 | Torque constant and the scale of inertia and friction | Commanded current and encoder only: all four are known up to one factor | A known payload, or a torque sensor |
| 5 | Actuator delay and clock offset | §30.7, rule 3 | A shared hardware mark |
| 6 | Friction coefficient and the torque limit | F = min(k_t·I/r, μ·N) with k_t = 0.2 N·m/A and r = 0.06 m: at 1 A and 2 A under 30 N and 40 N the drawbar force is 3.33 N and 6.67 N for μ = 0.4 and for μ = 0.8; at 3 A under 10 N it is 4 N and 8 N | Exciting the traction-limited regime as well as the torque-limited one |
| 7 | Static friction and everything else | A run that never dwells at zero velocity carries no information on breakaway | Slow torque ramps from rest, both directions |
| 8 | Contact stiffness and contact damping | One drop height gives one rebound ratio | Two drop heights, or a force trace |

**Design.** Candidate experiments come from a small grammar, each with a duration, a safety envelope (limits on
torque, speed, travel and temperature) and a predicted J.

| Experiment | Instruments | Informs |
|---|---|---|
| Slow torque ramp from rest, both directions | Current, encoder | Static friction, Stribeck speed |
| Velocity steps and coast-down | Encoder | Coulomb and viscous friction |
| Multisine torque, unloaded and with a known payload | Current, encoder | Armature, torque-constant scale |
| Step command with a shared mark | Command log, encoder | Delay, first-order lag |
| Stall and speed sweep at two bus voltages | Current, voltage, encoder | Torque–speed envelope |
| Thermal soak at fixed current | Winding and case temperature | Thermal resistances and capacitances |
| Incline to first slip; sled drag at two normal loads | Angle, force | Friction coefficient per material pair |
| Drop from two heights | Force or acceleration | Contact stiffness and dissipation |
| Scale, balance and bifilar pendulum | Mass, period | Mass, centre of mass, inertia |

Selection maximizes log det(I + Σ_g J_gᵀ·J_g/ridge) greedily over whole experiments under a time budget, using
`fs-uq::experimental_design`. Where instruments share a calibration error, the cost-budgeted plan of
`fs-assimilate::groups::stream::design` is used instead, because it conditions on the shared nuisance.
`ground.excite` returns the plan, the predicted bound per parameter, the combinations still aliased and the
experiment that would separate each. It needs no data and it never returns a fit.

Two cautions bind the design.

- **Model class.** A probe that is perfect inside its assumed law class can be wrong outside it. In one executed
  study a probe built for three material laws classified 675 of 675 cases inside the class and 0 of 225 under
  an omitted delayed law, while measuring the target quantity directly was right in 900 of 900 (FACT, Appendix
  H). Where measuring the decision quantity directly costs about what probing a parameter costs, Ground
  recommends the direct measurement, and the rehearsal always contains an omitted-law control (§30.21).
- **Decision relevance.** An experiment is recommended for the decision it could change, not for the parameter
  it would sharpen. Ranking uses value of information per unit cost (§30.15). If every admissible parameter
  value implies the same design decision, the recommendation is to measure nothing.

### 30.9 Identification: actuators first [F]

**Why actuators first.** Recent sim-to-real studies agree on where the gap lives (§15). Identifying armature,
joint friction and delay gave 46% more travel distance and 75% less rotational deviation in one 2026 study
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10351); static friction is commonly missing from randomization; joint friction and
actuator delay were called particularly critical for a point-foot biped (FACT, Appendix H). Simulation agrees in
kind. In a planar threshold-crossing pilot, changing one winding feed-forward term from R·i_ref to R·i changed
24 of 156 outcome labels, and success fractions of 0.093 and 0.780 at a 100 Hz loop with no rotor inertia became
1.000 and 1.000 at 1 kHz or with rotor inertia added (FACT, Appendix H). A contact parameter fitted before the
actuator is right absorbs the actuator's error and then predicts the wrong thing on the next surface.

**Order.** (0) Clocks. (1) Actuators, joint by joint, unloaded on a stand where possible: delay and lag; static,
Coulomb and viscous friction with the Stribeck speed; armature; the scale of the torque constant; then the
torque–speed envelope and thermal constants of level L2 (§15). (2) Contact and surface: friction per material
pair under at least two normal loads, dissipation, rolling resistance. (3) Inertia and geometry, from scale and
pendulum measurements and from as-built scans (§30.14). (4) Sensors: bias, noise, latency (§20).

**Model.** Per joint at level L1,

(I(q) + J_a)·q̈ + b·q̇ + τ_f(q̇) + g(q) = N·η·k_t·i(t − d),
τ_f(q̇) = [τ_c + (τ_s − τ_c)·exp(−(q̇/v_s)²)]·sgn(q̇) while moving,

with the joint-level stiction row of §18 holding q̇ = 0 while the applied torque is inside ±τ_s. The parameter
vector is θ = (J_a, b, τ_c, τ_s, v_s, d, τ_lag, k_t scale).

**Estimator.** Three passes, owned by `frob-ground` except where a sibling is named.

- **Pass A, linear.** Away from sticking and saturation the model is linear in (I + J_a, b, τ_c, bias). A
  least-squares regressor fit on band-limited signals gives a warm start and a first residual plot.
- **Pass B, output error.** The joint, or the whole robot, is simulated with candidate θ and compared with the
  measured encoder, current and torque traces. The Gaussian update is the iterated Gauss–Newton Kalman update
  of `fs-assimilate::nonlinear::iterated`, with model Jacobians from the referee; outliers go through its
  Huber-loss fit; a shared encoder bias enters as a common-mode source through `groups`, so a thousand samples
  that share one bias do not count as a thousand measurements. Because that update admits at most 256 rows,
  traces enter as windows of summary features, and the window definition is part of the calibration record.
- **Pass C, nonsmooth parameters.** Delay is profiled, not differentiated: the other parameters are fitted at
  each delay on a grid of rational fractions of the control period (§20), and the result is the profile, its
  minimum, and the set of delays inside the 95% likelihood-ratio bound (TARGET). Static friction and the
  Stribeck speed are estimated from the ramp experiments by the ensemble lane, run as batched rollouts with
  per-world parameter variants (§17), because the stick–slip likelihood has no useful Jacobian.

**Receipt.** The calibration record holds the prior, the windows, each pass, the posterior (mean, joint
covariance, delay profile), misfit before and after, and the autocorrelation of residuals at control-rate lags.
It also holds one independent cross-check: the energy ledger of §29 evaluated on the real log. A model can fit
nominal motion for the wrong physical reason, with current draw and mechanical work disagreeing while speed
looks right. A fit whose electrical-to-mechanical energy residual exceeds the instrument bound is marked
`wrong-reason` and cannot be admitted.

### 30.10 Card updates from logs [F]

The posterior is written as a new card version. Every identified field gets provenance `measured`, the dataset
hash, the cell and its marginal uncertainty. The card also stores the joint covariance of the identified fields.
Armature and friction estimates are correlated, and randomization that samples the marginals independently is
wider than the evidence in some directions and narrower in others; correlated draws use
`fs-uq::product_copula` (FACT).

1. **Candidate, then admitted.** `ground.identify` writes a `candidate`. It becomes `admitted` only when
   `ground.score` has scored a frozen prediction made with it on sealed data and the registered interval
   covered. Randomization for training (§23) keeps the prior width until then (INV-GROUND-05).
2. **A discrepancy floor.** The posterior variance of a field is never reported below the variance that
   validation residuals attribute to model form. The floor is the common-mode term of
   `fs-assimilate::groups`, estimated on validation data. More samples shrink sampling error and leave the floor.
3. **Unit and class.** One robot calibrates one robot. A per-unit posterior describes that unit's left knee. A
   part-class card is hierarchical: a class mean and a unit-to-unit spread. With one unit measured the spread
   stays at its prior; with k units it is estimated on k − 1 degrees of freedom.

### 30.11 Frozen predictions, sealed trials, holdout retirement [F]

A real trial is preceded by a prediction record. It extends the registered predictions of §28.

```toml
# frob.ground.prediction/1 (DESIGN)
[prediction]
version = 1; cell = "…"; model = "ModelId"; cards = ["…"]; scene = "SceneId"; task = "TaskId"
conditions_draw = "philox:seed=…"       # trial conditions are drawn and recorded before the trial
world_addr      = "…"                   # the simulated side is replayable at its recorded determinism class
metrics = [ { name = "crossing_time_s", interval = [4.05, 4.42], level = 0.90 },
            { name = "peak_current_A",  interval = [4.8, 6.1],   level = 0.90 } ]
scoring   = "interval-score"
decision  = "B beats A by at least 5 points"
tolerance = "from instrument sigma and the decision margin"
study     = { type = "fixed-n", n = 20, reachable = ["promote", "inconclusive"] }
frozen_at = "ledger-seq:…"              # precedes the first read of the sealed chunk
```

- **Tolerances are registered** and are derived from measurement uncertainty and the decision margin. One
  unitless epsilon across angles, currents and temperatures is refused.
- **Scoring is proper.** The interval score is S = (u − l) + (2/α)·(l − y)₊ + (2/α)·(y − u)₊, reported with
  bias, coverage, and residual structure against condition.
- **The study type is declared** (§24): fixed-n exact with a pre-analysis of reachable verdicts, or
  anytime-valid, never both. For real trials n is small and the pre-analysis decides whether the trial is worth
  running. With zero failures in n trials, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the failure probability is
  1 − 0.05^(1/n): 13.9% at n = 20, 9.5% at n = 30, 4.95% at n = 59. A claim of "under 5%" is unreachable below
  59 failure-free trials (FACT, arithmetic).
- **A refit opens a new version.** A posterior changed after validation data was seen needs version 2 of the
  prediction and fresh sealed data. A published failure keeps its original threshold.
- **A scored holdout is spent** for that cell and model lineage. It moves to the calibration pool or the
  regression set and is never a holdout again (INV-GROUND-04). Repeated confirmation attempts spend error as in
  the qualification lane: the i-th attempt on fresh data runs at α_i = α/[i·(i+1)] (§24).
- **A reversed ranking is a result.** If the real ranking of two designs contradicts the simulated one, the
  outcome is `ranking-falsified`: the cell's domain narrows to where the ranking held, and the pair, its
  conditions and its logs become a regression case in the divergence ledger (§16).

### 30.12 Conformal bands per calibrated cell [F]

**Definition.** Inside one cell, calibration pairs (ŷ_i, y_i) are a simulated prediction and the real outcome
under the same registered conditions. The residual is r_i = |y_i − ŷ_i| for a two-sided band, or
r_i = ŷ_i − y_i for a one-sided statement "real is at least sim minus q̂" on a performance metric. The
half-width q̂ is the ⌈(n+1)·(1−α)⌉-th smallest residual, and the band is infinite when that index exceeds n.
The implementation is `fs-eproc::hardening::MondrianConformal` with the cell as the bucket and a minimum
calibration count of 19 (TARGET; the sibling's own fixture uses 19, FACT).

**What the evidence supports** (FACT, Appendix H: https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511 and arithmetic from its
coverage law).

- Marginal coverage of at least 1 − α holds for any simulator quality, provided test draws are exchangeable with
  the calibration draws. A poor simulator gives a wide band, not a wrong one.
- Conditional on the calibration set, realized coverage is distributed Beta(n + 1 − l, l) with l = ⌊(n+1)·α⌋.

| n | Target | Mean coverage | Standard deviation | P(coverage < 0.80) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.90 | 0.905 | 0.063 | 0.069 |
| 200 | 0.90 | 0.900 | 0.021 | below 0.001 |

- A finite band needs n ≥ (1 − α)/α: 9 at 90%, 19 at 95%, 99 at 99%. At n = 20 a 95% band is the largest
  residual seen, and any level above 20/21 = 95.2% is unbounded.
- **The exchangeable unit is the robot, not the episode.** Twenty episodes on one machine are one cluster. For
  the same unit in the same cell, episodes are the draws. For a new unit of the same design, units are the
  draws, so a band across units needs at least 9 calibrated units at 90% and is refused below that.
- **Paired simulation buys little hardware time.** One study reports savings of 20–25% of hardware evaluation
  effort for similar bounds, and that with low correlation between simulation and reality, real-only evaluation
  gives the tightest bounds (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04354). The ceiling of the benefit on real-trial count
  is therefore about 1.3, stated before any test. Ground reports the measured correlation in each cell and
  recommends real-only evaluation when it is below 0.5 (TARGET).
- The known bound on the coverage gap under shift is Σ w_i·d_TV(Z, Z_i)/(1 + Σ w_i)
  (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415). For an unbuilt design the distance cannot be measured, so nothing is
  issued.

**Rules.**

1. A band is issued per cell, and refused for an uncalibrated cell, for n below the minimum, for a new unit when
   too few units are calibrated, for any unbuilt design, for data pooled across cells or `ModelId`s, and for
   synthetic data mixed with real (FROB-REF-889, -846, -850).
2. A simulated metric in a calibrated cell is reported as [ŷ − q̂, ŷ + q̂] with n, the level, and the sentence
   "coverage is marginal over calibration draws; at this n realized coverage varies by about ±(one standard
   deviation from the table)".
3. A band never raises an evidence class. It is Estimated, and in a claim product (§8) the weakest input wins.
4. A scorecard with many banded metrics spends an explicit false-coverage budget: each claim reserves its share
   up front (`admission_alpha`) and violations are flagged by e-BH (`fcr_flag`).

**Counterexample: pooling hides a failing regime.** Pooled calibration at a 90% target covers 20 of 20 episodes
in one regime and 16 of 20 in a shifted one. The total is 36 of 40, nominal overall and 80% where it matters
(FACT: `fs-eproc/tests/conformal_shift_receipt.rs`). This is why the cell is the bucket.

### 30.13 Drift detection that retires a calibration [F]

| Trigger | Mechanism | Fires |
|---|---|---|
| Scope change | Controller or firmware identity changes; a part is replaced or re-lubricated; the surface class changes; a recorded condition leaves the envelope; `ModelId` changes, including a kernel change that alters bits (§21); an instrument's calibration identity changes | Immediately and deterministically |
| Rank drift | Each new residual is ranked in the frozen calibration residuals. Under exchangeability the normalized rank is uniform. `DriftMonitor` bets against that in both directions and averages the two e-processes | At e ≥ 1/α_d, α_d = 0.05 (TARGET) |
| Miss counting | A likelihood-ratio e-process on band misses, null "miss rate ≤ p₀(n)", alternative p₁ = 0.5 | At e ≥ 1/α_d |
| Expiry | Age or operating hours since calibration (OPEN-GROUND-3) | At the limit |

Two facts about power, printed by `ground.band --status` so that a quiet monitor is not misread.

- The rank monitor's slack is δ = max(1/√n, 0.02): 0.224 at n = 20 and 0.071 at n = 200 (FACT). At n = 20 it
  sees only gross shifts.
- For the miss monitor, p₀(n) is the 95th percentile of the conditional miss rate: about 0.22 at n = 20 and
  about 0.14 at n = 200 for a 90% band (FACT, arithmetic from the Beta law). At n = 20 a miss multiplies the
  evidence by 2.27 and a cover by 0.641, so four misses in a row give 26.7 and retire the calibration, and
  three give 11.7 and do not. This monitor cannot tell drift from an unlucky calibration draw. Both mean the
  band under-covers, and both call for recalibration.

On retirement the status becomes `retired(cell, reason)`, reports revert to `reality: no-claim`, and verdicts
that cited the band are marked stale through the dependency-directed revalidation of §28. The alarm ends the
regime, and calibration restarts inside the new one. The monitors are e-processes, so they are read after every
episode without inflating error (§24).

### 30.14 As-built ingestion [F]

A built part is one more representation of the design.

1. The build packet declares datums and at least three non-collinear fiducials per part at design time
   (§31.12). That is what makes registration well-posed.
2. A scan with fiducial correspondences is admitted like any dataset.
3. `fs-asbuilt::rigid3::register3` aligns scan to design in closed form. The similarity variant reports scale
   with a standard error and a unit-suspicion diagnostic: a scale near 25.4 or 0.001 is a unit error, not
   shrinkage. `estimate_calibrated_rigid3` returns a six-parameter pose covariance.
4. `field` computes signed deviation, wall thickness and form fields from supplied surface correspondences.
5. `propagate` carries pose covariance into link length, axis offset and axis parallelism with their
   cross-correlation, and downgrades a term to unknown when its sigma-point check shows the linearization is
   poor.
6. Ground converts geometric deltas into posteriors on `.fbot` parameters, and converts volume and a scale
   reading into mass, centre of mass and inertia through `fs-mbd-material`, which compiles geometry and card
   density into the existing spatial inertia (FACT). Measured mass against computed volume gives the effective
   density of a printed part.
7. The result is an as-built model variant with provenance `as-built`, hence a new `ModelId`. Per-world model
   variants (§17) hold the as-designed and as-built robots in one batch, so "does the built unit still pass?"
   is one run.
8. Each measured feature is compared with its allocated tolerance and sensitivity (§31.11).

If the registration residual exceeds the deviation under review, the signal is below the noise and no deviation
is reported (`well_posed`). The residual is a fit diagnostic, not a transform uncertainty. Because the sibling
does not search for correspondences, Ground accepts only scans with fiducials, or scans registered by an
identified external tool whose identity is recorded as provenance (FROB-REF-812 otherwise).

### 30.15 The decision model for reality calibration [F]

This is the reality-calibration decision core of §28, specified for its owner. Its deterministic fallback is
already the default state of the chapter.

- **State, per cell.** Uncalibrated; calibrated and exchangeable; calibrated and shifted.
- **Actions.** Issue a band; refuse; recommend k more real episodes; retire.
- **Loss.** A band that under-covers and is relied on costs C_false. A valid band withheld costs C_refuse. A
  real episode costs c_real, expressed in GPU-second equivalents at a declared exchange rate (OPEN-GROUND-4).
  C_false is far larger than C_refuse: reporting an artifact-dependent result as established costs more than a
  wasted episode. The alarm level follows from the loss as α_d = c_recal/C_stale, with 0.05 as the default.
- **Artifacts.** (a) The residual quantile per cell, whose calibration set is its only calibration source and
  whose conditions come from a pre-registered draw. (b) A guard table (n, level) → finite band, infinite band
  or refusal, regenerated in CI and diffed. (c) The two monitors of §30.13. (d) Rows of the experiment table
  for real batches: cost, expected value of sample information, and their ratio.
- **Buying real episodes.** With Δ ~ N(m, s²) the posterior on the score difference of two finalists and s̃² the
  variance an experiment removes, EVSI = s̃·[φ(z) − z·Φ(−z)] with z = |m|/s̃. Here s² is the sum of sampling
  variance, tier-bias variance and the reality-gap variance σ_r² of the cell.
- **Substituted (illustrative).** m = 0.03, sampling 0.03², tier bias 0.04². In an uncalibrated cell with
  σ_r = 0.10, twenty real pairs with per-pair noise 0.3 remove s̃ = 0.096 and are worth 0.0251; all remaining
  simulation can remove at most the first two terms (s̃ = 0.05) and is worth 0.0084. In a calibrated cell with
  σ_r = 0.02 the same real batch is worth 0.0035 and the simulation 0.0084.
- **Intuition.** Calibration does not make simulation faster. It changes which experiment is worth buying.
- **Obligations.** Coverage at least 1 − α for exchangeable draws. Type-I error of each monitor at most α_d
  under optional stopping. The planner never issues a verdict, so a wrong variance model costs efficiency and
  never validity. The planner chooses how many real episodes, never which conditions: assigned conditions equal
  the registered draw, and that equality is the interference test.
- **Failure modes.** Survivor-only calibration; starved cells; conditions chosen by the operator; drift.
- **Deterministic fallback.** `reality: no-claim`. Triggers: fewer than 19 calibration points; a monitor at or
  above 20; an audited miss rate above its bound; any scope change.
- **Budgeted mode.** At most 30 real episodes are recommended per decision (TARGET); beyond that the answer is
  "inconclusive, no-claim".
- **Timescale.** Once per real batch. Kernel builds and effort profiles are constants to it; per-episode
  promotion treats the band as a constant.

### 30.18 The replication kit [F]

A kit lets a third party rebuild a result without access to this machine or to anything private.

- **Contents.** The design, cards, scene pack, task and prediction records; the dataset if its licence permits,
  otherwise its hash and the acquisition protocol; the bench protocol (rig description, excitation plan as data,
  instrument list); the analysis configuration; expected outputs with tolerances and determinism classes; any
  cited `.frobcert`; the no-authorization flags of §12; and the synthetic rehearsal, so the known answers run
  with no hardware.
- **Nothing private.** The builder refuses any input that carries or inherits the private mark of §37, and any
  asset whose licence forbids redistribution (FROB-REF-899). There is no override flag.
- **Self-test.** A kit passes when a clean machine reproduces its numbers: bit-for-bit on the same backend
  (class D2), inside the recorded envelope otherwise (class D3).
- **Losing results too.** A kit is built for a falsified prediction as readily as for a confirmed one.

### 30.24 Exposure delay and mount translation on a straight pass

**What it is.** [S] A ninth structural alias beside the eight of §30.8, kept as its own fixture so that the count of
GATE-GROUND-03 is unchanged. A synchronized clock does not separate a sensor's exposure delay from a translation of
its mount along the direction of travel. Owner: `frob-ground`. Verb: `ground.excite`.

**The alias.** A range sensor with fixed orientation approaches a wall at coordinate L along a straight line at speed
v. With an exposure delay δ and a forward mount offset b its reading at time t is

    L − v·(t + δ) − b.

Only v·δ + b appears. At +2 m/s a delay of 20 ms and an offset of 40 mm give the same 101 samples of a one-second pass
at 100 Hz.

**What separates them.** DESIGN. `ground.excite` flags the pair when every planned pass has the same velocity along
the sensor's axis, and proposes, in order of the separation they give:

- the same pass reversed without rotating the robot: at −2 m/s the two explanations differ by 80 mm;
- a second speed: at +1 m/s they differ by 20 mm;
- an independent exposure mark on the shared hardware line of §30.7, which measures δ directly.

A fit requested on data with one axial velocity returns the identified combination v·δ + b and updates neither the
card's delay nor the mount transform. This is the spatial counterpart of the delay-against-offset alias of §30.7, and
the same alias binds the extrinsic graph of §20.14 when a faulty edge is a time offset on one pair.

### 31.1 What Foundry owns

so in machine-readable flags (INV-FOUNDRY-01).

### 31.2 An output-quality subsystem, not a speed lever

Foundry makes the answer better. It does not make the answer arrive sooner, and no part of the ten-fold thesis
of §2 rests on it. The numbers (FACT, Appendix H; each to be re-read from its primary source before a gate cites
it):

- **Savings against engineered parts are roughly 7–30% of link mass.** Reported cases: an industrial forearm 7%,
  an exoskeleton arm 10.4%, a hydraulic leg unit 24.5%, and two links of a table-tennis arm 25% and 29%
  (2.27 → 1.70 kg and 0.84 → 0.60 kg; https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26643v1). That last study optimized under
  natural-frequency constraints only, evaluated stiffness, stress and fatigue afterwards, and excluded two links
  as already near their limits. Against primitive shapes the saving is larger and means nothing, because nobody
  ships a solid capsule.
- **Link structure is a small share of a robot.** In one 15 kg quadruped the four legs weigh about 4 kg (27%) and
  the twelve actuators about 6 kg (40%) (https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/15203.pdf). Seven percent off
  the whole leg mass is about 2% of the robot, and thirty percent about 8%, before shafts, gears and bearings
  inside the leg are excluded. The expectation is 2 to 8% of robot mass on this one quadruped, the only one with a
  published mass breakdown; there is no figure for humanoids.
- **It lengthens the loop.** Each design gains finite-element solves per link, and three-dimensional
  optimization of 12 to 20 links is CPU-hours. Nothing here shortens a decision.
- **It is established practice.** Feeding multibody loads into topology optimization is routine in commercial
  tools, and the cited arm study used one. No novelty is claimed.
- **Link fatigue is a safeguard, not an observed failure mode.** Published fatigue analyses of legged robots
  concern gears; a bounded search found no report of a link failing by fatigue. The check exists because an
  optimizer that removes material can create the problem.

**Ceiling, stated before any test.** With link structure a fraction f_L of robot mass and a saving s, the direct
ceiling is f_L·s ≤ 0.27 × 0.30 ≈ 0.08. The mass spiral (worked card GB-03, loop gain κ) multiplies a structural
saving by 1/(1 − κ) only if actuators are then re-selected from a discrete catalogue, which is a separate
decision with its own revalidation (HYPOTHESIS; the loop of §31.9 measures the realized multiplier).

Consequence (ADR-FOUNDRY-01): Foundry's topology rung, finite-element checks and build packet run on finalists, after
qualification (§24). Its beam rung also runs inside screening, as a validity check on declared link walls (§31.23).

### 31.6 Load cases extracted from telemetry [F]

**Source.** Chronicle's T2 traces (§29) hold joint constraint wrenches, contact wrenches and body twists at
control rate. A *load campaign* is the qualified task set plus push-recovery and drop tasks, run with T2 on every
world at fixed world addresses, so the campaign is reproducible and is paired across loop iterations.

**The load on a link at one instant** is the wrench at each port (joint interfaces and, for a foot or wheel, the
contact patch) in the link frame, together with the rigid-body acceleration field
a(x) = a₀ + α × x + ω × (ω × x), with gravity folded into a₀. The free-body check is that port wrenches and the
body load −ρ·a(x) sum to zero in force and moment. Its residual is recorded per sample; a sample above tolerance
is dropped and counted, and more than 0.1% dropped (TARGET) refuses the campaign (FROB-REF-167).

**Unit loads.** The structural model is linear, so stress anywhere is linear in the load coordinates: six per
port except the port used as a statically determinate 3-2-1 restraint, plus twelve for the body field (three for
a₀, three for α, six for the products ω_i·ω_j). A two-port link has 18 coordinates and a three-port link 24
(FACT, arithmetic). One solve per coordinate gives a stress influence matrix, and the stress history at any
gauge over a whole campaign is then a matrix product.

**Envelope cases** drive optimization. For a fixed, deterministic set of directions in scaled load space, the
case is the sample that maximizes the projection; every case is a real instant with all ports and the body field
taken from the same sample. **Spectra** drive fatigue: signed stress histories at gauges, in episode order.

Rules:

1. **Time-coherent cases only** (ADR-FOUNDRY-03). Counterexample: a simply supported beam of span L carries P at
   L/3 at one instant and P at 2L/3 at another. The mid-span moment is P·L/6 each time. Assembling both peaks
   into one case gives P·L/3, a twofold over-design. If the second load has the opposite sign, the assembled
   case gives zero while each real case gives ±P·L/6: the assembled case is unsafe.
2. **Symmetric limbs are unioned, not assumed equal.** Each limb's history is transformed into the canonical
   limb frame, with forces reflecting as polar vectors and moments as axial ones under a mirror (§10, C-09),
   and the cases are unioned. A payload offset sideways breaks equal loading.
3. **Impact loads carry a model-form label.** Peak contact forces depend on contact stiffness. The campaign is
   re-extracted with stiffness halved and doubled and under the alternative contact law (§18). The envelope
   over variants is the load set, and a peak that moves by more than 20% (TARGET) marks the case
   `model-conditional`, a label the structural margin inherits.
4. **Quasi-static superposition needs separation of frequencies.** It is used only when the link's first
   natural frequency is at least five times the highest frequency carrying 99% of load power (TARGET; the
   dynamic amplification 1/(1 − (f/f₁)²) is then at most 1.042). Otherwise impact windows go through the
   transient path of `fs-solid` (FROB-REF-262 if that is not enabled).
5. **A port wrench becomes tractions by a declared interface pattern** on the port boss: a traction field
   affine in position whose resultant force and moment equal the wrench exactly. Stress within one boss
   diameter of an interface is `no-claim`; bolted joints and press fits are outside this chapter.
6. **Missing samples refuse the metric** (FROB-REF-169). Nothing is interpolated across a telemetry gap.

### 31.7 Sizing and topology optimization of links [F]

**Beam rung.** Each link is idealized between its ports as a thin-walled tube or a rectangular section. For a
tube of mean radius r and wall t: area 2π·r·t, second moment π·r³·t, section modulus π·r²·t, torsion constant
2π·r³·t; stress σ = N/A ± M/Z and τ = T/(2π·r²·t); the check is √(σ² + 3·τ²) ≤ S_y/SF, Euler buckling
P_cr = π²·E·I/(K·L)², local wall buckling with a card knock-down, and a first bending frequency from the beam
formula with end masses. Sizing picks the lightest stock size that passes every envelope case and the fatigue
utilization at declared notches. Comparisons with rational inputs run in exact arithmetic (class D0). This rung
answers A-15, is the engineered baseline, and is the fallback of KILL-TOPOPT.

**Topology rung, finalists only.** The design region is the keep-in envelope, minus the volumes swept by
neighbouring bodies over the joint ranges (the swept charts of A-13), minus actuator, bearing and cable
keep-outs, with port bosses frozen solid. The formulation minimizes projected volume subject to a stress cap
S_y/SF on the relaxed measure (`sdf3::design`), a stiffness cap per envelope case from the control budget, a
first-mode floor above actuator bandwidth (A-12; `sdf3::modal`), a minimum length scale (`robust`), and the
process constraints of §31.10. Where a sibling driver takes compliance at fixed volume (`multi_load`), an outer
bisection on volume fraction finds the lightest feasible design. Plate parts use `fs-topols` for shape in two
dimensions and are checked in three. Mirror-image links are one problem (`fs-geocon::QuotientChart`).

**Extraction.** The surface is the level ½ of the projected density. Smoothing may change volume by at most 1%
(TARGET) and is always followed by the independent check.

**Budgets (TARGET, unmeasured).** At most 120 s per unique link class for the coarse solve and 10 minutes for a
finalist solve on the CPU cores of the M5 Ultra. A symmetric quadruped has three to five classes.

### 31.8 Stress, buckling, modes and cumulative fatigue damage [F]

The check runs on a body-fitted tetrahedral mesh of the extracted surface (`fs-mesh`), a different
discretization from the optimizer's cut cells. `fs-topopt` depends on `fs-solid`, so the independence is of mesh
and formulation, not of codebase; the cantilever and two-bar known answers anchor both paths.

1. **Stress.** Every envelope case, plus the instants that govern each gauge under the influence matrix of the
   final geometry. Utilization is u = σ_vm/(S_y/SF) per element. The mesh is refined until the peak moves by
   under 5% (TARGET). A peak at a re-entrant corner that does not converge is reported as
   `singular: fillet required`, with the radius as the repair.
2. **Buckling.** The first positive load factor from `stability` is at least 2 (TARGET) in every envelope case.
3. **Modes.** The first frequency clears its floor.
4. **Cumulative fatigue damage.** Gauges sit at the 16 highest-stress locations per unit load (TARGET) and at
   declared notches. Signed stress on each gauge plane feeds `RainflowMiner` across the campaign. With damage D
   accumulated over H_c campaign hours, utilization for a service duration H is U_f = (D/H_c)·H against a limit
   of 1 divided by the card's scatter factor.
   - Signed stress only. Unsigned von Mises is never fed to a cycle counter.
   - Residues are carried between episodes and `finish` is called once (see the window-split case in §31.18).
   - The mean-stress policy is named in the result.
   - The curve must be valid for the material state, the process and the surface finish. Otherwise the result
     is `fatigue: no-claim` (FROB-REF-163).
   - Cycles that span tasks (a loaded leg, then an unloaded one) are counted, because the campaign's duty
     sequence is part of the history.
5. **Class.** Every number here is Estimated. A structural margin enters a claim product as its weakest input.

### 31.9 Inertia fed back into the model, and the convergence test [F]

The checked geometry and its material card go through `fs-mbd-material`, and the resulting mass, centre of mass
and inertia replace the link's primitive Region by a typed edit (§14). That is a new `DesignId`. The baseline
controller is re-synthesized (§22), the campaign re-runs on the same world addresses, and loads are re-extracted.

**Why it can fail to converge.** Structural mass of a link sized to a stress cap grows with the loads, and the
loads grow with total mass. In the linear model, link mass S and other mass M₀ satisfy S = A + λ·(M₀ + S), with
fixed point S* = (A + λ·M₀)/(1 − λ) and error contracting by λ per iteration. The actuator spiral of GB-03 has
the same form with gain κ. If both run in one loop the gain is about κ + λ: at κ = 0.59 and λ = 0.15 it is 0.74,
and five iterations contract the first step only to 0.74⁴ = 0.30 of itself (FACT, arithmetic). That is the reason
for ADR-FOUNDRY-07.

**Test.** Let x_k collect link masses and principal inertias after iteration k, and Δ_k = x_k − x_{k−1}.

- Estimated contraction: λ̂_k = ‖Δ_k‖/‖Δ_{k−1}‖, reported every iteration.
- Converged when the relative change is at most 2% for two successive iterations, the a-posteriori bound
  λ̂/(1 − λ̂)·‖Δ_k‖ is at most 2% of ‖x_k‖, the load envelope moved by at most 5%, and the final geometry passes
  §31.8 under the loads it itself generates (all TARGET). The budget is five iterations; Aitken extrapolation
  x_k − Δ_k²/(Δ_k − Δ_{k−1}), applied componentwise, may propose the next iterate, which is then evaluated like
  any other.
- Diverged when λ̂ ≥ 1 on two successive iterations: refused (FROB-REF-260) with the beam rung as the repair.
- Cycling when Δ alternates in sign with λ̂ near 1, which a discrete stock size produces: one under-relaxed step
  x ← x + ½·(G(x) − x) is tried, then refusal.

The map G is deterministic only because the campaign reuses its world addresses (§35). With fresh random worlds
each iteration, sampling noise sets a floor above 2% and the test cannot pass.

Each made part declares a process: `cnc-3axis`, `cnc-5axis`, `sheet`, `fdm`, `sls` or `slm`. `fs-fab` holds the

|---|---|---|---|

| Internal corner radius | Curvature of the distance field (ours) | `fs-fab::cnc_tool_radius` | Milling |

### 31.11 Tolerance allocation driven by sensitivities [F]

**Quantities of interest.** Foot or tool position error, joint-axis offset and parallelism, closure error of a
loop, backlash, minimum clearance, wheel radius as an odometry scale. **Sensitivities.** For kinematic
quantities they are exact derivatives of the product-of-exponentials map with respect to geometric parameters
(§13), class Certified when evaluated in exact or interval arithmetic. For simulated task metrics they come from
A-16 (§28), class Estimated. The allocation inherits the weaker class.

1. `variance_budget(spec_margin, target)` turns the specification into a variance budget.
2. `allocate(features, budget, k)` returns t_i ∝ (c_i/s_i²)^(1/3), normalized to meet the budget.
3. Features that share a setup or a datum go through `propagate_correlated_stack` or `allocate_grouped`.
4. `robustness_check` evaluates the true quantity at band extremes. Features that fail fall back to worst-case
   boxes by interval evaluation (`fs-uq::product_epistemic`), which is also the fallback of KILL-TOLERANCE.
5. Clearances and backlash, which switch modes, go through `structured_propagation`.
6. `gdt_report` enters the packet. It is a list of suggested tolerances with their justification, not a
   drawing annotation: datum frames are declared by Foundry in the packet (§31.12).
7. The as-built audit of §30.14 compares each measured feature with its allocation.

**Worked case.** Two features with sensitivities 1 and 4, equal cost coefficients, k = 3, budget √B = 10 µm.
The allocation is 16.0 µm and 6.35 µm. Equal tolerances meeting the same budget are 7.28 µm each, and the
allocation costs 19.9% less under the 1/t cost model (FACT, arithmetic).

**Counterexample: a zero first-order sensitivity is not insensitivity.** For q(x) = x² at nominal zero the
sensitivity is zero and a first-order allocator would loosen without limit, while the deviation at tolerance t
is t². The sibling requires positive sensitivities; Foundry refuses the feature (FROB-REF-165) and routes it to
the band-extreme check.

### 31.12 Bounded export and the build packet `.fbuild` [F]

**Geometry export.** The extracted surface passes repair and validity checks (watertight, manifold, oriented)
through the quarantine path of `fs-io`, then is written as STL, 3MF and GLB. STEP is produced without an
upstream change: Foundry builds a syntax-level `StepDocument` of the faceted-solid entity chain, writes it with
`write_step`, parses and decodes it with `decode_faceted_brep_with_limits`, and re-emits it with
`export_decoded_faceted_brep`, which refuses unless every coordinate bit and triangle index replays. **Bound.**
The default limit of 10⁶ instances admits about 285,000 triangles for a closed mesh, at three instances per
triangle, about half a point per triangle, and two more (FACT, arithmetic). A larger part is decimated under a
stated Hausdorff tolerance and re-checked, or refused (FROB-REF-824). The file has no curved surfaces and no
units entity; the manifest states metres.

**The packet** is one directory written atomically and addressed by the hash of its manifest (FMT-FBUILD,
schema `frob.build/1`).

### 31.13 Firmware export [M]

**Scope.** Joint-level controllers and monitors only: a proportional–derivative or proportional–integral loop,
the energy-budget monitor of §22, a shield projection with a few rows. Whole-body controllers and state
estimators in fixed point are out of scope. **Output.** Source for a `no_std`, heap-free crate that forbids
unsafe code and sits behind a caller-owned bus trait; Q-format declarations; a test-vector manifest; overflow
certificates; quantization error bounds; the flags. The exporter emits no register access, no bus driver and no
stop-circuit code.

**Checks, in order.**

1. **Range analysis.** Declared input ranges are propagated through the kernel IR (§21) by interval arithmetic,
   fixing a Q format for every intermediate.
2. **Bit-vector queries.** Where intervals are too coarse (correlated terms such as a·x − a·y), the question
   "is there an input in range for which this intermediate overflows?" is bit-blasted and decided by
   `fln-verdict::bv_decide`. An unsatisfiable answer with a replayed proof stream is an instance certificate,
   class Certified. It is not Proved, because the proof-term step is not built upstream (§31.4). This is
   theorem card T-14 of §12.
3. **State.** Integrators and filters need an inductive range invariant: one query for the base case and one for
   the step.
4. **Quantization.** The difference from the real-valued controller is bounded by interval arithmetic, and its
   closed-loop effect is measured by running the fixed-point controller in the loop in the exact-integer kernel
   class of §35, so the simulated controller computes the same integers on every backend. Class Tested.
5. **Vectors.** 10⁶ vectors agree bit for bit between the exported crate built for the host and the integer
   kernel (TARGET). That is a test, and it is not called equivalence.
6. **Not claimed.** Execution time, stack depth and behaviour on a board. `timing: no-claim` until a receipt
   from a linked binary on real hardware is imported.

Counterexamples that seed the registry: in Q15, (−1)·(−1) is 0x8000·0x8000 = 0x40000000, which shifts to 0x8000
and reads as −1; the negation of −32768 overflows; an integrator adding 400 counts per tick from zero overflows
sixteen bits at tick 82, and without a clamp the one-step query returns the state 32,400 at once.

**Ceiling.** No speed claim. The benefit is that the controller evaluated in simulation is, integer for integer,
the arithmetic that would be shipped.

### 31.14 Lifecycle compression [M]

**The question it answers.** Whether the design that passes today still passes after months of its duty cycle;
which component reaches its limit first, and when; what maintenance interval that implies; and what a lighter
link costs in service duration. Outputs: time to first threshold per component with an interval, aged model
variants at stated ages for Arena and Nemesis (§24, §26) as per-world variants in one batch (§17), and a front
of mass against service duration. Every output is Estimated.

**Method.** The state splits into fast x and slow z with ẋ = f(x, z, u) and ż = ε·g(x, z). At 500 Hz, 1,000
hours is 1.8 × 10⁹ steps (FACT, arithmetic). Two-timescale averaging advances z with macro steps and estimates
the averaged rate ⟨g⟩ from short micro windows at fixed z: a settling phase with a periodicity residual, then
about twenty gait cycles of averaging (TARGET), or for a mixed duty an ergodic sample of the mission profile
with its confidence interval. Macro steps use step doubling for error control.

| Slow state | Law (empirical; coefficients from cards) | Feeds back into |
|---|---|---|
| Backlash β per joint | Archard volume k·F_n·s/H mapped to recession by a card geometry factor | The backlash rows of §18 |
| Fatigue damage per gauge | Rainflow with Miner's sum, residues carried across windows; cycles between duty modes counted at the macro level | A threshold only |
| Winding insulation | An Arrhenius rate in winding temperature | A threshold only |
| Cell capacity and resistance | Calendar fade in √t plus fade per unit of charge throughput | Bus voltage sag in the actuator model of §15 |

**Return to full resolution** when a Floquet multiplier of the gait orbit (§22) approaches the unit circle, when
the macro error estimate fails, or when the slow rate is no longer small. **Rare overloads** are invisible to
short windows: their damage enters as rate times damage per event, with rates from Nemesis (§26).

**Counterexample: the law of the average is not the average of the law.** With the common halving of insulation
life per 10 K, a duty that spends half its time at T_ref and half at T_ref + 20 K consumes life at 2.5/τ_ref.
Evaluating the law at the mean temperature gives 2.0/τ_ref and overstates service duration by 25%. Micro
windows therefore average the rate, never the input.

**Ceiling.** A run of 100 macro steps with three micro evaluations each, of 30 gait cycles at 0.5 s, simulates
2.25 × 10⁶ steps for 1,000 hours, a ratio near 800 in simulated steps (DESIGN, illustrative). It is a count of
steps, not of wall time and not of field experience. Accelerated estimates can also change the failure
mechanism; each law carries a validity domain and refuses outside it (FROB-REF-831).

### 31.21 Packet review as an imported record

This subsection extends §31.12 ("Review is an import lane") and keeps ADR-FOUNDRY-11: no convergence gate depends on a
reviewer. It gives the import lane a protocol and a record format, so that an imported review means the same thing for
every packet, and it separates what a program can check from what only a person can. Tier [S]. Owner: `frob-foundry`.
Verb: `foundry.check`, which runs the mechanical pre-review and, with `--review-record <file>`, imports a record.

**Hidden briefs.** DESIGN: a set of 12 written briefs (TARGET), authored by someone who has not seen the development
fixtures of the packet pipeline. Each states a mission, an approved parts catalogue, a budget, the interfaces and the
deliverables. The digest of the set is committed before the pipeline version under test is frozen, and the briefs are
opened only to the frozen pipeline. A brief is used once and then retired to the development corpus, by the rule that
governs confirmation clusters in §24.13.

**Unattended production.** Each brief runs from mission query to design, qualification verdict and packet with no
human edit in between; attempts and refusals stay in the record. A brief the pipeline cannot satisfy must end in a
typed refusal that names the binding constraint. A correct refusal is a valid outcome and is reviewed as one.

**Mechanical pre-review** (`foundry.check <packet>`), each item pass or fail with the offending file:

| Item | Check |
|---|---|
| M1 | Schema, every digest, the four flags present and false (§31.12) |
| M2 | Cross-export identity: mass, centre of mass and inertia agree within 10⁻⁶ relative (TARGET) among the model the evidence used, `mass.json`, the values recomputed from each of the four geometry exports, and the URDF and MJCF exports of §14.12, with units checked |
| M3 | Referential integrity: every joint has an actuator card, every net has two ends, every made part has its geometry files, every purchased part resolves to a card |
| M4 | Every number carries a claim class and an evidence reference that resolves in the artifact graph (§29.13) |
| M5 | The cited qualification verdict is for this `DesignId` and `ModelId` |
| M6 | Every snapped part was revalidated after the snap |

**The review record** (schema `frob.review/1`), a sidecar addressed by the packet's manifest digest:

```json
{ "schema": "frob.review/1", "packet": "manifest digest", "brief": "brief digest or null",
  "reviewer": { "independent": true, "role": "mechanical designer" },
  "rubric": { "R1_producibility": "pass", "R2_assembly_and_tool_access": "fail",
              "R3_harness_and_service": "pass", "R4_quotable_from_packet_alone": "pass",
              "R5_evidence_legible_and_not_overstated": "pass" },
  "notes": [{ "item": "R2", "part": "bracket-b", "text": "second fastener is enclosed after step 3" }],
  "outcome": "accept-with-changes", "minutes": 55, "date": "YYYY-MM-DD" }
```

`independent` means the reviewer took no part in developing the pipeline or the design. Importing a record changes no
byte of the packet; the manifest view then shows `review` as the record's outcome with the record digest in place of
`no-claim`. A record whose packet digest does not match is refused.

**What is gated and what is recorded.** The gate below covers the mechanical half only. The human half is a recorded
count with a TARGET: at least 11 of the 12 packets receive `accept` or `accept-with-changes`. The count, the reviewer
minutes and the list of rubric failures are printed in the release rubric (§47). While no record exists the count
reads `no-claim`, and no text may describe a packet as reviewed.

**Counterexample: a mechanical pass is not a review.** A two-part bracket whose second fastener sits behind the first
part once it is mounted. Each part passes the tool-access check of §31.10 as a part, the packet passes M1 to M6, and
the assembly cannot be completed in the listed order. Rubric item R2 exists for this case, which is why a mechanical
pass is never rendered as `review: accept`.

**Known answers.** The tube of §31.18 (0.0950 kg) is reproduced from each geometry export within 10⁻⁶. A surface file
written in millimetres and read as metres has 10⁹ times the volume; M2 reports the ratio and names the file. A packet
that cites a verdict for the pre-snap design fails M5.

**Ceiling, stated before any time comparison.** No time claim is made. FACT: no reference packet has been timed by
this project, so a figure such as hours for a scripted reference against minutes for the pipeline would be
illustrative. Time from brief to accepted packet is measured only inside the protocol of §39, in a stratum that
includes packets, with reviewer minutes inside the clock.

**Gate.** GATE-FOUNDRY-13 (Known answer, M5). On the 12 hidden briefs every outcome is a packet that passes M1 to M6
or a typed refusal naming a binding constraint (12 of 12); no cross-export difference exceeds 10⁻⁶; the planted unit
error and the planted stale verdict are each caught in 100 of 100 runs; importing a record leaves the packet digest
unchanged; a record with a wrong packet digest is refused. The fail-closed default without a reviewer is
`review: no-claim`, and KILL-PACKET (§31.19) is unchanged.

### 31.22 Referee re-extraction of load cases that enter a build packet

**Load cases are promoted like any other result [S].** This adds a seventh rule to §31.6. A load campaign runs on a
fast tier, because it needs every world traced and many episodes. Peak contact and joint wrenches are exactly the
quantities a compliant fast-tier contact gets wrong, and rule 3 of §31.6 bounds only the part of the error that moves
with contact stiffness. A load case is therefore a fast-tier result under the trust ladder of §16.8, and it is
`provisional` until promoted.

7. **Envelope cases are re-extracted on the referee before they enter a packet.** For each envelope case, the world
   and instant that produced it are replayed on the referee from the nearest snapshot (the teleport of §29.8) over a
   window of 0.5 s on either side (DESIGN), and the port wrenches and body field are read again at the instant of
   the referee's own maximum projection inside the window. The case then carries both values, the tier pair and one
   of three labels: `established` when the two agree inside the fidelity envelope for wrenches, with the referee's
   value used; `tier-sensitive` when they do not, in which case the referee's value replaces the fast one and the
   direction is searched again among the neighbouring fast-tier samples; `provisional: referee-unavailable` when the
   referee cannot complete the scenario (§29.11), in which case the `strict` profile of `batch-cpu` adjudicates and
   the label names it.

A build-packet request that contains a `provisional` envelope case is refused; an exploratory structure run may use
provisional cases and is labelled, as §31.3 already requires. Fatigue spectra are checked the same way on a keyed
sample of windows: the ratio of referee to fast-tier stress range at each gauge is recorded, and a spectrum whose
ratio leaves the band declared in the packet is rescaled to the referee's range before damage is summed.

*Why a small tier bias matters here.* Cumulative damage scales as stress range to the power m of the material card's
S-N law. A load that is 10% high gives 1.1⁵ = 1.61 times the damage at m = 5 and 1.1¹⁰ = 2.59 times at m = 10. An
error that would be negligible in a task score changes a predicted life by a factor of two.

*Cost.* One referee window per envelope case: a two-port link has 18 load coordinates (§31.6), so a direction set of
2 × 18 axis directions gives at most 36 windows of 1 s per link class, and three to five classes on a symmetric
quadruped give 108 to 180 referee-seconds of simulated time per iteration of the structure loop (DESIGN arithmetic).

*Owner and verb.* `frob-foundry`; `foundry.loads` returns each case with both values, its label and the replay
address. No new kill gate: the rule is a use of promotion, and the fallback when the referee and `strict` are both
unavailable is the existing one, no packet.

### 31.23 Member sizing as a validity check in screening

**Member sizing inside screening [S].** This amends ADR-FOUNDRY-01. The topology rung, the finite-element checks
and the build packet stay with qualified finalists. The beam rung of §31.7 costs milliseconds and also runs
earlier, as a validity check on what a design declares. Without it a screening campaign rewards links that could
not be built: a limb segment whose shell is thinner than any section that survives its own loads has less distal
inertia, lower swing torque and a better score than its buildable version would have.

*The check.* For each link whose fill is a shell or a lattice (§14.5), `foundry.size` computes the lightest stock
section between the link's ports that passes the criteria of §31.7 under a load set. It compares that section's
mass m_min and its inertia about the proximal joint with the values the design's fill produces.

- *Before any rollout*, at stage 6 of the preflight beside the actuator checks: a bounded load set from statics.
  Each foot, wheel or end-effector port carries the robot's weight times a class load factor (DESIGN defaults on
  the archetype card: 3 for legged feet, 2 for wheels, 1.5 for manipulator ports with the rated payload added),
  in the worst direction the port admits. The finding is `under-built`, with the ratio m_min over declared mass.
- *After the first rollouts*: the same check under the peak port wrenches of T1 telemetry, taken time-coherently
  as in §31.6. A design that is `under-built` under measured loads keeps its scores with the label
  `structure-unchecked`. It cannot be promoted (§16.8) until it is re-run with the sized section, or until a typed
  edit changes the declared one.

A link may ask to be sized instead of declaring a wall: the fill `{shell: sized}` takes the thinnest stock wall
that passes, and the model's mass properties are set from it before the first rollout (DESIGN; the default for
archetype templates if the gate below supports it).

*Known answer.* A cantilevered tube 0.3 m long carries 600 N across its tip, about three times the weight of a
20 kg robot. The root moment is 180 N·m. In an aluminium alloy with S_y = 276 MPa and a safety factor of 2 the
allowable stress is 138 MPa. At a mean radius of 15 mm the thin-wall section modulus π·r²·t requires
t ≥ 180/(138 × 10⁶ × π × 0.015²) = 1.85 mm. At 2,700 kg/m³ the mass 2π·r·t·L·ρ is 0.141 kg, and the inertia about
the root, as a uniform rod, is m·L²/3 = 4.2 × 10⁻³ kg·m². A design that declares this segment at 0.05 kg is
under-built by a factor of 2.8, and its swing inertia is understated by the same factor.

*What it is not.* It is a floor on mass, not a design: no fatigue, no joints, no local buckling beyond the card's
knock-down, and impact enters only through the load factor until telemetry exists. It does not make structure
part of the decision-time thesis (§31.2). It removes one way for screening to mislead.

*The number that decides whether it matters.* HYPOTHESIS: enforcing sized members changes the winning actuator
card or gear ratio in a non-trivial share of campaigns. The test: 10 quadruped and 5 mobile-manipulator campaigns
from the design corpus, each run with declared walls and with sized members, reporting how often the winner or its
actuator selection changes.

*Owner, verb and refusal.* `frob-foundry` sizes; `frob-analyze` calls it at stage 6; `frob-arena` applies the
`structure-unchecked` label. The verb is the existing `foundry.size`, called by `design.check`. Promotion of a
design with a link that is `under-built` under measured loads is refused, with the link, the ratio and the sized
section as a typed repair.
