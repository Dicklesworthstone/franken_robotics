## 33. Suite consumption

This chapter fixes what FrankenRobotics takes from its sibling projects, in which form, behind which boundary,
what happens when a sibling does not hold still, and how this program changes a sibling where the sibling is what
has to change (§33.8). Every statement about sibling code below was read on 2026-11-21
at the heads listed in §33.13 and is a FACT with its path. Every statement about how this project behaves is a
DESIGN. A sibling fact goes stale when the sibling moves, so a fact that a gate depends on is re-read by that
gate's admission test and is never copied from this page into a record.

### 33.1 Postures

Five postures, the vocabulary FrankenMining established (FACT: Mining §27):

| Posture | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Consume** | A Cargo dependency at an exact pin, default features off |
| **Subprocess** | A binary or CLI we invoke; no crate in our build graph |
| **Lift** | Copied source with a provenance header, a row in `registries/lifts.toml` and a golden test; re-owned here |
| **Oracle** | Development and test only; never in a production graph |
| **Pattern** | We copy the design, not the code |

Each foundation has an `FD-` row with its posture, its pin, the crates allowed to touch it, an admission gate and
a fallback, because no milestone may be blocked by a sibling that has not converged. A posture belongs to a use,
not to a repository: one sibling may be consumed for one purpose and be an oracle for another, and the ledger
(§33.4) says which. Two refinements follow from the facts in this chapter. A consumed FrankenSim crate may sit
behind a Cargo feature that is off by default, so that its closure is compiled only by the milestone that needs
it (§33.9). And the Subprocess posture is the first fallback for FrankenSim itself (§33.14).

### 33.2 The FrankenSim decision

**ADR-SUITE-01: consume FrankenSim by path dependency, confined behind the airlock crate `frob-fsim`, pinned by
commit in `franken-stack.lock`.**

The owner's brief is to use FrankenSim for the physics to the greatest extent possible. The referee tier needs
`fs-mbd` and `fs-contact` whole, together with geometry, integrators, optimizers and the ledger. Those two crates
are about 438 thousand lines between them with their tests (FACT: 291,848 and 146,603 lines of Rust under
`frankensim/crates/fs-mbd` and `frankensim/crates/fs-contact`). Copying that would be a fork.

What is consumed and what is owned must be stated plainly. FrankenSim is the referee and the source of geometry
kernels, structure-preserving integrators, optimizers, statistics and the design ledger. The batched engine is
FrankenRobotics code: the kernel library, the fixed-step convex compliant solve, the model-variant tables, the GPU
backends and the gym (§16, §17, §21, §23). FrankenSim's `fs-control` crate covers sampled motor current loops
only (FACT: `frankensim/crates/fs-control/Cargo.toml`, description). Four further limits are reported and are
re-read at INT-FSIM-03 before any gate cites them: FrankenSim has no batched environments, `fs-mbd` is plain
`f64` and not generic over a differentiation scalar, it emits no articulated adjoints, and it steps over contact
sets the caller supplies.

The two sibling products chose otherwise, and their reasons are facts this plan must answer. FrankenMining
rejected a Cargo dependency on FrankenSim because it would link two asupersync versions, needs a different
nightly for one crate's const-generic feature, failed `cargo check` on fifteen targets at the time, and uses
out-of-repo relative paths. Its stack lock records the result as `posture = "lift"` (FACT:
`franken_mining/franken-stack.lock`, row `FD-FSIM-00`). FrankenTextiles lifts files one by one. Why the decision
differs here:

1. **The need is different in kind.** Those products took numerics files. This one needs the multibody and
   contact engines.
2. **The mechanism has precedents, and their limit is known.** Five crates sit outside FrankenSim's workspace with
   their own `[workspace]` table and consume `fs-*` crates by path: `apps/instrument-lab/render-gpu`,
   `apps/instrument-lab/render-gpu-piano`, `apps/pocket-watch/engine-watch`, `crates/fs-cmaes-viz-wasm` and
   `crates/fs-flyer-wasm` (FACT: their `Cargo.toml` files). All five live inside FrankenSim's directory tree, so
   they inherit its `.cargo/config.toml`. `crates/fs-g1-train` is also excluded from the workspace, but it
   depends on no `fs-*` crate (FACT: its only dependency is `libm`), so it is not a precedent. One project
   outside the tree consumes four crates by path (FACT:
   `jazz_chord_progression_editor_html/tools/piano-v2-soundboard-pack/Cargo.toml`), and it carries no patch
   entry, no toolchain file and no build-identity setting, so it is not evidence that an out-of-tree build passes
   today. The precedents establish the mechanism. INT-FSIM-01 is the first measurement of the out-of-tree case.
3. **Each objection has a mitigation that is a gate.** The whole build graph, FrankenSim's crates included,
   builds on one toolchain T (INT-TOOLCHAIN-01, §33.13). The workspace root repeats three things that Cargo does
   not propagate from a path dependency: FrankenSim's `[patch.crates-io] asupersync` entry, its FrankenSQLite
   profile overrides, and the build-identity environment its linear-algebra crate requires (hazard 5 below).
   FrankenSim's own manifest states the rule for profiles (FACT: `frankensim/Cargo.toml`, comment above
   `[profile.dev.package.fsqlite]`); that the same holds for patches and for `[env]` in our graph is a
   HYPOTHESIS settled by INT-FSIM-01. A build may contain exactly one asupersync package (INT-ASUP-01), and the
   siblings are aligned on one published version in their own repositories (FD-ASUP-01). Volatile FrankenSim
   types are named in one crate only.
4. **The fallbacks are written down, in order.** First a pinned FrankenSim worker process, then a lift
   (§33.8, §33.14).

Known hazards on the day of writing (FACT, each with its path):

1. **Churn.** 409 commits touched `crates/fs-mbd` in the thirty days to 2026-11-21, on 17 distinct days
   (`git log --since=2026-10-22 -- crates/fs-mbd` in `frankensim`).
2. **An unfrozen composition contract.** `COMPONENT_CONTRACT_VERSION` is 0
   (`frankensim/crates/fs-ports/src/component.rs:52`).
3. **A stale constellation lock.** `frankensim/constellation.lock` pins asupersync 0.5.0 at `7a0f2f55047c`,
   while `frankensim/Cargo.lock` resolves asupersync 0.6.2 by path and the local checkout is 0.6.2 at
   `b32c5ba66315` with 267 modified paths. The lock's pins for franken_numpy, frankenscipy, frankensqlite,
   frankentorch and franken_networkx also differ from the local heads. FrankenSim's verify-only preflight
   treats an off-pin or dirty sibling as a refusal (`frankensim/AGENTS.md`, "Cargo-Independent Constellation
   Preflight"), so by its own rule it would refuse today; the preflight was not run for this chapter. Whether
   each difference is a fast-forward or a divergence is classified at INT-FSIM-01, not assumed.
4. **A nightly feature in the units crate.** `fs-qty` enables `generic_const_exprs`
   (`frankensim/crates/fs-qty/src/lib.rs:20`). §33.12 treats this as a named risk.
5. **A build script that fails closed outside the tree.** `frankensim/crates/fs-la/build.rs` panics unless
   `FRANKENSIM_DEPGRAPH_RECEIPT` or `FRANKENSIM_DEPGRAPH_SALT` is set; inside FrankenSim the salt comes from
   `.cargo/config.toml`, which a workspace outside the tree does not read. `fs-la` is in the closure of `fs-ad`,
   `fs-mbd` and `fs-contact`. The core workspace therefore sets its own salt, naming its own development
   equivalence class, in its root `.cargo/config.toml`, and evidence-bearing performance builds export a
   receipt (§33.5).
6. **A large closure.** A path dependency on `fs-mbd` compiles 42 other FrankenSim crates and about 1.57 million
   lines of library source, because `fs-contact` depends on `fs-material` (633 thousand lines), `fs-thermal`,
   `fs-thermochem` and `fs-feec` (computed from the `Cargo.toml` files under `frankensim/crates`). The crate set
   M1 needs compiles 65 crates and about 2.06 million lines. FrankenSim's lockfile holds 479 packages, of which
   262 come from the registry (`frankensim/Cargo.lock`). INT-FSIM-01 records cold and warm build times and peak
   memory, and the lift fallback is priced against these numbers, not against the size of two crates.
7. **A contact capacity.** The sustained contact solver admits at most 21 contacts
   (`frankensim/crates/fs-contact/src/sustained/solve.rs:26`), and its articulated step keeps fixed material
   witnesses (§33.11, row 4).
8. **Dirty trees.** FrankenSim's own checkout has 32 modified paths at head `483d09c682f2`. A pin names a
   commit; the resolution receipt (§33.5) hashes bytes when a tree is dirty.

A pinned commit, the airlock and the receipt are what make those survivable. Hazards 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 are also
upstream work items that this program does in FrankenSim's repository, under its rules (FD-FSIM-08; FD-FSIM-16
with FD-ASUP-01; FD-FSIM-15 and FD-FSIM-31; FD-FSIM-14; FD-FSIM-13; §33.8, Appendix F). Each keeps the interim
posture stated in this chapter until its change lands. Hazard 1 is why the airlock stays whatever lands.

Three rules bind the adapter. It converts, and never reinterprets: a FrankenSim refusal becomes a typed referee
refusal carrying the upstream reason, and is never retried with weakened inputs. It does not cache across pins:
every memoized referee result carries the stack-lock digest in its key. And it exposes capacities as data: the
21-contact limit of hazard 7 is a field of `RefereeContact`, checked when a model is compiled and again at run
time, so that an over-capacity contact set is a refusal by name and is never silently truncated (the contact set
of a tracked robot overflows where a point-foot robot's does not, and dropping contacts would bias exactly the
comparison the studio exists to make).

### 33.5 The stack lock

`franken-stack.lock` is TOML in FrankenMining's format (FACT: `franken_mining/franken-stack.lock`). One row per
foundation, checked by `xtask check-stack`:

```toml
toolchain = "<T, chosen at INT-TOOLCHAIN-01; moved only by a toolchain train (§33.13)>"

[[foundation]]
id = "FD-FSIM"
name = "frankensim"
posture = "consume-path"
path = "../frankensim"
git_rev = "<40-hex commit recorded at INT-FSIM-01>"
toolchain = "<the channel in the sibling's own toolchain file at git_rev; equal to T after the train>"
features = ["referee", "geometry", "ledger"]            # airlock groups compiled by default
build_env = { FRANKENSIM_DEPGRAPH_SALT = "franken-robotics-dev-v1" }
confined_to = ["frob-fsim", "bedrock-direct"]
admission_gate = "INT-FSIM-01"

[[foundation]]
id = "FD-ASUP"
name = "asupersync"
posture = "consume"

admission_gate = "INT-ASUP-01"

```

**The resolution receipt.** A pin is a statement of intent. What was built is a different fact, and the two have
already diverged inside the suite (hazards 3 and 8 of §33.2). `xtask check-stack` therefore writes one receipt
per admission and per pin move. DESIGN, schema `frob.stack-receipt/1`, canonical JSON:

```json
{
  "schema": "frob.stack-receipt/1",
  "repo_rev": "<this repository's commit>",
  "toolchain": "<T>",
  "siblings": [
    {"fd": "FD-FSIM", "rev": "<40 hex>", "dirty": false, "tree_hash": null,
     "features": ["referee", "geometry", "ledger"],
     "own_lock": {"file": "constellation.lock", "hash": "<BLAKE3>", "agrees": false,
                  "class": "stale-lock | drift | on-pin"}},
    {"fd": "FD-ASUP", "rev": "<40 hex>", "dirty": true, "tree_hash": "<BLAKE3 of tracked bytes>"}
  ],
  "lockfiles": [{"workspace": "core", "hash": "<BLAKE3 of Cargo.lock>", "packages": 0, "registry": 0}],
  "patches": ["asupersync -> ../asupersync"],
  "profile_overrides": ["fsqlite*: opt-level 1"],
  "build_env": {"FRANKENSIM_DEPGRAPH_SALT": "franken-robotics-dev-v1"},
  "duplicates": [],
  "commands": [{"argv": ["cargo", "tree", "-d"], "exit": 0, "output_hash": "<BLAKE3>"}]
}
```

Rules for the receipt:

- It captures source trees, requested features, compiler channels, lockfiles and the constraints of direct and
  transitive siblings in one file. A sibling's own lock (`constellation.lock`, `SUITE.lock`) is hashed and
  compared, and a disagreement is classified the way FrankenSim classifies it: on-pin, stale-lock (the checkout
  is a fast-forward of the lock), or drift (dirty, retreated or diverged).
- When a tree is dirty the receipt hashes the tracked bytes. A dirty sibling admits development builds only; no
  gate records a PASS on a dirty tree.
- Identities are independent of machine paths. The receipt stores repository-relative paths and revisions, never
  `/Users/...`.
- Every admission record (§33.6) cites a receipt hash. A result in the Chronicle ledger carries the receipt hash
  of the binary that produced it, which is what lets a later reader decide whether two results may be compared
  as results of the same stack.
- `xtask check-stack` fails if the tracked lockfiles, the toolchain files or the sibling heads disagree with the
  newest receipt. It never moves a sibling checkout. Receipts are kept under `receipts/`, named by their hash.

Known answer: a receipt computed twice on an unchanged tree is byte-identical. Negative control: touching one
tracked file in a sibling flips `dirty` and changes `tree_hash`, and `check-stack` fails.

### 33.6 Admission gates

An admission record is valid only if it cites a commit of this repo whose tracked `Cargo.lock` contains the
foundation, with the command, its output and the receipt hash (the rule FrankenMining adopted after finding
admission records for crates that were not in the build, FACT). Records live in `DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`, a
convention taken from FrankenThreeD (FACT: `franken_threed/DEPENDENCY_ADMISSION.md`). Thresholds below are
TARGETs until the gate records measured values.

| Gate | Pass criterion |
|---|---|
| INT-TOOLCHAIN-01 | The one toolchain T is chosen and recorded in `franken-stack.lock` and `rust-toolchain.toml`, in the order of preference of §33.13: (a) the suite's common nightly, (b) FrankenSim's present pin. The whole build graph (the core, the GPU annexes, `derive/frob-derive` and `surface/frob-mcp`) builds and tests on T. ### 33.8 Upstream work and drift

Every sibling this plan consumes is the owner's project (FACT, stated by the owner on 2026-11-22). DESIGN: a
limitation, defect or mismatch in a sibling is therefore work this program does, in the sibling's repository,
under that repository's own `AGENTS.md`, gates and conventions. Each such change is an **upstream work item**
(Appendix F): a bead in this program's graph with the label `upstream`, mirrored as a bead in the sibling's
tracker, with a priority, an interim posture here, and a switch-over rule. The interim posture and the
switch-over rule exist so that no milestone waits on a sibling change landing. Upstream changes are additive: a
new mode, type or feature, never a change to existing behaviour, with any lossiness self-described (the rule the
Wright Flyer plan sets for its upstream extensions, FACT). Each upstream work item carries one reproducible
failing fixture and a description of the needed argument or result.

The sibling's rules bind the work. For FrankenSim they include its layer checks, its contracts, its
unsafe-capsule discipline, its ban on in-process foreign kernels in production paths, which is why the GPU
annexes stay in this repository, and its ban on branches and worktrees (FACT: `frankensim/AGENTS.md`,
`frankensim/docs/ACCELERATOR_DOCTRINE.md`; §21.2, §34.4, §34.7). Other agents work in the same sibling
repositories. Before an upstream change edits a file it reserves that file through Agent Mail and follows the
sibling's own coordination rules, and it lands on the sibling's `main` through the sibling's own gates.

Pins advance through a compatibility train: move the pin, write a new receipt, rerun the admission and parity
gates, relock. A sibling's newer clean head is never rolled back locally. The airlock crate `frob-fsim` stays,
because FrankenSim's API still moves daily (hazard 1 of §33.2). The fallbacks stay written down (KILL-FSIM,
KILL-MCP and the others); they are fallbacks, and no default wiring rests on one.

inequality when Â < 0. Take Â = −1 and ε = 0.2. At ρ = 0.5 the two terms are −0.5 and −0.8, the minimum is the
clipped term and the gradient is zero, but the code lets it flow. At ρ = 2 the terms are −2 and −1.2, the

standard deviation at ratios 0.5, 1 and 2 are 0, −0.5 and −1 against the code's −0.25, −0.5 and 0. For the

write is easy to write twice.

**Row 2, the arithmetic.** The step takes its straight branch only when |ω| ≤ 10⁻¹² rad/s. Otherwise it forms
θ′ = θ + ω·Δt and returns R·(sin θ′ − sin θ) and −R·(cos θ′ − cos θ). Take θ = 32 rad, wheel speeds 1 and
1 + 6·10⁻¹³ m/s, track 0.5 m and Δt = 1/400 s. Then ω = 1.2·10⁻¹² rad/s, just above the threshold, and
ω·Δt = 3·10⁻¹⁵ rad. Half the spacing of doubles at 32 is 2⁻⁴⁸ ≈ 3.55·10⁻¹⁵, so θ′ rounds to θ and both
differences are exactly zero. Both wheels advance and the chassis does not move. Over 400 steps the true
displacement is one metre along the heading, about (0.83422336, 0.55142668) m. The independent formula is
Δp = v·Δt·sinc(δ/2)·(cos(θ + δ/2), sin(θ + δ/2)) with δ = ω·Δt, sinc(x) = sin(x)/x and sinc(0) = 1. Its
hand-checkable case: unit speed through a half turn from θ = 0 gives the chord (0, 2/π) m. INT-FSIM-05 tests
exact straight travel, the near-zero-curvature case above, wrapped against unwrapped headings, and incremental
against one-shot integration to 10⁻¹².

**Row 3, the hand case.** A contiguous 2×2 single-precision view has strides (8, 4) in bytes and (2, 1) in
elements. Copying (8, 4) into an element-stride view over a 16-element buffer addresses element 8 + 4 = 12 at
index [1, 1], where element 3 belongs. Both reads are in bounds, so nothing faults. A view with byte origin 4
and strides (8, −4) addresses elements (1, 0, 3, 2), which unsigned strides cannot express. The converter at the
export boundary carries the logical origin, the allocation extent, the stride unit and the signedness. A view
that the target cannot represent is materialized and the copy is charged to the caller, or refused under a
zero-copy contract. A writable view is also checked for independence, separately from bounds: a 2×2 view with element strides (1, 1) maps index [0, 1] and index [1, 0] to one element, so stores of 7 and 11 leave a result that depends on their order. Shared elements are admitted for reads; a write needs an injective index map, a declared reduction or separate storage, and is refused otherwise. Neither library is wrong by itself; the trap is at our boundary.

**Row 4, the consequence.** The limit is declared upstream and enforced by refusal, which is the behaviour this
plan wants from a referee. It still decides what M1 can claim: referee rollouts over planes and primitive
obstacles are admitted, and a wheel climbing a riser is admitted only through the event-located transactional
step, which localizes new witness pairs at a consistent world contact point and transports or resets tangential
memory. INT-FSIM-07 passes when the flat-to-edge-to-top transition returns the typed refusal through the airlock
at the pin, and the same transition completes under witness migration with the energy ledger closed to its

### 33.14 The worker-process fallback

**ADR-SUITE-02: before any FrankenSim source is lifted, the referee moves behind a process boundary.** [S]

What it is. `frob-fsim` has two build modes behind the same traits. In mode `inproc` (the default) the adapters
call FrankenSim through path dependencies. In mode `remote` every volatile `fs-*` dependency is compiled out and
the adapters speak a job protocol to a worker. The worker is the binary target `frob-fsim-worker` of the same
crate, built in mode `inproc` from the small workspace `worker/` on FrankenSim's toolchain at the pinned commit.
No new crate is created, and `frob-fsim` remains the only code that names volatile types. One worker runs per
device or per process slot; the daemon starts, supervises and stops it.

The protocol exchanges whole jobs and bounded chunks. It never carries a per-link, per-ray or per-tick call: the
solver and its working set stay together on one side.

```
job    = { job_hash, stack_receipt, model (compiled referee model, canonical bytes), scene, initial_states,
           control (a schedule, or a controller program the worker evaluates), horizon, cadence, seeds,
           outputs (state columns, events, digests, energy-ledger rows) }
chunk  = { job_hash, index, first_step, last_step, columns, events, digest, final: bool }
refuse = { job_hash, code, upstream_reason }
```

- A job is identified by the BLAKE3 hash of its canonical bytes, and submission is idempotent on that hash.
- Chunks follow a fixed logical partition of the horizon, so a restarted worker regenerates the same chunk
  boundaries and the client resumes from the last contiguous chunk whose digest it holds.
- The worker reports the stack receipt it was built under. A mismatch with the job's receipt is a refusal
  (`FROB-REF-791`), not a warning.
- Transport is a local socket or a pipe, framed by length, with integers above 2⁵³ as strings where JSON is used
  and binary columns elsewhere.

What the fallback preserves: the referee is FrankenSim's own code; teleport from a device tier to the referee
(a job whose initial state is a snapshot); shadow sampling; promotion. What it costs: a fixed overhead per job,
so the referee is called in rollouts and never inside a loop; and a second build to keep, though only at pin
moves. What it does not solve: an API change still costs the adapter, but the worker can stay on an old pin while
the core moves.

INT-FSIM-18 runs at M0 even though the fallback is not active, because a fallback that has never run is a hope.
Pass criteria (TARGET): a 2,000-step G1 referee rollout submitted through the worker returns chunk digests equal,
bit for bit, to the same job run in process; killing the worker mid-job and resubmitting returns the same
digests; fixed overhead is at most 50 ms per job and at most 5% of wall time on jobs of one second or more.
Negative control: a worker built at a different commit refuses the job.

The trigger for fallback (b) is numeric too (DESIGN). If shadowing through the worker falls below 0.1% of device
world-seconds at the M2 batch size, which is the bottom of the range §36 gives for a CPU referee, the worker
cannot do the referee's job and the lift is considered.

Six workspaces exist: the core, which includes `surface/frob-mcp`, the two GPU annexes, `apps/`, `derive/` and
`bindings/`, with `worker/` as a seventh that only re-targets an existing crate. All of them build on T, and no
annex carries a toolchain file of its own (§33.13); `lean/` is not Rust. One exception is a fallback by design:
when KILL-FSIM's fallback (a) keeps the worker at an older pin, `worker/` builds on that pin's toolchain
(§33.14). The reason for an annex boundary is never the compiler. It is the unsafe and foreign-interface policy:
a dependency the core's lockfile may not contain (a third-party driver binding, an FFI capsule, a Python
interpreter, FrankenSymPy at build time).

The two GPU annexes keep their own lockfiles for that reason, and it rests on FACTs of §21.2 and §33.13: the
suite keeps its working GPU code outside FrankenSim's workspace, `cudarc` is a third-party driver binding, and
`ft-kernel-metal` needs FrankenTorch's `[patch.crates-io] block` entry at the root of whichever workspace
resolves it. A separate lockfile keeps the driver bindings, the registered capsules and that patch out of the
core's resolution (rules L-3 and L-4, §34.4). `surface/frob-mcp` has no such reason: fastmcp_rust is a Franken
foundation that forbids unsafe code (FACT, §32.12.1), so the server is an ordinary member of the core workspace
and its directory name says only that it is a surface.

### 34.3 Layer and dependency rules

- **L-1** A crate depends only on lower crates. `xtask check-layers` fails a deliberate upward edge. Lower means a
  lower layer number, or the same layer and an earlier row of the crate map. Each crate declares both in its
  manifest (`[package.metadata.frob] layer = 3`, `rank = 2`), the convention FrankenSim uses for its own layers
  (FACT: `[package.metadata.frankensim] layer` in `frankensim/crates/fs-exec/Cargo.toml`). A chapter that needs a
  different order inside a layer changes the rank by ADR; a cycle is never admitted.
- **L-2** Only `frob-fsim` names types from FrankenSim's volatile crates. The bedrock-direct list is the only
  exception, and `fs-qty` is named only through `frob_core::qty` (§33.12).
- **L-3** The core never depends on an annex. Backends implement core traits.
- **L-4** Each foundation is confined to the crates in its `FD-` row.
- **L-5** Core runtime dependencies are `std` and Franken foundations. `serde` and `serde_json` at exact pins are
  allowed in boundary crates only (`frob-io`, `frob-verbs`, `frob-chronicle`, `frob-design`, `frob-spec`,
  `surface/frob-mcp`), never in a hot path. Forbidden everywhere: tokio and its ecosystem, a direct Rayon pool,
  BLAS and LAPACK, C or C++ numerical libraries. A Python interpreter is forbidden everywhere except
  `bindings/frob-gymnasium`.
- **L-6** No bare `f64` physical quantity in a public signature above layer 1. Quantities are `frob_core::qty`
  types or group elements.
- **L-7** Library crates do not print. Hot loops emit no per-iteration JSON.
- **L-8** Generated files are never edited. `xtask check-generated` regenerates and compares.
- **L-9** `frob-cert` has an empty dependency table, and only `frob-verbs`, `frob-cli` and `frob-conformance`
  may depend on it (§34.11).
- **L-10** A lower crate reaches a service that lives above it only through a trait it defines itself, as
  backends do under L-3. The standing inversions are five: the device backend (defined in `frob-dyn`,
  implemented in the annexes); the evidence sink and the trace source (both defined in `frob-core`, implemented
  in `frob-chronicle`); the job context for cancellation and budgets (defined in `frob-core`, supplied by the
  daemon); and the certificate sink (defined in `frob-core`, implemented in `frob-lemma`). This is what lets a layer-5 crate such as `frob-autopsy` fork a
  recorded run without depending on layer 6.
- **L-11** Two planes. Kernels and the step never depend on a dispatcher, a memo policy, an estimator or a
  scheduler: no crate of layers 1 to 3 holds one. Decisions about what to run live at layer 4 and above and in
  the daemon (§34.12). Compile memoization in `frob-model` is a pure cache keyed by identity with a fixed bound,
  not a policy; the memo graph for results sits in front of verb executors in `frob-verbs`, with its bytes in
  `frob-chronicle`.
- **L-12** Features that select a tier of ambition are named and off by default: `frontier-*` and `moonshot-*`
  features gate every [F] and [M] mechanism in a mixed crate, and no default build enables one.

Honesty about the closure: path dependencies on FrankenSim bring its transitive closure, which includes
third-party crates such as `nalgebra` and `rayon` through other siblings (FACT: 262 registry packages among the
479 in `frankensim/Cargo.lock`; `nalgebra` 0.35.0 and `rayon` 1.12.0 are among them). "Franken-only" describes
direct edges.

Counterexample for L-1's intra-layer clause. If rows inside a layer were unordered, `frob-arena` could depend on
`frob-opt` for comparison while `frob-opt` depends on `frob-arena` for batteries, and Cargo would reject the
cycle only after both were written. The rank makes the second edge a `check-layers` failure on the day it is
added.

### 34.4 Unsafe code

The core has `unsafe_code = "deny"` and plans zero capsules: SIMD comes from `fs-simd`, arenas from FrankenSim's
substrate. `frob-cert` goes further and forbids it at the crate root. The annexes may contain capsules, each
registered in `registries/capsules.toml`, under 300 lines, behind a safe API, with a `SAFETY.md` (FrankenSim's
capsule discipline, FACT: `frankensim/unsafe-capsules.json` and `frankensim/AGENTS.md`, "Unsafe Code"). No
total is promised for the annexes: a driver binding with pools, graphs and runtime compilation needs what it
needs, and the count is reported per capsule by `check-unsafe`. Unsafe is never used to work around a lifetime,
aliasing, cancellation or synchronization problem.

### 34.5 Contracts

Every crate ships `CONTRACT.md` with the ten sections of §0.2 and a `tests/conformance.rs`. A crate becomes a
dependency target only after its contract lands. Beside the prose sits a small machine-readable record,
`contract.toml`, with the crate's layer and rank, its determinism class, the evidence class of its outputs, its
budgets, its feature flags with their tiers, and its safe mode (§34.12). `check-contracts` validates the record
against the manifest and the registries; it does not grade the prose.

`xtask` contains exactly: `check-layers`, `check-deps`, `check-unsafe`, `check-generated`, `check-contracts`,

### 34.7 Working rules inherited from the machine and the suite

FACT, from the machine-level agent instructions and from `frankensim/AGENTS.md`, adopted here:

- Cargo runs through the build-offload shim, which queues for a worker and never compiles locally; cargo is not
  invoked by absolute path. Projects stay under `~/projects`. Isolated target directories use
  `${RCH_TARGET_BASE:-${TMPDIR:-/tmp}}/<name>`, and large scratch uses `${BIG_SCRATCH:-${TMPDIR:-/tmp}}/<name>`.
- A disk preflight precedes heavy lanes.
- **The machine envelope.** No run on any host uses more than 16 GiB of memory, 8 cores or one hour of wall time
  without the owner's written approval given before it starts; builds and tests count; a run longer than a few
  minutes starts under a timeout; when an agent stops, its jobs stop. Lanes of this plan that need more (the
  world bank on the M5 Ultra, training campaigns on the two RTX 4090, the tournament of §39) name their budget in
  their work package and wait for that approval. Until it is given, the envelope is the default of OPEN-SPARK-2.
- No file is deleted without explicit permission. No branches and no worktrees: work lands on `main`, and file
  reservations through Agent Mail replace branches.
- `ubs` runs on changed files before a commit. Formatting, lints and tests run before a commit. Beads are
  managed with `br` and triaged with `bv --robot-*`.
- Source that was never compiled does not land. The commit message carries the commands and their results.
- Code is changed by focused patches, not by scripts or broad regular-expression rewrites. An existing file is
  revised in place; a new file must be new functionality or a required contract or test artifact.
- Before a build failure is attributed to this repository, `xtask check-stack` runs. An off-pin sibling is
  classified as a fast-forward or a divergence before any remedy is proposed, and a sibling checkout is never
  moved back to its pin to make a check pass.
- Process is never the product. Before non-product work an agent answers three questions: which capability,
  defect or blocker does this address, is it the smallest direct action, and is it worth its cost now. If any
  answer is unclear the work is not done.
- The machine-level rule on Apple Simulator audio applies to any use of a device simulator. This plan uses none.

Why two formats are owned by `frob-spec` and not by their evaluators: a task's requirements and a mission's
quantified statement are written in one requirement language, and a certificate about a mission must quote that
statement exactly. One parser and one canonical form remove the question of whether the learner, the arena and
the mission evaluator read the same sentence.

Registry rules (DESIGN):

1. **One owner.** The owner crate holds the schema, the canonicalization rule, the reference reader and the
   version refusal. No other crate parses the bytes; a crate below the owner's layer receives typed values. The
   one exception is deliberate: `frob-cert` reads only `.frobcert`, and a bundle embeds the canonical bytes and
   hashes of the statements it is about, so the auditor never needs another crate's reader.
2. **Identity.** The identity of an artifact is the BLAKE3 hash of its canonical bytes under a domain string
   equal to its schema name. A view has no identity.
3. **Versions.** The schema name carries a major version. Adding an optional field keeps it. A reader refuses an
   unknown major version and an unknown required field, and never parses on a best-effort basis. Migration is a
   typed edit that produces a new artifact with a link to the old one; the old identity stays valid for citing
   old evidence.
4. **Numbers.** Integers above 2⁵³ travel as strings. Not-a-number and infinity are not representable. Each owner
   names one rule for floating-point fields that bear identity (shortest round-trip decimal, or exact rationals)
   and tests it.
5. **Payloads.** Large data are blobs addressed by hash and referenced from a canonical manifest. A manifest
   never embeds a path on the machine that wrote it.
6. **Provenance on export.** An artifact that leaves the tool carries the no-authorization flags (§12, §37), the
   schema name, and the stack receipt of the binary that wrote it.

Refusals:

| Code | Name | When |
|---|---|---|
| `FROB-REF-891` | `format-version-unsupported` | Unknown major version, or an unknown required field |
| `FROB-REF-892` | `view-is-not-identity` | A view is offered where an identity is required: to hash, to sign, to cite as evidence, or as a cache key |
| `FROB-REF-893` | `format-owner-bypass` | A file with a registered extension reaches a verb through a path that did not go through its owner's reader |

Known answers and counterexamples:

- The objects `{"b":1,"a":2}` and `{"a":2,"b":1}` have one identity.
- Counterexample to "hash the file as written": two files that differ in key order or whitespace would be two
  designs, and every memoized analysis of the first would miss on the second.
- Counterexample to "hash the in-memory value": a hash of a debug rendering or of a struct's layout changes with
  the compiler, so the same design would change identity at a pin move and every cached result would be orphaned.
- Counterexample to "the view is lossless, so hash either": the S-expression and the JSON of one design are
  different bytes. A cache keyed on whichever arrived first would hold two entries for one design and, worse,
  two verdicts that a reader could cite as independent.
- Counterexample to "tolerate unknown fields": a reader that ignores an unknown required field such as a
  contact-law selector would simulate a different robot from the one described and report it under the same
  identity.

Gate (TARGET). **GATE-WS-02**, claim type Known answer: for each of the thirteen formats, 10,000 seeded
round trips canonical → value → canonical with zero byte differences; where a view exists, 10,000 round trips
view → canonical → view with zero differences; every one of 10,000 single-byte mutations either fails to parse or
changes the identity; and each reader survives 10⁷ fuzz executions with zero panics before the format's first
release. Negative control: a reader built with key-order canonicalization disabled must fail the first clause.

### 34.10 The rule for creating a new crate

**ADR-WS-01: the crate list of §34.2 is closed. A new crate is created only for a distinct dependency, a
distinct device target, or a proof boundary.** [S]

- A distinct dependency: the code needs a foundation, a feature group of the airlock or a third-party crate that
  its natural home must not link. `frob-foundry` is the plain case: the `structure` group adds 13 FrankenSim
  crates and about 412 thousand lines of library source beyond what M1 already compiles (FACT, counted as in
  §33.3), and no other crate needs them. `derive/frob-derive` is another: FrankenSymPy, which no runtime crate
  may link.
- A distinct device target: the code compiles for a different processor, runtime or host. The two `spark`
  annexes, `frob-tui` and `bindings/frob-gymnasium` are of this kind.
- A proof boundary: the code states or checks something whose trust must not depend on the code around it.
  `frob-cert` is the plain case. `frob-spec` is one because certificates and qualification verdicts quantify
  over its sentences. `frob-phylum` is one because the statement "a world's trajectory does not depend on the
  batch it ran in" is tested at its boundary. `frob-nemesis` is one because the discovery lane may not read
  confirmation worlds (§24), and a crate boundary lets `check-deps` enforce that the qualification code does not
  link the searcher.

"It is getting long" is not a reason; a module is the unit of organization inside a crate. "A different person
owns it" is not a reason either. An ADR that proposes a crate names which of the three reasons applies, the edge
that `check-deps` or `check-layers` will then enforce, and the cap. The rule governs additions. The 43 crates of
§34.2 were fixed together with the subsystems of Parts III to V, one bounded context each, and are not
re-litigated by it.

The opposite pressure is handled the same way. Two crates are merged when no dependency, target or proof
boundary separates them any longer; `frob-lifecycle`, the one all-Moonshot crate, is the first candidate if its
pilot fails, and its fallback is deletion, not absorption.

### 34.11 The independent auditor

**ADR-WS-02: `frob-cert` and its binary `frob-verify` depend on the Rust standard library and on nothing else,
and no proposer depends on them.** [S]

A certificate is only as good as the independence of what checks it. If the checker shares a parser, a hash
implementation, an arithmetic library or a units crate with the code that produced the bundle, one defect can
make both agree. The rule is therefore structural, and `check-deps` enforces it:

1. `crates/frob-cert/Cargo.toml` has an empty `[dependencies]` table: no `frob-*` crate, no FrankenSim crate
   (so no `fs-qty` and no nightly feature), no asupersync, no `serde`. It inherits nothing from the workspace
   manifest.
2. It carries its own toolchain file naming a stable release. A copy of the directory alone builds with
   `cargo build --release` on that compiler. This also frees the auditor from the risk of §33.12.
3. It forbids unsafe code at the crate root, reads only the files named on its command line, opens no socket,
   reads no environment variable and spawns no process.
4. It implements for itself what it needs: a canonical JSON reader, BLAKE3 (checked against the published test
   vectors), exact integer and rational arithmetic, outward-rounded interval arithmetic, and the checkers for
   each certificate kind of §12. Its cap of 12 thousand lines accounts for that.
5. No crate that produces a claim or a certificate depends on it. Only `frob-verbs` (to expose the check as a
   verb), `frob-cli` and `frob-conformance` may (rule L-9). The bundle writer lives in `frob-lemma` and is
   written against the schema, sharing no code with the reader.
6. A FrankenSymPy-based re-check of the same bundles runs in `frob-conformance` as a second opinion (§33.4). It
   is an oracle for the auditor, never a part of it.

Known answer: `cargo tree -p frob-cert` prints one line. Counterexample to the shortcut "reuse `frob-core`'s
hashing in the checker": a canonicalization defect in `frob-core` that drops a field before hashing would be
reproduced by the checker, and a bundle whose statement differs from the one the proposer evaluated would pass.
With two implementations the hashes disagree and the bundle is rejected.

Gate (TARGET). **GATE-WS-03**, claim type Known answer: the dependency count of `frob-cert` is zero; the copied
directory builds on its stable compiler with no network access; the standalone binary and the in-daemon verb
return identical verdicts on the whole conformance corpus of §38; and a system-call trace of an audit shows no
network and no process creation. The behavioural gates of the auditor (what it must accept and reject) are in
§12 and §38.

### 34.12 Suite conventions this project adopts

Six conventions of the wider suite are adopted. Each replaces an ad hoc habit with something a tool can check,
and each is assigned to one of the six checks so that adopting it adds no seventh.

- **Artifact-graph identifiers.** Every gate claim has a `claim_id` that resolves to the `evidence_id`s of the
  records supporting it, the `policy_id` of the rule and thresholds in force, and the `trace_id` of the run.
  FACT: `franken-kernel` 0.6.0 defines `TraceId`, `DecisionId`, `PolicyId` and `SchemaVersion` and has no claim
  or evidence identifier (`asupersync/franken_kernel/src/lib.rs`). DESIGN: `frob-core` mirrors the text form of
  the three it has, adds `ClaimId` and `EvidenceId` in the same form, and adds them upstream (FD-FKER-01).
  Rows of `registries/gates.toml` carry their `claim_id`.
- **Contracts as code.** Each crate's `contract.toml` (§34.5) states budgets and a safe mode: what the crate does
  when a budget is exhausted or a calibration check fails, which is always to fall back to its reference path or
  to refuse by name. It is validated inside `check-contracts`.
- **A demo file linked to claims for every release path.** `demos/<demo_id>/demo.yaml` lists the `demo_id`, the
  `claim_id`s it demonstrates, commands that finish in under 60 seconds on the CPU tier (TARGET), and checksums
  of their outputs. A capability with no demo is not released (§47). A demo is evidence that a path runs; it is
  never evidence for the claim's numbers, which come from the gate.
- **Shadow-run as the adoption wedge.** A mechanism that replaces a reference path first runs beside it: both
  results are reported and the old one is acted on. It is promoted when its agreement gate passes and demoted
  by one flag. The incumbent is always this plan's own reference path: full recomputation for the memo graph,
  one batch per job for shared batches, the paired e-process for a variance-reduced estimator, the CPU referee
  for a device tier.
- **Two planes.** Computation and decision are separate (rule L-11). A kernel can be replayed without the policy
  that chose to run it, and a policy can be replaced without touching a digest.
- **The controller gate.** No adaptive controller ships (a scheduler, an allocator, a racing rule, a cache
  policy) without a replay scenario, evidence records, a calibration report and a demonstrated fallback. Two
  controllers that read the same telemetry need an interference test before both are enabled.

### 34.13 Invariants, gates and open questions

Invariants, each enforced by the check named:

### 34.14 The canonical reader and writer, and JSON features that unify across the build

**What it is.** [S] The mechanism behind the rule "a duplicate key refuses" (§14.4), for every artifact format of
§34.9, and a guard against a dependency feature changing canonical bytes.

**The reader.** DESIGN: admission validates the raw bytes before any map is built or any digest is computed.

1. Member names are compared after escape decoding, at every nesting level, and a repeated name is refused.
   Smallest case: `{"wheel_radius_mm": 60, "wheel_radius_mm": 75}` names one member twice. A reader that keeps
   the first value simulates a 60 mm wheel, one that keeps the last simulates 75 mm, and both hash the same bytes.
2. An identifier in an envelope is a nonempty string of printable characters: no code point below 32, and not 127.
   An identifier therefore survives a round trip through a shell variable.
3. Integers above 2⁵³ and exact decimals are strings (§32.8, §14.4), so no value depends on a float parser.
4. The refusal is issued by the CLI and by the MCP server before a job is planned or a budget is charged
   (refusals: duplicate member name after escape decoding; identifier not printable).

**The writer.** Canonical bytes are produced by this project's writer from the typed term, with keys sorted by code
point explicitly. No canonical byte is produced by serializing a library map or a library number.

**Why the writer may not lean on the library.** FACT, read 2026-11-21:

- The `preserve_order` feature of `serde_json` switches its maps from sorted to insertion order, and Cargo unifies
  features across a build graph. `frankensqlite/Cargo.toml` records that the feature "leaked into every downstream
  consumer via feature unification, silently switching serde_json maps to insertion order across their whole
  build", and removed it from its workspace for that reason.
- `frankenscipy/Cargo.toml:59` enables `preserve_order` and `float_roundtrip`. FrankenSim's bedrock depends on
  FrankenSciPy (`fs-la` on `fsci-linalg`, `fs-math` on `fsci-special`), and `frankensim/Cargo.lock` resolves
  `serde_json` 1.0.154 with `indexmap` among its dependencies, which only `preserve_order` adds.
- `fastmcp_rust/Cargo.toml:54` enables `arbitrary_precision`, which changes how numbers are represented.
- `franken_textiles/Cargo.toml` records that without `float_roundtrip` a decimal may be read one unit in the last
  place off, which "flipped point-in-polygon verdicts" in its fixtures.

The feature set of the JSON library in a `frob` binary is therefore decided by whichever sibling is linked, and not
by this workspace. `frob-mcp` is a member of the core graph (§32.12.2), so fastmcp_rust's `arbitrary_precision` is
in the unified set of every binary that links it. Smallest case: `{"b": 1, "a": 2}` re-serialized through a library
map is `{"a":2,"b":1}` in one build and `{"b":1,"a":2}` in another, and a digest over those bytes changes when an
unrelated crate enters the graph.

**The guard.** `xtask check-deps` prints, for every binary, the resolved `serde_json` version and its unified
feature set, and fails when either differs from the stack lock. A conformance binary is built four ways, with and
without a dummy dependency that enables `preserve_order` and one that enables `arbitrary_precision`, and must
produce identical canonical bytes and digests on the golden corpus. Owner: `frob-core` (reader and writer),
`frob-verbs` (admission order), `xtask`.

A golden corpus of 10³ artifacts, including duplicates that differ only by escapes at depths 1 to 8, integers at 2⁵³ ± 1, non-printable identifiers, and decimals whose nearest double differs between parsers: every duplicate and every bad identifier is refused on both surfaces before a job identifier exists; canonical digests are identical across the four feature combinations; the mutant writer that serializes a library map fails under `preserve_order`. M0. |
