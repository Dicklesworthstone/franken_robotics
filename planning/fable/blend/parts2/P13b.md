## 39. The ten-fold test

This chapter defines what "ten-fold" means as a measurement and how the claim is confirmed, reported as something
smaller, or withdrawn. §2 states the thesis and its arithmetic; this chapter is the protocol that tests it. It fixes
the estimand, what an accepted answer is and who says so, the sealed mission suite, the arms, the phase ledger that
every run records, the statistics, the ceilings that are computed before any speed test is run, the grounds for
disqualification, and the outcome rules, which are written here before any measurement exists. Nothing in this
chapter is a result. Every threshold is a DESIGN choice or a TARGET, and every figure about an incumbent is a FACT
from Appendix H, to be re-read from its primary source before a gate cites it.

### 39.1 Position

- **The claim under test is BET-13**, and the tie rule is BET-14 (§4). The test is the only evidence the plan accepts
  for either. Engine throughput is a parity requirement with its own gates (§36); it is an input to this test and
  never a substitute for it.
- **The harness** lives in `frob-conformance` (§34, §38). It has no product verbs of its own. It measures
  FrankenRobotics through the public verbs of §32, the same surface a user has, and it drives every rival tool out
  of process, as §38 requires of foreign oracles. Its records use the schema names `frob.tenfold.mission/1`,
  `frob.tenfold.freeze/1`, `frob.tenfold.ledger/1`, `frob.tenfold.receipt/1` and `frob.tenfold.report/1`. They are
  harness records, not product formats, and they travel in the replication kit (§39.14).
- **Three parties.** The *builders* wrote FrankenRobotics. The *rival advocates* build and tune the rival arms and
  have written no FrankenRobotics code. The *adjudicator* writes the sealed missions and the referee protocol, runs
  acceptance and releases receipts, and belongs to neither group. A person or an agent session holds exactly one of
  the three roles for the life of a gate (INV-TENFOLD-01).
- **Two program gates.** A partial gate at M3 on the classes that exist by then, and the tournament at M7 on the
  whole suite. Before both, the incumbent baseline is measured at M0 and M1 (§39.11), because no end-to-end phase
  ledger of an agent-driven design loop on the current incumbent stacks has been published (FACT, Appendix H), and a
  ratio without a measured denominator is not a ratio.

### 39.2 The estimand

**The clock.** T is the wall-clock time from the moment the agent receives the mission card and its permitted inputs
to the moment the adjudicator accepts the first correct answer with all requested scope and precision. Unsuccessful
runs, training, tuning, compilation, device transfer, queueing, investigation of unknowns, certificate generation and
checking, repair, review and every rejected submission are inside it. Time the harness itself spends between an
arm's request and its service is measured and charged to the phase `wait` (§39.6). It is not subtracted.

**The restricted mean time.** Each stratum s has a common deadline H_s, fixed before the suite is sealed. A run that
is not accepted by H_s is censored and contributes H_s. The restricted mean time of an arm on a stratum is
RMT = E[min(T, H_s)] over the sealed missions and campaign seeds of that stratum. No run is dropped: a crash of the
arm's own software is time passing, and a run that never finishes costs H_s.

**The ratio.** For arms A (FrankenRobotics) and B (the strongest composed rival, §39.5),

    R_s = RMT_B ÷ RMT_A = E[min(T_B, H_s)] ÷ E[min(T_A, H_s)].

It is estimated by the pooled ratio of §39.7, which is a ratio of sums over paired runs and not an average of
per-mission ratios. An average of ratios is dominated by the missions where arm A is fastest and hides the mission
it never finishes. A ratio of sums charges that mission its full deadline. This restricted-time ratio is the only
quantity the word "ten-fold" attaches to; the ratio of accepted-output rates is a different estimand and is cited
only for statements about decisions per hour (§39.20). Where the composed incumbent of §39.22 (arm C here, arm W
there) has a smaller restricted mean time than arm B, the word is governed by the ratio against it: the word is
never awarded on arm B alone.

**Regimes.** Cold and warm runs are separate results and are never pooled (§39.4). A ratio is always reported with
its regime: "cold", or "warm at N amendments".

**Why not steps per second.** DESIGN: the unit of success is an accepted answer. Three FACTs settle the choice
(Appendix H). An infinitely fast environment step shortens training by at most about 7.7 times (1/(1 − 0.87)) on
one incumbent stack and about 1.4 times on another, because the step is 87% and 29% of the respective loops. An
incumbent already
measures about 2.55 M humanoid world-steps/s. And in the one published agent-driven design loop with a time
breakdown, a round takes about an hour and is dominated by policy training, which no step rate describes. A step
rate also says nothing about whether the answer it produced is right. Steps per second are therefore measured and
gated in §36 as parity, and they enter this test only through the phases they shorten.

**Columns reported beside the clock.** Under identical resource caps, each run also records GPU-hours, CPU-hours,
energy where a credible meter exists (§36), agent tokens and tool calls, human minutes (the default is zero, and any
intervention is logged), and money at declared prices. Two derived units are reported for design strata: designs
decided per hour, and compute per accepted decision at a matched error rate. The primary claim is wall time. A cost
claim would need its own prices and its own registered test, and none is made here.

### 39.3 What counts as an accepted answer, and who adjudicates

**Answer kinds.** A mission card names exactly one.

| Kind | What the arm submits | Accepted when |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified design | A design in the mission's family, a controller behind the standard observation and action interface, and the declared clocks | The acceptance battery passes in the adjudicating simulator: over n sealed world clusters, the exact one-sided lower confidence bound on the requirement pass rate is at least the mission's threshold |
| Family answer | Inner and outer design sets with witnesses, exclusions with impossibility witnesses, and the unresolved band | Every mandatory interior, exterior and boundary obligation of the card is discharged; the unresolved band or optimality gap is within the card's maximum; every certificate checks; direct evaluation at the adjudicator's sealed probe points contradicts nothing. Strict and closed endpoints are part of acceptance where an exact boundary is asked for. A vacuous enclosure discharges nothing |
| Amendment verdict | For a changed mission: which earlier verdicts survive, which are withdrawn, and the repaired answer | The repaired answer passes as its own kind; no kept verdict is one the adjudicator's ground truth withdraws. A missed withdrawal fails the mission; unnecessary invalidations are counted and reported |
| Truthful unknown | A statement that the supplied evidence does not identify the choice, with the required witness: two admissible worlds or models under which the best choices differ, or the distinguishing experiment the card asks for | Only on missions whose question is whether the evidence identifies the choice. Everywhere else `unknown` is not an answer and the clock keeps running |
| Counterexample and repair | A replayable failing world inside the legality bounds, and a repaired design | The failure reproduces in the adjudicating simulator, and the repaired design passes the battery of the first row |

**The neutral adjudicating simulator.** DESIGN: dynamic acceptance batteries run in a simulator that neither arm
used for training, tuning or screening on that stratum, whose contact model was derived independently of both arms,
that runs deterministically on a CPU, and that is pinned by revision. The reason is that an arm scored in its own
engine is scored on its own modelling errors, and two programs that share a solver have correlated error (§8). The
default candidate is Drake at a pinned release (FACT, Appendix H: a CPU multibody engine with its own convex contact
solver). On a stratum where an arm uses that engine as a tool, or where it cannot represent the mission, the
adjudicator substitutes another engine that meets the four conditions. If none exists the stratum falls back to
**cross-home adjudication**: an answer must pass in the highest-trust tier of both arms (the referee of §16 and the
rival's CPU reference), run by the adjudicator, and the report labels the stratum `adjudication: cross-home`
(KILL-TENFOLD-ADJ). Models reach the adjudicating simulator through a neutral exchange description that the
adjudicator's own importer reads. A controller runs as a separate process behind the observation and action
interface with the mission's clocks. The gap between an arm's home simulator and the adjudicator is that arm's risk,
equally for both.

**Certificates and family answers.** Every submitted mathematical assertion is replayed by `frob-verify` (§12),
whose schema is public and which any arm may target. Because the builders wrote that checker, it is not the last
word in this test. The adjudicator also evaluates each family answer directly against the mission, with tools of the
adjudicator's choosing: exact rational evaluation at sealed probe points, an SMT or interval tool on the raw mission
statement, dense sampling in the adjudicating simulator. A disagreement between an accepted certificate and the
direct evaluation is a false certificate (§39.9).

**The referee protocol is not written by the builders.** The adjudicator writes it from the public class
descriptions, the mission grammar and the schemas. Builders and rival advocates may comment on drafts through one
logged channel until the suite hash is committed. After that the protocol is frozen and its hash is in the ledger
(GATE-TENFOLD-04). The builders supply schemas, the Gymnasium-compatible adapter of §23 and the development
missions. They supply no acceptance code.

**Submissions and retries.** Acceptance uses the protocol of the blinded qualification lane (§24), run by the
adjudicator on the adjudicator's own sealed key. A submission is committed before any world is drawn. Each
submission draws fresh world clusters. The i-th submission on a mission is tested at level α_i = α / [i·(i + 1)]
with α = 0.05. The arm receives a receipt holding only the decision, the index, the level spent, the units used and
a hash. A rejected submission returns no per-world outcome. Retrying is allowed, costs clock time, and cannot buy
acceptance by repetition. Every arm is under the same schedule. Where the submission format allows it the
adjudicator scores an answer without being told which arm produced it, and the paired results (times, per-world
outcomes and transcripts) are released only after every arm of the gate has closed.

**Who the adjudicator is.** DESIGN: an external party, a person or a team with no authorship in any arm.
OPEN-TENFOLD-2 names it. The fail-closed default, if none can be engaged, is a fresh-context agent session with no
write access to any arm, operating from the protocol document alone under a named human, with every action logged.
Every ratio measured that way is labelled `self-adjudicated`. Under that label a ratio may be reported, and the
words "independently accepted" and "ten-fold" wait until an external party has re-run the replication kit (§39.14).

**Three rules that protect the bar.** The time score is never improved by lowering what counts as accepted. An
answer that was accepted and is later shown wrong within its scope scores that mission as unresolved at H_s for the
arm that submitted it. A correct refusal that arrives after the deadline is a missed deadline.

**Sizes (DESIGN).** Each stratum has 12 public development missions and an initial 24 sealed mission families. The
final sealed count is fixed by the pre-analysis of §39.7 before the suite is opened, and is never below 12. A
stratum that declares a population result needs at least 32 independent families at α = 0.05, and 46 where four
strata share the level; a stratum with fewer declares a finite-suite result (§39.20). A
mission family is the independent unit: missions that share a generator draw, a site, a material batch or a parent
design belong to one family and stay together in every split. Twenty-four missions on one site are one replicate.

**How missions are written.** The adjudicator writes each mission as a card: the robot family and its bounds
(grammar, masses, actuator catalogue, and the symmetry knowledge every arm is given equally); the world family and
its generator parameters; the task, stated in prose and as a requirement in the language of §24 with the
adjudicator's own monitor; the answer kind and its obligations; the acceptance battery (n, threshold, tier); the
deadline; the resource caps; the permitted inputs (calibration data, prior answers, caches); for warm missions the
amendment sequence; and the control flags below. Cards are written from the public class descriptions. Some worlds
are built with an evaluator independent of every proposal engine, because a sampling run and a solver that share one
mistranslated world model agree on the wrong answer.

**Anti-shortcut controls, locked into every stratum.**

1. *Easy wins for conventional methods.* At least one family in six is a mission on which a conventional method is
   expected to win or tie. If the frozen rival wins none of them on the development suite, the rival is presumed
   under-built and the freeze is refused (GATE-TENFOLD-04).
2. *Genuinely unresolved answers.* At least one family in twelve has `unknown` with a witness as its correct answer.
3. *Independent mutations.* Unit, contact-order, causal-clock and passive-load mutations are derived by the
   adjudicator, not by an arm.
4. *Hidden consequence.* In an amendment sequence the card does not say which edit matters. Missed withdrawals and
   unnecessary invalidations are both measured.
5. *The quantifier-order fixture* is in `envelope` permanently: every design has a command that works and no single
   command works for all, so a per-build query and a shared query must return different answers (§25).
6. *Tier traps.* At least one family per design stratum has a candidate that passes on a fast tier and fails in the
   adjudicating simulator.

**Sealing.** The order is fixed, and each step is a ledger entry with a hash and a time (INV-TENFOLD-02): (1) class
descriptions and the development suite are published; (2) the adjudicator commits the hash of the sealed suite and of
the referee protocol; (3) arms are tuned on the development suite only; (4) each arm's freeze manifest is published
(§39.5); (5) the pre-analysis is recorded; (6) the suite is opened to the harness, and a mission card is shown to an
arm only when that arm's clock starts. Step 2 precedes step 4 so that no mission can be written against a frozen
arm. Step 4 precedes step 6 so that no arm can be tuned against a mission.

**Rotation.** A sealed family serves one gate. When the gate closes, its missions, worlds and receipts are revealed
and the family is retired into the development pool. It never serves as a sealed family again. The tournament at M7
uses no family that the partial gate at M3 used. A family touched by a leak is retired at once (§39.9).

**Cold and warm.** A cold run starts from the mission card and nothing else that is specific to the mission:
authoring the environment, the design domain, the reward or requirement and the evaluation set is inside the clock
for every arm. A warm campaign runs a declared sequence of amendments to an accepted cold answer, with identical
reusable inputs and equal cache budgets for every arm. A fair rival also caches graphs, factors, compiled kernels
and policies. A failed amendment stays in the sequence and costs its deadline. Accepted count, total time and time
per acceptance are reported separately.

**Standing assets and the crossover reuse count.** A standing asset is anything an arm built for a mission class
before a clock started: a pretrained family policy, a kernel or shader cache, a compiled scene bank, a tuned reward
library. Each arm declares its standing assets in its freeze manifest with their measured build cost S. Let ā and b̄
be the pooled restricted mean times per amendment of arms A and B. The warm ratio at reuse count N is

    R_warm(N) = (S_B + N·b̄) ÷ (S_A + N·ā),

reported at N = 1, 10 and 100 and in the limit b̄ ÷ ā. The **crossover reuse count** N* is the smallest N at which
S_A + N·ā ≤ S_B + N·b̄. The warm verdict of §39.7 is evaluated at N = 10 (DESIGN: a class that needs a hundred reuses
to pay for itself is reported as such and is not summarized by its limit). A warm result is never quoted without S
and N*.

after the suite opens voids the affected cells, which are re-run at the next gate; it is never patched mid-gate.

### 39.6 The phase ledger

Every run of every arm records where its clock went. The ledger is what turns a ratio into an explanation, and it
is the only input the ceilings of §39.8 accept.

| Phase | What is charged to it | Chapter phases that map here |
|---|---|---|
| `author` | Writing the environment, design domain, reward or requirement, and evaluation set from the mission card | §28 authoring |
| `propose` | An agent turn that reads results and emits the next request: model latency and tool-call overhead | §28 review |
| `check` | Design realization and static analysis: edit to a valid model, preflight, pre-simulation analyses | §28 check |
| `synthesize` | Controller synthesis without training: baseline controllers, gait orbits, trajectory optimization | §28 synthesis |
| `compile` | Compilation or cache lookup, model upload, variant admission, graph capture, scene compile, up to the first device step | §36 compilation, admission |
| `screen` | Rollouts on cheap rungs that train nothing | §28 simulation; §36 screening |
| `train` | Policy training, fine-tuning and distillation, learner time included | §28 training; §36 training |
| `evaluate` | Evaluation batteries, comparisons, promotion to a higher tier | §28 promotion; §36 evaluation, promotion |
| `qualify` | The arm's own blinded confirmation before it submits | §36 qualification |
| `certify` | Proof search, certificate writing and the arm's own checking | — |
| `diagnose` | Failure analysis: replays, forks, attribution, counterexample search, reading logs | §28 review |
| `wait` | Queueing, device transfer, idle time, harness service time | §36 waiting |
| `adjudicate` | From a committed submission to its receipt | — |

**Attribution (DESIGN).** The raw ledger is a list of intervals, each with a start, an end, a phase, a resource and,
for agent turns, token counts. From it the harness derives two tables. The *exclusive* table assigns every instant
of the clock to one phase: an agent turn in progress owns its instants; while the agent is blocked on jobs, the job
that finishes last among those it waits for owns them; while it is blocked on the harness or the adjudicator, `wait`
or `adjudicate` owns them. The *resource* table gives device-seconds and core-seconds per phase, including work that
ran in the background of an agent turn. The exclusive table sums to the clock.

**Closure.** The exclusive phases sum to T within 2%. Any remainder is recorded as `unattributed`. A run with more
than 2% unattributed keeps its place in the ratio, is flagged `ledger-open`, and is excluded from ceiling
arithmetic (INV-TENFOLD-04). For arms B and C the advocate's wrapper tags every process it launches, and the tag map
is part of the freeze manifest.

### 39.7 Statistics

**Two studies, never mixed (§24).** The verdict of a gate is a fixed-n study: its sample is fixed before the suite
opens, its rule is fixed, it has a pre-analysis of which verdicts are reachable, and it is computed once, at n. A
separate anytime-valid study monitors the incoming runs for operational decisions. At the partial gate the monitor
can stop a cell and withdraw a claim. It can never award one.

**Design.** A **cell** is a stratum and a regime. A **campaign** is one arm's attempt at one mission with one
campaign seed, which fixes the agent's sampling seed where the provider allows it, the optimizer seeds and the arm's
own world seeds. A **pair** is the two campaigns of arms A and B on the same mission with the same campaign index,
scored on the same sealed acceptance worlds. A cell has m mission families and c campaigns per family, n = m·c pairs.
DESIGN: m = 24 and c = 1 by default, c = 2 where fewer than 24 families exist. No verdict of any kind is issued on
fewer than ten paired campaigns per arm (n ≥ 10), and the family is the unit of inference. Ten is a floor for
reporting anything, not a licence for a population claim. A cell of 24 families gives a finite-suite result: its
verdict is a statement about the sealed suite. A population result needs at least 32 independent families at
α = 0.05, and 46 where four confirmatory cells share the level, and the cell declares which of the two it is
before sealing (§39.20).

**The pooled ratio.** For a cold cell, with the sum over all pairs j of the cell,

    R̂ = Σ_j min(T_B,j, H) ÷ Σ_j min(T_A,j, H).

For a warm cell R̂ is R_warm(10) of §39.4, with ā and b̄ pooled the same way. The per-family ratio ρ_i is the same
expression restricted to the pairs of family i.

**The censoring rule.** (a) A campaign not accepted by H contributes H. (b) A pair in which both arms are censored
stays in both sums. (c) A harness fault that is not the arm's own is repaired by re-running the whole pair once
under the next campaign index; a second harness fault excludes the pair for every arm, and it is counted. A cell with
more than 5% of pairs excluded is descriptive only. (d) When arm B is censored, ρ_i is a lower bound on that
family's ratio, which is conservative for arm A; when arm A is censored, ρ_i is at most 1. (e) A run is never
dropped because its outcome is inconvenient, and an exclusion is decided before the pair's times are compared.

**The interval.** One-sided 95% bounds L and U on R come from a bias-corrected bootstrap that resamples mission
families with replacement and keeps each family's pairs together, with 10,000 replicates, on ln R̂. The interval is
reported with every ratio. It is approximate and descriptive, and it does not decide the word: resampling cannot
show a slow tail that the sample does not contain, and on a 24-family suite drawn from a population whose true
ratio is 1.82 a percentile bootstrap reports 20 in 79% of suites (§39.20). A population claim rests on the bounded
paired contrast of §39.20 and never on this interval.

**The exact guard.** The count X of families with ρ_i > 5 is compared with a binomial(m, ½) tail: the null
hypothesis "the median per-family ratio is at most 5" is rejected at level α when P(X ≥ x) ≤ α. The test is exact
and distribution-free at fixed m. Its critical counts at α = 0.05 are 5 of 5, 6 of 6, 7 of 8, 10 of 12, 12 of 16,
17 of 24 and 22 of 32 (FACT: binomial arithmetic; for example P(X ≥ 17) = 0.032 and P(X ≥ 16) = 0.076 at m = 24).
At m = 4 even 4 of 4 has tail 1/16 = 0.0625, so the guard cannot be passed: that verdict is unreachable, and a cell
of four families is refused as a confirmatory cell.

**The verdict rule for a cell (DESIGN).** The rows are tested in order, and the first that applies is the verdict.

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| `disqualified` | A ground of §39.9 applies to arm A in this cell |
| `descriptive` | n < 10, or more than 5% of pairs excluded, or the cell was not named confirmatory, or the pre-analysis found the cell underpowered (KILL-TENFOLD-REACH) |
| `withdrawn` | R̂ < 3; or, at the partial gate, the futility monitor stopped the cell |
| `ten-fold` | R̂ ≥ 10 against the stronger of arm B and the composed incumbent (arm C, the W of §39.22); and the exact guard rejects at the cell's adjusted level; and arm A's completion and coverage are non-inferior to arm B's; and the ablation arm has not tied (§39.10); and the adjudication condition of §39.3 holds |
| `measured` | Every other case: the report states R̂ with [L, U] and does not use the word |

On a cell of fewer than 32 independent families the verdict `ten-fold` is a finite-suite result: the report says
"ten-fold on the sealed suite" and uses no population wording. Population wording needs, in addition, a cell of at
least the family count its registered bound requires and a nonnegative lower bound on the paired contrast of
§39.20.

The floor of 5 in the guard comes from the first draft of this rule, whose lower bound had to exceed 5 and which
never confirmed a true ratio of 5. The pooled rule keeps that property: a true ratio of 5 is called ten-fold with
probability near zero in every design below. The threshold of 3 is a convention. It sits at the bottom of the range
§2.6 expects for quadruped briefs, so withdrawal of that class at the partial gate is a live outcome and not a
remote one.

**Why the rule was re-specified.** The first draft required, on at least four of six briefs including both held-out
ones, a ratio of medians of at least 10 with a lower bound above 5, over five seeds. A Monte Carlo of that rule
confirmed a true ratio of 10 in at most 18% of campaigns, 0 to 11% with five seeds, and reached 80% only near true
ratios of 15 to 20 (FACT, Appendix K). The cause was compounding: every brief needed its own point estimate above
10, which a true 10 delivers half the time, and the requirement was multiplied across briefs. The pooled rule has
one estimate per cell. No honest rule can confirm a true ratio of exactly 10 more than about half the time, because
a fair measurement of 10 falls below 10 half the time; the pooled rule reaches that half, and reaches 80% at a true
ratio near 11 to 12.

**Pre-analysis (illustrative).** The table is a planning computation under a stated model, not a result: lognormal
times, a per-family difficulty spread of 0.5 in the logarithm, per-family log-ratio spread s, no censoring, one
confirmatory cell, 20,000 simulated gates per entry. HYPOTHESIS: s lies between 0.5 and 0.8 on the design strata.
The binding pre-analysis is recomputed from the development suite's measured times, with censoring at H and the
multiplicity adjustment, and is recorded before the suite opens (GATE-TENFOLD-04).

| Families m | Spread s | Guard needs | P(`ten-fold`) at a true ratio of 5 / 7 / 8.5 / 10 / 12 / 15 / 20 | P(`withdrawn`) at 2 / 3 / 5 | True ratio at which P(`ten-fold`) reaches 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 0.5 | 17 of 24 | 0.000 / 0.002 / 0.08 / 0.50 / 0.94 / 1.00 / 1.00 | 1.00 / 0.50 / 0.000 | 11.25 |
| 24 | 0.8 | 17 of 24 | 0.000 / 0.03 / 0.20 / 0.49 / 0.83 / 0.98 / 1.00 | 0.98 / 0.50 / 0.005 | 12.0 |
| 12 | 0.8 | 10 of 12 | 0.002 / 0.05 / 0.19 / 0.39 / 0.65 / 0.89 / 0.98 | 0.93 / 0.50 / 0.03 | 13.75 |
| 6 | 0.8 | 6 of 6 | 0.006 / 0.05 / 0.12 / 0.23 / 0.38 / 0.57 / 0.77 | 0.86 / 0.49 / 0.08 | above 20 |

Two readings. Between true ratios of 7 and 10 the word can be awarded by chance, in up to a fifth of gates at 8.5;
that is the price of a threshold on a measured quantity, and it is why the interval is printed beside the word.
And six families give a rule no better than the one that was rejected.

**Reachability conditions for opening a cell.** A cell is confirmatory only if its recorded pre-analysis shows:
P(`ten-fold`) at least 0.8 at a true ratio of 15; P(`withdrawn`) at most 0.05 at a true ratio of 5; the guard
reachable at its adjusted level; and the horizon cap H ÷ RMT_A on the development suite at least 10, since
R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A whatever the rival does. A cell that fails any of these is opened as `descriptive`, and the report
says so before any sealed run (KILL-TENFOLD-REACH).

**Multiplicity.** At most four confirmatory cells are named per gate (DESIGN). The exact guard is applied with
Holm's step-down procedure at family level 0.05 across them, so the chance that any cell whose median per-family
ratio is at most 5 is awarded the word is at most 5% for the gate as a whole. With four cells the first step needs
18 of 24. All other cells are reported with unadjusted intervals and cannot carry the word.

**Completion and coverage.** For the word, arm A's completion rate by H, and on family strata its share of
mandatory obligations discharged, must be non-inferior to arm B's: the exact one-sided 95% lower bound on the
difference A minus B is above −0.05 (DESIGN margin, frozen before sealing). Rare failures get an interval that is
valid at zero counts: zero misses in 24 independent families still leaves a one-sided 95% upper bound of
1 − 0.05^(1/24) = 11.7% on the miss rate, where a bootstrap of 24 zeros would report zero width.

**The monitor.** One anytime-valid study per cell, declared separately, on the per-family ratios in their
randomized run order. Its null hypothesis is "the median per-family ratio is at least 3". With Y_i = 1 when
ρ_i ≥ 3 and 0 otherwise, the process E_n = Π (1 + λ·(1 − 2·Y_i)) with λ = ½ is a supermartingale under the null, and
the cell is stopped for futility when E_n ≥ 20. Each family below 3 multiplies E by 1.5 and each family at or above
3 multiplies it by 0.5, so eight consecutive families below 3 stop the cell (1.5⁸ = 25.6) and seven do not
(1.5⁷ = 17.1). At the partial gate a futility stop yields `withdrawn` on the monitor's own authority at level 0.05,
and saves the compute of a cell that cannot succeed. At the tournament the stop is disabled: the monitor reports,
and every cell runs to n, because the published record is worth its compute and a tournament is not stopped by an
interim number. The monitor also carries the operational alarms: ledger closure, excluded pairs, a leak signal. No
monitor output enters the fixed-n computation, and a request to compute the fixed-n verdict at a monitor's stopping
time is refused, as §24 refuses it for any study.

**Descriptive reporting, always.** Time-to-acceptance curves per arm; completion probability by H; conditional
times; the per-phase ratio of the two ledgers; every cost column; every false positive, false negative and
abstention; the fastest qualifying baseline and all arms with raw pairings. A per-mission best-of-rivals ensemble
may be shown as an extra lower bound and is labelled as one. Nothing is inferred about systems that were not run.

### 39.8 The ceilings

A speed test is run only after its ceiling has been computed from a measured ledger of the rival. Each ceiling is
arithmetic, and each exists because a tempting comparison ignores it.

| # | Ceiling | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Removing a phase | If a phase holds the share p of the rival's restricted time and a mechanism removes it entirely, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − p). Stated on what remains, q = 1 − p, it is 1/q. Removing half the loop gives 2; removing 85% gives 6.7; ten needs p ≥ 0.9 |
| 2 | Improving a part | If the share f is improved by the factor r and the mechanism adds overhead h, the speed-up is at most 1/(1 − f + f/r + h). The three terms are measured on the rival's critical path |
| 3 | Unremovable cost | If arm A's time has a floor u that no mechanism removes (authoring, the common agent's turns, adjudication, checking, serialization), then R ≤ RMT_B ÷ u. When u exceeds one tenth of the rival's time, a ratio of ten needs another stage changed or the claim changed |
| 4 | Completion | If arm A misses the share q of deadlines, RMT_A ≥ q·H and RMT_B ≤ H, so R ≤ 1/q. With 12% unfinished the ratio cannot exceed 25/3 ≈ 8.33 |
| 5 | Common latency | With the same agent latency t per turn in both arms and a rival cost C per decision, deciding one design per turn gives at most (C + t)/t |
| 6 | The horizon | R ≤ H ÷ RMT_A, because the rival's restricted time cannot exceed H |

**The funding rule.** A mechanism whose removable cost is under 10% of the measured loop cannot be funded as a speed
lever: by ceiling 1 its whole effect is at most 1/0.9 = 1.11. It may still be funded for what it does to trust,
coverage or correctness, with that reason written in its work package and no speed figure attached
(KILL-TENFOLD-LEVER). The rule is applied to the rival's ledger at M1 and again with our own profile at M2.

**The order rule.** A pruning ratio is never multiplied by a speed-up measured on work the pruning already removed.
Each factor in a product of levers is measured on the time that the earlier factors left.

**The refusal.** A registered speed comparison records p or (f, r, h) from a `ledger-closed` run of the strong
baseline, and is refused when its ceiling is below the ratio it sets out to show (GATE-TENFOLD-09). FACT, Appendix
K: in one planning pilot the faster arm's batch held a residual of 575.7 µs that the solver does not touch (parsing,
assembly, checking, serialization and harness, not separately timed), so a solver of zero cost could have reached
about 1.46 at most (842.8 ÷ 575.7); the measured ratios were 1.345 warm and 1.088 cold, and the speed gate of that
pilot failed. A ceiling computed first would have refused the test.

### 39.9 Disqualification

Correctness is a gate and not a term in a score. Two events disqualify, and both apply to every arm alike.

**One false certificate.** A certificate that an arm submitted, that its declared checker accepted, and that the
adjudicator's direct evaluation or any later audit shows false within its stated scope. The arm is disqualified
from the scoped claim: every cell of the gate in which it used that proof kind has no ratio for that arm, and the
verdict of the cell is `disqualified`. For arm A the procedure of §25 follows as well: the proof kind is suspended,
its cells fall to `Tested`, and KILL-AUGUR-FALSECERT decides whether the rule or its implementation was at fault. A
truthful `unknown`, a refused certificate and a certificate with a narrower scope than was asked are not false
certificates; they cost coverage and time.

**Any leak from sealed confirmation worlds.** Any read, by an arm's agent, tools or developers, of sealed content
before the gate closes: a mission card before its clock starts, a sealed world address, a per-world outcome, a
probe point, another arm's transcript. Detection uses the firewall audit of §24 and §37, access logs, and canary
values that the adjudicator plants in sealed worlds and that must never appear in an arm's transcript. The cells
touched by the leak are void for that arm, the families are retired at once, and if the extent cannot be bounded
the whole gate is void and is re-run on a rotated suite. A leak is never repaired by excluding the affected runs
and keeping the rest of the cell.

**Also disqualifying.** A change to a frozen arm after its freeze; use of the adjudicating simulator in development
or tuning; an edited ledger or receipt. A breach of a resource cap is not a disqualification: the run is censored
at the instant of the breach and costs H.

**What a disqualification does not do.** It does not award anything to the other arm. A cell whose rival is
disqualified has no denominator, is reported as such, and is re-run at the next gate.

### 39.13 The tournament at M7

Every stratum of §39.4 that has a product to run, on sealed families that no earlier gate used, with every pin of
every arm and of every sibling frozen for its length (§33), because a ratio of times measured across a pin move is
not one measurement. The order of work is: refresh the rival audit; publish the enlarged development pool, which now
holds the families the partial gate retired; commit the suite and protocol hashes; tune; freeze and publish every
arm; record the pre-analysis and name at most four confirmatory cells; run with the arms interleaved; close and
reveal; publish the report and the kit; and have an external party re-run the kit. DESIGN: adjudication at M7 is
external, or the word is not used until the external replication has passed. No cell of the tournament is stopped
on an interim number. A tournament is voided and re-run only for a breach of protocol: a pin moved during the
freeze, sealed material read by an arm, or an arm changed after its freeze.

The report is one table per cell (R̂, [L, U], the verdict, completion and coverage for each arm, the tie test
against arm C, N* and S for warm cells, the adjudication label) and one phase ledger per arm per cell, with the
ceilings of §39.8 evaluated on the rival's ledger. GATE-TENFOLD-07 passes when that report is published with its
ledgers and its kit, whatever it says.

### 39.14 The replication kit

A gate's result is published with everything needed to recompute it and to re-run it.

- **Contents.** The pre-registration and the pre-analysis. The mission cards, the world generators and the revealed
  keys. The referee protocol and the adjudicator's acceptance code. Every freeze manifest, with the scripts,
  wrappers, tag maps and agent guides it names. Every ledger, receipt and agent transcript. The scorer, which, like
  `frob-verify`, depends on nothing outside the standard library. Hardware fingerprints and disclosure blocks (§21,
  §36). A manifest of hashes over all of it.
- **Level 1, recompute.** From ledgers and receipts alone, the scorer reproduces every published ratio, interval,
  count and verdict, bit for bit, on a clean machine.
- **Level 2, re-run.** From the frozen manifests, on hardware with a comparable fingerprint, one sampled pair per
  stratum is run again for every arm. TARGET: the acceptance outcome is the same and the time is within a factor of
  1.5 in at least 90% of the sampled pairs.
- **Limits.** Incumbent code is not redistributed: the kit pins revisions and gives fetch instructions. A private
  scene or dataset cannot enter a kit, and a kit built from one is refused (§37). Every exported artifact carries
  the no-authorization flags, all false.

### 39.15 What the test does not measure

- **Reality.** Acceptance happens in simulation. The adjudicating simulator is a third model, not the world, and
  every report of this test prints `reality: no-claim` unless a band of §30 covers the mission.
- **How good the best design is.** The clock stops at the first accepted answer. Quality beyond the acceptance
  threshold is not scored, except where a mission states an optimality gap.
- **People.** Both arms are driven by one agent. Human engineering time, learning curves and usability for a person
  are outside the test, and the ratio is conditional on the pinned agent.
- **Money.** Cost columns are reported and no cost claim is made.
- **Systems that were not run.** Nothing is inferred about an incumbent, a version or a configuration outside the
  frozen arms, or about missions outside the sealed population. Manipulation with hands, flight and soft bodies are
  outside the suite.
- **The cost of building either system.** Standing assets for a mission class are charged; the engineering that
  produced FrankenRobotics or an incumbent is not.
- **Step rate, rendering and safety.** Throughput is §36's. Rendering is outside every claim here. Nothing in this
  test is a safety statement about a machine.

### 39.20 Estimating the ratio, and the smallest suite that can certify

**What it is.** [S] The inference rules for the ratio of §2.1, fixed before the sealed suite is opened: what is
estimated, which procedure is invalid, and how many independent mission families a population claim needs.

**Two estimands, never interchanged.** The restricted-time ratio is
R_time = Σᵢ min(T_rival,i, H) ÷ Σᵢ min(T_frob,i, H). The accepted-output rate ratio is
R_rate = (accepted_frob ÷ makespan_frob) ÷ (accepted_rival ÷ makespan_rival). Known answer: 25 serial missions with
H = 100 s; the rival takes 50 s on each (1,250 s, 25 accepted); the candidate finishes 24 in 1 s each and times out
once (124 s, 24 accepted). Then R_time = 1,250/124 = 10.081 and R_rate = (24/124)/(25/1,250) = 9.677. The word
ten-fold attaches to R_time only; a statement about decisions per hour cites R_rate. With parallel jobs the makespan
is the measured campaign makespan under matched resource caps. Each arm's independent acceptance is timestamped
before the joint reveal.

**The unit of inference is the independent mission family.** Missions that share a family, an originating site or a
material batch form one cluster; 24 rows from one site are one site replicate. Connected groups stay together
across the development and sealed splits. The resampling unit, H and the cluster structure are registered with the
suite.

**A procedure that is invalid here.** Let H = 1,000 s, the rival always take 20 s, and the candidate take 1 s on 99%
of families and 999 s on 1%. Both always complete. The population ratio is 20/10.98 = 1.8215. A suite of 24 families
contains only fast missions with probability 0.99²⁴ = 0.78568, and then every percentile-bootstrap resample reports
20. Resampling cannot show a tail that the sample does not contain.

**The test.** For the claim R_time ≥ 10, form the bounded paired contrast
Xᵢ = min(T_rival,i, H) − 10·min(T_frob,i, H), which lies in [−10·H, H]; the claim is E[X] ≥ 0. For n independent
families a distribution-free one-sided lower bound is X̄ − 11·H·√(ln(1/α)/(2n)). The claim is made only when a
registered valid bound is nonnegative.

**How many families a population claim needs.** DESIGN (derived; hand-checkable). If a fraction p of families has
X = −10·H and the rest have X = H, then E[X] = H·(1 − 11p), which is negative for p > 1/11, and a suite of n
families shows no slow family with probability (1 − p)ⁿ. A procedure with one-sided error α that reports a
nonnegative bound on the best possible sample must therefore have (10/11)ⁿ ≤ α, so n ≥ ln(1/α)/ln(1.1): 32 families
at α = 0.05, 46 at α = 0.0125 (four strata under a Bonferroni split), 49 at α = 0.01. The bound of the previous
paragraph needs n ≥ 121·ln(1/α)/2, which is 182 at α = 0.05, even on that best sample. With 24 families, zero slow
missions is compatible at the 5% level with a slow-family rate of 1 − 0.05^(1/24) = 11.7%, and 11 × 0.117 exceeds 1.

**Consequence.** Each stratum declares one of two result types before sealing. A *population result* has at least
the family count that its registered bound requires, and never fewer than ln(1/α)/ln(1.1). A *finite-suite result*
states the ratio on the sealed missions as an exact fact about that suite, with its completion table and its phase
ledger, and carries no population wording. Completion and useful coverage are tested as paired differences against
non-inferiority margins frozen with the suite. A rare binary failure is reported with an interval valid at zero
counts (0 of n gives the upper bound 1 − α^(1/n)); a bootstrap of n zeros has zero width and is not used.

`eval.preanalyze` accepts a ten-fold study plan (n, cluster structure, H, α, result type) and prints the reachable
verdicts. It refuses a population result whose n is below the necessary count (refusal: suite too small for a
population claim). Owner: `frob-arena`; the harness is in `frob-conformance`.

### 39.22 Three arms, the recovery ratio and the phase ablations

**Three arms [S].** One ratio cannot answer the two questions the test is asked: how far this system is ahead of
the incumbent pipeline as it is commonly run, and how much of that lead an incumbent engine recovers once every
portable method is layered over it. Every stratum is therefore run with three arms on the same sealed missions,
acceptance protocol, hardware and resource caps.

| Arm | What it is | Frozen by |
|---|---|---|
| B, the declared incumbent | A leading incumbent simulator with per-design reinforcement learning and a standard outer-loop optimizer | Its own advocates, on development fixtures |
| W, the composed incumbent | The same engine or engines with every portable method layered on: native state restore and forks, per-topology buckets or the engine's own heterogeneous solver, a sampling controller or a multi-embodiment prior with checkpoint resume, racing and stopping, importance-weighted failure search, a second engine as referee | A competent baseline engineer (§2.1), on development fixtures |
| F, this system | The release under test | The release |

With T_X the restricted mean time of arm X on a stratum and C_X its GPU-hours, the **recovery ratio** of the
composed incumbent is

    r_T = (T_B − T_W) / (T_B − T_F)        r_C = (C_B − C_W) / (C_B − C_F)

defined when F beats B on that measure. A value of 1 means the layer recovers the whole saving and the engine adds
nothing on that stratum; 0 means the layer recovers nothing. If T_F = 0.10·T_B, then r_T ≥ 0.8 exactly when
T_W ≤ 0.28·T_B. Three cases are fixed before any measurement. If T_F ≥ T_B the stratum is reported as "no saving to
recover" and r is undefined. If T_W < T_F then r exceeds 1 and the stratum is a loss to the composed incumbent.
A mission unresolved at the deadline H costs H in every arm.

Decision rules (TARGET thresholds, registered before the sealed suite opens):

- r_T ≥ 0.8 and r_C ≥ 0.8 on a stratum: a thin layer over an incumbent is enough there. The report says so and the
  fallback of BET-14 applies to that stratum.
- Both below 0.5: the engine is a source of the gain on that stratum.
- Otherwise the stratum is reported as mixed, with the phase the layer could not recover named from the ledger.
- The word "ten-fold" is governed by F against the stronger of B and W (§2.1), never by F against B alone.

*One receipt first.* FACT (Appendix H): in the incumbent documents read, run-to-run determinism, heterogeneous
worlds and the fastest solver sit on different solver paths, so each is available and their conjunction is not
shown. Arm W is therefore built as one pipeline, on one fixed-topology brief and one mixed-topology contact-rich
brief, before F exists, and the property that binds when all are demanded at once is recorded. One expensive
incompatibility can sink the layer; several rarely exercised gaps may leave it competitive. Feature counts decide
neither.

**Phase ablations [S].** The phase ledger separates three accounts of where a gain comes from. On two briefs (one
narrow wheeled, one contact-rich legged), on arm B's engine, three single-change replays run against the traced
baseline.

1. *Information only.* Batch proposals and a sound cheap diagnostic filter; the same controller acquisition and
   the same solver.
2. *Controller acquisition only.* The same candidates, with per-design training replaced by a qualified cheaper
   controller: a prior with fine-tuning, inheritance, or a delivered sampling controller (§22).
3. *Stepping only.* The same candidates and controllers on a faster solver at matched fidelity.

Each returns its own ratio and the share of the baseline's critical path that it touched. The ablations must
respect two bounds computed from the baseline trace. If training that an intervention does not remove is a share
s of elapsed time, that intervention's ratio is at most 1/s (1.43 at s = 0.7). If an intervention removes that
share entirely its ratio is at most 1/(1 − s) (3.33). Illustration: 50 trained designs at 35 minutes each with 1.5
minutes of agent time per design make training 1,750 of 1,825 minutes, 95.9%, and cap information alone at 1.04.
With controller acquisition at 45 s per design, agent time is 75 of 112.5 minutes, two thirds, and information
becomes the larger lever. Which regime a stratum is in is a measurement, not a premise.

*Owner and verbs.* The protocol is this chapter's; the phase ledger is `frob-chronicle`'s. `report.campaign --arms
B,W,F` prints T, C, r_T and r_C with intervals, and `report.campaign --ablate {information, controller, stepping}`

## 40. The gate register

This chapter is the single list of every gate in the plan. The subsystem chapters define their gates where the
mechanisms are described; the register collects them, gives each one claim and one earliest milestone, lists every
kill gate with its trigger and fallback, and says what each milestone exit must have on record. It contains 32
admission gates and five sibling admission gates, 278 convergence gates with the Gauntlet's own gate and the
thirteen gates of the ten-fold test beside them, 112 kill gates with the seven kill gates of the ten-fold test
beside them, and the program gates. The gates of subsections added after these tables were compiled are indexed in
§40.8. All thresholds are TARGETs until a gate records a measured value.

### 40.1 Semantics

1. **A gate passes only with a recorded measurement against its threshold.** The record is a row of
   `registries/gates.toml` that cites a commit of this repository, the command, its output and a receipt hash, and
   that resolves in the artifact graph of §29. A gate with no record has the status `not run`, which is not a pass.
   Statuses are `not run`, `pass`, `fail`, `killed: fallback adopted` and `blocked: ⟨open question⟩`.
2. **A kill gate (✖) names its trigger and its fallback in advance and is never quietly re-scoped.** Its decision is
   recorded as an ADR at the milestone named here. A trigger, a threshold or a fallback changes only by a dated
   amendment to this register, with its reason, made before the measurement it governs. A kill gate that fires is
   not a failure of the program: the fallback is the plan.
3. **Each gate names exactly one of the seven claims of §8**: Proof, Known answer, Parity, Fidelity, Design
   comparison, Reality or Performance. A row that a chapter labelled "method" or "Performance of the method" is a
   Design comparison, as §8.1 rules. A row that named two claims is given the claim of its governing part, and the
   note under §40.3 says which part that is.
4. **Where a chapter and the register differ, the register governs**: on a gate's identity, its claim, its earliest
   milestone, a kill gate's trigger, fallback and decision point, and on every number the register states. The
   threshold column is a brief. The full pass criterion, with its fixtures and negative controls, is the row in the
   defining subsection, and a clause that the brief leaves out is not waived.
5. **Every convergence gate has a registered negative control that it catches** (GATE-GAUNT-01, §38). A gate whose
   control has not been shown to fail has not passed.
6. **Earliest milestone.** The first milestone at whose exit the gate must have a record. Where two milestones are
   given, a part is due at each, as stated. A milestone exits when every gate with a part due there has a record
   that is a pass or a kill decision with its fallback adopted. A failed convergence gate that has no kill gate
   blocks the exit.
7. **Admission is never inherited** (§33). A foundation first used at a later milestone is admitted then, by its own
   record, at the pin of that day.
8. **Recurring gates.** GATE-GROUND-01, GATE-SAFE-01, GATE-GAUNT-01 and GATE-TENFOLD-09 are re-run at every exit
   after their first.
9. **Owner.** The chapter of the defining subsection owns the gate and its fixtures. Six kill gates are stated in
   two chapters; the register lists each once under its owner and names the other chapter as a consumer (§40.7).
