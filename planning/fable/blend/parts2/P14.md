## 41. Risks and the pre-mortem

This chapter asks how the program fails, names the earliest measurement that would show each failure coming, and
points at the mechanism that answers it. It has three parts: a pre-mortem written from a failed future, a second
pre-mortem for the case where every gate passed and the claim still failed, and the risk register. Labels: every
threshold in a signal or mitigation column is a TARGET repeated from the gate named beside it, and §40 governs where
the two differ; incumbent figures are FACTs registered in Appendix H; counts of crates, milestones and gates are
FACTs about this document (§34.2, §40, §43). Nothing in this chapter is a result.

### 41.1 It is late 2029 and FrankenRobotics failed. Why?

DESIGN: each row is a story told from the failure backwards. The earliest signal is a quantity the program already
records, chosen so that it moves while the failure is still cheap. The countermeasure names the section that owns
it. The residual is what remains after the countermeasure works as designed. Stories 1 to 15 are carried from
version 0.2 with their countermeasures corrected to this version's mechanisms. Stories 16 to 27 are new: five about
the thesis, three about the evidence, four about the program.

Three clusters deserve a sentence each. Stories 16 to 20 are the thesis failing lever by lever, and §2 already says
that no single lever delivers the result; what the plan adds is that each lever has its own early experiment and a
fallback that ships under its own name. Stories 22 to 24 are the evidence failing, and they share a remedy: the
party that proposes a result never checks it. Stories 21 and 25 to 27 are the program failing under its own weight,
and their remedy is structural: M1 is small, the upper tiers are off by default, and the document delegates
progress to beads and registries.

### 41.4 How the register is used

- **Review.** The register is read at every milestone exit by the fresh-context session that records the exit
  (§43.2). For each row the session records whether the earliest signal has fired.
- **Escalation.** A fired signal ends in one of three recorded outcomes: a kill gate decided, an ADR that changes a
  mechanism or a scope, or a written reason why neither applies. A fired signal with no record is a defect of the
  exit review.
- **Growth.** A new risk continues the series. A risk that can no longer occur is tombstoned with the gate that
  retired it and is not renumbered.
- **No risk is mitigated by a hope.** A mitigation column names a mechanism with a gate, a fallback named in advance,
  or an owner decision with a fail-closed default. An upstream work item is never a mitigation by itself; its interim
  posture is (RISK-20).

## 42. Open questions

An open question is a decision the plan has not taken, written so that not taking it is safe. This chapter states
the rule every question obeys, lists the questions only the owner can answer, and then registers every open
question of the plan by subsystem. Labels: every row is OPEN; a number in a default column is a DESIGN default or a
TARGET, never a measurement.

### 42.1 The decision rule

Every OPEN has an owner, a deadline expressed as the gate or milestone it blocks, a fail-closed default, and the
evidence that would decide it. Nothing is decided by silence: at its deadline an undecided question takes its
default, and the default is recorded as an ADR. Five clauses make the rule operational.

1. **A default removes a claim or a capability. It never adds one.** "GPU results are labelled exploratory", "no
   pretraining", "`reality: no-claim`" and "refuse" are defaults. "Assume the catalogue price" is not.
2. **A question is asked one milestone before the gate it blocks.** The owner-input line of each milestone in §43
   lists the questions due. A question that reaches its deadline at its default is reported in the exit review
   with the capability it removed.
3. **Evidence decides where evidence exists.** A question whose row names a gate, a profile or a pilot is decided
   by that measurement and not by preference. Only the questions of §42.2 are decided by the owner's judgement.
4. **A closed question is tombstoned, not reused.** Its identifier stays in the register with the ADR that closed it.
5. **This register adopts the identifiers the chapters defined.** A chapter's text governs the mechanism behind a
   question, and §40 governs any threshold a question touches. Where a chapter named no owner, the lead of its
   subsystem owns the question.

The register holds 147 questions: 141 raised in §12 to §37, of which four are closed, and six at program level.
OPEN-PROG-1 and OPEN-GAUNT-1 are carried from version 0.2. OPEN-PROG-2 to OPEN-PROG-5 are new. The questions that
§38 and §39 raise are adopted under the same rule with the owners and defaults those chapters state:
OPEN-GAUNT-2 to -8 and OPEN-TENFOLD-3 to -9. OPEN-TENFOLD-1 and -2 are OPEN-PROG-2. With these fourteen the
register holds 161.

### 42.4 What the defaults add up to

If every question stayed at its default, the program would still exit M0 to M5 and M7. DESIGN: that is the test a
default must pass before it is written. The resulting product would be a CPU-first studio with exploratory GPU
results, no family policy, cheap rungs that only discard, generic cards and assumed norms, no Reality claim, a
ten-fold ratio labelled `self-adjudicated`, and a private repository. Every one of those limits would be printed on
the results it touches. The owner's answers in §42.2 are therefore the difference between that product and the one
this plan describes, and the six that matter most, in the order they fall due, are OPEN-SPARK-1 and OPEN-SPARK-2
at M0, OPEN-PROG-4 at M1, OPEN-PROG-2 at M2, OPEN-CORTEX-1 at M3 and OPEN-PROG-1 before the pins are frozen for M7.

Four questions are closed. OPEN-SKEL-1 was closed by ADR-SKEL-02 and OPEN-AUGUR-6 by ADR-WS-02; both identifiers
are retired. OPEN-SUITE-2 and OPEN-TETHER-1 were decided by the owner on 2026-11-22: one toolchain T for the whole
build graph, fastmcp_rust brought onto T by FD-FMCP-01, and `frob-mcp` a member of the core graph that runs verbs in
process (§33.13, §32.12.2). Both identifiers stay in the register with the decision. Four defaults are confirmed by
the gate register and are open only to new evidence: the anchored cell of OPEN-NUM-3, the parity tolerance of
OPEN-PERF-1, the stricter threshold pair of OPEN-REFLEX-2, and the single revalidation engine of OPEN-ATEL-4.

## 43. Milestones M0–M8

A milestone is a set of demonstrations with recorded values, not a date. This chapter gives the shape of the
program, the rules for an exit, and one subsection per milestone with the same seven fields: purpose, artifact,
what it demonstrates, acceptance, kill gates decided, owner input wanted, and the decision to stop or re-plan.
Labels: thresholds repeated here are TARGETs and §40 governs them; week counts are DESIGN planning estimates;
North-Star numbers follow §7.

### 43.1 The shape of the program, and why M1 is a CPU studio

The first wedge is chosen for the same reason FrankenMining chose its own: start where value is deliverable with
the fewest unproven dependencies. **M1 is a design studio on the CPU referee tier, with the first mission slice.** It
needs only FrankenSim, asupersync and FrankenSQLite (§33.7). It already demonstrates the thesis in small: a typed
design, static analyses from Lie theory, a synthesized controller, a rollout on the transactional referee,
structured telemetry, and a mission query answered over a family of designs with a certificate that an independent
auditor re-checks, all through one verb registry. GPUs arrive at M2 on top of something that already works.

Three further reasons belong to this version of the plan.

1. **The result is not in the step.** §2.3 shows that an infinitely fast environment step cannot deliver the
   ten-fold ratio. The parts of the thesis that no GPU can rescue (what an answer is, how it is checked, how an
   amendment withdraws it) are exercised at M1 on fixtures whose answers are known exactly (NS-13, NS-22).
2. **The rival is measured while M1 is built.** M0 and M1 time the incumbent loop on `trj` (§2.7), so M2's engine
   work is funded against a measured denominator and not against a published one.
3. **A CPU studio survives every admission failure.** If the GPU, symbolic and proof foundations all failed
   admission, M1 would still ship (§33.7).

The order of evidence follows §2.7: measure the rival (M0, M1), put the mission slice on the CPU (M1), run the
rank-agreement experiment as soon as two rungs exist (a pilot on incumbent tools by M3, on our tiers at M4), hold a
partial ten-fold gate (M3), and run the tournament (M7).

### 43.2 Exit rules, and the dependency firewall in brief

An exit is recorded by a fresh-context session that did not implement the milestone (§38). It requires all of the
following.

- **Every gate of the milestone has a recorded value.** A failing value is a record. A kill gate that fired is
  recorded as an ADR with the fallback now in force, and the capability is described by the fallback's name
  from then on (§0.2).
- **Every demonstration runs from a clean checkout**, through `frob --json` and, where the registry exposes the
  verb to agents, through `frob-mcp`.
- **GATE-GAUNT-01 holds**: every gate that passed has a registered negative control that it catches.
- **Every report prints its `reality:` line** (GATE-GROUND-01, from M1 onward).
- **The risk register has been read** (§41.4), and the open questions due have been answered or have taken their
  defaults (§42.1).

A gate may pass before its milestone. It may not move later without an ADR. §40 fixes each gate's milestone and
threshold; the lists below name the key gates, and where a list and the register differ, the register governs. A
milestone does not exit on a count of gates. Its exit line in §43.1 is the observable. An admission is never
inherited: a foundation or an airlock group first used at a milestone is admitted at that milestone, by its own
record, at the pin of that day (§33.6). At most four [M] items are active at once in any milestone (D-6).

The dependency firewall is detailed in §33.7. In brief:

### 43.3 M0: spine and airlock

- **Purpose.** Retire the risks that could invalidate the architecture or the thesis, with the smallest possible
  amount of code. Two questions are answered by measurement: can the foundations be consumed from outside their
  trees, and is the rival's loop what §2.2 composes from published figures. No subsystem is built out.
- **Artifact.**
  - `frob doctor` lists the admitted foundations with their records and the stack receipt.

    flags exist in `frob-core`, with compile-fail tests.

## 46. The first implementation session

Before anything else, in this order. Each step produces an observation, not a scaffold. Steps 1 to 5 and step 8 are
carried from version 0.2 with the settings §33 found necessary; steps 6 and 7 are new and exist because this
version's thesis rests on a measured rival and on exact known answers. Bead numbers are those of §45. Labels: the
expected values in step 7 are known answers (Appendix M); the incumbent figure quoted in step 6 is a FACT from
Appendix H; nothing in this chapter is a measurement until the session records it.

Working rules for the session (§34.7): every build goes through the offload shim; every run stays inside the
default envelope of 16 GiB, 8 cores, one hour and one device; a sibling repository is changed only as an upstream
work item, in that repository, under its own rules and with its files reserved (§33.8); a failing result is recorded
as failing, and no step is repeated until it happens to pass.

1. **Create the core workspace** with one `rust-toolchain.toml` that names candidate (a) of §33.13 as the
   provisional T, and `franken-stack.lock` (BD-001). Repeat at the workspace root the three things Cargo does not
   propagate from a path dependency: the asupersync patch entry, the FrankenSQLite profile overrides, and the

   resolution receipt, with the drift class of every sibling.

   check times, peak memory and package count that INT-FSIM-01 asks for. This is the measurement BET-04 stands
   on. Perturb one input and confirm that the digest changes. Then step the model for two seconds on the referee
   and record the final digest.
3. **Count runtimes and compilers, and build fastmcp_rust on T.** Run `cargo tree -d` and record how many
   asupersync packages the build contains (BD-008). Record which of the nightlies in play compile `fs-qty` alone:

## 47. Definition of done and the release rubric

This chapter says when the program has delivered, and what must be true before anything is released on the way.
Labels: every clause is a DESIGN statement of acceptance; the gates named beside a clause carry its thresholds
(TARGETs, §40); no clause requires a particular measured number except where its gate does.

### 47.1 Definition of done

The program has delivered when each clause is true and its gate has a recorded value. A clause whose mechanism
fell back at a kill gate is true when the fallback is in force, recorded as an ADR, and described by its own name.

- **The spine is real.** Every construction C-01 to C-40 has its type, its verb, its failing-when-wrong test and
  its measured payoff, or has been moved to Appendix C with the reason (D-2).
- **The referee is real.** Reference robots simulate on the referee tier under the transactional, event-located
  step, with known answers and parity recorded, and with coverage reported per robot class and an adjudicator
  named wherever coverage is under 80% (GATE-KIN-01, -02, -04; GATE-FID-03).
- **The batch is real.** One kernel source runs on the CPU, CUDA and Metal. The divergence ledger explains every
  difference. A world's trajectory does not depend on the batch it ran in (GATE-SPARK-15). Throughput and
  training time are measured against the incumbent on the same cards and disclosed, whatever they are
  (GATE-SPARK-03, -04, -21; GATE-CORTEX-05).
- **The families are real.** Many different robots run in one batch, and each world reproduces the digest of its
  single-design run (GATE-PHYLUM-02). The head-to-head is published with all four arms and the plan has acted on
  its outcome (GATE-PHYLUM-03). A family policy decides designs without per-design training wherever its rank
  agreement was measured, or KILL-FAMILY is on record and specialists with inheritance stand in its place
  (GATE-CORTEX-11, -13).
- **The ladder is real.** A planted exploit is caught by promotion, and no report cites a provisional result as
  established (GATE-FID-02). Rank agreement between rungs is on record per morphology class and task family, a
  rung without a record is refused, and a screen has been audited by sampling what it discarded and by planting
  a known winner (GATE-CORTEX-12, GATE-ATEL-07, -09).
- **The places are real.** Seven interior archetypes and the terrain families generate valid, reproducible
  scenes with feasibility tags (GATE-HAB-02). A clearance complex returns a route witness or an impossibility
  cut (GATE-HAB-10). One real place has been ingested privately, or the absence of a capture is recorded as
  `no-claim` (GATE-HAB-03).
- **The adversary is real.** Search inside legality bounds finds planted traps faster than randomization, every
  reported failure carries a solvability status, a shrunk world replays with identical digests, and a stated
  failure-rate bound covers (GATE-NEMESIS-02, -03, -04).
- **The autopsy is real.** A planted cause ranks first, a planted chaotic failure is given no cause, and a
  design-parameter intervention is re-run from the episode start (GATE-AUTOPSY-03, -05, -06). The effect on an
  agent's repair turns is measured and reported, whichever way it falls (GATE-AUTOPSY-08).
- **The mission engine is real.** A mission query returns inner and outer design sets with witnesses, exclusions
  and an unresolved remainder. Every `Certified` cell is accepted by `frob-verify` built from an empty
  environment on a machine with no GPU, and every registered mutation is rejected. An amendment withdraws
  exactly what it must (GATE-AUGUR-02 to -06, -12; GATE-LEMMA-03, -04).
- **The studio is real.** A fresh agent with only `robot-docs` designs, checks, simulates, explains, edits,
  compares and qualifies a robot inside its budget, through a CLI and an MCP surface generated from one registry
  (GATE-TETHER-01, -02, -13). Qualification holds its error rate under adaptive retries (GATE-QUAL-01).
- **The record is real.** Any world replays alone from its address. The energy ledger closes to its stated
  residual. Every claim in the gate registry resolves to evidence whose hashes recompute, and retention removes
  no pinned object (GATE-CHRON-01, -03, -10, -12).
- **The ground is real.** Every report prints its reality line. Predictions are frozen before they are compared.
  A band is issued only inside a calibrated family and refused everywhere else. Where no data was admitted the
  line reads `no-claim`, and that is recorded as the state and not as a pass (GATE-GROUND-01, -05, -06; -10 as an
  import lane).
- **The foundry is real.** A qualified design yields a `.fbuild` packet whose hashes match, whose geometry round
  trips, whose tolerance stack closes, and whose four flags are present and false; or the reduced packet of
  KILL-PACKET is in force (GATE-FOUNDRY-08, -09, -10).
- **The ten-fold result is published, whatever it is.** The report of §39 exists: per stratum, the ratio of
  restricted mean times with its interval, cold and warm separately, the crossover reuse count, a phase ledger
  for each arm, the certificate audit and the tie statement. Its wording follows BET-13: the word ten-fold
  appears only where the decision rule confirms it, a ratio between 3 and 10 is described by its value, and
  under 3 the product is described by what it lets a reader audit.
- **The honesty is real.** Every result carries its claim product: the kind of claim, the evidence class set by
  the weakest input, tier, determinism class, model identity, seed, and the no-authorization flags, all false.
  Every gate has a caught negative control. `reality: no-claim` is printed wherever it is true. A mechanism that
  fell back is described by the fallback's name. The word "verified" describes no result (GATE-SAFE-01, -08;
  GATE-LEMMA-06; GATE-GAUNT-01).
- **The foundations are respected.** No sibling was forked without a recorded kill decision, and none was patched
  locally: every change to a sibling was made in its own repository, under its own rules, as an upstream work item
  with a bead here and a mirrored bead in its home tracker. The whole build graph is on one toolchain T. Every
  admission record cites a commit whose lockfile contains the foundation (§33).

Three things are not required for done. No [M] item is required: the plan works without every one of them. No
particular ratio is required: a published ratio of 2 with an honest ledger satisfies the clause, and an
unpublished ratio of 12 does not. No calibration against hardware is required: a recorded `no-claim` satisfies
the ground clause.

### 47.2 Rules for any release

A release is anything a user outside the implementing swarm is told they can use. Nine rules apply to every one.

1. **Implemented means §0.2.** Each capability in the release meets all eight conditions of §0.2, or is listed as
   partial with the missing conditions named.
2. **No demo, no release.** Each release path has a `demos/<demo_id>/demo.yaml` whose claims exist in
   `registries/gates.toml` and whose commands finish in under 60 seconds on the CPU tier (TARGET, §34.12). A demo
   shows that a path runs; the numbers come from the gate.
3. **The status table is generated.** The README's honest-status table is produced from the gate registry. A
   failing gate appears as failing, and a fallback appears under the fallback's name.
4. **Each cell names its capability and its limit.** No capability is described more broadly than the fixture
   that qualified it (§25.23). "Exact static family with independent active vertical reactions" is a release
   cell; "mission synthesis" is not.
5. **A losing experiment reproduces as readily as a winning one.** The release ships the commands, addresses and
   seeds for at least one result that went against the plan.
6. **Performance comes with its disclosure block**, and any ratio against an incumbent with the protocol of §39.
7. **Every exported artifact carries the flags**, all false, and nothing in the release documentation contradicts
   them.
8. **The exit review was done by a fresh-context session** (§43.2), and its record is part of the release.
9. **Reality is stated.** Every report in the release prints `reality: no-claim` or names its calibrated cell.

### 47.4 The order in which evidence is procured, and complete dispositions

**What it is.** [S] Two rules for §47.2, and the check that enforces the first. They add to the rubric of §47.3 and
change none of its rows.

**Evidence is procured in dependency order.** DESIGN: every release cell names the kind of evidence it rests on, and
a cell is never scheduled ahead of the kind it needs. There are four kinds, in order.

| Kind | What it is | Available |
|---|---|---|
| Authorable at once | Analytic fixtures, exact checkers, certificates, CPU runs on the referee and on `batch-cpu` | From M0 |
| Pinned external comparison | An incumbent re-measured on the same hardware under the disclosure block of §36.7 | From the M0 phase ledger |
| Operator or dataset | Bench logs, scans of real places, proficiency-matched operator trials | When the import lane has an admitted dataset (§30) |
| Native device | A completed run on the device family that the cell names, with a placement receipt that shows no fallback (§21.12) | When that device ran it |

A successful CPU run on the M5 Ultra, or in a shell session on `trj`, establishes the CPU path and nothing about a
device path. A cell that names CUDA or Metal therefore needs a receipt whose actual backend equals the requested one
for every stage it claims. Counterexample: a step plan in which one stage fell back to the host produces correct
results and a receipt; a release cell that cites it for device throughput describes a path that did not run.

**Tied, infeasible, incomparable and inconclusive are complete dispositions.** A study that ends in one of them has
delivered its result. The release prints it under that name, with the evidence that produced it, and no release
note rewords it as a win, a loss or work in progress. Reason: a rubric that counts only decisive outcomes pays for
overstated ones.

`xtask check-contracts` reads the release manifest and, for each cell, verifies the kind of evidence it declares,
the class of each cited receipt and, for a device cell, the equality of requested and actual backend on every stage
(refusal: release cell lacks evidence of the kind it declares). Owner: `xtask`, with the manifest schema in
