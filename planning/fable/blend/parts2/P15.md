## 45. The beads

These are the seed for conversion with `br`. Titles name the artifact. "Done when" is one observable, usually with a
negative control: a planted failure the bead must catch. Beads of M0 to M5 are tasks. Beads of M6, M7 and M8 are
epics, one per frontier item and one group for the ten-fold tournament; an epic is split into tasks when its milestone
opens, and part of its done-condition is that a short plan exists and its pilot has run (§0.7). Each milestone ends
with an exit review performed by a fresh-context session that did not implement the work. Every dependency points to a
lower-numbered bead. Every bead inherits §0.2 and the false-progress guards of §38.

How to read a row (DESIGN):

- **A gate identifier in "Done when"** means the gate's recorded value together with its caught negative control, as
  defined in the chapter that owns the gate and listed in §40. "Rows of", "on the referee" and "for the M2 verbs"
  restrict the bead to the part of the gate that can be measured when the bead closes. The gate's PASS is recorded
  when the last bead that cites it closes. Admission gates (INT-…) are read the same way, with the admission record of
  §33.6 as the value.
- **"Decided" after a kill gate** means that its trigger was evaluated on measured values and that the outcome, either
  way, is recorded as an ADR. A kill gate that fires replaces the bead's capability by the fallback named in advance.
  The bead then closes on the fallback, and its dependents are re-read against it (§45.11).
- **Numbers in a done-condition** are TARGET thresholds or exact known answers taken from the owning chapters. None is
  a measurement.
- **Placement.** A bead sits in the milestone that §43 assigns to its mechanism. Where a gate table names a different
  earliest milestone than the bead that closes the gate, §45.10 lists the difference, and the value is recorded when
  the bead closes.
- **Upstream work is bead work.** A change to a sibling is a bead of this graph with the label `upstream`, done in the
  sibling's repository under its own rules and mirrored as a bead in the sibling's tracker (§33.8). BD-037 opens the
  upstream work items of Appendix F, the two P0 items are part of BD-008 and BD-023, and every bead that stands on a
  sibling has an interim posture that holds until the upstream change lands.
- **Numbering.** The list is numbered from BD-001 so that the numbering is a topological order. BD-001 to BD-024 keep
  the subjects they had in version 0.2, with corrected content, so that §46 reads against the same numbers. Numbers
  above BD-024 are new assignments.

### 45.11 Conversion rules

Each row becomes one `br` issue: a task for M0 to M5 and for every exit review, an epic for the other rows of M6 to
M8. The BD number is kept as the issue's reference, "Depends on" becomes its blocking dependencies, and labels carry
the milestone, the owning crate of §34.2, the tier and the work package. `bv --robot-*` triages the result (§34.7).
The rules below are DESIGN.

- **What a bead inherits.** Every bead inherits the definition of "implemented" (§0.2) for the capability it delivers,
  and the false-progress guards of §38: typed close reasons, negative controls, live-dependency proofs, the
  `synthetic` label, behaviour and not plumbing, no self-certification. A bead that delivers part of a capability
  states which clauses of §0.2 stay open, and the capability is described as partial until the bead that closes its
  gate is closed.
- **How a bead closes.** With a typed reason that cites its own test and that test's output: a line in its log
  `docs/delivery/logs/<bead>.ndjson` (`frob.beadlog/1`, §29.14) whose verdict is `pass`, whose claim identifiers
  resolve in the artifact graph, and whose recording session is not the session that implemented the bead. A close
  reason shared by several beads is a failure signal, and the exit review rejects it. A closed bead is not acceptance
  (§0.7); acceptance is the gate's row in `registries/gates.toml`.
- **Kill gates.** A bead whose kill gate fires closes with the reason `fallback` and the name of the fallback. Its
  dependents are then re-read: one that needs the killed capability closes with the reason `superseded` and the
  identifier of the ADR; one that can stand on the fallback keeps its dependency.
- **Work packages.** Work packages (§44) group beads by crate and milestone. A bead belongs to exactly one: the
  package that owns its crate at its milestone or, where a bead touches several crates, the package that owns the
  crate in which its gate is measured. Exit reviews belong to none.
- **Pilots and compute.** A bead for an [F] or [M] mechanism is not ready until the pilot of its chapter has a
  recorded result (§0.7). A bead whose run exceeds the machine envelope of §34.7 (the parity baselines, training, the
  head-to-head, the rank-agreement experiment, the partial gate, the tournament) names its budget and is not ready
  until the owner's approval under OPEN-SPARK-2 is recorded.
- **Splitting and adding.** A bead too large for one session is split into children that carry its number with a
  suffix (BD-nnn.1, BD-nnn.2). The children inherit its dependencies, and the parent closes when its last child
  closes. A bead discovered during implementation takes the next free number and depends only on existing beads, and
  the exit review of its milestone gains it as a dependency. That is the one case in which a dependency may point to a
  higher number. After conversion the guarantee against cycles is `br`'s cycle check, not the numbering.
- **Exit reviews.** An exit review implements nothing. It closes when every gate of its milestone has a recorded value
  and a caught negative control in `registries/gates.toml` (GATE-GAUNT-01), every kill gate due at the milestone has
  its ADR, and the honest-status table regenerates from the logs.
