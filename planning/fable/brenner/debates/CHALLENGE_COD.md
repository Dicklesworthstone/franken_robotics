## What would change the contested verdicts

### H-01 · The acceptance crossover depends on the controller budget

**Evidence.** The pack is persuasive for continuous full-order sampling under the assumed settings. A 20 s episode at 20 GPU-s costs 5,980 GPU-s for 299 confirmations, against roughly 600 GPU-s for one quadruped training run before evaluation. If a budgeted controller achieves matched decision quality for under 600/299 = **2.01 GPU-s per episode**, the same comparison changes even before adding specialist evaluation. Cheaper class-specific rollouts, static tasks and CPU/GPU allocation have different crossover points. A held-out packet for a frozen MPC configuration can be valid within simulation. Its cost and error decide eligibility. DIAL-MPC’s demonstrated embodiments and the pack’s single-world result help calibrate the high-cost case. [H-01 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-01.md) · [DIAL-MPC results](https://lecar-lab.github.io/dial-mpc/)

**Cheapest test.** One native MJX or Warp controller with two fixed sample/horizon budgets, a Go2 brief and a wheeled docking brief. Measure cost per 20 s episode and held-out failure outcomes, sharing the same observation limits and task contract with a cached or warm-started policy. Drop any arm that cannot cross the measured cost threshold at the required quality. Escalate only a candidate within budget to the full acceptance sample.

### H-04 · The Amdahl bound applies to the profiled step

**Evidence.** The cited trace has 53.13 ns of affected phases in a 262.33 ns step, so its transferable fact is a **20.25% share for that trace**. The absolute ceiling there is 1.254×. Even 1.2× requires roughly 5.65× acceleration of those phases. A 1.5× gain with a 3× phase speedup needs a 50% share. A low-contact step or a universal step with unusually expensive fixed-model transforms can meet a different share. This is a conditional reason to preserve the experiment, while holding padding fixed so H-03 earns its own gains. Code-generation and compile overhead still make a full e-graph investment unjustified. [H-04 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-04.md) · [MuJoCo Warp trace](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp/pull/1119)

**Cheapest test.** Profile an unpadded universal quadruped step on one backend when it exists. If the measured affected share is below one third, the original 1.5× whole-step target is impossible for that slice even with zero phase cost. If the share clears a realistic threshold, compare a manually folded/unrolled version on identical contact and solver settings. Charge cold compile and warm throughput, and apply the 60 s and 1.2× falsifiers. No equality-saturation machinery is needed for the decision.

### H-06 · Coupled constraints can admit large boxes

**Evidence.** The 10⁴-cell calculation bounds the resolution of an isotropic grid. An anisotropic partition can exploit large differences in variable sensitivity. On a normalized 20-variable box, let δ = 0.01 × mean(2x₂−1, …, 2x₂₀−1)² and require both x₁ + δ ≤ 0.8 and 1 − x₁ + δ ≤ 0.8. Both constraints depend on 20 variables and are nonmonotone in the other 19 across the box. The enclosure 0 ≤ δ ≤ 0.01 certifies x₁ between 0.21 and 0.79 as feasible and x₁ outside [0.2, 0.8] as infeasible. Five slabs decide **98%** of the volume, up to boundary measure zero. This is a counterexample to the generic dimensional verdict. It gives no evidence that self-clearance and reach in a particular robot have useful bounds. Count downstream evaluations avoided and return unresolved cases. [H-06 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-06.md)

**Cheapest test.** Use the proposed 12-parameter static quadruped task. Compare anisotropic splitting with outward width estimates and per-constraint decomposition against the same strong leaf constraints. Include a tightly coupled clearance case as a trapped negative control and independently check any claimed exclusion. If the extra complexity removes few candidate evaluations or leaves over 90% unresolved at the stated budget, keep only the small constraint checks.

### H-07 · A defensive mixture controls weights and can still miss a mode

**Evidence.** The pack’s proposed 10–100× gain is more optimistic than its cited evidence. One driving study reports 2–20×. Its temporal-monitor example estimates 3.5×10⁻⁴ against a nominal reference of 4.8×10⁻⁵ with large spread. A defensive mixture with nominal weight ε = 0.1 bounds the importance weight by 10. If an omitted failure mode gets only that defensive mass, then 0 failures in N proposal draws give an upper bound of about 3/(0.1N) on that mode’s nominal probability. To push that below 10⁻⁵ can require about **3 million** draws, 10× the crude zero-count requirement. That worst-case calculation applies to the omitted mode. Conversely a frozen scene-parameter proposal can be valid and efficient **without** snapshot branching. Per-step noise matters to branch reuse. [H-07 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-07.md) · [Temporal-monitor splitting results](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15771v2) · [Driving evaluation results](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00145)

**Cheapest test.** Plant two equi-probable, disjoint failure mechanisms totaling 10⁻⁵ on a cheap deterministic evaluator. Make one a rare static patch arrangement and the other a timed perturbation. Fit a proposal on separate seeds, freeze it including its defensive mass, and compare empirical interval coverage and cost to a crude bound over repeated draws. Kill the gain claim on poor coverage or under 10× cost savings at matched interval width. Keep discovery even if certification falls back to crude sampling.

### H-08 · Stability is about the intervention effect

**Evidence.** The cited Lyapunov result is for particular Walker/Cheetah closed-loop systems. The extrapolation to an 80-tick robotics fork imports both its exponent and a remembered 25 ms clock. That is a useful stress scenario. It cannot require double precision for every diagnosis. Precision also cannot rescue a physically inadmissible geometry edit. A temporary change to an external command or material condition can start from the same valid prefix by construction, even if a permanent design change requires a reset. Prefer a scoped contrast with a flip rate and explicit refusal. [H-08 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-08.md) · [Young and Pugeault’s measured systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10674v1)

**Cheapest test.** A same-backend placebo fork, a late injected command intervention with a large planted effect, and a marginal contact case. Repeat FP32 and FP64 on matched initial perturbations. If effect sign and repair order hold on the robust case but disagree in the marginal case, admit the FP32 robust lane and escalate marginal cases. Treat a changed body’s dynamically incompatible snapshot as a distinct negative control.

### H-09 · Account separately for invalidated runs and avoided reacquisition

**Evidence.** H-09 explicitly includes warm starts; the pack’s 10% ceiling assumes exact artifact reuse only, with rollouts at least 90% of work. Even its own compiled example gives 95.91/(95.91 + 240) ≈ **28.6%** for the compile share in a *cold illustrative* workload and 0.89/(0.89 + 240) ≈ **0.37%** when warm. Neither estimates a typical edit corpus. Under a 90%-training workload, a warm start avoiding 80% of training iterations could avoid 72% of all work before any caching benefit, provided the wall-clock cost follows the steps and the child meets the same quality. That is precisely the unmeasured H-21 condition. Do not count this factor twice. Exact prefix reuse remains disallowed when the old trajectory was affected by the parameter change. [H-09 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-09.md) · [H-21 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-21.md)

**Cheapest test.** Tally one frozen edit lineage with mass, mount, controller-only and mission-only changes. In a dependency trace, label separately exact reuse, certified evidence carried forward, and reacquisition avoided. Price cold and warm runs separately. Compare scratch with cache-only and with cache plus one warm start, charging qualification. Apply the original <30% falsifier to the declared total-work estimand and publish the exact-reuse figure beside it.

### H-11 · Coverage is in world-seconds and usefulness

**Evidence.** The evidence pack divides steps/s for a 5 ms CPU humanoid and a GPU reference with different or unspecified conditions. A world-seconds ratio requires multiplying each by its simulated step time and using equivalent worlds. The plan’s own illustrative envelope at a 2 ms, 500k-step/s humanoid target is 1,000 simulated world-seconds/s. A 0.2× referee on 8 cores supplies only 1.6 world-seconds/s, or **0.16%** under perfect scaling. Thirty-six cores would yield **0.72%** on that same illustrative workload before contention, snapshot admission and policy inference. These are upper budgets under the chosen rates. Useful detection delay also depends on what the snapshot branches can observe and how they were selected. [H-11 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-11.md) · [Plan §28](../../../../COMPREHENSIVE_PLAN_FOR_FRANKEN_ROBOTICS.md)

**Cheapest test.** Measure matched one-second snippets on an incumbent shadow pair with the actual synchronization and CPU budget. Plant one repeatable tier discrepancy and one placebo. Record admitted independent snippet coverage, detection lag and foreground throughput change. Keep H-11 only in a budget where it catches the planted difference without breaching the 5% throughput limit. Use completed buffers or explicit readback on either hardware.

### H-12 · One quadruped’s component shares are insufficient for a mass cap

**Evidence.** The reported XDog example has 4 kg of legs in a 15 kg robot. Removing 15% of that *entire* mass would save 4% of the total. It is an illustration under one assumed saving and one maximum eligible mass, not an upper bound for all robot classes. Even applying the pack’s 30% example to the same 4/15 share gives 8%. The actual eligible link share could be much smaller. A 15% link-mass change can also alter moving inertia without a large whole-robot mass change, which makes a dynamic rerun relevant. Sibling source shows bounded optimization and fatigue primitives. It leaves load-case extraction, physically valid attachments, and end-to-end convergence to the project. [H-12 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-12.md) · [Published arm case](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26643v1)

**Cheapest test.** Inspect the mass breakdown of one quadruped and one carrier and price a single load-bearing link against a hollow section at equal margins. Use the same port interface, independent checking and a rerun with revised inertia. Reject the quality pilot if the original mass/convergence falsifiers fire. Keep it outside the time-to-decision argument unless matched structural acceptance is added to both pipelines’ task contract.

### H-16 · The local-coupling hypothesis needs a local test

**Evidence.** Full-room LBM is a valid exclusion for that architecture. The slate asks about sparse islands only when 5% of worlds are active. On the pack’s serial weighted-work model, a local active world costing 100× a rigid world gives 0.95 + 0.05×100 = **5.95×** cost, or about **16.8%** of rigid throughput. The 10% throughput gate allows at most 181× *under that model*. GPU divergence, state movement and scheduling can cut the realized ratio, so bucket the islands and measure it. Production support can still fail on sibling readiness or lack of differentiation because incumbent coupling already exists. The pack itself records proxy adequacy as untested. Treat a model that can reproduce cord entanglement or a flexible rug jam as a potential high-tier oracle. [H-16 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-16.md) · [Newton solver capabilities](https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/latest/solvers/index.html)

**Cheapest test.** The cord-across-path fixture on Newton VBD already proposed in the pack. Compare a rigid-chain proxy and a coupled cable on the same wheel/cord geometry, approaching across several seeded angles. Blind-label stop, wheel wrap and escape, and measure 5%-active throughput including handoff. Drop the island’s decision value if the proxy meets the original 90% agreement threshold under its declared tolerances. If it misses decisive failures, preserve off-fast-path escalation while native coupling stays deferred.

### H-18 · Training-dominated examples leave the third alternative open

**Evidence.** Published single-design training times and co-design budgets are priors. They miss agent wait, failed trials, and held-out acceptance on the same brief. A fixed phase budget makes the competing stories explicit. If 70% of elapsed critical path is training that diagnostics do not remove, deleting every other phase gives at most 1/0.70 = **1.43×**. If stepping alone somehow eliminated that entire 70%, the ceiling would be **3.33×**. Which numerator changes depends on the intervention. Avoiding training ten candidates changes the count of expensive phases. Faster physics changes the rate within one phase, usually leaving updates, observations and orchestration. H-23’s published population work shows that changing search schedule can alter the count, but does not establish a universal winner. The register has no H-18 evidence pack and no trace of a complete matched mission. [Hypothesis slate](../hypothesis_slate.md) · [H-09 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-09.md) · [H-23 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-23.md) · [Loki co-design study](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21665)

**Cheapest test.** Trace one narrow wheeled brief and one contact-rich legged brief on the strongest incumbent you can commission. Keep timestamps for proposal, build, controller, evaluation, idle/queue and acceptance, with the critical path identified. Against the same backend and controller, replay the candidate plan under batch proposals and a sound cheap diagnostic filter. In a separate arm, give fixed candidates/controllers a faster solver only if fidelity matches. First test whether training unchanged by the information intervention consumed at least 70% of baseline wall time and whether the seed’s <2× engine-only claim survives. Pre-register the joint budgets before running a full study.

### H-19 · The wrapper needs an end-to-end cost comparison

**Evidence.** The source audit supports the initial challenger. MuJoCo has restorable CPU state, some Newton solvers expose same-backend determinism, and Kamino supports heterogeneous worlds. Timing those capabilities separately does not show that one composed solver provides all three on the accepted workload. The local pack lists determinism and heterogeneous support on different solver paths, and Newton documents solver-specific limits. One expensive incompatibility can push the wrapper past its cost threshold, and three rarely exercised gaps may leave it competitive. A Metal advantage is separate from the matched CUDA thesis. To make 80% meaningful, define recovered savings against the declared PPO baseline B. For elapsed time let the new engine cost F and the wrapper cost W. With F < B, require (B − W)/(B − F) ≥ 0.8, and analogously for compute. If F = 0.10B, then **W ≤ 0.28B** recovers 80% of that metric’s saving. Define unresolved or equal-B cases before measuring. [H-19 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-19.md) · [Newton determinism by solver](https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/latest/concepts/determinism.html) · [Kamino](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16536)

**Cheapest test.** Build one incumbent studio slice first, using native state restore, tuned per-topology batching and a competent warm-started or sampling controller. Run one fixed-topology family and one genuinely heterogeneous mixed-contact brief. Apply the same held-out evidence contract and charge all adapters and fallback runs. A first matched receipt can reveal a single binding incompatible property. Once the native slice exists, compare B, W and F under the same contracts. Keep the wrapper as the default CUDA path if it recovers the predeclared benefit on both costs at matched quality, subject to the same qualification constraints. Treat the isolated backend spikes from the pack as diagnostics.

### H-21 · Transfer examples do not qualify a default or reset schedule

**Evidence.** The 1–20% range pools pretraining and transfer examples with different denominators. In the pack’s H-Zero example, 2,000/50,000 = 4%. The pack itself allows 20,000 as an equal-return scratch stopping time, making the same ratio 10%. The 5,000-epoch adaptation becomes 25% under that comparison. A cross-humanoid pretrain also differs from inheriting the actual parent after a sequence of typed edits. Evidence of policy plasticity loss warrants measurement, while fixed periodic resets can waste an otherwise useful donor. [H-21 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-21.md) · [H-Zero](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00971) · [URMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06366v4)

**Cheapest test.** Start with one three-edit mass/gear/length chain and one single-limb edit. Compare same-return scratch, raw checkpoint warm start, and the edit-aware donor with matched seeds and observations. Include a minimum terminal quality and count evaluations, wall time and failed children. Admit parent transfer for an edit class only if it beats the strongest existing warm start or training-free option in realized cost. Escalate from suspect lineage on a measured held-out regression before introducing a reset schedule.

### H-22 · Separate rank selection from estimation and test each directly

**Evidence.** The 0.40 number is a critic predicting returns of its *own* shared policy, rather than ranking independent specialists. No direct specialist-ranking evidence supports a prescribed retention fraction. Even a high top-region Kendall τ can miss the winner. For 40 candidates, move the true #1 to cheap rank #3 and keep all other relative orders. Within the true top ten this gives two inversions of 45, hence τ = 1 − 4/45 ≈ **0.911**, while keeping two candidates (5%) drops the winner. Separately, for normalized unit expensive variance, a fitted control variate with free cheap samples leaves 1 − ρ². The ideal gains are **1.96× at 0.70**, **2.78× at 0.80**, and **5.26× at 0.90** before sample and transfer cost. Which ρ matters depends on the **paired design contrast or risk outcome** and on what changes between rungs. No a priori 2× follows. [H-22 evidence](../evidence/packs/EV-pack-H-22.md) · [Source of the 0.40 proxy](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00702)

**Cheapest test.** Reuse the first transfer pilot’s frozen candidates with both a shared controller and a proven specialist as cross-arms on the fast and referee tiers. First generate the 2×2 table of controller and solver changes on the same addresses. Report top-k recall, accepted-candidate regret, and the best missed design for the screen. Independently replay paired contrasts to price the two-level estimator at equal achieved precision with pilots and abstentions included. A reversal kills an aggressive discard rule even if global τ is high. Poor residual variance kills the variance lever without deciding the screen.

## Two hypotheses missing from the slate

### Proposed H-25 · Bound embodiment potential before paying to acquire a controller

**Claim.** On a held-out set of mass-, power- or payload-limited ramp and docking missions, controller-independent upper bounds and validated executable-policy lower bounds reduce controller-acquisition cost per accepted design by at least 3×. A 10× controller-stage gain is possible where bound-based exclusions dominate. H-06 certifies static constraints and H-17 proposes a solution. Neither tests whether *any* admissible controller could clear the same acceptance objective and uses that gap to allocate controller effort.

**Mechanism.** In `frob-opt` and `frob-analyze`, freeze an objective and controller information contract. For each design and scenario, build an optimistic relaxation whose feasible set contains every admissible trajectory. Begin with integrated energy and reach bounds for known elevation, payload, deadline, and initial/final state, with named stored or environmental energy inputs. An outward wrench or power relaxation can add coverage. Label what each bound assumes and never substitute one contact schedule for all policies. A feasible frozen controller yields a lower bound on attainable utility when evaluated on independent scenarios with stated confidence. Only discard when the upper bound excludes the threshold or, for an explicitly comparative objective, cannot beat an established lower bound. Otherwise direct the next training or search budget to the unresolved gap. Use the plan’s units, port-energy records and scene geometry. [Primary work on bounds via optimal-control relaxation](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703377)

**Falsifier.** Exhaust a small planar ramp example with one planted feasible design needing a nonstandard controller and one impossible under its power contract. A false exclusion kills the bound. Extend only to a frozen held-out family. Kill the lever below 1.5× controller-cost reduction at fixed acceptance or if computed brackets almost never change a design decision.

**Expected evidence.** Machine-replayable bound witnesses that survive the planted false-bound control, measured lower bounds from frozen controllers, an unresolved fraction and matched costs against static leaf checks plus tuned controller acquisition. Separate true infeasibility from controller failure.

### Proposed H-26 · Compile physically meaningful common random numbers

**Claim.** For paired simulator or design decisions with high exposure-driven variance, a declared semantic coupling reduces matched referee episodes by at least 3× versus strong named-stream and shared-seed baselines while preserving each arm’s scenario law. H-22 and H-23 assume shared draws. This hypothesis tests whether compiling *what to share* changes the size and validity of the useful contrast.

**Mechanism.** In `frob-arena`, compile disturbances into independent counter-address domains for stable world/fixture fields, absolute physical time or properly coupled coarse/fine increments, and explicitly task-triggered semantic events. A slick strip stays in the same coordinates after a gait retiming. A fixed-clock push stays at the same time. A task-declared docking disturbance occurs at its declared gate. Use typed role mappings for common channels and reserve independent keys for unmatched roles, with the seed diagram and distribution in the task receipt. Choose only legal couplings using pilot seeds and freeze before confirmation. Equal marginal variance with a paired correlation increase from 0.50 to 0.95 would reduce contrast variance by 10×, since it scales with 1 − ρ in that illustration. Compare actual cost to reach the stated confidence width. [Common random numbers and the effect of timing](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4261/glasserman_yao_guidelines.pdf)

**Falsifier.** On a tiny wet-strip and docking example, insert unused random calls and retime steps. Independently verify world marginals and that each disturbance follows its declared occurrence rule. Any material or trajectory distribution changed by coupling is a failure. Kill the extra compiler if a held-out comparison shows less than 1.5× cost improvement over well-engineered named streams with the same error guarantees.

**Expected evidence.** Deterministic metamorphic receipts for time and changed topology, independent marginal checks or exact construction proof, and paired variance/decision-cost curves including failures and rare reversals on held-out seeds. The strongest negative result would be that lawful exposure matching changes little because policy and contact differences already dominate the contrast.
