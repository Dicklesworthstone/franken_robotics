## Answer to Q-001

Q-001 is open: no matched mission has been run on any pipeline, and the record holds no measurement of time to an
accepted decision. The earlier sentence "the realistic range is three to ten" is withdrawn as an answer. No single
mechanism delivers ten-fold, and no conjunction has been shown to. The denominator is an accepted decision, not a
training run: a 1% failure bound at 95% confidence costs 299 failure-free episodes per accepted design in every
arm. Two comparators give two questions. Against per-design training with a standard outer loop, paper arithmetic
gives about 3–3.5× on quadruped design cycles and about 15× on humanoid ones, if per-design training can be
replaced when designs are ranked; that condition rests on the two least measured links (H-02, H-22a). Against a
composed incumbent that hosts the same portable methods, the expected ratio on fixed-topology CUDA missions is
near one unless an engine-internal property binds; two are documented (a mixed-topology batch with run-to-run
replay; a Metal tier) and one speed question is open (H-04b). Engine speed is a parity requirement, with that one
qualification. The registered threshold of the question is unchanged.

## Disagreements kept open

Each has a test that settles it; `CHALLENGE_ADJUDICATION.md` §6 lists all nineteen with their tests.

- Whether a thin layer over an incumbent recovers the gain (H-19): the recovery ratio of a three-arm test.
- Whether design missions are dominated by training or by agent turns and diagnosis (H-18): a phase ledger and
  three single-change ablations.
- Whether cheap rungs rank designs as specialists do, and how well tiers correlate (H-22a, H-22b): the
  rank-agreement experiment on 40 designs.
- Whether a family policy holds within a class on interior terrain, and what an inherited edit costs (H-02, H-21):
  early-stopped from-scratch controls.
- Whether a sampling controller is more than a screen (H-01): a sample-count sweep on a design corpus, and the
  delivered-controller lane.
- Whether a structure-exploiting solve beats parity, and whether per-design code adds to it (H-04b, H-04): an
  operation ledger and our own profile.
- Whether rigid proxies reproduce the failures of coupled models (H-16): the proxy-adequacy pilot.
- Whether formal certificates change any design decision: no evidence either way; a record, for every certificate
  a decision consumed, of whether the decision would have differed without it.
