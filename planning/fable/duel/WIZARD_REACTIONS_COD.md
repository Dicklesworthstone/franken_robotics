## Where I changed my mind

I would promote heterogeneous model batching from a later optimization to an early interface decision, test class-scoped sampling MPC as a controller candidate, and use Claude’s coupled multilevel estimator as the default experiment for cheaper referee decisions. I would narrow my controller-transfer and route-evidence proposals. I still favor checked requirements search early, now as a bounded discrete-catalog solver. The distinction changes the implementation order and what each gate is allowed to claim. ### #1 · Requirements compiled into morphology search

**I agree on the expensive part.** A heavier transmission can alter contact and stability in opposite directions. A single interval or resource order will rarely certify all completions of an arbitrary articulated tree. My original design moved too quickly from a bounded catalog to a general partial-term compiler. Start with serial drive and payload assemblies with typed interfaces and a finite grammar. Give every cut an independently checked reason. Extend by constructor only where a new proof obligation pays for itself.

**I dispute the leaf-check comparison.** A leaf filter and a cut over a family of unconstructed leaves eliminate different work. A tight mass bound tied to a payload requirement can exclude a whole subtree before assembly, duplicate detection or controller evaluation. Exact interface summaries can also preserve a nondominated alternative that an agent’s point-by-point search overlooks. The reviewers are right that this advantage disappears when good incumbent proposal generators already visit only a few credible variants. Compare against a strongly seeded grammar search with the *same* leaf constraints, symmetry rules and bounds where it can use them. Include that strong baseline in the frozen 1,024-leaf test and report search cost and completeness. I keep the pilot, and restrict the product bet to composition where bounds are useful. [Claude’s review §4.5](WIZARD_SCORES_CC_ON_OTHERS.md) · [AGY’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_AGY_ON_OTHERS.md)

### #2 · Controller transfer across typed edits

**Both objections land.** An unchanged action dimension gives a competitive warm-start baseline. Stable frames and signs help prevent silent errors and make an edit diagnosable, but the proposed role-indexed residual stack needs a demonstrated performance gain to justify its coupling to controller structure. My low confidence on wheel-to-foot or limb changes is a reason to demote transfer as a standalone pillar.

**The stronger counterclaim is untested.** AGY treats ordinary fine-tuning as a proven 5–10× baseline for relevant edits and sampling MPC as a cold-start solution for arbitrary support changes. I would run both as competitive arms. DIAL-MPC demonstrates hard examples on tested embodiments, including quadrupedal hardware and simulated humanoid tasks. A novel wheel-leg with new actuation limits or a discontinuous support strategy still needs a tested prior, objective and finite-budget planner. An MPC teacher also offers a useful donor, so cold RL training need not be part of every transfer test. Keep `COD-18`’s semantic manifest and benchmark a reordered/mirrored actuator mapping and a modest gearing or mount change. Promote learning machinery only on measured time to matched held-out competence, with specialist fallbacks charged. [Claude’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_CC_ON_OTHERS.md) · [AGY’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_AGY_ON_OTHERS.md) · [DIAL-MPC project and demonstrations](https://lecar-lab.github.io/dial-mpc/)

### #3 · Randomized referee audits

**AGY changes my default.** Where pairs can be scheduled from a frozen distribution, a two-level coupled estimator is simpler to operate, avoids the small-propensity range penalty and already has a close upstream seam. Claude is also right to question my 0.05 illustrative propensity. A 20× maximum residual weight can eat the entire savings in an anytime decision with a rare reversal. Start with two-level allocation and paired referee-only racing. Include a prespecified risk-stratified variant before trying online audit allocation.

**I disagree with both categorical arguments.** AGY’s mathematical dominance claim needs assumptions about the information available before scheduling a referee case. If expensive residual risk is highly concentrated in a pattern visible only after a cheap rollout, it can be useful to sample that pattern more often and still account for the entire target distribution. The appropriate contest is against good offline or pilot-based stratification with the same access to features, including the cost of drawing cheap candidates. Claude’s claim that putting randomized audits on top of multilevel estimation automatically makes the combination better needs that same test. Correctly coupled telescoping is unbiased at any correlation. Correlation controls efficiency. Randomized audits instead provide validity for the particular *selective observation* rule.

For the same fixed bounded referee outcome `Y`, prediction `m(X)`, and an independent conditional audit draw with probability `p(X)`, the original estimator has excess variance `E[(1 − p)/p · (Y − m)²]` over observing `Y` on every row. That term is the price of saving evaluations. Let a cheap observed feature concentrate the residual variance in one stratum and the rate assignment can help under a fixed budget. Let rare reversals live in the low-rate stratum and wide intervals or poor resolution will expose the cost. If a bounded replay harness with a planted reversal and uniform null cases fails to show a decision-level win over stratified coupling at matched coverage, retire the online protocol. This is now a second estimator experiment, rather than a product feature. ### #4 · Reusable route-feasibility evidence

**Both reviewers are right about scope.** I ranked a precision tool for geometry-heavy wheeled tasks as if it would help general articulated design. Once doors, active fixtures or uncertain support modes enter, a rigid swept enclosure can create a large unresolved region. Drake IRIS/GCS and a cached planar SDF are credible comparisons. A simple dilation is the right first experiment, and interval reuse should earn its keep on a family of variants. [Drake geometry optimization](https://drake.mit.edu/pydrake/pydrake.geometry.optimization.html)

**The two-percent claim is a guess.** The useful comparison is repeated feasibility screening and downstream evaluations owed, including checking an approach turn, rerouting and distinguishing unknown scan cells from geometric impossibility. An accessibility-first warehouse brief can have several tight transitions that determine whether an entire layout is useful. If index setup fails to break even against a competent cached incumbent on that workload, the extra data structure should go. Keep the three-valued finding and a cheap footprint screen. Defer claimed cross-design geometric certificates until that gate. [Claude’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_CC_ON_OTHERS.md) · [AGY’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_AGY_ON_OTHERS.md)

### #5 · Replay interventions to rank repairs

**Their shared warning is correct.** Most permanent design changes still pay for reset, control and validation. My 0.07× example prices a short branch in a deliberately late failure. It cannot price the eventual repair. The new break-even question is how many expensive candidates or agent turns the branch avoids. If the fresh incumbent can propose the same top three from a checkpoint and a simple patch adapter, the branching speed ceiling alone buys little.

**I keep the idea and change the pilot.** Take AGY’s solver-artifact and actuator groups and Claude’s matched interventions. Stage 8–16 explicit temporary probes, run them under the same frozen controller and matched key schedule, and price optional replanning as a different arm. Rank candidate repairs and confirm a bounded shortlist with permanent reset runs on fresh seeds. Same-topology diagnostic branches can already batch. Topology changes and expensive adaptive control motivate heterogeneous batching and cheaper controllers for the broader loop. They are unnecessary dependencies for the first validation experiment. A solver perturbation triggers a numerical-sensitivity flag and referee follow-up. It cannot, by itself, say which model is correct. [Claude’s review and revised MPC branch budget](WIZARD_SCORES_CC_ON_OTHERS.md) · [AGY’s review](WIZARD_SCORES_AGY_ON_OTHERS.md)

### Other changes I would carry forward

- **Benchmark.** Treat my four briefs as smoke fixtures. Claude is right that they cannot support a product-wide speedup estimate. Freeze a larger held-out set sized for useful paired uncertainty after a separate timing pilot. Commission one strong end-to-end incumbent first and report per-family coverage. A full multi-engine bake-off belongs after the harness has one fair matched pair.
- **Architecture and control.** Both reviewers saw a dependency I underweighted. Make per-world model identity, snapshot identity and execution-shape separation early contracts. Exact-topology batches are a reasonable first implementation. Measure ragged padding and occupancy before replacing them. Evaluate CC #2’s scoped planner against v0.2 synthesis and native DIAL-MPC. Optimize only the measured kernel bottleneck. My 3× batching target was a useful component gate and a poor statement of the portfolio upside.

### Claude #4 · Paired multilevel referee estimation

**Best case.** FrankenRobotics will have to buy slower referee outcomes whenever accelerated physics can change a decision. Its strongest technical asset is the ability to run the *same question*, with like-for-like scenarios and outcomes, in two ways. Treat their disagreement as the quantity to pay for. On tasks where cheap and expensive tiers preserve the same ordering, every cheap run contributes information and the expensive tier samples the residual rather than the full scene-to-scene variability. That turns promotion from a late recurring toll into a measured part of designing a robot.

**Implementation I would back.** Use a single declared paired contrast for a frozen A-versus-B comparison. Start with cheap GPU contrasts on one seed block and matched expensive-minus-cheap contrasts on an independently drawn correction block. If a pilot supports it, use a frozen coefficient `β` and estimate `β E[L] + E[H − βL]`, with separate anytime sequences and a declared error split. Preserve pairing *within* each correction. Start with bounded success, capped energy conditional on a declared operational rule for failures, and planted equality and tier-reversal controls. Evaluate each tier under the same controller contract and distinguish a frozen controller from one that replans against its own tier. Compare paired-referee-only, the two-level estimator, and a version with the middle tier if the latter earns its cost. Record scene-constrained outcome variance, budget, ratio of resolved comparisons and unresolved reversals. Use disjoint pilot and confirmation data or an explicitly predictable allocation covered by the procedure. [Claude’s proposal §3.4](WIZARD_IDEAS_CC.md) · [Giles’s multilevel Monte Carlo review](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/~gilesm/mlmc.html) · [FrankenSim’s MLMC allocator](../../../../frankensim/crates/fs-uq/src/product_mlmc_allocation.rs)

**Why the numbers could matter.** In a fixed-precision two-level illustration with unit referee contrast variance and unit cheap variance, a cheap run at 0.01 of referee cost and correlation 0.99 gives unit-coefficient correction variance 0.02. The standard continuous allocation cost relative to referee-only is then approximately `(√0.01 + √(1.01 × 0.02))² ≈ 0.059`, about **17× lower cost** before pilots and sequential overhead. At 0.95 correlation under the same cost assumptions the factor is about 0.175, or **5.7×**. These are worked assumptions. The first test is to price this contour using contrast residuals already collected for the fidelity battery.

**Second-order payoff.** The residual becomes an engineering signal. If the correction budget is dominated by wheel slip or near misses on a stair, that points to where collision or stiction work is worth paying for. Well-coupled scenarios can make a referee with different numerical failure modes cheap enough to adjudicate discrepancies, within its declared validity limits.

**Two objections, both manageable within scope.** Contact divergence can destroy savings. Expose residual variance by prespecified regime and use direct referee racing for expensive residual strata. Repeatedly testing the best-looking challenger can create false promotions. Freeze finalists and confirm on fresh seeds with a declared error budget. The residual concern is that rare failures and close contests may occupy precisely the strata with poor coupling. Short segments from GPU initial states cannot repair that without changing the estimand or proving a valid recombination rule.

### AGY #10 · Pareto antichains for discrete catalog co-design

**Best case.** Every robot edit today is posed as a complete candidate. The expensive part is often the coupling between choices. A better motor pulls in a gearbox with different reflected inertia, changes controller bandwidth, increases cooling or battery demand, and moves the mass budget. A typed catalog solver can return every viable resource tradeoff at an interface, so the agent stops revisiting already dominated combinations. The mechanism is mathematical co-design as an engineering query. When a payload limit changes, it can ask which parts or interface assumptions are binding and recover a different viable frontier. [AGY’s roster #10](WIZARD_IDEAS_AGY.md) · [Censi’s mathematical theory of co-design](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08055)

**Implementation I would back.** Begin with a drive module for a cart or tracked carrier. Model motor, gear and battery interfaces by capability requirements and resource vectors, plus discrete compatibility keys for voltage, cooling, mounting and configuration. Recover all feasible leaves in a tiny exhaustive oracle. Intern compatible subassemblies and compose antichains of conservative capability and cost labels. A dominance witness is valid only within a matching continuation interface and a checked monotone order. For example, two motors in the same voltage and mount class can sometimes be compared on certified continuous torque across a stated speed band, total mass and cost. A claim based on peak torque alone would fail that test. Start with exact discrete labels, charge interval work and front sizes, optimize only the bottleneck, and expose resumable output with proof witnesses and an unresolved list. Use full control evaluation to adjudicate dynamic differences among survivors. The orchestrator can answer `frob design solve` with competing affordable architectures and active constraints.

**Second-order payoff.** The interface summaries give the agent a search space with meaningful engineering knobs, support reproducible replacement of discontinued parts, and make partial handoffs between agents auditable. A nondominated set makes a new price, payload or thermal limit cheap to reconsider. In a restricted catalog this can eliminate many repeated complete evaluations, even with an incumbent simulator on the downstream adjudication path.

**Two objections, both manageable within scope.** Dynamic rankings can be nonmonotone. Prune only on checked necessary requirements or guaranteed continuation dominance, and leave everything else for control and physics. Exact fronts can grow exponentially. Test adversarial front growth and cap exact exploration with explicit unresolved bounds. An approximate frontier may guide search, but an epsilon rule must not silently discard designs the task would distinguish. My residual concern is the fraction of useful branches that survive the strong incumbent’s prefilters. The small exhaustive pilot and a measured downstream rollout-effort comparison can settle that without a general compiler project.

## Two blind spots across the three slates

### 1. Bracket what the embodiment can achieve before paying to train every controller

**Gap.** CC #20 proposes a controller-free potential score from a chosen contact schedule. AGY #11 proposes safety envelopes for a reduced model. My #1 uses necessary conditions to prune partial designs. The policy-transfer and MPC proposals test rank agreement with PPO. The missing capability is an explicit **one-sided bound in each direction** on the best admissible controller for a design. A rollout from a competent frozen policy gives evidence of achieved performance. A sound optimistic relaxation gives a limit on possible performance. When those intervals overlap on a design decision, more controller effort may be valuable. When the best possible result is already below a competitor’s demonstrated performance, no more training is needed for that decision.

**Mechanism.** In `frob-opt` and `frob-analyze`, fix a bounded task utility, an actuator and observation contract, and a target scene law. For each design and scenario, construct an analytic or convex upper bound that admits every valid controller trajectory and can admit more. Start with one route having a documented elevation change, deadline, payload and initial/final state constraints. Use integrated energy, work and reach limits and an outward relaxation of wrench and driver constraints. A relaxation that silently omits valid trajectories or substitutes a single contact schedule fails. Use an independent feasible-controller rollout for the lower bound, with frozen seeds for development and fresh confirmation for the selected controller. For a stochastic brief, estimate the scene-averaged upper and achieved lower quantities with the declared uncertainty and simultaneous error budgets. Report proven exclusions within the relaxation’s scope, achieved feasibility, and undecided gaps. A stronger controller can tighten the lower bound without invalidating an independently justified upper bound. The sandbox example needs only the plan’s typed effort, energy and geometric primitives. Whole-body certifying relaxations are optional research. [Optimal-control relaxations, Lasserre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703377)

**Lever.** Target **10× lower controller effort per accepted design** on one energy- or torque-limited brief, versus standard static filters followed by tuned MPC, warm starts or successive halving with the same training budget and truth protocol. A simple budget illustration is that ruling out 93% of candidates before training leaves 7% of their baseline controller work and 3% overhead for a 10× controller-stage gain. Report the system-level time and resolution rate separately. The real unlock is being able to tell an agent whether effort belongs on the body or the controller.

**Cheapest falsifier.** Use a small exhaustively solvable planar ramp or carrier model with a planted feasible candidate requiring a different control strategy, plus variants where battery limits make the task impossible. Validate the bound against the independent exhaustive solution and seed one deliberately unsound relaxation. Kill automatic exclusions on the first false rejection. Kill the extra optimizer work if a held-out task family shows less than 3× controller-cost reduction at matched decisions, or if the gaps stay so wide that the uncertainty label rarely changes what the agent does. Keep direct task limits and the MPC/rollout comparison.

### 2. Compile common randomness from the task, with an explicit test of what “the same world” means

**Gap.** All three slates rely on shared seeds or world addresses. None gives the engine a first-class mapping from the *physical meaning of a disturbance* to its random variable. A robot with two extra contacts can shift call order. A shorter footfall period can give the same tick-indexed disturbance a different physical meaning. A wet patch encountered at a different time should remain the same patch, while a wall-clock push must still occur at the scheduled time. Ambiguous coupling damages the benchmark, multilevel residuals and replay interventions.

**Mechanism.** Add a task scenario compiler to `frob-arena` and a shared stream registry in `frob-scene/chronicle`. Author a finite typed schema with three deterministic address domains. World fields cache keyed samples at stable coordinate cells or fixture IDs. Exogenous processes key by physical time or use properly coupled coarse/fine increments. Event-triggered rules key by semantic mission role and occurrence, and exist only when the scenario explicitly defines that exposure, such as a disturbance at a docking gate. Stable role maps come from typed designs and edits. On the device these are packed domain IDs with counter-addressable kernels, in the spirit of `fs-rand` Philox. Maintain fixed marginals for every engine, control policy and morphology under the declared scenario. Unshared role noise draws from a reserved domain. Select among legal couplings on pilot seeds, then lock the coupling, version the generated mapping and freeze confirmation seeds. Measure both marginals independently as well as the resulting contrast variance. [Common random numbers, Glasserman and Yao](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4261/glasserman_yao_guidelines.pdf) · [Philox implementation](../../../../frankensim/crates/fs-rand/src/philox.rs)

**Lever.** Aim for **10× less referee work at equal decision uncertainty** in a scenario suite where the dominant variance came from mismatched exposure. For equal marginal variance, a change from paired correlation 0.50 to 0.95 gives 10× lower variance of the contrast at fixed sample count. That is an illustrative threshold, not a prediction. Compare independent seeds, conventional seeded matched scenes with named streams, and the compiled exposure protocol. Use the strongest one on each brief. Also measure how often replay diagnoses change when unused channels, contact opportunities or substeps are perturbed.

**Cheapest falsifier.** Build a tiny deterministic fixture with a spatially keyed slick strip, a fixed-time disturbance and an explicitly event-triggered disturbance, then evaluate a baseline and deliberately retimed controller. Insert unused RNG calls and switch the physics rate. The spatial patch and wall-clock event must remain fixed, the declared event-triggered exposure must follow its gate, and per-arm marginal outcomes must match independent sampling within a prespecified tolerance. A changed marginal or a time-triggered push that follows gait phase kills that coupling. Drop the compiler to a few explicit stream adapters if a held-out slip and docking battery shows less than 2× improvement over competent named streams at matched confidence cost. The proposal earns broader scope only if it improves the decisions that actually consume referee time.
